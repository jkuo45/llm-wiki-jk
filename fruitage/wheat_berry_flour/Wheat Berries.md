# Wheat Berries

[[Wheat Berries]] are the whole kernel of the [[Wheat]] plant, consisting of the [[Bran]], [[Germ]], and [[Endosperm]] after the [[Husk]] (chaff) has been removed. They are the most complete, unprocessed form of the wheat grain.

## Nutritional Profile
- **Composition**: Contains all three parts of the grain: the fiber-rich [[Bran]], the nutrient-dense [[Germ]], and the starchy [[Endosperm]].
- **Protein**: Varies from **6–9%** (soft wheat) to **10–14%** (hard wheat).
- **Fiber**: High dietary fiber content, primarily found in the [[Bran]].
- **Micronutrients**: Rich in B-vitamins, iron, and magnesium, particularly when the [[Germ]] is intact.

## Culinary Uses
Wheat berries have a nutty flavor and a chewy texture, making them versatile in various cuisines.
- **Whole Grain Salads**: Used as a hearty base for salads, often paired with vegetables and vinaigrettes.
- **Porridges and Cereals**: Boiled or soaked for traditional dishes like [[Kutia]] (Eastern European) or [[Frumenty]] (Medieval English).
- **Side Dishes**: A nutritious alternative to rice or couscous, such as in the French dish [[Ebly]].
- **Sprouting**: Can be sprouted to increase nutrient bioavailability and used in salads or breads.

## Processing and Derivatives
- **Hulling**: The mechanical removal of the outer [[Husk]].
- **Milling**: Grinding the berries to produce [[Whole-wheat flour]] or [[Milled Wheat]].
- **Parboiling**: Specifically processed to create [[Bulgur]].
