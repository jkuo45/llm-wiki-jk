# Neurotoxin

A [[Neurotoxin]] is any endogenous or exogenous substance that disrupts the structure or function of the nervous system, typically by inducing neuronal injury or death. In neuromelanin and Parkinson's research, a defined set of selective neurotoxins is used to model the selective loss of catecholaminergic neurons and to probe why [[Neuromelanin]]-containing cells are preferentially vulnerable. These agents validate the environmental and mitochondrial etiologies of [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Key Neurotoxins in Parkinson's Research

- **[[MPP+]]** (1-methyl-4-phenylpyridinium): the active metabolite of [[MPTP]], taken up by the [[Dopamine Transporter]] and inhibiting mitochondrial [[Complex I]].
- **6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA):** an oxidized catecholamine analog that generates [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and is selectively accumulated by catecholaminergic neurons; widely used in rodents.
- **[[Paraquat]]** and **rotenone:** environmental compounds that, respectively, produce ROS and inhibit Complex I, mimicking sporadic PD.
- **[[Manganese]]** (causing [[Manganism]]): a metal toxin impairing [[Complex II]] and basal ganglia function.

## Mechanisms of Selective Neuronal Injury

Most of these toxins converge on three intersecting pathways: (1) [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and energy failure; (2) [[Oxidative Stress]] from redox cycling and catecholamine auto-oxidation into [[Aminochromes]]; and (3) impaired [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]] and protein clearance. Their selectivity for dopaminergic neurons arises from the very biology that defines those cells—high catecholamine turnover, DAT-mediated uptake, and accumulation of [[Neuromelanin]], which both concentrates toxins/metals and generates radical species.

## Neuromelanin as a Vulnerability Factor

The observation that pigmented neurons are preferentially killed by MPP⁺ and related toxins suggests [[Neuromelanin]] marks a metabolically "hot" and oxidatively stressed compartment. Whether pigment protects by sequestering quinones or endangers by harboring redox-active iron remains a central debate, but toxins clearly exploit pigment-associated vulnerabilities.

## Research and Ethical Use

Neurotoxins are indispensable for replicating PD pathology in animals and for screening [[Neuroprotection]] drugs. They are also central to occupational and environmental epidemiology linking pesticide/herbicide exposure to human PD risk.
