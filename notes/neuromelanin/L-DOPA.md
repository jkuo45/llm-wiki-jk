# L-DOPA

[[L-DOPA]] (L-3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine) is a naturally occurring aromatic amino acid and the immediate biosynthetic precursor to the neurotransmitter [[Dopamine]]. It is the most effective symptomatic treatment for [[Parkinson's Disease]] and a central molecule linking catecholamine synthesis, [[Neuromelanin]] biosynthesis, and dopaminergic neuropharmacology.

## Biosynthesis and Metabolism
L-DOPA is produced from [[L-Tyrosine]] by the rate-limiting enzyme [[Tyrosine Hydroxylase]] (using tetrahydrobiopterin as cofactor), then decarboxylated to dopamine by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC; also called DOPA decarboxylase). Dopamine is subsequently oxidized to [[Dopamine o-quinone]], the branchpoint that feeds both redox cycling and neuromelanin polymerization. In peripheral and central melanogenesis, L-DOPA is also a substrate for [[Tyrosinase]], linking it to [[Eumelanin]] formation.

## Pharmacological Action and Clinical Use
L-DOPA crosses the blood-brain barrier via the large neutral amino acid transporter (unlike dopamine, which cannot), then is decarboxylated intracranially to replenish striatal dopamine depleted by nigral neuron loss. It is almost always given with a peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitor (carbidopa or benserazide) to prevent peripheral conversion and nausea, and sometimes with a COMT inhibitor to prolong half-life.
- **Efficacy**: It is the "gold standard" for managing PD motor symptoms ([[Hypokinesia]], rigidity, tremor).
- **Chronic complications**: Long-term use is associated with motor fluctuations and [[Dyskinesia|dyskinesias]], wearing-off, and on-off phenomena, driven by pulsatile dopamine receptor stimulation and progressive loss of storage capacity in remaining [[Substantia Nigra]] terminals.

## Relationship to Neuromelanin
Because L-DOPA is a direct precursor to dopamine and to dopamine quinone, it is also a substrate for neuromelanin formation. Some research explores whether exogenous L-DOPA might alter pigment burden or oxidative load in pigmented neurons, though its clinical benefit overwhelmingly reflects dopamine restoration rather than pigment effects.

## Pathology and Research Relevance
L-DOPA remains first-line PD therapy, but its limitations motivate continuous-release formulations, duodenal infusions, and adjuncts (MAO-B inhibitors, amantadine). Understanding L-DOPA pharmacokinetics (absorption competition with dietary amino acids, erratic gastric emptying) is essential to managing fluctuations. Its discovery validated the dopaminergic basis of PD anticipated by the nigral pathology described by [[Konstantin Nikolaevich Tretiakoff]], and it remains the benchmark against which neuroprotective and device-based therapies are measured.
