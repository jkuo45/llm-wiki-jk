# Antigen Presentation

[[Antigen Presentation]] is the process by which cells process intracellular or extracellular proteins into peptide fragments and display them on major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules for surveillance by T cells. In the context of [[Parkinson's Disease]], aberrant antigen presentation by [[Neuromelanin]]-containing neurons and surrounding glia has been proposed as a mechanism of immune-mediated selective vulnerability.

## Overview

Classical MHC class I (MHC-I) presentation loads cytosolic peptides onto MHC-I for recognition by CD8+ cytotoxic T cells; MHC class II (MHC-II) presents extracellular-derived peptides to CD4+ helper T cells. In the healthy central nervous system, neurons are generally "immune-privileged," expressing low levels of MHC. However, inflammatory cues—interferons, [[Cytokines]], and [[Neuroinflammation]]—can induce MHC expression on neurons and microglia.

## Neuronal MHC-I in Parkinson's Disease

Post-mortem studies of PD substantia nigra report upregulated neuronal MHC-I in dopaminergic, [[Neuromelanin]]-laden neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]]. Because these neurons accumulate abundant intracellular protein turnover (including [[Alpha-synuclein Aggregation]] and damaged mitochondria from [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]), they may present neo-antigens or altered self-peptides. Cytotoxic CD8+ T cells infiltrating the nigra could then target these neurons, contributing to selective loss.

## Links to Pigment and Proteostasis

[[Neuromelanin]] granules sequester peptides and can modulate antigen processing, but their failure with aging may expose neo-antigens. Shared genetic risk between PD and immune loci (e.g., HLA) further supports an immune component. The model connects peripheral [[Lymphocytes]], microglial [[Antigen Presentation]], and neuronal vulnerability into a unified pathogenic loop.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

If antigen presentation drives neuronal loss, immunomodulatory strategies (checkpoint modulation, T-cell tolerance induction) become candidate therapies. Biomarkers of CNS immune activation are under investigation. This pathway also explains how [[Neuroinflammation]] and protein misfolding converge on the same vulnerable cell population.
