# Dolichol

[[Dolichol]] is a long-chain polyisoprenoid lipid identified as a major lipid component of human [[Neuromelanin]] granules in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]]. Its presence reshapes the view of neuromelanin from a simple pigment to a complex lipopigment organelle with membrane-like properties.

## Structure & Biosynthesis

Dolichols are α-saturated polyisoprenols (typically 17–21 isoprene units in humans) synthesized via the mevalonate pathway and modified in the endoplasmic reticulum. Best known for serving as the lipid anchor for the oligosaccharide carrier in N-linked glycosylation (dolichol phosphate), free dolichol also accumulates in lysosomes and aging tissue as a marker of senescence.

## Role in Neuromelanin Granules

Biochemical isolation of neuromelanin granules shows they are rich in dolichol and dolichyl esters, co-localized with protein, lipid, and the pigment polymer. Dolichol's long hydrophobic chain likely integrates into the granule membrane/matrix, influencing its fluidity, permeability, and interaction with catechol quinones such as [[Aminochrome]] and [[5,6-indolequinone]]. Elevated granule dolichol may reflect altered isoprenoid metabolism or lysosomal storage in aging dopaminergic neurons.

## Physiological & Pathological Implications

As neurons age, free dolichol rises in many tissues, and its accumulation in neuromelanin granules could alter granule stability and metal handling. Because dolichol synthesis shares the mevalonate pathway with [[Coenzyme Q10]] and cholesterol, perturbations may couple pigment biology to mitochondrial and membrane homeostasis. Whether dolichol content differs in [[Parkinson's Disease]] substantia nigra is an open question with implications for granule fragility and [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Research & Clinical Relevance

Dolichol in neuromelanin highlights the organelle-like nature of pigment granules and their intersection with lysosomal and isoprenoid metabolism. It connects to [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]] and to the broader lipofuscin/pigment-aging literature, suggesting that granule lipid composition is a modifiable factor in neuronal resilience and a potential biomarker of dopaminergic aging.
