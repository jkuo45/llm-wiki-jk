# Lymphocytes

[[Lymphocytes]] are agranular white blood cells of the adaptive immune system, comprising B cells, T cells (CD4+ helper, CD8+ cytotoxic, regulatory), and natural killer (NK) cells. They mediate antigen-specific immunity through antibodies (B cells), cell-mediated cytotoxicity, and cytokine signaling (T cells). In [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD), lymphocytes—particularly T cells—have been found to infiltrate the [[Substantia Nigra]], especially in regions containing [[Neuromelanin]], implicating adaptive immunity in neurodegeneration.

## Types and Immune Functions
- **B cells** produce antibodies (including [[Immunoglobulin G]]) and present antigen.
- **T cells** coordinate responses and directly kill infected or stressed cells via MHC class I/II recognition.
- **NK cells** provide innate-like cytotoxicity without prior sensitization.
Central to CNS immunity is the major histocompatibility complex (MHC), which presents peptide antigens to T-cell receptors; neurons under stress may upregulate MHC class I, becoming targets for CD8+ T cells.

## Mechanism: Lymphocyte Involvement in PD
Postmortem PD brains show perivascular and parenchymal T-cell infiltrates in the substantia nigra, often clustered around neuromelanin-laden neurons. Proposed mechanisms include:
- **Blood-brain barrier breach**: Age and [[Oxidative Stress]] permit lymphocyte trafficking into the parenchyma.
- **Antigen presentation**: Neuromelanin or [[Alpha-synuclein]] peptides presented on MHC may activate autoreactive T cells.
- **Cytotoxic attack**: CD8+ T cells release perforin/granzyme or Fas ligand, killing stressed dopaminergic neurons.
- **Bystander inflammation**: Lymphocyte cytokines amplify microglial [[Neuroinflammation]], worsening injury.

This adaptive response complements the innate microglial activation long recognized in PD.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance
While immune surveillance is protective against infection and clears debris, maladaptive lymphocyte activity may accelerate the loss of pigmented neurons. Peripheral blood studies report altered T-cell subsets and alpha-synuclein-reactive T cells in PD patients, supporting an autoimmune component. The intimacy of lymphocytes with neuromelanin-rich regions suggests pigment-related antigens (quinone-modified proteins, iron-bound complexes) could be triggers.

## Clinical and Research Implications
Lymphocyte profiling in blood or cerebrospinal fluid is being developed as a PD biomarker, and immunomodulatory strategies (e.g., suppressing autoreactive T cells, fostering regulatory T cells) are under investigation as disease-modifying approaches. Understanding the lymphocyte–neuromelanin interface also clarifies why [[Immunoglobulin G]] deposits colocalize with nigral degeneration. Ultimately, balancing protective versus destructive immunity is a frontier in PD therapeutics, with lymphocytes as both diagnostic signals and therapeutic targets.
