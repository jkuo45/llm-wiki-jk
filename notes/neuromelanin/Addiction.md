# Addiction

[[Addiction]] is a chronic, relapsing disorder characterized by compulsive drug seeking and use despite harmful consequences, driven by persistent neuroplastic changes in reward and salience circuits. The mesolimbic and [[Nigrostriatal System]] dopaminergic pathways—originating in the [[Ventral Tegmental Area]] and [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]]—are central to its neurobiology.

## Overview

Addiction spans substances (e.g., [[Nicotine]], opioids, psychostimulants, alcohol) and behaviors. A unifying mechanism is the supraphysiological dopaminergic surge in the nucleus accumbens that reinforces drug-associated cues, coupled with gradual desensitization of reward signaling and recruitment of anti-reward stress systems (CRF, dynorphin) that drive negative affect during withdrawal.

## Neurocircuitry and Dopamine

Drugs of abuse converge on the [[Basal Ganglia]] reward circuitry. Psychostimulants block reuptake or reverse transport of [[Dopamine]]; nicotine engages nicotinic receptors on dopamine neurons to increase firing; opioids disinhibit VTA dopamine cells. Over time, corticostriatal synaptic plasticity consolidates cue–reward associations, and habits shift control from the ventral to the dorsal striatum, contributing to compulsive use.

## Dopamine, Neuromelanin, and Parkinson's Disease

The dopaminergic system links addiction to [[Parkinson's Disease]] in two ways. First, chronic dopaminergic stimulation (e.g., misuse of [[L-DOPA]] or dopamine agonists) can precipitate impulse-control disorders in PD patients. Second, epidemiological data show that [[Nicotine]] smoking is associated with a reduced risk of developing PD, an observation attributed to nicotinic receptor-mediated protection of the [[Nigrostriatal System]] and perhaps modulation of [[Neuromelanin]]-containing neurons. This inverse association has motivated research into nicotine-receptor agonists as neuroprotective agents.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Addiction and PD illustrate the dual-edged nature of dopamine signaling: too little produces parkinsonism, too much can hijack motivational learning. Understanding shared dopaminergic substrates informs both addiction treatments (e.g., dopamine-receptor modulators, cue-exposure therapy) and PD management, where dopaminergic therapy must balance motor benefit against dyskinesia and behavioral side effects.
