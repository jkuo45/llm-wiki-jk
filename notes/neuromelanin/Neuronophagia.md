# Neuronophagia

[[Neuronophagia]] is a pathological process in which activated microglia or macrophages surround, invade, and phagocytize a degenerating neuron or its debris. In the parkinsonian brain, neuronophagia is a histological hallmark signalling an active, ongoing neurodegenerative process centered on [[Neuromelanin]]-containing neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]].

## Cellular Mechanism

When a pigmented dopaminergic neuron dies—whether from [[MPTP]] toxicity, [[Oxidative Stress]], or α-synucleinopathy—it releases intracellular [[Neuromelanin]] into the extracellular space. This extracellular pigment is a potent chemoattractant and activator of microglia, which extend processes, envelop the doomed neuron, and engulf both the cell body and the liberated pigment granules. The process is a specialized form of [[Phagocytosis]] executed by cells in a state of [[Microgliosis]].

## Role in Parkinson's Disease

- **Extracellular Neuromelanin as trigger:** The released pigment drives further glial activation, feeding [[Neuroinflammation]].
- **Microglial response:** Clusters of activated microglia containing neuromelanin granules are routinely observed in PD substantia nigra, evidence of attempted clearance.
- **Localization:** Neuronophagia is highly concentrated in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] and other neuromelanin-rich regions ([[Locus Coeruleus]], [[Medulla Oblongata]] DMV), paralleling the topography of [[Lewy Bodies]].

## Dual Significance

Neuronophagia is initially beneficial—removing toxic debris and pigment that would otherwise amplify inflammation. However, incomplete or chronic phagocytosis perpetuates glial activation and cytokine release, contributing to the spreading, progressive nature of neurodegeneration. The presence of neuromelanin within microglia also serves as a useful neuropathological marker of prior neuronal loss.

## Research and Biomarker Value

Quantifying neuronophagic foci and intracellular pigment in post-mortem tissue helps stage disease severity. In vivo, the same process is indirectly tracked via markers of [[Microgliosis]] (TSPO-PET) and pigment-sensitive [[Magnetic Resonance Imaging]]. Understanding how to enhance safe clearance without sustaining inflammation is a [[Neuroprotection]] goal.
