# Iron

[[Iron]] is an essential transition metal central to oxygen transport, electron transfer, and enzyme catalysis. In the brain, [[Neuromelanin]] is the principal iron-storage molecule within dopaminergic neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]], binding the metal via its catechol-quinone and indole moieties. While iron is required for normal metabolism, excess or miscompartmentalized iron promotes [[Oxidative Stress]] through Fenton chemistry, contributing to neurodegeneration in [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Biological Roles
Iron exists physiologically as Fe2+ (ferrous) and Fe3+ (ferrous). It is incorporated into heme (cytochromes, hemoglobin) and iron-sulfur clusters (respiratory chain complexes I–III), and serves as a cofactor for enzymes such as tyrosine hydroxylase and [[Monoamine oxidase B]]. Tight regulation by ferroportin, transferrin, ferritin, and iron-regulatory proteins maintains safe pools and prevents free catalytic iron.

## Iron and Neuromelanin
Neuromelanin granules bind substantial iron—up to ~30% of total nigral iron—acting as a neuronal metal reservoir. This sequestration is likely protective, detoxifying reactive free iron. However, the same strong binding means aged, iron-laden neuromelanin accumulates over decades, and the redox-active metal can participate in cycling reactions at the granule surface.

## Chemical Process: Fenton Reaction and Oxidative Stress
Free or weakly bound Fe2+ reacts with hydrogen peroxide to generate the hydroxyl radical (•OH), a highly damaging species (Fenton reaction: Fe2+ + H2O2 → Fe3+ + •OH + OH−). Neuromelanin itself can both catalyze and buffer these reactions. When antioxidant defenses (glutathione, ferritin) are overwhelmed, iron-driven ROS damages lipids, proteins, and DNA. Iron also accelerates dopamine oxidation to [[Dopamine o-quinone]], feeding the neuromelanin-building and quinone-toxic cycles.

## Pathology and Clinical Relevance
Postmortem PD brains show elevated iron in the substantia nigra, with displaced iron from neuromelanin as neurons degenerate. Iron accumulation correlates with motor severity and with [[Lewy Bodies]] pathology. MRI-based铁 quantification (R2*/susceptibility mapping) is therefore studied as a PD biomarker and progression tracker. Therapeutically, iron chelators (e.g., deferiprone) and modulation of iron homeostasis are under investigation to slow nigral degeneration. Iron thus sits at the intersection of pigment biology, oxidative stress, and selective vulnerability: a necessary nutrient that, in the catecholaminergic neuron, becomes a liability amplified by neuromelanin's own metal affinity.
