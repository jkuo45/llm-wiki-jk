# Medulla Oblongata

The [[Medulla Oblongata]] is the caudal segment of the brainstem, continuous with the spinal cord below and the [[Pons]] above. It contains vital autonomic and sensorimotor nuclei—including the cardiovascular and respiratory centers, the nucleus ambiguus, and the [[Dorsal Motor Nucleus of the Vagus]] (DMV). The DMV is one of the principal collections of [[Neuromelanin]]-pigmented neurons outside the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]], making the medulla a key node in the topographic spread of [[Parkinson's Disease]] pathology.

## Neuromelanin in the Medulla

In humans, DMV neurons accumulate neuromelanin and, like nigral neurons, are catecholaminergic (cholinergic in the DMV, but pigmented). Braak and colleagues demonstrated that PD-associated [[Lewy Bodies]] and [[Alpha-synuclein]] pathology appear first in the olfactory bulb and the DMV of the medulla (Braak stage 1) — preceding and potentially seeding later involvement of the substantia nigra. Thus medullary pigmented neurons are among the earliest targets of the synucleinopathy cascade.

## Functional and Clinical Relevance

Degeneration of DMV neurons contributes to the non-motor, autonomic manifestations of PD: constipation, gastroparesis, and cardiac vagal denervation. Because the vagus is a proposed conduit for ascending α-synuclein pathology (the "gut-to-brain" hypothesis), the medullary DMV may be an entry or relay station. The presence of [[Neuromelanin]] in these neurons parallels their vulnerability, reinforcing the link between pigment and selective degeneration seen throughout the brainstem.

## Relationship to Other Pigmented Nuclei

The medulla's DMV, together with the locus coeruleus and substantia nigra, forms a rostrocaudal continuum of neuromelanin-rich nuclei that degenerate in a stereotypic sequence in PD. Neuromelanin-sensitive [[Magnetic Resonance Imaging]] is beginning to visualize these medullary changes, offering biomarkers for prodromal disease.
