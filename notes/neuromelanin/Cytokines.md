# Cytokines

[[Cytokines]] are small secreted signaling proteins (including interleukins, chemokines, interferons, and growth factors) that mediate communication between immune and resident cells. In the CNS, microglia and infiltrating [[Lymphocytes]] release cytokines that coordinate [[Neuroinflammation]], a process increasingly implicated in [[Parkinson's Disease]] progression.

## Overview & Major Families

Key pro-inflammatory cytokines include TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6; anti-inflammatory members include IL-10 and TGF-β; chemokines (e.g., CCL2, CXCL10) recruit leukocytes. Cytokines act via cell-surface receptors triggering JAK/STAT, NF-κB, and MAPK cascades that alter gene expression, cell survival, and antigen presentation (see [[Antigen Presentation]]).

## Cytokines in the Parkinsonian Brain

In PD, activated [[Microglia]] in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] release TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, which can (a) exacerbate [[Oxidative Stress]] in [[Neuromelanin]]-laden neurons, (b) promote [[Alpha-Synuclein Aggregation]] through inflammatory signaling, and (c) upregulate neuronal MHC-I, enabling immune targeting. Extracellular α-synuclein itself acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern, further activating glia—a self-amplifying inflammatory loop.

## Peripheral and Systemic Signals

Peripheral immune cells and circulating cytokines can access the brain via circumventricular organs and a compromised blood–brain barrier. The systemic inflammatory state ("inflammaging") may lower the threshold for nigral degeneration. Depression and fatigue in PD correlate with elevated peripheral cytokine profiles, linking immunity to non-motor symptoms.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Anti-inflammatory and cytokine-modulating strategies (TNF inhibitors, IL-6 blockade, CCR2 antagonists) are under investigation for PD disease modification. Cytokine levels in serum/CSF are studied as biomarkers of neuroinflammation. Understanding cytokine networks is essential to integrate the immune axis with oxidative, lysosomal, and proteostatic hypotheses of parkinsonism.
