# A53T

A53T is a specific missense mutation in the [[Alpha-synuclein]] gene (SNCA) that substitutes threonine for alanine at position 53 of the protein. It is one of the earliest identified autosomal-dominant causal mutations for early-onset familial [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Overview

The SNCA gene encodes α-synuclein, a small (140-amino-acid) intrinsically disordered presynaptic protein. The A53T substitution lies within the central hydrophobic non-amyloid-β component (NAC) region that mediates aggregation. First described in Greek and Italian kindreds, A53T causes parkinsonism typically beginning before age 50, often with prominent autonomic and neuropsychiatric features and sometimes atypical presentations such as dementia or amyotrophy.

## Mechanism of Pathogenesis

Biophysical studies show that the A53T substitution destabilizes the native monomeric ensemble and accelerates the formation of fibrillogenic intermediates, shifting the aggregation landscape toward toxic oligomers. This promotes [[Alpha-synuclein Aggregation]] and the deposition of [[Lewy Bodies]]. The mutation also appears to increase membrane-binding affinity and may impair the normal physiological roles of α-synuclein in vesicle trafficking and [[Synaptic Plasticity]]. Unlike simple overexpression of wild-type α-synuclein, the A53T variant exhibits a qualitatively altered structure–toxicity relationship.

## Familial Context

A53T is one of several pathogenic SNCA mutations (alongside A30P and E46K) that establish α-synuclein pathology as a primary driver of PD. Its autosomal-dominant inheritance with high penetrance provided critical evidence that α-synuclein misfolding is sufficient to cause parkinsonism, anchoring the [[Alpha-Synuclein Aggregation]] hypothesis of disease.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Patients with A53T mutations show severe loss of dopaminergic neurons in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] and marked reductions in striatal dopamine. The mutation informs therapeutic strategies aimed at lowering α-synuclein production (antisense oligonucleotides, small-molecule stabilizers) or enhancing clearance via the [[Autophagy]]–lysosome pathway. Genotyping for SNCA mutations is relevant in early-onset or strongly familial PD.
