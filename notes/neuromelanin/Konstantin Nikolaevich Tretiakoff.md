# Konstantin Nikolaevich Tretiakoff

[[Konstantin Nikolaevich Tretiakoff]] (also spelled Constantin Tretiakoff; 1892–1956) was a Russian-born neuroanatomist whose 1919 doctoral thesis established the loss of pigmented neurons in the [[Substantia Nigra]] as the cardinal pathological correlate of [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD). His work linked the motor syndrome described by James Parkinson (1817) to a discrete brainstem lesion, laying the anatomical foundation for modern parkinsonian research.

## Biography and Training
Tretiakoff trained in Imperial Russia and pursued his medical doctorate in Paris under the guidance of the neuropathologist Jules-Joseph Dejerine at the Salpêtrière. His dissertation, "Étude sur les lésions de la substance noire de Sommering et leurs rapports avec la maladie de Parkinson" (1919), was based on meticulous histological examination of postmortem brains from parkinsonian patients.

## Scientific Contribution
Prior to Tretiakoff, PD was understood largely as a clinical entity. He demonstrated that the [[Substantia Nigra]]—the "black substance" rich in [[Neuromelanin]]—underwent severe depigmentation and neuronal loss in PD, and he described the associated fibrillary changes (later recognized as related to [[Lewy Bodies]], though Friedrich Lewy's descriptions came slightly later). By pinpointing the nigra as the seat of pathology, Tretiakoff connected clinical parkinsonism to catecholamine neuron degeneration, a insight validated decades later by the discovery of striatal dopamine deficiency and the efficacy of [[L-DOPA]].

## Historical Significance
Tretiakoff's findings were initially slow to gain traction but became cornerstones of PD neuropathology. His identification of the pigmented nucleus explained why [[Neuromelanin]]-containing neurons are selectively vulnerable and anticipated the neurotransmitter-based therapeutics that transformed the disease. The "Tretiakoff nucleus" convention and his staging observations influenced later frameworks, including Braak staging of synucleinopathy spread through the brainstem.

## Legacy and Research Relevance
Today Tretiakoff is honored as the anatomist who first localized PD to the substantia nigra, bridging clinical neurology and cellular pathology. His work underpins every subsequent advance—from [[MPTP]] models of selective nigral toxicity to neuromelanin imaging with [[Flortaucipir]]. Recognizing his contribution clarifies the historical throughline from pigment observation to molecular understanding of PD, and emphasizes that the neuromelanin neuron was the original pathological landmark of the disease.
