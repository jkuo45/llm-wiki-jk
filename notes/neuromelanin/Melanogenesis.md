# Melanogenesis

[[Melanogenesis]] is the biosynthesis of melanin pigments, best characterized in cutaneous [[Melanocytes]] but also operative—in modified form—in neuromelanin-producing neurons. The pathway converts the amino acid [[L-Tyrosine]] into a heterogeneous polymer through a cascade of oxidations and cyclizations catalyzed chiefly by [[Tyrosinase]]. Understanding melanogenesis is central to neuromelanin research because the same catecholamine-handling chemistry that makes skin pigment also generates the [[Aminochromes]] that polymerize into [[Neuromelanin]] in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]].

## The Core Enzymatic Cascade

1. **Tyrosinase initiation:** Tyrosinase hydroxylates L-Tyrosine to [[L-DOPA]], then oxidizes L-DOPA to dopaquinone.
2. **Branch point:** Under low cysteine, dopaquinone cyclizes to dopachrome, rearranges (via dopachrome tautomerase/TRP2 and DHICA oxidase/TRP1) to indoles, and polymerizes into [[Eumelanin]]. Under high cysteine, it forms cysteinyldopas and ultimately [[Pheomelanin]].
3. **Regulation:** The [[MC1R]]/cAMP axis promotes eumelanogenesis; ASIP/Agouti opposes it.

## Neuromelanogenesis in the Brain

In dopaminergic neurons, the substrate is not tyrosine alone but the catecholamines [[Dopamine]], [[L-DOPA]], and [[Norepinephrine]] themselves. Their auto-oxidation and enzyme-catalyzed oxidation yield dopaquinone, aminochromes, and quinone methides that covalently cross-link proteins, lipids, and metals into the insoluble, granular [[Neuromelanin]]. This process is partly non-enzymatic and accelerates with age and catecholamine turnover. Unlike skin melanin (exported in melanosomes), neuromelanin remains intracellular, accumulating lifelong and interacting with [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]].

## Protective vs. Pathological Roles

Melanogenesis produces a redox-active polymer: eumelanin can buffer free radicals (protective), yet the quinone intermediates and pigment-bound iron can promote [[Oxidative Stress]] (pathological). The balance determines whether pigment shields the neuron or contributes to its vulnerability in [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Research Relevance

Melanogenesis enzymes (tyrosinase, TRP1/2) and their inhibitors are studied both for pigmentation disorders and as probes of neuromelanin formation. Modeling neuromelanogenesis in vitro helps dissect how [[Alpha-synuclein]] and pigment co-localize in [[Lewy Bodies]].

## Connections

- [[Tyrosinase]] - rate-limiting enzyme initiating melanogenesis.
- [[L-Tyrosine]] - amino acid precursor of the pathway.
- [[L-DOPA]] - intermediate and also a neuromelanin precursor.
- [[Neuromelanin]] - brain pigment formed by neuronal melanogenesis.
- [[Eumelanin]] - black/brown end-product in skin.
- [[Pheomelanin]] - red/yellow cysteine-derived product.
- [[MC1R]] - master regulator of eumelanin vs. pheomelanin fate.
- [[Dopamine]] - catecholamine substrate for neuromelanogenesis.
