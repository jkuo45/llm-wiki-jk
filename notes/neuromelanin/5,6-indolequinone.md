# 5,6-indolequinone

[[5,6-indolequinone]] is a late-stage intermediate in the oxidative polymerization of dopamine-derived quinones into the dark, insoluble biopolymer [[Neuromelanin]] that accumulates in catecholaminergic neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]].

## Overview

The biosynthesis of neuromelanin proceeds through the spontaneous autoxidation of dopamine. Dopamine is first oxidized to dopamine o-quinone (also called aminochrome in its cyclized form), which undergoes further cyclization and rearrangement to form 5,6-dihydroxyindole and its semiquinone species. Subsequent oxidation yields [[5,6-indolequinone]], a reactive indolic quinone that serves as the principal monomeric building block for the melanic polymer backbone. Unlike the eumelanin pathway of skin melanocytes, the neuromelanin pathway is largely non-enzymatic, depending instead on the redox chemistry of catecholamine metabolites and the micro-environmental conditions (pH, metal ions, oxidants) within the neuromelanin granule.

## Structure & Chemical Behavior

5,6-indolequinone is a bicyclic indole bearing a para-quinone group at the 5 and 6 positions. It is highly electrophilic and participates in nucleophilic addition reactions with sulfur-containing species (e.g., cysteine residues and glutathione) and with amino groups, forming covalent cross-links that contribute to the heterogeneous, protein-bound nature of neuromelanin. It can also polymerize via oxidative coupling at the indolic 2-, 4-, and 7-positions, generating the branched polyindole network characteristic of the pigment.

## Physiological Function

By sequestering labile redox-active intermediates such as [[Aminochrome]] and 5,6-indolequinone into an inert polymer, neuromelanin effectively reduces the intracellular burden of reactive quinones and redox-cycling species. Neuromelanin granules also bind and detoxify metal ions (iron, copper) and a range of xenobiotics, functioning as a protective "sink" within the neuron. The polymer's semiquinone content, however, confers modest redox-buffering capacity that can both donate and accept electrons.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

The same electrophilicity that enables polymerization can be deleterious: 5,6-indolequinone and related quinones form adducts with proteins and lipids, and residual unpolymerized quinones contribute to [[Oxidative Stress]]. Aberrant accumulation of indolic quinone intermediates has been proposed to promote [[Alpha-synuclein Aggregation]] and to compromise the protective role of neuromelanin in aging and [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Determining the balance between benign sequestration and toxic adduct formation remains an active area of research into why melanized neurons are selectively vulnerable.
