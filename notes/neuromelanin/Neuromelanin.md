# Neuromelanin

[[Neuromelanin]] (NM) is a dark brown to black intracellular pigment found primarily in the human brain, specifically within catecholaminergic neurons. It is most abundant in the [[Substantia Nigra]] (SN) and the [[Locus Coeruleus]].

## Biological Functions
Under normal physiological conditions, neuromelanin is thought to serve several neuroprotective roles:
- **Metal Chelation:** NM has a high affinity for transition metals, particularly [[Iron]]. By sequestering free iron, it prevents the formation of reactive oxygen species via the Fenton reaction.
- **Detoxification:** It is formed from the oxidation of cytosolic [[Dopamine]] and other catechols, effectively removing potentially toxic quinones from the cytoplasm.
- **Antioxidant Activity:** NM can act as a scavenger for free radicals, protecting neurons from [[neuromelanin/Oxidative Stress]].

## Role in Parkinson's Disease
In [[Parkinson's Disease]], the relationship between neuromelanin and neuronal health becomes pathological:
- **Selective Vulnerability:** Neurons containing high levels of NM, such as those in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]], are the ones that preferentially degenerate in PD.
- **Pathogenic Threshold:** Progressive accumulation of NM with age may eventually reach a [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]], where it interferes with cellular proteostasis and lysosomal function.
- **Neuroinflammation:** The release of NM from dying neurons into the extracellular space triggers [[Microgliosis]] and a chronic inflammatory response, which contributes to further neurodegeneration.
