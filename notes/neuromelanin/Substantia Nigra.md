# Substantia Nigra

The [[Substantia Nigra]] (SN) is a critical midbrain structure that forms part of the basal ganglia. Its name, Latin for "black substance," is derived from the dark color provided by high concentrations of [[Neuromelanin]] in its neurons.

## Anatomical Divisions
The substantia nigra is divided into two primary regions:
1.  **Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta (SNpc):** Contains a high density of dopaminergic neurons that project to the [[Striatum]] via the [[Nigrostriatal System]].
2.  **Substantia Nigra Pars Reticulata (SNpr):** Consists mainly of GABAergic neurons and serves as one of the primary output nuclei of the basal ganglia.

## Function
The SNpc is the brain's primary site for [[Dopamine]] production. It plays a vital role in:
- **Motor Control:** Modulating the direct and indirect pathways of the basal ganglia to facilitate smooth, coordinated movement.
- **Reward and Motivation:** Participating in the brain's reward circuitry.
- **Learning:** Facilitating the reinforcement of motor habits and executive functions.

## Clinical Significance
The progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] is the hallmark of [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Motor symptoms such as tremors and [[Bradykinesia]] typically emerge when approximately 60-80% of these neurons have degenerated.
