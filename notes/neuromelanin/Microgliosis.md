# Microgliosis

[[Microgliosis]] refers to the activation, proliferation, and morphological transformation of microglia—the resident macrophages and principal innate immune cells of the central nervous system. In the healthy brain, microglia surveyed the parenchyma in a ramified, quiescent state; upon insult they assume an amoeboid, phagocytic phenotype. In [[Parkinson's Disease]], microgliosis is a cardinal element of [[Neuroinflammation]] within the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] and is tightly coupled to [[Neuromelanin]] release and [[Neuronophagia]].

## Triggers in Parkinson's Disease

Multiple cues activate nigral microglia:
- **Extracellular Neuromelanin:** Pigment released from dying dopaminergic neurons is a potent microglial activator via scavenger and Toll-like receptors.
- **Alpha-synuclein:** Aggregated, misfolded [[Alpha-synuclein]] (including in [[Lewy Bodies]]) is sensed as a danger signal (PRR engagement).
- **Mitochondrial and lysosomal stress:** [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]] release DAMPs that sustain activation.

## Consequences: Protective vs. Detrimental

Initially, microgliosis is protective—clearing debris, phagocytizing extracellular [[Neuromelanin]], and secreting trophic factors. Chronically, however, activated microglia release [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], nitric oxide, and pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), establishing a self-perpetuating inflammatory loop that damages neighboring neurons and accelerates progression. This explains why [[Neuroinflammation]] is regionally restricted to pigment-rich nuclei in PD.

## Neuronophagia and Pigment Clearance

Activated microglia are frequently observed encircling and engulfing extracellular neuromelanin granules—[[Neuronophagia]]—a histological hallmark of ongoing neurodegeneration. Impaired clearance perpetuates inflammation, linking [[Macromolecular Crowding]] of pigment-laden lysosomes to glial activation.

## Therapeutic Implications

Modulating microgliosis (e.g., inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome, boosting anti-inflammatory phenotypes) is a major [[Neuroprotection]] strategy. Biomarkers of microglial activation (TSPO-PET) are under study to stratify patients.
