# Glial cells

[[Glial cells]] are the non-neuronal supporting cells of the central nervous system, comprising astrocytes, microglia, oligodendrocytes, and NG2 glia. In the context of [[Neuromelanin]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]], glia are critical both as mediators of [[Neuroinflammation]] and as metabolic converters that activate exogenous neurotoxins.

## Types and Roles
- **Astrocytes** maintain homeostasis, regulate extracellular glutamate and potassium, support the blood-brain barrier, and store glycogen. They also express monoamine oxidase B ([[Monoamine oxidase B]]), the enzyme that converts the protoxin [[MPTP]] into its active toxic metabolite [[MPP+]].
- **Microglia** are the resident immune cells of the brain; they surveil for damage and, when activated, release cytokines, reactive oxygen and nitrogen species that can both clear debris and exacerbate neuronal injury.
- **Oligodendrocytes** produce myelin and, increasingly appreciated, also participate in metabolic support of axons.

## Mechanism: Glial Conversion of MPTP
MPTP itself is not directly toxic; it crosses the blood-brain barrier and is taken up by astrocytes and other cells, where [[Monoamine oxidase B]] oxidizes it to MPP+ (1-methyl-4-phenylpyridinium). MPP+ is then released and selectively accumulated by dopaminergic terminals via the dopamine transporter, where it inhibits [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial complex I]], depleting ATP and precipitating oxidative stress. This glial "activation" step explains why MPTP so selectively destroys the [[Substantia Nigra]] and why MAO-B inhibitors are partially protective.

## Physiological Function
Under basal conditions glia nurture neuromelanin-containing neurons through metabolic coupling, antioxidant supply (e.g., glutathione transfer), and clearance of extracellular catecholamines and quinones. Astrocytic networks buffer the redox environment around pigmented neurons, potentially mitigating damage from [[Dopamine o-quinone]] and [[Iron]]-mediated reactions.

## Pathology and Clinical Relevance
In PD, chronic microglial activation and astrogliosis are observed in the substantia nigra, often colocalized with [[Neuromelanin]]-laden neurons and [[Lewy Bodies]]. Activated glia release pro-inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-1β, ROS) that may drive the selective vulnerability of pigmented neurons, and impaired glial clearance of alpha-synuclein may perpetuate pathology. Glia are thus both a therapeutic target (anti-inflammatory strategies, MAO-B inhibitors) and a potential delivery vehicle for neuroprotective agents. The interplay between glial metabolism of toxins and neuromelanin neuron loss remains a central theme in experimental parkinsonism models.
