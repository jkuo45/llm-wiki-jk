# Neuroprotection

[[Neuroprotection]] encompasses the endogenous mechanisms and therapeutic strategies that preserve the structure and function of the central nervous system against injury, aging, and neurodegeneration. In [[Parkinson's Disease]], neuroprotective (disease-modifying) approaches aim to slow or halt the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]], contrasting with symptomatic treatments such as [[L-DOPA]] that merely replace [[Dopamine]]. Because [[Neuromelanin]]-containing neurons are selectively vulnerable, many strategies target the pigment-linked axes of oxidative, mitochondrial, and lysosomal stress.

## Major Neuroprotective Strategies

- **Antioxidant defense:** Boosting glutathione, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial]] antioxidants, or scavenging [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] to counter the oxidative burden amplified by neuromelanin-bound metals.
- **Mitochondrial support:** Enhancing [[Complex I]] function and bioenergetics (e.g., [[Coenzyme Q10]], [[Creatine]]) to resist toxin injury such as that from [[MPP+]].
- **Lysosomal/autophagic clearance:** Inducing [[Autophagy]] and [[Lysosomal Exocytosis]] to relieve [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]] and pigment crowding.
- **Anti-inflammatory modulation:** Dampening [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Microgliosis]] (NLRP3 inhibitors, MC1R agonists) to break the inflammatory cycle.
- **Trophic and regenerative support:** [[Neurotrophic Factors]] (GDNF, BDNF) and α-synuclein-lowering approaches to preserve neuronal integrity.

## Neuromelanin as a Therapeutic Node

The dual nature of [[Neuromelanin]]—redox buffer vs. pro-oxidant sink—makes it a strategic target. Approaches include limiting catecholamine auto-oxidation (MAO-B inhibitors like [[Selegiline]]), sequestering bound iron, or facilitating pigment export via lysosomal exocytosis. Determining whether to enhance or reduce pigment accumulation is an open question in the field.

## Challenges and Biomarkers

True neuroprotection is hard to prove clinically; trials require sensitive progression biomarkers such as neuromelanin-sensitive [[Magnetic Resonance Imaging]], DAT imaging, and α-synuclein assays. Most candidates (coenzyme Q10, creatine, rasagiline) have shown mixed results, underscoring the need for combination and earlier intervention.

## Research Outlook

Repurposing toxin models ([[MPTP]], [[6-hydroxydopamine]], [[Paraquat]]) and patient-derived neurons continues to identify targets. The convergence of pigment biology, immunity ([[Major histocompatibility complex class I]]), and bioenergetics frames a multipronged neuroprotective paradigm.
