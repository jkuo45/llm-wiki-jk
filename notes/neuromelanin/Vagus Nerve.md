# Vagus Nerve

The [[Vagus Nerve]] (cranial nerve X) is the longest cranial nerve, arising from the [[Medulla Oblongata]] and projecting widely to provide parasympathetic innervation to the heart, lungs, and most of the gastrointestinal tract. It is a mixed nerve carrying both afferent (∼80%, visceral sensory) and efferent (motor/parasympathetic) fibers. Its dorsal motor nucleus (DMV) is notable in [[Neuromelanin]] research because it contains neuromelanin-positive neurons that degenerate early in [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD), plausibly explaining the prominent premotor gastrointestinal manifestations of the disorder.

## Anatomy and Nuclei

The vagus originates from the dorsal motor nucleus and the nucleus ambiguus in the medulla, exits the skull via the jugular foramen, and descends through the neck and thorax to the abdomen. Key medullary components:

- **Dorsal Motor Nucleus of the Vagus (DMV):** parasympathetic preganglionic motor neurons controlling GI motility and secretion; contains catecholamine-handling, neuromelanin-pigmented cells.
- **Nucleus Ambiguus:** branchial motor neurons to pharyngeal/laryngeal muscles.
- **Nodose Ganglion:** visceral afferent cell bodies relaying gut and cardiorespiratory signals.

## Neuromelanin and Early PD Pathology

The DMV is among the earliest sites of α-synuclein pathology in PD, part of the "premotor" brainstem stage described by Braak. Its neuromelanin-laden neurons, though fewer and less pigmented than those of the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] or [[Locus Coeruleus]], share the catecholaminergic, oxidatively stressed phenotype that makes such cells vulnerable. Their degeneration—together with loss of enteric and other brainstem neuromelanin neurons—precedes classic motor signs by years.

## Clinical Relevance: GI Dysfunction

Damage to vagal motor output disrupts gastric emptying and intestinal transit, producing constipation, early satiety, and dysphagia—among the most common and earliest non-motor symptoms of PD. The "gut-first" hypothesis posits that α-synuclein pathology may ascend the vagus from the enteric nervous system to the DMV and upward, making the vagus a conduit for prodromal spread. This has motivated trials of vagotomy and vagal afferent modulation as potential disease-modifying or symptomatic strategies.

## Broader Neuromelanin Context

The vagus exemplifies that neuromelanin-associated vulnerability is not confined to the nigra but spans dispersed catecholaminergic nuclei. As pigment accrues with [[Aging]] toward the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]], these neurons become increasingly fragile, linking peripheral autonomic failure to the same pigment-driven biology that drives nigral loss.
