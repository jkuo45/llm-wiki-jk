# Synaptic Vesicles

[[Synaptic Vesicles]] are small (∼40 nm), electron-lucent, membrane-bound organelles within the presynaptic terminal that store [[Neurotransmitters]]—including [[Dopamine]], [[Norepinephrine]], and glutamate—and release them by calcium-triggered exocytosis at the [[Synapse]]. In catecholaminergic neurons, their contents and cycle are intimately tied to [[Neuromelanin]] biology and to the pathogenesis of [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD).

## Structure and Cycle

Vesicles are coated with a defined protein set: the lumenal proton gradient is established by the vacuolar H⁺-ATPase, and monoamines are concentrated by specific transporters—[[Vesicular Monoamine Transporter 2]] (VMAT2) for catecholamines. After fusion with the presynaptic membrane (mediated by SNARE proteins), vesicles are retrieved by clathrin-mediated endocytosis and refilled, forming a recycling pool. [[Alpha-synuclein]] binds vesicle membranes and is thought to facilitate the clustering of the readily releasable pool and the SNARE assembly needed for efficient cycling.

## Dopamine Storage and Neuromelanin Link

In nigral dopaminergic terminals, synaptic vesicles are the primary safe reservoir for dopamine. VMAT2 packing keeps cytosolic dopamine low, preventing its auto-oxidation to [[Quinones]] and limiting [[Neuromelanin]] precursor formation. When VMAT2 function is reduced—genetically or by toxins such as [[MPTP]]/rotenone—cytosolic dopamine rises, increasing quinone stress, [[Oxidative Stress]], and pigment accrual toward the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]]. Thus vesicle integrity upstream protects both the synapse and the parent neuron's pigment balance.

## Role in Neurodegeneration

α-Synuclein aggregation in PD directly impairs vesicle trafficking and recycling, reducing [[Neurotransmission]] and promoting presynaptic dysfunction that precedes cell death. Misfolded α-synuclein may also propagate between neurons via exocytic vesicle pathways, spreading [[Lewy Bodies]] pathology. Synaptic vesicle protein loss (e.g., decreased VMAT2, synaptophysin) is an early biomarker detectable by [[Positron Emission Tomography]], and correlates with the [[Nigrostriatal System]] degeneration underlying [[Bradykinesia]] and [[Rigidity]].

## Research Relevance

Vesicle proteins are therapeutic and diagnostic targets: VMAT2 enhancers may reduce cytosolic dopamine toxicity, while synaptic vesicle imaging probes track early PD. Understanding vesicle–α-synuclein interactions is key to interrupting pathological spreading.
