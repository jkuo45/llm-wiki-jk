# Proteostasis

**Proteostasis** (protein homeostasis) is the integrated network of cellular pathways that controls the synthesis, folding, trafficking, and degradation of proteins to maintain a functional proteome. It is essential for neuronal survival, and its decline is a conserved hallmark of [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|Neurodegenerative Disease]]. In catecholaminergic neurons, proteostasis is intimately linked to [[Neuromelanin]], because pigment accumulation, metal load, and catechol [[Quinones]] together challenge the cell's protein-quality-control capacity.

## The Proteostasis Network

The network comprises chaperones, degradation systems, and signaling hubs:

- **Molecular chaperones:** HSP70/HSP90 and co-chaperones assist nascent-chain folding and refold stressed proteins; loss permits aggregation.
- **[[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]] (UPS):** tags short-lived and misfolded proteins with [[Ubiquitin]] for 26S proteasome degradation—the chief routt for most soluble proteins.
- **[[Autophagy]]–lysosome pathway:** macroautophagy, chaperone-mediated autophagy, and mitophagy clear protein aggregates, [[Synaptic Vesicles]], and damaged organelles, including neuromelanin-laden vacuoles.
- **Endoplasmic Reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD):** resolves misfolded secretory proteins and relieves [[Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress|Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress]].

## Proteostasis Failure in Neurodegeneration

When capacity is exceeded, misfolded proteins accumulate and form toxic species—a state of "proteotoxicity." In [[Parkinson's Disease]], impaired UPS and autophagic flux permit [[Alpha-synuclein]] to oligomerize and deposit into [[Lewy Bodies]] and [[Pale Bodies]]. Genetic PD risk factors (e.g., *PRKN*, *PINK1*, *LRRK2*, *GBA*) converge on mitophagy and lysosomal degradation, underscoring proteostasis as a core axis of vulnerability.

## Interaction with Neuromelanin

In the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]], neuromelanin is a double-edged participant in proteostasis. Early in life it may sequester toxic catechols and metals, protecting protein folding. But as pigment accrues with age and approaches the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]], it burdens lysosomes and autophagosomes, impedes degradation, and—through metal-catalyzed [[Oxidative Stress]]—damages chaperones and proteases themselves. The resulting proteostatic collapse selectively afflicts the most pigmented neurons, explaining their disproportionate loss. Chronic [[Neuroinflammation]], driven by activated microglia, further suppresses proteostatic gene expression, creating a feed-forward loop.

## Therapeutic Avenues

Strategies to restore proteostasis include pharmacological chaperones, UPS/autophagy enhancers (e.g., TFEB activation), and anti-aggregation agents against α-synuclein. Lowering cytosolic dopamine auto-oxidation via [[Vesicular Monoamine Transporter 2]] support may reduce the pigment-associated proteostatic load.

## Connections

- [[Autophagy]]: Lysosomal arm clearing aggregates and pigment-laden organelles.
- [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]]: Degrades the bulk of misfolded proteins.
- [[Ubiquitin]]: Tag marking substrates for proteasomal destruction.
- [[Neuromelanin]]: Pigment whose load challenges proteostatic capacity.
- [[Alpha-synuclein]]: Client protein that aggregates when proteostasis fails.
- [[Lewy Bodies]]: Inclusions arising from proteostatic collapse.
- [[Oxidative Stress]]: Damages proteostasis machinery and is amplified by pigment.
- [[Aging]]: Process that erodes proteostatic reserve.
