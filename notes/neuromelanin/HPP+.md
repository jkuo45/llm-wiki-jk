# HPP+

[[HPP+]] (4-(4-chlorophenyl)-1-[4-(4-fluorophenyl)-4-oxobutyl]pyridinium) is a toxic pyridinium metabolite of the antipsychotic [[Haloperidol]]. It is structurally and mechanistically analogous to [[MPP+]], the active neurotoxin derived from [[MPTP]], and is implicated in haloperidol-associated dopaminergic injury and drug-induced parkinsonism.

## Chemical Properties
HPP+ is a permanently charged pyridinium cation, which renders it membrane-impermeant and therefore dependent on transporter-mediated uptake to enter neurons. Its aromatic chlorophenyl and fluorobenzoyl substituents resemble the lipophilic "tails" of MPP+, allowing recognition by the dopamine transporter (DAT) on dopaminergic terminals. Once inside, HPP+ is trapped in the cytosol and mitochondria because it cannot diffuse back across membranes.

## Mechanism of Neurotoxicity
- **Uptake**: HPP+ is selectively accumulated by dopaminergic neurons via DAT, mirroring MPP+ and explaining the selectivity for the [[Substantia Nigra]] [[Neuromelanin]] neurons.
- **Mitochondrial inhibition**: Like MPP+, HPP+ inhibits complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. This blocks oxidative phosphorylation, depletes ATP, increases [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], and promotes [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].
- **Oxidative stress**: The combination of ATP loss and ROS production overwhelms antioxidant defenses (e.g., glutathione), leading to [[Oxidative Stress]], lipid peroxidation, and ultimately apoptotic/necrotic demise of pigmented neurons.

## Physiological and Clinical Relevance
Although HPP+ is a minor metabolite, its potent mechanism explains why chronic [[Haloperidol]] exposure can produce parkinsonian features. The HPP+ hypothesis provides a toxicological bridge between antipsychotic therapy and the same nigral vulnerability seen in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. It also highlights the role of CYP2D6/CYP3A4 metabolism in determining individual HPP+ formation and thus inter-patient differences in neurotoxic risk.

## Pathology and Research Implications
HPP+ is a valuable experimental surrogate for MPP+ in studies of selective dopaminergic neurotoxicity, allowing dissection of transporter dependence and mitochondrial mechanisms without the blood-brain-barrier complexities of MPTP. Its existence cautions that drugs targeting the dopaminergic system may, via bioactivation, secondarily damage the very neurons they do not intend to. Research continues into whether HPP+ contributes to long-term motor complications (e.g., tardive dyskinesia) and whether DAT blockers or mitochondrial protectants can mitigate haloperidol toxicity. The HPP+/MPP+ analogy reinforces the centrality of complex I inhibition and [[Neuromelanin]] neuron selectivity in parkinsonian neurodegeneration.
