# Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase

[[Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase]] (AADC), also known as DOPA decarboxylase (DDC), is a pyridoxal phosphate (vitamin B6)-dependent enzyme that catalyzes the decarboxylation of [[L-DOPA]] to [[Dopamine]] and of 5-hydroxytryptophan to serotonin. It is the penultimate step in catecholamine biosynthesis.

## Structure & Cofactor

AADC is a homodimeric cytosolic enzyme (~50 kDa subunits) requiring pyridoxal 5′-phosphate as a cofactor. The active site positions the substrate's α-carboxyl for decarboxylation while the aromatic side chain is accommodated in a hydrophobic pocket, explaining its broad specificity for aromatic amino acids. Expression is widespread—highest in kidney, liver, and catecholaminergic/serotonergic neurons.

## Role in Dopamine Biosynthesis

Within the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]], AADC converts L-DOPA (produced by tyrosine hydroxylase) into dopamine. Because AADC is also present in peripheral tissues, orally administered L-DOPA is substantially decarboxylated outside the brain; this is countered clinically by co-administering peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitors (carbidopa, benserazide) that do not cross the blood–brain barrier, sparing central conversion.

## Relation to Neuromelanin and Parkinson's Disease

Dopamine produced by AADC is the precursor pool for both neurotransmission and [[Neuromelanin]] pigmentation, and its oxidative metabolites feed the [[Aminochrome]] and [[5,6-indolequinone]] pathway. In [[Parkinson's Disease]], loss of nigral neurons reduces AADC activity, limiting the capacity to convert exogenous L-DOPA—hence the need for adjunct therapies. In AADC deficiency (a rare pediatric disorder), severely reduced enzyme causes movement disorders and autonomic dysfunction.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

AADC is a therapeutic target and biomarker: gene therapy delivering the DDC gene to the striatum has been explored to enhance intra-brain dopamine synthesis. Enzyme activity assays and imaging probes (e.g., 18F-fluorodopa PET) exploit AADC to visualize presynaptic dopaminergic integrity, aiding PD diagnosis and monitoring. Its serine/threonine phosphorylation and interaction with other biosynthetic enzymes fine-tune catecholamine tone.
