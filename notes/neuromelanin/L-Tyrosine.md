# L-Tyrosine

[[L-Tyrosine]] is a non-essential aromatic amino acid synthesized endogenously from phenylalanine by phenylalanine hydroxylase, and obtained from dietary protein. It is the foundational precursor for the catecholamine neurotransmitters—[[Dopamine]], norepinephrine, and epinephrine—and for the melanin pigments, placing it at the origin of the biochemical pathways that generate [[Neuromelanin]] in the brain.

## Biosynthesis of Catecholamines
The catecholamine pathway begins with hydroxylation of L-tyrosine to [[L-DOPA]] by [[Tyrosine Hydroxylase]] (the rate-limiting step, requiring tetrahydrobiopterin and O2). L-DOPA is then decarboxylated to dopamine by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase; dopamine is further hydroxylated to norepinephrine (by dopamine β-hydroxylase) and methylated to epinephrine. In neuromelanin-containing neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]], tyrosine flux through this pathway supplies the dopamine/norepinephrine that is later oxidized into pigment.

## Link to Melanin and Neuromelanin
In melanogenesis, L-tyrosine is hydroxylated by [[Tyrosinase]] to L-DOPA, then oxidized to dopaquinone and polymerized into [[Eumelanin]] and pheomelanin. The brain's neuromelanin uses the same tyrosine→L-DOPA→quinone chemistry, except the upstream catecholamine is dopamine/norepinephrine rather than externally supplied L-DOPA. Thus L-tyrosine availability indirectly governs both neurotransmitter tone and pigment accumulation over a lifetime.

## Physiological Function
As a precursor, L-tyrosine supports dopaminergic and noradrenergic signaling that regulates movement, reward, stress response, and autonomic tone. It is also a precursor to thyroid hormones and, via melanogenesis, to photoprotective pigment. Supplementation is sometimes studied for cognitive resilience under stress, though evidence is mixed.

## Pathology and Clinical Relevance
Disrupted tyrosine metabolism is implicated across neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disease. Reduced tyrosine hydroxylation lowers dopamine, featuring in PD pathogenesis where [[L-DOPA]] replacement compensates for lost nigral output. Tyrosine availability can modulate neuromelanin formation, and the oxidative branches of its metabolism generate [[Dopamine o-quinone]] and [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], linking precursor supply to the oxidative milieu that stresses pigmented neurons. Genetic defects in tyrosine metabolism (e.g., tyrosinemia, albinism-related tyrosinase deficiency) further illustrate its centrality. In PD research, tyrosine-based imaging (e.g., [[Flortaucipir]] off-target and [18F]-FDOPA uptake) reflects the health of tyrosine-hydroxylating neurons, making L-tyrosine metabolism a biomarker-relevant axis.
