# Lewy Bodies

[[Lewy Bodies]] (LB) are intracytoplasmic proteinaceous inclusions that define the synucleinopathy spectrum, including [[Parkinson's Disease]], dementia with Lewy bodies, and Parkinson's disease dementia. They are the neuropathological hallmark used to confirm clinical PD at autopsy.

## Composition
The principal component is misfolded, phosphorylated, fibrillar [[Alpha-synuclein]]—a small presynaptic protein normally involved in vesicle trafficking. Lewy bodies also recruit [[Ubiquitin]], p62/SQSTM1, neurofilament proteins, and chaperones, reflecting the cell's failed attempt to degrade aggregated protein via the [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]] and autophagy-lysosomal pathways. Lipid and [[Iron]] may also be incorporated, and [[Neuromelanin]] often appears in intimate association with LB in pigmented neurons.

## Formation and Spread
Alpha-synuclein monomers misfold and self-assemble into oligomers, protofibrils, and fibrils. Soluble oligomers are considered the most toxic species, disrupting membranes and mitochondria, while insoluble fibrils pack into the characteristic LB core. According to Braak staging, pathology spreads in a stereotyped ascending pattern—from the olfactory bulb and [[Dorsal Motor Nucleus of the Vagus]] upward through the [[Locus Coeruleus]] and [[Substantia Nigra]] to limbic and neocortical regions—possibly via templated propagation of misfolded protein between connected neurons.

## Cellular Location and Relationship to Neuromelanin
In dopaminergic neurons, Lewy bodies frequently form adjacent to or enveloping [[Neuromelanin]] granules. Neuromelanin's quinone chemistry and iron load may catalyze alpha-synuclein aggregation, and pigment may sequester oligomers, creating a physical nexus where proteinopathy and pigment biochemistry intersect. "Pale bodies" are regarded as precursor lesions to mature Lewy bodies.

## Diagnostic and Clinical Relevance
The presence of Lewy bodies in the substantia nigra is a cardinal diagnostic criterion for PD; their cortical distribution distinguishes dementia with Lewy bodies. LB pathology correlates with motor severity, cognitive decline, and non-motor features. Biomarkers under study include alpha-synuclein seed amplification assays in cerebrospinal fluid and skin, and imaging of nigral [[Neuromelanin]] loss. Therapeutic strategies target alpha-synuclein (immunotherapies, antisense oligonucleotides, aggregation inhibitors) and upstream modifiers such as [[Glucocerebrosidase]] activity and [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]]. Lewy bodies thus sit at the center of PD pathophysiology, linking protein misfolding, pigment biology, and selective neuronal vulnerability.
