# Glucocerebrosidase

[[Glucocerebrosidase]] (GCase, also known as acid β-glucosylceramidase, EC 3.2.1.45) is a lysosomal hydrolase encoded by the GBA1 gene. It catalyzes the cleavage of glucosylceramide (GlcCer) into glucose and ceramide, a key step in the degradation of glycosphingolipids derived from membrane turnover. GCase is a soluble luminal lysosomal enzyme that requires the sphingolipid activator protein saposin C and an acidic pH for optimal activity.

## Structure and Mechanism
The mature enzyme is a ~55 kDa monomeric glycoprotein. Loss-of-function mutations in GBA1 (over 300 variants described, including N370S, L444P, and Gaucher disease-causing alleles) impair folding, trafficking, or catalytic activity of GCase. Reduced enzymatic activity causes accumulation of glucosylceramide and downstream glycosphingolipids within lysosomes.

## Physiological Function
By recycling glycosphingolipids, GCase maintains lysosomal and membrane homeostasis across tissues, including neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]]. Proper GCase activity supports [[Lysosomal Dysfunction|lysosomal]] health, autophagic flux, and lipid-dependent signaling.

## Pathology and Clinical Relevance
Mutations in GBA1 are the single most common genetic risk factor for [[Parkinson's Disease]], with heterozygous carriers showing a 2–10-fold elevated PD risk depending on the cohort. The relationship is bidirectional:
- **GCase deficiency → α-synuclein aggregation**: Impaired GCase perturbs lysosomal function, slowing the degradation of [[Alpha-synuclein]] and promoting its accumulation into [[Lewy Bodies]]. Aggregated α-synuclein itself further inhibits residual GCase, creating a feed-forward loop.
- **α-synuclein → GCase inhibition**: Soluble and fibrillar α-synuclein can bind and suppress GCase activity, linking proteinopathy to lipid-handling failure.

This GBA1–α-synuclein axis connects [[Neuromelanin]] neuron vulnerability to [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]] and [[Proteostasis]] failure. Clinically, GBA1 status influences PD age of onset, cognitive decline, and likely response to therapies. Enzyme replacement (for Gaucher disease) does not cross the blood-brain barrier, so CNS-directed strategies are under investigation: pharmacological chaperones (e.g., ambroxol) to stabilize mutant GCase, substrate reduction therapy, and gene therapies to restore GBA1 expression. GCase activity in cerebrospinal fluid and peripheral cells is also explored as a PD biomarker.
