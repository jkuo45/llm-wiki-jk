# Flortaucipir

Flortaucipir (also known as [18F]-AV-1451 or [18F]-T807) is a fluorinated positron emission tomography (PET) radioligand originally designed to bind aggregated tau protein in Alzheimer's disease. Surprisingly, in vitro and ex vivo studies demonstrated that flortaucipir also binds with high affinity to [[Neuromelanin]], making it a useful—though off-target—probe for visualizing pigmented brainstem nuclei.

## Chemical Properties and Pharmacological Action
Flortaucipir is an arylquinoline-derivative radiotracer labeled with fluorine-18, a positron emitter with a ~110-minute half-life suitable for PET imaging. Its tau-binding design targets beta-sheet-rich protein aggregates, but its planar, cationic aromatic structure also shows strong affinity for the indolic, metal-rich matrix of neuromelanin. This cross-reactivity is believed to reflect shared physicochemical features—hydrophobic planar surfaces and metal/quinone interactions—rather than specific receptor binding.

## Imaging Technique and Applications
As a PET tracer, flortaucipir is administered intravenously and its regional brain uptake is quantified by dynamic PET with arterial input or reference tissue modeling. In the living human brain, flortaucipir readily accumulates in the [[Substantia Nigra]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]]—the two major neuromelanin-rich nuclei—producing high signal that reflects pigment burden more than tau pathology in those regions. This property has been exploited to:
- Map the integrity and loss of neuromelanin-containing neurons non-invasively in [[Parkinson's Disease]].
- Quantify age-related decline in pigment load in the locus coeruleus as a marker of noradrenergic degeneration.
- Distinguish Parkinsonian syndromes by assessing the pattern of neuromelanin-bearing nucleus involvement.

## Clinical and Research Relevance
The tracer's neuromelanin binding is a double-edged finding. On one hand it provides a unique in vivo window into the very neurons that degenerate in PD, enabling longitudinal tracking of [[Lewy Bodies]]-related neurodegeneration before overt motor signs. On the other hand, it complicates interpretation in patients with comorbid tauopathy, since signal in the substantia nigra cannot be assumed to represent tau. Researchers therefore correct for neuromelanin binding when using flortaucipir to assess tau in movement disorders. The discovery also spurred interest in developing more specific melanin- or catecholamine-targeted radiotracers (e.g., [18F]-FDOPA and VMAT2 ligands) to complement flortaucipir's incidental neuromelanin contrast.
