# Tyrosinase-related protein-2

[[Tyrosinase-related protein-2]] (TRP-2), also called [[Dopachrome tautomerase]] (DCT), is a copper-dependent enzyme of the tyrosinase superfamily that catalyzes the isomerization of dopachrome to 5,6-dihydroxyindole-2-carboxylic acid (DHICA)—a key branching step in [[Eumelanin]] biosynthesis. Like its paralogs [[Tyrosinase]] and [[Tyrosinase-related protein-1]], TRP-2 is a melanosomal membrane protein best known in skin pigmentation, but it has drawn neurological interest because it is reportedly upregulated in dopaminergic neurons derived from [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD) patients, implicating altered pigment metabolism in [[Neuromelanin]] pathology.

## Catalytic Role in Melanogenesis

After tyrosinase oxidizes L-dopa to dopaquinone, the molecule cyclizes non-enzymatically to dopachrome (a red intermediate). TRP-2 accelerates dopachrome's tautomerization to DHICA, steering the pathway toward eumelanic polymers and away from pheomelanic side reactions. The ratio of TRP-1/TRP-2 activities thus shapes the final eumelanin-to-[[Pheomelanin]] balance and the antioxidant/redox profile of the pigment. TRP-2 is the most conserved member of the family and a common antigen in melanoma immunotherapy.

## Expression in the Brain and Neuromelanin Link

Neuromelanin is a catechol-derived pigment of human SNpc and [[Locus Coeruleus]] neurons whose formation likely involves both spontaneous dopamine oxidation and enzymatic melanogenesis. Detection of TRP-2 (and related family transcripts) in human brain raises the possibility that the same enzymatic toolkit used in peripheral melanocytes templates neuromelanin's eumelanic outer shell. If TRP-2 activity is heightened in PD, as patient-derived neuron studies suggest, the resulting shift in pigment composition could alter the pigment's metal-binding and radical-scavenging behavior, influencing the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]].

## Relevance to Parkinson's Disease

Upregulated TRP-2 in PD patient dopaminergic neurons implies a reprogramming of catechol handling toward melanogenesis, potentially as a stress response to [[Oxidative Stress]] or as a maladaptive consequence of elevated cytosolic dopamine and [[Quinones]]. Transgenic models show that forced expression of the upstream enzyme [[Tyrosinase]] drives age-dependent neuromelanin accumulation and eventual nigral degeneration, supporting the idea that the whole enzymatic cascade—including TRP-2—contributes to pigment-linked vulnerability. However, the precise contribution of TRP-2 versus non-enzymatic chemistry remains unresolved.

## Research Relevance

Clarifying TRP-2's CNS role may explain inter-individual differences in neuromelanin content, refine PD biomarkers (e.g., [[Positron Emission Tomography]] and neuromelanin MRI), and suggest targets for modulating pigment biosynthesis to reduce neurotoxicity.
