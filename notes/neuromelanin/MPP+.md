# MPP+

[[MPP+]] (1-methyl-4-phenylpyridinium) is the cationic, active neurotoxic metabolite of the prodrug [[MPTP]]. Generated in astrocytes by [[Monoamine oxidase B]], MPP⁺ selectively destroys dopaminergic neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]], reproducing the core features of [[Parkinson's Disease]] in humans and animals. Its mechanism—mitochondrial [[Complex I]] inhibition—makes it a foundational tool for studying [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and selective neuronal vulnerability, including the role of [[Neuromelanin]].

## Chemistry and Formation

MPTP, a lipophilic piperidine, crosses the blood-brain barrier and is oxidized by MAO-B to MPDP⁺ (1-methyl-4-phenyl-2,3-dihydropyridinium), which then spontaneously oxidizes to the stable pyridinium cation MPP⁺. The positive charge and structural mimicry of [[Dopamine]] allow MPP⁺ to hijack the [[Dopamine Transporter]] (DAT) for neuronal uptake.

## Mechanism of Selective Neurotoxicity

1. **Uptake:** MPP⁺ enters dopaminergic neurons via DAT (and to a lesser extent NET), concentrating the toxin where catecholamine handling is highest.
2. **Mitochondrial accumulation:** It is taken up by mitochondria through the inner-membrane dicarboxylate carrier.
3. **Complex I inhibition:** MPP⁺ binds the hydrophobic subunit of NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase ([[Complex I]]), blocking electron flow, collapsing the proton gradient, and causing [[ATP Depletion]].
4. **Oxidative cascade:** Electron leakage generates [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and [[Oxidative Stress]], damaging lipids, proteins, and DNA, culminating in apoptosis/necrosis.

## Interaction with Neuromelanin

Dopaminergic neurons containing [[Neuromelanin]] are more susceptible to MPP⁺ than non-pigmented neurons, suggesting the pigment either facilitates toxin retention, harbors redox-active metals that amplify oxidative injury, or reflects a metabolic state (high catecholamine turnover) that favors damage. This pigment–toxin synergy is a key clue that neuromelanin content marks vulnerability rather than mere inert accumulation.

## Research and Therapeutic Relevance

MPP⁺ is the gold-standard chemical lesion for PD models, used to screen [[Neuroprotection]] agents and dissect nigral pathology. DAT inhibitors and [[Complex I]] protectants block its action. The MPP⁺/MPTP paradigm established the mitochondrial hypothesis of PD and validated [[Neurotoxin]]-based modeling.
