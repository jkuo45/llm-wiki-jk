# Manganism

[[Manganism]] is a progressive neurological disorder caused by chronic occupational or environmental overexposure to [[Manganese]]. First described in miners ("locura manganica"), it produces a movement disorder superficially resembling [[Parkinson's Disease]] but with distinct neuroanatomical, neurochemical, and pathological signatures. Understanding manganism clarifies how metals and [[Neuromelanin]] interact in selective neurodegeneration.

## Clinical Presentation

Early manganism features neuropsychiatric changes—irritability, apathy, psychosis ("manganese madness")—followed by a mixed motor syndrome. Unlike idiopathic PD, manganism typically shows prominent gait disturbance, postural instability, and dystonia rather than resting tremor, and patients often show poor or absent response to [[L-DOPA]]. The predominant pathology lies in the globus pallidus and striatum rather than the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]].

## Pathophysiology

Manganese accumulates in basal ganglia, where it inhibits mitochondrial [[Complex II]], disrupts neurotransmitter handling, and promotes [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Oxidative Stress]]. The metal perturbs GABAergic and dopaminergic signaling, impairing [[Neurotransmission]] and motor control. In the context of [[Neuromelanin]], manganese's high affinity for the pigment means pigmented neurons can act as a Mn sink, with redox-cycling at the pigment surface generating [[Reactive Oxygen Species]].

## Differentiating from Parkinson's Disease

Crucially, manganism lacks the [[Lewy Bodies]] and alpha-synuclein pathology that define PD, and nigral cell loss is less prominent. This dissociation is informative: it shows that Parkinsonian motor features can arise from toxin-metal injury independent of synuclein aggregation, while still implicating dopaminergic circuitry. Co-exposure to [[MPP+]]-like compounds and Mn may act synergistically, a concern for occupational cohorts.

## Diagnosis and Management

Diagnosis rests on exposure history and characteristic T1-hyperintensity of the globus pallidus on [[Magnetic Resonance Imaging]], supported by blood Mn levels. Management is primarily removal from exposure; chelation is of limited benefit once neuronal injury is established. The condition remains a key human model of metal-induced [[Neurotoxin]] injury.

## Connections

- [[Manganese]] - causal toxicant whose chronic exposure produces the disorder.
- [[Parkinson's Disease]] - clinically overlapping but pathologically distinct neurodegeneration.
- [[Neuromelanin]] - pigment that binds and concentrates Mn in neurons.
- [[Complex II]] - mitochondrial enzyme inhibited by manganese.
- [[Oxidative Stress]] - redox cycling at pigment-bound Mn drives injury.
- [[Lewy Bodies]] - notably absent in manganism, distinguishing it from PD.
- [[Magnetic Resonance Imaging]] - T1 signal used for diagnosis.
- [[Neurotransmission]] - GABA/dopamine imbalance underlies motor signs.
