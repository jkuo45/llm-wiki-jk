# Quinones

[[Quinones]] are a broad class of cyclic diketones derived from aromatic compounds, including the catecholamines [[Dopamine]], norepinephrine, and epinephrine. They are highly electrophilic and redox-active, capable of participating in one-electron reductions to semiquinone radicals and subsequent redox cycling that generates reactive oxygen species. In catecholaminergic neurons, quinones sit at the intersection of [[Oxidative Stress]], [[Alpha-synuclein]] modification, and [[Neuromelanin]] formation.

## Formation from Catecholamines

Catecholamines contain a catechol (1,2-dihydroxybenzene) ring that is readily oxidized—enzymatically by monoamine oxidase/cytochrome P450, or non-enzymatically by metals and auto-oxidation—to the corresponding *o*-quinone (e.g., dopamine *o*-quinone, norepinephrine *o*-quinone). The semiquinone intermediate is a free radical that can reduce molecular oxygen to superoxide, establishing a redox cycle that amplifies ROS. Quinones are also precursors in the spontaneous chemical cascade that polymerizes into neuromelanin: dopaquinone cyclizes and oxidizes through indolequinone and benzothiazine intermediates, ultimately forming the pigment's polymeric backbone.

## Chemical Properties and Reactivity

Quinones are Michael acceptors; their electrophilic β-carbon reacts with nucleophilic groups—thiols of cysteine, amino groups of lysine, and the imidazole of histidine—forming stable protein adducts. This covalent modification can inactivate enzymes, disrupt [[Proteostasis]], and cross-link proteins. Cyclized aminochrome derivatives (e.g., dopaminochrome) are particularly toxic and can promote [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]]. Under reducing conditions, quinones can be detoxified by glutathione conjugation (catalyzed by glutathione S-transferase) or reduced back by NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1).

## Role in Neuromelanin and Neurodegeneration

Quinone flux is the chemical engine of neuromelanin biosynthesis, linking dopamine metabolism to lifelong pigment accrual and, ultimately, the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]]. Simultaneously, quinone-mediated adduction of [[Alpha-synuclein]] and other proteins may seed aggregation and [[Lewy Bodies]]. [[Vesicular Monoamine Transporter 2]] limits cytosolic dopamine (and thus quinone generation), conferring resilience; neurons with low VMAT2 show higher neuromelanin and greater vulnerability in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Toxicant models such as [[MPTP]] (via MPP⁺) and rotenone similarly elevate intracellular quinone/ROS stress.

## Research Relevance

Quinone scavengers, NQO1 inducers, and glutathione-supporting strategies are explored as neuroprotective approaches. Quantifying catechol-quinone adducts serves as a biomarker of oxidative dopamine catabolism in PD research.
