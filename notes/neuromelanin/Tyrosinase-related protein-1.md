# Tyrosinase-related protein-1

[[Tyrosinase-related protein-1]] (TRP-1; also known as gp75 or DCT-related protein) is a type I membrane-bound, copper-containing enzyme of the melanosome superfamily, best characterized in cutaneous melanocytes where it participates in [[Eumelanin]] (black-brown melanin) biosynthesis. As a paralog of [[Tyrosinase]] and [[Tyrosinase-related protein-2]] (TRP-2/dopachrome tautomerase), TRP-1 shares the conserved histidine-rich copper-binding motif required for catalytic activity. Its potential expression and role in the central nervous system—particularly in [[Neuromelanin]] synthesis and [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD)—are active research questions.

## Structure and Catalytic Function

TRP-1 is anchored in the melanosomal membrane with its catalytic domain facing the lumen. In skin, it stabilizes the melanogenic enzyme complex and catalyzes later oxidative steps in eumelanin polymerization (it exhibits weak DHICA oxidase activity, converting 5,6-dihydroxyindole-2-carboxylic acid to its quinone). TRP-1 deficiency shifts pigment toward pheomelanic (yellow-red) tones, underscoring its role in determining eumelanin vs. [[Pheomelanin]] ratio. The enzyme is a major target of autoantibodies in vitiligo, attesting to its immunogenicity in peripheral tissue.

## Relevance to Neuromelanin and the Brain

Neuromelanin in human catecholaminergic neurons is a catechol-derived, metal-binding pigment whose formation may involve both spontaneous dopamine auto-oxidation and enzymatic catalysis. Whether TRP-1 is functionally expressed in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] remains controversial: some studies report TRP-1/tyrosinase-family transcripts in human brain, while others attribute neuromelanin to non-enzymatic chemistry. If present, TRP-1 could influence the eumelanin coating that envelops the proposed pheomelanic core of neuromelanin granules, thereby modulating pigment redox properties and the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]].

## Link to Parkinson's Disease

Alterations in melanogenic enzymes have been observed in PD models. For instance, [[Tyrosinase-related protein-2]] is upregulated in patient-derived dopaminergic neurons, suggesting reprogrammed pigment biosynthesis. A similar shift involving TRP-1 could alter the eumelanin/pheomelanin balance, affecting antioxidant capacity and susceptibility to [[Oxidative Stress]]. Transgenic overexpression of [[Tyrosinase]] in rodents—which normally lack neuromelanin—recapitulates age-dependent pigment accumulation and PD-like pathology, indirectly implicating the entire tyrosinase family, including TRP-1, in pigment-driven vulnerability.

## Research Relevance

Defining TRP-1's CNS presence and function could clarify whether neuromelanin is enzymatically templated, inform [[Positron Emission Tomography]]/MRI biomarker interpretation, and reveal targets for modulating pigment toxicity in PD.
