# DNA

[[DNA]] (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the macromolecule that stores genetic information in nearly all organisms. Its sequence encodes the proteins and regulatory RNAs that govern cellular function, including the enzymes of catecholamine synthesis and the pigmentation pathways that produce [[Neuromelanin]]. While neuromelanin research emphasizes biochemistry, DNA provides the heritable framework that determines pigment-cell fate and PD susceptibility.

## Structure & Function

DNA is a double helix of nucleotide base pairs (A–T, G–C) organized into chromosomes and packaged with histones into chromatin. Genes are transcribed to RNA and translated to protein; regulatory elements control when and where expression occurs. In neurons, DNA must be protected from damage because post-mitotic cells cannot dilute lesions by division.

## Genetic Contributions to Neuromelanin & Parkinson's Disease

Several genes influence whether and how dopaminergic neurons synthesize and handle pigment. Tyrosinase-related enzymes ([[Tyrosinase]], [[Dopachrome tautomerase]]) are encoded in the genome, as are the melanogenesis regulators (MITF, MC1R) linking cutaneous and neural pigment. PD risk is shaped by DNA variants in SNCA (e.g., [[A53T]]), LRRK2, PARKIN, PINK1, and GBA1, the last connecting pigment-cell lysosomal biology to [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]]. Mitochondrial DNA mutations affecting [[Complex I]] further modulate vulnerability.

## DNA Damage and Selective Vulnerability

[[Neuromelanin]] granules bind iron and redox-active quinones ([[Aminochrome]]), and their failure can increase [[Oxidative Stress]] that damages nuclear and mitochondrial DNA in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]]. Accumulated DNA damage, coupled with diminished repair in aging neurons, contributes to the selective death of pigmented neurons. mtDNA deletions in SNpc accumulate with age and correlate with Parkinson's pathology.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Genotyping (SNCA, GBA1, LRRK2, MC1R) refines PD diagnosis, prognosis, and trial stratification, and illuminates the melanoma–PD link (see [[Cutaneous Melanoma]]). Mitochondrial DNA integrity and nuclear repair capacity are emerging therapeutic targets. Thus DNA bridges inherited risk and acquired oxidative damage in pigment-related neurodegeneration.
