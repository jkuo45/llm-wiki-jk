# Aminochrome

[[Aminochrome]] (also known as dopamine o-quinone imine or dopaminechrome) is the cyclized, oxidized form of dopamine produced during catecholamine autoxidation. It is a pivotal branch-point intermediate: it can either polymerize into the protective pigment [[Neuromelanin]] or react aberrantly to form neurotoxic species and protein adducts, including complexes with [[Alpha-synuclein]].

## Overview

When [[Dopamine]] is oxidized, the initial dopamine o-quinone rapidly cyclizes to aminochrome (leukoaminochrome/aminochrome equilibrium). Aminochrome is a highly reactive electrophile and redox-active quinone imine. Its fate depends on the cellular redox environment and the availability of detoxifying enzymes such as DT-diaphorase (NQO1), which two-electron-reduces quinones back to less reactive catechols, and glutathione, which conjugates them.

## Chemical Behavior

Aminochrome can (1) polymerize via oxidative coupling to generate indolic intermediates such as [[5,6-indolequinone]] and ultimately neuromelanin; (2) bind to protein nucleophiles (cysteine, lysine) forming stable adducts that impair protein function; and (3) promote α-synuclein oligomerization, thereby bridging catecholamine redox chemistry to [[Alpha-Synuclein Aggregation]] and [[Lewy Bodies]] formation. It also undergoes redox cycling, generating [[Oxidative Stress]] through superoxide production.

## Physiological vs Pathological Role

In healthy melanized neurons, aminochrome is largely channeled into neuromelanin, contributing to the pigment's metal-chelating and radical-scavenging roles. When detoxifying capacity is overwhelmed—by aging, low NQO1 activity, or excessive dopamine turnover—aminochrome accumulates and becomes toxic. This dual role makes aminochrome a key candidate in explaining why dopaminergic, melanin-rich neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] are selectively vulnerable in [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Elevated aminochrome adducts and associated [[Oxidative Stress]] markers are found in PD substantia nigra. Strategies that bolster two-electron quinone reduction (NQO1 inducers) or scavenge aminochrome are proposed neuroprotective approaches. The aminochrome–α-synuclein cross-talk also implicates catecholamine metabolism directly in proteostasis failure.
