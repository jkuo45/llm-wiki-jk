# Indole

[[Indole]] is a bicyclic aromatic heterocycle consisting of a benzene ring fused to a pyrrole ring, with the formula C8H7N. It is a fundamental scaffold in biological chemistry, present in the amino acid tryptophan, in many neurotransmitters and plant alkaloids, and—critically for this topic—as the core building block of the indolic oligomers that compose [[Neuromelanin]].

## Structure and Chemistry
The indole nucleus is electron-rich and aromatic, enabling electrophilic substitution and oxidation. Its nitrogen lone pair participates in the aromatic sextet, giving indole both basic and π-stacking properties. These features allow indole-derived units to polymerize and to bind metals and cations—properties exploited by neuromelanin for sequestration.

## Role in Neuromelanin Biosynthesis
Neuromelanin is not synthesized by a dedicated enzyme but arises from the spontaneous oxidation of catecholamines. The pathway begins when [[Dopamine]] (or norepinephrine) is oxidized to its o-quinone ([[Dopamine o-quinone]]), which cyclizes to aminochrome ([[5,6-indolequinone]]). This 5,6-indolequinone is an indole derivative and represents the foundational repeat unit of the pigment. Further oxidation, rearrangement, and polymerization of indolequinone and related dihydroxyindole species produce the cross-linked, heterogeneous neuromelanin polymer. Thus, indole chemistry underlies both the dark color and the metal-binding/radical-scavenging capacity of the pigment.

## Comparison with Peripheral Melanins
In cutaneous melanin, the indole scaffold is supplied by L-DOPA via the Raper–Mason pathway, yielding eumelanin (an indole-rich polymer) and pheomelanin (a sulfur-containing variant). In the brain, the indole units of neuromelanin are derived from dopamine and norepinephrine rather than external L-DOPA, but the indolic core chemistry is conserved. The [[Eumelanin]]-like surface of neuromelanin is therefore rich in indole-derived quinone structures.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance
The indole-rich matrix gives neuromelanin its ability to chelate [[Iron]] and other redox metals and to redox-cycle, functioning as both an antioxidant sink and—when overloaded—a pro-oxidant source in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Indole quinone adducts can also modify proteins, potentially cross-linking [[Alpha-synuclein]] and contributing to [[Lewy Bodies]] formation. Chemically, targeting or mimicking indole polymerization is relevant to designing neuromelanin imaging agents (e.g., [[Flortaucipir]]) and to understanding pigment-related selective vulnerability of the [[Substantia Nigra]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]].
