# Caudate Nucleus

The [[Caudate Nucleus]] is a C-shaped striatal structure forming, together with the [[Putamen]], the dorsal striatum (the input nucleus of the [[Basal Ganglia]]). It receives topographically organized dopaminergic input from the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] via the [[Nigrostriatal System]] and glutamatergic cortical input, integrating motor, cognitive, and limbic information.

## Anatomy & Connections

The caudate arches around the lateral ventricle and is functionally divisible into head, body, and tail. It participates in the cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical loops: sensorimotor cortex → putamen (motor loop), associational/prefrontal cortex → caudate (cognitive loop), and limbic cortex → ventral striatum (motivational loop). Spiny projection neurons (D1 direct, D2 indirect) carry striatal output to basal ganglia targets.

## Dopamine & Neuromelanin Link

The SNpc neurons projecting to the caudate are rich in [[Neuromelanin]]; their degeneration in [[Parkinson's Disease]] depletes striatal dopamine, impairing both motor and non-motor loops. While the putamen is more tightly linked to [[Bradykinesia]] and rigidity, caudate dopamine loss contributes to executive dysfunction, working-memory deficits, and the cognitive/depressive features of PD. The shared pigment biology emphasizes why these projection neurons are vulnerable.

## Physiology

The caudate is critical for procedural learning, sequence execution, and action selection. It helps suppress competing motor programs and reinforces habit formation—processes that overlap with the reward-learning circuitry implicated in [[Addiction]].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

In PD, caudate involvement correlates with cognitive and affective symptoms; in Huntington's disease, striatal (especially caudate) atrophy produces chorea and dementia. Neuroimaging (DaTscan, 18F-FDG PET) visualizes caudate dopamine integrity. The caudate thus bridges motor and cognitive basal ganglia function and is a key node in understanding the full PD phenotype.
