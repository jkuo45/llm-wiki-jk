# Monoamine oxidase B

[[Monoamine oxidase B]] (MAO-B) is a flavin-dependent enzyme bound to the outer mitochondrial membrane that catalyzes the oxidative deamination of monoamine neurotransmitters—principally [[Dopamine]], phenylethylamine, and [[Norepinephrine]] (minor substrate)—into aldehydes, ammonia, and hydrogen peroxide. In the neuromelanin field, MAO-B is pivotal because it bioactivates the protoxin [[MPTP]] into the dopaminergic neurotoxin [[MPP+]], and because its activity rises with age, increasing oxidative burden in the aging brain.

## Structure and Cellular Distribution

MAO-B is encoded by the MAOB gene on Xp11.4 and forms dimers anchored in the outer mitochondrial membrane, with the FAD cofactor exposed to the cytosol. It is enriched in [[Glial cells]] (astrocytes, microglia) and serotonergic neurons; MAO-A (a closely related paralog) preferentially deaminates serotonin and norepinephrine. Selective MAO-B inhibitors ([[Selegiline]], rasagiline, safinamide) are used in PD therapy.

## Role in MPTP Toxicity

MPTP crosses the blood-brain barrier, enters astrocytes, and is oxidized by MAO-B to 1-methyl-4-phenyl-2,3-dihydropyridinium (MPDP⁺), then spontaneously to [[MPP+]]. MPP⁺ is released, taken up by dopaminergic neurons via the [[Dopamine Transporter]], and concentrated in mitochondria, where it inhibits [[Complex I]], causing [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], [[Oxidative Stress]], and cell death—mimicking [[Parkinson's Disease]]. MAO-B thus acts as the metabolic gateway converting an inert compound into a selective neurotoxin.

## Relation to Neuromelanin and Aging

By generating H₂O₂ during dopamine catabolism, MAO-B contributes to the oxidative milieu in which catecholamines auto-oxidize into [[Aminochromes]] and ultimately [[Neuromelanin]]. Age-related MAO-B upregulation may accelerate both pigment formation and oxidative damage. Interestingly, neuromelanin-rich neurons are especially vulnerable to MPP⁺, suggesting pigment–toxin synergy.

## Therapeutic Relevance

MAO-B inhibitors are symptomatic and possibly [[Neuroprotection|neuroprotective]] in PD, reducing dopamine breakdown and oxidative stress. They are also chemoprophylactic against MPTP-like envirotoxins (e.g., [[Paraquat]]). Genetically, MAOB variation influences PD risk and treatment response.
