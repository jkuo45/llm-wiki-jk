# Putamen

The [[Putamen]] is a lens-shaped nucleus of the dorsal striatum, together with the [[Caudate Nucleus]] forming the neostriatum within the [[Basal Ganglia]]. It receives the densest dopaminergic innervation from the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] via the [[Nigrostriatal System]] and is a principal substrate of voluntary motor control. Depletion of putaminal dopamine is the proximal cause of the motor disability in [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD).

## Anatomy and Connectivity

The putamen sits lateral to the globus pallidus and is functionally coupled to sensorimotor cortex. Striatal medium spiny neurons integrate cortical glutamatergic input with nigral dopaminergic modulation and project to the globus pallidus internus/externus and [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] (pars reticulata), gating thalamocortical motor output through the direct and indirect pathways. Dopamine, released from [[Synaptic Vesicles]] at nigral terminals, acts on D1 (facilitatory, direct) and D2 (inhibitory, indirect) receptors to calibrate movement amplitude and initiation.

## Dopamine Dependence and PD Pathophysiology

Because the putamen is the most heavily dopaminergic striatal region, it is exquisitely sensitive to nigral degeneration. As dopaminergic terminals retract, the direct/indirect balance shifts toward excessive indirect-pathway inhibition, producing [[Bradykinesia]], [[Rigidity]], and [[Hypokinesia]]. The posterior putamen is affected earliest, accounting for the classic asymmetric motor onset. Presynaptic dopaminergic dysfunction in the putamen can be detected by [[Positron Emission Tomography]] and DAT imaging years before symptoms, reflecting the loss of [[Neuromelanin]]-bearing SNpc neurons upstream.

## Neuromelanin Link

The putamen itself contains no neuromelanin, but its function is wholly dependent on the pigmented SNpc neurons that produce it. The [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]] hypothesis situates the upstream pigment burden as a determinant of how much dopaminergic input the putamen ultimately loses. Thus putaminal dopamine levels serve as a downstream readout of nigral neuromelanin-associated vulnerability.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

The putamen is the target of PD pharmacotherapy: [[L-DOPA]] and dopamine agonists restore striatal signaling, while [[Deep Brain Stimulation]] of the subthalamic nucleus or globus pallidus internus compensates for the circuit imbalance. Putaminal imaging is a standard biomarker in neuroprotective trials aiming to preserve nigrostriatal terminals.
