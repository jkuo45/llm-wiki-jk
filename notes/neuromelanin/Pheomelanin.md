# Pheomelanin

[[Pheomelanin]] is one of the two principal forms of melanin, alongside [[Eumelanin]]. It is a sulfur-rich, alkali-soluble heteropolymer responsible for yellow and red biological coloration (e.g., red hair, freckles). In the brain, pheomelanin is hypothesized to constitute the dense core of [[Neuromelanin]] granules, which are then enveloped by a layer of eumelanin. This bipartite structure distinguishes neuromelanin from cutaneous melanin and has important implications for the pigment's chemistry and toxicity.

## Chemical Structure and Biosynthesis

Pheomelanin derives from the oxidation of the amino acid L-cysteine conjugated to dopaquinone, forming cysteinyldopas that cyclize into benzothiazine and benzothiazole intermediates, which then polymerize. The incorporation of sulfur (from cysteine) is the defining chemical feature and the source of the pigment's name (Greek *phaios*, dusky). Biosynthesis occurs in melanosomes under the control of [[Tyrosinase]], [[Tyrosinase-related protein-1]] (TRP-1), and [[Tyrosinase-related protein-2]] (TRP-2/dopachrome tautomerase). In neural tissue, the relative contribution of enzymatic versus spontaneous catecholamine oxidation to pheomelanin/eumelanin assembly remains debated.

## Properties Relevant to Neuromelanin

Compared with eumelanin, pheomelanin is less effective at broad-spectrum UV/radical scavenging and is more prone to generate reactive oxygen species under illumination or oxidizing conditions. Its sulfur centers and benzothiazine rings can redox-cycle, producing superoxide and other radicals. Within neuromelanin granules, a pheomelanic core surrounded by eumelanin could create an interior microenvironment that, while shielded, is potentially more chemically reactive—particularly when loaded with metal ions. This structural arrangement may explain both the metal-binding capacity of neuromelanin and its dichotomous role as both a sink for toxic catechols and a latent source of [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Relevance to Neurodegeneration

The proportion of pheomelanin versus eumelanin in neuromelanin is not firmly quantified, but the presence of a sulfur-rich core is central to models of pigment toxicity. Pheomelanin's weaker antioxidant capacity and pro-oxidant potential may contribute to the vulnerability of melanized neurons in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]]. Once a [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]] is exceeded, the reactive pheomelanic core could amplify local damage, promoting [[Alpha-synuclein]] aggregation and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Epidemiologically, the poorer photoprotection and higher oxidative tone of pheomelanin-rich tissues echo findings that red-haired (pheomelanin-dominant) individuals show distinct melanoma risk profiles, illustrating how pigment composition shapes cellular fate.
