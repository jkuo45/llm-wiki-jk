# Complex I

[[Complex I]] (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) is the first and largest enzyme complex of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. It oxidizes NADH, transfers electrons to ubiquinone, and pumps protons, contributing to the proton-motive force that drives ATP synthesis. Its inhibition is a central mechanism of [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] in [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Structure & Function

Complex I is a ~1 MDa L-shaped assembly of ~45 subunits (14 conserved mitochondrial-encoded core subunits plus nuclear-encoded accessory proteins). The membrane arm houses the proton pumps; the peripheral arm contains the NADH-binding and flavin (FMN) and iron-sulfur cluster electron-transfer chain. Electrons flow NADH → FMN → Fe-S clusters → ubiquinone, coupled to translocation of four protons per NADH.

## Inhibition and Parkinson's Disease

Post-mortem PD substantia nigra shows a specific ~30–35% reduction in complex I activity, independent of [[Lewy Bodies]] distribution, supporting mitochondrial failure as an early pathogenic event. The prototypic inhibitor is [[MPP+]], the active metabolite of [[MPTP]], which binds the ND2/ND4 region and blocks electron flow; the herbicide [[Paraquat]] and rotenone act similarly. Loss of complex I elevates [[Oxidative Stress]] via increased superoxide and depletes ATP, preferentially killing [[Neuromelanin]]-rich SNpc neurons.

## Relation to Neuromelanin & Selective Vulnerability

Dopaminergic neurons have high baseline oxidative demand and accumulate iron within neuromelanin granules, amplifying complex I–derived radical formation. The combination of metabolic stress, [[Alpha-Synuclein Aggregation]], and impaired [[Mitophagy]] creates a feed-forward loop of damage. This explains why agents that inhibit complex I produce parkinsonism and why mitochondrial resilience is a therapeutic target.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Mitochondrial enhancers (e.g., inhibitors of the MPT pore, PGC-1α activators, [[Coenzyme Q10]]) and complex I–sparing strategies have been trialed in PD. Complex I activity in platelets/monocytes is explored as a peripheral biomarker. The complex I story integrates the toxic, genetic, and aging axes of PD pathogenesis.
