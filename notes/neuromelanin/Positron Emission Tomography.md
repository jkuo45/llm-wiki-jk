# Positron Emission Tomography

[[Positron Emission Tomography]] (PET) is a noninvasive functional imaging modality that detects pairs of gamma photons emitted after the annihilation of positrons released by a radiolabeled tracer. By administering molecule-specific radioligands, PET quantifies regional physiology, receptor occupancy, enzyme activity, and protein aggregation in living brain tissue. In the context of [[Neuromelanin]] and catecholaminergic neurodegeneration, PET provides the primary in vivo window into the integrity of the [[Nigrostriatal System]] and is indispensable for diagnosing and staging [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD).

## Technical Principles

A cyclotron-produced positron emitter (typically ¹⁸F, ¹¹C, or ⁶⁸Ga) is attached to a biologically active molecule. After administration, the tracer accumulates in target tissue; its decay emits positrons that annihilate with electrons, producing two 511-keV photons traveling in opposite directions. Coincidence detectors and reconstruction algorithms yield quantitative maps of tracer binding (often expressed as binding potential or standardized uptake value ratios). Hybrid PET/CT or PET/MRI systems provide anatomical coregistration.

## PET in Dopaminergic and Neuromelanin-Related Disease

Several radiotracers are central to studying neuromelanin-bearing neurons:

- **Presynaptic dopaminergic tracers:** ¹⁸F-FDOPA (aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase substrate reflecting dopamine synthesis), and [[Vesicular Monoamine Transporter 2]] ligands such as ¹⁸F-AV-133 (measures terminal density). Loss of striatal binding reveals nigral dopaminergic degeneration before motor symptoms.
- **Dopamine transporter (DAT) tracers:** ¹²³I-FP-CIT (SPECT) and ¹⁸F-FE-PE2I quantify reuptake sites, a proxy for terminal integrity.
- **Neuroinflammation tracers:** translocator protein (TSPO) ligands visualize activated microglia, a hallmark of [[Neuroinflammation]] accompanying neuromelanin neuron loss.
- **Proteinopathy tracers:** although tau tracers such as [[Flortaucipir]] primarily target Alzheimer-type pathology, novel synuclein ligands are in development for PD.

While PET does not image neuromelanin directly, the dopaminergic terminals it maps belong to the heavily pigmented [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] neurons, so PET-derived deficits indirectly index the loss of neuromelanin-containing cells. Emerging neuromelanin-sensitive MRI complements PET by directly visualizing pigment in the nigra and [[Locus Coeruleus]].

## Clinical and Research Relevance

PET enables early and differential diagnosis of PD versus atypical parkinsonism, tracks disease progression, and serves as a biomarker in therapeutic trials (e.g., neuroprotective or [[L-DOPA]]-sparing interventions). It also probes the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]] concept by relating residual dopaminergic capacity to clinical thresholds.
