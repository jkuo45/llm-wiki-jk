# Bradykinesia

[[Bradykinesia]]—slowness in the initiation and execution of movement—is the cardinal and most disabling motor symptom of [[Parkinson's Disease]]. It is often accompanied by reduced movement amplitude (hypokinesia) and difficulty with repetitive actions (fatigue or "decrementing" amplitude).

## Clinical Features

Bradykinesia manifests as prolonged reaction times, reduced facial expression (hypomimia), decreased blink rate, soft speech (hypophonia), micrographia, and impaired fine motor sequencing. On examination, it is assessed by finger-tapping, hand gripping, and rapid alternating movements. Unlike weakness (which preserves movement speed relative to force), bradykinesia reflects a deficit in movement scaling and internal cueing.

## Pathophysiology

Bradykinesia arises from loss of dopaminergic input to the [[Basal Ganglia]], particularly the [[Nigrostriatal System]] projecting from the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] to the striatum ([[Putamen]], [[Caudate Nucleus]]). Dopamine normally facilitates the direct motor pathway and inhibits the indirect pathway; its loss tilts basal ganglia output toward excessive inhibition of thalamocortical drive, starving motor cortex of enabling signals. [[Neuromelanin]]-laden SNpc neurons are the selectively lost cells, linking pigment biology to symptom genesis.

## Relationship to Other PD Features

Bradykinesia is one member of the PD motor triad (with rest tremor and rigidity) and is the feature most responsive to dopaminergic therapy. It distinguishes PD from conditions with pure tremor. Cognitive [[Chemical memory]]-like deficits and freezing of gait emerge as disease advances.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Bradykinesia is a core diagnostic criterion for PD and a primary treatment target. [[L-DOPA]] and dopamine agonists reliably improve it, whileDeep brain stimulation of the subthalamic nucleus restores circuit facilitation. Quantifying bradykinesia (wearable sensors, UPDRS sub-scores) tracks disease progression and therapy response. Its presence also helps differentiate PD from atypical parkinsonian syndromes, where bradykinesia may be less levodopa-responsive.
