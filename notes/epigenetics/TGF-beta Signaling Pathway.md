# TGF-β Signaling Pathway

The **Transforming Growth Factor-beta (TGF-β) Signaling Pathway** is a pleiotropic cytokine system regulating cellular proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, autophagy, immune modulation, and extracellular matrix (ECM) production. Three mammalian isoforms (TGF-β1, TGF-β2, TGF-β3) signal through a heteromeric complex of type II (TGFBRII) and type I (TGFBRI/ALK5) serine/threonine kinase receptors.

## Canonical SMAD-Dependent Signaling

Ligand binding induces TGFBRII-mediated phosphorylation of TGFBRI, which phosphorylates receptor-regulated SMADs (R-SMADs): [[SMAD2]] and [[SMAD3]]. Phosphorylated R-SMADs complex with co-SMAD [[SMAD4]], translocate to the nucleus, and regulate transcription of target genes in concert with context-dependent co-factors. Key targets include [[CDKN1A]] (p21), [[CDKN2B]] (p15), [[BIM]], [[PAI-1]], [[CTGF]], [[COL1A1]], and [[SNAI1]]. Inhibitory SMADs ([[SMAD6]], [[SMAD7]]) provide negative feedback by competing for receptor binding and recruiting ubiquitin ligases.

## Non-Canonical SMAD-Independent Signaling

TGF-β activates MAPK pathways (ERK, JNK, p38), PI3K/AKT/mTOR, RHOA/ROCK, and integrin-FAK signaling, enabling context-dependent regulation of cytoskeletal dynamics, cell migration, and survival. Crosstalk with Wnt/β-catenin, Notch, and Hippo pathways further expands signaling diversity.

## Dual Role in Cancer: Tumor Suppressor to Oncogenic Driver

- **Early stage (tumor suppressor)**: TGF-β induces cell cycle arrest via [[CDKN1A]]/[[CDKN2B]], apoptosis via [[BIM]], and maintains genomic stability. Loss of TGF-β signaling (TGFBRII mutations, SMAD4 loss) is common in pancreatic, colorectal, and gastric cancers.
- **Late stage (oncogenic)**: Tumor cells acquire resistance to TGF-β growth inhibition while gaining TGF-β-induced [[Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition|EMT]], invasion, immunosuppression, and angiogenesis. TGF-β activates [[SNAI1]], [[SNAI2]], [[ZEB1]], and [[TWIST1]] to repress [[CDH1|E-cadherin]], driving metastasis. TGF-β creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment by inhibiting cytotoxic T cells, NK cells, and promoting Treg differentiation.

## Fibrosis and Tissue Remodeling

Chronic TGF-β activation drives pathological fibrosis in liver (cirrhosis), lung (IPF), kidney (diabetic nephropathy), and heart (cardiac fibrosis). TGF-β activates fibroblasts to [[Myofibroblast|myofibroblasts]], inducing [[Collagen]] deposition, [[CTGF]], and [[PAI-1]]-mediated inhibition of matrix degradation. This parallels its role in wound healing, where transient TGF-β activity is beneficial but persistent signaling causes scarring.

## Immune Regulation

TGF-β is a master regulator of immune tolerance. It suppresses Th1/Th2/Th17 differentiation, promotes Treg (FoxP3+) and Th9 development, and inhibits macrophage and dendritic cell activation. Dysregulation contributes to autoimmunity and cancer immune evasion.

## Connections

- [[SMAD2]] — Primary R-SMAD phosphorylated by TGFBR1.
- [[SMAD3]] — Co-primary R-SMAD; forms complexes with SMAD4.
- [[SMAD4]] — Co-SMAD; common mediator for TGF-β and BMP pathways.
- [[SMAD7]] — Inhibitory SMAD; negative feedback regulator.
- [[Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition]] — TGF-β is the canonical inducer via SNAIL/ZEB/TWIST.
- [[ZEB1]] — EMT transcription factor directly repressed by TGF-β/SMAD.
- [[Fibrosis]] — TGF-β is the central driver of organ fibrosis.
- [[Cancer]] — Dual tumor suppressor/oncogenic role depending on stage.
- [[CDH1]] — E-cadherin repressed during TGF-β-induced EMT.
- [[CTGF]] — Connective tissue growth factor; TGF-β target amplifying fibrosis.
- [[Yamanaka Factors]] — TGF-β inhibition enhances reprogramming efficiency.
- [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] — TGF-β signaling modulates pluripotency states.
- [[Aging]] — TGF-β signaling increases with age, contributing to inflammaging and fibrosis.
- [[Senescence]] — TGF-β induces senescence in certain contexts; SASP includes TGF-β.
- [[Inflammation]] — TGF-β is anti-inflammatory acutely but pro-fibrotic chronically.
- [[Wnt]] — TGF-β and Wnt pathways synergize in EMT and fibrosis.
- [[Hypoxia]] — HIF-1α and TGF-β crosstalk in fibrosis and cancer.
