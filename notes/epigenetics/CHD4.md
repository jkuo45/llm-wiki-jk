# CHD4

**[[CHD4]]**, also known as Chromodomain Helicase DNA-binding protein 4 or **Mi-2β**, is the catalytic ATPase core of the Nucleosome Remodeling and Deacetylase ([[NuRD complex]]). It is an ATP-dependent [[Chromatin Remodeling]] enzyme central to transcriptional repression, DNA repair, and genome stability.

## Structure & Domains

CHD4 belongs to the CHD (chromodomain-helicase-DNA-binding) family. Its architecture includes two N-terminal chromodomains (which bind methylated H3K9/H3K27), a central SNF2-like ATPase/ helicase domain that hydrolyzes ATP to slide or evict nucleosomes, and a C-terminal domain that scaffolds other NuRD subunits (HDAC1/2, MBD2/3, RbAp46/48, MTA1-3, p66α/β). Chromodomains link CHD4 to pre-existing repressive histone marks, targeting remodeling to silent chromatin.

## Mechanism/Function

As the NuRD ATPase, CHD4 repositions or removes nucleosomes to compact chromatin, promoting [[Transcriptional Repression]] in cooperation with the complex's [[Histone Deacetylase]] activity. It is recruited by sequence-specific transcription factors (e.g., REST, EGR1, NURF/MTA complexes) to silence differentiation genes, proliferative programs, and repetitive elements. By coupling deacetylation with nucleosome movement, NuRD establishes a deeply repressed, methylation-permissive state.

## DNA Damage Response

CHD4 is a rapid, early responder to DNA lesions. Following [[DNA Damage]], CHD4 is recruited by ATM/ATR-dependent factors (e.g., the MRN complex and PARylated proteins) to double-strand breaks, where it remodels chromatin locally to permit access by repair machinery — a prerequisite for homologous recombination and non-homologous end joining. CHD4 depletion causes radiosensitivity and genomic instability.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

CHD4 shapes developmental gene programs, maintains stem-cell identity, and safeguards the [[Epigenome]]. Dysregulation of CHD4 and [[NuRD complex|NuRD]] is implicated in many [[Cancer]] types, where mutation or misexpression drives widespread chromatin alterations and inappropriate silencing of tumor suppressors. CHD4 is also linked to neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative disorders, reflecting the importance of precise chromatin remodeling during brain development.
