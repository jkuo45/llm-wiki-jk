# DGCR8

## Overview

**DGCR8** (DiGeorge Syndrome Critical Region 8; ortholog **Pasha** in invertebrates) is a nuclear, heme-binding, **double-stranded RNA-binding protein** that serves as the essential cofactor of the RNase III enzyme **[[Drosha]]**. Together they constitute the **[[Microprocessor complex|Microprocessor]]**, which initiates canonical [[MicroRNA]] biogenesis by cleaving primary miRNA transcripts (pri-miRNAs) into precursor hairpins (pre-miRNAs).

## Structure & Domains

- Contains a **heme-binding (Rhed) domain**, two tandem **double-stranded RNA-binding domains (dsRBDs)**, and a C-terminal tail that anchors [[Drosha]].
- Binds a **Fe(III) heme cofactor** as a dimer; heme binding is required for accurate and efficient pri-miRNA processing.
- Two DGCR8 molecules associate with one Drosha to form the functional Microprocessor.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

> [!info] Substrate recognition
> DGCR8 acts as the molecular "ruler/anchor" that recognizes the **apical junction and single-stranded flanking regions** of the pri-miRNA hairpin, positioning [[Drosha]]'s catalytic center ~11 bp away at the basal junction for precise cleavage.

1. RNA Pol II transcribes a **pri-miRNA** stem-loop.
2. **DGCR8** binds the apical ssRNA–dsRNA junction and sequence motifs (e.g., UGU).
3. **[[Drosha]]** cleaves the stem to release **pre-miRNA**.
4. Pre-miRNA is exported by **[[Exportin-5]]** and matured by **[[Dicer]]** and **[[Argonaute]]** in the [[RISC]].

## Physiological Function & Regulation

- Essential for embryonic development and stem-cell self-renewal; *Dgcr8* deletion abolishes canonical miRNA production.
- **Auto-regulatory feedback:** Drosha cleaves hairpins within *DGCR8* mRNA to limit DGCR8 levels, while DGCR8 stabilizes Drosha — a reciprocal homeostatic loop.
- Regulated by heme availability, phosphorylation, and localization.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- *DGCR8* lies within the **22q11.2 deletion** region; its haploinsufficiency contributes to the miRNA-processing defects seen in **[[DiGeorge Syndrome]]/22q11.2 deletion syndrome**.
- Microprocessor dysfunction globally alters the miRNA-based [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] network, influencing [[Senescence]], the [[SASP]], and [[Inflammaging]].
- Electrophilic quinones such as [[Adrenochrome]] may covalently modify DGCR8/Drosha, potentially altering pri-miRNA processing rates.
