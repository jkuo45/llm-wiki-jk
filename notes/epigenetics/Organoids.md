# Organoids

[[Organoids]] are tiny, self-organized three-dimensional tissue cultures grown from stem cells that recapitulate key structural and functional features of the organ of origin. Unlike traditional monolayers, organoids undergo cell sorting, polarization, and lumen formation to build mini-organs with physiologically relevant cellular diversity and spatial organization.

## Derivation & Types

Organoids can be generated from adult stem cells (e.g., intestinal crypt Lgr5⁺ cells), embryonic stem cells, or [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]. Depending on the cues supplied, they form brain, intestinal, hepatic, renal, pancreatic, retinal, and many other organoids. Directed differentiation with morphogens (Wnt, FGF, BMP, TGF-β) and embedding in Matrigel or other matrix supports self-organization into polarized epithelial cysts, villi, or layered structures.

## Mechanism of Self-Organization

Organoid formation relies on the same developmental signaling programs that pattern organs in vivo. Local gradients of morphogens drive symmetry breaking, progenitor expansion, and lineage commitment, while cell–cell and cell–matrix interactions (including [[Extracellular Matrix]] cues) establish apical–basal polarity and tissue architecture. The result is a system that models ontogeny and homeostasis in vitro.

## Applications

Organoids are central to [[Disease Modeling]], enabling patient-specific studies of monogenic and complex disorders, infection (e.g., viral tropism), and cancer evolution. They support [[Regenerative Medicine]] by providing transplantable tissue and by screening differentiation protocols. Related constructs such as [[Assembloids]] — fused organoids modeling inter-tissue interactions — extend this to multi-organ and neural-circuit contexts.

## Limitations & Advances

Organoids lack vasculature and immune components, limiting size and physiological fidelity; vascularization strategies and co-culture are active areas. Nevertheless, organoid-based platforms now inform drug toxicity, personalized medicine, and developmental biology, making them a cornerstone of modern stem-cell research.
