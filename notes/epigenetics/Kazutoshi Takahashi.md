# Kazutoshi Takahashi

Kazutoshi Takahashi is a Japanese stem cell biologist whose doctoral and postdoctoral work in the laboratory of [[Shinya Yamanaka]] at Kyoto University produced the foundational experiments that established [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs). Through a systematic, large-scale screening campaign, Takahashi tested candidate transcription factors to identify the minimal combination capable of reverting differentiated [[Somatic Cells]] to a pluripotent state.

## Key Contributions

In 2006, Takahashi and Yamanaka reported in *Cell* that the retroviral introduction of 24 candidate factors into mouse [[Fibroblasts]] could generate pluripotent colonies, and that a subset of just four factors — [[Oct4]], [[Sox2]], [[Klf4]], and [[c-Myc]], collectively known as the [[Yamanaka Factors]] or [[OSKM]] — was sufficient. This was the first demonstration that a fully differentiated somatic cell could be reprogrammed into a state functionally equivalent to an [[Embryonic Stem Cells|embryonic stem cell]]. In 2007, the team extended the method to human cells, published in *Cell* and *Science*, catalysing a global shift away from ethically contentious embryo-derived lines.

Takahashi's experimental approach — including the rigorous validation of pluripotency through teratoma formation, chimera contribution, and tetraploid complementation — set the methodological standard for the field. His work also explored the stoichiometry and integration of the reprogramming factors, illuminating how factor dosage and the coordination of the [[OSKM]] gene-regulatory network govern reprogramming efficiency and kinetics.

## Recognition and Legacy

The iPSC discovery was recognized with the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, awarded to Yamanaka (shared with [[John Gurdon]] for somatic cell nuclear transfer). Takahashi, as first author of the landmark papers, is widely credited as the co-discoverer of iPSCs. His contributions underpin modern [[Disease Modeling]], [[Drug Discovery]], and [[Regenerative Medicine]], as well as later advances such as [[Partial Reprogramming]] for [[Rejuvenation]]. He continues research on reprogramming mechanisms and the epigenetic basis of pluripotency at Kyoto University.
