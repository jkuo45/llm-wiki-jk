# HDAC

**[[HDAC]]** stands for **[[Histone Deacetylase]]**. These are a class of enzymes that remove acetyl groups from ε-N-acetyl-lysine residues on histones (and many non-histone proteins), allowing chromatin to wrap DNA more tightly and generally repressing transcription.

## Classification

Mammalian HDACs are divided into four classes based on structure and cofactor:

- **Class I** (HDAC1, 2, 3, 8): predominantly nuclear, zinc-dependent; HDAC1/2 are core components of the [[NuRD complex]] and Sin3a repressor complexes.
- **Class II** (HDAC4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10): shuttle between nucleus and cytoplasm; HDAC6 deacetylates cytoplasmic targets such as α-tubulin and HSP90.
- **Class IV** (HDAC11): zinc-dependent, structurally distinct.
- **Class III — [[Sirtuins]] (SIRT1–7)**: NAD+-dependent deacetylases that link metabolic state to chromatin regulation and are strongly implicated in [[Aging]] and longevity.

## Mechanism of Action

By removing acetyl groups, HDACs restore the positive charge of lysine side chains on histone tails, strengthening their electrostatic interaction with the negatively charged DNA backbone. This leads to a more condensed [[Chromatin]] structure ([[Heterochromatin]]), which generally results in gene silencing. Beyond histones, HDACs deacetylate transcription factors (e.g., p53, [[FOXO]]), tubulin, and metabolic enzymes, broadening their regulatory reach.

## Physiological Function

HDACs are essential for development, [[Cell Cycle]] control, [[DNA Damage Response]], and cellular differentiation. Balanced HDAC activity maintains transcriptional programs and genome stability. Class III sirtuins mediate responses to [[Caloric Restriction]] and metabolic stress, coupling energy availability to chromatin state.

## Impact on Aging and Disease

As organisms [[Aging|age]], the activity of certain HDACs becomes dysregulated, contributing to "epigenetic drift" and loss of genomic stability. HDAC inhibitors (e.g., vorinostat, romidepsin, [[Sodium Valproate]]) are approved anticancer agents and are under active [[Geroscience]] investigation for restoring youthful gene expression in brain and heart. Conversely, sirtuin activation is a leading longevity strategy.
