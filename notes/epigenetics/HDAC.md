# HDAC

**[[HDAC]]** stands for **[[Histone Deacetylase]]**. These are a class of enzymes that remove acetyl groups from an ε-N-acetyl lysine amino acid on a histone, allowing the histones to wrap the DNA more tightly.

### 1. Mechanism of Action
By removing acetyl groups, HDACs restore the positive charge of the histones, strengthening their bond with DNA. This leads to a more condensed [[Chromatin]] structure ([[Heterochromatin]]), which generally results in [[Transcription|gene silencing]].

### 2. Classification
HDACs are categorized into four classes based on their structure and cofactor requirements:
*   **Classes I, II, and IV**: Use zinc as a cofactor.
*   **Class III**: Also known as [[Sirtuins|Sirtuins]], these use NAD+ as a cofactor and are highly linked to longevity and metabolic health.

### 3. Impact on [[notes/_link/Aging]]
As we age, the activity of certain HDACs can become dysregulated, leading to "epigenetic drift" and the loss of genomic stability. HDAC inhibitors are currently a major area of research in [[Geroscience]] for their potential to treat [[Cancer]] and extend healthspan.

### Connections
- **[[Histone Deacetylase]]**: The full name for this class of enzymes.
- **[[Histone Acetylation]]**: The process that HDACs reverse.
- **[[Sirtuins]]**: A specialized class of HDACs critical for aging research.
- **[[Heterochromatin]]**: The condensed state of chromatin promoted by deacetylation.
