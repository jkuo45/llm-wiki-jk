# Decitabine

**Decitabine** (5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine; trade name Dacogen) is a deoxyribose nucleoside analog that functions as a potent [[DNA Methylation|DNA methyltransferase]] inhibitor (DNMTi). It is a cornerstone drug for [[Epigenetic Modifiers|epigenetic therapy]] in [[Myelodysplastic Syndrome|myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS)]] and [[Acute Myeloid Leukemia|acute myeloid leukemia (AML)]]. At subcytotoxic concentrations, decitabine is also a potent inducer of [[Stress-Induced Premature Senescence]].

## Mechanism of Action

Decitabine acts as a prodrug that must be activated through phosphorylation to exert its epigenetic and cytotoxic effects.

1. **DNA Incorporation**: Decitabine is phosphorylated by [[Deoxycytidine kinase]] (dCK) to its triphosphate form (5-aza-dCTP). This active form is then incorporated into [[DNA]] during the [[S phase]] of the [[Cell cycle]] through the action of [[DNA Polymerase]].

2. **DNMT Trapping**: Once incorporated into the DNA, decitabine forms covalent adducts with [[DNMT1]], the primary maintenance methyltransferase. This covalent binding prevents the enzyme from completing its methylation cycle, effectively "trapping" it on the DNA.

3. **Passive DNA Demethylation**: The trapping and subsequent proteasomal degradation of the DNMT1-DNA complex leads to a depletion of the available enzyme pool. As the cell undergoes successive rounds of [[DNA Replication]], the lack of functional DNMT1 results in a progressive, passive [[DNA Demethylation]] of CpG sites across the genome. This can lead to the re-expression of hypermethylated tumor suppressor genes.

4. **Induction of DNA Damage and Senescence**: The formation of these covalent adducts can also cause replication fork stalling, which triggers a [[DNA Damage Response]] (DDR). This DDR, combined with the epigenetic reprogramming, can drive cells into either [[Apoptosis]] or [[Stress-Induced Premature Senescence]].

## Clinical Relevance

Decitabine is widely used in the management of hematological malignancies:

- **Myelodysplastic Syndrome (MDS)**: It is a standard-of-care treatment for MDS. A common regimen involves 15–20 mg/m² administered via intravenous infusion daily for 5 days per cycle. Response rates are approximately 30–50%, and efficacy is often higher in patients harboring [[TET2]] mutations.
- **Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML)**: It is used in the treatment of AML, particularly in older patients or those who are not candidates for intensive induction chemotherapy.
- **Re-expression of Tumor Suppressors**: A key therapeutic goal is the reactivation of silenced genes, such as [[CDKN2B|p15^INK4b^]], [[CDH1|E-cadherin]], and [[MLH1]], which can restore normal cell cycle control or DNA mismatch repair.

## Resistance

Resistance to decitabine can arise through several cellular adaptations:
- **Metabolic Changes**: Decreased expression or activity of [[Deoxycytidine kinase]] (dCK), which is required for activation, or increased expression of [[Cytidine deaminase]] (CDA), which inactivates the drug.
- **Compensatory Methylation**: Upregulation of other methyltransferases, such as [[DNMT3a]] and [[DNMT3b]], to compensate for the loss of DNMT1.
- **Efflux Mechanisms**: Increased expression of drug efflux pumps, such as [[ABCB1]] (P-glycoprotein).
- **Genomic Alterations**: Selection for cell populations with pre-existing demethylated genomes or altered DNA repair capacities.
