# DNA Methylation

**[[DNA Methylation]]** is one of the most extensively studied epigenetic modifications, involving the addition of a methyl group to the 5th carbon of the cytosine ring, typically within [[CpG Island|CpG dinucleotides]]. It plays a fundamental role in regulating gene expression, maintaining genomic stability, and guiding cellular differentiation.

### 1. Role in [[Aging]]
Aging is characterized by a phenomenon known as "epigenetic drift," which involves two primary changes to the DNA methylome:
*   **Global Hypomethylation:** A widespread loss of methylation across the genome, particularly in repetitive elements like [[LINE-1]] and [[Alu element]]. This can lead to [[Genomic Instability]] and the activation of [[Transposable Elements]].
*   **Site-Specific Hypermethylation:** Increased methylation at specific locations, often within the promoters of tumor suppressor genes or genes involved in developmental pathways. This can lead to inappropriate [[Transcription|gene silencing]].

### 2. [[Epigenetic Clock|Epigenetic Clocks]]
The consistency of these methylation changes across individuals has allowed researchers to develop "clocks" (e.g., the Horvath clock, [[DNAmGrimAge]]) that can predict biological age with high accuracy. These clocks use the methylation status of a specific set of CpG sites to estimate the physiological state of tissues and organs.

### 3. Maintenance and Regulation
The patterns of DNA methylation are established and maintained by a family of enzymes called [[DNA Methyltransferase|DNA methyltransferases]] (DNMTs). [[DNMT1]] is primarily responsible for maintenance during [[DNA Replication]], while [[DNMT3a]] and [[DNMT3b]] handle de novo methylation.

### Connections
- **[[Aging]]**: DNA methylation patterns are a primary biomarker for biological aging.
- **[[DNA Methyltransferase]]**: The enzymes responsible for establishing and maintaining methylation marks.
- **[[Epigenetic Clock]]**: Tools that use DNA methylation data to measure the rate of aging.
- **[[CpG Island]]**: Genomic regions where DNA methylation frequently occurs to regulate gene expression.
