# DNA Methylation

[[DNA Methylation]] is a fundamental epigenetic mechanism involving the covalent addition of a methyl group to the 5-position of the cytosine ring in DNA, typically within CpG dinucleotides. It plays a critical role in [[Gene Silencing]], genomic imprinting, and the maintenance of chromatin structure.

## Overview

DNA methylation is one of the most well-studied and important epigenetic modifications. It provides a stable, yet reversible, way for cells to regulate gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequence. By adding methyl groups to specific sites in the genome, the cell can control which genes are "on" or "off," a process that is essential for cellular differentiation, development, and the maintenance of tissue-specific identities.

## Mechanism/Function

The process of DNA methylation is catalyzed by a family of enzymes called [[DNA Methyltransferase]]s (DNMTs). In mammals, methylation occurs almost exclusively at CpG dinucleotides (where a cytosine is followed by a guanine). High levels of methylation in promoter regions, often concentrated in areas known as [[CpG Islands]], are strongly associated with [[Gene Silencing]].

The silencing of genes through DNA methylation can occur via several interconnected mechanisms:
1. **Direct Interference**: The presence of methyl groups in the major groove of the DNA can physically prevent the binding of transcription factors to their target sequences.
2. **Recruitment of Repressive Complexes**: Methylated DNA can be recognized by specialized proteins called methyl-CpG-binding domain (MBD) proteins. These MBD proteins act as scaffolds to recruit other repressive complexes, including [[Chromatin Remodeling]] factors and histone deacetylases (HDACs). This leads to the formation of a highly condensed, transcriptionally inactive chromatin state (heterochromatin).

DNA methylation is essential for several key biological processes:
- **Cellular Differentiation**: It helps define and maintain the specific gene expression profiles of different cell types.
- **Genomic Imprinting**: It is the molecular basis for imprinting, where certain genes are expressed in a parent-of-origin-specific manner.
- **X-Chromosome Inactivation**: It plays a role in the epigenetic silencing of one of the X chromosomes in females.
- **Transposable Element Silencing**: It helps to repress potentially harmful repetitive elements and retrotransposons within the genome.

## Clinical Relevance

Aberrant DNA methylation patterns are a hallmark of many diseases, especially [[Cancer]]. In many cancers, a characteristic pattern of "epigenetic dysregulation" is observed, consisting of:
- **Global Hypomethylation**: A general loss of methylation across the genome, which can lead to genomic instability and the aberrant activation of oncogenes or transposable elements.
- **Focal Hypermethylation**: The targeted increase of methylation in the promoter regions of tumor suppressor genes, leading to their silencing and contributing to tumorigenesis.

Additionally, defects in the machinery of DNA methylation or in the processes of imprinting can lead to various neurodevelopmental and growth disorders.
