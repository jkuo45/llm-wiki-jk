# E2F

[[E2F]] designates a family of transcription factors in higher eukaryotes that function as master regulators of cell cycle progression, DNA replication, and apoptosis. First identified as a factor required to activate the adenovirus E2 promoter, E2F is now recognized as the effector arm through which the [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] (pRb) tumor-suppressor pathway controls the [[G1 Phase]] → S phase transition of the [[Cell Cycle]]. The mammalian family comprises eight members (E2F1–E2F8) that fall into activators (E2F1–E2F3a), repressors (E2F3b–E2F5), and the atypical, predominantly mitotic regulators E2F6–E2F8.

## Structure & Domains

All E2F proteins share an N-terminal DNA-binding domain (DBD) recognizing the consensus TTTCGCGC motif, a dimerization domain that binds partner proteins of the [[DP]] family (DP1/DP2) required for high-affinity DNA binding, and a C-terminal transactivation/repression domain. E2F1–E2F3 additionally carry a conserved "marked box" and a TRRAP-binding motif that recruit histone acetyltransferases, linking E2F to [[Chromatin]] remodeling. The tumor-suppressor [[RB1]] protein binds the C-terminal domain of activating E2Fs, masking their transactivation surface.

## Mechanism of Action

In early [[G1 Phase]], hypophosphorylated [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] (pRb) is bound to E2F, actively repressing target genes by recruiting [[Histone Deacetylase]] (HDAC) and chromatin-condensing activities. Progression through G1 is driven by [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]] complexes (cyclin D–CDK4/6, then cyclin E–CDK2) that hyperphosphorylate pRb, releasing E2F. Freed E2F activates a coordinated transcriptional program including *MYC*, *Cyclin E*, *Cyclin A*, *CDC6*, *ORC1*, *thymidylate synthase*, *dihydrofolate reductase*, and DNA polymerase subunits — the machinery for S-phase entry and [[DNA Replication]]. E2F also induces [[Apoptosis]] through p53-dependent and -independent routes when deregulated, acting as a fail-safe against unscheduled proliferation.

## Physiological Function

E2F coordinates the temporal expression of genes required for nucleotide biosynthesis, DNA repair, centrosome duplication, and mitotic entry, thereby ensuring genome duplication occurs exactly once per cycle. Beyond proliferation, E2F family members participate in terminal differentiation, development, and the maintenance of [[Quiescence]] (where repressive E2F–pRb complexes lock cells out of cycle). E2F7/E2F8 are essential for placental development and restrain the G2/M program.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Loss of [[RB1]] or upstream CDK deregulation (common in [[Cancer]]) liberates E2F constitutively, driving unscheduled S-phase entry and genomic instability — a hallmark of retinoblastoma, small-cell lung cancer, and many other tumors. Conversely, E2F1 can act as a tumor suppressor via apoptosis. E2F activity is a biomarker of proliferation (e.g., Ki67-correlated signatures) and a therapeutic node: CDK4/6 inhibitors ([[Palbociclib]], [[Abemaciclib]]) restore pRb-mediated E2F repression and are approved in breast cancer. E2F's interplay with the [[NuRD complex]] and other chromatin remodelers further ties cell-cycle control to [[Epigenetic Remodeling]] and [[Pluripotency]] networks.
