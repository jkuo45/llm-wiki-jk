# let-7 microRNA

The [[let-7 microRNA]] family is a conserved group of short (~22 nt) non-coding RNAs that regulate developmental timing and act as tumor suppressors. First identified in *Caenorhabditis elegans* as a controller of larval-to-adult transition, let-7 is now recognized as a master regulator of [[Differentiation]] and a barrier to oncogenic transformation across vertebrates.

## Biogenesis & Regulation

let-7 is transcribed as a primary transcript (pri-let-7) and processed by Drosha/DGCR8 and Dicer. Its maturation is tightly controlled: the RNA-binding protein [[Lin28]] (Lin28A/B) blocks let-7 processing by binding the terminal loop and recruiting [[ZCCHC11]] (TUT4/7) to add oligo-U tails that provoke degradation — a mechanism crucial in embryonic stem cells and re-activated in many cancers. Additional regulators include LIN28B, hnRNP A1, and cold-induced RNA-binding proteins. Reduced let-7 abundance thus marks immature, proliferative, and pluripotent states.

## Mechanism of Action

Mature let-7 represses target mRNAs by seed-sequence base-pairing, typically at 3′ UTRs, leading to translational inhibition and mRNA decay. Key oncogenic targets include *RAS* (KRAS, NRAS, HRAS), *MYC*, *HMGA2*, *CDK6*, *CDC25A*, and *LIN28* itself, forming a double-negative feedback loop with Lin28. By dampening these effectors, let-7 restricts proliferation, promotes [[Differentiation]], and limits [[Pluripotency]] and self-renewal.

## Physiological Function

During development let-7 orchestrates the timing of cell-fate exit and tissue maturation. In adult tissues it maintains differentiation and suppresses inappropriate proliferation. Its expression rises as cells leave the pluripotent state, and ectopic let-7 can accelerate the loss of [[Pluripotency]] during reprogramming or differentiation.

## Pathological & Research Relevance

let-7 is frequently downregulated in [[Cancer]], where low levels correlate with poor prognosis, chemoresistance, and enhanced tumor-initiating capacity. Restoration of let-7 exerts tumor-suppressive effects in preclinical models. Because Lin28/let-7 governs the pluripotency network, the axis is also a focal point in stem-cell biology, reprogramming, and the design of differentiation protocols. let-7 mimics are under investigation as therapeutic agents.
