# Tissue Engineering

[[Tissue Engineering]] is the interdisciplinary practice of combining cells, engineering materials, and biochemical or physical signals to improve, repair, or replace biological tissues. It sits at the interface of biology, materials science, and medicine, aiming to restore lost function through de novo tissue construction rather than conventional devices alone.

## Core Components

A tissue-engineered construct typically integrates three elements:

- **Cells** — often [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]], adult stem cells, or primary cells (e.g., [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]], chondrocytes) chosen for proliferative or differentiated potential.
- **Scaffolds** — biodegradable matrices providing mechanical support and topological cues. These may be natural (collagen, [[Extracellular Matrix]]-derived hydrogels, [[Laminin]]) or synthetic polymers tuned for porosity and degradation.
- **Signals** — growth factors, mechanical strain, and oxygen tension that direct [[Differentiation]], migration, and [[Tissue Repair]].

## Mechanism & Strategy

Cells are seeded onto or within scaffolds and exposed to spatiotemporal cues that recapitulate developmental patterning, guiding self-organization into organized tissue. Bioreactors supply perfusion and mechanical loading to mature constructs. The goal is to yield vascularized, innervated tissue that integrates with host biology — a central aim of [[Regenerative Medicine]].

## Applications

Tissue engineering underpins skin substitutes for burns, cartilage and bone grafts, engineered blood vessels, and bioartificial liver or kidney assist devices. It synergizes with [[Organoids]] and [[Assembloids]] for modeling and with patient-specific iPSCs to reduce immunogenicity. Scaffold-free and 3D-bioprinted approaches are expanding the complexity achievable in vitro.

## Challenges

Major hurdles include vascularization, immune acceptance, scale-up, and long-term functional durability. Matching the mechanical and biochemical niche of native tissue remains critical, as does controlling fibrosis and ensuring host integration.
