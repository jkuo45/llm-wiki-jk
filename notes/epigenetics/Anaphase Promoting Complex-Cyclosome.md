# Anaphase Promoting Complex-Cyclosome

The **[[Anaphase Promoting Complex-Cyclosome]]** (APC/C) is a massive, multi-subunit **[[E3 Ubiquitin Ligase]]** that serves as the central engine of the eukaryotic cell cycle. It is responsible for orchestrating the irreversible transitions from metaphase to anaphase and facilitating the exit from mitosis. The APC/C achieves this by targeting a specific set of regulatory proteins for polyubiquitination, which marks them for rapid degradation by the **[[26S Proteasome]]**, thereby ensuring that cell cycle stages proceed in a precise, unidirectional, and temporal order.

## Overview

The APC/C is a complex assembly of numerous subunits, including scaffold proteins and catalytic components. Its activity is not constant but is strictly regulated by its association with two primary co-activator proteins: **[[CDC20]]** and **[[CDH1]]**. These co-activators recognize specific destruction motifs (such as the D-box or KEN-box) on target substrates, thereby determining the substrate specificity of the APC/C at different stages of the cell cycle.

The fundamental role of the APC/C is to control the proteolysis of key regulators that govern mitotic progression, sister chromatid separation, and the inactivation of mitotic kinases. By degrading these proteins, the APC/C ensures that the cell successfully completes division and enters the next phase of the cell cycle.

## Mechanism and Function

The APC/C operates through a highly regulated sequence of substrate recognition and degradation, primarily categorized by its association with different co-activators:

### 1. Metaphase to Anaphase Transition (APC/C-CDC20)

During early mitosis, the APC/C associates with the co-activator **[[CDC20]]**. This complex is the primary driver of the transition from metaphase to anaphase. The APC/C-CDC20 targets two critical proteins for degradation:

- **[[Securin]]**: Securin is a potent inhibitor of the protease **[[Separase]]**. When the APC/C-CDC20 complex degrades securin, separase is released from inhibition. Active separase then cleaves the **[[Cohesin]]** complexes that hold sister chromatids together, allowing them to segregate to opposite poles of the cell during anaphase.
- **[[Cyclin B]]**: Cyclin B is the essential regulatory subunit of **[[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 1]]** (CDK1). The degradation of Cyclin B by APC/C-CDC20 leads to the rapid inactivation of the CDK1 complex, which is a prerequisite for mitotic exit, including cytokinesis and the reformation of the nuclear envelope.

### 2. Mitotic Exit and G1 Phase (APC/C-CDH1)

As the cell exits mitosis and progresses into the G1 phase, the co-activator CDC20 is replaced by **[[CDH1]]**. The APC/C-CDH1 complex has a broader and more persistent substrate specificity compared to APC/C-CDC20. It functions to maintain low levels of mitotic cyclins and other proteins (such as Geminin) throughout G1. This activity is essential for preventing the premature initiation of DNA replication (S phase) and ensuring the cell remains in a stable G1 state.

### 3. Regulation by the Spindle Assembly Checkpoint (SAC)

The APC/C is the ultimate effector of the **[[Spindle Assembly Checkpoint]]** (SAC). The SAC is a crucial surveillance mechanism that ensures all chromosomes are correctly attached to the mitotic spindle and are under bipolar tension before anaphase begins. If any kinetochore remains unattached, signaling proteins—such as **[[Mad2]]** and **[[BubR1]]**—inhibit the APC/C-CDC20 complex. This effectively halts the cell cycle in metaphase, preventing the catastrophic missegregation of chromosomes.

## Clinical Relevance

Because the APC/C is the master regulator of chromosomal segregation, its dysfunction is profoundly linked to several pathological states:

- **Cancer and Aneuploidy**: Many cancers are characterized by **[[Aneuploidy]]** (an abnormal number of chromosomes). Mutations in APC/C subunits, their co-activators (CDC20 or CDH1), or the components of the SAC can lead to premature or improper anaphase onset. This causes chromosome missegregation, contributing to the genomic instability and phenotypic diversity observed in many tumor types.
- **Chromosomal Instability (CIN)**: Defects in APC/C-mediated degradation can lead to continuous chromosomal instability, which promotes the evolution and adaptation of cancer cells, often leading to increased malignancy and drug resistance.
- **Developmental Disorders**: Proper APC/C activity is essential for the correct timing of cell divisions during embryogenesis; disruptions can lead to severe developmental abnormalities.
