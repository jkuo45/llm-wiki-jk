# Klf4

Klf4 (Krüppel-like factor 4) is a zinc-finger [[Transcription Factor]] of the Krüppel-like factor family. It regulates proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and somatic-cell reprogramming, and is one of the four [[Yamanaka Factors]] (alongside Oct4, Sox2, and c-Myc) used to generate [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]. Klf4 can act as both tumor suppressor and oncogene depending on context.

## Structure and DNA Binding

Klf4 contains three C2H2 zinc fingers at its C-terminus that bind GC-rich elements (e.g., the "CACCC" and "GT-box" motifs) in target promoters and enhancers. Its N-terminal transactivation/repression domains and an inhibitory domain allow context-dependent switching between activation and repression. Klf4 is sumoylated and acetylated, modulating stability and activity.

## Roles in Differentiation and Homeostasis

In the gut, Klf4 is expressed in terminally differentiated epithelial cells, where it enforces quiescence and goblet-cell differentiation while repressing proliferation. It maintains epithelial integrity, responding to injury and microbiota signals. Similar differentiation-promoting roles appear in skin, lung, and thymus.

## Reprogramming and Pluripotency

As a Yamanaka factor, Klf4 contributes to induced [[Pluripotency]] by activating pluripotency genes (e.g., *Nanog*, *Sox2*) and suppressing the somatic program. It cooperates with the [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]] (MET) during reprogramming, opposing [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] and the EMT drivers [[Snail]] and [[Slug]]. Klf4 thus sits at the polarity between epithelial identity (MET) and mesenchymal dispersal (EMT).

## Pathological Relevance

Klf4 is frequently downregulated in colorectal, breast, and bladder carcinomas, where loss correlates with poor differentiation and EMT, supporting its tumor-suppressor role. Conversely, Klf4 can be upregulated and pro-tumorigenic in gastric and some squamous cancers. Its expression is exploited as a biomarker of differentiation state, and KLF4 transduction is investigated for cell-fate engineering.
