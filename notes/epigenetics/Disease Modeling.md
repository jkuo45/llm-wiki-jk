# Disease Modeling

[[Disease Modeling]] is the scientific practice of recreating a disease's molecular, cellular, and tissue-level features outside the human body to study mechanism and test interventions. The advent of [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) transformed the field by providing renewable, patient-specific human cells that retain the donor's genetic background — overcoming the limited access to primary human tissue (especially brain, heart, and pancreas).

## iPSC-Based Modeling

Patient fibroblasts or blood cells are reprogrammed to iPSCs and differentiated into disease-relevant lineages: motor neurons for [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]], dopaminergic neurons for [[Parkinson's Disease]], cardiomyocytes for arrhythmia syndromes, and [[Retinal Pigment Epithelium]] for [[Macular Degeneration]]. These "disease-in-a-dish" models let researchers observe pathological hallmarks — protein aggregation, mitochondrial dysfunction, altered electrophysiology — that animal models often fail to recapitulate.

## Integration with Genome Editing

Modern disease modeling frequently pairs iPSCs with [[CRISPR]] genome editing to create [[Isogenic Pair|isogenic pairs]] — cell lines identical except for a single pathogenic variant. Comparing such pairs isolates the causal effect of a mutation from the "noise" of individual genetic background, strengthening causal inference. Base/prime editing further enables the study of protective and risk alleles.

## Complex 3D Systems

Two- and three-dimensional assemblies extend modeling fidelity: [[Organoids]] (self-organizing tissue buds) and [[Assembloids]] (combined organoids) model tissue architecture, cell–cell signaling, and long-range processes like neuronal migration and axon guidance. Vascularized and immune-inclusive organoids now approach aspects of systemic pathophysiology.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Disease modeling underpins human-relevant [[Drug Discovery]] (providing assay-ready platforms for [[High-Throughput Screening]] and [[Toxicity Testing]]), validates therapeutic targets, and supports [[Regenerative Medicine]] by defining the defects that cell therapy must correct. It also feeds [[Rejuvenation]] research by offering aged, diseased cellular substrates for [[Partial Reprogramming]] studies. Limitations include incomplete maturation (fetal-like states), batch effects, and the simplification of whole-organism context.
