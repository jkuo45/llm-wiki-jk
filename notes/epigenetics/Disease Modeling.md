# Disease Modeling

[[Disease Modeling]] is a scientific approach that uses patient-derived cells, such as [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs), to recreate a disease in a laboratory environment. This allows for the detailed study of a disease's molecular and cellular mechanisms outside the human body.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

[[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] have revolutionized [[Disease Modeling]] by providing an accessible and renewable source of human cells that carry the exact genetic background of a patient. This is particularly valuable for studying [[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]], where primary human tissues (like brain neurons) are difficult to obtain. By differentiating iPSCs into specific lineages—such as motor neurons for [[notes/_link/Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]] or dopaminergic neurons for [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]]—researchers can observe the disease's hallmarks and progression "in a dish."

Modern [[Disease Modeling]] often integrates iPSC technology with [[CRISPR]] genome editing to create [[Isogenic Pair|isogenic pairs]]. By comparing cells that differ only by a single pathogenic variant, scientists can confirm the causal link between a genetic mutation and a disease phenotype, effectively filtering out the "noise" of individual genetic variation. Furthermore, three-dimensional systems like [[Organoids]] and [[Assembloids]] enable the modeling of complex tissue architecture and long-range interactions, such as neuronal migration.
