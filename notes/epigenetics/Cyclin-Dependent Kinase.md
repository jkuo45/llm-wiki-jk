# Cyclin-Dependent Kinase

**[[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]]**, or [[CDK]], refers to a family of serine/threonine kinases that serve as the catalytic engines of the [[Cell Cycle]]. They drive the cell through G1, S, G2, and M phases and are also engaged in transcriptional control and differentiation.

## Overview & Regulation

CDKs are central regulators of cell division but are inactive alone. Their activity is controlled by: (1) binding to regulatory [[Cyclin]] subunits; (2) phosphorylation/dephosphorylation by CAK and the Wee1/Cdc25 system; and (3) inhibition by CDK inhibitors (CKIs) such as [[CDKN1A|p21]], [[CDKN1B|p27]], and [[CDKN1C|p57]]. This multilayered control ensures division occurs only under permissive conditions.

## Mechanism/Function

CDK activation requires cyclin binding, which reorients the T-loop to open the active site. Active CDK-cyclin complexes phosphorylate substrates that execute cell-cycle transitions:

- **CDK4/6–Cyclin D** and **CDK2–Cyclin E** drive the G1/S transition and [[Restriction Point]].
- **CDK2–Cyclin A** is required for S-phase progression and origin firing.
- **CDK1–Cyclin B** is the master driver of entry into [[Mitosis]].

CDK activity also phosphorylates Rb, releasing E2F transcription factors to induce S-phase genes. CKIs bind directly to CDK-cyclin complexes, blocking catalysis in response to DNA damage or differentiation cues.

## Cross-talk with Epigenetics

Cell-cycle CDKs phosphorylate numerous epigenetic regulators — including components of the [[NuRD complex]], histone-modifying enzymes, and chromatin remodelers — coupling proliferation to chromatin state. CDK-dependent phosphorylation thus helps reset the [[Epigenome]] during division, linking cycle control to [[Epigenetic Remodeling]] and [[DNA Replication]].

## Clinical Relevance

Because uncontrolled CDK activity drives oncogenesis, CDKs are major cancer targets. CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib) are approved for hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer; CDK1/2 and CDK9 inhibitors are in development. CDK inhibition also induces [[Senescence]] and can synergize with epigenetic drugs.
