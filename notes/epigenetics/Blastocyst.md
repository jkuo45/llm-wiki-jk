# Blastocyst

The **Blastocyst** is a pre-implantation embryonic structure that forms approximately 5–6 days after fertilization in humans. At this stage the embryo consists of 100–200 cells organized into three distinct compartments: the **inner cell mass (ICM)**, which gives rise to the embryo proper; the **trophectoderm**, which forms the placenta; and the **blastocoel**, a fluid-filled cavity essential for subsequent expansion and hatching.

## Formation and Morphogenesis

The blastocyst arises from the **[[Morula]]** (a 16–32 cell compacted ball) through **cavitation**. Tight junctions form between outer cells to seal the embryo, while Na+/K+-ATPase pumps on the basolateral surface of trophectoderm cells transport ions into the interior, drawing water osmotically to create the blastocoel. The ICM segregates into **epiblast** (future fetus) and **primitive endoderm/hypoblast** (future yolk sac). Hatching from the zona pellucida around day 6–7 enables [[Implantation]] into the uterine endometrium.

## Embryonic Stem Cells and Epigenetics

The ICM is the canonical source of **[[Embryonic Stem Cells]] (ESCs)**, which are pluripotent — capable of generating all three germ layers. Human ESC derivation was first reported by Thomson et al. (1998); these cells express pluripotency factors [[OCT4]], [[NANOG]], and [[SOX2]] and are maintained with [[FGF]] and [[Activin]]/[[Nodal]] signaling. Their naïve pluripotent state is defined by a permissive [[Chromatin]] landscape — bivalent [[Histone Modification]] (simultaneous H3K4me3/H3K27me3) at lineage genes — and low global [[DNA Methylation]]. The development of [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells|iPSCs]] via [[Yamanaka Factors]] bypassed the ethical concerns of blastocyst destruction, enabled by epigenetic resetting of the somatic epigenome.

## Clinical and Diagnostic Relevance

- **In Vitro Fertilization (IVF)**: Blastocyst-stage transfer (day 5–6) yields higher implantation rates than cleavage-stage transfer.
- **Preimplantation Genetic Testing (PGT)**: Trophectoderm biopsy at this stage permits chromosomal (PGT-A) and monogenic (PGT-M) screening without compromising ICM viability.
- **Cryopreservation**: Blastocysts tolerate vitrification, enabling frozen embryo transfer.
- **Grading**: Assessed by expansion, ICM quality, and trophectoderm quality to predict implantation success.
- **[[Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer]]**: Requires remodeling of a donor nucleus to a blastocyst-like, totipotent epigenome.
