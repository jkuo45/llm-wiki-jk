# Blastocyst

The **Blastocyst** is a pre-implantation embryonic structure that forms approximately 5–6 days after fertilization in humans. At this stage the embryo consists of 100–200 cells organized into three distinct compartments: the **inner cell mass (ICM)**, which gives rise to the embryo proper; the **trophectoderm**, which forms the placenta; and the **blastocoel**, a fluid-filled cavity essential for subsequent expansion and hatching.

## Formation and Morphogenesis

The blastocyst arises from the **[[Morula]]** (a 16–32 cell compacted ball) through the process of **cavitation**. Tight junctions form between outer cells to seal the embryo, while Na+/K+-ATPase pumps on the basolateral surface of trophectoderm cells transport ions into the interior, drawing water osmotically to create the blastocoel. The ICM becomes segregated into **epiblast** (future fetus) and **primitive endoderm/hypoblast** (future yolk sac). Hatching from the zona pellucida occurs around day 6–7, enabling implantation into the uterine endometrium.

## Embryonic Stem Cells

The ICM is the canonical source of **[[Embryonic Stem Cells]] (ESCs)**, which are pluripotent — capable of giving rise to all three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm). Human ESC derivation was first reported by Thomson et al. (1998). These cells express markers such as [[OCT4]], [[NANOG]], and [[SOX2]] and are maintained in culture with [[FGF]] and [[Activin]]/[[Nodal]] signaling. ICM-derived ESCs have been instrumental in developmental biology, disease modeling, and drug screening. The development of [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells|iPSCs]] via [[Yamanaka Factors]] bypassed the ethical concerns associated with blastocyst destruction.

## Clinical and Diagnostic Relevance

- **In Vitro Fertilization (IVF)**: Blastocyst-stage transfer (day 5–6) is standard practice, yielding higher implantation rates than cleavage-stage transfer.
- **Preimplantation Genetic Testing (PGT)**: Trophectoderm biopsy at the blastocyst stage allows comprehensive chromosomal screening (PGT-A) and monogenic disease detection (PGT-M) without compromising ICM viability.
- **Cryopreservation**: Blastocysts tolerate vitrification well, enabling frozen embryo transfer cycles.
- **Morphological grading**: Blastocysts are graded by expansion status, ICM quality, and trophectoderm quality to predict implantation success.
