# CDH1

CDH1 (located at chromosome 16q22.1) encodes **E-cadherin** (epithelial cadherin), a 120 kDa calcium-dependent transmembrane glycoprotein that mediates homophilic cell-cell adhesion at [[Adherens Junction]]s. E-cadherin is the prototypical member of the classical cadherin superfamily and functions as a master suppressor of epithelial–mesenchymal transition ([[EMT]]), invasion, and metastasis. Its frequent epigenetic silencing by promoter [[Methylation]] in epithelial cancers and its germline mutations in hereditary cancer syndromes make it one of the most clinically important cell adhesion genes.

## Structure & Domains

E-cadherin comprises five extracellular cadherin (EC) repeats that coordinate Ca²⁺ ions for rigidification, a single-pass transmembrane domain, and a highly conserved cytoplasmic tail that binds β-catenin and p120-catenin. Through β-catenin, E-cadherin links to the actin cytoskeleton via α-catenin, and via p120-catenin it stabilizes the cadherin at the plasma membrane by preventing endocytosis. The extracellular EC1 domain mediates the trans-homophilic binding interface essential for cell–cell recognition and adhesion. Proteolytic cleavage of E-cadherin produces soluble fragments (sE-cad) that serve as biomarkers in cancer.

## Function in Epithelial Homeostasis

E-cadherin is the dominant cadherin expressed in epithelial tissues. Its adhesive function is essential for maintaining tissue architecture, polarity, and barrier integrity. In the skin, lungs, gastrointestinal tract, and mammary gland, E-cadherin organizes the [[Adherens Junction]] belt that encircles each cell, coordinating with tight junctions and desmosomes. Beyond adhesion, E-cadherin engages in outside-in signaling that suppresses proliferation via contact inhibition and sequesters β-catenin at the membrane, preventing its nuclear translocation and Wnt pathway activation.

## CDH1 in Epigenetic Regulation and EMT

Transcriptional repression of CDH1 is a hallmark of [[EMT]]. Key repressors — [[Snail1]], [[Snail2]] (Slug), [[Twist1]], and [[ZEB1]]/[[ZEB2]] — bind E-box elements in the CDH1 promoter and recruit corepressor complexes (CtBP, histone deacetylases) as well as [[DNA Methyltransferase]]s, leading to CpG island hypermethylation and repressive histone marks (H3K9me3, H3K27me3). Promoter [[Methylation]] of CDH1 occurs in >50% of breast, gastric, hepatocellular, and thyroid carcinomas and is an early event in diffuse-type gastric cancer. Reversal of CDH1 methylation by DNA methyltransferase inhibitors (e.g., [[5-Azacytidine]]) or histone deacetylase inhibitors (e.g., [[Trichostatin A]]) restores E-cadherin expression and reverses some mesenchymal traits. This epigenetic plasticity makes CDH1 a paradigm for targeted epigenetic therapy.

## Clinical Relevance

**Hereditary Diffuse Gastric Cancer (HDGC):** Germline loss-of-function mutations in CDH1 cause HDGC, an autosomal dominant cancer syndrome with ~70% penetrance for diffuse gastric cancer and ~40% for lobular breast cancer by age 80. Prophylactic total gastrectomy is recommended for carriers.

**Lobular Breast Cancer:** CDH1 inactivation — by mutation, loss of heterozygosity (LOH), or promoter methylation — is nearly pathognomonic for invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC). ILC cells lack E-cadherin and grow as single-file, non-cohesive strands.

**Prognostic & Predictive Value:** Loss of E-cadherin correlates with high grade, advanced stage, lymph node metastasis, and poor prognosis across multiple epithelial cancers. In ovarian and gastric cancers, detection of methylated CDH1 DNA in plasma or peritoneal fluid is under investigation as a liquid biopsy marker.
