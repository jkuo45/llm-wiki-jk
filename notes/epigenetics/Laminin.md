# Laminin

[[Laminin|Laminins]] are major heterotrimeric glycoproteins of the [[Basal Lamina]], one of the layers of the basement membrane. They form a cross-linked network that provides a protein foundation for most cells and organs, mediating [[Cell Adhesion]], polarization, migration, and survival through interactions with cellular [[Integrin|integrins]] and other receptors.

## Structure

Each laminin is an αβγ heterotrimer (~400–900 kDa) assembled into a characteristic cross- or T-shaped molecule. Five α, four β, and three γ chains combine into at least 16 laminin isoforms, each with distinct tissue distribution — e.g., laminin-111 (α1β1γ1) in early embryonic basement membranes and laminin-511 (α5β1γ1) in the epidermal basement membrane. Globular domains at the termini bind receptors and self-assemble into networks via nidogen and type IV collagen bridges.

## Mechanism of Action

Laminins engage cell-surface receptors including [[Integrin]] α6β1, α7β1, and dystroglycan. Integrin binding activates focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and ILK pathways that organize the actin cytoskeleton, establish apical–basal polarity, and transduce mechanical and chemical cues. Laminin-511/521 are especially potent in sustaining pluripotency of human embryonic stem cells and iPSCs cultured on defined matrices, and are standard components of [[Tissue Engineering]] scaffolds.

## Physiological Function

Laminins are essential for basement membrane assembly in nearly every tissue — skin, muscle, kidney glomeruli, lung, and nerve. They guide epithelial morphogenesis, endothelial stability, and Schwann-cell myelination. During development, laminin signaling directs epithelial budding and tube formation.

## Pathological Relevance

Mutations in laminin chains cause blistering disorders (junctional epidermolysis bullosa, linked to LAMA3/LAMB3/LAMC2), muscular dystrophy (LAMA2-related congenital muscular dystrophy), and nephrotic syndrome (laminin β2 in glomerular filtration). Altered laminin composition also accompanies fibrosis, tumor invasion, and aging-related basement-membrane stiffening. As part of the [[Extracellular Matrix]], laminins thus bridge structural integrity and cellular behavior.
