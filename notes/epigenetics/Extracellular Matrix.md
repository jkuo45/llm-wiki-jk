# Extracellular Matrix

The [[Extracellular Matrix]] ([[ECM]]) is a three-dimensional network of extracellular macromolecules—principally fibrous proteins, proteoglycans, and glycoproteins—that provides structural scaffolding and biochemical signaling to surrounding cells. Far from being an inert framework, the ECM is a dynamic, cell-instructing niche that regulates adhesion, polarity, migration, proliferation, and [[Quiescence]]. Its composition and stiffness are continually remodeled by the cells it surrounds, most notably [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]].

## Composition and Structure

The ECM is built from several classes of molecules. **[[Collagen]]** (especially fibrillar types I, II, and III) provides tensile strength; elastic fibers (elastin, fibrillins) confer recoil; and the [[Basal Lamina]] a specialized sheet of laminin, type IV collagen, nidogen, and perlecan underlying epithelia and endothelia. Proteoglycans such as versican and decorin bind water and growth factors, while glycoproteins including fibronectin and tenascin organize assembly and cell contact. Cells attach to the ECM through **[[Integrin|integrins]]** and other adhesion receptors that link the external network to the internal [[Cytoskeleton]] via [[Focal Adhesion|focal adhesions]].

## Mechanical and Biochemical Signaling

The ECM transmits both chemical and physical cues. Ligand binding by integrins triggers intracellular signaling (e.g., [[FAK Signaling]]) that modulates survival, motility, and gene expression. Matrix stiffness—sensed through actomyosin tension and mechanotransduction—feeds back on cell fate: soft matrices favor quiescence and differentiation, whereas stiffened matrices promote proliferation and can drive [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]]. Growth factors (TGF-β, FGF, VEGF) are sequestered in the matrix and released by proteases during remodeling, shaping temporal signaling.

## Physiological Function

The ECM maintains tissue architecture, distributes mechanical load, and forms selective barriers (e.g., [[Basal Lamina]] in kidney glomeruli and vessels). It establishes stem-cell niches that enforce [[Quiescence]] and guides morphogenesis during development. In bone, dentin, and cartilage the mineralized ECM is the principal load-bearing material.

## Pathological Relevance

Excessive deposition of ECM underlies [[Fibrosis]] in liver, lung, kidney, and heart, where stiff scar tissue replaces functional parenchyma and perpetuates a pro-fibrotic signaling loop. ECM degradation by matrix metalloproteinases facilitates tumor invasion and metastasis, while altered matrix composition influences immune infiltration. Therapeutics targeting ECM crosslinking (e.g., lysine oxidase inhibitors) or integrin signaling are under investigation.
