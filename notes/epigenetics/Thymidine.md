# Thymidine

**Thymidine** (5-methyl-2'-deoxyuridine) is a naturally occurring pyrimidine nucleoside that is a fundamental building block of [[DNA]]. It is incorporated into DNA during the [[S phase]] of the [[Cell cycle]] in place of the base thymine. While essential for genomic integrity, imbalances in thymidine levels are a significant driver of cellular dysfunction.

## Mechanism of Action

Thymidine's biological role is centered on its role in DNA synthesis, but its impact becomes pathological when its concentration is dysregulated.

1. **DNA Synthesis**: As a deoxyribonucleoside, thymidine is phosphorylated by [[Thymidine kinase]] into thymidine triphosphate (dTTP), one of the four essential [[dNTP]]s required by [[DNA Polymerase]] for the synthesis of new DNA strands.

2. **Induction of DNA Replication Stress**: An excess of thymidine (relative to other dNTPs) can lead to a profound imbalance in the intracellular nucleotide pools. This imbalance is a major cause of [[DNA Replication Stress]]. Specifically, an overabundance of dTTP can:
    - Increase the misincorporation of nucleotides into the nascent DNA strand.
    - Interfere with the efficient incorporation of other, essential nucleoside analogs (e.g., gemcitabine or 5-azacytidine), potentially conferring resistance to chemotherapy.
    - Disrupt the fidelity of DNA polymerases, leading to increased mutation rates.

3. **Cellular Senescence**: The resulting replication stress and DNA damage (such as single-strand and double-strand breaks) trigger the [[DNA Damage Response]] (DDR). Chronic or severe replication stress caused by thymidine imbalance is a well-documented inducer of [[Stress-Induced Premature Senescence]]. This process involves the activation of the p53/p21 and p16/Rb pathways, leading to a permanent arrest of the cell cycle.

## Research Applications

Thymidine is a widely used tool in molecular and cellular biology:

- **Nucleoside Labeling and Analogs**: Thymidine is the chemical basis for several important analogs used in research, most notably [[Bromodeoxyuridine]] (BrdU), which is used to label proliferating cells.
- **Inducing Senescence**: In experimental models, exogenous administration of high concentrations of thymidine is a standard method for inducing [[DNA Replication Stress]] and studying the mechanisms of cellular senescence.
- **Metabolic Studies**: It is used to study the salvage pathway of nucleotide synthesis, where cells recycle pre-formed nucleosides rather than synthesizing them *de novo*.
