# Retinoblastoma Protein

The [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] (pRb, or [[RB1|Rb]]) is a tumor-suppressor nuclear phosphoprotein encoded by the *RB1* gene. It is a founding member of the pocket-protein family (pRb, p107, p130) and a central brake on cell-cycle progression. pRb is functionally inactivated in the majority of human cancers, either by *RB1* mutation/deletion, by upstream [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]] (CDK) deregulation, or by oncoviral proteins (HPV E7, SV40 large T) that bind and neutralize it.

## Structure

pRb contains a conserved "pocket" domain (formed by A and B subdomains bridged by a flexible hinge) that binds E2F transcription factors and chromatin-modifying co-repressors, plus an N-terminal domain with inhibitory and regulatory motifs. The pocket is the docking site for both E2F and the [[Histone Deacetylase]] (HDAC)–[[NuRD complex]] repressor machinery.

## Mechanism of Action

In early [[G1 Phase]], hypophosphorylated pRb binds [[E2F]] transcription factors, sterically blocking their transactivation domains and recruiting HDAC/NuRD to condense chromatin at E2F target promoters. This represses genes required for S-phase entry (e.g., *Cyclin E*, *MYC*, *DHFR*, DNA synthesis enzymes). Progression through G1 is driven by cyclin D–CDK4/6 and cyclin E–CDK2, which hyperphosphorylate pRb on multiple serines/threonines, causing it to release E2F. Freed E2F then activates the [[Cell Cycle]] transcriptional program. pRb thus acts as the gatekeeper deciding between proliferation, [[Quiescence]], [[Differentiation]], and [[Senescence]]. It also collaborates with the [[NuRD complex]] and other chromatin remodelers, coupling cell-cycle control to [[Epigenetic Remodeling]].

## Physiological Function

pRb enforces the post-mitotic state of differentiated tissues, restricts stem-cell self-renewal, and cooperates with developmental cues to lock cells out of cycle. Its activity is modulated by [[Quiescence]] entry/exit and by differentiation signals that keep pRb hypophosphorylated.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Germline *RB1* mutations cause hereditary retinoblastoma (the cancer that named the protein), and somatic *RB1* loss characterizes small-cell lung, bladder, and osteosarcoma. Loss of pRb function liberates E2F constitutively, driving uncontrolled proliferation and genomic instability — a hallmark of [[Cancer]]. Therapeutically, CDK4/6 inhibitors ([[Palbociclib]], [[Abemaciclib]], ribociclib) restore pRb-mediated E2F repression and are approved in hormonereceptor-positive breast cancer; their efficacy depends on an intact *RB1* pathway, making pRb status a predictive biomarker. pRb's role in maintaining [[Quiescence]] also intersects with [[Aging]] and regenerative control of [[Muscle Stem Cell|satellite cells]] and other progenitors.
