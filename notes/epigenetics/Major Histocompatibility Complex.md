# Major Histocompatibility Complex

The [[Major Histocompatibility Complex]] (MHC) is a large genomic locus encoding cell-surface glycoproteins essential for the adaptive immune system to recognize peptide fragments in vertebrates, thereby determining histocompatibility. In humans the MHC is termed HLA (human leukocyte antigen); in mice it is H-2. MHC molecules present processed antigen to the [[T Cell Receptor]] and are the principal determinants of transplant compatibility and [[Immune Rejection]].

## Classes & Structure

MHC is divided into three classes:

- **MHC class I** (HLA-A, -B, -C) — expressed on nearly all nucleated cells; presents endogenous (intracellular) peptides to CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells via a peptide-binding groove formed by α1/α2 domains.
- **MHC class II** (HLA-DP, -DQ, -DR) — expressed on professional antigen-presenting cells; presents exogenous peptides to CD4⁺ helper T cells.
- **MHC class III** — encodes complement components and cytokines (e.g., TNF, C4) rather than peptide-presenting structures.

The peptide-binding groove accommodates 8–13 residue ligands with anchor residues, enabling each allele to present a broad but biased peptide repertoire.

## Mechanism of Antigen Presentation

Intracellular proteins are degraded by the proteasome, transported by TAP into the endoplasmic reticulum, loaded onto class I, and displayed at the surface. Exogenous antigens are processed in endolysosomes and loaded onto class II via the invariant-chain pathway. T-cell recognition of peptide–MHC complexes, together with co-stimulation, initiates adaptive responses; failure of self–MHC restriction underlies thymic negative selection and tolerance.

## Relevance to Stem-Cell & Regenerative Medicine

In iPSC-derived therapies, MHC mismatch drives [[Immune Rejection]]. Patient-specific [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] ideally avoid this, but off-target mutations or residual antigens can still provoke immunity. [[HLA Haplobank]] strategies bank homozygous HLA-matched iPSC lines to cover large populations. MHC matching is also central to disease-association studies (e.g., HLA-B27 with ankylosing spondylitis) and to designing hypoimmune engineered cell lines.
