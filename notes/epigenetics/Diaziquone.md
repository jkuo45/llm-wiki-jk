# Diaziquone

Diaziquone (AZQ) is a synthetic aziridinylbenzoquinone alkylating agent developed as a rationally designed anticancer drug capable of crossing the blood-brain barrier (BBB). The compound incorporates a quinone core flanked by two aziridine rings, which serve as the DNA-reactive alkylating moieties. The benzoquinone structure confers lipophilicity while the aziridine substituents provide balanced aqueous solubility, yielding favorable CNS pharmacokinetics.

## Mechanism of Action

Diaziquone functions as a bifunctional alkylating agent. Under physiological conditions, the aziridine rings undergo protonation and nucleophilic attack by DNA bases, primarily at the N7 position of guanine residues. The two reactive aziridine groups enable the formation of both intrastrand and interstrand DNA cross-links, which block DNA replication and transcription, ultimately triggering cell cycle arrest and apoptosis. The quinone moiety can also undergo bioreductive activation via cellular reductases such as NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1), generating semiquinone radicals that produce reactive oxygen species and further contribute to DNA damage.

## CNS Penetration and Brain Tumor Studies

Diaziquone was specifically designed to achieve therapeutic concentrations in the central nervous system following systemic administration. Its balanced lipophilicity (logP ≈ 0.5) enables efficient passive diffusion across the BBB, and preclinical biodistribution studies confirmed significant brain tissue uptake relative to other alkylating agents. In phase I and II clinical trials in the 1980s, diaziquone was evaluated in recurrent high-grade gliomas (glioblastoma multiforme, anaplastic astrocytoma), medulloblastoma, and leptomeningeal carcinomatosis. While modest objective response rates were observed (10–15% in glioblastoma), the agent demonstrated CNS penetration and clinical activity in a subset of patients. Pharmacodynamic studies confirmed measurable DNA cross-link formation in tumor tissue following AZQ administration.

## Side Effects

Myelosuppression, particularly thrombocytopenia and leukopenia, was the dose-limiting toxicity of diaziquone in clinical trials. Non-hematologic toxicities included mild nausea, hepatotoxicity at high doses, and rare cases of allergic reactions. Unlike many other alkylating agents, diaziquone showed minimal nephrotoxicity and no evidence of hemorrhagic cystitis.

## Senescence Research

Diaziquone is used experimentally to induce cellular senescence in cell culture. Treatment with AZQ in the low micromolar range triggers persistent DNA damage signaling, activation of the p53/p21^WAF1/CIP1 checkpoint axis, and the characteristic features of senescence including SA-β-gal positivity, cell cycle arrest, and SASP acquisition. The agent provides a useful tool for studying alkylator-induced senescence in the context of CNS tumor biology.
