# Histone Methyltransferase

**[[Histone Methyltransferase]] (HMT)** is a class of enzymes that add methyl groups to lysine or arginine residues on histone proteins. Unlike acetylation, which almost always relaxes chromatin, histone methylation can either activate or repress gene expression depending on the residue and the number of methyl groups (mono-, di-, or tri-methylation) added.

## Major Families

- **SET-domain HMTs**: The largest family, including SUV39H1/2 (H3K9), EZH2 of Polycomb PRC2 (H3K27), SET7/9 (H3K4), and ASH1L. They use S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as methyl donor.
- **DOT1-like (DOT1L)**: The sole non-SET methyltransferase, depositing H3K79 methylation.
- **PRMTs (protein arginine methyltransferases)**: Methylate arginine residues (e.g., H4R3me2), often associated with active transcription.

## Key Regulatory Marks

- **Repressive marks**: Trimethylation of H3K9 ([[H3K9me3]]) and H3K27 ([[H3K27me3]]) are hallmarks of [[Heterochromatin]] and silencing. H3K9me3 is deposited by SUV39H1/2 and SETDB1; H3K27me3 by the [[Polycomb Group Proteins]] PRC2 complex (EZH2 catalytic subunit).
- **Activating marks**: Trimethylation of H3K4 (H3K4me3) at active promoters and H3K36me3 over gene bodies are deposited by SET1/COMPASS-family and SET2 enzymes, respectively.

## Mechanism and Spreading

HMTs are often recruited by sequence-specific transcription factors or by preexisting marks via "reader" domains, enabling propagation of repressive domains. H3K9me3 is recognized by HP1, which recruits SUV39H1 to methylate neighboring nucleosomes, spreading heterochromatin.

## Role in Aging

Histone methylation patterns shift markedly during [[Aging]]:

- **Loss of silencing**: Declining H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 is observed in normal aging and progeroid syndromes such as [[HGPS]], correlating with [[Genomic Instability]] and de-repression of repeats.
- **Longevity regulation**: In [[Model Organisms]] such as *[[Caenorhabditis elegans]]*, modulation of HMTs (e.g., ASH-2, SET-2) extends lifespan, indicating conserved metabolic–epigenetic control.

## Epigenetic Inheritance

Histone methylation contributes to [[Epigenetic Inheritance]], helping cells "remember" expression states through divisions and, in some organisms, across generations, via reader-writer complexes that copy marks onto daughter nucleosomes.
