# Hippo Signaling Pathway

The [[Hippo Signaling Pathway]], also known as the Salvador–Warts–Hippo (SWH) pathway, is a highly conserved kinase cascade that controls [[Organ Size]] in animals through the regulation of cell proliferation, [[Apoptosis]], and [[Contact Inhibition]]. At its core, the pathway restrains the activity of the transcriptional coactivators [[YAP]] (Yes-associated protein) and TAZ (WWTR1), which drive growth-promoting gene expression when active. Inactivation of Hippo signaling thus unleashes YAP/TAZ and promotes tissue overgrowth.

## Core Cascade

The mammalian core comprises the upstream serine/threonine kinases MST1/2 (orthologs of Hippo) and SAV1, which activate the downstream kinases LATS1/2 together with MOB1. Active LATS1/2 phosphorylate YAP and TAZ at multiple serine residues, creating 14-3-3 binding sites that exclude them from the nucleus and promote their [[Ubiquitin Ligase]]-mediated degradation. When the pathway is OFF (e.g., in response to cell crowding, lack of apical-basal polarity, or soluble cues), unphosphorylated YAP/TAZ accumulate in the nucleus, bind TEAD-family transcription factors, and induce genes regulating proliferation, survival, and stemness.

## Regulatory Inputs

Hippo signaling integrates diverse cues: mechanical tension and cell density (via adherens junctions and the F-actin cytoskeleton), G-protein-coupled receptor signaling, and upstream tumor suppressors such as [[NF2]] (Merlin) and [[LATS1/2]]. The pathway cross-talks extensively with Wnt, [[Hippo Signaling Pathway]]-related [[YAP]] outputs, and with mechanotransduction at the cell cortex. Importantly, YAP/TAZ activity is also regulated by mechanical cues independent of LATS, underscoring the pathway's role as a sensor of tissue architecture.

## Physiological Function

By balancing YAP/TAZ activity against growth restraints, the Hippo pathway ensures organs stop growing once they reach correct size — a phenomenon classically demonstrated by the regeneration-limit of Drosophila imaginal discs and mammalian liver. It governs progenitor pool size in many tissues and supports tissue repair and regenerative outgrowth.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Dysregulation of Hippo signaling is intimately linked to [[Cancer]]. Loss of NF2 (neurofibromatosis type 2), SAV1, or LATS1/2, or amplification/activation of YAP/TAZ, drives unrestrained proliferation, evasion of [[Contact Inhibition]], and metastatic progression across mesotheliomas, glioblastomas, breast, liver, and colorectal cancers. Conversely, pharmacological YAP/TAZ inhibition is pursued as an anticancer strategy. The pathway's mechanosensitive nature also makes it relevant to fibrosis and to stem-cell niche control, with implications for regenerative medicine and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]].
