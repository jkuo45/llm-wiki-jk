# Histone Acetyltransferase

**[[Histone Acetyltransferase]] (HAT)** is a class of enzymes that transfer an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to specific lysine residues on histone tails. This modification is a key component of the epigenetic regulation of gene expression.

## Classification

HATs are grouped into families based on sequence and function:

- **GNAT family** (Gcn5, PCAF, [[HAT|p300/CBP-associated factor]]): acetylate nucleosomal histones and many transcription factors.
- **MYST family** (MOF, Tip60, HBO1): linked to dosage compensation, DNA repair, and replication licensing.
- **p300/CBP**: large, versatile co-activators that acetylate histones and a broad set of non-histone substrates, integrating signaling with transcription.
- **Nuclear receptor co-activators** (SRC-1, ACTR): recruited by ligand-bound nuclear receptors.

## Mechanism of Action & Impact on Chromatin

Acetylation neutralizes the positive charge of the histone tail, reducing its affinity for the negatively charged DNA. This causes [[Nucleosome|nucleosomes]] to loosen, transitioning chromatin from a condensed state to a more "open," transcriptionally active state known as [[Euchromatin]]. HATs thus function as transcriptional co-activators.

## Regulation of Transcription

By opening DNA, HATs allow transcription factors and RNA polymerase II to access promoter and enhancer regions. They act in dynamic opposition to [[Histone Deacetylase|Histone Deacetylases]] (HDACs). Signal-dependent recruitment of HATs (e.g., by p300/CBP downstream of growth-factor or stress pathways) couples extracellular cues to gene activation.

## Role in Aging and Disease

The HAT/HDAC balance is often disrupted in [[Aging]]:

- **Neurodegeneration**: Reduced histone acetylation is observed in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and related [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]], where HAT activators and HDAC inhibitors are explored as therapies.
- **Longevity Pathways**: Metabolic regulators such as [[AMPK]] and [[Sirtuins|Sirtuins]] intersect with HAT-controlled pathways, linking diet and environment to the [[Epigenome]].
- **Cancer**: Aberrant HAT activity (e.g., translocations involving p300/CBP in lymphoma) contributes to oncogenesis.
