# Focal Adhesion

[[Focal Adhesion|Focal adhesions]] are large, dynamic macromolecular assemblies through which mechanical force and regulatory signals are transmitted between the [[Extracellular Matrix]] ([[ECM]]) and the interacting cell's [[Cytoskeleton]]. They are the principal sites of cell–matrix adhesion and mechanotransduction, linking external ligands to internal contractility and signaling.

## Structure and Composition

Focal adhesions are built around transmembrane **[[Integrin|integrins]]**, which bind ECM ligands (fibronectin, collagen, laminin) on the outside and recruit a cytoplasmic plaque on the inside. The plaque includes talin, vinculin, paxillin, α-actinin, and tensin, which connect integrin cytoplasmic tails to actin stress fibers. Focal adhesion kinase (**FAK**, see [[FAK Signaling]]) and ILK nucleate downstream signaling. Nascent adhesions mature into focal adhesions and, under high tension, into giant "focal complexes"/stress-fiber adhesions, with lifetime and size tuned to force.

## Mechanosensing and Dynamics

Adhesions act as force-clamp devices: actomyosin-generated tension stretches talin, exposing cryptic binding sites and reinforcing the complex—a positive feedback that converts mechanical load into structural stabilization. Adhesion assembly at the leading edge and disassembly at the trailing edge drive **[[Cell Migration]]**. Force magnitude and matrix stiffness are read out through adhesion-associated kinases, feeding into YAP/TAZ and the [[Hippo Signaling Pathway]].

## Physiological Function

Focal adhesions anchor cells, resist shear, and translate ECM topography and stiffness into fate decisions such as proliferation, [[Quiescence]], and differentiation. They are essential in development, [[Tissue Repair]], and immune-cell trafficking.

## Pathological Relevance

Aberrant adhesion signaling supports [[Cancer]] invasion and metastasis, angiogenesis, and [[Fibrosis]]. Overexpression or activation of adhesion components (FAK, integrins) promotes anoikis resistance and [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]]. Integrin antagonists and FAK inhibitors are explored clinically to disrupt tumor–matrix interactions.
