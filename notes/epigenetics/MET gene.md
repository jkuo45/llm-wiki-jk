# MET gene

The [[MET gene]] (proto-oncogene MET) provides instructions for making the [[Receptor Tyrosine Kinase]] [[c-Met]] (hepatocyte growth factor receptor), the sole known receptor for [[Hepatocyte Growth Factor]] (HGF, also called scatter factor). MET is a classic proto-oncogene whose aberrant activation drives proliferation, survival, motility, and invasive "scattering" of epithelial cells, and is frequently implicated in [[Cancer]] initiation, progression, and resistance to therapy.

## Structure & Protein Architecture

The MET locus on chromosome 7q31 encodes a single-pass transmembrane receptor synthesized as a precursor and cleaved into a disulfide-linked α/β heterodimer. The extracellular α subunit contains the Sema (semaphorin), PSI, and four IPT domains that bind HGF. The transmembrane β subunit carries an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain plus a multifunctional docking site (the "juxtamembrane" tyrosines Y1234/Y1235 and the Y1349/Y1356 multi-substrate site) that recruits downstream adaptors. Activation requires HGF binding and receptor dimerization, leading to trans-autophosphorylation.

## Mechanism of Action & Downstream Pathways

HGF binding induces MET dimerization and activation of its kinase, phosphorylating the multi-substrate docking site. This recruits GRB2/SOS (activating [[RAS]]–[[MAPK]]), PI3K–AKT (survival and growth), SHC, STAT3, and SRC family kinases, collectively producing the "invasive growth" program: branching morphogenesis, migration, and [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]]-like scattering. MET cross-talks with Wnt, Notch, and integrin signaling and cooperates with [[TP53]] loss and [[RB1]] pathway alterations in tumor evolution.

## Physiological Function

In development, the HGF–MET axis orchestrates placental, muscle, and liver organogenesis, and guides migration of myogenic and endothelial progenitors. In adults, MET supports tissue regeneration and wound healing, stimulating [[Progenitor Cell]] and stem-cell behaviors at sites of injury.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

MET is activated in cancer by gene amplification (notably gastric and esophageal carcinomas), activating mutations (papillary renal cell carcinoma, childhood hepatoblastoma), overexpression, and autocrine/paracrine HGF loops. MET amplification also mediates acquired resistance to EGFR and ALK inhibitors in lung adenocarcinoma. Germline MET mutations cause hereditary papillary renal carcinoma (type 1). Therapeutically, MET is targeted by small-molecule [[Tyrosine Kinase]] inhibitors (crizotinib, capmatinib, tepotinib, savolitinib) and by anti-MET antibodies (onartuzumab), with approvals in METex14-mutant and MET-amplified NSCLC. MET-driven tumors also show heightened [[Tumorigenicity]] in xenograft models, and MET status is used as a biomarker for patient selection.
