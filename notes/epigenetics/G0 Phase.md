# G0 Phase

The [[G0 Phase]] is a resting, non-proliferative state outside the active [[Cell Cycle]]. It is entered from [[G1 Phase]] after cells exit cycling and is the characteristic state of cells in [[Quiescence]]. G0 is not a single uniform condition but a spectrum ranging from rapidly re-enterable arrest to deep, long-term dormancy.

## Entry and Maintenance

Exit into G0 is governed at the **[[Restriction Point]]** (late G1), where the [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] (pRb), product of [[RB1]], binds and inhibits E2F transcription factors, locking cells out of S-phase entry. Sustained G0 requires cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors (p21^Cip1^, p27^Kip1^, p16^INK4a^) and low CDK–cyclin activity. Anti-proliferative signals (TGF-β, contact inhibition, lack of mitogens) and intact tumor-suppressor control maintain the arrest. Cells in G0 downregulate replicative machinery while preserving metabolic and tissue-maintenance functions.

## Reversibility and Exits

Quiescent G0 is reversible: upon mitogenic stimulation, cells re-express cyclins D/E, phosphorylate pRb, release E2F, and re-enter G1→S. Some cells (e.g., differentiated neurons, skeletal muscle) remain in an essentially permanent G0, whereas others (stem cells, hepatocytes) retain robust re-entry capacity—the basis of [[Liver Regeneration]] and tissue repair. G0 must be distinguished from [[Senescence]], an irreversible arrest with SASP, and from terminal differentiation.

## Physiological Function

G0 underlies tissue homeostasis, allowing cell numbers to match demand without exhaustion. Stem-cell pools (e.g., [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell]]) spend most of their lives in G0 to preserve self-renewal and avoid mutation accumulation. Quiescent G0 also protects genomes during stress and contributes to longevity by limiting replication-linked damage.

## Pathological Relevance

Premature or aberrant G0 exit can drive hyperproliferation and [[Cancer]], whereas failure to arrest (loss of [[RB1]]/pRb) removes the G0 gate. Conversely, therapy-induced quiescence is exploited to manage tumors, and senescence-like permanent arrest is a goal of some treatments. Aging alters G0 dynamics, contributing to stem-cell exhaustion.
