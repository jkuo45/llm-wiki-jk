# Epigenetic Modifiers

**Epigenetic Modifiers** (also termed epidrugs) are a class of compounds that alter the [[Epigenome|epigenetic state]] of a cell by inhibiting or activating the enzymes responsible for writing, reading, and erasing epigenetic marks. They are broadly categorized by their target: [[DNA Methylation]] modifiers, [[Histone Modification]] modifiers, and [[Chromatin Remodeling]] agents.

## Major Classes

### DNA Methyltransferase Inhibitors (DNMTi)

These nucleoside and non-nucleoside inhibitors reverse [[DNA Methylation|DNA hypermethylation]]:

- **[[5-Azacytidine]]** (Vidaza): Cytidine analog incorporated into RNA and DNA, trapping [[DNMT1]] in covalent complexes and causing proteasomal degradation. Approved for [[Myelodysplastic Syndrome|MDS]] and [[AML]].
- **[[Decitabine]]** (5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine, Dacogen): Deoxyribose analog selectively incorporated into DNA, with similar DNMT1 trapping mechanism.
- **[[Guadecitabine]]** (SGI-110): Second-generation DNMTi with improved pharmacokinetic stability.

### Histone Deacetylase Inhibitors (HDACi)

HDAC inhibitors increase [[Histone Acetylation|histone acetylation]], generally promoting open [[Euchromatin|chromatin]]:

- **[[Sodium Butyrate]]**: Short-chain fatty acid endogenous HDACi, primarily inhibits class I and IIa HDACs.
- **[[Suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid|SAHA (Vorinostat)]]**: Pan-HDACi approved for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma.
- **[[Romidepsin]]**: Selective class I HDACi (prodrug, activated by glutathione reduction).
- **[[Sodium Valproate]]**: Branched fatty acid HDACi, also used as a mood stabilizer.

### Histone Methyltransferase Inhibitors (HMTi)

- **[[Tazemetostat]]** (EZH2 inhibitor): Approved for epithelioid sarcoma and follicular lymphoma.
- **[[DOT1L inhibitors]]** (e.g., [[Pinometostat]]): Target [[H3K79]] methylation in MLL-rearranged leukemias.

### BET Bromodomain Inhibitors

- **[[JQ1]]**: Prototype [[BRD4]] inhibitor, displaces BET proteins from acetylated histones, suppresses [[MYC]] transcription.
- **[[INCB054329]]**: Clinical-stage pan-BET inhibitor.

## Mechanisms of Action

Epidrugs operate through a shared paradigm: by blocking the catalytic activity of epigenetic enzymes, they shift the landscape of chromatin modifications genome-wide. However, selectivity is highly contextual. For example, [[HDAC inhibitors]] upregulate both tumor suppressors (e.g., p21 via H3K9ac at the CDKN1A locus) and pro-metastatic genes depending on the cell type. The net effect depends on the existing [[Epigenome|chromatin landscape]] and transcription factor repertoire.

## Experimental and Therapeutic Use

- **In vitro tools:** [[5-Azacytidine]] and [[Decitabine]] are routinely used at 0.5–5 µM for 72 hours to deconvolute the role of DNA methylation in gene regulation. [[Trichostatin A]] (TSA) at 100–300 nM is a potent HDACi for short-term perturbation studies.
- **Combination therapy:** Epidrugs synergize with [[Immunotherapy|immunotherapies]] by upregulating [[MHC class I]] and [[PD-L1]], and with [[Senolytic Drugs|senolytics]] by modulating the [[SASP]].
- **Limitations:** Genome-wide effects cause dose-limiting toxicities, and off-target effects are common. Resistance emerges through drug efflux (e.g., [[ABCB1]] upregulation) and compensatory pathway activation.

## Open Questions

- Can epidrugs be targeted to specific genomic loci (e.g., via [[CRISPR]]-dCas9 epigenetic editors) to avoid global toxicity?
- What is the optimal dosing schedule to achieve epigenetic reprogramming without selecting for resistance?
