# DNAmGrimAge

**[[DNAmGrimAge]]** is a second-generation [[Epigenetic Clock]] developed by Steve Horvath and colleagues. Unlike first-generation clocks that were trained primarily to predict chronological age, GrimAge was designed to predict lifespan and "healthspan" by incorporating clinical [[Biomarkers]] and lifestyle factors.

### 1. Methodology
GrimAge uses [[DNA Methylation]] levels at specific CpG sites to estimate the levels of seven plasma proteins (including GDF15, cystatin C, and TIMP-1) and smoking history (pack-years). These estimates are then combined into a single composite score that reflects the individual's biological age.

### 2. Clinical Significance in [[notes/_link/Aging]]
GrimAge has proven to be highly accurate in predicting:
*   **Mortality:** It is a stronger predictor of time-to-death than chronological age or many other epigenetic clocks.
*   **Age-Related Diseases:** High GrimAge scores are associated with an increased risk of [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]], [[notes/_link/Cancer]], and physical disability.
*   **Lifestyle Impact:** It can reflect the biological toll of factors like smoking, obesity, and poor diet.

### 3. Comparison to Other Clocks
While earlier clocks like the Horvath clock or Hannum clock are excellent at estimating chronological age, [[DNAmGrimAge]] is considered superior for clinical and forensic applications where the goal is to assess an individual's actual physiological decline and mortality risk.
