# HGPS

**[[HGPS]]** (Hutchinson-Gilford Progeria Syndrome) is an extremely rare, fatal genetic condition characterized by an appearance of dramatic, rapid [[notes/_link/Aging]] beginning in childhood. It serves as a critical human model for understanding the biological processes of normal aging.

### 1. Molecular Cause
HGPS is caused by a sporadic mutation in the LMNA gene. This mutation leads to the production of an abnormal version of the protein [[Lamin A]], called "progerin." Progerin remains permanently attached to the nuclear membrane, causing it to become misshapen and disorganized.

### 2. Epigenetic and Genomic Impact
The presence of progerin causes profound changes in the cell's [[Epigenome]]:
*   **[[Genomic Instability]]**: The disorganized nucleus leads to significant DNA damage and structural abnormalities in the genome.
*   **Loss of [[Heterochromatin]]**: Cells from HGPS patients show a massive loss of repressive heterochromatin, particularly the H3K9me3 mark, leading to aberrant gene expression.
*   **[[Cellular Senescence]]**: Progeria cells enter senescence prematurely and secrete pro-inflammatory factors (the [[SASP]]).

### 3. Clinical Presentation
Children with HGPS typically appear healthy at birth but begin to show signs of accelerated aging (hair loss, thin skin, joint stiffness) within the first year of life. Most die from heart disease or stroke, which are also the leading causes of death in the elderly population.

### Connections
- **[[notes/_link/Aging]]**: HGPS is the primary model for "segmented progeria" (accelerated aging).
- **[[Lamin A]]**: The protein whose mutation drives the disease.
- **[[Genomic Instability]]**: A core feature of the HGPS cellular phenotype.
- **[[Werner Syndrome]]**: Another prominent progeroid syndrome.
