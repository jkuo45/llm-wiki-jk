# Fibroblasts

[[Fibroblasts]] are a versatile type of cell that synthesizes the [[ECM|extracellular matrix]] and collagen, forming the structural framework (stroma) for animal tissues. They are the most common cells of connective tissue and play a critical role in wound healing.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

In the history of [[Stem Cell Research]], [[Fibroblasts]] are most notable for being the first and most widely used somatic cell type for [[Cellular Reprogramming]]. Shinya Yamanaka's landmark 2006 study used mouse embryonic [[Fibroblasts]] (MEFs) to generate the first [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs). Shortly thereafter, human [[Fibroblasts]] (such as [[HFF1]] - Human Foreskin [[Fibroblasts]]) were successfully reprogrammed using the same [[Yamanaka Factors]] (OSKM).

Because [[Fibroblasts]] are mesenchymal cells, they must undergo [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]] (MET) during the reprogramming process to reach a pluripotent state. This involves significant morphological and transcriptional changes. While [[Fibroblasts]] remain the standard "starting material" due to their ease of culture and accessibility (e.g., via skin biopsy), researchers have found that other cell types, like keratinocytes or blood progenitors, can sometimes be reprogrammed more efficiently because they are epigenetically "closer" to the pluripotent state.

## Connections

- [[Cellular Reprogramming]]: [[Fibroblasts]] are the primary substrate for most reprogramming experiments and protocols.
- [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]]: The essential biological transition that [[Fibroblasts]] must undergo to become iPSCs.
- [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]: The pluripotent state reached when [[Fibroblasts]] are successfully reprogrammed.
- [[Yamanaka Factors]]: The specific combination of proteins used to override the fibroblast identity.
- [[Somatic Cells]]: [[Fibroblasts]] are the quintessential example of a somatic cell used in stem cell biology.
