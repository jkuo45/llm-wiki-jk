# Fibroblasts

[[Fibroblasts]] are the principal resident cells of connective tissue. They synthesize and maintain the [[Extracellular Matrix]] (ECM) and [[Collagen]] fibers that form the structural stroma supporting epithelia, blood vessels, and organs. They are mesenchymally derived, spindle-shaped, and motile, and they play a central role in tissue homeostasis, wound repair, and fibrosis.

## Structure and Identity

Fibroblasts are characterized by an elongated, fibroblast-like morphology, a prominent rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi (reflecting active protein secretion), and expression of markers such as vimentin, fibroblast-specific protein 1 (FSP1/S100A4), platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha/beta (PDGFRα/β), and TE7. They are distinct from the related but contractile myofibroblasts (α-SMA+), which appear during wound healing and fibrosis. Fibroblasts are heterogeneous and tissue-specific: dermal, pulmonary, cardiac, and tendon fibroblasts differ in transcriptional profile and function.

## Physiological Function

The core function of fibroblasts is ECM production—secreting structural proteins (collagen types I, III, and V), glycoproteins (fibronectin, laminin), and proteoglycans—together with matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) that remodel the scaffold. They respond to injury by proliferating and migrating into the wound bed, depositing granulation tissue, and coordinating with immune cells. In homeostasis they continually turnover matrix to preserve tissue mechanical integrity.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

In the history of [[Stem Cell Research]], fibroblasts are most notable for being the first and most widely used somatic cell type for [[Cellular Reprogramming]]. Shinya Yamanaka's landmark 2006 study used mouse embryonic [[Fibroblasts]] (MEFs) to generate the first [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs); human fibroblasts (e.g., human foreskin fibroblasts, HFF1) were reprogrammed shortly thereafter with the same [[Yamanaka Factors]] (OSKM).

Because fibroblasts are mesenchymal, they must undergo [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]] (MET) during reprogramming to reach a pluripotent state, involving profound morphological and transcriptional change. Although fibroblasts remain the standard starting material—owing to ease of culture and accessibility via skin biopsy—other cell types (keratinocytes, blood progenitors) can reprogram more efficiently because they are epigenetically "closer" to pluripotency. Fibroblasts also serve as the canonical substrate for [[Partial Reprogramming]] and [[Rejuvenation]] studies.

## Pathological Relevance: Fibrosis

Persistent fibroblast activation drives [[Fibrosis]] in organs such as the lung (idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis), liver (cirrhosis), kidney, and heart. Fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition, often stimulated by TGF-β, is a major therapeutic target.
