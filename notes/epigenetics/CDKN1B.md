# CDKN1B

**[[CDKN1B]]**, also known as [[p27]] (Kip1), is a gene encoding the p27 protein, a member of the Cip/Kip family of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors (CKIs). Like [[CDKN1A|p21]], p27 is essential for cell cycle regulation, but it often plays a more prominent role in the G1→S transition and in controlling proliferation and differentiation.

## Structure & Expression

p27 is a ~197-amino-acid protein whose N-terminal region (residues 1–90) binds and inhibits [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]] complexes. Its expression is constitutive at the transcript level but tightly controlled post-translationally: in quiescent cells p27 is stable, whereas in cycling cells it is degraded. Expression is induced by anti-proliferative signals including [[TGF-β]], cAMP, and contact inhibition.

## Mechanism/Function

p27 binds and inhibits CDK2-[[Cyclin E]] and CDK4/6-[[Cyclin D]] complexes, preventing phosphorylation of the retinoblastoma protein (Rb) and maintaining cells in G1. This positions p27 as a gatekeeper ensuring cells enter S phase only upon appropriate size, nutrient, and mitogenic signals.

Its levels and activity are regulated by:

- **PI3K/Akt pathway**: Akt phosphorylates p27 (Thr157), exporting it from the nucleus to the cytoplasm where it is degraded via the SCF (Skp2) ubiquitin ligase; this promotes proliferation.
- **[[Quiescence]]**: p27 accumulation enforces reversible cell-cycle exit in response to differentiation or senescence cues.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

p27 is critical in embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, and wound healing, where precise proliferation control is required. Dysregulation is common in [[Cancer]]: reduced p27 (via degradation or reduced expression) correlates with poor prognosis in breast, lung, and prostate cancers. Conversely, excessive p27 can cause impaired proliferation or developmental defects. p27 also exerts CDK-independent roles in migration, apoptosis, and [[Autophagy]], compartmentalized in the cytoplasm.

## Clinical Relevance

p27 loss is a biomarker of aggressive tumors and a candidate target for therapies that stabilize CKIs. It functions as a [[Tumor Suppressor]] alongside the related [[CDKN1A]] and [[CDKN1C]].
