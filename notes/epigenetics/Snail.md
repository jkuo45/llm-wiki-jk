# Snail

[[Snail]] (SNAI1) is a C2H2 zinc-finger [[Transcription Factor]] and a principal inducer of the [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EMT). By repressing epithelial adhesion and activating mesenchymal gene expression, Snail drives gastrulation, wounds, and [[Cancer]] invasion.

## Structure and Mechanism

Snail contains four N-terminal zinc fingers that bind E-box regulatory elements (CANNTG). It directly represses *CDH1* (E-cadherin), claudins, and occludins, recruiting co-repressors (HDAC, LSD1, G9a) that deposit repressive [[H3K9me3]] and silence epithelial programs. Snail also upregulates vimentin, N-cadherin, and matrix remodelers, and cooperates with [[SMAD Proteins]] downstream of TGF-β. It is stabilized by PI3K/Akt and GSK-3β phosphorylation, and opposed by [[Klf4]] and miR-34.

## Biological Roles

During development, Snail initiates EMT at gastrulation, enabling mesodermal ingression. In adults it contributes to wound healing and tissue remodeling. Its expression is induced by TGF-β, Wnt, Notch, and hypoxia, and suppressed by epithelial-differentiation cues.

## Pathological Relevance

Snail is markedly upregulated in carcinomas, where it correlates with loss of E-cadherin, EMT, metastasis, chemoresistance, and poor survival. It synergizes with [[Slug]] and [[Zeb1]] to enforce a stem-like, invasive phenotype. Because Snail is centrally placed in EMT networks, it is a candidate therapeutic target, though functional redundancy among EMT factors complicates inhibition.
