# High-Throughput Screening

[[High-Throughput Screening]] (HTS) is an automated experimental methodology that enables the rapid assaying of large compound, siRNA, or CRISPR libraries — often hundreds of thousands to millions of samples — to identify bioactive hits. It couples robotics, liquid handling, sensitive detectors, and informatics to test many conditions in parallel with minimal reagent use. HTS is a cornerstone of modern [[Drug Discovery]], compressing the hit-identification phase from years to weeks.

## Workflow & Technologies

A typical HTS campaign begins with a defined assay (biochemical, cell-based, or phenotypic) miniaturized into microtiter plates (384-, 1536-, or 3456-well). Robotic arms dispense cells/compounds, incubate, and read out signals via fluorescence, luminescence, absorbance, or high-content imaging. Hits are selected using statistical thresholds (Z′/Z-factor for assay quality; robust Z-scores for activity). Increasingly, HTS is coupled to label-free modalities such as cellular impedance and phenotypic profiling, and to machine learning for hit prioritization and structure–activity modeling.

## Applications in Epigenetics & Reprogramming

In regenerative and aging research, HTS is deployed to discover small molecules that modulate [[Reprogramming]] efficiency, control [[Pluripotency]], or clear senescent cells. Notably, HTS campaigns have identified [[Senolytic Drugs]] — compounds such as navitoclax and fisetin derivatives — that selectively induce apoptosis of [[Cellular Senescence|senescent cells]] by exploiting their pro-survival networks. HTS also screens for epigenetic modulators (e.g., [[Histone Methyltransferase]] or demethylase inhibitors) and for compounds that remodel chromatin states such as [[H3K27me3]].

## Physiological & Translational Relevance

HTS accelerates the translation of basic discoveries into lead compounds for [[Cancer]], neurodegeneration, and age-related disease. It also underpins target validation, where genome-wide knockdown/knockout screens reveal genes essential to a phenotype. The integration of patient-derived cells and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] as screening substrates enables human-relevant, genetically diverse assays and reduces reliance on animal models.

## Limitations & Developments

False positives (aggregators, fluorescent artifacts, cytotoxicity) require confirmatory dose–response and counterscreens. Ultra-high-throughput formats and droplet microfluidics now push screening into the millions-of-reaction regime, while phenotypic and image-based screens capture complex morphologies impossible in biochemical assays. Combining HTS with CRISPR perturbations (CRISPRi/a screens) blurs the line between chemical and genetic screening.
