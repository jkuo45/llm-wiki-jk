# Muscle Stem Cell

[[Muscle Stem Cell|Muscle stem cells]], synonymous with [[Satellite Cells|satellite cells]], are Pax7-expressing mononuclear progenitors wedged between the sarcolemma and the basement membrane of skeletal muscle fibers. Quiescent in homeostasis, they are the principal source of new myonuclei for growth and repair. Their identity as the authentic muscle-resident stem cell was established by Pax7 lineage tracing, which shows they self-renew and give rise to all regenerated myofibers.

## Structure & Niche

Quiescent satellite cells are small, virtually cytoplasm-poor cells marked by Pax7, low Myf5, and [[Integrin]] α7β1 binding to laminin in the niche. They maintain [[Quiescence]] through [[Notch signaling]] (with the transmembrane ligand Delta-like 1 on myofibers) and negative regulators such as [[Sprouty1]] and [[Fbxo32]]. The niche integrates signals from the [[Extracellular Matrix]], motor neurons, and fibro-adipogenic progenitors.

## Mechanism of Activation & Differentiation

Upon injury, signals including [[Hepatocyte Growth Factor]] (HGF), [[Wnt signaling]], and [[IGF-1]] trigger exit from [[Quiescence]]. Activated cells (Pax7+/MyoD+) proliferate as myoblasts, then withdraw from the [[Cell Cycle]] to [[Differentiation|differentiate]] (myogenin+, fusion into myotubes) or self-renew to replenish the quiescent pool. [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] and [[E2F]] govern the proliferation-to-differentiation switch. The [[NuRD complex]] and other chromatin remodelers reset the epigenetic landscape during these transitions, linking muscle repair to [[Epigenetic Remodeling]].

## Physiological Function

Satellite cells are indispensable for postnatal muscle growth, hypertrophy, and regeneration after damage or exercise. They supply nuclei to growing myofibers and maintain tissue homeostasis across the lifespan.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Aging ([[Aging]]) impairs satellite cell number, function, and niche communication, contributing to sarcopenia. Transient expression of [[Yamanaka Factors]] in myofibers can rejuvenate the satellite-cell niche and enhance regenerative capacity, a focus of [[Rejuvenation]] research. Satellite-cell dysfunction underlies muscular dystrophies and cachexia; cell-based and gene therapies (e.g., [[CRISPR]]-corrected progenitors) are under investigation. Understanding [[Quiescence]] exit is also relevant to [[Cancer]]-associated myopathies and to [[Tissue Repair]] strategies in [[Regenerative Medicine]].
