# Cellular Senescence

**Cellular Senescence** is a state of permanent cell-cycle arrest characterized by a profound and dynamic remodeling of the [[Chromatin]] landscape and [[Epigenetics]] state. These changes are functional drivers of its two primary hallmarks: **permanent cell-cycle arrest** and the **[[SASP]]** (senescence-associated secretory phenotype).

### 1. Higher-Order Chromatin Reorganization
*   **[[SAHF]] (Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci):** One of the most iconic features of senescence is the formation of SAHFs. These are dense, transcriptionally silent domains that sequester and repress pro-proliferation genes.
*   **Loss of Lamin B1:** A significant reduction in Lamin B1 (related to [[Lamin A]]) leads to the detachment of Lamina-Associated Domains from the nuclear periphery. This triggers a global "shuffling" of the genome, where previously silenced [[Heterochromatin]] can become accessible, and [[Euchromatin]] can be repressed.
*   **Centromere Distension:** Senescent cells often show a loss of silencing at centromeres, leading to [[Genomic Instability]].

### 2. [[Histone Modification]]s and Variants
*   **Global Histone Loss:** Senescent cells experience a significant decrease in the total amount of core histones, leading to increased DNA accessibility and "leaky" transcription.
*   **Repressive Marks:** While SAHFs are enriched in repressive marks like H3K9me3, there is often a global decrease in these marks elsewhere, contributing to the activation of retrotransposons like [[LINE-1]] elements.
*   **[[Histone Variant]]s:** Specific variants like macroH2A and H2A.J accumulate in senescent cells. MacroH2A stabilizes SAHFs, while H2A.J is linked to the activation of SASP genes.

### 3. [[DNA Methylation]] Dynamics
*   **Global Hypomethylation:** Similar to [[notes/_link/Aging]], senescent cells exhibit a global loss of DNA methylation, particularly in repetitive elements.
*   **Focal Hypermethylation:** Conversely, specific promoter regions—often associated with tumor suppressor genes—become hypermethylated and silenced.
*   **[[Epigenetic Clock]]:** Predictable shifts in DNA methylation patterns form the basis of clocks used to estimate the biological age of cells.

### 4. Epigenetic Regulation of the [[SASP]]
The SASP is a complex program of pro-inflammatory factors regulated by:
*   **Enhancer Remodeling:** Senescent cells develop "super-enhancers" near SASP gene loci.
*   **Innate Immune Sensing:** Fragments of chromatin can leak into the cytoplasm, triggering the **cGAS-STING pathway**, which drives SASP production and chronic inflammation.

### Connections
- **[[notes/_link/Aging]]**: The accumulation of senescent cells is a primary driver of tissue aging and age-related diseases.
- **[[notes/_link/Cancer]]**: Senescence acts as an initial barrier to cancer but can later promote tumor growth via the SASP.
- **[[SAHF]]**: Dense chromatin structures that are a hallmark of the senescent state.
