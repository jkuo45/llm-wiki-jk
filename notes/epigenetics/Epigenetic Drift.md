# Epigenetic Drift

**[[Epigenetic Drift]]** refers to the gradual and random changes in the [[notes/_link/Epigenome]] that occur as an individual ages. These changes are distinct from programmed developmental alterations and are thought to contribute to the loss of cellular identity and function over time.

## Key Features
*   **Randomness**: Unlike developmental epigenetics, drift involves stochastic changes that vary between individuals and even between cells in the same tissue.
*   **[[notes/_link/Methylation]] Changes**: Drift is most commonly observed as global hypomethylation of repetitive elements and site-specific hypermethylation of gene promoters.
*   **Environmental Influence**: Drift can be accelerated by environmental factors such as diet, stress, and exposure to toxins.

## Relationship to [[notes/_link/Aging]]
Epigenetic drift is a core component of the **[[notes/_link/Hallmarks of Aging]]**. It provides the basis for **[[notes/_link/Epigenetic Clock|Epigenetic Clocks]]**, which measure the cumulative "noise" or drift to estimate biological age.
