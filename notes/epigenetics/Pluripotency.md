# Pluripotency

[[Pluripotency]] is the capacity of a cell to differentiate into any of the three germ layers—ectoderm, endoderm, and mesoderm—giving rise to all somatic cell types of the body, while lacking the ability to form extra-embryonic tissues such as the placenta (which defines totipotency). Pluripotent cells are the foundation of [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] (ESCs) and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs).

## Core Regulatory Network

Pluripotency is sustained by an interconnected transcription-factor circuitry. The triad [[Oct4]] (POU5F1), [[Sox2]], and [[Nanog]] autoregulates to maintain an open, transcriptionally permissive chromatin state at pluripotency loci while repressing lineage-commitment genes. Cooperating factors include [[Klf4]], [[Esrrb]], and [[c-Myc]] (the latter two within the [[Yamanaka Factors]]). This network is reinforced by [[Epigenetic Remodeling]]: broad [[DNA Methylation]] of differentiation genes, bivalent chromatin (simultaneous H3K4me3/H3K27me3) at lineage loci, and active histone acetylation at core pluripotency promoters.

## Naive versus Primed States

Pluripotency exists in layered states. "Naive" (ground) state ESCs resemble the pre-implantation inner cell mass and are maximally plastic; "primed" epiblast-like cells are poised for lineage specification. [[Cellular Reprogramming]] aims to reset somatic cells to the naive state, requiring [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]] and erasure of somatic epigenetic memory.

## Sources and Generation

Pluripotent cells are derived from the blastocyst inner cell mass (ESCs) or generated by [[Cellular Reprogramming]] of [[Somatic Cells]] using the [[Yamanaka Factors]]. Exit from pluripotency—[[Differentiation]]—is triggered by extrinsic cues and gradual silencing of the core network.

## Relevance to Aging and Reprogramming

Pluripotency erases age-associated epigenetic marks, motivating [[Partial Reprogramming]] and [[Rejuvenation]] strategies that capture youthful reset without full dedifferentiation. Understanding the network also informs [[Cancer]] stemness, where pluripotency genes are re-activated.
