# Gastrulation

[[Gastrulation]] is a pivotal phase of [[Embryonic Development]] during which the single-layered blastula is reorganized into the multilayered gastrula. It is the stage at which the three primary germ layers — ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm — are established, providing the foundational tissue architecture from which all organs and cell types arise. Gastrulation converts the radially symmetric blastula into a bilaterally organized body plan and positions the primitive streak (in amniotes) or blastopore (in other vertebrates) as the site of ingressing cells.

## Cellular Mechanisms

Gastrulation is driven by coordinated cell movements — invagination, involution, ingression, and convergent extension — that require large-scale cytoskeletal remodeling and changes in cell adhesion. A central event is the [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EMIT), in which cells of the epiblast lose apical–basal polarity and adherents junctions, adopt mesenchymal motility, and delaminate through the primitive streak to internalize as mesendoderm. This EMT is guided by transcription factors such as Snail, Slug, Twist, and Foxc2, and by signaling from Nodal, Wnt, and FGF pathways.

## Epigenetic Remodeling

The transition from naive [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] to lineage-committed germ layers depends on profound [[Epigenetic Remodeling]]. As cells ingress, pluripotency regulators (Oct4, Nanog, Sox2) are silenced while lineage-specifying genes are activated. This involves redistribution of repressive and active chromatin marks: loss of [[H3K27me3]] (Polycomb-mediated repression) at poised developmental loci, gain of H3K4me3 at active promoters, DNA demethylation of lineage genes, and reorganization of higher-order chromatin architecture. Epigenetic priming in the epiblast therefore pre-patterns which genes become available for activation as germ layers specify.

## Physiological & Developmental Significance

Gastrulation establishes the body axes and the progenitor pools for every subsequent tissue. Defects in gastrulation movements produce catastrophic congenital malformations (e.g., failure of axis formation, situs inversus, neural tube defects). The germ-layer allocation is tightly coupled to later organogenesis, and the signaling centers established during gastrulation (node, organizer) continue to pattern the embryo.

## Research Relevance

Because gastrulation is inaccessible in intact human embryos, model systems — human embryonic stem cells, gastruloids, and non-human embryos — are used to dissect its mechanics. Understanding gastrulation EMT and epigenetic priming informs efforts in directed differentiation of [[Progenitor Cell]] populations and in modeling developmental disorders. Aberrant re-activation of gastrulation-like EMT programs is also a feature of [[Cancer]] metastasis, linking developmental and oncogenic biology.
