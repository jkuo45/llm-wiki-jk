# Ubiquitin Ligase

A [[Ubiquitin Ligase]] (more precisely an E3 ubiquitin ligase) is an enzyme that recruits an E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme loaded with ubiquitin, recognizes a specific protein substrate, and assists or directly catalyzes the transfer of ubiquitin from the E2 to the substrate. This reaction — ubiquitination — tags proteins for [[Protein Degradation]] by the [[Proteasome]], for altered localization, or for non-proteolytic signaling. With >600 E3 ligases in humans, the E3 step confers the substrate specificity that makes ubiquitination a precise regulatory language.

## Classes & Mechanism

E3 ligases fall into major families defined by catalytic domain. **RING (Really Interesting New Gene)** E3s (e.g., MDM2, β-TrCP) act as scaffolds, positioning the E2~ubiquitin conjugate to transfer ubiquitin directly to the substrate lysine. **HECT** E3s (e.g., E6AP, WWP1) first accept ubiquitin onto their own catalytic cysteine before passing it to the substrate. **RBR (RING-between-RING)** E3s (e.g., Parkin) combine RING and HECT-like features. Substrate recognition often uses degrons or phosphorylation-dependent motifs (phosphodegrons), linking ubiquitination to kinase signaling.

## Substrate Circuits

The [[Anaphase Promoting Complex-Cyclosome]] (APC/C), a multisubunit RING E3, drives mitotic exit by ordering degradation of securin and cyclins, thereby controlling [[Mitosis]] and the [[Cell Cycle]]. The SCF (SKP1–CUL1–F-box) family targets canonical inhibitors (e.g., phospho-IκB, phospho-β-catenin) for destruction, integrating with Wnt and NF-κB pathways. MDM2 ubiquitinates [[TP53]], a key node in senescence and tumor suppression (see [[Tumor-Suppressor Loss-Induced Senescence]]).

## Physiological Function

Ubiquitination regulates nearly every process: proteostasis, signal transduction, DNA repair, [[Translation]], circadian rhythms, and immune antigen presentation. Dynamic ubiquitin chains (K48-linked for degradation; K63-linked for signaling/trafficking) encode outcomes, and deubiquitinases (DUBs) reverse them, forming a reversible code.

## Pathology & Research Relevance

E3 ligase dysfunction underlies [[Cancer]] (MDM2 amplification stabilizing oncoproteins; VHL loss in renal cancer; [[RB1]] pathway perturbations), neurodegeneration (Parkin mutations in Parkinson's), and developmental disease. Therapeutically, the E3 substrate-recognition interface is exploited by PROTACs (proteolysis-targeting chimeras) and molecular glues that recruit neo-substrates to ligases such as CRBN or VHL, degrading pathological proteins. Inhibiting specific E3s is also pursued to stabilize tumor suppressors.
