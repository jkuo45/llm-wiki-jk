# Oct4

Oct4 (Octamer-binding transcription factor 4), also known as POU5F1 (POU domain, class 5, transcription factor 1), is a [[Transcription Factor]] encoded by the *POU5F1* gene in humans. It is a definitive marker and master regulator of [[Pluripotency]], essential for the self-renewal of undifferentiated [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]. Oct4 is one of the four [[Yamanaka Factors]] (with [[Sox2]], [[Klf4]], and [[c-Myc]]; the [[OSKM]] cassette) used to induce pluripotency in [[Somatic Cells]].

## Structure & Domains

Oct4 belongs to the POU-homeodomain family, containing a bipartite DNA-binding region: an N-terminal POU-specific (POU_S) domain and a C-terminal POU-homeodomain (POU_H), connected by a flexible linker. The POU domains recognize the octamer motif (ATGCAAAT) and related sequences. A C-terminal transactivation domain and N-terminal modules mediate interactions with cofactors including [[Sox2]] and [[Nanog]]. Alternative splicing produces Oct4A (nuclear, pluripotency-active) and Oct4B (cytosolic, stress-related) isoforms.

## Mechanism of Action

Oct4 functions as a pioneer-like factor, co-binding with [[Sox2]] at enhancers and promoters of pluripotency genes. The Oct4–Sox2 heterodimer (bridged by the co-activator [[Nanog]]–associated complexes) opens chromatin and recruits chromatin remodelers such as the [[NuRD complex]] and [[Mediator]], establishing the pluripotent transcriptional network. Oct4 dosage is exquisitely sensitive: too little permits differentiation, too much drives trophectoderm or [[Teratoma]]-forming states. During [[Cellular Reprogramming]], Oct4 initiates the mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition and resets the epigenome, with [[H3K9me3]]/[[H3K27me3]] repressive marks erased at target loci. Oct4 levels decline as cells undergo [[Terminal Differentiation]] and exit the [[Cell Cycle]].

## Physiological Function

Oct4 is expressed in the inner cell mass of the blastocyst and in primordial germ cells, maintaining naive pluripotency. It is downregulated upon lineage commitment and is absent from most adult somatic tissues — a property exploited in detecting residual undifferentiated cells in cell therapies (where its persistence signals [[Tumorigenicity]] risk).

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

 Aberrant Oct4 re-expression is observed in germ-cell tumors and some carcinomas, where it can sustain a stem-like, therapy-resistant phenotype. In regenerative medicine, precise Oct4 dosing underpins safe [[Reprogramming]] and the use of iPSCs for [[Disease Modeling]] (e.g., [[Frontotemporal Dementia]], [[Olivopontocerebellar Atrophy]]) and [[Rejuvenation]] (transient reprogramming to reset epigenetic age). Oct4 is also a target of [[CRISPR]] and small-molecule (re)activation strategies aiming to control pluripotency without genomic integration.
