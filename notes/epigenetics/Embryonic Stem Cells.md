# Embryonic Stem Cells

[[Embryonic Stem Cells]] (ESCs) are pluripotent stem cells derived from the inner cell mass (ICM) of the [[Blastocyst]], an early pre-implantation embryo (approximately days 5–7 post-fertilization in humans). ESCs are defined by two hallmark properties: unlimited self-renewal and the capacity to differentiate into cell types of all three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm) — the essence of [[Pluripotency]].

## Derivation & Culture

ESCs are isolated by immunosurgical or mechanical dissection of the ICM and cultured on feeder layers (e.g., inactivated mouse embryonic fibroblasts) or in feeder-free conditions with defined media (e.g., containing FGF2 and inhibitors of MEK and GSK3β — "2i/LIF" conditions in mouse). Human ESCs require FGF2 and activin/nodal signaling to remain undifferentiated. ESC lines are typically maintained as tightly packed colonies expressing markers such as OCT4 ([[Oct4]]), NANOG, SOX2 ([[Sox2]]), SSEA-3/4, and TRA-1-60/81, and they can be characterized by their ability to form teratomas, embryoid bodies, and contribute to chimeras.

## Molecular Identity

The core pluripotency network is wired by the transcription factors [[Oct4]], [[Sox2]], and NANOG, which co-occupy promoters/enhancers of both pluripotency genes and differentiation genes, keeping the latter poised but silenced. This circuitry is epigenetically reinforced by bivalent chromatin domains (simultaneous H3K4me3 and H3K27me3 marks) and by high telomerase activity. ESCs exhibit a hyperdynamic, open chromatin state conducive to rapid fate changes.

## Comparison with iPSCs

[[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) share most molecular and functional features with ESCs and are now the more widely used human pluripotent source because they avoid the destruction of embryos. Remaining differences include subtle epigenetic memory in iPSCs, residual transgene expression, and slightly divergent differentiation propensity, though these gaps continue to narrow with improved reprogramming methods.

## Physiological, Pathological & Research Relevance

ESCs were the original substrate for studying mammalian [[Embryonic Development]] and for validating the Yamanaka reprogramming principle later applied to somatic cells. They remain essential for developmental biology, [[Disease Modeling]], [[Drug Discovery]], and [[Regenerative Medicine]]. The major limitation is ethical and legal restriction on human embryo use, alongside the risk of [[Teratoma]] formation if undifferentiated cells are transplanted.
