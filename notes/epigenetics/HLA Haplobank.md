# HLA Haplobank

An [[HLA Haplobank]] is a curated repository of [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) generated from donors who are homozygous for common [[Major Histocompatibility Complex]] (MHC/HLA) haplotypes. By matching a patient's HLA type to a banked line, differentiated cell products can be transplanted with markedly reduced [[Immunogenicity]] and lowered risk of immune rejection, realizing the goal of "off-the-shelf" allogeneic cell therapies for [[Regenerative Medicine]].

## Rationale & Design

The HLA region encodes the classical class I (HLA-A, -B, -C) and class II (HLA-DR, -DQ, -DP) molecules that present peptides to T cells. An individual who is homozygous at HLA-A, -B, and -DRB1 (the most polymorphic, rejection-driving loci) carries a single haplotype on each chromosome and can therefore be matched to many recipients sharing that haplotype. Population genetics shows that a limited set of homozygous haplotypes — often a few dozen to ~100 lines, depending on the target population — can cover a large fraction of a given ethnic group. Each banked iPSC master line is extensively characterized for pluripotency, genomic integrity, and absence of pathogens before being banked under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) conditions.

## Manufacturing Pipeline

Haplobank lines serve as universal starting material. They are differentiated into the desired cell type (e.g., cardiomyocytes, dopaminergic neurons, retinal pigment epithelium, pancreatic β-like cells, or hematopoietic progenitors), quality-controlled, and cryopreserved as allogeneic products. Because the master iPSC line is renewable, a single approved haplotype can supply essentially unlimited doses, sharply reducing per-patient cost relative to autologous [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] made from each patient.

## Physiological & Clinical Relevance

The central clinical promise is durable engraftment without lifelong, broad immunosuppression. Matching at HLA-A/B/DRB1 minimizes direct allorecognition by host T cells; residual mismatch at less polymorphic loci may be tolerated or managed with transient immunosuppression. Haplobanks thus underpin trials for macular degeneration, Parkinson's disease, heart failure, and type 1 diabetes. They also reduce the [[Tumorigenicity]] risk associated with undifferentiated iPSCs by enabling rigorous selection and release testing of a fixed set of master lines.

## Challenges

Limitations include incomplete coverage of ethnically diverse populations, minority HLA types requiring many more lines, and the need to avoid minor histocompatibility and ABO mismatches. Emerging strategies combine haplobanking with CRISPR-based HLA engineering (knockout or introduction of "universal" HLA alleles) to broaden compatibility further.
