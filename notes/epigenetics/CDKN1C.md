# CDKN1C

**[[CDKN1C]]**, also known as [[p57]] (Kip2), is a gene encoding the p57 protein, a member of the Cip/Kip family of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors (CKIs). It is distinguished by being **genomically imprinted** — expressed only from the maternal allele — and is essential for embryonic and placental development.

## Genomic Imprinting

*CDKN1C* lies in the imprinted domain on chromosome 11p15.5 (the Beckwith-Wiedemann region). Its maternal-allele-specific expression is controlled by an imprinting control region (ICR2/KvDMR1) that is methylated on the maternal chromosome, blocking an antisense transcript that would otherwise silence *CDKN1C* on the paternal allele. This parent-of-origin [[DNA Methylation]] is a classic example of [[Genomic Imprinting]] and an [[Epigenetic Inheritance]] mechanism established in the germline.

## Mechanism/Function

p57 inhibits [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]] complexes (Cyclin E-CDK2, Cyclin D-CDK4/6), promoting G1 arrest. Its expression is spatially and temporally restricted during development — high in differentiating tissues such as skeletal muscle, brain, and the placenta. It balances proliferation with differentiation, ensuring cells mature at correct locations and times. p57 also contributes to [[Quiescence]] and can modulate apoptosis independently of CDK inhibition.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

p57 is a critical brake on proliferation during key developmental windows, particularly in the placenta, where it regulates trophoblast differentiation and [[Placental Development]]. Unlike [[CDKN1A|p21]] and [[CDKN1B|p27]], p57 shows highly restricted expression rather than ubiquitous presence.

## Clinical Relevance

Maternally inherited loss-of-function mutations or loss of imprinting (LOI) at *CDKN1C* are the primary cause of **[[Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome]]**, an overgrowth disorder with macroglossia, organomegaly, and elevated risk of embryonal tumors (e.g., Wilms tumor). Reduced p57 permits unchecked proliferation, illustrating how imprinting defects produce disease. *CDKN1C* is therefore both a developmental regulator and an epigenetic-disease locus.
