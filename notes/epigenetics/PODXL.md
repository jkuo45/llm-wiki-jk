# PODXL

[[PODXL]] (Podocalyxin-like protein 1) is a heavily glycosylated type I transmembrane sialomucin, originally identified as the major glycoprotein on the apical surface of kidney [[Podocyte|podocytes]], where it maintains the intricate foot-process architecture through charge-based anti-adhesion. Beyond the kidney, PODXL is now recognized as one of the most specific cell-surface markers of undifferentiated [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] (ESCs) and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs), making it a valuable tool for monitoring and enriching [[Cellular Reprogramming]].

## Structure & Properties

PODXL consists of an N-terminal mucin-like extracellular domain rich in sialylated O-glycans, a single-pass transmembrane region, and a short cytoplasmic tail containing a PDZ-domain-binding motif. The dense negatively charged glycosylation creates a hydrated, repulsive glycocalyx that prevents cell–cell and cell–matrix adhesion — the same anti-adhesion function that, in pluripotent stem cells, correlates with a rounded, non-attached colony morphology. PODXL is coordinately expressed with other pluripotency markers such as SSEA-1, and its expression is driven downstream of the core pluripotency circuitry including [[Oct4]] and [[Sox2]].

## Role in Reprogramming

During the early stages of [[Cellular Reprogramming]] (24–72 hours after induction of the [[OSKM]] factors), PODXL is among the pluripotency-associated surface antigens rapidly "switched on" as somatic cells undergo [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]] (MET). This early, surface-exposed expression makes PODXL ideal for fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS):
- Pre-selection or live enrichment of PODXL-positive cells improves the speed and efficiency of iPSC line derivation.
- It allows non-invasive tracking of reprogramming progression without relying on transgenic reporter constructs.
- Loss of PODXL coincides with differentiation, so its level serves as a quality-control readout for stemness.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Outside stem cell research, PODXL dysregulation is implicated in several cancers, where its anti-adhesion and pro-migratory properties promote [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EMT) and metastasis — notably in breast, ovarian, and prostate carcinomas. In the kidney, reduced podocyte PODXL expression contributes to foot-process effacement and proteinuria in glomerular disease. Thus PODXL exemplifies a molecule whose anti-adhesion function is beneficial for stemness yet pathological when misexpressed in epithelia.
