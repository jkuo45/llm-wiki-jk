# Induced Neural Stem Cells

Induced Neural Stem Cells (iNSCs) are neural stem cells generated directly from [[Somatic Cells]] (such as fibroblasts or mesenchymal stromal cells) using [[Cellular Reprogramming]] techniques.

Unlike [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs), which go through a fully pluripotent state, iNSCs can be generated through direct lineage reprogramming (transdifferentiation), which may reduce the risk of [[Teratoma]] formation. iNSCs have shown potential in treating [[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]] like [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]] by differentiating into functional neurons and glia.

## Connections
- [[Cellular Reprogramming]]: The process used to generate iNSCs from non-neural cells.
- [[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]]: iNSCs are a promising source for cell replacement therapies.
- [[Teratoma]]: Direct reprogramming to iNSCs aims to bypass the teratoma-prone pluripotent state.
- [[BIG1]]: A protein regulated by [[Klf4]] that is involved in the migration and growth of iNSCs.
