# Slug

[[Slug]] (SNAI2) is a C2H2 zinc-finger [[Transcription Factor]] of the Snail superfamily. It is a principal inducer of the [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EMT), repressing epithelial adhesion molecules and activating mesenchymal programs during embryonic migration, wound healing, and [[Cancer]] metastasis.

## Structure and Mechanism

Like its paralog [[Snail]] (SNAI1), Slug contains four or five N-terminal zinc fingers that bind E-box motifs (CANNTG) in target promoters. It directly represses *CDH1* (E-cadherin), claudins, and occludins via recruitment of co-repressors (e.g., LSD1, HDACs, G9a) that deposit repressive [[H3K9me3]] and remove activating marks. Slug also induces mesenchymal markers (vimentin, N-cadherin, fibronectin) and stemness genes, and suppresses apoptosis through BMI1 and survivin.

## Biological Roles

During development, Slug controls neural crest delamination and migration, and placodal EMT. In adult tissues it contributes to wound re-epithelialization and hair-follicle cycling. Its activity is induced by TGF-β (via [[SMAD Proteins]]), Wnt/β-catenin, Notch, and hypoxia (HIF-1α), and restrained by [[Klf4]] and [[Zeb1]] antagonism in epithelial programs.

## Pathological Relevance

Slug overexpression correlates with EMT, invasion, chemoresistance, and poor prognosis across carcinomas. It cooperates with [[Snail]], [[Zeb1]], and Twist to establish metastatic and cancer-stem-cell phenotypes. Targeting Slug (or its upstream inducers) is pursued to block EMT-driven dissemination, though redundancy among EMT factors complicates single-target inhibition.
