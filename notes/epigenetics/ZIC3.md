# ZIC3

[[ZIC3]] (Zic Family Member 3) encodes a C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor belonging to the ZIC (Zinc finger in cerebellum) family of five conserved proteins. It is a dual-function regulator: it is essential for establishing left-right [[Laterality]] during [[Embryonic Development]], and it acts as a downstream effector of the reprogramming and [[Pluripotency]] network, functioning alongside the classic Yamanaka factors. Loss-of-function mutations in human ZIC3 cause heterotaxy (situs ambiguus), a congenital disorder of laterality associated with complex cardiac and splanchnic defects.

## Structure and Mechanism

The ZIC3 protein contains five tandem C2H2 zinc finger domains at its C-terminus that mediate sequence-specific DNA binding to consensus Gli-binding sites (5′-TGGGTGGTC-3′), reflecting its evolutionary relationship to the Gli family of Hedgehog-effector transcription factors. ZIC3 operates at the intersection of the Nodal/Lefty signaling cascade that establishes the left-right organizer (the node) and downstream asymmetric gene expression (e.g., Nodal, Pitx2) in the lateral plate mesoderm. It cooperates with GFI1 and acts downstream of the Hedgehog pathway. In the pluripotency context, ZIC3 is a direct transcriptional target and co-activator within the core network: it directly activates the [[Nanog]] promoter and helps maintain the undifferentiated state of [[Embryonic Stem Cells]], while repressing endodermal differentiation. It participates in a reprogramming amplification pathway — [[Klf4]] → CREBBP → GLI3 → ZIC3 — that reinforces the pluripotent ground state.

## Physiological Function

During [[Embryonic Development]], ZIC3 expression is first detected in the node and notochord, then in the nascent neural tube and developing cerebellum, consistent with its laterality and neurodevelopmental roles. In pluripotent stem cells, ZIC3 sustains self-renewal and counteracts lineage commitment, making it a functionally relevant (if non-classical) member of the [[Pluripotency]] circuitry alongside [[Sox2]], Oct4, and Nanog.

## Pathological and Research Relevance

Heterozygous ZIC3 mutations underlie X-linked recessive heterotaxy with incomplete penetrance, implicating it in congenital heart disease. In regenerative medicine, ZIC3 is studied as a facilitator of somatic cell reprogramming and as a marker of naive pluripotency. Its overlap with Gli-family binding raises interest in Hedgehog–ZIC3 crosstalk in development and in certain malignancies.
