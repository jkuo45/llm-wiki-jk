# SAHF

**Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci (SAHF)** are specialized domains of facultative heterochromatin that assemble in the nuclei of senescent cells, reinforcing the stable, irreversible growth arrest of [[Senescence]]. They physically compartmentalize and silence proliferation-promoting genes, locking cells out of the cell cycle.

## Structure and Composition

SAHF are visualized as punctate DAPI-dense foci by microscopy. Biochemically they are enriched in heterochromatic marks—[[H3K9me3]] (trimethylated histone H3 lysine 9), macroH2A, HP1 proteins, and HMGA proteins—while depleted of active marks such as H3K4me3 and H3K9ac. They form through the action of the [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] (pRB/RB1) pathway: pRB recruits HIRA to deposit H3.3 and cooperates with SUV39H1/SETDB1 methyltransferases to lay down H3K9me3, which nucleates HP1-mediated compaction.

## Formation and Function

SAHF arise during oncogene-induced and replicative senescence. By condensing E2F target loci (e.g., cyclin A, CDC6) into heterochromatin, SAHF ensure these genes remain off, preventing escape from senescence—a potent tumor-suppressive barrier. Their assembly is therefore a marker of deep senescence and a brake against malignant proliferation.

## Relationship to Aging

Because senescence accumulates in aged tissues and contributes to [[Aging]] and age-related dysfunction, SAHF are a cellular correlate of organismal decline. They also intersect with [[Epigenetic Drift]]: the global redistribution of heterochromatin (loss at repetitive regions, gain at promoters) mirrors SAHF-type silencing. Unlike programming, SAHF formation is a damage-response epigenetic adaptation.

## Connections

- [[Senescence]]: SAHF are the structural hallmark of the senescent growth arrest.
- [[H3K9me3]]: The defining heterochromatin mark enriched in SAHF.

- [[Aging]]: SAHF accumulate as senescence burden rises with age.
