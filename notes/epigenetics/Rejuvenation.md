# Rejuvenation

Rejuvenation is the partial or complete reversal of age-associated decline in cellular and organismal function. In the context of epigenetic and stem cell biology, rejuvenation is pursued by resetting the molecular hallmarks of [[Aging]] — including epigenetic drift, accumulated DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, and [[Senescence]] — without necessarily replacing lost cells. The dominant experimental strategy is [[Cellular Reprogramming]] with the [[Yamanaka Factors]] ([[OSKM]]).

## Mechanisms of Epigenetic Rejuvenation

Aging is accompanied by progressive, stochastic alterations in DNA methylation, histone modification, and chromatin accessibility that are captured quantitatively by [[Epigenetic Clock|epigenetic clocks]] (e.g., Horvath's pan-tissue clock, Hannum clock). Reprogramming factors — particularly [[Oct4]] and [[Sox2]] — drive a global erasure and re-establishment of these marks, resetting the epigenetic age of a cell to a near-embryonic profile. Notably, transcriptomic and proteostatic "aging clocks" also shift toward youthful states, and markers such as telomere length, mitochondrial function, and lysosomal content can improve.

## Partial vs. Full Reprogramming

Full reprogramming converts a somatic cell into an [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells|iPSC]], erasing cell identity and carrying [[Teratoma]] risk. [[Partial Reprogramming]] instead expresses OSKM transiently, allowing cells to "remember" their somatic fate while shedding age-associated damage — a process termed epigenetic rejuvenation. Landmark studies (e.g., Ocampo et al., 2016) showed that cyclic, transient OSKM expression reversed signs of aging in progeroid mice and extended lifespan without tumor formation, while preserving tissue function. This approach has since been extended toward normal aged animals and specific tissues.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Rejuvenation intersects with nearly every age-related condition: [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]], cardiovascular decline, [[Macular Degeneration]], sarcopenia, and immunosenescence. Resetting epigenetic marks can restore youthful gene expression programs and improve regenerative capacity. However, the relationship between rejuvenation and true lifespan extension, as well as the risk of erasing beneficial adaptive methylation, remains actively debated.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Therapeutic avenues include (1) in vivo partial reprogramming via gene therapy or small molecules that mimic OSKM downstream effectors; (2) ex vivo rejuvenation of patient cells before [[Regenerative Medicine]] grafts; and (3) combination with [[Senolytic Drugs]] to clear senescent cells. Key open questions are durability of the rejuvenated state, off-target epigenetic effects, and safe delivery. Rejuvenation remains one of the most promising translational frontiers linking [[Epigenetics]] to longevity science.
