# DNMT1

**DNMT1** (DNA Methyltransferase 1) is the primary enzyme responsible for maintaining [[DNA Methylation]] patterns during the process of [[DNA Replication]]. It is often referred to as the "maintenance" methyltransferase because it copies the methylation pattern of the parental strand onto the daughter strand, ensuring that epigenetic information is inherited by daughter cells after division.

## Structure & Domains

DNMT1 is the largest mammalian DNA methyltransferase (~183 kDa). Its N-terminal regulatory domain contains a proliferating cell nuclear antigen (PCNA)-binding domain, a replication foci-targeting (RFT) domain, a CXXC zinc-finger domain (which binds unmethylated CpG islands), and a nuclear localization signal. The C-terminal catalytic domain shares the conserved cytosine-C5 methyltransferase fold with [[DNMT3a]] and [[DNMT3b]].

## Mechanism of Action

DNMT1 has a high preference for hemi-methylated DNA — DNA where only the parent strand is methylated. During [[S Phase]], DNMT1 is recruited to replication foci through its interaction with PCNA and with [[UHRF1]] (also called Np95/Icbp90), which recognizes hemi-methylated CpG sites via its SET-and-RING-associated (SRA) domain and recruits DNMT1. DNMT1 then transfers a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the C5 position of cytosine on the nascent strand, faithfully mirroring the template pattern.

## Physiological Function

Maintenance methylation is essential for cellular identity: it preserves the tissue-specific [[Epigenome]] through countless divisions during development and adult life. DNMT1 is critical for [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] self-renewal, [[Genomic Imprinting]] stability, [[X-Chromosome Inactivation]], and silencing of retrotransposons.

## Role in Aging

The efficiency and expression of DNMT1 decline as organisms [[Aging|age]].

- **Epigenetic Drift:** Reduced DNMT1 activity leads to gradual loss of methylation marks over successive divisions, contributing to the global hypomethylation seen in aged tissues.
- **[[Cellular Senescence]]**: Decreased DNMT1 levels have been linked to senescence pathways, as loss of repressive methylation can de-repress loci such as *CDKN2A* (p16INK4a).

## Clinical Relevance

DNMT1 is a critical target in [[Cancer]] research: its overexpression can silence tumor suppressor genes, while pathogenic heterozygous mutations in *DNMT1* cause an autosomal dominant neurodegenerative/immunodeficiency syndrome (ADCA-DN). Its decline is a hallmark of the aging epigenome, making it central to longevity and genomic stability research.
