# Drug Discovery

[[Drug Discovery]] is the comprehensive process by which new candidate medications are identified. It involves a range of scientific disciplines, including biology, chemistry, and pharmacology, and traditionally progresses from target identification to clinical trials.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

[[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) have transformed [[Drug Discovery]] by providing highly accurate human models for high-throughput screening and toxicity testing. Historically, the field relied on animal models, which often fail to predict human responses due to species-specific differences. iPSC-derived tissues—such as cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, and neurons—offer a more physiologically relevant platform for assessing drug efficacy and cardiotoxicity, hepatotoxicity, and neurotoxicity.

[[Drug Discovery]] has also been accelerated through [[Drug Repositioning|drug repurposing]] using iPSC models. For example, ropinirole and bosutinib were identified as potential treatments for [[notes/_link/Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]] through iPSC-based screening and have since entered clinical trials. By using patient-specific iPSCs, researchers can capture the genetic diversity of a population, which is crucial for capturing inter-individual differences in drug response and advancing the goal of personalized medicine.
