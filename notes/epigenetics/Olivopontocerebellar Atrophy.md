# Olivopontocerebellar Atrophy

[[Olivopontocerebellar Atrophy]] (OPCA) denotes a pattern of progressive neurodegeneration selectively affecting the cerebellum, pontine nuclei, and inferior olives. It is not a single disease but a neuropathological phenotype that appears in multiple inherited ataxias — most notably the spinocerebellar ataxias (SCA1, SCA2, SCA3/Machado–Joseph disease, SCA6) and multiple system atrophy–cerebellar type (MSA-C) — and in some sporadic/idiopathic cases. Clinically it manifests as progressive [[Ataxia]] (gait and limb incoordination), dysarthria, and oculomotor disturbances, often with autonomic involvement in MSA-C.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Inherited OPCA is usually autosomal dominant with CAG trinucleotide repeat expansions (polyglutamine tract) in genes such as *ATXN1* (SCA1), *ATXN2* (SCA2), and *ATXN3* (SCA3). These mutant proteins misfold and aggregate, causing proteotoxic stress, impaired [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]] clearance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and selective death of cerebellar Purkinje and pontine neurons. Sporadic OPCA (including MSA-C) features α-synuclein-positive glial cytoplasmic inclusions. [[Aging]] accelerates the accumulation of proteotoxic damage and reduces regenerative capacity of [[Neural Stem Cell|neural stem cells]]. The disease exemplifies how protein homeostasis failure drives [[Neurodegeneration]] in selectively vulnerable circuits.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

Presentation is insidious, with gait ataxia, scanning dysarthria, nystagmus, and later bulbar and autonomic failure. Diagnosis combines family history, neurological exam, and MRI showing cerebellar and pontine atrophy (the "hot-cross-bun" sign in MSA). Biomarkers are limited; research relies on genetic testing for repeat expansions and, increasingly, patient-derived cellular models.

## Therapeutic Landscape & Research Relevance

No curative therapy exists; management is supportive (physical therapy, assistive devices, symptomatic pharmacotherapy). Antisense oligonucleotides targeting mutant ataxin transcripts are in clinical development. Patient fibroblasts have been reprogrammed via [[Yamanaka Factors]] into [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] and differentiated into cerebellar neurons and [[Neural Stem Cell|neural progenitors]], providing isogenic models that recapitulate polyglutamine toxicity and enabling drug screening. Such iPSC platforms also permit study of [[Rejuvenation]] and [[Reprogramming]]-mediated reset of cellular age as modifiers of disease progression, paralleling their use in [[Frontotemporal Dementia]] and other neurodegenerative conditions.
