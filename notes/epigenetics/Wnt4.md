# Wnt4

[[Wnt4]] is a secreted glycoprotein ligand of the [[Wnt signaling]] pathway, encoded by the *WNT4* gene. It is one of 19 human Wnt family members and is distinctive for its ability to signal both through canonical β-catenin-dependent routes and through non-canonical (β-catenin-independent) pathways (e.g., [[Wnt/Ca2+]], planar cell polarity). Wnt4 is a critical morphogen in [[Embryonic Development]], notably in nephrogenesis, Müllerian duct formation, and ovarian [[Sex Determination]], and it helps maintain [[Quiescence]] in certain stem-cell niches (including [[Muscle Stem Cell|muscle stem cells]]).

## Structure & Secretion

Like other Wnts, Wnt4 is a ~350–400 aa cysteine-rich secreted protein requiring palmitoleoylation and glycosylation for proper secretion and signaling range. It binds Frizzled (Fz) receptors and low-density-lipoprotein-receptor-related proteins (LRP5/6 for canonical signaling, or Ror2/RYK for non-canonical), often with heparan-sulfate proteoglycan co-receptors.

## Mechanism of Action

Wnt4 activates distinct branches depending on receptor context:

- **Canonical (β-catenin)**: stabilizes β-catenin, driving TCF/LEF transcription — relevant to progenitor maintenance and, in reprogramming contexts, to stabilization of factors such as [[MYCN]] (via GSK3B inhibition).
- **Non-canonical**: engages Ca2+ flux and small-GTPase/Rho pathways that govern polarity and quiescence without β-catenin.

In the muscle niche, Wnt4 contributes to the signaling milieu that keeps [[Satellite Cells]]/[[Muscle Stem Cell|muscle stem cells]] quiescent until injury. It also modulates the [[Extracellular Matrix]] and crosstalks with [[Integrin]] adhesion, integrating positional cues with cell fate.

## Physiological Function

During development, Wnt4 is essential for metanephric kidney formation (induction of the nephronic lineage from the [[Mesenchyme]]), female reproductive tract development (Müllerian duct survival), and ovarian determination (with *RSPO1*/*FOXL2* antagonizing the testis program). It patterns the limb and central nervous system and supports stem/progenitor pools. In adults it helps preserve the quiescent reserve of tissue-specific stem cells.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

*WNT4* mutations cause SERKAL syndrome (ovarian, renal, and lung defects) and are implicated in disorders of sex development. Aberrant Wnt4/β-catenin signaling contributes to several [[Cancer]] types (e.g., Wilms tumor, breast, ovarian) by sustaining a stem-like, proliferative state. Because Wnt4 influences quiescence and [[Reprogramming]]-relevant pathways (including [[MYCN]] stabilization and [[Pluripotency]] networks), it is of interest in [[Rejuvenation]] and regenerative strategies that manipulate stem-cell activation. Its niche role also connects to [[Aging]]-related decline in regenerative competence.
