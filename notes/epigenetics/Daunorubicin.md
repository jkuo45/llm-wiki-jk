# Daunorubicin

Daunorubicin is an anthracycline antibiotic originally isolated from the actinobacterium *Streptomyces peucetius* var. *caesius*. Along with its hydroxylated derivative doxorubicin, it is among the most widely used chemotherapeutic agents in oncology. The core structure consists of a tetracyclic aglycone (daunosamine) linked via a glycosidic bond to an aminosugar, enabling intercalation between DNA base pairs and interaction with the DNA-topoisomerase II complex.

## Mechanism of Action

Daunorubicin exerts its cytotoxic effects through two primary mechanisms. The first involves intercalation between adjacent DNA base pairs, which distorts the helical structure and inhibits both DNA and RNA synthesis. The second and more potent mechanism is topoisomerase II (TOP2A/TOP2B) poisoning: daunorubicin stabilizes the transient cleavage complex formed between DNA and topoisomerase II, preventing religation of the enzyme-induced double-strand breaks. This converts topoisomerase II into a DNA-damaging toxin, resulting in persistent double-strand breaks that trigger apoptosis. A secondary mechanism involves redox cycling of the anthracycline quinone moiety, generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) that contribute to both antitumor activity and cardiotoxicity.

## Clinical Indications

Daunorubicin is a cornerstone of induction chemotherapy for acute myeloid leukemia (AML), typically administered in the classical 7+3 regimen (seven days of continuous cytarabine infusion plus three daily doses of daunorubicin). It is also used in the treatment of acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL), particularly in pediatric protocols, and has activity against chronic myeloid leukemia in blast crisis. The standard dose for AML induction is 60–90 mg/m² daily for three days, adjusted according to age and cardiac risk profile.

## Structural Derivatives

The substitution of a hydroxyl group at C-14 of the daunosamine sugar yields doxorubicin, which has broader activity including breast, ovarian, and gastric cancers. Idarubicin, a 4-demethoxy derivative of daunorubicin with improved lipophilicity and cellular uptake, is used in AML and has reduced cardiotoxicity compared to daunorubicin. Epirubicin, a stereoisomer of doxorubicin, is employed in breast and gastric cancer regimens.

## Cardiotoxicity and Management

The cumulative dose of daunorubicin correlates with the risk of developing irreversible dilated cardiomyopathy and congestive heart failure. The recommended lifetime maximum cumulative dose is 550 mg/m² (450 mg/m² in patients with prior chest irradiation or pre-existing cardiac disease). The cardioprotectant dexrazoxane, an iron-chelating agent that inhibits anthracycline-iron complex formation and subsequent ROS generation, is approved for use in patients who have already received cumulative anthracycline doses exceeding 300 mg/m². Monitoring of left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) by echocardiography or MUGA scan is standard practice during therapy.

## Senescence Research

Like other DNA-damaging chemotherapeutics, daunorubicin is used experimentally to model chemotherapy-induced senescence. Cultured fibroblasts and cancer cells exposed to daunorubicin develop persistent p53/p21-dependent growth arrest, SA-β-gal positivity, and SASP factor secretion, providing a platform to study the contribution of senescent cells to tumor relapse, treatment resistance, and accelerated aging in cancer survivors.
