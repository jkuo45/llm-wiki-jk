# Zeb1

[[Zeb1]] (Zinc finger E-box-binding homeobox 1) is a two-zinc-finger [[Transcription Factor]] that binds E-box motifs to repress epithelial genes and drive the [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EMT). It is a central regulator of mesenchymal identity, invasion, and stemness in development and [[Cancer]], and it antagonizes [[Pluripotency]] and epithelial differentiation.

## Structure and Mechanism

Zeb1 contains N- and C-terminal zinc-finger clusters (the homeodomain-like ZF-NC and ZF-C2H2 domains) that bind bipartite E-box sequences. It recruits co-repressors (CtBP, HDAC, LSD1, polycomb) to silence *CDH1* (E-cadherin) and other epithelial adhesion genes, while inducing vimentin, N-cadherin, and matrix remodelers. Zeb1 also forms a double-negative feedback loop with the miR-200 family—Zeb1 represses miR-200, which in turn targets Zeb1 mRNA—stabilizing EMT states. It cooperates with [[Snail]], [[Slug]], and SMAD signaling.

## Biological Roles

Zeb1 is required for embryonic EMT (somite, neural-crest, and endothelial-mesenchymal transitions) and for adult tissue repair. It maintains tissue fibroblasts and represses premature differentiation, and it supports stem-cell plasticity. In pluripotent cells, Zeb1 is suppressed to preserve epithelial/epithelial-like identity; its re-expression marks exit from [[Pluripotency]].

## Pathological Relevance

Zeb1 is overexpressed in carcinomas, where it correlates with EMT, metastasis, chemoresistance, and poor prognosis, and with immune evasion. It enforces a cancer-stem-cell phenotype and is linked to [[Cancer]] dissemination. Targeting the Zeb1–miR-200 axis or upstream TGF-β/[[SMAD Proteins]] input is explored to reverse EMT, though redundancy with other EMT factors is a challenge.
