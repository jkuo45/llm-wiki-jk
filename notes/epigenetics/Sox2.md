# Sox2

Sox2 (SRY-box transcription factor 2) is a [[Transcription Factor]] of the SOXB1 subgroup (HMG-box family) that is essential for maintaining self-renewal and [[Pluripotency]] of undifferentiated [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]. It is one of the four [[Yamanaka Factors]] (with [[Oct4]], [[Klf4]], and [[c-Myc]]; the [[OSKM]] cassette) capable of reprogramming differentiated cells to pluripotency, and is a member of the [[SOX gene family]].

## Structure & Domains

Sox2 contains a central HMG-box DNA-binding domain that recognizes the AACAAAG motif and, like other HMG proteins, bends DNA to facilitate enhancer–promoter looping. An N-terminal domain mediates transactivation and interactions with partners, while a C-terminal domain harbors a [[Transcription Factor]] transactivation/repression interface and a nuclear localization signal. Sox2 can act as an architectural factor, organizing chromatin topology at pluripotency loci.

## Mechanism of Action

Sox2 forms a核心 heterodimer with [[Oct4]], the two factors co-occupying enhancers and promoters of pluripotency genes (including [[Nanog]]) and opening chromatin via recruitment of remodelers such as the [[NuRD complex]] and [[Mediator]]. The Oct4–Sox2 pair activates the self-renewal network while repressing lineage-commitment genes, thereby preventing premature [[Differentiation]]. During [[Cellular Reprogramming]], Sox2 helps erase somatic identity and reset the epigenome; its expression is among the earliest markers of successful reprogramming. Sox2 dosage is critical — both insufficiency and excess disrupt the pluripotent state, and ectopic Sox2 in differentiated tissues can drive tumorigenesis (e.g., in squamous cancers and glioblastoma).

## Physiological Function

Sox2 is expressed in the developing neural tube, sensory epithelia, and the inner cell mass, where it sustains progenitor and stem cell pools. It maintains [[Neural Stem Cell]] identity in the adult SVZ/SGZ and is required for tissue homeostasis in multiple epithelia.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Dysregulated Sox2 contributes to [[Cancer]] stemness and poor prognosis in several malignancies, and Sox2 rearrangements/amplifications occur in squamous cell carcinomas. In regenerative medicine, controlled Sox2 expression underpins safe [[Reprogramming]] and iPSC generation for [[Disease Modeling]] (e.g., [[Frontotemporal Dementia]], [[Olivopontocerebellar Atrophy]]) and [[Rejuvenation]] (transient reprogramming to reset epigenetic age). Sox2 is also a target of [[CRISPR]] and small-molecule approaches to manipulate pluripotency without genomic integration.
