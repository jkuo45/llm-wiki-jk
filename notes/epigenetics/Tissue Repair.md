# Tissue Repair

Tissue Repair is the integrated biological response that restores the structure and function of damaged tissue following injury, surgical wounding, or pathological insult. It proceeds through overlapping phases—hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling—and engages resident and recruited cells, the extracellular matrix, and systemic signals. Outcomes range from complete regeneration (in highly labile or facultative tissues) to scar formation (fibrosis) in complex organs.

## Overview

Repair balances the need for rapid barrier restoration against the longer-term goal of recovering normal architecture. The response is staged: immediate clotting limits blood loss; an inflammatory wave clears debris and pathogens; proliferative cells rebuild parenchyma and vasculature; and a remodeling phase matures the new tissue. Epigenetic and metabolic reprogramming of repairing cells shapes whether tissue regenerates or scars.

## Mechanism and Phases

- **Hemostasis and inflammation**: Platelets and the coagulation cascade form a clot; neutrophils and macrophages clear debris and orchestrate subsequent phases via cytokines.
- **Proliferation and [[Wound Healing]]**: [[Fibroblast]]s synthesize collagen and glycosaminoglycans; endothelial cells form new vessels (angiogenesis); epithelial cells migrate to re-epithelialize the wound.
- **Stem and progenitor cell involvement**: Tissue-resident stem cells and pericytes contribute to parenchymal regeneration, particularly in skin, intestine, muscle, and liver.
- **Remodeling**: Matrix metalloproteinases and tissue inhibitors remodel collagen, increasing tensile strength while reducing cellularity.
- **Epigenetic control**: DNA methylation and histone modifications toggle repair genes on and off, influencing fibroblast activation and the fibrosis-versus-regeneration decision.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Effective repair prevents infection and restores function, but dysregulation causes chronic wounds, excessive scarring (keloids, fibrosis), or defective healing in diabetes and aging. Impaired repair underlies morbidity in burns, ulcers, and myocardial infarction. Conversely, aberrant repair signaling can promote fibrosis and tumor stroma.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

[[Regenerative Medicine]] and [[Tissue Engineering]] harness repair biology—via growth factors, scaffolds, cell therapy, and bioactive molecules—to improve outcomes. Research focuses on tipping the balance from scar to regeneration, modulating the inflammatory milieu, and using induced pluripotent or adult stem cells. Agents that remodel the epigenetic landscape are being explored to rejuvenate aged repair capacity.
