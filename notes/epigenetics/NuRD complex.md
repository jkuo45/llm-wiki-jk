# NuRD complex

The Nucleosome Remodeling and Deacetylase ([[NuRD complex]]) is a major multi-subunit chromatin-regulatory machine that couples ATP-dependent [[Nucleosome Remodeling]] with [[Histone Deacetylase]] (HDAC1/2) activity. It is the only complex that intrinsically links these two activities, allowing it to both slide/evict nucleosomes and compact chromatin through deacetylation — a powerful dual mechanism for transcriptional repression and [[Epigenetic Remodeling]].

## Composition

Core subunits include:

- **Chromodomain-helicase-DNA-binding (CHD) ATPases** — CHD3 ([[CHD4]]) or CHD4 (Mi-2β); the motor that remodels nucleosomes.
- **Histone deacetylases** — HDAC1 and HDAC2.
- **Metastasis-associated proteins (MTA1/2/3)** — scaffold/recruitment subunits, often deregulated in [[Cancer]].
- **Retinoblastoma-binding proteins (RBBP4/7)** — bridge to histones H3/H4.
- **GATA zinc-finger proteins (GATAD2A/B)** — mediate gene repression.

Additional associated factors (p66α/β, MBD2/3) modulate substrate specificity and DNA-methylation crosstalk.

## Mechanism of Action

The CHD ATPase uses ATP hydrolysis to slide or eject nucleosomes, while HDAC1/2 remove acetyl groups from H3/H4 tails, promoting chromatin compaction and lowering accessibility to the transcriptional machinery. NuRD is recruited by sequence-specific transcription factors — including [[Sox2]], [[Nanog]], [[REST]], and pRb — and by methylated DNA-binding proteins, positioning it at both active and silent loci. It thus fine-tunes gene expression during development, differentiation, and the pluripotency–differentiation switch.

## Physiological & Developmental Function

NuRD is essential for embryogenesis, X-chromosome inactivation, and lineage commitment. In [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]], it cooperates with the [[Yamanaka Factors]] and [[Oct4]]/[[Sox2]] to reset the epigenetic landscape during [[Cellular Reprogramming]], facilitating the transition between [[Pluripotency]] and [[Differentiation]]. It also maintains [[Quiescence]] programs in stem and progenitor cells by silencing proliferation genes.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Dysregulated NuRD subunits (notably MTA and [[CHD4]]) are implicated in carcinomas, leukemia, and neurodevelopmental disorders. CHD4 mutations occur in Snijders Blok–Campeau syndrome. Because NuRD bridges chromatin compaction to gene silencing, it is a candidate target for epigenetic therapies and a critical node in understanding how [[Reprogramming]] and cellular rejuvenation reset age-associated [[Epigenetics]]. Its interplay with [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] and [[E2F]] further connects chromatin remodeling to cell-cycle control.
