# Sodium Valproate

Sodium Valproate (valproate, VPA) is the sodium salt of valproic acid, a branched short-chain fatty acid used primarily as an antiepileptic and mood-stabilizing agent. Beyond its classical neurological actions, it is a well-characterized [[Histone Deacetylase]] (HDAC) inhibitor and chromatin-modifying agent, making it a prototype epigenetic modulator with both therapeutic and teratogenic implications.

## Overview

Valproate exerts multiple mechanisms: enhancement of GABAergic neurotransmission, blockade of voltage-gated sodium channels, and inhibition of HDAC enzymes. By suppressing HDAC activity, it raises acetylation of histone tails, loosening chromatin and altering the transcription of differentiation, stress-response, and developmental genes. This positions it among [[Epigenetic Modifiers]] with pleiotropic biological effects.

## Mechanism of Action

- **HDAC inhibition**: Valproate inhibits class I and IIa HDACs, increasing histone H3/H4 acetylation and generally promoting a transcriptionally permissive state.
- **GABAergic enhancement**: It raises brain GABA levels by inhibiting GABA transaminase and boosting synthesis.
- **Ion channel effects**: Use-dependent blockade of voltage-gated sodium channels stabilizes hyperexcitable neurons.
- **Differentiation induction**: Chromatin relaxation reactivates silenced developmental programs, driving cellular [[Differentiation]] in embryonic, hematopoietic, and neuronal contexts.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Therapeutically, valproate controls seizures and stabilizes bipolar mood. Epigenetically, its HDAC inhibition has been exploited to promote maturation of induced pluripotent and cancer cells and to sensitize tumors to therapy. Critically, HDAC inhibition during pregnancy disrupts fetal chromatin remodeling, making valproate a potent [[Teratogen]] associated with neural tube defects and neurodevelopmental impairment.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

Valproate is used in epilepsy, bipolar disorder, and migraine prophylaxis, and investigated in oncology (e.g., with histone deacetylation to overcome differentiation blocks) and neurodegeneration. Its teratogenicity has restricted use in women of childbearing potential. As a tool compound, it remains central to studies of [[Histone Modification]]–driven fate changes and combination epigenetic therapy.
