# Liver Regeneration

[[Liver Regeneration]] is the process by which the liver restores lost tissue mass from the remaining parenchyma, primarily through re-entry of quiescent [[Hepatocyte|hepatocytes]] into the [[Cell Cycle]]. Unlike most organs, the liver regenerates by *hypertrophy and hyperplasia of existing cells* rather than by stem-cell replacement, and it does so without fibrosis when injury is controlled.

## Initiation and Phases

After partial hepatectomy (e.g., two-thirds resection in rodents) or toxic injury, hepatocytes exit [[Quiescence]]/[[G0 Phase]] within hours. The priming phase is driven by cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) from Kupffer cells that activate STAT3 and NF-κB. The proliferative phase follows, with growth factors—[[Hepatocyte Growth Factor]] (HGF), TGF-α/EGF, and insulin—inducing cyclin D1 and driving G1→S progression. Biliary and endothelial cells also proliferate to rebuild ducts and sinusoids. A termination phase restores the original mass and halts division via TGF-β and contact inhibition.

## Cellular Players

Mature hepatocytes (polyploid, normally quiescent) are the dominant regenerative source; they can undergo several rounds of division. If hepatocyte proliferation is blocked, facultative **liver progenitor cells** (oval cells) and [[Biliary epithelial cells]] expand and differentiate. Non-parenchymal cells (stellate, endothelial, immune) provide matrix and cytokine support, and the [[Extracellular Matrix]] is transiently remodeled.

## Links to Reprogramming and Quiescence

Liver regeneration recapitulates partial **[[Partial Reprogramming]]** features: transient expression of Yamanaka-like factors and dedifferentiation markers permits proliferation before redifferentiation. The tight coupling of exit from [[Quiescence]] to controlled re-entry is a model for studying how tissues balance renewal with genome stability, and why failed termination yields [[Fibrosis]].

## Clinical Relevance

Understanding regeneration informs living-donor transplantation, post-resection recovery, and therapies for acute liver failure. Chronic injury (viral hepatitis, alcohol, NASH) overwhelms regenerative capacity, shifting the response toward fibrosis and [[Cancer]]. Promoting safe regeneration (HGF mimetics, metabolic support) while avoiding dysplasia is an active research goal.
