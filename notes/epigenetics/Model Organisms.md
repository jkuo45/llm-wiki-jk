# Model Organisms

**Model organisms** are non-human species selected for experimental tractability, genetic malleability, short generation time, and conserved biology, enabling the dissection of fundamental mechanisms that often generalize to humans. In epigenetics and aging research they provide the genetic and pharmacological tools needed to test how chromatin states, DNA methylation, and lifespan are regulated.

## Common Models in Aging and Epigenetics Research

- **[[Saccharomyces cerevisiae]]** (budding yeast) — A unicellular eukaryote with conserved chromatin machinery; used to study replicative aging via daughter-cell scars and the role of [[Sirtuins]] (Sir2) in longevity.
- **[[Caenorhabditis elegans]]** (nematode) — A multicellular organism with a fixed cell lineage and ~3-week lifespan; central to discoveries linking [[Insulin/IGF-1 Signaling]] and chromatin modifiers (e.g., ASH-2 HMT) to lifespan.
- **[[Drosophila melanogaster]]** (fruit fly) — Enables sophisticated genetics and is used to study histone methylation, heterochromatin, and age-related loss of silencing (e.g., position-effect variegation).
- **[[Mus musculus]]** (mouse) — The primary mammalian model for epigenetic inheritance, imprinting, [[DNA Methylation]] editing, and [[Epigenetic Clock]] validation.

## Why They Matter for Epigenetics

Model organisms permit interventions impossible in humans: genome-wide CRISPR screens, inducible transgenes, tissue-specific knockouts, and controlled environmental manipulations. Conserved pathways — the [[mTOR]], [[AMPK]], and insulin-signaling axes, plus chromatin remodelers such as [[Polycomb Group Proteins]] and [[Sirtuins]] — were first linked to lifespan and epigenetic state in these systems. Cross-species comparison reveals which epigenetic features (e.g., [[Histone Modification]] patterns) are conserved and which are species-specific.

## Methodological Advantages and Limits

Strengths include genetic uniformity, short lifespans, and ethical tractability for invasive studies. Limitations include incomplete conservation of aging phenotypes, differences in [[Telomere]] dynamics, and the challenge of translating findings to human physiology. Increasingly, human relevance is bridged using [[Organoids]] and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] derived from patient cells, complemented by non-human primate and companion-animal studies.
