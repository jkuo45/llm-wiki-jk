# Basal Lamina

The **Basal Lamina** is a thin (50–100 nm), sheet-like specialization of the [[Extracellular Matrix]] (ECM) secreted by epithelial, endothelial, and certain mesenchymal cells. It forms the structural interface between parenchymal cells and the underlying connective tissue stroma. The term "basement membrane" is often used synonymously but technically refers to the basal lamina plus an additional reticular lamina (collagen fibrils) visible by light microscopy.

## Composition and Structure

The basal lamina is composed of four core components: **[[Laminin]]** (the major scaffolding protein), **[[Collagen IV|Collagen IV]]** (forming a network via end-to-end associations), **nidogen/entactin** (bridging laminin and collagen IV networks), and **[[Perlecan]]** (a heparan sulfate proteoglycan providing charge selectivity). These components self-assemble into a dense meshwork. Additional tissue-specific components include agrin (neuromuscular junction), fibulin, and SPARC. The basal lamina is anchored to cells primarily through [[Integrin]] receptors (α6β4, α3β1, α7β1) and dystroglycan.

## Physiological Functions

- **Cell adhesion and polarity**: Integrins and dystroglycan bind laminin to establish apicobasal polarity in [[Epithelium|epithelial cells]].
- **Filtration barrier**: In the kidney glomerulus, the glomerular basement membrane (GBM) provides size- and charge-selective filtration together with podocyte slit diaphragms.
- **Tissue organization**: The basal lamina defines tissue boundaries, guides cell migration during development, and sequesters growth factors (e.g., [[FGF]], [[BMP]], [[VEGF]]) via heparan sulfate chains.
- **Stem cell niche**: The basal lamina in tissues such as skeletal muscle (satellite cells), skin (epidermal stem cells), and the intestinal crypt maintains stem cell quiescence and regulates self-renewal.

## Clinical Relevance

Mutations in basal lamina genes cause a spectrum of inherited disorders:

- **Laminin α2 (LAMA2) mutations** cause congenital muscular dystrophy type 1A (MDC1A), characterized by muscle weakness and white matter abnormalities.
- **Collagen IV (COL4A3/4/5) mutations** cause **Alport Syndrome**, a progressive kidney disease characterized by hematuria, proteinuria, sensorineural hearing loss, and glomerular basement membrane thinning/splitting.
- **Integrin α7 (ITGA7) mutations** cause a rare congenital myopathy.
- Autoantibodies against collagen IV (Goodpasture syndrome) and laminin-332 (pemphigoid) target the basal lamina in autoimmune disease.
