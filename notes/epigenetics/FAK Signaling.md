# FAK Signaling

[[FAK Signaling|Focal Adhesion Kinase (FAK) signaling]] is a central integrin-dependent pathway through which cells sense and respond to the physical properties, geometry, and composition of their environment, primarily the [[Extracellular Matrix]]. FAK (encoded by *PTK2*) is a cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase that nucleates a signaling hub at **[[Focal Adhesion|focal adhesions]]**, coupling mechanical adhesion to intracellular survival, motility, and proliferation circuits.

## Activation Mechanism

Engagement of **[[Integrin|integrins]]** with ECM ligands (fibronectin, collagen, laminin) clusters receptors and recruits FAK to the adhesion complex via its FERM domain. Autophosphorylation at Tyr397 creates a Src-homology-2 (SH2) docking site for Src family kinases, which then phosphorylate FAK at Tyr576/577 (activating its kinase domain) and Tyr925, linking FAK to the Grb2/Sos–Ras–ERK cascade. FAK also binds paxillin, p130^Cas^, and talin, organizing the adhesion scaffold that connects to the [[Cytoskeleton]] (actin and microtubules). Mechanical tension across the adhesion reinforces FAK activation, making it a true mechanosensor.

## Downstream Functions

Through these effectors, FAK signaling promotes **[[Cell Migration]]** by coordinating adhesion turnover at the leading edge, enhances survival via PI3K–Akt and suppression of apoptosis, and supports proliferation through ERK and YAP/TAZ engagement. FAK therefore integrates "outside-in" ECM cues with "inside-out" cytoskeletal states. It also cross-talks with growth-factor receptors and the [[Hippo Signaling Pathway]].

## Physiological Function

FAK is essential for embryonic development, wound healing, and angiogenesis. In [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]] and endothelial cells, FAK-mediated adhesion dynamics drive tissue repair and neovessel sprouting. FAK activity helps define whether cells adopt a migratory, proliferative, or quiescent program depending on matrix stiffness and ligand density.

## Pathological Relevance

FAK is frequently overexpressed or hyperactivated in [[Cancer]], where it fuels invasion, metastasis, anoikis resistance, and [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]]. It is a validated drug target: small-molecule FAK inhibitors (e.g., defactinib, VS-4718) are in clinical trials, often combined with chemotherapy or immunotherapy, and can sensitize tumors by disrupting mechanotransductive survival signals.
