# Satellite Cells

[[Satellite Cells]] (SCs), also known as [[Muscle Stem Cell|muscle stem cells]], are small Pax7-expressing mononuclear cells situated between the sarcolemma and the basement membrane of skeletal muscle fibers. They are the canonical muscle-resident stem cell, responsible for postnatal muscle growth, hypertrophy, and regeneration after injury. Identified ultrastructurally by their scant cytoplasm and subsarcolemmal position, they are maintained as a quiescent reservoir that is mobilized on demand.

## Structure & Niche

Quiescent SCs express Pax7 and low Myf5, and anchor to the niche via [[Integrin]] α7β1 binding to laminin. [[Notch signaling]] from myofiber-resident Delta-like 1 maintains quiescence, balanced by inhibitors such as [[Sprouty1]]. The niche integrates cues from the [[Extracellular Matrix]], fibro-adipogenic progenitors, and motor neurons. SCs transition between a deep quiescent state and an activated, proliferative state under injury signals.

## Mechanism of Activation & Repair

Upon damage, [[Hepatocyte Growth Factor]] (HGF), [[Wnt signaling]], and [[IGF-1]] trigger SCs to exit [[Quiescence]]. Activated myoblasts (Pax7+/MyoD+) proliferate, then withdraw from the [[Cell Cycle]] to [[Differentiation|differentiate]] (myogenin expression, myotube fusion) or self-renew to replenish the stem pool. The proliferation–differentiation switch is governed by [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] and [[E2F]], and epigenetic resetting during these transitions involves the [[NuRD complex]], linking repair to [[Epigenetic Remodeling]].

## Physiological Function

SCs supply new myonuclei for growth and repair, preserving muscle mass and function across the lifespan. Their self-renewal sustains a lifelong reserve.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

With [[Aging]], SC number and function decline and niche communication falters, contributing to sarcopenia and impaired [[Tissue Repair]]. Notably, short-term expression of the [[Yamanaka Factors]] in myofibers can rejuvenate the SC niche and enhance regenerative capacity, a leading paradigm in [[Rejuvenation]] research. SC dysfunction underlies muscular dystrophies and cachexia; cell and gene therapies (including [[CRISPR]]-corrected SCs) are in development. Because SCs are non-tumorigenic (unlike [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] that form [[Teratoma|teratomas]]), they are attractive for [[Regenerative Medicine]].
