# Nanog

[[Nanog]] is a homeodomain transcription factor that is a central guardian of [[Pluripotency]] and [[Self-Renewal]] in [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]. It is named after *Tír na nÓg*, the mythical Irish land of the ever-young, reflecting its role in sustaining an indefinitely youthful, undifferentiated state.

## Structure and Regulation

Nanog contains a conserved N-terminal homeodomain that binds DNA at TAAT motifs, and a C-terminal transactivation domain. Its expression is governed by the core pluripotency circuitry: [[Oct4]] and [[Sox2]] bind the *Nanog* enhancer to activate it, while Nanog in turn reinforces Oct4 and Sox2, forming a self-sustaining feed-forward loop. [[Klf4]] and [[Esrrb]] are also required for Nanog activation during reprogramming. Nanog levels are exquisitely tuned—high Nanog drives self-renewal, while its down-regulation permits lineage commitment.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

In [[Cellular Reprogramming]], Nanog is a "late" marker of pluripotency. Although not part of the original [[Yamanaka Factors]] (OSKM), it is essential for stabilizing the pluripotent state: once the Oct4–Sox2–Nanog network is locked in, cells transition from unstable intermediates to a deterministic pluripotent phase. Nanog is also used in alternative cocktails such as OSLN ([[Oct4]], [[Sox2]], [[Lin28]], [[Nanog]]), developed to derive human iPSCs.

Mechanistically, Nanog represses differentiation-promoting genes (e.g., *Gata6*, *Cdx2*) and supports an open chromatin landscape at pluripotency loci. It also interfaces with the epigenome, collaborating with Polycomb complexes to silence lineage-specific programs.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Nanog is expressed in the inner cell mass of the blastocyst and silenced upon differentiation. Aberrant Nanog re-activation is observed in several cancers, where it supports tumor-initiation and therapy resistance. Its role in [[Rejuvenation]] and [[Partial Reprogramming]] of aged cells is an active research area.
