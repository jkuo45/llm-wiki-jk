# Nanog

[[Nanog]] is a homeodomain transcription factor that plays a critical role in maintaining [[Pluripotency]] and self-renewal in [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] and [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]. It is named after "Tír na nÓg," the mythical Irish land of the ever-young.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

In the context of [[Cellular Reprogramming]], [[Nanog]] is often considered a "late" marker of pluripotency. While it was not part of the original [[Yamanaka Factors]] (OSKM), it is essential for the stabilization of the pluripotent state. Once the core pluripotency network—consisting of [[Oct4]], [[Sox2]], and [[Nanog]]—is established through interconnected feedback loops, the cell transitions from an unstable intermediate state to a deterministic phase of pluripotency.

[[Nanog]] is also used in alternative reprogramming cocktails, such as the OSLN combination ([[Oct4]], [[Sox2]], [[Lin28]], and [[Nanog]]), which was developed to derive human iPSCs. Its expression levels are tightly regulated; high levels of [[Nanog]] induce cellular self-renewal, whereas low expression leads to differentiation.

## Connections

- [[Oct4]]: Works alongside [[Nanog]] and [[Sox2]] to form the core regulatory network of pluripotency.
- [[Sox2]]: A key transcription factor that interacts with [[Oct4]] to regulate [[Nanog]] expression.
- [[Lin28]]: Often paired with [[Nanog]] in the OSLN reprogramming cocktail.
- [[Klf4]]: Required for the activation of [[Nanog]] during the reprogramming process.
- [[Pluripotency]]: [[Nanog]] is a fundamental regulator and hallmark of the pluripotent state.
