# Phytochemicals

[[Phytochemicals]] are a diverse [[Chemical Class]] of plant-derived secondary metabolites — including polyphenols, flavonoids, isothiocyanates, terpenoids, and alkaloids — often synthesized by plants in response to environmental stressors such as UV radiation, drought, or pathogen attack. When consumed by animals, many exert biological effects through [[Antioxidant]] activity, modulation of signaling pathways, and direct action on [[Epigenetic Modifiers]], making them relevant to [[Cancer]] prevention, [[Aging]], and metabolic health. The xenohormesis hypothesis posits that organisms have evolved to sense these plant stress signals as proxies for environmental challenge, engaging adaptive resilience pathways.

## Major Classes & Sources

Polyphenols (curcumin from turmeric, resveratrol from grapes, epigallocatechin gallate from green tea, quercetin from onions) feature phenolic rings with radical-scavenging capacity. Isothiocyanates (sulforaphane from cruciferous vegetables) are thiol-reactive electrophiles. Terpenoids (ginsenosides, carnosic acid) and alkaloids (berberine) complete a broad chemodiversity. Many are weak agonists of nutrient-sensing pathways ([[Sirtuin]] activation, AMPK) and of cytoprotective transcription factors such as Nrf2.

## Epigenetic Modulation

Numerous phytochemicals are natural [[Epigenetic Modifiers]]. They inhibit [[Histone Methyltransferase]] and demethylase activities, modulate histone acetyltransferases/deacetylases, and alter DNA methyltransferase (DNMT) function — thereby reshaping marks such as [[H3K27me3]] and DNA methylation at tumor-suppressor loci. Sulforaphane and other isothiocyanates can inhibit histone deacetylases, reactivating silenced genes; polyphenols influence microRNA expression and can shift chromatin toward a less repressive state. These actions underlie interest in dietary phytochemicals as gentle, pleiotropic epigenetic modulators.

## Physiological & Protective Effects

Beyond [[Antioxidant]] scavenging, phytochemicals induce phase-II detoxification enzymes, modulate the [[Sirtuin]] network (e.g., resveratrol activation of [[SIRT1]]), and promote [[Cellular Senescence]] clearance when combined in senolytic strategies. They support metabolic homeostasis and may attenuate inflammaging. Their effects are generally cytoprotective at physiological doses but can be pro-oxidant or toxic at high concentrations.

## Pathology & Research Relevance

Epidemiological and preclinical evidence links phytochemical-rich diets to reduced [[Cancer]] incidence and improved cardiovascular and cognitive aging, though human interventional data are mixed and dose-dependent. In research, phytochemicals calibrate cellular models, sensitize tumors to chemotherapy, and inform the design of synthetic epigenetic drugs. Their [[Tumorigenicity]]-suppressing properties are studied in chemoprevention, while their impact on [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] and reprogramming efficiency is an emerging topic.
