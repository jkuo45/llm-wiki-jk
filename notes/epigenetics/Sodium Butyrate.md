# Sodium Butyrate

**Sodium Butyrate** (NaB) is the sodium salt of [[Butyrate|butyric acid]], a short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) produced by gut microbial fermentation of dietary fiber. It functions as an endogenous [[Histone Deacetylase Inhibitor|histone deacetylase inhibitor (HDACi)]] and is a widely used tool compound in epigenetic and senescence research.

## Mechanism of Action

Sodium butyrate inhibits class I (HDAC1, HDAC2, HDAC3, HDAC8) and class IIa (HDAC4, HDAC5, HDAC7, HDAC9) [[HDAC|histone deacetylases]] with an IC50 of 50–200 µM. By blocking HDAC activity, NaB increases [[Histone Acetylation|histone H3 and H4 acetylation]] (particularly H3K9ac, H3K14ac, H4K5ac, H4K12ac), promoting open [[Euchromatin|chromatin]] and transcriptional activation.

## Effects on Senescence

At 1–5 mM in cell culture, sodium butyrate induces potent growth arrest and features of [[Cellular Senescence]]:

- **Mechanism:** Transcriptional activation of [[p21|CDKN1A/p21]] through increased promoter [[H3K9ac]]/[[H4K12ac]], independent of [[p53]].
- **Phenotype:** [[SA-beta-gal]] positivity, flattened morphology, [[SAHF]] formation.
- **[[SASP]] profile:** Induces [[IL-8]] and [[CXCL1]] but represses [[IL-6]] in some cell types — a distinct SASP signature from genotoxic senescence.
- **Cell type specificity:** More potent in [[HCT116]], [[HT29]], [[HeLa]]; weaker in primary [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]].

## Physiological Relevance

Butyrate is produced at millimolar concentrations in the colonic lumen, where it is the primary energy source for colonocytes and shapes the colonic [[Epigenome]]. At these concentrations, it:
- Promotes [[Histone Acetylation]] at tumor suppressor loci.
- Suppresses proliferation of genetically damaged cells via senescence induction.
- Contributes to the anti-cancer effects of high-fiber diets.

## Research Applications

NaB is used at 2–5 mM for 24–48 h to induce [[Histone Acetylation|hyperacetylation]] and study HDAC-dependent gene regulation. It is the least expensive HDACi and a common positive control in [[HDAC activity assays]].

## Limitations

NaB is rapidly metabolized ([[β-oxidation]] in most cells), limiting the duration of HDAC inhibition. It also has off-target effects: GPR41/43 ([[GPCR]]) activation at low µM, and modulation of cellular metabolism via β-oxidation. [[Trichostatin A]] (TSA; IC50 ~1 nM) or [[Suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid|SAHA]] are preferred when isoform selectivity is not required.
