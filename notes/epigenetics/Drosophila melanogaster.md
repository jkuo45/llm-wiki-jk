# Drosophila melanogaster

**Drosophila melanogaster**, the common fruit fly, is a premier [[Model Organisms|Model Organism]] in genetics, developmental biology, and aging research. Its short generation time (~10 days), prolific progeny, and compact, fully sequenced genome—combined with unmatched genetic toolkits (P-element/transposon mutagenesis, Gal4-UAS, CRISPR)—make it ideal for dissecting conserved pathways.

## Advantages as a Model

Drosophila shares core signaling pathways ([[Wnt Signaling]], Hedgehog, Notch, [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway]], insulin/IGF, [[mTOR]]) and chromatin machinery with mammals. Its transparent embryos allow live imaging of development, and conserved aging phenotypes (locomotor decline, cognitive loss, oxidative damage) enable longevity screens.

## Role in Genetics and Development

Landmark contributions include the identification of the [[Polycomb Group Proteins]] (PcG) and Trithorax group systems that maintain [[Epigenetic Remodeling]] of Hox gene expression—discoveries foundational to epigenetic repression. The discovery of miRNA mechanisms and planar cell polarity also originated in flies.

## Role in Aging Research

Flies are a leading system for [[Aging]] genetics. Screens established the [[mTOR]] and [[Sirtuin]] pathways, insulin/IGF signaling, and [[Caloric Restriction]] as conserved longevity regulators. Notably, the fly Sirtuin dSir2 links [[Silent Mating-Type Information Regulation]]-like chromatin silencing to lifespan, and PcG-mediated heterochromatin loss is associated with age-related decline.
