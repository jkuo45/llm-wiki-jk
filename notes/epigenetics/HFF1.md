# HFF1

[[HFF1]] (Human Foreskin Fibroblast 1) is an immortalized diploid fibroblast line derived from neonatal human foreskin. It is among the most widely used primary-like fibroblast models in stem-cell and [[Reprogramming]] research, serving both as a somatic cell source for generating [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) and as a feeder layer that supports the growth of pluripotent cultures.

## Origin & Culture Characteristics

HFF1 cells are adherent, spindle-shaped [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]] that proliferate readily in standard media (e.g., DMEM with serum) and retain a stable karyotype over many passages when handled appropriately. Their accessibility, robustness, and low baseline transformation make them a preferred substrate for reproducible reprogramming and differentiation experiments.

## Role as Feeder Layers

Irradiated or mitotically inactive HFF1 cells secrete extracellular matrix components and soluble factors (FGF2, LIF, BMP antagonists) that maintain pluripotency and support survival of human embryonic stem cells and iPSCs plated on top. Feeder-derived signals complement defined media and reduce spontaneous differentiation during expansion.

## Role in Reprogramming

HFF1 fibroblasts are a canonical somatic starting population for [[Yamanaka Factors]]-mediated reprogramming. Their efficient conversion to iPSCs has enabled systematic studies of the molecular trajectory of [[Pluripotency]] acquisition, including the mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition (MET), metabolic shifts, and epigenetic remodeling that accompany [[Reprogramming]]. Because HFF1 is genetically distinct from most disease-derived lines, it also provides a normal comparator in isogenic and disease-modeling studies.

## Research Applications

Beyond reprogramming, HFF1 cells are used in senescence, wound-healing, ECM production, and toxicology assays. Their genome has been sequenced as a reference, and they appear in high-throughput screening of senolytics and anti-fibrotic compounds.
