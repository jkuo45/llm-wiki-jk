# Phenylbutyrate

## Overview

4-Phenylbutyrate (4-PBA, PB) is a short-chain aromatic fatty acid originally approved by the FDA for the treatment of urea cycle disorders (UCDs), where it serves as an alternative pathway for nitrogen excretion via conjugation with glutamine to form phenylacetylglutamine. Beyond its metabolic role, PB has emerged as a pleiotropic epigenetic modulator with class I/IIA histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitory activity, chemical chaperone properties, and the ability to alleviate endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress. These properties have prompted investigation across neurodegenerative diseases (ALS, Huntington's disease, Parkinson's disease), protein misfolding disorders, and various cancers.

## Mechanism & Function

### HDAC Inhibition and Epigenetic Modulation

PB inhibits class I (HDAC1, HDAC2, HDAC3, HDAC8) and class IIA (HDAC4, HDAC5, HDAC7, HDAC9) HDACs at millimolar concentrations (IC₅₀ ~0.5-5 mM). This inhibition increases histone acetylation at promoters of stress-response genes, notably *HSP70* (HSPA1A) and *HSP40*, enhancing the heat shock response. The resulting chromatin relaxation facilitates transcription of proteostasis networks.

### Chemical Chaperone and ER Stress Reduction

PB acts as a low-molecular-weight chemical chaperone that stabilizes protein conformation in the ER lumen. It reduces ER stress by:
- Decreasing accumulation of misfolded proteins
- Attenuating the unfolded protein response (UPR) — reducing IRE1α, PERK, and ATF6 signaling
- Lowering CHOP (DDIT3) expression, a pro-apoptotic transcription factor induced by chronic ER stress
- Enhancing ER-associated degradation (ERAD) capacity

### Ammonia Scavenging (Urea Cycle Disorders)

In UCDs, PB conjugates with glutamine via phenylacetyl-CoA to form phenylacetylglutamine (PAGN), which is renally excreted. This provides an alternative nitrogen disposal pathway, bypassing the defective urea cycle. Typical dosing: 450-600 mg/kg/day divided TID.

## Clinical Relevance

### Urea Cycle Disorders (Approved Indication)

PB (as sodium phenylbutyrate/Buphenyl®, glycerol phenylbutyrate/Ravicti®) is standard-of-care for NAGS, CPS1, ASS1 (citrullinemia), and ASL (argininosuccinic aciduria) deficiencies. Long-term data demonstrate improved survival and neurocognitive outcomes when initiated early.

### Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)

Preclinical models (SOD1^G93A^ mice) show PB extends survival, reduces motor neuron loss, and decreases mutant SOD1 aggregation. Phase II trials (NCT00997083) reported safety and target engagement (Hsp70 induction in CSF), though Phase III has not proceeded. Combination with taurursodiol (AMX0035) showed promise in CENTAUR trial.

### Huntington's Disease

In R6/2 and YAC128 mouse models, PB reduces mutant huntingtin aggregates, improves motor phenotypes, and extends lifespan via HDAC inhibition and Hsp70 upregulation. Human trials (NCT00212316, NCT00747826) showed CSF Hsp70 induction but limited clinical benefit at tested doses.

### Cancer

PB induces cell cycle arrest (p21^CIP1^ upregulation), apoptosis (via BAX/BAK), and differentiation in leukemia, hepatocellular carcinoma, and pancreatic cancer models. It synergizes with DNMT inhibitors (azacitidine) and proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib). Clinical trials in AML and MDS show modest single-agent activity; combination strategies ongoing.
