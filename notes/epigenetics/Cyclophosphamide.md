# Cyclophosphamide

**Cyclophosphamide** is a nitrogen mustard-based [[Alkylating agent]] and a prodrug that requires metabolic bioactivation to exert its cytotoxic and immunosuppressive effects. It is widely used in oncology and immunology due to its ability to cause DNA damage, leading to cell cycle arrest and death.

## Mechanism of Action

The biological activity of cyclophosphamide is dependent on its conversion into highly reactive metabolites.

1. **Bioactivation**: Cyclophosphamide is a prodrug that must be activated by hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes (primarily [[CYP2B6]] and [[CYP3A4]]). The initial oxidation produces 4-hydroxycyclophosphamide, which exists in equilibrium with its tautomer, aldophosphamide. 

2. **Formation of Active Metabolites**: Aldophosphamide undergoes spontaneous β-elimination to yield **phosphoramide mustard**—the primary DNA-crosslinking species—and the byproduct **acrolein**.

3. **DNA Alkylation and Cross-linking**: Phosphoramide mustard is highly reactive and targets the N7 position of guanine residues in [[DNA]]. This leads to the formation of both interstrand and intrastrand DNA cross-links. These cross-links physically prevent the separation of the DNA strands, which is essential for [[DNA Replication]] and [[Transcription]].

4. **Cellular Outcomes**: The resulting DNA damage and subsequent stalling of replication and transcription forks trigger a robust [[DNA Damage Response]] (DDR). Depending on the extent of the damage, this can lead to:
    - **Apoptosis**: Through the activation of p53-mediated cell death pathways.
    - **Senescence**: Through the induction of [[Stress-Induced Premature Senescence]].

## Clinical Indications

Cyclophosphamide is utilized across several medical domains:

- **Oncology (Chemotherapy)**: It is a component of numerous combination regimens for various cancers, including:
    - **Lymphomas**: Used in regimens like CHOP (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone).
    - **Breast Cancer**: A core agent in several protocols (e.g., AC regimen).
    - **Other Solid Tumors**: Used in the management of ovarian, sarcoma, and neuroblastoma.
- **Immunology (Immunosuppression)**: At lower doses, it is used to manage autoimmune diseases by suppressing the proliferation of lymphocytes. Key indications include:
    - **Lupus Nephritis**
    - **Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis** (Wegener's)
    - **Myasthenia Gravis**
- **Hematology**: Used as a conditioning regimen for bone marrow and stem cell transplantation.

## Side Effects and Management

The clinical use of cyclophosphamide is often limited by its toxicity profile:

- **Myelosuppression**: The dose-limiting toxicity, characterized by leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia, resulting from the suppression of hematopoietic stem cells.
- **Hemorrhagic Cystitis**: This is caused by the accumulation of **acrolein** in the urinary bladder. To prevent this, the drug **[[Mesna]]** is frequently co-administered to bind and detoxify acrolein in the urine.
- **Gastrointestinal Toxicity**: Nausea, vomiting, and mucositis.
- **Reproductive Toxicity**: Potential for infertility (azoospermia and ovarian failure) and long-term risks of secondary malignancies.
- **Cardiotoxicity**: High doses can lead to myopericarditis and other cardiac complications.
