# Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition

[[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EMT) is a conserved developmental program in which polarized epithelial cells dismantle their cell–cell junctions and apical–basal polarity, undergo cytoskeletal reorganization, and acquire the motile, invasive, and extracellular-matrix-producing phenotype of mesenchymal cells. First described by Elizabeth Hay in the context of embryogenesis, EMT is now understood as a reversible, biochemically graded (partial) transition rather than a binary switch, with cells occupying hybrid epithelial/mesenchymal (E/M) states that retain elements of both programs.

## Mechanism and Transcriptional Regulation

EMT is orchestrated by a core set of EMT-inducing transcription factors (EMT-TFs) that repress epithelial genes and activate mesenchymal ones. Central drivers include the zinc-finger factors [[Snail]] (SNAI1) and [[Slug]] (SNAI2), the zinc-finger E-box binding homeobox proteins [[Zeb1]] and ZEB2, and the basic helix–loop–helix factor TWIST1. These factors directly repress the master epithelial adhesion molecule E-cadherin ([[CDH1]]) by binding E-box elements in its promoter, while up-regulating N-cadherin ([[CDH2]]), vimentin, fibronectin, and N-cadherin–dependent motility.

Upstream signaling pathways converge on these TFs. The [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway]] is a potent and canonical EMT inducer: TGF-β activates SMAD2/3, which cooperates with SNAI1 and ZEB1, and also engages non-SMAD routes (PI3K/AKT, MAPK, Rho GTPases) to reinforce the mesenchymal state. Additional drivers include receptor tyrosine kinase signaling (EGF, HGF via [[c-Met]]), Wnt/β-catenin, Notch, and hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF-1α). Epigenetic plasticity underlies EMT: EMT-TFs recruit Polycomb and histone-modifying complexes that silence epithelial loci, and the transition is associated with widespread [[DNA Methylation]] and chromatin remodeling.

## Physiological Function

In normal development, EMT enables gastrulation (formation of the three germ layers), neural crest delamination, heart valve formation, and somite-derived mesenchyme. It is also physiologically engaged during wound healing, where fibroblasts and epithelial cells transiently acquire mesenchymal features to close defects, and during tissue fibrosis as a pathological exaggeration of this repair program.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

In [[Cellular Reprogramming]], suppression of EMT is essential for generating [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) from mesenchymal somatic cells such as [[Fibroblasts]]. Because pluripotent stem cells are epithelial, somatic cells must instead undergo the reverse [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]] (MET) to reach [[Pluripotency]]. The [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway]] poses a major roadblock to reprogramming; inhibiting it (e.g., with SB431542) or its downstream effectors dramatically improves efficiency. The [[Yamanaka Factors]] (OSKM) facilitate MET: [[Oct4]] and [[Sox2]] repress [[Snail]] and [[Zeb1]], while [[c-Myc]] suppresses TGF-β receptor expression.

## Pathological Relevance: Cancer and Metastasis

EMT is co-opted by carcinomas to disseminate. By adopting a mesenchymal phenotype, epithelial tumor cells invade the basement membrane, survive anoikis, enter the circulation, and seed distant metastases. EMT also promotes stem-cell-like properties, therapy resistance, and immune evasion in the tumor microenvironment, making it a focal point for anti-metastatic drug discovery.
