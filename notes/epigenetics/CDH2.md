# CDH2

CDH2 (located at chromosome 18q12.1) encodes **N-cadherin** (neuronal cadherin), a 130 kDa classical type I cadherin that mediates calcium-dependent homophilic cell-cell adhesion. Unlike [[E-cadherin]] (encoded by [[CDH1]]), which is expressed in epithelial tissues, N-cadherin is predominantly expressed in neural tissue, cardiac muscle, and mesenchymal cells. Its dynamic regulation during development and its aberrant upregulation during the [[Cadherin Switch]] in cancer invasion make it a central player in both developmental morphogenesis and tumor progression.

## Structure & Domains

N-cadherin shares the canonical cadherin architecture: five extracellular cadherin (EC) repeats that coordinate Ca²⁺ ions to form rigid rod-like structures, a single transmembrane domain, and a cytoplasmic domain that binds p120-catenin and β-catenin/plakoglobin for linkage to the actin cytoskeleton. The outermost EC1 domain provides specificity for homophilic N-cadherin trans-interactions, though heterophilic binding with [[E-cadherin]] or R-cadherin can occur under certain conditions. N-cadherin is distinguished by a shorter, more hydrophobic signal peptide and glycosylation sites that influence its trafficking and stability.

## Function in Neural and Cardiac Development

In the developing nervous system, N-cadherin mediates neurite outgrowth, axon guidance, and synaptic plasticity. It is enriched at synaptic junctions where it stabilizes nascent contacts and recruits presynaptic and postsynaptic machinery. N-cadherin engagement activates [[FGF Receptor]] signaling to promote neurite extension, and its adhesive function is essential for retinal layering and neural tube formation. Knockout of CDH2 in mice is embryonic lethal (∼E10) due to severe cardiac defects, including failure of myocardial compaction and impaired intercalated disc formation, underscoring N-cadherin's essential role in [[Adherens Junction]] integrity and electrical coupling in the heart.

## The Cadherin Switch in EMT and Cancer

The **[[Cadherin Switch]]** — downregulation of [[E-cadherin]] and concomitant upregulation of N-cadherin — is a hallmark of [[EMT]] and is associated with increased cell motility, invasion, and metastasis. N-cadherin promotes a mesenchymal phenotype through several mechanisms:

- **Homophilic adhesion to stromal N-cadherin** enables cancer cells to migrate along fibroblasts and endothelial cells, facilitating invasion.
- **N-cadherin/FGFR signaling** activates [[MAPK]] and [[PI3K]]/[[AKT]] pathways, promoting survival and proliferation in the mesenchymal state.
- **N-cadherin/β-catenin signaling** can contribute to Wnt-independent transcriptional activation.
- **N-cadherin/p120-catenin** interactions regulate Rho GTPases ([[RhoA]], [[Rac1]], [[Cdc42]]) to remodel the actin cytoskeleton and drive protrusive motility.

Upregulation of N-cadherin is documented in breast, prostate, bladder, melanoma, and lung cancers, and correlates with high grade, metastasis, and poor prognosis. N-cadherin antagonists, including ADH-1 (linear peptide) and monoclonal antibodies, have been evaluated in preclinical and early clinical trials as anti-invasive therapies.

## Role in Reprogramming and Pluripotency

During [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cell]] (iPSC) reprogramming with [[Yamanaka Factors]] ([[OCT4]], [[SOX2]], [[KLF4]], [[c-MYC]]), the mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition (MET) is a critical early step that requires downregulation of N-cadherin and upregulation of [[E-cadherin]]. CDH2 represses MET, and its silencing via promoter methylation or transcriptional repression facilitates the acquisition of epithelial morphology and pluripotency gene expression. Conversely, N-cadherin expression is high in certain pluripotent states and is dynamically regulated during differentiation.
