# Restriction Point

The [[Restriction Point]] (R), known as Start in yeast, is a checkpoint in late [[G1 Phase]] of the animal [[Cell Cycle]] at which the cell becomes irreversibly "committed" to a round of division. Once a cell passes R, it no longer requires extracellular mitogenic signals (growth factors, nutrients) to complete S phase, mitosis, and cytokinesis — passage therefore converts a conditional, signal-dependent state into an autonomous proliferative program.

## Molecular Control

The restriction point is governed by the [[RB1]] (retinoblastoma) tumor-suppressor pathway. In early G1, hypophosphorylated Rb binds and represses E2F transcription factors, suppressing S-phase genes. Sequential activation of [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]] complexes — cyclin D–CDK4/6 and cyclin E–CDK2 — phosphorylates Rb, releasing E2F to drive transcription of DNA-replication machinery, cyclin A, and additional CDKs. Phosphorylated Rb can no longer restrain E2F, locking the cell into cycle progression past R.

## Integration of External Cues

Growth-factor availability, nutrient status, and stress signals modulate CDK activity upstream of R. Mitogens induce cyclin D; antiproliferative signals (TGF-β, contact inhibition, DNA damage) oppose CDK activation and can arrest cells before R. Cells that fail to receive adequate signals before R exit to a quiescent (G0) state, from which they can re-enter the cycle upon renewed stimulation.

## Physiological & Pathological Significance

The restriction point is a canonical tumor-suppressive gate. Loss of Rb, overexpression of cyclin D or CDK4/6, or inactivation of CDK inhibitors (p16^INK4a, p21, p27) allows cells to bypass R and proliferate independently of external control — a hallmark of cancer. Conversely, pharmacological CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib) enforce R-arrest and are approved cancer therapeutics. Understanding R is also central to stem-cell expansion and senescence, where passage through or arrest at R determines proliferation versus quiescence.
