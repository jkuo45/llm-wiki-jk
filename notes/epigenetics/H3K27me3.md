# H3K27me3

[[H3K27me3]] (trimethylated histone H3 at lysine 27) is a repressive chromatin mark deposited by Polycomb repressive complex 2 (PRC2) and recognized by Polycomb repressive complex 1 (PRC1). It is a defining feature of transcriptionally silent or "poised" chromatin and plays central roles in development, [[Gene Silencing]], and the maintenance of cell identity. Levels of H3K27me3 change significantly with [[Aging]] — increasing at some loci (consolidating repression) and decreasing at others (permitting aberrant activation), a pattern linked to age-associated transcriptional drift.

## Deposition & Erasure

The catalytic subunit of PRC2 is the [[Histone Methyltransferase]] [[EZH2]] (and its paralog EZH1), which transfers three methyl groups from S-adenosylmethionine to H3K27. H3K27me3 is read by the chromodomain of PRC1 subunits (e.g., CBX proteins), which in turn ubiquitinates H2AK119 (H2AK119ub) to compact chromatin and block transcription elongation. The mark can be erased by demethylases of the KDM6 family (UTX/KDM6A, JMJD3/KDM6B), which remove methylation at K27, reactivating silenced genes during differentiation and reprogramming.

## Genomic Distribution & Function

H3K27me3 typically occupies large contiguous domains (Polycomb domains) at the promoters and gene bodies of developmental regulatory genes — homeotic clusters (Hox), transcription factors, and signaling molecules. Many such loci are "bivalent" in embryonic and [[Progenitor Cell]] populations, carrying both H3K27me3 (repressive) and H3K4me3 (active) marks, keeping them poised for rapid activation upon lineage commitment. By keeping alternative fates silent, H3K27me3 stabilizes cell identity and prevents inappropriate transdifferentiation.

## Physiological & Developmental Roles

During [[Gastrulation]] and organogenesis, dynamic gain and loss of H3K27me3 orchestrate the orderly activation of lineage programs while silencing pluripotency and competing fate genes. In somatic cell reprogramming toward [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]], H3K27me3 must be removed from endogenous Oct4/Sox2/Nanog loci to permit reactivation, a barrier overcome in part by KDM6B or by the [[Yamanaka Factors]] themselves.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Aberrant H3K27me3 is implicated in [[Cancer]] and developmental disease. EZH2 gain-of-function mutations (e.g., Y641) in lymphoma, and EZH2 overexpression in prostate and breast cancers, broaden repressive domains and silence tumor suppressors. Conversely, loss of PRC2 components or H3K27me3 reduction de-represses developmental oncogenes. EZH2 inhibitors (tazemetostat) are approved for epithelioid sarcoma and relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma, validating PRC2 as a therapeutic target. H3K27M onco-mutations in diffuse midline glioma block methyltransferase activity and globally deplete H3K27me3, driving a lethal pediatric tumor.
