# SOX gene family

The [[SOX gene family]] (SRY-related HMG box) encodes [[Transcription Factor|transcription factors]] defined by a conserved ~80-amino-acid high-mobility-group (HMG) box that binds minor groove DNA and bends the helix, thereby facilitating transcriptional activation or repression. SOX proteins are master regulators of [[Embryonic Development]], tissue specification, and stem-cell identity.

## Classification & Structure

Vertebrate SOX genes are grouped into subgroups (A–J) based on HMG-box homology. The HMG domain confers both DNA binding and architectural roles, often recruiting partner factors (e.g., Pax, Oct, enhancer-binding proteins) to stabilize enhanceosome assemblies. Many SOX proteins lack intrinsic strong activation domains and function as pioneer-like modulators that open chromatin and license target-gene expression.

## Key Members & Developmental Roles

- **Sox2, Sox1, Sox3 (group B)** — central to neural development and to the maintenance of [[Pluripotency]]; Sox2 is a core [[Yamanaka Factors|reprogramming factor]].
- **Sox9 (group E)** — master regulator of chondrogenesis, testis determination, and neural crest; mutations cause campomelic dysplasia.
- **Sox17 (group F)** — endoderm specification and vascular development.
- **Sox10 (group E)** — neural-crest-derived glia and melanocyte development.

## Mechanistic Function

SOX factors cooperate with tissue-specific partners to activate lineage programs while repressing alternatives, thus enforcing cell-fate decisions. During reprogramming, Sox2 together with Oct4, Klf4, and c-Myc re-establishes the pluripotent network by remodeling chromatin and inducing core stemness genes. SOX proteins also interact with epigenetic modifiers, linking sequence-specific binding to broader [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] state.

## Pathological & Research Relevance

SOX mutations and misexpression are implicated in developmental disorders, cancers (e.g., Sox2 amplification in squamous cancers, Sox9 in chondrosarcoma), and sex-development defects. Their roles make SOX factors important both as lineage markers and as engineering tools in stem-cell and regenerative research.
