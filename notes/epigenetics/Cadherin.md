# Cadherin

[[Cadherin]]s are a superfamily of transmembrane proteins that mediate calcium-dependent cell-cell adhesion. They are fundamental to the formation and maintenance of tissues in multicellular organisms, playing central roles in cell sorting during development, maintaining tissue architecture, and mediating essential intracellular signaling pathways.

## Overview

The adhesive function of cadherins is primarily driven by their extracellular domains. These domains contain multiple repeats that coordinate calcium ions ($Ca^{2+}$). The presence of calcium is essential, as it rigidifies the extracellular portion of the protein, enabling it to engage in **homophilic binding**—the interaction between cadherin molecules of the same type on adjacent cells.

Cadherins are categorized into several types based on their structure and function. The **classical cadherins** are the most well-characterized and include:
- **[[E-cadherin]]**: Predominantly expressed in epithelial tissues.
- **[[N-cadherin]]** (encoded by [[CDH2]]): Primarily expressed in neural and mesenchymal cells.
- **[[P-cadherin]]**: Often found in the basal layers of the epidermis.

## Mechanism and Function

The role of cadherins extends far beyond simple "molecular glue." They act as dynamic hubs that connect the extracellular environment to the intracellular machinery.

### 1. Linkage to the Cytoskeleton
The cytoplasmic tail of classical cadherins serves as a scaffold for a complex of adaptor proteins known as the **[[Catenin]]** family. This complex establishes a critical mechanical link to the **[[Actin Cytoskeleton]]**:
- **[[Beta-catenin]]** and **[[Plakoglobin]]** bind directly to the distal end of the cadherin tail.
- **[[Alpha-catenin]]** then links the cadherin-catenin complex to actin filaments.

This connection allows for the transmission of mechanical forces between cells and enables the cytoskeleton to remodel the junction in response to environmental stimuli.

### 2. Cell Sorting and Morphogenesis
During embryonic development, cadherins are essential for **cell sorting**. Cells expressing different types or levels of cadherins will preferentially adhere to each other, allowing for the organized assembly of diverse tissues and organs. This differential adhesion is a fundamental principle of morphogenesis.

### 3. Signaling and Contact Inhibition
Cadherin-mediated contact is a key regulator of cell behavior. Through the sequestration of proteins like [[Beta-catenin]], cadherin junctions help maintain cell polarity and regulate the **[[Wnt Signaling]]** pathway. Furthermore, cadherin-mediated contact is a primary driver of **[[Contact Inhibition]]**, a process that prevents cells from over-proliferating when they are in physical contact with their neighbors.

## Clinical Relevance

Dysregulation of cadherin-mediated adhesion is a major driver of several pathological states:

- **Cancer Metastasis and EMT**: The loss of E-cadherin-mediated adhesion is a hallmark of the **[[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EMT)**. During EMT, cells often undergo a "cadherin switch," where E-cadherin is downregulated and N-cadherin is upregulated. This switch facilitates the transition from a stationary epithelial phenotype to a migratory, invasive mesenchymal phenotype, promoting cancer cell dissemination and metastasis.
- **Developmental Disorders**: Mutations in cadherin genes can lead to severe defects in tissue morphogenesis, affecting organ development and the structural integrity of various tissues.
- **Tumorigenesis**: Alterations in cadherin signaling can contribute to uncontrolled cell proliferation by disrupting contact inhibition and activating oncogenic pathways.
