# Roscovitine

Roscovitine, also known as seliciclib (CYC202), is a synthetic purine analog that acts as a broad-spectrum [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]] (CDK) inhibitor. It was among the first CDK inhibitors advanced toward the clinic and remains a widely used chemical probe for CDK-dependent processes, including cell-cycle control, transcription, and neuronal survival.

## Overview

Roscovitine competes for the ATP-binding site of several CDKs, with notable potency against CDK2 (cell-cycle regulation), CDK5 (neuronal function), CDK7 and CDK9 (transcriptional CDKs). By inhibiting these kinases, it disrupts both mitotic progression and RNA polymerase II–dependent transcription, producing antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic effects.

## Mechanism of Action

- **CDK2 inhibition**: Blocks cyclin E/A–CDK2 activity, arresting cells at the G1/S and G2/M transitions of the [[Cell Cycle]].
- **Transcriptional CDK inhibition**: Inhibition of CDK7/9 suppresses phosphorylation of the RNA polymerase II C-terminal domain, curtailing global and stress-induced transcription (including anti-apoptotic messengers).
- **CDK5 inhibition**: In neurons, CDK5 dysregulation contributes to toxicity; roscovitine can attenuate CDK5-driven pathology, motivating neuroprotection studies.
- **[[Apoptosis]] induction**: Loss of survival transcription and cell-cycle arrest lowers the threshold for programmed cell death in malignant cells.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Because CDKs govern proliferation and transcription universally, roscovitine is broadly antiproliferative. Its selectivity profile—distinct from selective CDK4/6 agents such as ribociclib—makes it useful where transcriptional CDK blockade is desirable, such as in cancers driven by transcriptional addiction (e.g., certain lung, breast, and hematologic malignancies). As a [[CDK Inhibitor]], it exemplifies the pleiotropic consequences of CDK targeting.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

Roscovitine/seliciclib entered early-phase trials in non-small cell lung cancer, breast cancer, and other tumors, and has been studied for antiviral and neuroprotective effects. Although its clinical development has been limited by tolerability and selectivity, it remains a cornerstone research tool for dissecting CDK function and for combination strategies with DNA-damaging [[Chemotherapy]].
