# Translation

[[Translation]] is the central biological process in which [[Ribosome]]s in the cytoplasm (or on the rough endoplasmic reticulum) synthesize proteins from the nucleotide sequence of [[mRNA]], executing the final step of [[Gene Expression]] after [[Transcription]] of DNA into RNA. Collectively, transcription plus translation constitute [[Protein Synthesis]]. In eukaryotes, mature mRNA is exported from the nucleus to the cytosol, where the genetic code is decoded into a polypeptide chain.

## Stages & Machinery

Translation proceeds in three phases. **Initiation** begins with assembly of the 43S pre-initiation complex (40S subunit, eIFs, Met-tRNA_i) that scans the 5′ cap to the start codon AUG, positioning the 60S subunit to form the 80S ribosome. **Elongation** cycles through codon recognition by aminoacyl-tRNA (delivered by eEF1A), peptide-bond formation by the ribosomal peptidyl transferase center, and translocation (eEF2), extending the chain at the rate of several residues per second. **Termination** occurs when a stop codon enters the A site; release factors (eRF1/eRF3) hydrolyze the bond, freeing the nascent protein for folding.

## Regulation

Translation is a major control point for cell growth and is tightly regulated by nutrient- and stress-sensing pathways. mTORC1 stimulates initiation (via 4E-BP and S6K), while energy stress (AMPK) and unfolded-protein stress repress it. Many mRNAs carry regulatory 5′/3′ UTRs bound by RNA-binding proteins and microRNAs (e.g., [[let-7 microRNA]]) that tune efficiency. Specific transcripts — such as those driving the [[G1 Phase]] and proliferation — are preferentially translated under growth signaling, linking translation control to the [[Cell Cycle]].

## Physiological Function

By determining which proteins are made and in what amount, translation converts the transcriptome into the functional proteome that maintains homeostasis, stress responses, and differentiation. Differential translation of stored maternal mRNAs governs early embryogenesis before the zygotic genome activates.

## Pathology & Research Relevance

Dysregulated translation underlies [[Cancer]], where oncogenic [[RAS]]/PI3K–AKT–mTOR signaling drives global and mRNA-specific upregulation supporting proliferation. Mutations in ribosomal proteins and translation factors cause ribosomopathies; viral hijacking of the translational apparatus is central to infection. Translation inhibitors (e.g., cycloheximide, rapamycin via mTOR) are research and therapeutic tools. Conversely, translational reprogramming during cellular [[Reprogramming]] and in [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] is an active field, as is the interplay between translation and [[Cellular Senescence]].
