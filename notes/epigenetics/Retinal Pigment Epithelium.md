# Retinal Pigment Epithelium

## Overview

The retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) is a monolayer of hexagonal, heavily pigmented epithelial cells situated between the photoreceptor outer segments and Bruch's membrane/choriocapillaris. As a critical component of the blood-retina barrier, the RPE performs multiple essential functions: phagocytosis of shed photoreceptor outer segments (POS), regeneration of 11-cis-retinal via the visual cycle (RPE65), transepithelial transport of ions, nutrients, and metabolites, secretion of growth factors (PEDF, VEGF), and absorption of stray light by melanin granules. RPE dysfunction is central to the pathogenesis of age-related macular degeneration (AMD), retinitis pigmentosa, and other retinal dystrophies.

## Structure & Microanatomy

- **Apical microvilli**: Interdigitate with photoreceptor outer segments; mediate phagocytosis via αvβ5 integrin and MERTK signaling
- **Basal lamina**: Secretes collagen IV, laminin, and fibronectin forming Bruch's membrane
- **Tight junctions**: ZO-1, occludin, claudin-19 form the outer blood-retina barrier
- **Melanosomes**: Mature melanosomes absorb scattered light; lipofuscin accumulates with age (A2E, lipofuscin fluorophores)
- **Mitochondria-rich**: High metabolic demand for phagocytosis and visual cycle

## Key Functions

### Phagocytosis of Photoreceptor Outer Segments

Photoreceptors shed ~10% of their outer segments daily. RPE recognizes phosphatidylserine on POS via MFG-E8 and αvβ5 integrin, triggering MERTK-dependent phagocytosis. Lysosomal degradation requires RPE65, LRAT, and cathepsins. Defects cause retinitis pigmentosa (MERTK, RPE65) and AMD (impaired clearance).

### Visual Cycle (Retinoid Cycle)

All-trans-retinal released from bleached rhodopsin is reduced to all-trans-retinol by RDH8/12 in photoreceptors, transported to RPE via IRBP, esterified by LRAT, isomerized to 11-cis-retinol by RPE65 (isomerohydrolase), oxidized to 11-cis-retinal by RDH5/RDH11, and transported back to photoreceptors. RPE65 mutations cause Leber congenital amaurosis (LCA2) and retinitis pigmentosa.

### Blood-Retina Barrier & Transport

Apical (apical Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, BEST1 Cl⁻ channel) and basolateral transporters maintain ionic homeostasis, remove metabolic waste (lactate, CO₂), and supply glucose (GLUT1), fatty acids, and vitamin A. BEST1 mutations cause Best vitelliform macular dystrophy.

### Trophic Factor Secretion

- **PEDF** (anti-angiogenic, neurotrophic) — apical secretion
- **VEGF** (pro-angiogenic) — basolateral secretion; dysregulated in wet AMD
- **CFH**, **CFB**, **C3** — complement regulation; CFH Y402H variant major AMD risk factor

## Clinical Relevance

### Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)

- **Dry AMD (Geographic Atrophy)**: RPE atrophy precedes photoreceptor loss; driven by oxidative stress, complement dysregulation (CFH, C3, ARMS2/HTRA1), lipofuscin toxicity (A2E), and impaired autophagy.
- **Wet AMD (Neovascular AMD)**: RPE-derived VEGF drives choroidal neovascularization (CNV) through Bruch's membrane breaks. Anti-VEGF therapy (aflibercept, ranibizumab, faricimab) is standard-of-care.
- **Risk variants**: CFH Y402H (OR ~3-7), ARMS2/HTRA1, C3, APOE, TIMP3.

### Retinitis Pigmentosa & Leber Congenital Amaurosis

RPE65, LRAT, RDH5, MERTK, RLBP1 mutations cause autosomal recessive RP/LCA. RPE65 gene therapy (voretigene neparvovec/AAV2-RPE65, Luxturna®) approved 2017 — first FDA-approved gene therapy for inherited retinal disease.

### iPSC-Derived RPE for Cell Therapy

iPSC-RPE transplantation (subretinal delivery) in clinical trials for dry AMD (NCT02903576, NCT03102138, NCT04339764) and Stargardt disease. Key challenges: immune rejection, subretinal fibrosis, integration with host Bruch's membrane, and functional maturation. Polarized monolayer formation, phagocytic competence, and RPE65 expression are release criteria.

### Other RPEopathies

- **Best disease** (BEST1): Subretinal fluid accumulation, lipofuscin deposition
- **Central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR)**: RPE pump dysfunction, choroidal hyperpermeability
- **Pattern dystrophies** (PRPH2, BEST1): Lipofuscin accumulation in RPE
