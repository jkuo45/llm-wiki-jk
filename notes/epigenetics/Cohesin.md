# Cohesin

Cohesin is an evolutionarily conserved multi-subunit protein complex belonging to the structural maintenance of chromosomes ([[SMC]]) family. It mediates [[Sister Chromatid Cohesion]] from S phase until anaphase, organizes higher-order chromatin architecture by stabilizing [[CTCF]]-mediated loop extrusion and topologically associating domains ([[TAD]]s), and facilitates [[DNA Repair]] via homologous recombination (HR). Dysregulation of cohesin is implicated in developmental disorders ([[Cornelia de Lange Syndrome]]), cancer, and aging-related chromosome segregation errors.

## Subunit Composition

The canonical cohesin complex consists of four core subunits:

- **[[SMC1]] (SMC1A)** and **[[SMC3]]**: Members of the SMC ATPase family. Each forms a V-shaped dimer via hinge-domain heterodimerization, with long coiled-coil arms ending in ATP-binding head domains. ATP binding and hydrolysis regulate loading, translocation, and release of cohesin from chromatin.
- **RAD21 (SCC1/MCD1)**: The kleisin subunit that bridges the SMC1 and SMC3 head domains, forming the tripartite ring architecture thought to entrap DNA strands.
- **STAG1 or STAG2 (SA1/SA2)**: Stromal antigen subunits that bind RAD21 and stabilize the complex. STAG1 and STAG2 define functionally distinct cohesin complexes with partially overlapping roles.

Additional regulatory factors include:
- **NIPBL (SCC2)** and **MAU2 (SCC4)**: The cohesin loader complex that deposits cohesin onto chromatin.
- **WAPL** and **PDS5**: Regulators of cohesin dynamics that promote unloading from chromatin to enable loop extrusion and remove non-acetylated cohesin.
- **Separase (ESPL1)**: The protease that cleaves RAD21 at anaphase onset, triggering sister chromatid separation.
- **ESCO1/ESCO2 (EFO1/EFO2)**: Acetyltransferases that acetylate SMC3 at K105/K106, locking cohesin onto chromatin and establishing cohesion.

## Functions

### Sister Chromatid Cohesion

Cohesin is loaded onto chromatin in G1 and establishes [[Sister Chromatid Cohesion]] during S phase as the replication fork passes through the complex. The ring-shaped cohesin is thought to topologically entrap both sister chromatids. Cohesion is maintained through G2 and is essential for accurate chromosome alignment on the metaphase plate — cohesion generates the tension required for bi-orientation. At anaphase, separase cleaves RAD21, opening the ring and releasing the sisters for segregation.

### Chromatin Architecture and Transcriptional Regulation

Cohesin cooperates with the insulator protein [[CTCF]] to organize chromatin into [[TAD]]s through loop extrusion. The cohesin complex translocates along DNA, extruding a loop until it encounters CTCF bound at convergent CTCF-binding motifs, where it stalls. These loops create insulated neighborhoods that promote enhancer–promoter interactions within a TAD and restrict cross-TAD contacts. Cohesin is thus essential for the regulation of developmentally important genes, including the [[Hox]] clusters, [[IGF2]]/[[H19]] imprinting control region, and proto-oncogenes such as [[MYC]]. Disruption of TAD boundaries by cohesin mutations can rewire enhancer–promoter contacts and cause ectopic gene activation.

### DNA Repair

Cohesin accumulates at sites of [[DNA Double-Strand Break]]s, where it promotes [[Sister Chromatid]]-templated homologous recombination by keeping the two sister chromatids in close proximity. Cohesin's role in DNA repair is independent of its role in mitotic cohesion and is regulated by [[ATM]]-dependent phosphorylation.

## Cohesin and Aging

Cohesin dysfunction is linked to age-related [[Meiosis]] errors, particularly in [[Oocyte]]s. Cohesin complexes loaded onto chromosomes during fetal oogenesis must persist for decades until ovulation. Age-dependent loss of cohesin — especially of REC8 and SMC1β — from meiotic chromosomes underlies the increased frequency of aneuploidy in oocytes from older women. Similarly, weakening of mitotic cohesion contributes to age-related chromosome mis-segregation and aneuploidy in somatic cells. The progressive loss of cohesin may be driven by oxidative damage, absence of dedicated repair mechanisms for cohesin subunits, and natural protein turnover without replacement in post-mitotic cells.

## Clinical Relevance

**Cornelia de Lange Syndrome (CdLS):** Heterozygous mutations in NIPBL (~60%), SMC1A, SMC3, RAD21, or HDAC8 cause CdLS, a multisystem developmental disorder characterized by craniofacial dysmorphism, hirsutism, intellectual disability, upper limb reduction defects, and growth retardation. Cohesinopathy mutations impair cohesin loading, ATPase activity, or chromatin interaction dynamics, leading to altered gene expression (particularly of limb development and neuronal genes) without global cohesion defects.

**Cancer:** Somatic mutations in STAG2 occur in ~7% of [[Acute Myeloid Leukemia]], ~10% of [[Bladder Cancer]], and at lower frequency in Ewing sarcoma and myelodysplastic syndromes. STAG2 loss disrupts CTCF-mediated looping at specific loci, leading to transcriptional activation of oncogenes or silencing of tumor suppressors. RAD21 and SMC1A mutations also occur across cancers. Cohesin-mutant cancers often exhibit aneuploidy, though the degree depends on the specific subunit and mutation type.
