# Histone Deacetylase

**[[Histone Deacetylase]] (HDAC)** is a class of enzymes that remove acetyl groups from histone tails. This action increases the positive charge of the histones, causing them to bind more tightly to DNA and producing a more condensed, transcriptionally silent [[Chromatin]] structure.

## Classification and Mechanism

HDACs fall into zinc-dependent classes I, II, and IV, and the NAD+-dependent class III [[Sirtuins]]. By hydrolyzing the ε-N-acetyl bond on lysine residues, HDACs restore histone positive charge, strengthen histone–DNA contacts, and promote [[Heterochromatin]] formation. HDACs also deacetylate non-histone proteins (p53, [[FOXO]], tubulin), extending their regulatory scope.

## Role in Gene Silencing

HDACs generally act as transcriptional repressors. By promoting heterochromatin, they prevent transcription machinery from accessing DNA — essential for maintaining cellular identity, silencing repetitive elements, and [[Genomic Imprinting]]. HDAC1/2 are integral to the [[NuRD complex]] and other co-repressor assemblies.

## Sirtuins: A Specialized Class

One of the most important HDAC families in [[Aging]] research is the [[Sirtuins]] family (Class III). Unlike other HDACs, sirtuins require [[NAD+]] as a cofactor, making them sensitive to cellular energy status and linking their activity to metabolism. They are key mediators of the life-extending effects of [[Caloric Restriction]].

## HDACs and Aging

As organisms [[Aging|age]], HDAC levels and activity change, contributing to "epigenetic drift."

- **Inhibitors as Therapy**: HDAC inhibitors (vorinostat, romidepsin, [[Sodium Valproate]]) are used in [[Cancer]] and investigated in [[Geroscience]] for restoring youthful expression in brain and heart.
- **Balance with HATs**: The dynamic interplay between [[Histone Acetyltransferase|HATs]] and HDACs lets the [[Epigenome]] respond to environmental stimuli.
