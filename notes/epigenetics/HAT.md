# HAT

**HAT** stands for **[[Histone Acetyltransferase]]**, a family of enzymes that transfer an acetyl group from acetyl-CoA to the ε-amino group of lysine residues on histone tails. This neutralizes the positive charge of lysine, weakening histone–DNA interaction and producing an open, transcriptionally permissive [[Chromatin]] state ([[Euchromatin]]).

## Classes and Mechanism

HATs are divided into two main families by homology: the GNAT superfamily (including [[Gcn5]]/PCAF, HAT1) and the MYST family (including [[MOF]], Tip60, [[Sas2]]). A separate group of "general" HATs ([[p300]]/CBP, [[SRC1]]) acetylates both histones and many non-histone substrates (p53, NF-κB, tubulin), linking acetylation to diverse signaling outputs. HATs are "writers" of the [[Epigenome]], opposing the "erasers," the [[Histone Deacetylase|Histone Deacetylases]] (HDACs). The HAT/HDAC balance sets local chromatin accessibility and gene expression.

## Role in Gene Expression and the Epigenome

HATs are recruited by sequence-specific transcription factors to activate promoters and enhancers. They deposit marks such as H3K9ac and H3K27ac that are read by bromodomain proteins, bridging acetylation to the transcriptional machinery. They cooperate with other [[Epigenetic Modifiers]]—including the [[Sirtuin]] class of deacetylases—in maintaining chromatin homeostasis.

## Connection to Aging and Disease

Dysregulated HAT activity is linked to [[Cancer]], [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]], and metabolic disorder. Many longevity interventions, such as [[Caloric Restriction]], modulate HAT/HDAC balance. [[Saccharomyces cerevisiae]] Sir2 (a founding [[Sirtuin]]) and HATs jointly regulate silent chromatin, connecting acetylation to lifespan.
