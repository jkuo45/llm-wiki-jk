# HAT

**[[HAT]]** stands for **[[Histone Acetyltransferase]]**. These are enzymes that add an acetyl group to the amino acid lysine on histone proteins.

### 1. Functional Overview
This process, known as [[Histone Acetylation]], typically reduces the positive charge of the histone, weakening its interaction with the negatively charged DNA. This results in a more "open" [[Chromatin]] structure ([[Euchromatin]]), making the DNA accessible for [[Transcription]].

### 2. Role in the [[Epigenome]]
HATs are the "writers" of the epigenetic code. They work in opposition to [[Histone Deacetylase|Histone Deacetylases]] (HDACs), and the balance between these two enzyme families is critical for maintaining healthy gene expression patterns.

### 3. Connection to [[notes/_link/Aging]]
Dysregulation of HAT activity has been linked to various age-related conditions, including [[Cancer]] and [[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]]. Many longevity-promoting factors, such as [[notes/_link/Caloric Restriction]], influence the activity of these enzymes.

### Connections
- **[[Histone Acetyltransferase]]**: The full name for this class of enzymes.
- **[[Histone Acetylation]]**: The biochemical process performed by HATs.
- **[[Histone Deacetylase]]**: The "erasers" that counteract HAT activity.
- **[[Euchromatin]]**: The open state of chromatin promoted by acetylation.
