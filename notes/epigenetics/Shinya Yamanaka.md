# Shinya Yamanaka

Shinya Yamanaka (born September 4, 1962) is a Japanese stem cell biologist and Nobel Prize laureate. He is best known for discovering [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) — the demonstration that a fully differentiated [[Somatic Cells|somatic cell]] can be reprogrammed to pluripotency by forced expression of a defined set of transcription factors, the [[Yamanaka Factors]]. He was awarded the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, shared with [[John Gurdon]], who had established nuclear reprogramming via [[Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer]].

## Career

Yamanaka trained in orthopaedic surgery before shifting to basic science. He is a professor at [[Kyoto University]] and founding director of the Center for iPS Cell Research and Application (CiRA). His laboratory's landmark 2006 (mouse) and 2007 (human) papers — with first author [[Kazutoshi Takahashi]] — identified the minimal four-factor cocktail [[Oct4]], [[Sox2]], [[Klf4]], and [[c-Myc]] ([[OSKM]]) sufficient for reprogramming. His later work has emphasized the safety, quality control, and clinical translation of iPSCs, including allogeneic HLA-matched iPSC banks.

## Scientific Impact

The iPSC discovery transformed [[Stem Cell Research]] by circumventing the ethical and immunological limitations of [[Embryonic Stem Cells]]. It enabled patient-specific [[Disease Modeling]], human-relevant [[Drug Discovery]], and [[Regenerative Medicine]] applications such as the first iPSC clinical trial for [[Macular Degeneration]] (led by Masayo Takahashi). Yamanaka has also advanced the mechanistic understanding of [[Reprogramming]] kinetics, the role of [[Epigenetic Clock|epigenetic resetting]], and the principles behind [[Partial Reprogramming]] for [[Rejuvenation]].

## Recognition

Beyond the Nobel Prize, Yamanaka has received the Albert Lasker Basic Medical Research Award (2009), the Millennium Technology Prize, and election to numerous academies. He continues to champion responsible, equitable translation of pluripotent stem cell technology.
