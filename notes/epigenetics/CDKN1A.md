# CDKN1A

**[[CDKN1A]]**, also known as [[p21]] (WAF1/CIP1), encodes the p21 protein, a potent member of the Cip/Kip family of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors (CKIs). p21 is the primary downstream effector of the tumor suppressor [[p53]], acting as a molecular bridge between DNA-damage sensing and cell-cycle arrest.

## Structure & Regulation

p21 binds and inhibits a broad range of [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]] complexes (Cyclin E/A/CDK2, Cyclin D/CDK4/6, Cyclin B/CDK1). Its transcription is directly activated by p53 binding to a p53-responsive element in the *CDKN1A* promoter following DNA damage; basal expression is also maintained by growth-inhibitory signals. p21 can be regulated post-transcriptionally via stability and subcellular localization.

## Mechanism/Function

p21 inhibits CDK complexes, preventing phosphorylation of the retinoblastoma protein (Rb) and halting the cell cycle at the G1/S (and, at high levels, G2/M) transition. This arrest allows time for DNA repair, or, if damage is irreparable, triggers [[Cellular Senescence]] or apoptosis. p21 also binds PCNA, inhibiting DNA polymerase δ/ε processivity and directly blocking DNA synthesis. Beyond the cell cycle, p21 modulates [[Chromatin Remodeling]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[Autophagy]].

## Physiological & Pathological Function

p21 is central to genome stability and tumor suppression. Because it is a major mediator of p53-induced arrest, loss of [[p53]] (common in cancer) often abolishes p21 induction, facilitating tumorigenesis. p21 is a defining marker of [[Senescence]] and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), which protects against cancer but contributes to [[Aging]] and age-related disease. CDKN1A also constrains stemness and pluripotency exit.

## Clinical Relevance

Loss or dysregulation of p21 is frequent in human cancers, promoting genomic instability and uncontrolled proliferation. The p21 pathway is a target for cancer therapeutics (inducing senescence in tumors) and for [[Geroscience]] (modulating senescence in aging tissues). p21, with [[CDKN1B|p27]] and [[CDKN1C|p57]], forms the Cip/Kip CKI axis. The DNA-damage arm additionally intersects with agents such as [[Busulfan]] and [[Aphidicolin]] that trigger p53/p21-dependent arrest.
