# ZCCHC11

[[ZCCHC11]] (zinc finger CCCH-type containing 11), also known as TUT4 (terminal uridylyltransferase 4), is a non-canonical poly(A) polymerase that adds uridine residues to the 3′ ends of RNAs. It is best known as the enzyme through which the RNA-binding protein [[Lin28]] blocks biogenesis of the [[let-7 microRNA]] family, a regulatory axis with profound effects on development, [[Terminal Differentiation]], [[Cancer]], and cellular [[Reprogramming]].

## Structure & Domains

ZCCHC11 contains three CCCH-type zinc finger motifs that bind single-stranded RNA, and a catalytic PAP-associated nucleotide transferase (PAP/NT) domain that performs uridylylation. Related family members include ZCCHC11 paralogs and TUTase family enzymes. The zinc fingers confer sequence/structure preference, allowing ZCCHC11 to act preferentially on pre-let-7 terminal loops when recruited by Lin28.

## Mechanism & the Lin28/let-7 Axis

In embryonic and undifferentiated cells, [[Lin28]] (Lin28A/B) binds the terminal loop of primary and precursor let-7 (pre-let-7). Lin28 recruits the [[ZCCHC11]] TUTase, which oligo-uridylates the 3′ end of pre-let-7. This uridine tail blocks Dicer processing and recruits the 3′–5′ exonuclease PARN/Dis3L2, leading to pre-let-7 degradation. The net effect is suppression of mature let-7, a tumor-suppressive and pro-differentiation microRNA. Upon differentiation, Lin28 is downregulated, ZCCHC11-mediated uridylation ceases, and let-7 accumulates, reinforcing [[Terminal Differentiation]] and dampening proliferation and pluripotency.

## Broader RNA Targets

Beyond let-7, ZCCHC11 uridylates other structured noncoding RNAs (e.g., certain snRNAs, snoRNAs, and viral RNAs), marking them for turnover or altering stability — a form of [[RNA Modification]]-mediated regulation. Its activity thus contributes to a post-transcriptional regulatory layer that complements chromatin-based control such as [[H3K27me3]].

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

The Lin28–ZCCHC11–let-7 circuit is a master switch of stemness versus differentiation: high Lin28/low let-7 maintains [[Pluripotency]] and supports the efficiency of the [[Yamanaka Factors]] during [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] generation, whereas let-7 restoration promotes maturation. Pathologically, Lin28 overexpression and ZCCHC11-dependent let-7 suppression are found in several [[Cancer]]s and are associated with enhanced proliferation, [[Tumorigenicity]], and poor prognosis. ZCCHC11 is therefore a candidate node for modulating differentiation and for anticancer intervention.
