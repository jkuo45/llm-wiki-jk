# Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer

Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer (SCNT) is a laboratory technique that creates a viable embryo — or, when cultured in vitro, a pluripotent cell line — by transferring the nucleus of a differentiated [[Somatic Cells|somatic (body) cell]] into an enucleated [[Oocyte]] (egg cell). The oocyte cytoplasm contains maternally supplied reprogramming factors that reset the donor genome to a totipotent/pluripotent state, supporting development to the [[Blastocyst]] stage and the derivation of nuclear-transfer embryonic stem cells (ntESCs).

## Mechanism

The procedure involves four stages: (1) harvesting a metaphase II oocyte and removing its spindle–chromosome complex (enucleation); (2) introducing a donor somatic nucleus (by electrofusion or microinjection); (3) activating the reconstructed embryo with calcium ionophore and an inhibition of protein synthesis/phosphatase (e.g., strontium + MG132) to mimic fertilization; and (4) culturing to blastocyst and isolating the inner cell mass. The oocyte's abundant stores of reprogramming molecules — including histone-modifying enzymes, chromatin remodelers, and maternal transcription factors — orchestrate global DNA demethylation, X-chromosome reactivation, and re-establishment of [[Pluripotency]].

## Historical & Conceptual Significance

SCNT provided the first definitive proof that the genome of a terminally differentiated somatic cell remains totipotent — the conceptual foundation later exploited by [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells|iPSC]] technology. [[John Gurdon]]'s 1962 cloning of frogs with differentiated intestinal nuclei earned the 2012 Nobel Prize (shared with [[Shinya Yamanaka]]). In 1996, SCNT produced Dolly the sheep, the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell. The method remains the gold standard for studying developmental [[Reprogramming]] and for generating patient-genetically-matched pluripotent lines without the exogenous-factor integration used in iPSC derivation.

## Physiological, Pathological & Clinical Relevance

Clinically, SCNT underpins therapeutic cloning concepts and has been used to derive disease-specific ntESCs for [[Disease Modeling]] and [[Drug Discovery]]. It also illuminated why aged, differentiated nuclei can be "rejuvenated" by ooplasmic factors — informing later [[Rejuvenation]] strategies. Limitations include low efficiency, mitochondrial heteroplasmy (oocyte mtDNA persists), and epigenetic memory/aberrant imprinting that can cause placental and developmental abnormalities. These drawbacks, together with ethical concerns over embryo use, motivated the shift toward iPSC-based approaches after [[Kazutoshi Takahashi]] and Yamanaka's 2006/2007 discoveries.
