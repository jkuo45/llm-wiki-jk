# Aphidicolin

**Aphidicolin** is a tetracyclic diterpene antibiotic originally isolated from the fungus *Cephalosporium aphidicola*. It is a potent, reversible inhibitor of [[DNA Replication|DNA replication]] through competitive inhibition of [[DNA polymerase α]] (Pol α) and, to a lesser extent, Pol δ, by competing with dCTP for the catalytic site.

## Mechanism of Action

Aphidicolin binds directly to the catalytic subunit of [[DNA polymerase α]], preventing the incorporation of dCTP into the nascent DNA strand. This inhibition blocks the elongation phase of replication without affecting primase activity. At concentrations of 1–10 µg/mL, aphidicolin arrests proliferating cells at the G1/S phase boundary, making it a standard tool for cell cycle synchronization. The block is fully reversible upon drug removal, allowing synchronous release into S phase.

## Applications in Research

Aphidicolin is widely used to induce **replication stress** — a condition of stalled or collapsed replication forks that activates the [[DNA Damage Response]] (DDR). This model system has been instrumental in elucidating checkpoint activation mechanisms mediated by [[ATM]], [[ATR]], and [[Chk1]]. Aphidicolin treatment triggers [[Senescence|replicative senescence]] in primary cells after extended exposure, linking replication stress to aging and tumor suppression. It is also employed in DNA combing and fiber analysis to study replication fork dynamics, origin firing, and fork restart.

## Clinical and Pathological Relevance

While not used therapeutically, aphidicolin has served as a critical chemical probe to understand the etiology of genomic instability in cancer. Tumors with high replication stress (e.g., [[MYC]]-amplified, [[RB1]]-deficient) are selectively sensitized to aphidicolin, and the compound has been used preclinically to identify vulnerabilities in [[DNA Repair]] pathways. Aphidicolin-mediated replication stress models are also relevant to studies of [[Fanconi Anemia]], [[Bloom Syndrome]], and other genome instability disorders.
