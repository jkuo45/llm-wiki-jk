# Apoptosis

[[Apoptosis]] is the process of programmed cell death that occurs in multicellular organisms. It is a highly regulated mechanism used to eliminate damaged, infected, or redundant cells.

## Role in Reprogramming Roadblocks
In [[Cellular Reprogramming]], apoptosis serves as a significant barrier. The stress of viral transduction and the over-expression of factors like [[c-Myc]] can trigger apoptotic pathways.
- The [[Innate Immunity|innate immune response]] to viruses leads to [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) and [[DNA Damage]].
- These stressors activate [[p53]], which can then induce apoptosis.
- High rates of apoptosis among transduced cells significantly lower the overall efficiency of generating [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]].

## Connections
- [[p53]]: A key regulator that can trigger apoptosis in response to severe DNA damage.
- [[Cellular Reprogramming]]: Apoptosis is one of the primary "fail-safe" mechanisms that must be managed to improve reprogramming efficiency.
- [[DNA Damage]]: A major trigger for apoptosis in somatic cells during the reprogramming process.
