# Reactive Oxygen Species

[[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) are highly reactive chemicals formed from oxygen. In biological systems, ROS are produced as byproducts of normal metabolism but can also be induced by external stressors, such as viral infection.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming
During the early stages of [[Cellular Reprogramming]] via viral transduction of [[OSKM]], levels of ROS significantly increase. Elevated ROS can:
- Cause oxidative [[DNA Damage]].
- Trigger the activation of [[p53]].
- Promote [[Apoptosis]] and [[Senescence]].
- Enhance the differentiation of [[Embryonic Stem Cells]], thereby antagonizing the induction of [[Pluripotency]].

## Mitigation Strategies
The use of ROS scavengers (antioxidants like Vitamin C) and hypoxic growth conditions have been shown to improve the efficiency of inducing pluripotency by buffering ROS-induced damage and preventing premature differentiation.

## Connections
- [[Cellular Reprogramming]]: High ROS levels are a major roadblock in the reprogramming process.
- [[Innate Immunity]]: Viral-induced innate immune responses are a primary source of ROS during reprogramming.
- [[DNA Damage]]: A direct consequence of elevated ROS levels.
- [[p53]]: Activated as a response to ROS-induced stress and DNA damage.
