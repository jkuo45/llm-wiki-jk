# DNA Damage

[[DNA Damage]] refers to physical or chemical alterations to the DNA structure. In the context of [[Stem Cell Research]], maintaining genomic integrity is crucial for the safety and functionality of derived cell lines.

## Induction During Reprogramming
Viral transduction of [[Yamanaka Factors]] ([[OSKM]]) can elicit a significant host cell response that includes the production of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) and subsequent oxidative DNA damage. This damage is often marked by the presence of 8-OHdG foci in both nuclear and mitochondrial DNA.

## Consequences
DNA damage triggers the activation of [[p53]], which can lead to:
- [[Apoptosis]] (programmed cell death).
- [[Senescence]] (permanent cell cycle arrest).
- Reduced [[Cellular Reprogramming]] efficiency.

If [[p53]] is inhibited to increase reprogramming efficiency, the resulting [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs) may be more susceptible to chromosomal aberrations and genomic instability.

## Connections
- [[p53]]: The primary protein responsible for responding to DNA damage and deciding cell fate.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]]: A common cause of oxidative DNA damage during reprogramming.
- [[Innate Immunity]]: The cellular defense response that often precedes ROS and DNA damage in viral protocols.
