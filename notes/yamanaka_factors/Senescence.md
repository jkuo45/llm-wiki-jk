# Senescence

[[Senescence]], or cellular senescence, is a state of permanent cell cycle arrest that occurs when cells are subjected to various stressors, including [[DNA Damage]], oxidative stress, or oncogene activation.

## Roadblock to Reprogramming
Senescence is considered a major roadblock during the induction of pluripotency.
- The process of [[Cellular Reprogramming]] using [[OSKM]] factors, especially via viral delivery, can trigger a senescence-like response in [[Fibroblasts]].
- This is often mediated by the activation of [[p53]] and its downstream targets in response to [[Innate Immunity|innate immune]] signaling and [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS).
- Activation of senescence prevents the cells from continuing the division cycles necessary to complete the reprogramming process.

## Markers
Genes like *GDF15* and *SERPINE1* are often associated with the senescence response observed during early reprogramming events.

## Connections
- [[p53]]: A central mediator of the senescence response.
- [[Cellular Reprogramming]]: Senescence must be bypassed or suppressed for successful reprogramming.
- [[Fibroblasts]]: The most common somatic cell type used in reprogramming that is susceptible to senescence.
