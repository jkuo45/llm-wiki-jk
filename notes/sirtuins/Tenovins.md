# Tenovins

The **Tenovins** represent a family of small-molecule compounds, primarily comprising **Tenovin-1** and its more water-soluble analog **Tenovin-6**, that function as potent inhibitors of class III histone deacetylases, specifically **[[SIRT1]]** and **[[SIRT2]]**. They were originally discovered in a phenotypic screen of 30,000 compounds designed to identify small molecules capable of activating [[p53]] and suppressing tumor growth.

---

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- **Compounds**:
  - **Tenovin-1**: The parent compound, characterized by poor water solubility, which limits exact in vitro $IC_{50}$ determination.
  - **Tenovin-6**: A highly water-soluble derivative optimized for in vivo evaluation.
- **Target Selectivity**: Dual inhibitors of [[SIRT1]] and [[SIRT2]], with moderate inhibitory activity against [[SIRT3]]:
  - **SIRT1**: $IC_{50} = 21\ \mu\text{M}$ (Tenovin-6)
  - **SIRT2**: $IC_{50} = 10\ \mu\text{M}$ (Tenovin-6)
  - **SIRT3**: $IC_{50} = 67\ \mu\text{M}$ (Tenovin-6)

---

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

### Mechanism of Action
Tenovins inhibit the deacetylase activity of SIRT1 and SIRT2, preventing them from removing acetyl groups from target proteins. The primary downstream consequence of this inhibition is the **hyperacetylation and activation of [[p53]]**, a master tumor suppressor.

### Oncological & Physiological Effects
- **p53-Dependent Apoptosis**: By promoting the hyperacetylation of p53, Tenovins reactivate p53-dependent transcriptional networks. This induces the expression of pro-apoptotic genes and arrests the cell cycle, triggering cell death specifically in cancer cells.
- **In Vivo Tumor Suppression**: Tenovin-1 and Tenovin-6 significantly delay tumor growth in human cancer mouse xenograft models (such as BL2 and ARN8 xenografts) at single-digit micromolar concentrations.
- **Low General Toxicity**: Strikingly, Tenovins exert their potent anticancer and apoptotic effects in tumor cells with minimal general systemic toxicity in pre-clinical animal models.

---

## Applications

- **Cancer Therapy Research**: Heavily studied in oncology to target sirtuin-mediated survival pathways in hematological malignancies (e.g., leukemia, lymphoma) and solid tumors.
- **p53 Activation Studies**: Used as positive controls to evaluate p53 induction and the downstream apoptotic cascade.
