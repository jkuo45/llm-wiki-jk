# Allosteric Activator

An **allosteric activator** is a molecule that binds to a site on a [[Protein|protein]] distinct from its orthosteric (active) site, inducing a [[Conformational change|conformational change]] that increases the protein's catalytic or functional activity. This is a form of [[Allosteric Regulation|allosteric regulation]].

## Mechanism

Allosteric activators bind to topographically distinct pockets — often hydrophobic cavities or regulatory domains — and stabilize the protein's active conformation. Unlike orthosteric agonists, they do not compete with the endogenous substrate or co-substrate, which can confer greater selectivity and a saturable ceiling effect that reduces the risk of overactivation.

## Role in Sirtuin Biology

The mammalian [[Sirtuins|sirtuins]] ([[SIRT1]]–[[SIRT7]]) are [[NAD+]]-dependent deacylases with elongated hydrophobic pockets adjacent to the catalytic site. These pockets are targetable by synthetic allosteric activators:

- **[[SIRT6]]**: [[UBCS039]], [[MDL-800]], and [[MDL-801]] bind a distal hydrophobic pocket, increasing deacetylase activity up to 22-fold by stabilizing the active conformation with bound [[NAD+]] and acylated substrate.
- **[[SIRT1]]**: [[Resveratrol]] and [[STACs|STACs (Sirtuin Activating Compounds)]] bind an N-terminal allosteric domain (the "STAC-binding pocket"), enhancing substrate affinity.

The allosteric approach is preferred over orthosteric modulation because direct NAD+ competition would affect all seven sirtuins and hundreds of other NAD+-dependent enzymes.

## Comparison to Orthosteric Modulation

| Feature | Allosteric | Orthosteric |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Binding site | Distinct from active site | Active site (competitive) |
| Selectivity | Higher (unique pockets) | Lower (shared co-substrate) |
| Ceiling effect | Saturated at high dose | Can exceed physiological max |
| Off-target risk | Lower | Higher (NAD+ pocket shared) |
