# PGC-1α

[[PGC-1α]] (encoded by *PPARGC1A*) is a potent transcriptional **coactivator** that drives mitochondrial biogenesis, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty-acid oxidation, and antioxidant defense. It is the central node linking exercise, fasting, and cold exposure to mitochondrial adaptation, and is a canonical downstream target of the [[Sirtuins|SIRT1]] deacetylase.

## Structure & Domains

- **N-terminal activation domain** — interacts with nuclear receptors ([[PPAR]]γ, ERRα) and [[CREB]].
- **RNA-binding / serine-arginine-rich domain** — couples transcription to splicing.
- **C-terminal domain** — harbors multiple lysine acetylation sites that are dynamically regulated by sirtuins and [[NAD+]] availability.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **SIRT1 deacetylation**: [[SIRT1]] deacetylates [[PGC-1α]] at several lysines, robustly increasing its transcriptional activity toward mitochondrial genes (landmark longevity finding; PMID 15592568, 17086196). This couples the cellular [[NAD+]]/NADH ratio to oxidative capacity.
- **AMPK phosphorylation**: [[AMPK]] phosphorylates PGC-1α during exercise/energy deficit, priming it for SIRT1 action and boosting [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]].
- **Transcriptional program**: Co-activates NRF1/2 and [[TFEB]]-related networks controlling respiratory-chain subunits, [[Mitophagy]] components, and [[Lysosome Biogenesis]].
- **CLEAR network overlap**: PGC-1α and the [[Coordinated Lysosomal Expression and Regulation|CLEAR]] network jointly coordinate mitochondrial and lysosomal quality control.

> [!tip] NAD+ dependence
> Because SIRT1 requires [[NAD+]] as a cosubstrate, PGC-1α activity reports on the cellular redox/energy state — a key reason boosting NAD+ or [[Sirtuin Activators]] enhances mitochondrial fitness.

## Physiological Function

PGC-1α governs adaptive thermogenesis (brown fat), endurance-exercise remodeling of skeletal muscle, hepatic gluconeogenesis, and protection against [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Aging & sarcopenia**: PGC-1α/mitochondrial decline tracks with age-related loss of oxidative muscle and metabolic disease.
- **Neurodegeneration**: PGC-1α deficiency lowers antioxidant capacity; SIRT1–PGC-1α activation is neuroprotective.
- **Metabolic disease**: PGC-1α modulation is a target for insulin sensitivity, [[Aging]], and mitochondrial cytopathies.
