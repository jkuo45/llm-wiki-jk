# OSCP

**OSCP** (Oligomycin Sensitivity Conferral Protein, encoded by *ATP5PO*) is a peripheral stalk subunit of mitochondrial F1Fo-ATP synthase (Complex V). It connects the catalytic F1 head to the membrane-embedded Fo rotor, transmitting conformational changes required for ATP synthesis. OSCP confers sensitivity to the ATP synthase inhibitor [[Oligomycin]]. As a key structural and regulatory component of the enzyme that produces the bulk of cellular ATP, OSCP is a direct molecular substrate through which the mitochondrial sirtuin [[SIRT3]] tunes energy metabolism.

## Structure and Function

OSCP forms the central component of the peripheral stalk that statically links the αβ-hexamer of F1 to subunit-a of Fo. Along with subunits b, d, F6, and the oligomycin-sensitivity conferral complex, it constitutes a rigid stator that prevents the catalytic domains from co-rotating with the central stalk. It is essential for the mechanical coupling of proton translocation through Fo to the rotation of the central stalk (γ subunit) and the conformational changes in F1 that drive ATP formation. Without OSCP, ATP synthase is uncoupled and hydrolytic activity is uncoupled from proton flux. OSCP also provides the binding surface for [[Oligomycin]] and related inhibitors such as citreoviridin, which block proton conduction through Fo.

## Regulation by SIRT3

[[SIRT3]] deacetylates OSCP at key lysine residues, enhancing the coupling efficiency of ATP synthase and boosting mitochondrial ATP output. The natural compound [[Honokiol]], a SIRT3 activator, potentiates this deacetylation, improving respiratory coupling and reducing proton leak. SIRT3-mediated OSCP deacetylation optimizes the efficiency of [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS)]], particularly under conditions of increased energy demand. Acetylation of OSCP is a marker of mitochondrial inefficiency: hyperacetylated OSCP is found in aged and metabolically diseased tissues, where it correlates with reduced ATP synthesis and increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation by the leaky respiratory chain. This regulatory mechanism is critical in tissues with high ATP turnover (heart, brain, skeletal muscle) and is impaired in ageing and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]].

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Efficient ATP synthase coupling is essential for cellular energy charge and for minimizing electron leakage that drives [[Oxidative Stress]]. By deacetylating OSCP, SIRT3 preserves the yield of ATP per unit of oxygen consumed — a cardinal feature of the [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Longevity]] programs. Reduced SIRT3 activity with age leads to OSCP hyperacetylation, mitochondrial uncoupling, and the bioenergetic decline seen in [[Heart Failure|heart failure]], [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome|metabolic syndrome]]. Experimental SIRT3 activation restores ATP synthase efficiency, suggesting OSCP deacetylation as a tractable therapeutic node.

## Clinical Relevance

OSCP acetylation increases with age and in metabolic disease, correlating with reduced ATP synthase efficiency and mitochondrial uncoupling. SIRT3 activators ([[Honokiol]], [[NAD+]] precursors) that target OSCP deacetylation could restore mitochondrial energetics in [[Heart Failure|heart failure]], [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome|metabolic syndrome]]. Beyond sirtuin-targeted approaches, modulation of OSCP stability and the peripheral stalk is an active area in mitochondrial medicine.
