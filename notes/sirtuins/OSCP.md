# OSCP

**OSCP** (Oligomycin Sensitivity Conferral Protein, encoded by *ATP5PO*) is a peripheral stalk subunit of mitochondrial F1Fo-ATP synthase (Complex V). It connects the catalytic F1 head to the membrane-embedded Fo rotor, transmitting conformational changes required for ATP synthesis. OSCP confers sensitivity to the ATP synthase inhibitor [[Oligomycin]].

## Structure and Function

OSCP forms the central component of the peripheral stalk that statically links the αβ-hexamer of F1 to subunit-a of Fo. It is essential for the mechanical coupling of proton translocation through Fo to the rotation of the central stalk and the conformational changes in F1 that drive ATP formation. Without OSCP, ATP synthase is uncoupled and hydrolytic activity is uncoupled from proton flux.

## Regulation by SIRT3

[[SIRT3]] deacetylates OSCP at key lysine residues, enhancing the coupling efficiency of ATP synthase and boosting mitochondrial ATP output. The natural compound [[Honokiol]], a SIRT3 activator, potentiates this deacetylation, improving respiratory coupling and reducing proton leak. SIRT3-mediated OSCP deacetylation optimizes the efficiency of oxidative phosphorylation ([[OXPHOS|OXPHOS]]), particularly under conditions of increased energy demand. This regulatory mechanism is critical in tissues with high ATP turnover (heart, brain, skeletal muscle) and is impaired in ageing and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]].

## Clinical Relevance

OSCP acetylation increases with age and in metabolic disease, correlating with reduced ATP synthase efficiency and mitochondrial uncoupling. SIRT3 activators ([[Honokiol]], NAD+ precursors) that target OSCP deacetylation could restore mitochondrial energetics in [[Heart Failure|heart failure]], [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome|metabolic syndrome]].
