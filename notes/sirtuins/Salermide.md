# Salermide

**Salermide** is a potent, cell-permeable synthetic small-molecule sirtuin inhibitor containing a $\beta$-naphthol group. Designed and developed through structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies of [[Sirtinol]], salermide possesses significantly improved inhibitory potency against both **[[SIRT1]]** and **[[SIRT2]]** and exhibits strong, tumor-selective antiproliferative effects.

---

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- **Class**: $\beta$-naphthol-containing sirtinol derivative.
- **Target Selectivity**: Improved potency against both [[SIRT1]] and [[SIRT2]] compared to the parent compound sirtinol.

---

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

### Mechanism of Action

Salermide blocks the NAD⁺-dependent deacetylation of sirtuin targets. A key mechanism underlying its potent antitumor activity is its ability to inhibit SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of Histone H4 Lys¹⁶ (H4K16ac). This leads to the epigenetic **reactivation of proapoptotic genes** that are aberrantly repressed in human cancer cells.

### Oncological & Antiproliferative Effects

- **Tumor-Selective Cell Death**: Salermide induces robust apoptosis in various human cancer cell lines, including human leukemia MOLT4 cells, breast MDA-MB-231 cells, and colon RKO cancer cells.
- **Cancer Stem Cell Suppression**: Salermide has been demonstrated to play a critical inhibitory role in **colorectal carcinoma cancer stem cells**, suppressing their self-renewal capacity and reducing tumorigenesis.
- **Favorable Therapeutic Index**: Similar to its predecessor sirtinol, salermide exhibits a high degree of selectivity for cancer cells, causing minimal toxic effects in normal cells.

---

## Applications

- **Oncology & Stem Cell Research**: Utilized to evaluate sirtuin dependencies in cancer stem cells and resistant solid tumor models.
- **Epigenetic Therapy**: Studied as a representative agent capable of reversing sirtuin-mediated epigenetic silencing of tumor suppressors.
