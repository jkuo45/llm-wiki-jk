# p33ING1b

**p33ING1b** (Inhibitor of Growth Protein 1, encoded by *ING1*) is a **tumor suppressor protein** that functions as a reader of the histone H3 trimethylation mark H3K4me3 through its plant homeodomain (PHD) finger. It is involved in chromatin remodeling, [[DNA Repair]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[Cellular Senescence]]. As a member of the ING (Inhibitor of Growth) family, p33ING1b links chromatin state to cell-cycle control and stress responses, and it interfaces directly with the sirtuin network through its regulation of [[SIRT1]] and the tumor suppressor [[p53]].

## Structure and Domain Function

ING1 gives rise to several splice variants, of which p33ING1b and p47ING1a are the best characterized in humans. The PHD finger of p33ING1b recognizes the active-promoter mark H3K4me3, directing the protein to transcriptionally active chromatin where it can recruit histone acetyltransferases (e.g., the [[p300]]/CBP complex) and the nucleosome remodeling complex. Through these interactions p33ING1b promotes a transcriptionally permissive state at growth-arrest and DNA-damage-responsive genes. Loss or downregulation of ING1 is observed in diverse cancers, consistent with its growth-suppressive role.

## SIRT1 Interaction and p53 Regulation

p33ING1b physically interacts with [[SIRT1]] and **inhibits SIRT1's ability to deacetylate [[p53]]**. By blocking SIRT1-mediated p53 deacetylation, p33ING1b maintains p53 in an acetylated, transcriptionally active state. Acetylated p53 binds DNA with higher affinity and drives expression of pro-apoptotic (e.g., PUMA, Bax) and cell-cycle arrest (e.g., p21) target genes. Thus p33ING1b acts as a context-dependent brake on SIRT1, converting a survival signal into a tumor-suppressive one.

### Hepatocellular Carcinoma Context

In hepatocarcinoma (HepG2) cells, the p33ING1b–SIRT1 interaction negatively regulates transcription of **alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)**, a diagnostic biomarker for hepatocellular carcinoma. Active (acetylated) p53 represses AFP transcription, while SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of p53 relieves this repression. p33ING1b therefore acts as a negative regulator of AFP expression by keeping p53 active via SIRT1 inhibition, establishing a **p33ING1b–SIRT1–p53–AFP regulatory axis**. This axis illustrates how chromatin readers can modulate the output of the [[NAD+]]-dependent deacetylase network to influence oncogenic gene expression.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

The p33ING1b–SIRT1 relationship exemplifies the dual nature of [[SIRT1]]: under stress, SIRT1 can blunt p53 to promote survival, but p33ING1b can override this to permit p53-driven elimination of damaged cells. This tension is central to [[Aging]] and tumor suppression, where excessive SIRT1 activity might suppress necessary apoptotic clearance. Therapeutically, agents that modulate the ING1–SIRT1 interface could tip the balance toward tumor suppression without broadly inhibiting sirtuins.
