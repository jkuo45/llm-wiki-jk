# CPS1 (Carbamoyl Phosphate Synthase 1)

**Carbamoyl Phosphate Synthase 1** (CPS1) is a mitochondrial enzyme that catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step of the urea cycle, converting ammonia and bicarbonate into carbamoyl phosphate. Ammonia is a highly toxic byproduct of amino acid catabolism, and its clearance via conversion into urea in the liver is essential for cellular and systemic health.

## Metabolic Role and Urea Cycle

CPS1 is primarily expressed in hepatocytes and intestinal mucosal cells. Within the mitochondrial matrix, CPS1 combines ammonium ($NH_4^+$), bicarbonate ($HCO_3^-$), and two molecules of [[ATP|ATP]] to form carbamoyl phosphate. This product then enters the urea cycle by condensing with ornithine to form citrulline, facilitated by ornithine transcarbamylase (OTC).

## Regulation by SIRT5-Mediated Desuccinylation

In the mitochondria, CPS1 activity is highly regulated by post-translational modifications, particularly succinylation on multiple lysine residues:
- **Succinylation Inhibits CPS1:** Lysine succinylation (the addition of a negatively charged succinyl group) on CPS1 disrupts its active site conformation and severely reduces its catalytic activity.
- **SIRT5 Restores Activity:** [[SIRT5]], a mitochondrial NAD⁺-dependent desuccinylase, demalonylase, and deglutarylase, binds to CPS1 and removes these succinyl marks (specifically at lysine residue K1291).
- **Metabolic Coordination:** Under fasting, caloric restriction, or high-protein diet conditions, amino acid catabolism increases, generating excess ammonia. Coordinated activation of SIRT5 and desuccinylation of CPS1 boosts urea cycle flux, facilitating rapid ammonia detoxification and maintaining systemic metabolic homeostasis.

## Clinical Relevance

- **CPS1 Deficiency:** Mutations in the *CPS1* gene cause carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency, a rare and severe autosomal recessive urea cycle disorder. It presents as hyperammonemia, lethargy, seizures, and developmental delay, and can be fatal if untreated.
- **Sirtuin Regulation Therapeutics:** Developing SIRT5 activators (such as certain 1,4-dihydropyridine derivatives) represents a promising therapeutic avenue for enhancing urea cycle capacity and boosting ammonia clearance in metabolic diseases or acute liver failure.
