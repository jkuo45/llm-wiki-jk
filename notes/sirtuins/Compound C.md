# Compound C

**Compound C** (also known as Dorsomorphin or BML-275) is a potent, selective, and cell-permeable small-molecule inhibitor of [[AMPK|AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK)]]. It is the most widely used pharmacological tool for probing AMPK dependence in cellular signaling pathways.

## Mechanism

Compound C is a reversible [[ATP]]-competitive inhibitor of AMPK ($K_i \approx 109\ \text{nM}$). At higher concentrations it exhibits some off-target activity against [[BMP|BMP (bone morphogenetic protein) type I receptors]] (ALK2, ALK3, ALK6), but within the typical concentration range (1–10 μM) it is considered AMPK-selective for acute signaling studies.

## Use in Sirtuin and Adrenochrome Research

Compound C is used to establish whether downstream effects of [[Adrenochrome]] and other redox-active compounds are AMPK-dependent:

- Demonstrating that [[Adrenochrome]]-induced [[TFEB]] nuclear translocation and autophagic enhancement require [[AMPK]] activity.
- Testing whether adrenochrome's upregulation of [[PGC-1alpha|PGC-1α]] and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] proceeds through [[AMPK]].
- Dissecting [[SIRT1]]-[[AMPK]] cross-talk: AMPK can activate [[SIRT1]] via [[NAMPT]]-mediated [[NAD+]] elevation, and Compound C helps determine directionality.
