# Compound C

**Compound C** (also known as Dorsomorphin or BML-275) is a potent, selective, and cell-permeable small-molecule inhibitor of [[AMPK|AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK)]]. It is the most widely used pharmacological tool for probing AMPK dependence in cellular signaling pathways, allowing researchers to dissect whether a given phenotype requires AMPK activity.

## Mechanism

Compound C is a reversible [[ATP]]-competitive inhibitor of AMPK (K_i ≈ 109 nM). It binds the kinase domain of the AMPK catalytic α subunit, preventing phosphorylation of downstream targets such as ACC and TSC2. At higher concentrations it exhibits off-target activity against [[BMP|BMP (bone morphogenetic protein) type I receptors]] (ALK2, ALK3, ALK6)—an activity that is in fact the basis of its "Dorsomorphin" alias in developmental biology—but within the typical 1–10 μM range it is considered AMPK-selective for acute signaling studies. Careful controls (e.g., genetic AMPK knockout) are nonetheless advised given the known promiscuity.

## Use in Sirtuin and Adrenochrome Research

Compound C is used to establish whether downstream effects of [[Adrenochrome]] and other redox-active compounds are AMPK-dependent:

- Demonstrating that [[Adrenochrome]]-induced [[TFEB]] nuclear translocation and autophagic enhancement require [[AMPK]] activity.
- Testing whether adrenochrome's upregulation of [[PGC-1α|PGC-1α]] and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] proceeds through [[AMPK]].
- Dissecting [[SIRT1]]–[[AMPK]] cross-talk: AMPK can activate [[SIRT1]] via [[NAMPT]]-mediated [[NAD+]] elevation, and Compound C helps determine directionality—i.e., whether a SIRT1 effect is upstream (activating AMPK) or downstream of AMPK in a given context.

## Broader Signaling Context

AMPK is a central energy sensor that is activated by rising AMP/ATP and by upstream kinases including [[CaMKKβ]] (Ca²⁺-driven) and LKB1. Compound C therefore interrupts not only metabolic AMPK signaling but also the Ca²⁺→CaMKKβ→AMPK limb (see [[Epac1]], [[Ca2+]]). By blocking AMPK, Compound C indirectly dampens [[NAMPT]] induction and NAD⁺ production, blunting SIRT1 activation—illustrating the tight metabolic coupling the inhibitor can be used to probe.

## Research Caveats

Because Compound C inhibits ATP-binding kinases broadly, prolonged exposure can affect cell viability and confound interpretation. Best practice pairs it with a specific AMPK activator (e.g., AICAR or A-769662) and genetic validation. Nevertheless, it remains the standard acute chemical probe for AMPK in sirtuin, autophagy, and redox biology workflows.
