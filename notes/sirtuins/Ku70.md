# Ku70

**Ku70** (encoded by the *XRCC6* gene; also known as Lupus autoantigen p70) is a highly conserved nuclear protein that forms a heterodimer with Ku80. The Ku70/Ku80 complex binds to double-stranded DNA ends and is essential for the non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) pathway of DNA double-strand break repair. Ku70 is also an autoantigen in systemic lupus erythematosus and scleroderma.

## Structure & Mechanism

Ku70 and Ku80 together form a ring-shaped heterodimer that threads onto DNA ends, serving as the recruitment platform for the DNA-PK catalytic subunit (DNA-PKcs) and downstream NHEJ factors (XRCC4, Ligase IV, XLF). Beyond repair, Ku70 modulates telomere maintenance, V(D)J recombination, and Bax-dependent apoptosis.

## Regulation of Apoptosis via SIRT1

In addition to its role in DNA repair, Ku70 acts as an important regulator of apoptosis, a function post-translationally modulated by [[SIRT1]]:

- **Bax Sequestration**: Under normal physiological conditions, Ku70 physically associates with the pro-apoptotic BCL-2 family protein **Bax**, sequestering it in the cytoplasm and preventing it from translocating to the mitochondria, where Bax would otherwise trigger mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP) and apoptosis.
- **SIRT1 Deacetylation**: Under stress, Ku70 is acetylated, which disrupts its interaction with Bax, releasing Bax to trigger apoptosis. [[SIRT1]] physically interacts with and deacetylates Ku70 on key lysine residues. Deacetylated Ku70 binds Bax with high affinity, thereby preventing stress-induced cell death and promoting cell survival.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

The Ku70–Bax interaction thus couples DNA repair competence to cell death decisions: cells with intact Ku70 retain a survival advantage, while acetylation (e.g., by p300/CBP) tips the balance toward apoptosis. The SIRT1–Ku70 axis intersects with the broader [[p53]] tumor-suppressor network, as SIRT1 also deacetylates p53, jointly enforcing a stress-adapted survival program.

## Clinical Relevance

Augmenting SIRT1 to preserve Ku70–Bax sequestration is being explored for neuroprotection (where excessive apoptosis drives degeneration), whereas disrupting the axis could sensitize cancer cells to therapy. Ku70 itself is a biomarker of radiosensitivity and a lupus autoantigen.
