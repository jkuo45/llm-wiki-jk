# SRT1720

[[SRT1720]] is a synthetic small-molecule [[STACs|STAC]] (sirtuin activating compound) developed by [[Sirtris Pharmaceuticals]] as a potent [[SIRT1]] activator. It belongs to the imidazothiazole class and is structurally distinct from [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]]. SRT1720 was among the first second-generation sirtuin activators designed to avoid resveratrol's polyphenolic liabilities (poor bioavailability, low potency) and to deliver robust metabolic benefit in preclinical models.

## Chemistry and Pharmacodynamics

- [[SRT1720]] activates [[SIRT1]] with an EC₁.₅ of approximately 0.16 μM in a fluorophore-based assay, though the mechanism of direct activation has been debated (some studies suggest the activation is substrate-dependent or an assay artifact driven by fluorophore release rather than true enzymatic activation). More recent structural work indicates that STACs such as SRT1720 bind a cryptic pocket adjacent to the SIRT1 catalytic site in the presence of certain acylated peptide substrates, lowering the Michaelis constant for the acetylated substrate.
- It improves [[Insulin Sensitivity]], reduces [[Hepatic Steatosis|hepatic steatosis]], and enhances [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] in diet-induced obese mouse models.
- [[SRT1720]] extends lifespan in mice fed a [[High-fat Diet|high-fat diet]], but not in chow-fed mice, suggesting context-dependent efficacy — benefiting metabolically stressed animals more than healthy ones.

## Mechanism and Downstream Effects

SRT1720 recapitulates many transcriptional hallmarks of [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Resveratrol]] treatment: it deacetylates targets including [[PGC-1α]], [[FOXO]] factors, and [[p53]], shifts metabolism toward oxidative phosphorylation, and reduces inflammatory signaling. These actions improve glucose homeostasis, lower lipid accumulation in liver, and enhance exercise capacity via [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]]. The compound therefore served as a key pharmacological tool establishing that SIRT1 activation alone could confer metabolic health benefits.

## Physiological and Clinical Relevance

SRT1720 advanced translational interest in sirtuin therapeutics, though its development and that of related compounds highlighted challenges: off-target effects, the substrate-dependence of activation, and questions about translatability to humans. A related STAC, [[SRT2104]], entered clinical trials for type 2 diabetes and inflammatory endpoints. Other agents such as [[MDL-801]] were developed with improved specificity. The SRT series remains foundational for probing the [[NAD+]]–[[SIRT1]] axis in aging and metabolism, and informs ongoing work on [[Longevity]] interventions.
