# Daratumumab

**Daratumumab** (brand name **Darzalex**) is a fully human IgG1κ monoclonal antibody (mAb) that specifically targets **[[CD38]]**, a transmembrane glycoprotein highly expressed on malignant plasma cells. It was first approved by the FDA in **November 2015** for the treatment of **[[Multiple Myeloma]]** (MM), making it one of the landmark anti-CD38 therapeutics.

## Mechanism of Action

Daratumumab exerts its anti-tumor effects through multiple complementary mechanisms:

| Mechanism | Description |
|---|---|
| **ADCC** | Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity via NK cells |
| **ADCP** | Antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis via macrophages |
| **CDC** | Complement-dependent cytotoxicity |
| **Direct apoptosis** | Crosslinking-induced direct killing (less prominent vs. isatuximab) |
| **Immunomodulation** | Depletes CD38⁺ Tregs and MDSCs, boosting T-cell and NK-cell anti-tumor responses |

It binds a unique epitope on [[CD38]] distinct from [[Isatuximab]], allowing different combination strategies.

## Clinical Use in Multiple Myeloma

Daratumumab is approved across multiple lines of therapy:

- **Newly diagnosed MM**: In combination with bortezomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone (VRd) or carfilzomib/lenalidomide/dexamethasone (KRd).
- **Relapsed/refractory MM**: Combinations with bortezomib-melphalan-prednisone (VMP), lenalidomide-dexamethasone (Rd), pomalidomide-dexamethasone, and others.
- **Subcutaneous formulation** (Darzalex Faspro, with hyaluronidase): Reduces infusion time significantly.

## Adverse Effects

- **Infections**: Increased risk of serious infections (pneumonia, upper respiratory tract infections) due to immune modulation and plasma cell depletion. Prophylaxis (antivirals, antibiotics) is routinely recommended.
- **Infusion-related reactions**: Common with IV formulation (mitigated by premedication and subcutaneous route).
- **Neutropenia / thrombocytopenia**: Especially in combination regimens.
- **CD38 interference with blood bank testing**: Can cause false-positive indirect antiglobulin tests (DAT); requires special blood bank notification.
