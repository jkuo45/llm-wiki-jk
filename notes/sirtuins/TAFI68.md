# TAFI68

**TAFI68** (TBP-associated factor I 68, encoded by the *TAF1B* gene) is the second largest subunit of the TBP-containing complex **TIF-IB/SL1**, which is an essential basal transcription factor regulating the transcription of ribosomal RNA (rDNA) by **RNA Polymerase I (RNA Pol I)**. TAFI68 serves as a major molecular switch for ribosome biogenesis through its reversible acetylation state.

## Regulation by Sirtuins and Acetyltransferases

The transcriptional activity of RNA Polymerase I is dynamically controlled by post-translational modifications of TAFI68:

1. **Deacetylation by SIRT1**: TAFI68 was the first non-histone substrate identified for mouse **SIRT1**. SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of TAFI68 reduces its DNA-binding affinity to the rDNA promoter, leading to the repression of RNA Pol I-mediated transcription.
2. **Opposing Role of SIRT7**: Unlike SIRT1, the nucleolar sirtuin **SIRT7** acts as a positive regulator of RNA Pol I-mediated transcription. SIRT7 interacts directly with the RNA Pol I transcription machinery and histones (predominantly H2A and H2B) at the rDNA locus, promoting ribosomal RNA synthesis.
3. **Acetylation by PCAF**: The acetyltransferase **PCAF** (p300/CBP-associated factor) acetylates TAFI68, which enhances its DNA-binding capacity and stimulates transcription, opposing the repressive action of SIRT1.

## Connections

- [[SIRT7]] — nucleolar sirtuin that stimulates RNA Pol I-mediated transcription, opposing SIRT1's nucleolar effects
