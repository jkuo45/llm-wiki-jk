# TAFI68

**TAFI68** (TBP-associated factor I 68, encoded by the *TAF1B* gene) is the second largest subunit of the TBP-containing complex **TIF-IB/SL1**, an essential basal transcription factor regulating the transcription of ribosomal RNA (rDNA) by **[[RNA Polymerase I (RNA Pol I)]]**. TAFI68 serves as a major molecular switch for ribosome biogenesis through its reversible acetylation state. It was the first non-histone substrate identified for the sirtuin [[SIRT1]], establishing a paradigm for sirtuin control of the nucleolar transcriptional program.

## Structure and Basal Factor Function

TAFI68 is a subunit of TIF-IB/SL1, the basal factor that directs Pol I to the rDNA promoter. SL1 comprises TBP and TAFI subunits (TAFI110, TAFI68/TAF1B, TAFI48, TAFI41), and TAFI68 contributes directly to promoter recognition and stable complex assembly with the upstream binding factor UBF. Through its DNA-binding and protein–protein interaction surfaces, TAFI68 helps establish the pre-initiation architecture required for high-fidelity, high-output rRNA synthesis — the rate-limiting step of [[Ribosome Biogenesis]].

## Regulation by Sirtuins and Acetyltransferases

The transcriptional activity of RNA Polymerase I is dynamically controlled by post-translational modifications of TAFI68:

1. **Deacetylation by SIRT1**: TAFI68 was the first non-histone substrate identified for mouse **SIRT1**. SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of TAFI68 reduces its DNA-binding affinity to the rDNA promoter, leading to the repression of RNA Pol I-mediated transcription. This restraint couples ribosome production to cellular energy and stress state via the [[NAD+]] sensor.
2. **Opposing Role of SIRT7**: Unlike SIRT1, the nucleolar sirtuin **SIRT7** acts as a positive regulator of RNA Pol I-mediated transcription. SIRT7 interacts directly with the RNA Pol I transcription machinery and histones (predominantly H2A and H2B) at the rDNA locus, promoting ribosomal RNA synthesis. SIRT7 can acetylate/activate components of the Pol I machinery, opposing SIRT1.
3. **Acetylation by PCAF**: The acetyltransferase **PCAF** (p300/CBP-associated factor) acetylates TAFI68, which enhances its DNA-binding capacity and stimulates transcription, opposing the repressive action of SIRT1.

The net rRNA output thus reflects a balance among SIRT1 (repressive), SIRT7 (activating), and PCAF (activating) — a sirtuin tug-of-war that adjusts protein synthesis capacity to metabolic and growth cues.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Because ribosome biogenesis is hijacked in cancer and declines in [[Aging]], TAFI68 acetylation status is functionally important. SIRT1-mediated repression of Pol I via TAFI68 may protect cells from oncogenic overgrowth, while SIRT7-driven activation supports proliferation. The opposing sirtuin actions at the nucleolus (also seen with [[RNA Polymerase I]]) make TAFI68 a nexus connecting the [[Sirtuins]] to growth control, [[Cellular Senescence]], and tumor biology. Loss of SIRT7 recapitulates the senescent, low-rRNA phenotype, underscoring the physiological weight of this regulatory node.
