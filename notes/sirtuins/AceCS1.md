# AceCS1

**AceCS1** (Acetyl-CoA Synthetase 1, cytoplasmic; encoded by *ACSS2*) is a cytoplasmic enzyme that catalyzes the ATP-dependent conversion of acetate and CoA into acetyl-CoA. Cytoplasmic acetyl-CoA is primarily used as a precursor for fatty acid synthesis, cholesterol biosynthesis, and—critically—histone acetylation, thereby linking acetate metabolism to epigenetic regulation of gene expression. AceCS1 is the cytosolic paralogue of the mitochondrial [[AceCS2]] (ACSS1), and both are members of the AMP-binding protein superfamily of acyl-CoA synthetases.

## Catalytic Mechanism

AceCS1 performs a two-step reaction. First, acetate and ATP form acetyl-AMP with release of pyrophosphate; second, the acetyl group is transferred to CoA, yielding acetyl-CoA. The enzyme contains the conserved AMP-binding domain and a C-terminal domain that binds CoA. Kinetic regulation by lysine acetylation is a hallmark of this enzyme family: acetylation at a key active-site-proximal lysine impairs catalysis, while deacetylation restores full activity—a toggle that lets the cell match acetyl-CoA production to its NAD⁺/energy state.

## Regulation by SIRT1

Like its mitochondrial counterpart [[AceCS2]], AceCS1 is post-translationally regulated by reversible lysine acetylation and is a direct target of the nuclear/cytoplasmic sirtuin [[SIRT1]]:

- **Activation by Deacetylation**: [[SIRT1]] deacetylates AceCS1 on a key conserved lysine residue, which directly activates the enzyme and increases cytosolic acetyl-CoA flux.
- **Role in Fatty Acid Synthesis**: During nutrient-rich states, active AceCS1 provides acetyl-CoA for lipogenesis and cholesterol synthesis. Under fasting or [[Caloric Restriction|Caloric Restriction]] conditions, SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of AceCS1 helps coordinate the cellular shift away from de novo fatty acid synthesis and toward fatty acid oxidation and acetate utilization, preserving energy homeostasis.
- **Epigenetic Coupling**: Because AceCS1-derived acetyl-CoA feeds histone acetyltransferases, SIRT1 control of AceCS1 indirectly shapes the acetylome, coupling nutrient sensing to chromatin state.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

AceCS1/ACSS2 is upregulated in many cancers, where acetate serves as an alternative carbon source supporting lipogenesis and histone acetylation under nutrient stress—a process termed "acetate scavenging." Its nuclear pool specifically fuels histone acetylation to sustain proliferative gene programs. The enzyme is therefore a candidate target in cancer metabolism and a node through which [[SIRT1]] activity intersects with both lipid synthesis and epigenetic control.
