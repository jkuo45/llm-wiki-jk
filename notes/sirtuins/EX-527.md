# EX-527 (Selisistat)

**EX-527** (commonly known as **Selisistat**) is a potent, cell-permeable, and highly selective small-molecule inhibitor of **[[SIRT1]]**. It is widely utilized as a standard pharmacological research tool to elucidate the physiological functions of SIRT1 deacetylation activity in cellular and animal models.

---

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- **Class**: Indole derivative sirtuin inhibitor.
- **Target Selectivity**: High selectivity for [[SIRT1]] ($IC_{50} = 0.098\ \mu\text{M}$) over [[SIRT2]] ($IC_{50} \approx 20\ \mu\text{M}$) and [[SIRT3]] ($IC_{50} \approx 50\ \mu\text{M}$).
- **Structure**: Simple indole-based scaffold.

---

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

### Mechanism of Action

EX-527 blocks SIRT1 by entering the nicotinamide-binding pocket and mimicking the reaction intermediate, thereby acting as a nicotinamide analog/competitor. This selectively halts the NAD⁺-dependent deacetylation of target proteins (such as [[p53]]) and induces their hyperacetylation.

### Pathological and Physiological Effects

- **Apoptosis Induction**: By preventing SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of [[p53]] at Lys³⁸², EX-527 stabilizes p53 and facilitates transcription of pro-apoptotic genes (like Bax/PUMA), causing apoptosis in cancer cells.
- **Chemosensitivity**: EX-527 reverses chemoresistance in several tumor types. For example, it sensitizes endometrial cancer cells (with mutated/non-functional p53) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cells to cisplatin and other chemotherapeutic agents.
- **Tissue Remodeling**: EX-527 has been reported to modulate autophagy. While SIRT1 inhibition generally compromises autophagic flux, under certain disease conditions, EX-527 has been shown to block abnormal autophagy, restoring tissue integrity.

---

## Applications

- **Oncology Research**: Employed in xenograft mouse models of endometrial and lung cancer to suppress tumor growth and evaluate synergy with chemotherapeutics.
- **Huntington's Disease**: Selisistat has been investigated in clinical trials (Phase I/II) for Huntington's disease due to its favorable brain penetration and therapeutic potential in modulating protein aggregation.
- **Kidney Injury**: Used to study the role of SIRT1 in acute kidney injury (AKI) and tubulointerstitial fibrosis, where hyperacetylation of regulatory factors (like FoxO3a) is examined.
