# SERCA2a

**SERCA2a** (Sarco/Endoplasmic Reticulum Ca2+-ATPase 2a, encoded by *ATP2A2*) is a P-type ATPase that pumps Ca2+ from the cytosol into the sarcoplasmic reticulum lumen. It is the dominant SERCA isoform in cardiac muscle and is indispensable for excitation–contraction coupling, myocyte relaxation (lusitropy), and diastolic function. SERCA2a is a direct substrate of the nuclear sirtuin [[SIRT1]], making it a key conduit by which [[NAD+]]-dependent deacetylation protects the heart.

## Structure and Mechanism

SERCA2a is a ~110 kDa transmembrane protein embedded in the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) membrane. Its catalytic cycle alternates between high-affinity Ca2+-binding (E1) and low-affinity Ca2+-releasing (E2) conformations, driven by ATP hydrolysis at the cytosolic nucleotide-binding domain. The pump sequesters two Ca2+ ions per ATP consumed, establishing the steep Ca2+ gradient required for rapid myofilament relaxation after each contraction. SERCA2a works in concert with phospholamban (PLN), whose phosphorylation relieves inhibition of the pump and further accelerates Ca2+ reuptake. The efficiency of SERCA2a thus determines both contractility (systole) and relaxation (diastole).

## Regulation by SIRT1

[[SIRT1]] directly deacetylates SERCA2a at lysine residue [[K492]], restoring its ATPase activity and Ca2+ uptake capacity. In failing hearts, SERCA2a expression and activity are diminished, contributing to diastolic Ca2+ overload and contractile dysfunction. SIRT1-mediated deacetylation at K492 counteracts this defect, improving SR Ca2+ load, contractility, and relaxation in cardiomyocytes. Pharmacological activation of SIRT1 (e.g., with resveratrol or [[NAD+]] precursors) enhances SERCA2a function and represents a therapeutic strategy for [[Heart Failure|heart failure]]. By maintaining Ca2+ homeostasis, SIRT1 also limits Ca2+-dependent activation of calcineurin/NFAT hypertrophic signaling, linking the deacetylase to protection against pathological remodeling.

## Physiological and Pathological Role

Beyond the heart, SERCA2a (and its splice isoform SERCA2b) is expressed in many tissues, including the brain and vascular smooth muscle, where it regulates intracellular Ca2+ signaling. Loss-of-function mutations in *ATP2A2* cause Darier disease, a skin disorder highlighting the pump's importance in epithelial adhesion. In ageing and cardiomyopathy, declining SIRT1 and rising SERCA2a acetylation impair Ca2+ handling, contributing to the [[Cardiovascular Disease]] burden of ageing and to the [[Aging]] phenotype more broadly.

## Clinical Relevance

Loss of SERCA2a activity is a hallmark of advanced heart failure. Gene therapy approaches delivering SERCA2a (e.g., AAV1/SERCA2a in the CUPID trials) have been explored clinically, with mixed results but proof-of-concept for enzymatic restoration. The SIRT1–SERCA2a axis provides an additional post-translational node for therapeutic intervention through sirtuin activation, offering a pharmacologic alternative to gene delivery.
