# RNA Polymerase I

[[RNA Polymerase I]] (Pol I) is the eukaryotic enzyme responsible for transcribing ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes — including 18S, 5.8S, and 28S rRNA — in the [[Nucleolus]]. Pol I activity is the rate-limiting step for [[Ribosome Biogenesis|ribosome biogenesis]] and is tightly coupled to cellular growth, proliferation, and energy status. As the engine of ribosomal RNA production, Pol I consumes the majority of transcriptional effort in rapidly dividing and metabolically active cells, and its regulation by the [[Sirtuins]] provides a direct link between the [[NAD+]] energy sensor and protein synthetic capacity.

## Structure and Transcription Cycle

Pol I is a 14-subunit multi-protein complex. In humans it is recruited to the rDNA promoter by the basal factor TIF-IB/SL1 (containing [[TAFI68]]/TAF1B and TBP), while the upstream binding factor UBF shapes the promoter into a productive architecture. Elongation is driven by Pol I with the help of factors such as PAF53 and PAF49. Because each ribosome requires a single 45S precursor rRNA transcript that is processed into the mature species, the flux through Pol I sets the ceiling on protein synthesis. Oncogenic and growth signals (mTOR, c-Myc, ERK) converge to maximize Pol I output, whereas stress signals suppress it.

## Sirtuin Connections

- [[SIRT7]] directly interacts with [[RNA Polymerase I]] to activate rRNA transcription. SIRT7 is enriched at rDNA loci and is required for efficient Pol I transcription, linking sirtuin activity to [[Ribosome Biogenesis]] and cell proliferation. SIRT7 deacetylates the Pol I machinery and associated histones (H2A/H2B), licensing rDNA transcription.
- [[SIRT1]] also localizes to the nucleolus under certain conditions and can modulate Pol I transcription through deacetylation of [[TAFI68]] (TAF1B), a Pol I-specific transcription factor. Counterintuitively, SIRT1 deacetylation of TAFI68 reduces its rDNA binding and represses Pol I — a restraint that may protect against unrestrained growth.
- [[NAD+]] availability and cellular energy status regulate sirtuin-dependent Pol I activity, coupling [[Ribosome Biogenesis]] to metabolic state.

The opposition of SIRT7 (activating) and SIRT1 (restraining) at the nucleolus highlights sirtuins as both accelerators and brakes on the ribosomal program depending on isoform and context.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Pol I hyperactivation is a near-universal feature of cancer, making it a therapeutic target (e.g., CX-5461). Conversely, declining Pol I output contributes to the reduced protein synthesis and [[Senescence]] of ageing. SIRT7 deficiency in mice reduces rRNA synthesis and accelerates senescence, tying Pol I regulation to [[Aging]]. The p53 tumor suppressor represses Pol I under stress, and SIRT1-mediated p53 deacetylation relieves this repression — a further node connecting the DNA-damage response to ribosome production.
