# RNA Polymerase I

[[RNA Polymerase I]] (Pol I) is the eukaryotic enzyme responsible for transcribing ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes — including 18S, 5.8S, and 28S rRNA — in the [[nucleolus]]. Pol I activity is the rate-limiting step for [[Ribosome Biogenesis|ribosome biogenesis]] and is tightly coupled to cellular growth, proliferation, and energy status.

## Sirtuin Connections

- [[SIRT7]] directly interacts with [[RNA Polymerase I]] to activate rRNA transcription. SIRT7 is enriched at rDNA loci and is required for efficient Pol I transcription, linking sirtuin activity to [[Ribosome Biogenesis]] and cell proliferation.
- [[SIRT1]] also localizes to the nucleolus under certain conditions and can modulate Pol I transcription through deacetylation of [[TAFI68]] (TAF1B), a Pol I-specific transcription factor.
- [[NAD+]] availability and cellular energy status regulate sirtuin-dependent Pol I activity, coupling [[Ribosome Biogenesis]] to metabolic state.

## Connections

- [[SIRT7]]: The primary sirtuin regulator of Pol I transcription; SIRT7 knockout mice exhibit reduced rRNA synthesis and [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]].
- [[Ribosome Biogenesis]]: The downstream process driven by Pol I transcription.
- [[p53]]: Represses Pol I transcription in response to stress; SIRT1 deacetylation of p53 relieves this repression.
- [[Cancer]]: Hyperactivation of Pol I transcription is a hallmark of many cancers, making it a therapeutic target.
