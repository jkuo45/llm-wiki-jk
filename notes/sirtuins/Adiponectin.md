# Adiponectin

**Adiponectin** (encoded by the *ADIPOQ* gene; also known as Acrp30) is an adipocyte-derived hormone (adipokine) secreted primarily by white adipose tissue. It plays an essential role in regulating glucose homeostasis, lipid metabolism, energy expenditure, and insulin sensitivity. Uniquely among adipokines, circulating adiponectin levels are inversely correlated with body fat percentage and are significantly reduced in obesity, insulin resistance, and type 2 diabetes—making it a protective, rather than inflammatory, adipose signal.

## Structure

Adiponectin is assembled from a collagenous N-terminal domain and a C-terminal globular domain homologous to complement factor C1q. It circulates as trimers, hexamers, and high-molecular-weight (HMW) multimeric complexes; the HMW form is considered the most biologically active with respect to insulin sensitization. It signals through two transmembrane receptors, AdipoR1 (abundant in muscle) and AdipoR2 (abundant in liver).

## Regulation by the SIRT1–FOXO1 Pathway

Adiponectin transcription and secretion are directly controlled by a nutrient-sensing sirtuin pathway:

- **Transcription Complex**: FOXO1 forms a complex at the adiponectin promoter with **C/EBPα (CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein α)**.
- **SIRT1 Deacetylation**: [[SIRT1]] deacetylates [[FoxO1]] on key lysine residues, enhancing its physical interaction with C/EBPα and directly activating the adiponectin promoter to boost expression and secretion.
- **Caloric Restriction**: [[Caloric Restriction|Caloric Restriction]] increases circulating adiponectin in rodents and primates, an effect mediated in part by SIRT1 activation under low nutrient availability.

This places adiponectin downstream of the same SIRT1 axis that governs [[Longevity]] and metabolic health, linking fat-tissue endocrine output to systemic nutrient sensing.

## Physiological Functions

Adiponectin acts via AdipoR1/AdipoR2 to:

- Activate **[[AMPK]] (AMP-activated protein kinase)**, stimulating glucose uptake and fatty acid oxidation.
- Improve insulin sensitivity in skeletal muscle and liver, suppressing hepatic gluconeogenesis.
- Protect against insulin resistance, [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], and [[Atherosclerosis]].
- Exert anti-inflammatory and endothelial-protective effects.

Adiponectin also activates [[PPARα]] and influences [[SIRT1]] itself, creating reinforcing loops that favor oxidative metabolism over lipid storage.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

Low adiponectin is a risk marker for metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Adiponectin replacement and SIRT1-activating interventions (e.g., [[Resveratrol]], caloric restriction mimetics) are explored to restore insulin sensitivity. The SIRT1–FOXO1–adiponectin axis is thus a mechanistic bridge between sirtuin biology and cardiometabolic disease prevention.
