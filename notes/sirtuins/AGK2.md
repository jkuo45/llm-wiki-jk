# AGK2

**AGK2** is a potent, cell-permeable, and selective small-molecule inhibitor of **[[SIRT2]]**. It is widely utilized in neurobiology and oncology research to study the pathogenic consequences of SIRT2 activity, particularly in neurodegenerative disorders and acute ischemic brain injuries.

---

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- **Class**: Synthetic small-molecule sirtuin inhibitor.
- **Target Selectivity**: Highly selective for [[SIRT2]] ($IC_{50} = 3.5\ \mu\text{M}$), with over 10-fold selectivity over [[SIRT1]] and [[SIRT3]].

---

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

### Mechanism of Action

AGK2 selectively binds to the catalytic cleft of [[SIRT2]], blocking its deacetylation activity. This prevents the deacetylation of primary cytosolic targets of SIRT2, including microtubule-associated $\alpha$-tubulin, **[[FOXO3a]]**, and [[p53]].

### Neuroprotective & Apoptotic Pathways

- **Parkinson's Disease (PD)**: A landmark therapeutic application of AGK2 is the rescue of dopaminergic neurons from misfolded $\alpha$-synuclein-mediated toxicity. SIRT2 inhibition via AGK2 prevents the deacetylation of $\alpha$-synuclein and microtubule elements, suppressing aggregation and protecting cell viability.
- **Ischemic Stroke & Brain Injury**: Administration of AGK2 confers significant neuroprotection under ischemic and hypoxic conditions both _in vitro_ and _in vivo_. Mechanistically, AGK2 suppresses the apoptotic cascade by downregulating phosphorylated JNK (phospho-JNK) and **[[FOXO3a]]** (the direct downstream target of SIRT2), which ultimately prevents Bim activation and subsequent caspase-3 cleavage.
- **Apoptosis Regulation**: In other pathological states, SIRT2 deacetylation can promote or inhibit cell survival. SIRT2 overexpression can drive apoptosis by upregulating cleaved caspase-3 and Bax while suppressing Bcl-2; AGK2 blocks this apoptotic induction.

---

## Applications

- **Neurodegenerative Research**: Widely used to model therapeutic inhibition of SIRT2 in Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, and Huntington's diseases.
- **Cerebral Ischemia**: Evaluated in pre-clinical rodent models of stroke to reduce infarct size and neuronal cell death.
- **Cancer Biology**: Utilized to evaluate SIRT2 dependencies in various human tumor cell lines.
