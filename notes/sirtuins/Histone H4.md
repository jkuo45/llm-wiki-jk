# Histone H4

**Histone H4** is one of the five core histone proteins that form the nucleosome, the fundamental repeating unit of chromatin. Acetylation of its N-terminal tail, particularly at Lys16 (H4K16ac), is a well-characterized epigenetic mark associated with active transcription and open chromatin conformation. H4 is the most evolutionarily conserved core histone, with a single acetylatable N-terminal tail extending from a structured globular domain.

## Structure & Mechanism

The H4 N-terminal tail harbors multiple lysine residues (K5, K8, K12, K16) subject to acetylation, methylation, and other modifications. H4K16ac is unique in that it disrupts internucleosomal contacts by hindering fiber folding rather than altering charge alone, making it a key mark of large-scale chromatin decompaction. The deposition of new H4 during replication is handled by the CAF-1 complex.

## SIRT1 Deacetylation

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates histone H4 at **Lys16** (H4K16ac), a modification critical for chromatin structure and gene regulation. H4K16ac is enriched at transcriptionally active gene promoters and along the X chromosome in male mammals. Deacetylation by SIRT1 removes this active mark, promoting a more compact, transcriptionally repressive chromatin state. This activity positions SIRT1 as a negative regulator of gene expression at least partly through H4K16ac erasure.

## SIRT2 Deacetylation and Cell Cycle

[[SIRT2]] also deacetylates H4 Lys16, specifically during the **G2/M transition** of the cell cycle. This deacetylation event coincides with chromatin condensation at mitosis and is thought to facilitate proper chromosome segregation. H4K16ac is absent from condensed mitotic chromosomes, and SIRT2-mediated deacetylation contributes to this mitotic chromatin remodeling. SIRT2 thus couples the cell-cycle clock to chromatin compaction, and its loss produces mitotic defects and aneuploidy.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

H4K16ac dynamics integrate metabolic state (via [[NAD+]]-dependent sirtuins) with chromatin architecture. Deregulated H4 acetylation is observed in cancer, where global H4K16 hypoacetylation is a hallmark of several tumor types, and in [[Aging]], where sirtuin activity declines.
