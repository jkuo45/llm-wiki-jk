# Histone H4

**Histone H4** is one of the five core histone proteins that form the nucleosome, the fundamental repeating unit of chromatin. Acetylation of its N-terminal tail, particularly at Lys16 (H4K16ac), is a well-characterized epigenetic mark associated with active transcription and open chromatin conformation.

## SIRT1 Deacetylation

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates histone H4 at **Lys16** (H4K16ac), a modification critical for chromatin structure and gene regulation. H4K16ac is enriched at transcriptionally active gene promoters and along the X chromosome in male mammals. Deacetylation by SIRT1 removes this active mark, promoting a more compact, transcriptionally repressive chromatin state.

## SIRT2 Deacetylation and Cell Cycle

[[SIRT2]] also deacetylates H4 Lys16, specifically during the **G2/M transition** of the cell cycle. This deacetylation event coincides with chromatin condensation at mitosis and is thought to facilitate proper chromosome segregation. H4K16ac is absent from condensed mitotic chromosomes, and SIRT2-mediated deacetylation contributes to this mitotic chromatin remodeling.

## Connections

- [[SIRT1]] — deacetylates H4K16ac in interphase, promoting transcriptional repression
- [[SIRT2]] — deacetylates H4K16ac during G2/M, facilitating mitotic chromatin condensation
- [[Histone H1]] — linker histone deacetylated by SIRT1; works cooperatively in chromatin compaction
