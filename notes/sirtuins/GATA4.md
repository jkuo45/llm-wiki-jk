# GATA Binding Protein 4 (GATA4)

**GATA Binding Protein 4 (GATA4)** is a zinc finger transcription factor belonging to the GATA family that recognizes the GATA motif (WGATAR) in gene promoters. It is essential for [[Cardiac Development]], [[Cardiomyocyte]] differentiation, and the hypertrophic response to pressure overload and neurohumoral stimuli. GATA4 is expressed predominantly in the heart, but also in liver, lung, gut, and gonads, where it governs tissue-specific gene programs.

## Structure & Domains

GATA4 contains two conserved zinc finger domains (N-terminal ZnF1 and C-terminal ZnF2). The C-terminal finger is the principal DNA-binding module, while the N-terminal finger stabilizes the interaction and mediates cofactor recruitment. Between and flanking the fingers lie transactivation domains that engage partners including [[NKX2-5]], [[TBX5]], and [[MEF2C]] within a core cardiac transcriptional network. GATA4 also harbors regulatory acetylation sites whose modification state controls its activity.

## Physiological Role

During embryogenesis GATA4 is required for formation of the primitive heart tube, endocardial cushion development, and ventricular septation. In the adult heart, GATA4 maintains expression of contractile and ion-handling genes and orchestrates adaptation to stress. Hypertrophic stimuli — [[Angiotensin II]], [[Endothelin-1]], phenylephrine, and mechanical stretch — drive GATA4 acetylation and nuclear accumulation, activating fetal gene reprogramming ([[ANP]], [[BNP]], β-[[Myosin Heavy Chain]]) that underlies pathological [[Cardiac Hypertrophy]].

## SIRT7 Regulation

[[SIRT7]] directly deacetylates GATA4, ameliorating stress-induced [[Cardiac Hypertrophy]]. SIRT7-mediated deacetylation suppresses GATA4 transcriptional activity at hypertrophic gene promoters, including those for [[ANP]], [[BNP]], and β-[[Myosin Heavy Chain]] (β-MHC). This anti-hypertrophic action positions SIRT7 as a cardioprotective sirtuin that counteracts pathological cardiac remodeling. SIRT7-deficient mice develop exaggerated hypertrophy and heart failure after pressure overload, underscoring the physiological importance of this axis.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Dysregulated GATA4 activity contributes to cardiomyopathy, congenital heart defects (loss-of-function variants), and heart failure. The SIRT7–GATA4 axis is a candidate therapeutic target: strategies that preserve or enhance SIRT7 deacetylase activity may limit maladaptive remodeling. [[Resveratrol]] and other sirtuin-modulating compounds may indirectly affect GATA4 acetylation through [[NAD+]]-dependent sirtuin activation, though SIRT7-specific pharmacology remains nascent.
