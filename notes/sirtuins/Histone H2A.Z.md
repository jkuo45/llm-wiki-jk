# Histone H2A.Z

**Histone H2A.Z** (encoded by *H2AFZ*) is a highly conserved histone H2A variant that replaces canonical H2A in a subset of nucleosomes. It plays specialized roles in transcriptional regulation, DNA repair, and heterochromatin boundary establishment. H2A.Z is enriched at promoters of actively transcribed genes and at regulatory elements.

## Structure & Mechanism

H2A.Z differs from canonical H2A by an extended acidic C-terminal tail and a structurally distinct L1 loop, which alter nucleosome stability and the ease of chromatin remodeling. Incorporation of H2A.Z into nucleosomes is mediated by the SWR1/[[SRCAP]] chromatin-remodeling complex and removed by the INO80 complex. At promoters, H2A.Z-containing nucleosomes are less stable, facilitating transcription initiation; at heterochromatin boundaries it blocks the spread of silent chromatin.

## Physiological Role

H2A.Z regulates developmental gene expression, X-chromosome inactivation, telomere integrity, and the DNA damage response. Its dosage is tightly controlled: both depletion and overexpression are deleterious, and *H2AFZ* is essential in mice.

## SIRT1 Deacetylation and Proteasomal Degradation

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates H2A.Z at **Lys115** and **Lys121**. Deacetylation of these residues promotes **ubiquitination and proteasome-dependent degradation** of H2A.Z. By controlling H2A.Z turnover, SIRT1 regulates the chromatin landscape and gene expression programs. Acetylation at these lysines normally protects H2A.Z from ubiquitin-mediated destruction; SIRT1 removes that protection, accelerating clearance of the variant and resetting promoter nucleosome composition.

## Cardioprotective Role

This SIRT1–H2A.Z axis has been specifically implicated in protection against **cardiac hypertrophy**. Under pathological hypertrophic stress, SIRT1-mediated deacetylation and degradation of H2A.Z prevents aberrant gene activation programs that drive cardiomyocyte enlargement and heart failure. This identifies a novel epigenetic mechanism for cardiac protection regulated by the sirtuin pathway, connecting metabolic sensing ([[NAD+]]) to chromatin dynamics in the heart.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Altered H2A.Z levels are reported in cancer and cardiovascular disease. Therapeutic modulation of the SIRT1–H2A.Z axis may limit maladaptive cardiac remodeling, though precise delivery remains challenging.
