# Sirtuins Overview

Sirtuins (SIRT1–SIRT7) are a family of seven NAD⁺-dependent enzymes (class III histone deacetylases/deacylases) in humans. They play key roles in metabolism, aging, stress response, DNA repair, inflammation, gene expression, and cellular homeostasis.

nature.com

They share a conserved catalytic core but differ in subcellular localization, enzymatic activities (deacetylation, ADP-ribosylation, desuccinylation, etc.), and specific functions. Their activity depends on NAD⁺ levels, linking them to cellular energy status. They are implicated in longevity, calorie restriction benefits, and age-related diseases like diabetes, cancer, neurodegeneration, and cardiovascular issues.

lifespan.io

Overview of Localization and Classes

- Nuclear/cytoplasmic (Class I): SIRT1, SIRT2 (SIRT1 shuttles between nucleus and cytosol).
- Mitochondrial (primarily): SIRT3, SIRT4, SIRT5.
- Nuclear (Class IV): SIRT6 (chromatin-associated), SIRT7 (nucleolar).
  nature.com

Localization can vary by cell type, cell cycle stage, and conditions.SIRT1 (Nuclear/Cytosolic, Class I)

- Primary roles: Master regulator of metabolism, stress resistance, and longevity. Deacetylates histones (e.g., H3K9, H3K26) and many non-histone proteins (p53, FOXO, NF-κB, PGC-1α, etc.).
- Key functions: Promotes mitochondrial biogenesis, fatty acid oxidation, gluconeogenesis regulation, insulin sensitivity, DNA repair, autophagy, reduces inflammation. Involved in caloric restriction benefits.
- Significance: Often called the "longevity sirtuin." Activation linked to anti-aging effects.
  lifespan.io

SIRT2 (Cytosolic/Nuclear, Class I)

- Primary roles: Mainly cytosolic; enters nucleus during mitosis. Deacetylase and deacylase.
- Key functions: Regulates cell cycle (e.g., tubulin deacetylation), mitosis, cytoskeletal dynamics, glucose and lipid metabolism. Involved in oxidative stress response and neurodegeneration (e.g., Parkinson's links).
- Significance: Dual roles in cancer (tumor suppressor or promoter depending on context).
  nature.com

SIRT3 (Mitochondrial, Class I)

- Primary roles: Major mitochondrial deacetylase (also decrotonylase). Has a mitochondrial targeting sequence.
- Key functions: Regulates mitochondrial metabolism, fatty acid oxidation, TCA cycle, electron transport chain, ROS detoxification (e.g., via SOD2/Mn-SOD). Protects against oxidative stress and maintains mitochondrial integrity.
- Significance: Key for energy homeostasis; decline linked to aging and metabolic diseases. Often protective in heart and liver.
  lifespan.io

SIRT4 (Mitochondrial, Class II)

- Primary roles: Primarily ADP-ribosyltransferase (weaker deacetylase).
- Key functions: Regulates insulin secretion, glutamine metabolism, fatty acid oxidation, and mitochondrial function. Inhibits glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH).
- Significance: More inhibitory role in metabolism compared to SIRT3; involved in nutrient sensing.
  aginganddisease.org

SIRT5 (Mitochondrial, Class III)

- Primary roles: Deacylase with preference for desuccinylation, demalonylation, and deglutarylation.
- Key functions: Regulates urea cycle (e.g., CPS1), fatty acid oxidation, ROS management, and ketogenesis. Modulates mitochondrial enzymes.
- Significance: Important in ammonia detoxification and metabolic adaptation (e.g., fasting).
  aginganddisease.org

SIRT6 (Nuclear/Chromatin, Class IV)

- Primary roles: Deacetylase (e.g., H3K9, H3K56) and deacylase; chromatin-associated.
- Key functions: Maintains genomic stability, telomere function, DNA repair (base excision repair), regulates glycolysis and lipid metabolism (represses HIF-1α, SREBP), inflammation control.
- Significance: Strong links to longevity; SIRT6 overexpression extends lifespan in mice. Tumor suppressor role.
  lifespan.io

SIRT7 (Nucleolar/Nuclear, Class IV)

- Primary roles: Deacetylase (histones) and desuccinylase; enriched in nucleolus.
- Key functions: Regulates rRNA transcription (via RNA Pol I), ribosome biogenesis, DNA damage repair, chromatin organization. Involved in stress response and metabolism.
- Significance: Supports cell growth/proliferation; roles in cancer and heart function.
  mdpi.com

Sirtuins are promising therapeutic targets. Activators (e.g., resveratrol for SIRT1, NAD⁺ precursors like NMN/NR) and inhibitors are under study for metabolic, neurodegenerative, and oncologic conditions. Effects can be context-dependent (tissue, disease state). Consult recent reviews or specialists for the latest research, as the field evolves rapidly.

Sirtuins are a family of NAD⁺-dependent enzymes (usually 7 in mammals: SIRT1–7) that play key roles in metabolic regulation, stress response, DNA repair, aging, and gene expression. They act primarily by removing specific chemical modifications from proteins (and sometimes other molecules).Here is a clear explanation of each activity mentioned:1. Mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase activity

- What it does: Sirtuins can transfer a single ADP-ribose group from NAD⁺ onto a target protein (usually on arginine, lysine, or other residues).
- Effect: This is a form of post-translational modification (ADP-ribosylation) that can alter protein function, localization, or interactions.
- Context: This was one of the earliest activities discovered for some sirtuins (especially SIRT4 and SIRT6). It is distinct from the more famous deacylase activities.

2. Deacylase activity (general term)Deacylase means the enzyme removes an acyl group (a fatty-acid-like chain or other carboxylic acid derivative) that is attached to a lysine residue on a protein. This is usually coupled with the hydrolysis of NAD⁺.  
   The specific deacylase activities listed are subtypes based on the exact acyl group being removed:• Deacetylase activity

- Removes: An acetyl group (–COCH₃) from lysine residues (Lys-acetyl).
- Most common and best-studied sirtuin activity.
- Examples: SIRT1, SIRT2, SIRT3 are strong deacetylases.
- Biological role: Regulates histones (gene expression), transcription factors (e.g., p53, FOXO, PGC-1α), metabolic enzymes, etc. Often linked to longevity and caloric restriction benefits.

• Demalonylase activity

- Removes: A malonyl group (–COCH₂COO⁻) from lysine.
- Less common.
- Malonylation is a metabolic mark often associated with high glucose/lipid conditions.
- Helps regulate mitochondrial metabolism and fatty acid synthesis.

• Desuccinylase activity

- Removes: A succinyl group (–COCH₂CH₂COO⁻) from lysine.
- Important in mitochondria (especially SIRT5).
- Succinylation is a marker of metabolic status; removing it regulates enzymes in the TCA cycle, fatty acid oxidation, and ketone body metabolism.

• Depalmitoylase activity

- Removes: A palmitoyl group (C16 saturated fatty acid chain) from lysine (or sometimes cysteine).
- S-Palmitoylation or K-palmitoylation is a reversible lipid modification that anchors proteins to membranes.
- Sirtuins (e.g., SIRT2, SIRT6) can remove this bulky lipid group, affecting protein trafficking, signaling, and membrane association.

• Demyristoylase activity

- Removes: A myristoyl group (C14 saturated fatty acid chain) from lysine.
- Similar to depalmitoylation but with a slightly shorter chain.
- Myristoylation is involved in protein targeting to membranes and in apoptotic pathways.
- Some sirtuins (e.g., SIRT6) show efficient demyristoylase activity.

Key point: Most sirtuins have multiple of these activities, but they differ in preference and efficiency depending on the isoform (SIRT1 is mainly a deacetylase, while SIRT5 is a strong desuccinylase/demalonylase, and SIRT6 has strong de-fatty-acylase activities).These activities allow sirtuins to act as metabolic sensors: they use NAD⁺ as a co-substrate, so their activity rises when cellular energy is low (high NAD⁺/NADH ratio), linking metabolism directly to protein regulation.
