# UCP2

**UCP2** (Uncoupling Protein 2) is a mitochondrial inner membrane transporter protein. It belongs to the family of mitochondrial uncoupling proteins, which act to uncouple oxygen consumption from ATP synthesis by facilitating proton leak across the inner mitochondrial membrane, converting the proton gradient into heat instead of ATP.

## Regulation by SIRT1

In pancreatic $\beta$-cells, UCP2 is transcriptionally repressed by [[SIRT1]], which coordinates insulin secretion:

- **Repression of UCP2**: [[SIRT1]] binds directly to the *UCP2* promoter, promoting histone deacetylation and transcriptional silencing.
- **ATP and Insulin Secretion**: By keeping UCP2 expression low, SIRT1 ensures that glucose-stimulated mitochondrial respiration is tightly coupled to ATP generation. The resulting high ATP-to-ADP ratio triggers potassium channel closure, calcium influx, and enhanced glucose-stimulated insulin secretion (GSIS).
- **Knockout Phenotype**: In the absence of SIRT1 (SIRT1 knockout mice), *UCP2* is highly upregulated in pancreatic $\beta$-cells. This results in uncoupled respiration, reduced ATP generation, and blunted insulin secretion in response to glucose.

## Neurological Relevance

UCP2 is highly expressed in the central nervous system, where it plays a neuroprotective role. It reduces the generation of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) and regulates intracellular calcium levels. UCP2 is neuroprotective in stroke, brain trauma, and Parkinson's disease models. Its expression is often reduced in the brains of Alzheimer's disease patients.
