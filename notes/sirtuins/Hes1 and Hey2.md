# Hes1 and Hey2

**Hes1** (Hairy and Enhancer of Split 1) and **Hey2** (Hairy-related Transcription Factor 2, also known as HRT2 or HERP1) are **basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcriptional repressors** that function as downstream effectors of the **Notch signaling pathway**. They play critical roles in cell fate determination, neurogenesis, cardiovascular development, and maintenance of stem/progenitor cell populations.

## Structure & Mechanism

Hes1 and Hey2 contain a basic region for DNA binding and a HLH domain for dimerization. Hes proteins bind E-box (CACNAG) motifs as Hairy/Enhancer of split (HES) family repressors, recruiting Groucho/TLE corepressors and histone deacetylases to silence target genes. Hey proteins (also called HERP, HRT) bind to E-box and N-box sequences downstream of Notch via the Notch intracellular domain (NICD) and RBPJκ. Notably, Hes1 is expressed in oscillatory waves (ultradian clocks) that pattern the developing nervous system, whereas Hey2 is constitutively expressed in the myocardium and vasculature.

## Physiological Role

Hes1 maintains neural progenitor pools by suppressing proneural genes (*Neurogenin*, *Mash1*), delaying differentiation until the appropriate temporal window. Hey2 is essential for cardiac chamber development, vascular smooth muscle maturation, and somitogenesis. Loss of Hey2 produces ventricular septal defects and dilated cardiomyopathy in mice, highlighting its cardioprotective role.

## SIRT1 Interaction

[[SIRT1]] physically interacts with both Hes1 and Hey2, modulating their ability to repress target promoters. This interaction places SIRT1 as a regulator of Notch-dependent transcriptional programs. By controlling the repressive activity of these bHLH factors, SIRT1 can influence developmental and differentiation processes, including neuronal and cardiac development. SIRT1 deacetylase activity may tune the DNA-binding affinity or co-repressor recruitment of Hes/Hey factors, thereby integrating nutrient/energy status with developmental signaling.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Deregulated Notch–Hes/Hey signaling is implicated in cancers, atherosclerosis, and neurodegeneration. The SIRT1–Hes1/Hey2 interface offers a conduit through which metabolic state (mediated by [[NAD+]] availability) could modulate stem cell quiescence and tissue regeneration, linking [[Longevity]] and [[Aging]] programs to developmental transcription factors.
