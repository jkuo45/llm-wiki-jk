# CDC14B

**CDC14B** (Cell division cycle 14 homolog B) is a highly conserved eukaryotic dual-specificity protein phosphatase. It plays an essential role in cell cycle progression, particularly during mitosis and the G2/M DNA damage checkpoint, by reversing cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) phosphorylations on key cell cycle machinery.

## Regulation and De-phosphorylation of SIRT2

CDC14B is a key negative regulator of the microtubule and cell cycle deacetylase **SIRT2**:

1. **Mitotic Exit**: During the cell cycle, **SIRT2** accumulates and regulates mitotic checkpoint pathways by deacetylating $\alpha$-tubulin and Histone H4 (Lys¹⁶).
2. **Dephosphorylation**: At the exit from mitosis, CDC14B directly dephosphorylates SIRT2.
3. **Proteasomal Degradation**: This dephosphorylation event destabilizes SIRT2, triggering its subsequent ubiquitination and rapid degradation by the **26S proteasome**. This rapid clearance of SIRT2 is required for proper mitotic exit and cytokinesis.
