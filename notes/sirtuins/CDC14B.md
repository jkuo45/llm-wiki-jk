# CDC14B

**CDC14B** (Cell division cycle 14 homolog B) is a highly conserved eukaryotic dual-specificity protein phosphatase. It plays an essential role in cell cycle progression, particularly during mitosis and the G2/M DNA damage checkpoint, by reversing cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) phosphorylations on key cell cycle machinery. Along with its paralogue CDC14A, CDC14B belongs to the conserved CDC14 phosphatase family whose founding member in budding yeast antagonizes CDK activity to permit mitotic exit and cytokinesis.

## Structure and Subcellular Localization

CDC14B contains an N-terminal catalytic domain typical of dual-specificity phosphatases and a C-terminal regulatory region that mediates its sequestration and release. In interphase, CDC14B is held inactive in the nucleolus and at centrosomes; in response to mitotic signals or DNA damage, it is released to the nucleoplasm and cytoplasm where it can act on substrates including CDK-phosphorylated proteins, the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome regulators, and Aurora kinase substrates. Its localization is tightly choreographed to ensure phosphatase activity is unleashed only when appropriate.

## Regulation and De-phosphorylation of SIRT2

CDC14B is a key negative regulator of the microtubule- and cell-cycle-associated deacetylase **[[SIRT2]]**:

1. **Mitotic Accumulation**: During the cell cycle, **SIRT2** accumulates and regulates mitotic checkpoint pathways by deacetylating α-tubulin (at Lys40) and [[Histone H4]] (Lys16), controlling microtubule dynamics and chromosome structure.
2. **Dephosphorylation**: At the exit from mitosis, CDC14B directly dephosphorylates SIRT2, removing phosphates that stabilize the protein during division.
3. **Proteasomal Degradation**: This dephosphorylation event destabilizes SIRT2, triggering its subsequent ubiquitination and rapid degradation by the **26S proteasome**. This clearance of SIRT2 is required for proper mitotic exit and cytokinesis, preventing aberrant microtubule deacetylation in the ensuing interphase.

This CDC14B→SIRT2 axis couples phosphatase-dependent cell-cycle transitions to the acetylation state of the cytoskeleton and chromatin.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Beyond mitosis, CDC14B participates in the DNA damage checkpoint, where its release and activity help arrest or repair damaged cells. Dysregulation of CDC14B or its SIRT2 target has implications for genomic stability and cancer: altered SIRT2 levels influence chromosome segregation fidelity, while loss of orderly SIRT2 degradation can disturb microtubule dynamics and promote aneuploidy. The interplay also intersects with other sirtuins ([[Sirtuins|the mammalian sirtuin family]]) in coordinating metabolism and proliferation.
