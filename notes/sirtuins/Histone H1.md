# Histone H1

**Histone H1** (Linker Histone H1) is a family of basic nuclear proteins that bind to linker DNA between nucleosomes, facilitating higher-order chromatin structure and compaction. Unlike core histones (H2A, H2B, H3, H4), H1 sits outside the nucleosomal core, stabilizing the 30-nm fiber and limiting DNA accessibility. It is the most heterogeneous histone, with multiple somatic subtypes (H1.1–H1.5) plus germline and oocyte variants.

## Structure & Domains

H1 comprises a short N-terminal tail, a central globular winged-helix domain that binds the nucleosome dyad, and a long, protease-sensitive C-terminal tail rich in lysine residues. The C-terminal domain mediates cooperative interactions along the linker DNA, driving chromatin compaction and chromatin fiber oligomerization.

## Physiological Role

By sealing nucleosomes into higher-order structures, H1 restricts access of transcription factors and the transcription machinery, generally repressing transcription and supporting heterochromatin. H1 also participates in [[DNA Repair]], replication timing, and chromosome segregation. Its modular acetylation state tunes the balance between compaction and accessibility.

## SIRT1 Deacetylation

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates histone H1 at **Lys9** and **Lys26**. Deacetylation of these residues modulates chromatin compaction and gene expression by altering H1's electrostatic interaction with DNA. H1 acetylation generally correlates with a more open, transcriptionally permissive state, while SIRT1-mediated deacetylation promotes chromatin condensation and transcriptional repression. Because H1 acetylation neutralizes positive charges and weakens DNA binding, SIRT1's deacetylase action restores tighter linker-DNA association and a repressive chromatin environment.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Aberrant H1 subtype expression and post-translational modification are observed in cancer and during cellular [[Aging]], where global H1 levels decline and chromatin becomes more open. The SIRT1–H1 axis links metabolic state (via [[NAD+]]) to chromatin architecture, providing a mechanism through which [[Caloric Restriction]] and sirtuin activation reshape the epigenome.
