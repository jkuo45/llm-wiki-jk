# Stilbene

**Stilbenes** are a class of [[Polyphenols|polyphenolic]] organic compounds characterized by a 1,2-diphenylethylene [[Stilbene|core structure]] (a central ethene bridge connecting two aromatic rings). They exist as both *cis* and *trans* isomers, with the *trans* configuration being the more biologically active form.

[[Resveratrol]] (*trans-3,4',5-trihydroxystilbene*) is the most extensively studied stilbene, recognized for its [[Antioxidants|antioxidant]], anti-[[Inflammation|inflammatory]], and [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]]-activating properties. Other notable stilbenes include [[Pterostilbene]] (a dimethoxylated analog with improved bioavailability), [[Piceatannol]], and [[Pinosylvin]].

## Biological Sources

Stilbenes are phytoalexins — antimicrobial compounds produced by plants in response to stress, injury, or infection. Key plant sources include:
- **Grapes and red wine** ([[Resveratrol]])
- **Japanese knotweed** (*Polygonum cuspidatum/Reynoutria japonica*)
- **Blueberries, cranberries** ([[Pterostilbene]])
- **Peanuts**
- **Pine trees** ([[Pinosylvin]])

## Pharmacological Relevance

Stilbenes have garnered significant interest for their potential in:
- [[Cardiovascular Disease]] prevention via [[Nitric Oxide]] production enhancement and [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] protection
- [[Aging|Anti-aging]] and [[Longevity]] through [[SIRT1]] activation
- Anti-[[Cancer|neoplastic]] and [[Apoptosis|pro-apoptotic]] activity
