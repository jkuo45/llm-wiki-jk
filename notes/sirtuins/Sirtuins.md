# Sirtuins: The Sirtuin Family in Health and Disease

**Sirtuins (SIRTs)** are a highly conserved family of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD⁺)-dependent enzymes belonging to Class III histone deacetylases (HDACs). Spanning all domains of life, sirtuins serve as metabolic sensors that couple cellular energy status (NAD⁺/NADH ratios) directly to the epigenetic and post-translational modification of key regulatory proteins. In mammals, seven distinct sirtuin homologs (SIRT1–SIRT7) have been identified, each possessing unique subcellular localizations, substrate preferences, and enzymatic profiles.

---

## History, Structure, and Classification

### Historical Context

The founding sirtuin member, **Sir2** (Silent Information Regulator 2), was identified in _Saccharomyces cerevisiae_ in the late 1990s as a key promoter of genomic stability and replicative lifespan extension. Subsequent discoveries established that Sir2's longevity-promoting effects depend on its NAD⁺-dependent HDAC enzymatic activity. Highly conserved mammalian homologs (SIRT1–SIRT7) were subsequently isolated, sparking decades of research into their roles as regulators of cellular health, metabolism, and aging.

### Structural Architecture

All seven mammalian sirtuins share a structurally conserved catalytic core of approximately 275 amino acid residues, containing:

1. **Large Domain**: An inverted open $\alpha/\beta$ Rossmann-fold structure characteristic of NAD⁺-utilizing enzymes.
2. **Small Domain**: A flexible zinc-binding domain containing a zinc ribbon motif.
3. **Catalytic Pocket**: A cleft formed between the two domains where NAD⁺ and the target acetylated peptide bind.

The variable N- and C-terminal sequences of SIRT1–7 determine their substrate specificities, protein-protein interactions, nucleocytoplasmic shuttling, and susceptibility to post-translational modifications (such as phosphorylation).

### Phylogenetic Classification

The mammalian sirtuins are divided into four phylogenetic classes:

- **Class I**: SIRT1, [[SIRT2]], and [[SIRT3]] (possess robust deacetylase activity).
- **Class II**: [[SIRT4]] (evolved primarily as a mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase).
- **Class III**: [[SIRT5]] (exhibits unique demalonylase, desuccinylase, and deglutarylase activities).
- **Class IV**: [[SIRT6]] and [[SIRT7]] (possess distinct deacetylase, deacylase, and mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase functions).

## Core Physiological Roles

Mammalian sirtuins operate in concert or compensate for one another to regulate central physiological networks:

### A. Inflammation and Immune Cell Function

- **SIRT1 and SIRT6**: Act as primary anti-inflammatory sirtuins by deacetylating the RelA/p65 subunit of [[NFKB|NF-κB]], suppressing its transcriptional activity and reducing the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-$\alpha$, IL-6, and IL-1$\beta$.
- **SIRT3**: Diminishes macrophage-mediated inflammation by reducing reactive oxygen species (ROS) and directly suppressing [[NLRP3]] inflammasome activation.
- **Inflammatory Polarization**: SIRT3 and SIRT6 promote the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype in macrophages, whereas loss of sirtuins leads to systemic and microvascular inflammation.

### B. Metabolism and Mitochondrial Homeostasis

- **Glucose Metabolism**: SIRT1 regulates systemic insulin sensitivity and gluconeogenesis via hepatic co-activation of [[PGC-1α]]. SIRT6 suppresses glycolysis by acting as a co-repressor for [[HIF-1α]].
- **Lipid Metabolism**: SIRT1 and SIRT6 deacetylate transcription factors SREBP-1c and ChREBP, repressing lipogenesis and preventing hepatic steatosis.
- **Amino Acid & Glutamine Metabolism**: SIRT4 mono-ADP-ribosylates and inhibits **Glutamate Dehydrogenase (GDH)**, suppressing insulin secretion in pancreatic $\beta$-cells. SIRT5 enhances glutaminolysis in a deglutarylation-dependent manner to support cell proliferation.

### C. Oxidative Stress and Redox Homeostasis

- **AMPK Loop**: SIRT1, SIRT3, and SIRT6 establish a reciprocal positive feedback loop with **[[AMPK|AMPK]]**, coordinating cell survival and ATP production under oxidative stress.
- **Nrf2 Activation**: SIRT1, SIRT2, and SIRT6 upregulate **Nrf2** signaling, promoting the expression of antioxidant defense enzymes.
- **FoxO-Mediated Defense**: SIRT1 and SIRT3 deacetylate and activate **[[FOXO3a]]**, driving the expression of manganese superoxide dismutase (MnSOD/SOD2) and catalase (CAT) to eliminate mitochondrial ROS.

### D. Apoptosis and Autophagy

- **Cell Survival**: SIRT1 deacetylates and inactivates **[[p53]]**, preventing p53-dependent apoptotic pathways during genotoxic stress.
- **Autophagy Regulation**: SIRT1 directly deacetylates crucial autophagy machinery (Beclin-1, LC3, Atg7, ULK1) and co-activates **[[TFEB]]** to drive lysosomal biogenesis.
- **Mitophagy**: Mitochondrial sirtuins (SIRT3–5) regulate mitochondrial quality control and selective clearance (mitophagy) of damaged mitochondria via PINK1/Parkin or Bnip3.

## Pathological Associations & Clinical Landscape

### Cardiovascular Diseases (CVDs)

Sirtuins play key protective roles in the cardiovascular system:

- **Cardiac Hypertrophy & Fibrosis**: SIRT1, SIRT3, and SIRT6 protect against pathological remodeling. SIRT3 deacetylates and activates FoxO3a to augment antioxidant defense. SIRT6 prevents fibrosis by blocking the profibrotic TGF-$\beta$/Smad3 cascade.
- **Atherosclerosis**: SIRT1 protects endothelial cell function, prevents vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) senescence, and suppresses foam cell formation.

### Oncology

The roles of sirtuins in cancer are highly context-dependent, serving as both tumor suppressors and oncogenes:

- **Breast Cancer**: SIRT2 and SIRT4 generally act as tumor suppressors (SIRT2 deacetylates BRCA1 to promote DNA repair), while SIRT1 and SIRT7 are frequently overexpressed and predict poor prognoses.
- **Metabolic Reprogramming**: SIRT5 promotes HCC (hepatocellular carcinoma) and CRC (colorectal cancer) growth by reprogramming metabolism from oxidative phosphorylation to glycolysis.

### Neurodegenerative Diseases

- **Alzheimer's Disease (AD)**: SIRT1 reduces amyloid-beta ($A\beta$) accumulation by activating $\alpha$-secretase (via ROCK1 inhibition) and reduces Tau pathology by promoting Tau deacetylation. In contrast, **SIRT2** hyperactivation promotes $A\beta$ and Tau phosphorylation; hence, early SIRT2 inhibition is neuroprotective.
- **Parkinson's Disease (PD)**: SIRT1 and SIRT3 protect against dopamine neuron loss by promoting autophagy-mediated clearance of misfolded $\alpha$-synuclein.

## Sirtuin Modulators

The development of specific sirtuin inhibitors and activators is a rapidly expanding pharmacological field:

### Sirtuin Activators

- **Natural Polyphenols**:
  - **[[Resveratrol]]**: The first identified allosteric activator of SIRT1.
  - **Fisetin, Butein, Quercetin**: Structurally related polyphenols that increase lifespan in model organisms.
- **Synthetic SIRT1 Activators (STACs)**:
  - **SRT1720, SRT2104, SRT2183, SRT3025**: Highly potent compounds that bind SIRT1 with high affinity, improving metabolic homeostasis and lifespan in models of obesity.
- **NAD+ Precursors (Pan-Sirtuin Activators)**:
  - **[[NMN]] (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide)**: Direct NAD+ precursor; elevates NAD+ to fuel all sirtuins.
  - **[[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR)**: Another NAD+ precursor phosphorylated to NMN before entering the NAD+ pool.
- **SIRT6-Specific Activators**:
  - **UBCS039**: The first synthetic activator of SIRT6 deacetylase activity.
  - **MDL-800**: A potent, highly selective allosteric activator of SIRT6 that reduces myofibroblast differentiation and accelerates wound healing.
  - **Cyanidin**: A natural anthocyanidin (from dark berries) capable of increasing SIRT6 activity >50-fold in vitro.

### Sirtuin Inhibitors

- **Endogenous**: **Nicotinamide (NAM)** acts as an endogenous feedback inhibitor of sirtuins.
- **SIRT1/SIRT2 Inhibitors**:
  - **Sirtinol, Salermide**: Induce apoptotic and autophagic cell death in breast and colon cancer lines.
  - **Cambinol**: Inhibits both SIRT1 and SIRT2, reducing cancer cell migration and viability.
- **SIRT1-Selective Inhibitors**:
  - **EX-527 (Selisistat)**: A highly selective, cell-permeable SIRT1 inhibitor.
- **SIRT2-Selective Inhibitors**:
  - **AK-7**: Improves behavioral phenotypes and prolongs survival in Huntington's disease mice.
  - **AGK2**: Selective inhibitor that attenuates $\alpha$-synuclein toxicity.
  - **SirReal2**: Potent, highly selective inhibitor of SIRT2 that induces ligand-based structural rearrangements of the active site.
- **Pan-SIRT / Other Inhibitors**:
  - **Suramin**: Originally an antiparasitic drug, acts as a potent inhibitor of SIRT1, SIRT2, and SIRT5.
  - **Tenovin-1 / Tenovin-6**: SIRT1 and SIRT2 inhibitors that activate p53 and reduce tumor growth.
