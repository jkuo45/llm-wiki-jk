# cAMP Response Element Binding Protein (CREB)

**cAMP Response Element Binding Protein (CREB/CREB1)** is a 43 kDa basic leucine zipper (bZIP) transcription factor that binds cAMP response elements (CRE: 5′-TGACGTCA-3′) in the promoters of target genes. It integrates signals from [[cAMP]], [[Ca2+]]/calmodulin, and growth factor pathways to regulate [[Gene Expression]] in metabolism, neuroplasticity, and survival. CREB is constitutively nuclear and becomes transcriptionally competent upon phosphorylation at Ser133, which recruits the coactivator CBP/p300.

## Structure and Activation

CREB contains a kinase-inducible domain (KID) harboring Ser133, a bZIP domain for DNA binding and dimerization, and a glutamine-rich activation domain. It is activated by phosphorylation at Ser133 through multiple upstream kinases:

- **[[PKA]]** (downstream of cAMP) — the canonical route.
- **[[CaMKIV]]** (downstream of Ca²⁺/calmodulin) — central in neurons.
- **[[RSK]]** (downstream of [[ERK]]/MAPK) — growth-factor driven.
- MSK1 and Akt also phosphorylate CREB in specific contexts.

Once phosphorylated, CREB binds CBP/p300, which acetylates histones and CREB itself, opening chromatin for transcription.

## SIRT1 Regulation

[[SIRT1]] directly deacetylates CREB at Lys136. Deacetylation at this site modulates CREB's transcriptional activity, particularly in the context of hepatic [[Lipid Metabolism]]. SIRT1-mediated CREB deacetylation influences gluconeogenic and lipogenic gene programs, linking cellular [[NAD+]] status to energy homeostasis. Because SIRT1 is itself activated by the same cAMP/PKA axis (via [[Resveratrol]]→PDE4 inhibition→cAMP→[[Epac1]]/CaMKKβ→AMPK→NAMPT→NAD⁺), CREB and SIRT1 sit in a reinforcing nutrient-sensing loop.

## Signaling Pathways and Resveratrol

The [[cAMP]]→[[PKA]]→CREB axis is central to [[Resveratrol]] action: resveratrol inhibits [[PDE4]], elevating cAMP, activating PKA, and driving CREB-dependent transcription—including [[BDNF]] expression in neurons, which supports memory and neuronal survival. This pathway also engages [[Epac1]], a cAMP-activated GEF that releases Ca²⁺ and activates CaMKKβ→AMPK, further raising NAD⁺ and SIRT1 activity. CREB therefore sits at the intersection of cAMP, calcium, and sirtuin signaling.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

CREB is essential for long-term memory, synaptic plasticity, circadian rhythms, and metabolic gene regulation. Reduced CREB/BDNF signaling is implicated in [[Depression]] and cognitive decline, whereas excessive CREB activity can promote survival of transformed cells. Its regulation by SIRT1 ties it to [[Longevity]] and metabolic health, making CREB a nodal transcription factor in geroscience.
