# DAF-16

**DAF-16** (abnormal dauer formation 16) is a *Caenorhabditis elegans* Forkhead box class O (FOXO) transcription factor homologue. It is a master regulator of longevity, dauer larva development, stress resistance, and metabolism in nematodes, functioning downstream of the conserved insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS) pathway. When IIS is low (fasting, stress), DAF-16 is dephosphorylated and enters the nucleus, where it activates genes encoding antioxidant enzymes, chaperones, antimicrobials, and metabolic regulators.

## Regulation by the Insulin/IGF-1 Pathway

In nutrient-rich conditions, the IIS receptor DAF-2 activates AGE-1/PI3K→PDK-1→AKT-1/2, which phosphorylates DAF-16 and excludes it from the nucleus, permitting reproductive growth. Under fasting or stress, reduced IIS leaves DAF-16 hypophosphorylated and nuclear, shifting physiology toward maintenance and longevity. This makes DAF-16 the canonical output of the longevity-regulating IIS axis.

## Sirtuin–DAF-16 Longevity Axis

The sirtuin–FOXO interaction is an evolutionarily ancient longevity mechanism:

- **Lifespan Extension Requirement**: *C. elegans* carrying a chromosomal duplication containing the sirtuin gene *sir-2.1* (the worm orthologue of yeast *SIR2* and mammalian *SIRT1*) exhibit up to a **50% increase in lifespan**. This longevity effect is **strictly dependent** on DAF-16.
- **Physical Association**: Under stress, SIR-2.1 physically associates with conserved **14-3-3 proteins**, which directly coordinate the physical interaction between SIR-2.1 and DAF-16 in an insulin/IGF-1-independent manner. This triggers DAF-16 nuclear translocation and transcriptional activation of stress-response and longevity genes.
- **Conserved Paradigm**: Mammalian SIRT1 likewise deacetylates FOXO orthologues ([[FoxO1]], FOXO3a, FOXO4), modulating their transcriptional output toward stress resistance and metabolic homeostasis.

This axis established the foundational principle that sirtuins promote longevity through FOXO transcription factors—a discovery that propelled the mammalian sirtuin field.

## Physiological Outputs

DAF-16 target genes encode superoxide dismutases, catalases, heat-shock proteins, and enzymes of lipid and carbohydrate metabolism. Activation of DAF-16 increases resistance to oxidative, thermal, and proteotoxic stress and extends healthspan. The requirement of DAF-16 for *sir-2.1* longevity demonstrated that sirtuins are not sufficient alone—they require intact FOXO signaling to extend life.
