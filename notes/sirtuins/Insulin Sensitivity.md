# Insulin Sensitivity

[[Insulin Sensitivity]] refers to the efficiency with which cells (primarily muscle, adipose, and liver) respond to [[Insulin Signaling|insulin signaling]] to uptake glucose from the bloodstream. Reduced insulin sensitivity — [[Insulin Resistance|insulin resistance]] — is a hallmark of [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Metabolic Syndrome|Metabolic Syndromes]], and [[Obesity|obesity]]. At the molecular level, sensitivity depends on the insulin receptor/IRS/PI3K/Akt cascade that stimulates GLUT4 translocation and inhibits hepatic glucose output.

## Mechanism of Insulin Action

Insulin binding to its receptor triggers tyrosine phosphorylation of IRS proteins, activation of PI3K, and membrane recruitment of [[GLUT4]] in muscle and adipose. In liver, insulin suppresses gluconeogenesis and promotes glycogenesis. Sirtuins influence each of these nodes through deacetylation of metabolic regulators.

## Sirtuin Connections

- [[SIRT1]] enhances [[Insulin Sensitivity]] by deacetylating and repressing [[PTP1B]] (a negative regulator of insulin signaling), and by activating [[PGC-1α]]-driven [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]].
- [[SIRT3]] supports [[Insulin Sensitivity]] through maintaining [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] [[OXPHOS|OXPHOS]] efficiency and reducing [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] in metabolic tissues.
- [[SIRT6]] deficiency causes profound [[Insulin Resistance|insulin resistance]] and fatty liver in mice; SIRT6 overexpression improves glucose homeostasis.
- [[NAMPT]] and [[NAD+]] levels decline in metabolic tissues with age, reducing sirtuin activity and impairing [[Insulin Sensitivity]].

## Lifestyle & Pharmacological Modulators

- [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Intermittent Fasting]]: Potent interventions for improving [[Insulin Sensitivity]] via SIRT1/[[NAD+]] elevation.
- [[Resveratrol]] and other sirtuin-activating compounds (e.g., [[SRT1720]]) improve insulin action in preclinical models.
- [[Exercise|Exercise]]: Induces mitochondrial biogenesis and raises NAD+ bioavailability, synergizing with sirtuin pathways.

## Clinical Relevance

Improving insulin sensitivity is the central goal of T2DM therapy (metformin, GLP-1 agonists, thiazolidinediones). Boosting the endogenous sirtuin–NAD+ axis offers a complementary, durability-focused strategy to preserve β-cell function and combat [[Aging]]-associated metabolic decline.

## Connections

- [[AMPK]]: Cooperates with SIRT1 to promote [[Insulin Sensitivity]] through [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] and [[GLUT4]] translocation.
- [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Intermittent Fasting]]: Potent interventions for improving [[Insulin Sensitivity]] via SIRT1/[[NAD+]] elevation.
- [[Adiponectin]]: An insulin-sensitizing adipokine whose secretion is modulated by sirtuins.
- [[Leptin Signaling]]: Interacts with insulin signaling at the hypothalamic level.
- [[Longevity]]: Insulin sensitivity is a consistent correlate of healthspan.
