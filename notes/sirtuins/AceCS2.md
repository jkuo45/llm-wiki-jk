# AceCS2

**AceCS2** (Acetyl-CoA Synthetase 2, mitochondrial; encoded by *ACSS1*) is a metabolic enzyme located in the mitochondrial matrix. It catalyzes the ATP-dependent conversion of acetate and CoA into acetyl-CoA, shuttling acetate carbon into the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle for ATP generation and metabolic energy production. AceCS2 is the mitochondrial paralogue of the cytoplasmic [[AceCS1]] (ACSS2), and together they constitute the acetyl-CoA synthetase family that reclaims acetate—often viewed as a waste product—as a usable carbon and energy source.

## Catalytic Mechanism

AceCS2 executes a two-step adenylation reaction analogous to other acyl-CoA synthetases: acetate is first activated with ATP to acetyl-AMP (releasing pyrophosphate), then transferred to CoA to yield acetyl-CoA. The enzyme belongs to the AMP-binding protein superfamily and contains the conserved AMP-binding and CoA-binding domains. Its activity is tightly governed by lysine acetylation—a post-translational modification that, in the basal state, leaves AceCS2 highly acetylated and relatively inactive.

## Regulation by SIRT3

AceCS2 is a primary target of the mitochondrial sirtuin [[SIRT3]]:

- **Activation by Deacetylation**: [[SIRT3]] deacetylates AceCS2 on a key conserved lysine residue, directly activating the enzyme. Under normal conditions AceCS2 is highly acetylated and inactive; SIRT3 removes these acetyl groups to unleash catalytic capacity.
- **Caloric Restriction Adaptation**: During food limitation or [[Caloric Restriction|Caloric Restriction]], mitochondrial [[NAD+]] levels rise, inducing SIRT3 expression and activity. Activated SIRT3 deacetylates AceCS2, allowing efficient metabolism of acetate to acetyl-CoA, which enters the TCA cycle to maximize ATP yield from scarce nutrients.

This SIRT3–AceCS2 axis is a canonical example of how sirtuins translate the NAD⁺/energy state into mitochondrial metabolic flexibility.

## Evolutionary Conservation

The reversible acetylation of acetyl-CoA synthetases is a highly conserved regulatory system, present from bacteria to mammals. In *Salmonella enterica* (and related bacteria), the sirtuin-like enzyme CobB deacetylates and activates acetyl-CoA synthetase (Acs), recapitulating the mammalian SIRT3–AceCS2 and SIRT1–AceCS1 systems. This conservation underscores acetylation as an ancient nutrient-sensing switch. In mitochondria, SIRT3 also deacetylates numerous other TCA-cycle and OXPHOS components, positioning AceCS2 within a broad SIRT3-controlled metabolic network.

## Physiological Relevance

By reclaiming acetate, AceCS2 supports energy production during fasting, exercise, and caloric restriction, and buffers acetyl-CoA supply when pyruvate-derived acetyl-CoA is limiting. Dysregulation of SIRT3 or AceCS2 impairs mitochondrial substrate flexibility and contributes to metabolic disease and age-related decline in mitochondrial function.
