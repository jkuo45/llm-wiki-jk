# Ca2+

[[Ca2+]] (calcium ion) is a ubiquitous intracellular second messenger that regulates numerous cellular processes including muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, gene expression, and [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]. Cytosolic Ca2+ concentrations are tightly controlled by pumps, exchangers, and channels on the [[Sarcoplasmic Reticulum|sarcoplasmic reticulum]] and plasma membrane.

## Sirtuin Connections

- [[SIRT1]] modulates [[Ca2+]] signaling through deacetylation of [[IP3 receptors]] and [[SERCA2a]], affecting [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]] and [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]].
- [[SIRT3]] regulates [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] Ca2+ uptake by deacetylating [[OSCP]] of [[ATP Synthase|ATP synthase]], impacting [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] responses.
- [[CaMKKβ]] (Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase kinase β) activates [[AMPK]] in response to elevated Ca2+, linking calcium signaling to [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin sensitivity]] and [[Exercise|exercise]] adaptation.

## Connections

- [[cAMP]]: Ca2+ and cAMP signaling pathways intersect to regulate [[CREB]]-dependent transcription.
- [[TRPM2]]: A Ca2+-permeable cation channel activated by [[ADP-ribosylation|ADP-ribose]] and [[OAADPr]], linking [[NAD+]] metabolism to Ca2+ influx.
- [[Calmodulin]]: Primary Ca2+ sensor protein that activates [[CaMKKβ]] and [[PKA]] cascades.
