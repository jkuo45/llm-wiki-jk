# Ca2+

[[Ca2+]] (calcium ion) is a ubiquitous intracellular second messenger that regulates numerous cellular processes including muscle contraction, neurotransmitter release, gene expression, and [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]. Cytosolic Ca²⁺ concentrations are maintained at ∼10⁻⁷ M—four orders of magnitude below the extracellular ∼10⁻³ M—and are tightly controlled by pumps, exchangers, and channels on the [[Sarcoplasmic Reticulum|sarcoplasmic reticulum]] and plasma membrane. Transient Ca²⁺ spikes and oscillations encode diverse signals, from action-potential–triggered vesicle fusion to transcriptional programs.

## Sources and Control of Cytosolic Ca2+

Ca²⁺ enters the cytosol from the extracellular space through voltage- and ligand-gated channels, and from intracellular stores (endoplasmic/sarcoplasmic reticulum) via [[IP3 receptors]] and ryanodine receptors. It is extruded or sequestered by PMCA and [[SERCA2a]] pumps and the sodium–calcium exchanger (NCX). The amplitude, duration, and localization of Ca²⁺ signals determine their downstream effect—ranging from localized synaptic release to global waves that activate calmodulin and kinases.

## Sirtuin Connections

- [[SIRT1]] modulates [[Ca2+]] signaling by deacetylating [[IP3 receptors]] and [[SERCA2a]], affecting [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]] and [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]]. Deacetylation of IP3 receptors tunes ER Ca²⁺ leak, while SERCA2a deacetylation influences diastolic Ca²⁺ handling in cardiomyocytes.
- [[SIRT3]] regulates [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] Ca²⁺ uptake and handling by deacetylating components such as [[OSCP]] of [[ATP Synthase|ATP synthase]] and other OXPHOS subunits, impacting [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] responses. Proper mitochondrial Ca²⁺ buffering protects against permeability transition and cell death.
- [[CaMKKβ]] (Ca²⁺/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase kinase β) is activated by Ca²⁺–calmodulin and in turn activates [[AMPK]], linking calcium signaling to [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin sensitivity]] and [[Exercise|exercise]] adaptation. This creates a feed-forward loop connecting cytosolic Ca²⁺ to the AMPK–NAD⁺–SIRT1 axis.

## Intersection with cAMP and Downstream Pathways

Ca²⁺ and [[cAMP]] signaling pathways intersect to regulate [[CREB]]-dependent transcription: Ca²⁺ activates CaMKIV, while cAMP activates PKA, and both phosphorylate CREB at Ser133. Ca²⁺-permeable channels such as [[TRPM2]] (activated by [[ADP-ribosylation|ADP-ribose]] and [[OAADPr]], linking [[NAD+]] metabolism to Ca²⁺ influx) further couple redox/NAD⁺ state to Ca²⁺ signaling. [[Calmodulin]] is the primary Ca²⁺ sensor, activating CaMKKβ, CaMKs, and calcineurin to shape gene expression, secretion, and contraction.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Dysregulated Ca²⁺ homeostasis underlies arrhythmias, excitotoxicity in neurodegeneration, and contractile dysfunction in heart failure. Sirtuin modulation of Ca²⁺-handling proteins therefore represents a mechanistic link between NAD⁺ metabolism, energy sensing, and Ca²⁺-dependent disease.
