# HIC1

**HIC1** (Hypermethylated in Cancer 1) is a sequence-specific zinc-finger transcriptional repressor containing an N-terminal POZ/BTB domain. It is a critical tumour suppressor gene located in a genomic region (17p13.3) frequently hypermethylated, mutated, or deleted in various human cancers. HIC1 binds GC-rich promoters and recruits corepressor complexes to silence target genes involved in cell cycle control, stress response, and DNA repair.

## Structure & Domains

The N-terminal POZ/BTB domain mediates homodimerization and recruitment of transcriptional corepressors (including SIRT1 and HDACs). Five C-terminal C2H2 zinc fingers mediate sequence-specific DNA binding. HIC1 frequently exists in a subnuclear body with [[p53]] and other tumor suppressors, reflecting its integrated stress-response role.

## HIC1–SIRT1–p53 Feedback Loop

HIC1 functions in a complex transcriptional feedback loop that modulates cell survival and apoptotic responses to DNA damage in concert with [[SIRT1]] and [[p53]]:

1. **Repression of SIRT1**: HIC1 forms a physical complex with the [[SIRT1]] promoter via its N-terminal POZ domain, directly repressing SIRT1 transcription. This keeps SIRT1 levels low, allowing [[p53]] to remain active (acetylated) and drive apoptotic pathways under stress.
2. **p53 Activates HIC1**: Under cellular stress, active p53 directly transactivates HIC1 expression.
3. **DNA Damage Recovery**: As cells recover from DNA damage, p53 downregulates HIC1, allowing a transient rise in SIRT1 expression. SIRT1 then deacetylates p53, dampening the apoptotic response and promoting cell survival and recovery.
4. **Aging and Cancer Risk**: During aging and tumorigenesis, the *HIC1* promoter frequently undergoes hypermethylation, causing gene silencing. This leads to aberrant upregulation of [[SIRT1]], which continuously deacetylates and inactivates [[p53]], thereby increasing cellular survival under stress and elevating the risk of neoplastic transformation.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

This feedback loop embodies a delicate balance: transient HIC1 expression enforces a robust p53-dependent apoptotic response to irreparable damage, while HIC1 downregulation after repair promotes survival. When *HIC1* is epigenetically silenced in cancer, the loop is broken in favor of SIRT1-driven p53 inactivation, fostering genomic instability and therapy resistance.

## Clinical Relevance

*HIC1* hypermethylation is a prognostic and predictive biomarker in several malignancies (breast, lung, glioma). Restoring HIC1 or selectively inhibiting SIRT1 deacetylase activity could reinstate p53 tumor surveillance. The axis also intersects with [[Aging]], as accumulating epigenetic drift in *HIC1* may bias tissues toward a pro-survival, pre-neoplastic state.
