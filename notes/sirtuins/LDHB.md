# LDHB

**LDHB** (Lactate Dehydrogenase B subunit) encodes the heart-type (H) subunit of lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), a tetrameric enzyme that catalyzes the reversible conversion of pyruvate to lactate with concomitant reduction of NADH to NAD+. LDHB preferentially converts lactate to pyruvate, favouring oxidative metabolism and gluconeogenic flux.

## Structure and Isozymes

LDH is a homo- or heterotetramer composed of LDHA (M, muscle) and LDHB (H, heart) subunits. The five LDH isozymes (LDH-1 through LDH-5) differ in subunit composition. LDH-1 (B4 homotetramer) predominates in heart and brain, tissues reliant on aerobic respiration.

## Regulation by SIRT5

[[SIRT5]] deacetylates LDHB at specific lysine residues, enhancing its enzymatic activity beyond normal physiological levels. Hyperactivation of LDHB by SIRT5 shifts the intracellular lactate/pyruvate balance, leading to metabolic stress that triggers excessive autophagy. This non-canonical activation of [[Autophagy|autophagy]] contributes to [[Tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]], providing cancer cells with recycled nutrients and resistance to metabolic stress. The SIRT5–LDHB axis therefore represents a pro-survival, tumour-promoting pathway linking a mitochondrial sirtuin to the autophagy machinery.

## Clinical Implications

LDHB is overexpressed in multiple cancers and supports the reverse Warburg effect, where lactate generated by stromal cells is imported and oxidized by cancer cells. Targeting SIRT5-mediated LDHB deacetylation could suppress the autophagic survival response and sensitize tumours to therapy.
