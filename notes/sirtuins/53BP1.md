# 53BP1

**53BP1** (p53-binding protein 1, encoded by *TP53BP1*) is a key DNA damage response protein that binds to [[γ-H2AX]] at [[DNA Damage|DNA double-strand break (DSB)]] sites and directs repair toward [[Non-Homologous End Joining]] (NHEJ). Despite its name, 53BP1 is not a p53-binding partner of primary physiological relevance; rather, it is a conserved scaffold that orchestrates chromatin remodeling and repair pathway choice. It contains an N-terminal Tudor domain and a C-terminal BRCA1 C-terminal (BRCT) domain, both of which mediate protein and histone interactions essential for focus formation.

## Structure and Domains

53BP1 is organized into several functionally distinct modules. The tandem Tudor domain recognizes methylated histone H4 lysine 20 (H4K20me2), a mark exposed on damaged chromatin. A centrally located oligomerization domain promotes 53BP1 focus assembly, and the BRCT repeats at the C-terminus mediate interactions with the effector complexes RIF1 and Shieldin. Critically, 53BP1 also contains a ubiquitin-dependent recruitment (UDR) motif that recognizes H2A ubiquitinated at lysine 15 (H2AK15ub), a modification placed by the [[RNF168]] ligase downstream of [[MDC1]] and [[γ-H2AX]].

## Mechanism of Action

53BP1 is recruited to DSBs through cooperative recognition of H4K20me2 and H2AK15ub, stabilized by interaction with [[γ-H2AX]] via the MDC1 mediator. Once localized, it executes several coordinated functions:

- **Pathway Choice**: 53BP1 promotes NHEJ by blocking DNA end resection, thereby inhibiting [[Homologous Recombination]]. This is achieved partly through its recruitment of the RIF1–Shieldin complex, which shields DNA ends from nucleolytic degradation.
- **Effector Recruitment**: It engages RIF1, PTIP (via the associated ARTEMIS complex), and the Shieldin complex (SHLD1–4, REV7), reinforcing the NHEJ commitment.
- **Chromatin Compaction**: 53BP1 promotes local chromatin compaction at break sites, contributing to repair focus stability.

The antagonistic relationship between 53BP1 and [[BRCA1]] is central to repair decision-making: BRCA1-counteracting activities and cell-cycle-dependent phosphorylation (e.g., by ATM/ATR) tilt the balance toward resection and homologous recombination in S/G2, whereas 53BP1 dominance favors NHEJ in G1.

## Role in Sirtuin Biology

[[SIRT6]] deacetylates [[Histone H3]] at lysine 9 (H3K9ac) at DSB-flanking chromatin, a modification that enables proper 53BP1 recruitment and NHEJ. SIRT6-deficient cells show impaired 53BP1 focus formation and persistent [[γ-H2AX]] signaling, indicating failed repair. The co-detection of 53BP1 and [[γ-H2AX]] foci by [[Immunofluorescence|immunofluorescence]] is the standard platform for assessing DSB repair kinetics in [[SIRT6]] activation studies using [[UBCS039]] or [[MDL-800]]. Thus, 53BP1 serves as both a mechanistic node and a biomarker readout for SIRT6-dependent genome maintenance.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Proper 53BP1 function is essential for immune diversity (V(D)J recombination), suppression of chromosomal translocations, and maintenance of genomic stability. Loss or mislocalization of 53BP1 sensitizes cells to ionizing radiation and impairs NHEJ, while its dysregulation contributes to genome instability in cancer. Because 53BP1 and BRCA1 define mutually exclusive repair routes, 53BP1 status is also exploited therapeutically: in BRCA1-deficient tumors, 53BP1 loss can restore resection and confer resistance to PARP inhibitors, illustrating its role as a modifier of therapy response.
