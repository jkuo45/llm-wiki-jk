# 53BP1

**53BP1** (p53-binding protein 1) is a key DNA damage response protein that binds to [[γ-H2AX]] at [[DNA Damage|DNA double-strand break (DSB)]] sites and directs repair toward [[Non-Homologous End Joining]] (NHEJ). It functions as a scaffold that recruits downstream effectors, coordinating chromatin remodeling and repair pathway choice.

## Mechanism of Action

53BP1 is recruited to DSBs through recognition of H4K20me2 and ubiquitinated H2AK15 marks, stabilized by interaction with [[γ-H2AX]] via the MDC1 mediator. Once localized, it:

- Promotes NHEJ by blocking DNA end resection, thereby inhibiting [[Homologous Recombination]].
- Recruits effectors including [[RIF1]], [[PTIP]], and the Shieldin complex.
- Facilitates chromatin compaction at break sites through interaction with the [[RNF168]] ubiquitin ligase.

## Role in Sirtuin Biology

[[SIRT6]] deacetylates H3K9ac at DSB-flanking chromatin, enabling proper 53BP1 recruitment and NHEJ. SIRT6-deficient cells show impaired 53BP1 focus formation and persistent [[γ-H2AX]] signaling. The co-detection of 53BP1 and [[γ-H2AX]] foci by [[Immunofluorescence|immunofluorescence]] is the standard platform for assessing DSB repair kinetics in [[SIRT6]] activation studies with [[UBCS039]] or [[MDL-800]].
