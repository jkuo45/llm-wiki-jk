# OAADPr

**OAADPr** (2′-_O_-acetyl-ADP-ribose) is a unique metabolite generated as a **by-product of all sirtuin-catalysed NAD⁺-dependent deacetylation reactions**. It is produced alongside nicotinamide when one molecule of [[NAD+]] is consumed per deacetylation event. OAADPr has been proposed as a sirtuin-triggered **signalling molecule** with roles in ion channel regulation and embryonic development.

## Biochemistry of Production

The sirtuin deacetylation reaction (described by Landry et al., 2000, and Tanner et al., 2000) is tightly coupled:

> **Acetyl-Lys-protein + NAD⁺ → deacetyl-Lys-protein + OAADPr + nicotinamide**

- Hydrolysis of one NAD⁺ produces one molecule of OAADPr and one molecule of nicotinamide.
- OAADPr is generated by _all_ sirtuins that possess deacetylase activity, including [[Sir2 (yeast)]], bacterial CobB, archaeal SIR2-AF, and human [[SIRT1]]–[[SIRT3]] and [[SIRT5]].
- The generation of this unique by-product distinguished sirtuin-mediated deacetylation from other deacetylase mechanisms and established OAADPr as a candidate sirtuin-specific signalling molecule.

## Biological Functions

### Ion Channel Regulation (TRPM2)

OAADPr binds to and **activates the cytoplasmic domain of TRPM2** (transient receptor potential melastatin-related channel 2), a non-selective cation channel whose prolonged activation leads to cell death. This provides a mechanism by which sirtuin activity can regulate membrane channel function:

- Sirtuin inhibitors or decreased expression of [[SIRT2]] or [[SIRT3]] reduces OAADPr production and attenuates cell death induced by puromycin (a selective TRPM2-activating drug).
- This suggests sirtuins may regulate channel gating and potentially other biological functions via OAADPr.

### Embryonic Development

Microinjection of **OAADPr into starfish oocytes** delays or blocks embryonic cell division, implicating OAADPr as a signalling molecule regulating early developmental processes.

### Signalling Potential

The generation of OAADPr as a unique by-product of sirtuin reactions established it as a candidate for **sirtuin-triggered signalling pathways** — a prediction subsequently validated experimentally. OAADPr may modulate:

- Chromatin structure (via interactions with histone-modifying complexes)
- Ion channel gating (via TRPM2)
- Cell cycle control (via embryonic cell division effects)

## Historical Significance

OAADPr was first described by Landry et al. (2000) as a unique acetyl-ADP-ribose metabolite tightly coupled to the sirtuin deacetylation mechanism. Prior to this, the ADP-ribosylation activity of sirtuins was considered a low-efficiency side-reaction; the characterisation of OAADPr clarified that deacetylation — not ADP-ribosylation — is the primary mechanism for most sirtuins.
