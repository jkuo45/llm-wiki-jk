# Cytochrome P450

**Cytochrome P450** (CYP450) is a superfamily of heme-containing [[Enzyme|monooxygenase enzymes]] that catalyze the phase I metabolism of endogenous compounds (steroids, fatty acids, vitamins) and xenobiotics (drugs, toxins, environmental chemicals). They are primarily expressed in the liver but also found in the intestines, lungs, kidneys, and brain.

## Nomenclature

CYP450 enzymes are named by family (e.g., CYP1, CYP2, CYP3), subfamily (letter), and individual gene (number), e.g., **CYP3A4**, **CYP2D6**, **CYP2C9**.

## Key Isoforms in Drug Metabolism

- **[[CYP3A4]]**: Metabolizes ~50% of clinical drugs, including [[HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors|statins]], [[Calcium Channel Blockers|calcium channel blockers]], and [[Amiodarone]]
- **[[CYP2D6]]**: Metabolizes antidepressants, antipsychotics, [[Beta blockers]]
- **[[CYP2C9]]**: Metabolizes [[Warfarin]], [[NSAIDs]]
- **[[CYP2C19]]**: Metabolizes proton pump inhibitors, clopidogrel ([[Plavix]])
- **[[CYP1A2]]**: Metabolizes caffeine, theophylline

## Relevance to Resveratrol

[[Resveratrol]] is both metabolized by and inhibits cytochrome P450 enzymes, particularly [[CYP3A4]]. This dual effect has implications for:
- **Bioavailability**: Rapid CYP-mediated glucuronidation and sulfation limit [[Resveratrol]]'s oral bioavailability
- **Drug interactions**: Co-administration with [[HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors|statins]], [[Calcium Channel Blockers|calcium channel blockers]], or [[Amiodarone]] may increase their systemic levels and risk of toxicity
- **Anticoagulant interactions**: Potential for increased bleeding risk with [[Warfarin]] or [[Heparin]]

## Genetic Variability

Polymorphisms in CYP450 genes (e.g., [[CYP2D6]], [[CYP2C9]]) produce extensive inter-individual variation in drug metabolism rates — categorized as poor, intermediate, extensive, or ultrarapid metabolizers.
