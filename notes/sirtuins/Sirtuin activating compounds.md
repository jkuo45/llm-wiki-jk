# Sirtuin activating compounds

[[Sirtuin activating compounds]] (STACs) are the concrete **chemical entities** that enhance [[Sirtuins]] activity. This note catalogs the molecules; the broader concept and mechanisms are in [[Sirtuin Activators]].

## Representative Compounds

- **Resveratrol** ([[Resveratrol]]) — naturally occurring [[Stilbenoid]] from grapes/red wine; the prototype SIRT1 activator and basis of the "French Paradox" hypothesis.
- **Pterostilbene** — methylated stilbenoid analog with superior oral bioavailability versus resveratrol (candidate note).
- **SRT-series synthetics** — engineered STACs (e.g., SRT1720) with higher potency/selectivity.
- **NAD+ precursors** — [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]] intermediates (nicotinamide riboside, NMN) that supply the NAD+ cosubstrate sirtuins consume.
- **Other polyphenols** — quercetin, fisetin, and curcumin show weak sirtuin-modulatory activity.

## Structure–Activity

- STACs such as resveratrol bind a hydrophobic pocket on [[SIRT1]], lowering the enzyme's KM for both peptide substrate and [[NAD+]] and accelerating deacetylation (PMID 18505964).
- Bioavailability is a key differentiator: methylation (pterostilbene) and formulation improve exposure versus native resveratrol.

> [!warning] Translation gap
> Robust SIRT1 activation by resveratrol is clear in vitro and in many animal models, but human trials have produced mixed metabolic outcomes, partly due to low systemic exposure.

## Applications

STACs are researched for [[Aging]], metabolic syndrome, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], and neuroprotection, frequently paired with NAD+ boosting.
