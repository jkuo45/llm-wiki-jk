# ACC1

**ACC1** (Acetyl-CoA Carboxylase 1, encoded by *ACACA*) is the rate-limiting enzyme of de novo fatty acid synthesis. It carboxylates acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA, the two-carbon donor for chain elongation by fatty acid synthase (FASN). ACC1 is a cytosolic enzyme highly expressed in lipogenic tissues (liver, adipose) and aberrantly upregulated in many cancers.

## Structure and Regulation

ACC1 is a large multidomain enzyme containing a biotin carboxylase domain, a biotin carboxyl carrier protein (BCCP) domain, and a carboxyltransferase domain. It is allosterically activated by citrate and inactivated by palmitoyl-CoA. Phosphorylation by [[AMPK]] at Ser79 potently inhibits ACC1 activity.

## Activation by SIRT3

[[SIRT3]] deacetylates ACC1 at multiple lysine residues, increasing its enzymatic activity and driving de novo lipogenesis. In the context of [[Cancer Metabolism|cancer metabolism]], SIRT3-mediated ACC1 deacetylation promotes the synthesis of lipids required for membrane biogenesis, signalling molecules, and energy storage in rapidly proliferating tumour cells. This metabolic reprogramming fuels [[Cancer Cell Invasion|cancer cell invasion]] and [[Metastasis|metastasis]] in models of breast and colorectal carcinomas. SIRT3-dependent ACC1 activation therefore represents a pro-tumorigenic metabolic switch, linking mitochondrial sirtuin activity to lipogenic flux.

## Clinical Relevance

ACC1 overexpression correlates with poor prognosis in several cancers. Pharmacological ACC1 inhibitors (e.g., ND-646, [[Soraphen A]]) are under investigation as anti-cancer agents. The SIRT3–ACC1 interaction adds a post-translational layer that may limit the efficacy of ACC1 inhibition if SIRT3 is hyperactive.
