# ACC1

**ACC1** (Acetyl-CoA Carboxylase 1, encoded by *ACACA*) is the rate-limiting enzyme of de novo fatty acid synthesis. It carboxylates acetyl-CoA to malonyl-CoA, the two-carbon donor for chain elongation by fatty acid synthase (FASN). ACC1 is a cytosolic enzyme highly expressed in lipogenic tissues (liver, adipose) and is aberrantly upregulated in many cancers, where it supplies the lipid building blocks required for membrane biogenesis and signalling.

## Structure and Regulation

ACC1 is a large multidomain enzyme (∼265 kDa in humans) containing three principal catalytic units arranged on a single polypeptide: a biotin carboxylase (BC) domain that fixes CO₂, a biotin carboxyl carrier protein (BCCP) domain that carries the covalently attached biotin cofactor, and a carboxyltransferase (CT) domain that transfers the carboxyl group to acetyl-CoA. ACC1 assembles into polymerized filaments whose formation correlates with activity. It is allosterically activated by citrate (which promotes polymerization and relieves inhibition) and inactivated by the end product palmitoyl-CoA. Phosphorylation by [[AMPK]] at Ser79 potently inhibits ACC1, relieving the malonyl-CoA–mediated suppression of fatty acid oxidation—an energy-stress adaptation.

## Activation by SIRT3

[[SIRT3]] deacetylates ACC1 at multiple lysine residues, increasing its enzymatic activity and driving de novo lipogenesis. In the context of [[Cancer Metabolism|cancer metabolism]], SIRT3-mediated ACC1 deacetylation promotes the synthesis of lipids required for membrane biogenesis, signalling molecules, and energy storage in rapidly proliferating tumour cells. This metabolic reprogramming fuels [[Cancer Cell Invasion|cancer cell invasion]] and [[Metastasis|metastasis]] in models of breast and colorectal carcinomas. SIRT3-dependent ACC1 activation therefore represents a pro-tumorigenic metabolic switch, linking mitochondrial sirtuin activity to lipogenic flux. Notably, the SIRT3–ACC1 axis operates orthogonally to the AMPK phosphorylation checkpoint: high NAD⁺/SIRT3 activity favors lipogenesis, whereas low-energy AMPK activation suppresses it.

## Malonyl-CoA and Metabolic Crosstalk

ACC1-derived [[Malonyl-CoA]] is not only the substrate for [[FASN]] elongation into palmitate but also a potent inhibitor of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT1), throttling mitochondrial fatty acid import and β-oxidation. By raising malonyl-CoA, active ACC1 simultaneously builds lipids and spares them from catabolism—a coordinated anabolic state. SIRT3 thus couples mitochondrial NAD⁺ sensing to whole-cell lipid homoeostasis through ACC1.

## Clinical Relevance

ACC1 overexpression correlates with poor prognosis in several cancers, and its activity is associated with aggressive, lipogenic tumor phenotypes. Pharmacological ACC1 inhibitors (e.g., ND-646, [[Soraphen A]]) are under investigation as anti-cancer agents, particularly in tumors dependent on de novo lipogenesis. The SIRT3–ACC1 interaction adds a post-translational layer that may limit the efficacy of ACC1 inhibition if SIRT3 is hyperactive, suggesting rational combinations that co-target SIRT3 and ACC1 in lipogenic malignancies.
