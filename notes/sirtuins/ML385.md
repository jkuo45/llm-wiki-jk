# ML385

**ML385** is a selective small-molecule inhibitor of [[NRF2|NRF2 (NF-E2-related factor 2)]]. It binds directly to the [[NRF2]] [[Cap'n'collar|Cap'n'Collar (CNC)]] basic-leucine zipper (bZIP) domain, blocking its heterodimerization with [[Small Maf proteins|small Maf proteins]] and preventing transcriptional activation of [[Antioxidant Response Element|antioxidant response element (ARE)]]-containing genes. Unlike earlier NRF2 inhibitors that operate upstream by disrupting the [[Keap1]]–NRF2 interaction or promoting NRF2 degradation, ML385 acts at the level of DNA binding, making it a precise chemical probe for distinguishing NRF2 transcriptional output from the broader antioxidant network in which [[Sirtuins]] participate.

## Overview

NRF2 is the master transcription factor coordinating the cellular antioxidant and detoxification response. Under basal conditions it is tethered in the cytoplasm by [[Keap1]] and targeted for proteasomal degradation. Upon oxidative or electrophilic stress, NRF2 stabilizes, translocates to the nucleus, heterodimerizes with small Maf proteins, and drives expression of cytoprotective enzymes including [[HO-1|heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1)]], [[NQO1]], glutathione biosynthetic enzymes, and [[Glutathione|glutathione]]. ML385 was identified through structure-based screening as a compound that occupies the CNC-bZIP interface, preventing the NRF2–Maf complex from engaging ARE sequences without altering NRF2 protein stability.

## Mechanism of Action

ML385 interferes with NRF2's DNA-binding activity without affecting NRF2 protein stability or [[Keap1]]-mediated degradation. It selectively inhibits the NRF2 transcriptional program, including induction of [[HO-1|heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1)]], [[NQO1]], and [[Glutathione|glutathione]] synthesis genes, without directly affecting [[NFKB|NF-κB]] or [[AP-1]] signaling. Biochemically, ML385 acts as a non-competitive inhibitor with respect to DNA: it binds the transcription factor rather than the promoter, so its effect persists irrespective of ARE copy number. This mechanism contrasts with the sirtuin-dependent regulation of antioxidant genes, which typically proceeds through [[NAD+]]-dependent deacetylation of downstream targets such as [[FOXO3a]] and [[SIRT3]]-mediated activation of [[MnSOD]].

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

NRF2 hyperactivation is a hallmark of many cancers, where it confers chemoresistance and proliferative advantage by sustaining reductive capacity. Consequently ML385 has been explored as a means to sensitize tumor cells to therapy. At the same time, intact NRF2 signaling is protective in neurodegeneration, cardiovascular disease, and ageing, so NRF2 inhibition must be contextually controlled. The intersection with sirtuin biology is particularly relevant: [[SIRT6]] can be transcriptionally induced via NRF2 binding to an ARE in its promoter, and [[SIRT3]]-dependent mitochondrial antioxidant defenses overlap functionally with the NRF2 program. ML385 therefore serves as a dissection tool to separate these axes.

## Use in Sirtuin Research

ML385 is used as a chemical tool to dissect NRF2-dependent vs. NRF2-independent effects of redox-active compounds and sirtuin modulators:

- Demonstrating whether agonists upregulate [[SIRT6]] via NRF2 binding to the [[Antioxidant Response Element|ARE]] in the SIRT6 promoter.
- Testing whether suppression of [[CD38]] transcription is mediated by NRF2-dependent antagonism of [[NFKB|NF-κB]].
- Differentiating NRF2-dependent and independent mechanisms in [[Hormesis|hormetic]] preconditioning experiments, where [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Resveratrol]] engage both sirtuin and NRF2 pathways.
