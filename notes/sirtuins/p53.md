# p53

**p53** (Tumour Protein 53, encoded by _TP53_) is a critical **tumour suppressor transcription factor** and one of the most important regulatory targets of [[SIRT1]] in the context of apoptosis, DNA damage response, cancer, and aging. The SIRT1–p53 axis is central to understanding how sirtuins modulate cell survival and tumorigenesis.

## SIRT1–p53 Regulatory Axis

SIRT1 and p53 are intimately linked — they physically associate and mutually regulate each other's activity:

### SIRT1 Deacetylates p53

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates multiple lysine residues of p53:

- **Mouse p53**: Lys³¹⁷, Lys³⁷⁰, Lys³⁷⁹
- **Human p53**: Lys³²⁰, Lys³⁷³, Lys³⁸²

**Consequence of deacetylation**: Inhibits p53 transactivation activity and **suppresses apoptosis** in response to oxidative stress and DNA damage.

### HIC1–SIRT1–p53 Feedback Loop

A complex regulatory circuit governs this interaction:

1. Under normal conditions: **HIC1 (hypermethylated in cancer 1)** represses SIRT1 expression → promotes p53 activity and apoptosis under stress
2. Recovery from DNA damage: **p53 down-regulates HIC1** → induces SIRT1 transcription → SIRT1 deacetylates p53 → promotes cell survival
3. During aging: HIC1 hypermethylation → increased SIRT1 expression → p53 deacetylation → **increased cellular risk for neoplastic transformation**

This represents a SIRT1–HIC1–p53 feedback loop with potential implications for age-related cancer risk.

## p53 and Cellular Senescence

[[SIRT1]] can rescue mouse embryonic fibroblasts from PML-mediated premature cellular senescence by inhibiting p53's pro-apoptotic activity (via PML nuclear body recruitment).

Conversely, in SIRT1-deficient MEFs:

- Extended replicative potential
- Greater proliferative capacity following chronic sublethal stress
- This phenotype requires **p19ᴬᴿᶠ** and its downstream target p53

## p53 in SIRT1 Knockout Phenotype

**SIRT1-deficient mice** show:

- p53 hyperacetylation after DNA damage
- Increased sensitivity of thymocytes to ionising radiation
- However, elimination of p53 in SIRT1 knockout mice does **not** relieve the knockout phenotype, suggesting p53 hyperacetylation is not the primary cause of the SIRT1 knockout effects

## p53 in Cancer Contexts

### Hepatocellular Carcinoma

In hepatocarcinoma cells (HepG2), **p33ᴵᴺᴳ¹ᵇ** interacts with SIRT1, inhibiting its ability to deacetylate p53 → negatively regulates transcription of AFP (α-fetoprotein), a diagnostic marker of hepatocellular carcinoma.

### Prostate Cancer

FOXO1 deacetylation by SIRT1 (enhanced by FHL2) in prostate cancer cells has been proposed to promote tumorigenesis in response to increased aging-related stress — representing a context where SIRT1/p53 pathway may have oncogenic implications.

### Kidney Disease

SIRT1 diminishes mesangial cell apoptosis induced by oxidative stress **via p53 deacetylation**, protecting against glomerular disease.

### Cardiac Protection

SIRT1 protects cardiac myocytes from programmed cell death (via serum starvation or PARP-1 activation) in a **p53-dependent manner**.

## Regulatory Complexity

The SIRT1/p53 pathway involves complex mechanisms beyond simple p53 deacetylation, including:

- Discrepant reports on whether SIRT1 impairs thymocyte/splenocyte survival following radiation
- Context-dependence of whether SIRT1 acts as tumour suppressor or oncogene
- Mouse transgenic studies now critical for resolving _in vivo_ roles
