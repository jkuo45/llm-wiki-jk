# Splitomicin

**Splitomicin** is a small-molecule, cell-permeable sirtuin inhibitor containing a $\beta$-naphthol ring structure. Discovered alongside [[Sirtinol]] in yeast cell-based screens for inhibitors of Silent Information Regulator 2 (Sir2), splitomicin is a foundational research tool utilized to investigate the structural biology and physiological roles of yeast Sir2 and its mammalian homologs, **[[SIRT1]]** and **[[SIRT2]]**.

---

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- **Class**: $\beta$-naphthol-containing lactone derivative.
- **Target Selectivity**: Inhibits both human [[SIRT1]] ($IC_{50} = 96\ \mu\text{M}$) and [[SIRT2]] ($IC_{50} = 113\ \mu\text{M}$) in vitro.
- **Origins**: Identified through phenotypic screens of over 1,000 compounds for yeast mating-type silencing inhibitors.

---

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

### Mechanism of Action
Splitomicin selectively blocks the NAD⁺-dependent deacetylation reaction catalyzed by SIRT1 and SIRT2. It occupies the pocket in the catalytic domain that normally binds the acetyl-lysine peptide substrate, preventing deacetylation.

### Pathological & Physiological Effects
- **Translocation of NF-κB**: Splitomicin treatment reduces overall intracellular deacetylase activity, resulting in the hyperacetylation and enhanced nuclear translocation of the NF-κB p65 subunit, which amplifies inflammatory transcription.
- **Vascular Coagulation**: Splitomicin enhances tissue factor (TF) mRNA expression in stimulated endothelial cells, thereby modulating intravascular coagulation cascades.
- **Cardioprotection Blockade**: In ischemic preconditioning models, splitomicin successfully reverses the protective lysine deacetylation and abolishes the preconditioning-induced cardioprotection of cardiac tissues.

---

## Applications

- **Yeast Genetics**: Extensively used as a chemical genetics tool to replicate the *sir2* gene deletion phenotype (loss of silent chromatin at telomeres and rDNA loci) in yeast.
- **Cardiovascular Research**: Employed to study the epigenetic mechanisms involved in ischemic preconditioning and vascular endothelial activation.
