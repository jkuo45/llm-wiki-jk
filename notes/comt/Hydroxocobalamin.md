# Hydroxocobalamin

**[[Hydroxocobalamin]]** (abbreviated OH-Cbl or HydroxyB12) is a naturally occurring, hydroxyl-coordinated form of [[Vitamin B12]] produced by bacteria and found in foods. It is a precursor that the body readily converts into the two active coenzyme forms—[[Methylcobalamin]] and [[Adenosylcobalamin]]—according to tissue need, without itself delivering a pre-formed methyl group.

## Structure & Biochemistry

Like other B12 forms, hydroxocobalamin features a cobalt ion at the center of a corrin ring; here the axial ligand is a hydroxyl group. Because it is not methylated, it is sometimes called "non-methyl B12." After absorption (intrinsic factor–dependent ileal uptake) and plasma transport (haptocorrin, transcobalamin), it is taken into cells and dealkylated/reduced to cob(II)alamin, the branching point for synthesis of both methyl- and adenosyl-coenzyme forms. This flexibility makes it a versatile B12 reservoir.

## Physiological & Clinical Uses

Hydroxocobalamin is the B12 form used parenterally to treat deficiency (pernicious anemia, malabsorption) and is the antidote of choice for cyanide poisoning—its hydroxyl ligand exchanges for cyanide to form nontoxic cyanocobalamin, excreted renally. It also supports the remethylation limb of the [[Methylation Cycle]] (via methylcobalamin generation) and mitochondrial energy metabolism (via adenosylcobalamin). Unlike folic acid, it does not mask B12 deficiency neuropathy.

## Use in Slow COMT

For individuals with slow [[COMT]] variants (e.g., [[Val158Met]] Met/Met) who are sensitive to aggressive methyl donation, hydroxocobalamin is often the preferred B12. It provides B12 that the body converts to active forms at its own pace, avoiding the sudden "methyl hit" and mood swings (anxiety, insomnia) associated with direct [[MethylB12]] or high-dose [[Methylfolate]]. Paired with [[Folinic acid]], it enables measured support of the [[Methylation Cycle]] without overloading it.
