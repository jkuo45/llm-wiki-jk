# Modafinil

**[[Modafinil]]** is a wakefulness-promoting agent approved for narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea–associated sleepiness, and shift-work sleep disorder. It is widely used off-label as a [[Nootropic]] for cognitive enhancement. Its mechanism is distinct from classical amphetines: it produces alerting effects without robust euphoria or pronounced rebound.

## Pharmacology and Mechanism

Modafinil's exact mechanism remains incompletely defined, but it increases extracellular levels of [[Dopamine]], [[Norepinephrine]], serotonin, orexin, and histamine in selective brain regions. It indirectly enhances dopaminergic signaling primarily by inhibiting the [[Dopamine Transporter]] (DAT) reuptake and altering DAT trafficking, rather than by direct receptor agonism. It also engages the [[Norepinephrine transporter]] ([[NET]]) and orexin neurons in the hypothalamus that sustain arousal.

## Interaction with COMT Genotype

A landmark pharmacogenetic finding is that the cognitive response to [[Modafinil]] is strongly modulated by the [[COMT]] [[Val158Met]] polymorphism. Individuals with the **fast COMT (Val/Val)** genotype — who degrade prefrontal dopamine rapidly — show marked improvement in sustained attention, working memory, and cognitive control after modafinil. In contrast, those with the **slow COMT (Met/Met)** genotype, who already maintain high synaptic dopamine, derive little benefit and may experience over-arousal or anxiety. This gene–drug interaction reflects the inverted-U relationship between dopaminergic tone and prefrontal performance: modafinil most helps those whose baseline dopamine clearance is fastest.

## Physiological and Cognitive Effects

Modafinil improves vigilance, reduces fatigue-related errors, and enhances certain executive functions in sleep-deprived and healthy populations. Its effects concentrate in the [[Prefrontal Cortex]], where DAT is sparse and dopamine clearance depends heavily on [[COMT]] and [[NET]] — precisely the circuitry where Val158Met status matters most.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

Beyond sleep disorders, modafinil is studied in [[ADHD]], depression-related fatigue, and [[Schizophrenia]] cognitive deficits. The COMT genotype is a candidate biomarker for personalizing dosing. Side effects (headache, insomnia, anxiety, rare skin hypersensitivity) are more common in slow-COMT individuals at higher doses.
