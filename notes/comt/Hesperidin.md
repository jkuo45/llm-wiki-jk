# Hesperidin

**[[Hesperidin]]** is a flavanone glycoside (hesperetin-7-*O*-rutinoside) abundant in the peel and membranes of citrus fruits—oranges, lemons, and especially immature *Citrus aurantium*. Aglycone hydrolysis by gut microbiota yields hesperetin, the absorbable form. It is classified within the [[Flavonoids]], specifically the flavanone subclass, distinct from the flavonol [[Quercetin]].

## Chemical Structure & COMT

Critically for methylation-sensitive individuals, hesperidin/hesperetin lacks the ortho-dihydroxyphenyl (catechol) arrangement. Where catechol flavonols such as [[Quercetin]], [[Fisetin]], and [[Luteolin]] are [[COMT]] substrates and can competitively inhibit the enzyme, hesperidin is not a catechol and does not interact with or inhibit [[COMT]]. Its metabolism proceeds via glycosidase and conjugating enzymes (sulfation, glucuronidation) rather than catechol *O*-methylation.

## Biological & Pharmacological Effects

Hesperidin exhibits [[Anti-inflammatory]] activity by suppressing NF-κB and cytokine (TNF-α, IL-6) signaling, and shows antioxidant, venotonic, and microcirculation-improving properties. It strengthens capillary resistance (historically used for chronic venous insufficiency) and may modestly improve endothelial function and lipid profiles. Preclinical work links it to neuroprotection and metabolic benefits, including improved insulin sensitivity.

## Relevance to Slow COMT

Because hesperidin does not engage the [[Methylation Cycle]] or consume [[Methyl groups]], it is a safe [[Anti-inflammatory]] and vascular supplement for individuals carrying slow [[COMT]] variants (e.g., [[Val158Met]] Met/Met), who must avoid catechol-loaden flavonoids that could blunt COMT and overload methylation. It is therefore frequently recommended alongside [[Berberine]] as a COMT-friendly botanical.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Hesperidin (often with diosmin) is used in venous disorders; it is generally well tolerated. Its value in personalized nutrition lies precisely in what it *lacks*—a catechol group—making it a template for selecting methylation-safe phytonutrients. Research continues into its roles in [[Aging]], cognition, and cardiometabolic health.
