# Adenosylcobalamin

**[[Adenosylcobalamin]]** (also known as dibencozide), abbreviated AdoCbl, is one of the two biologically active coenzyme forms of [[Vitamin B12]] (the other being [[Methylcobalamin]]). It is structurally distinguished by a 5'-deoxyadenosyl group covalently linked to the cobalt(III) center of the corrin ring, a configuration that supports a unique carbon–cobalt (organometallic) bond. This bond is weak enough to undergo homolytic cleavage, generating a 5'-deoxyadenosyl radical that drives intramolecular rearrangements—a catalytic strategy rare among coenzymes.

## Structure & Biochemistry

Adenosylcobalamin is synthesized in the cytosol from [[Hydroxocobalamin]] or [[Methylcobalamin]] through the action of ATP:cobalamin adenosyltransferases (e.g., MMAB) and subsequent mitochondrial import via ABCB10. Unlike methylcobalamin, which participates in [[Methylation]] reactions, AdoCbl serves exclusively as a cofactor for adenosylcobalamin-dependent enzymes, the best characterized being methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (MUT). In that reaction, AdoCbl catalyzes the isomerization of L-methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA, a critical step linking odd-chain fatty acid, cholesterol, and branched-chain amino acid catabolism to the citric acid cycle.

## Physiological & Metabolic Role

Because AdoCbl functions in mitochondria, it is central to cellular energy production and the clearance of propionyl-CoA-derived metabolites. Deficiency impairs the conversion of methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA, producing methylmalonic acidemia and disrupted intermediary metabolism. The form is prized in clinical nutrition because, unlike [[MethylB12]], it delivers B12 without an immediate influx of [[Methyl groups]], making it suitable for individuals whose [[Methylation Cycle]] is already saturated.

## Use in Slow COMT

For individuals carrying slow [[COMT]] variants (e.g., [[Val158Met]] Met/Met, rs4680 A/A), high-dose methyl-donating supplements can precipitate anxiety, insomnia, or mood instability. [[Adenosylcobalamin]] is therefore often recommended as a non-methylated alternative that supports mitochondrial health and energy production without overloading the methylation system. It complements [[Hydroxocobalamin]], which the body can convert to AdoCbl at its own pace, avoiding the "methyl trap" associated with aggressive [[Methylfolate]] or [[MethylB12]] repletion.
