# Norepinephrine transporter

The **[[Norepinephrine transporter]]** (also known as **[[NET]]**, gene *SLC6A2*) is a sodium- and chloride-dependent symporter of the SLC6 family that terminates noradrenergic signaling by reuptaking [[Norepinephrine]] from the synaptic cleft back into the presynaptic terminal. NET is the principal mechanism for clearing norepinephrine in most brain regions and the periphery.

## Structure and Mechanism

NET is a 12-transmembrane-domain glycoprotein that couples the inward transport of one norepinephrine (or dopamine) molecule with the co-transport of one Na⁺ and one Cl⁻, exploiting the sodium gradient maintained by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. It is the target of many antidepressants (NRIs such as reboxetine) and of psychostimulants; amphetamine-like compounds reverse NET transport, causing catecholamine efflux.

## Role in the Prefrontal Cortex

In the [[Prefrontal Cortex]] (PFC), where the [[Dopamine Transporter]] ([[DAT]]) is sparsely expressed, NET assumes an outsized role. Crucially, NET has appreciable affinity for [[Dopamine]] and, in the PFC, clears a substantial fraction of extracellular dopamine in addition to norepinephrine. This makes NET a critical determinant of catecholamine tone in the frontal lobes, working alongside [[COMT]] ([[Catechol-O-methyltransferase]]), which methylates the fraction of dopamine that escapes reuptake. The complementary actions of NET (uptake) and COMT (enzymatic inactivation) define prefrontal dopamine dynamics.

## Interaction with COMT

Because both NET and [[COMT]] govern local catechol concentrations, their activities are interdependent. Genetic variation in [[COMT]] (e.g., [[Val158Met]]) shifts the balance: in slow-COMT individuals, residual dopamine depends more on NET clearance, whereas fast-COMT individuals rely less on reuptake. Drugs that inhibit NET (atomoxetine, methylphenidate) raise both norepinephrine and dopamine in the PFC, enhancing attention and executive function — relevant to [[ADHD]] and the cognitive effects of [[Modafinil]].

## Clinical and Research Relevance

NET dysfunction is implicated in depression, [[ADHD]], and orthostatic hypotension. NET polymorphisms alter antidepressant response and PFC catecholamine tone. The NET–COMT partnership is a key axis in understanding prefrontal cognition and its genetic modulation.
