# Green tea

**[[Green tea]]** is a beverage prepared from the unoxidized leaves of *Camellia sinensis*. It is renowned for its polyphenol content, especially catechins—flavan-3-ols such as epigallocatechin gallate ([[EGCG]]), epicatechin gallate, epigallocatechin, and epicatechin. These compounds confer antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and metabolic effects and are the basis of green tea's broad research interest.

## Phytochemistry & COMT Metabolism

Several green tea catechins possess a catechol (ortho-dihydroxyphenyl) moiety—notably EGCG and epicatechin—and are therefore substrates for [[COMT]] *O*-methylation. *In vitro* and cell-culture studies show EGCG can inhibit COMT activity, raising theoretical concerns that high catechin exposure might slow catecholamine clearance. However, human pharmacokinetic studies indicate that at typical dietary intakes (3–5 cups/day), circulating catechin concentrations are far below those needed to meaningfully inhibit [[COMT]], and normal green tea consumption does not significantly impair catechol metabolism.

## Physiological & Health Effects

Green tea catechins scavenge free radicals, modulate [[Oxidative Stress]], and influence signaling pathways (AMPK, NF-κB). Epidemiological and trial data associate moderate consumption with improved insulin sensitivity, modest lipid benefits, and reduced cardiovascular risk. The methylated metabolites produced by [[COMT]] (e.g., 4'-*O*-methyl-EGCG) remain bioactive, illustrating how COMT transforms rather than merely inactivates these polyphenols.

## Relevance to Slow COMT

For individuals with slow [[COMT]] variants (e.g., [[Val158Met]] Met/Met), pharmacological-dose catechols (high-dose [[Quercetin]], concentrated EGCG extracts) may further burden an already sluggish enzyme and increase [[Methyl groups]] demand on the [[Methylation Cycle]]. Dietary green tea, by contrast, is generally considered acceptable, though very high supplemental EGCG could be approached cautiously. Non-catecholic alternatives such as [[Berberine]] or [[Hesperidin]] avoid the issue entirely.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Green tea extract is studied for weight management, neuroprotection, and [[Aging]]. Its catechins interact with drug-metabolizing enzymes (CYP1A2) and may modestly affect caffeine clearance. The COMT–catechin relationship remains a useful example of how genotype can modify dietary bioactive exposure.
