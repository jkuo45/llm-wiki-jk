# Medium spiny neurons

**[[Medium spiny neurons]]** (also known as **[[MSNs]]**) are GABAergic inhibitory projection neurons that constitute roughly 90–95% of the neuronal population in the [[Striatum]] (the caudate nucleus and putamen). They are the principal integrators of cortical, thalamic, and dopaminergic input within the basal ganglia and form the major output pathways that regulate voluntary movement and reward-based learning.

## Morphology and Physiology

MSNs are named for their medium-sized somata and densely spiny dendritic arbors, which receive massive glutamatergic input from the cortex and thalamus. They are tonically inactive at rest (down-state) and require convergent excitatory input to transition to an up-state depolarization that triggers action potentials. Their sole neurotransmitter is GABA, released onto downstream basal ganglia nuclei (globus pallidus and substantia nigra reticulata).

## Dopamine Receptor Subtypes and Pathways

MSNs express either [[D1 receptor|D1]] or [[D2 receptor|D2]] dopamine receptors, defining the two antagonistic output pathways:

- **Direct pathway (D1-MSNs):** facilitate movement; D1 activation is excitatory (via cAMP/PKA signaling) and promotes locomotion.
- **Indirect pathway (D2-MSNs):** suppress movement; D2 activation is inhibitory and reduces locomotor output.

This D1/D2 segregation is the cellular substrate of basal ganglia control over action selection.

## COMT Expression

[[COMT]] ([[Catechol-O-methyltransferase]]) immunoreactivity has been detected in the cell bodies and dendritic spines of [[MSNs]] in the human striatum. Because the striatum is densely innervated by [[Dopamine]] terminals from the substantia nigra but is relatively poor in the [[Dopamine Transporter]], extracellular dopamine in this region is cleared substantially by diffusion and local enzymatic methylation by [[COMT]]. MSN-associated COMT therefore shapes the temporal and spatial profile of dopaminergic signaling that gates corticostriatal plasticity and [[Stimulus-response learning]].

## Pathological Relevance

Loss of nigral dopamine (as in [[Parkinson's Disease]]) disrupts the direct/indirect balance, producing the classic motor phenotype. Altered MSN dopamine signaling and COMT activity are also implicated in [[Schizophrenia]] and addiction, where D2-MSN dysfunction contributes to aberrant salience and habit formation.
