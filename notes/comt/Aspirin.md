# Aspirin

**[[Aspirin]]** (acetylsalicylic acid, ASA) is a salicylate derivative and one of the most widely used medications globally. It irreversibly acetylates cyclooxygenase-1 and -2 (COX-1/COX-2), blocking the conversion of arachidonic acid to prostaglandins and thromboxanes for the lifetime of the platelet (~7–10 days). This gives aspirin both anti-inflammatory/analgesic properties and a durable antiplatelet effect that underlies its use in cardiovascular prophylaxis.

## Mechanism of Action

Aspirin's acetyl group covalently modifies a serine residue (Ser529 in COX-1, Ser516 in COX-2) in the enzyme's active channel, permanently disabling prostaglandin G/H synthase activity. COX-derived prostaglandins (especially PGI2 from endothelium and TxA2 from platelets) normally balance vasodilation/anticoagulation against vasoconstriction/aggregation. By suppressing TxA2 synthesis in platelets—which cannot resynthesize COX—aspirin shifts hemostasis toward reduced clot formation at low (75–325 mg) doses.

## Interaction with COMT Genotype

Beyond COX, emerging pharmacogenetic evidence links aspirin response to [[COMT]] variation. The [[Val158Met]] polymorphism (rs4680) determines catecholamine and dopamine clearance efficiency and influences vascular tone and platelet reactivity. Observational and subgroup analyses suggest a stratified effect on [[Cardiovascular Disease]] risk:

- **Slow [[COMT]] (rs4680 A/A, Met/Met):** Preserved assumptions of benefit; aspirin appears effective for primary and secondary prevention of atherosclerotic events.
- **Fast [[COMT]] (rs4680 G/G, Val/Val):** Some reports indicate aspirin may fail to confer protection and, in certain cohorts, may be associated with increased cardiovascular event rates, possibly reflecting differences in endothelial catecholamine signaling and oxidative stress handling.

These findings remain debated and are not yet standard in guideline-driven prescribing, but they illustrate the broader principle that methylation and catecholamine metabolism modulate drug response.

## Clinical Relevance

Aspirin is indicated for secondary prevention of myocardial infarction and stroke, and (at low dose) for preeclampsia prophylaxis and colorectal cancer risk reduction. Consideration of [[COMT]] status adds a personalized layer to the long-standing balance between bleeding risk and thrombotic prevention. Notably, aspirin itself is not a catechol and does not require [[COMT]] for metabolism, distinguishing it from polyphenols such as [[Quercetin]] or [[Green tea]] catechins.
