# Stimulus-response learning

**[[Stimulus-response learning]]** is a type of learning that involves the ability to learn to perform a particular behavior when a specific stimulus is present.

## Neural Basis
[[Stimulus-response learning]] is heavily influenced by [[notes/_link/Dopamine]] signaling in the [[Striatum]], particularly the dorsomedial striatum.

## Impact of COMT
Research in COMT-OE mice shows that increased [[COMT]] activity can lead to slower acquisition of stimulus-response tasks. This may be due to altered dopamine release capacity or poorer control over response actions (e.g., increased impulsivity).

## Connections
- [[Striatum]]: The brain region most critical for stimulus-response learning.
- [[notes/_link/Dopamine]]: The neurotransmitter that modulates the learning process.
- [[COMT]]: Increased activity can impair the rate of learning.
