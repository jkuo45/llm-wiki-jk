# D2 receptor

The **[[D2 receptor]]** (DRD2) is a G_i/o-coupled subtype of the [[Dopamine]] receptor family. Activation inhibits adenylyl cyclase, opens GIRK potassium channels, and reduces neuronal excitability, making it predominantly inhibitory in the central nervous system. It exists as long (D2L, postsynaptic) and short (D2S, presynaptic autoreceptor) splice variants that fine-tune dopamine signaling.

## Distribution & Biological Role

D2 receptors are densely expressed in the striatum (medium spiny neurons), [[Prefrontal Cortex]], hippocampus, pituitary, and ventral tegmental area. They are central to reward processing, motivated behavior, motor control, and [[Working Memory]]. Presynaptic D2 autoreceptors suppress dopamine release and synthesis, forming a negative feedback loop; postsynaptic D2 mediates the postsynaptic actions of dopamine on target circuits.

## Relationship to COMT

Extracellular [[Dopamine]] availability in the prefrontal cortex is governed by a balance between the [[Dopamine Transporter]] (DAT, dominant in striatum) and [[COMT]] (dominant in cortex where DAT is sparse). Genetic variation at [[COMT]]—especially [[Val158Met]]—alters the rate of dopamine clearance. Slow COMT (Met/Met) preserves higher tonic cortical dopamine, potentially hyperstimulating D2 (and D1) receptors and affecting cognition and affect; fast COMT (Val/Val) lowers tone, stressing the system. Thus COMT activity indirectly sets how much dopamine is available to engage D2 receptors, particularly in regions with inefficient reuptake.

## Clinical & Pharmacological Relevance

The D2 receptor is the principal target of antipsychotic medications: typical antipsychotics (haloperidol) and atypical agents (risperidone, olanzapine) block D2 to reduce psychosis in [[Schizophrenia]], but D2 blockade in the striatum also causes extrapyramidal side effects and elevated prolactin. D2 supersensitivity is implicated in tardive dyskinesia. Dopamine–D2 interactions are also relevant to [[Parkinson's Disease]] (where D2 agonism compensates for lost dopaminergic input) and to mood disorders such as [[Bipolar II Disorder]]. The cognitive interplay of D2 and cortical COMT makes DRD2 a candidate gene in addiction, impulsivity, and reward deficiency studies.
