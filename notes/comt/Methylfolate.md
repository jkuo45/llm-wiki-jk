# Methylfolate

**[[Methylfolate]]** — chemically L-5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) — is the active, methylated circulating form of [[Folate]]. It is the predominant folate species in plasma and the sole folate derivative that can cross the blood–brain barrier, making it central to CNS methylation and monoamine metabolism.

## Chemical Structure and Source

Methylfolate is a reduced tetrahydrofolate bearing a methyl group at the N5 position. Dietary folates are first reduced and methylated intracellularly, a final step catalyzed by [[MTHFR]] (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase). Unlike folic acid (the synthetic oxidized form), 5-MTHF requires no reductive activation and is directly usable, which is why it is the preferred supplemental form in individuals with MTHFR impairments.

## Mechanism in the Methylation Cycle

[[Methylfolate]] donates its methyl group to [[Homocysteine]] via [[Methionine Synthase]] (using [[MethylB12]] as cofactor), producing [[Methionine]] and regenerating tetrahydrofolate. Methionine is then converted to [[S-Adenosylmethionine]] ([[SAMe]]), the universal methyl donor for [[COMT]] and all other methyltransferases. Thus methylfolate sits upstream of the methyl-group pool that [[COMT]] ([[Catechol-O-methyltransferase]]) consumes to inactivate catecholamines.

## Interaction with COMT and Sensitivity

Individuals carrying slow [[COMT]] variants ([[Val158Met]] Met/Met) have reduced catechol-methylating capacity. High-dose [[Methylfolate]] supplementation can flood the cycle with methyl groups, accelerating catecholamine turnover and producing "overmethylation" symptoms: anxiety, irritability, rage, insomnia, and palpitations. This is most pronounced when methylfolate is combined with [[MethylB12]]. Clinical guidance often recommends starting at very low doses (e.g., 100–400 µg) and tapering, with monitoring for mood changes.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

5-MTHF is used to lower elevated homocysteine, support pregnancy (neural tube defect prevention), and as adjunctive therapy in depression and schizophrenia where methylation and [[SAMe]] availability are compromised. The [[MTHFR]] C677T polymorphism markedly reduces methylfolate generation, making direct 5-MTHF supplementation the standard intervention.
