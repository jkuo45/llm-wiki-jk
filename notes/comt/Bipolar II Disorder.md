# Bipolar II Disorder

**[[Bipolar II Disorder]]** is a psychiatric condition characterized by at least one lifetime episode of hypomania—a distinct period of elevated, expansive, or irritable mood lasting ≥4 days—and at least one major depressive episode, without ever meeting criteria for full mania (which would define Bipolar I). Hypomanic periods are associated with increased energy, reduced need for sleep, and often risky behavior, but generally do not cause the marked functional impairment or psychosis of mania.

## Neurobiology & Dopamine

The disorder sits within the broader spectrum of affective illness in which monoamine signaling is dysregulated. [[Dopamine]] is centrally implicated: incentive motivation, goal-directed activity, and the euphoric activation of hypomania track with phasic dopamine surges, particularly in the [[Prefrontal Cortex]] and striatal circuits. Because [[COMT]] is a major determinant of extracellular dopamine clearance in the prefrontal cortex, genetic variation in COMT is a credible modulator of illness expression.

## Role of COMT and Methylation

The [[Val158Met]] polymorphism (rs4680) in [[COMT]] yields functionally distinct enzyme activities: the Val (fast) allele degrades cortical dopamine rapidly (lower tonic tone, poorer [[Working Memory]] but resilience to stress-induced psychosis), whereas the Met (slow) allele preserves dopamine (better cognition but heightened vulnerability to affective lability). Studies have explored interactions between [[COMT]] and [[MTHFR]] variants—the latter governing [[Methylfolate]] production and the broader [[Methylation Cycle]]. Impaired methylation efficiency can alter catecholamine turnover, phospholipid synthesis, and epigenetic regulation of mood-related genes, potentially amplifying susceptibility to or severity of Bipolar II.

## Clinical Relevance

Pharmacotherapy typically combines mood stabilizers (lithium, valproate, lamotrigine) with, in depressive phases, careful antidepressant use to avoid precipitating hypomania. Dopamine-modulating agents and antipsychotics acting at the [[D2 receptor]] are often employed. Personalized approaches consider methylation status: individuals with slow [[COMT]] or [[MTHFR]] variants may be sensitive to methyl-donating supplements ([[Methylfolate]], [[MethylB12]]), where [[Folinic acid]] or [[Hydroxocobalamin]] may be better tolerated. Bipolar II shares genetic architecture with [[Schizophrenia]] and is studied alongside [[Parkinson's Disease]] for dopamine-system overlap.
