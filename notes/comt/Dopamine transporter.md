# Dopamine transporter

The **[[Dopamine transporter]]** (also known as **[[DAT]]**) is a protein that is responsible for the reuptake of [[notes/_link/Dopamine]] from the synaptic cleft back into the presynaptic neuron.

## Regional Distribution
The [[Dopamine transporter]] is highly abundant in the [[Striatum]], where it is the primary mechanism for terminating dopamine signaling. In contrast, it is much less abundant in the [[Prefrontal Cortex]], where [[notes/_link/COMT]] plays a more significant role in dopamine clearance.

## Connections
- [[notes/_link/Dopamine]]: The neurotransmitter transported by DAT.
- [[Striatum]]: The brain region where DAT activity is most prominent.
- [[Prefrontal Cortex]]: The region where DAT is sparse and COMT is more dominant.
- [[DAT]]: The common abbreviation for the dopamine transporter.
