# Creatine

**[[Creatine]]** is a nitrogenous guanidino compound synthesized endogenously and obtained from diet (meat, fish). It is central to cellular energy buffering via the phosphocreatine (PCr) system: creatine kinase reversibly transfers a phosphate from ATP to creatine, storing it as phosphocreatine that can rapidly regenerate ATP during high-demand states in muscle and brain.

## Biosynthesis & Methyl Demand

Creatine is formed in a two-step pathway. First, arginine and glycine are condensed by L-arginine:glycine amidinotransferase (AGAT) to guanidinoacetate in kidney. Then, in the liver, guanidinoacetate *N*-methyltransferase (GAMT) methylates guanidinoacetate using [[S-Adenosylmethionine]] (SAM) as the methyl donor, producing creatine. This methylation step is enormous: whole-body creatine synthesis consumes an estimated 40% of the body's daily [[Methyl groups]] flux, making it the single largest sink in the [[Methylation Cycle]].

## Physiological & Cognitive Role

In muscle, phosphocreatine sustains ATP during bursts of activity and aids recovery. In brain, creatine supports bioenergetic reserve in neurons and glia, protecting against metabolic stress. Supplementation (typically 3–5 g/day monohydrate) improves high-intensity exercise performance, and there is growing evidence for cognitive benefits—especially under sleep deprivation or in aging—given the brain's reliance on stable ATP. Creatine's actions intersect with [[Aging]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] resilience.

## Interaction with Methylation & COMT

Because creatine synthesis is such a major methyl consumer, endogenous demand competes with other SAM-dependent reactions, including those carried out by [[COMT]]. For individuals with slow [[COMT]] or [[MTHFR]] variants, methyl group availability can be limiting. Paradoxically, *supplementing* low-dose creatine can "take the strain off" the methylation system: by supplying pre-formed creatine, the body's need to methylate guanidinoacetate falls, freeing [[Methyl groups]] (and SAM) for other essential reactions such as catecholamine inactivation by [[COMT]] and [[Methylfolate]] production. This can ease symptoms of methyl overload (anxiety, overstimulation) common in slow-COMT phenotypes.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Creatine is ergogenic, potentially neuroprotective, and being studied in depression, traumatic brain injury, and neuromuscular disease. It is generally safe; concerns about renal strain are unsubstantiated at standard doses. Its methylation-sparing property makes it a pragmatic adjunct in personalized protocols for slow [[COMT]]/[[MTHFR]] individuals.
