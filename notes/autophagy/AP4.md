# AP4

AP4 (Adaptor Protein complex 4) is a heterotetrameric clathrin-independent adaptor (ε, β4, μ4, σ4 subunits) that sorts cargo at the trans-Golgi network (TGN) for transport toward the endosomal/lysosomal system. While AP4 was long studied for basolateral sorting in polarized epithelia, it is now recognized as a requirement for [[Autophagic Lysosome Reformation]] (ALR): AP4 enriches lysosomal membrane proteins such as [[LAMP1]] and [[LAMP2]] at sites of autolysosomal tubulation, ensuring that regenerated lysosomes are properly equipped for degradation.

## Structure & Sorting Mechanism

Like other adaptor complexes, AP4 uses its μ4 subunit to recognize cytosolic YXXΦ or dileucine-type sorting signals and engages phosphoinositides (notably PtdIns(4,5)P2 and PtdIns(3,5)P2) on TGN/endosomal membranes. AP4 lacks the clathrin-binding slots of AP1/AP2, instead cooperating with gadkin and other accessory factors to build uncoated vesicles and tubules destined for late endosomes/lysosomes.

## Role in Autophagic Lysosome Reformation

During ALR, the autolysosome resolves into tubules that mature into functional lysosomes. AP4 delivers LAMP1/2 and other lysosomal glycoproteins from the TGN to the reforming tubules, maintaining membrane identity and hydrolase competence. Loss of AP4 disrupts this delivery, producing ALR defects, enlarged vacuoles, and reduced [[Autophagic Flux]]. AP4 therefore complements [[AP2]] (which provides the clathrin coat) by supplying the correct lysosomal cargo during reformation.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Biallelic mutations in AP4 subunits (*AP4B1*, *AP4M1*, *AP4E1*, *AP4S1*) cause an autosomal-recessive form of hereditary spastic paraplegia (SPG47/SPG50/SPG51/SPG52) with intellectual disability and thin corpus callosum. Patient fibroblasts show mistrafficked LAMP proteins and impaired [[Autophagy]] resolution, supporting a lysosomal-reformation defect as a disease mechanism. AP4 dysfunction also intersects with [[Neurodegeneration]] and lysosomal storage biology, and its cargo-sorting role is relevant to [[Cancer]] lysosomal adaptation.
