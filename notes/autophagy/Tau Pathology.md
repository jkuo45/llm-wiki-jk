# Tau Pathology

**Tau pathology** refers to the abnormal post-translational modification, misfolding, and progressive accumulation of the microtubule-associated protein tau within neurons, a defining feature of Alzheimer's disease and related tauopathies (e.g., progressive supranuclear palsy, frontotemporal dementia). Pathologic tau transitions from physiological binding to microtubules into hyperphosphorylated, fibrillar species that form [[Neurofibrillary tangles]], impairing axonal transport and synaptic function. Defective [[Autophagy]] and lysosomal degradation permit these aggregates to accumulate, making autophagic–lysosomal induction a leading therapeutic strategy.

## Pathophysiology

Tau is normally a soluble phosphoprotein that stabilizes microtubules. Under pathological stress, kinases (GSK-3β, CDK5) outpace phosphatases, driving hyperphosphorylation that reduces microtubule affinity and promotes self-association into oligomers and filaments. These species spread trans-synaptically in a prion-like manner and seed monomers in recipient neurons. Aggregated tau disrupts mitochondria, elevates [[Oxidative Stress]], and overwhelms proteostasis. The autophagic–lysosomal system is the principal degradative route, but in disease it is often impaired—reduced [[TFEB]]-driven lysosomal biogenesis limits clearance.

## Autophagy-Based Therapeutic Axis

Enhancing TFEB activity transcriptionally upregulates lysosomal enzymes and autophagic receptors, accelerating tau degradation. Small molecules such as [[Celastrol]] and related TFEB activators, as well as mTORC1 inhibitors (rapalogues), stimulate autophagic flux and reduce tau burden in models. [[Selective Autophagy]] receptors (p62, NBR1) recognize ubiquitinated tau for delivery to phagophores. Restoring autolysosomal function is therefore a major disease-modifying approach.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Tau pathology correlates with cognitive decline more tightly than amyloid in several tauopathies, and its spread predicts clinical staging. Boosting autophagic clearance mitigates [[Neurodegeneration]] in preclinical studies, though timing and specificity remain challenges. Targeting the autophagy–TFEB axis offers potential to slow or halt tau propagation in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and related disorders of [[Aging]].
