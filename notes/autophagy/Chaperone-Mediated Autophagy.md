# Chaperone-Mediated Autophagy

**Chaperone-Mediated Autophagy** (CMA) is a highly selective type of [[Autophagy]] that targets specific proteins for degradation in the [[Lysosome]].

## Mechanism
- Targeted proteins containing a KFERQ-like motif are recognized by the chaperone Hsc70.
- The protein-chaperone complex is directed to the lysosomal membrane.
- The complex interacts with [[LAMP-2A]] (lysosome-associated membrane protein type 2A), which facilitates the translocation of the protein into the lysosome for enzymatic degradation.

## Significance
- CMA is crucial for the selective removal of damaged or misfolded proteins.
- It plays a vital role in metabolic regulation and the cellular response to stress.
- Its decline with [[Aging]] is associated with the accumulation of toxic protein aggregates.

## Process

CMA selectively degrades cytosolic proteins with KFERQ-like motifs via [[HSC70]] recognition and [[LAMP-2A]] translocation into lysosomes, independent of vesicle formation.
