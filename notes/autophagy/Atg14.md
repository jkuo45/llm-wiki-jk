# Atg14

Atg14 (also called Atg14L or Barkor) is a metazoan-specific subunit that confers autophagosome-specific localization on the class III [[PI3K]] complex I (Vps34–Vps15–Beclin 1–Atg14, or PIK3C3-C1). By targeting the complex to the [[Autophagosome]] isolation membrane (phagophore), Atg14 directs the production of phosphatidylinositol 3-phosphate (PtdIns3P) that seeds autophagosome nucleation. A distinct complex II, lacking Atg14 and containing UVRAG, instead regulates endosomal trafficking — so Atg14 is the key determinant of autophagy-versus-endosome specificity.

## Structure & Complex Assembly

Atg14 contains an N-terminal coiled-coil region that binds Beclin 1 ([[BECN1]]) and a C-terminal Barkor/Atg14 (BATS) domain that senses curved membranes enriched in PtdIns3P and phosphatidic acid. The BATS domain inserts into the highly curved edge of the phagophore, anchoring complex I at the site of autophagosome formation. Atg14 interacts with the [[ULK1]] complex via [[Atg13]] and is phosphorylated by ULK1, which enhances Vps34 lipid kinase activity.

## Mechanism in Autophagosome Nucleation

Following ULK1-complex activation, ULK1 phosphorylates Atg14 (and Beclin 1), stimulating Vps34 to generate PtdIns3P on the phagophore. PtdIns3P recruits WIPI2 and the [[Atg5]]–[[LC3B]] conjugation system, enabling LC3 lipidation and autophagosome expansion. Atg14 therefore translates the initiation signal from ULK1 into the membrane platform required for autophagosome biogenesis. Overexpression of Atg14 enhances autophagic clearance, while depletion blocks PtdIns3P generation and autophagosome formation.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Atg14 is required for basal and induced [[Autophagy]], mitophagy, and resistance to starvation and intracellular pathogens. Its BATS domain makes it a sensor of membrane curvature relevant to [[Selective Autophagy]]. Dysregulation of Atg14/Beclin 1 complex balance affects [[Cancer]] (where autophagy can suppress or support tumors), [[Neurodegeneration]], and [[Oxidative Stress]] responses. Atg14 also participates in Parkinson's-disease-linked [[Mitophagy]] through PINK1/Parkin pathways, and its phosphorylation is modulated by multiple kinases, making it a convergence point for autophagy therapeutics.
