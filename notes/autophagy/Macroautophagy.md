# Macroautophagy

**Macroautophagy** is the primary and most well-studied type of [[Autophagy]]. It involves the sequestration of cytoplasmic components into double-membrane vesicles called [[Autophagosome|autophagosomes]], which then fuse with [[Lysosome|lysosomes]] for degradation.

## Process
1. **Initiation**: Formation of the phagophore.
2. **Expansion & Completion**: The phagophore matures into an autophagosome, engulfing cargo.
3. **Fusion**: The autophagosome fuses with a lysosome to form an autophagolysosome.
4. **Degradation**: Hydrolases within the lysosome digest the cargo for recycling.

## Regulation
- Regulated by [[Atg]] proteins (e.g., [[Atg1]], [[Atg5]], [[Atg8]]/[[LC3]]).
- Induced by metabolic stress through the [[AMPK]]/[[mTORC1]] axis.

## Role in Health
- Essential for maintaining [[Cellular Homeostasis]] by removing damaged organelles and misfolded proteins.
- Dysregulation is linked to [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|Neurodegenerative Disease]], [[Cancer]], and [[Aging]].

## Process

Macroautophagy is the canonical autophagy pathway involving autophagosome formation, fusion with the lysosome, and degradation of cytoplasmic contents.
