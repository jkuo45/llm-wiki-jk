# Atg4

Atg4 (autophagy-related protein 4) is a cysteine protease that processes and deconjugates the [[Atg8]] family of ubiquitin-like proteins, which in mammals includes the [[LC3B]] and GABARAP subfamilies. Atg4 performs two essential, opposing reactions: it cleaves the C-terminal Arg/Gly extension of nascent Atg8/LC3 to expose a C-terminal Gly (processing), enabling its conjugation to phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) on the [[Autophagosome]] membrane; and it removes LC3 from PE (deconjugation/delipidation), recycling the protein and editing autophagosome size.

## Isoforms & Structure

Mammals express four Atg4 paralogs (Atg4A–D). Atg4B is the most active against LC3 isoforms, while Atg4A prefers GABARAP subfamily members. Each Atg4 contains a catalytic Cys/His/Asp triad within a papain-like fold and a flexible C-terminal tail that determines substrate specificity and membrane accessibility. Atg4B is the best-characterized and the predominant regulator of autophagic flux.

## Mechanism in LC3 Lipidation

Newly synthesized pro-LC3 bears a C-terminal extension; Atg4 proteolytically removes it to generate LC3-I, the cytosolic form. The [[Atg7]] (E1) and [[Atg3]] (E2) enzymes then conjugate LC3-I to PE, producing membrane-bound LC3-II that decorates elongating autophagosomes. Atg4 can reverse this by delipidating LC3-II, reclaiming LC3 for reuse and preventing excessive membrane expansion. This processing/deconjugation balance fine-tunes autophagosome biogenesis, cargo recruitment, and closure.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Atg4 is a critical node for [[Autophagy]] magnitude and selectivity, influencing [[Selective Autophagy]], receptor cargo loading (e.g., via [[p62]]/SQSTM1), and [[Mitophagy]]. Atg4B is frequently overexpressed in [[Cancer]], where it supports tumor cell survival under stress; conversely, its inhibition sensitizes cells to therapy. Atg4 activity declines with [[Aging]], contributing to defective autophagic clearance in [[Neurodegeneration]]. Small-molecule Atg4 inhibitors (e.g., NSC185058, fmoc-VF-CHO) are investigated as autophagy-modulating agents and cancer therapeutics. Atg4 is also relevant to [[Oxidative Stress]]-mediated regulation of autophagy.
