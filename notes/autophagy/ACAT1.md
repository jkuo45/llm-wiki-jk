# ACAT1

Acetyl-CoA acetyltransferase 1 (ACAT1, also known as thiolase) is a mitochondrial enzyme canonically known for catalyzing the condensation of two acetyl-CoA molecules into acetoacetyl-CoA — the first committed step of [[Ketogenesis]] and the mevalonate/cholesterol pathway. More recently, ACAT1 has been identified as a lysine acetyltransferase that post-translationally acetylates the master lysosomal transcription factor [[TFEB]] at multiple lysines (K91, K103, K116, and K430). This acetylation enhances TFEB nuclear translocation and its DNA-binding affinity at [[CLEAR Element]] loci, thereby promoting [[Lysosome]] biogenesis and [[Autophagy]].

## Structure & Enzymatic Activity

ACAT1 is a homotetrameric enzyme localized primarily to the mitochondrial matrix, with its catalytic Cys89 residue forming a thioester intermediate during acetoacetyl-CoA synthesis. Although classically a thiolase, ACAT1 also transfers acetyl groups from acetyl-CoA to lysine ε-amino groups on protein substrates, functioning as a non-canonical acetyltransferase. This dual activity links intermediary metabolism directly to epigenetic/transcriptional control of autophagy.

## Mechanism in TFEB Regulation

Under conditions of increased acetyl-CoA availability (e.g., fatty acid oxidation or ketogenic states), mitochondrial ACAT1 acetylates TFEB. Acetylation at K430 and other sites reduces TFEB interaction with cytosolic retention factors such as [[14-3-3 protein]] and enhances its nuclear import and promoter occupancy. Acetylated TFEB drives coordinated expression of lysosomal hydrolases and autophagic machinery, raising [[Autophagic Flux]]. The opposing enzyme, the deacetylase [[SIRT1]], removes these marks, placing ACAT1 and SIRT1 in a metabolic rheostat that tunes autophagic capacity to nutrient state.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because ACAT1 bridges ketone-body metabolism and autophagic clearance, it has implications for [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegeneration]], where declining TFEB activity impairs proteostasis. Pharmacological promotion of ACAT1-mediated TFEB acetylation is being explored as a strategy to boost lysosomal degradation in proteinopathy models. Conversely, ACAT1's canonical role in cholesterol/mevalonate metabolism links it to [[Cancer]] lipid homeostasis and statin-responsive pathways. ACAT1 deficiency (beta-ketothiolase deficiency) is a rare inborn error of metabolism causing ketoacidotic crises.
