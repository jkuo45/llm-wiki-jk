# Adaptive Immune Response

The **Adaptive Immune Response** is a highly specific immune mechanism characterized by memory and the ability to recognize specific antigens.

## Key Components
- **[[T Cell|T Cells]]**: Key to cell-mediated immunity; subsets acquire [[Senescence]]-like features with age.
- **[[B Cell|B Cells]]**: Responsible for antibody production; their production in bone marrow declines with age.
- **Memory Responses**: Perturbed in the aged immune system, leading to ineffective responses in older people.

## Modulation by Autophagy
- [[Autophagy]] facilitates adaptive immune cell activation and differentiation.
- [[Autophagy Inducer|Autophagy inducers]] like [[Spermidine]] can rejuvenate B cell responses and enhance the production of virus-specific memory CD8+ T cells.

## Overview

Adaptive immunity involves B and T cell responses. [[Autophagy]] regulates antigen presentation, lymphocyte survival, and immune memory.
