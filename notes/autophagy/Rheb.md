# Rheb

**Rheb** (Ras homolog enriched in brain) is a small GTPase that serves as the direct, essential activator of [[mTORC1]] kinase. In its GTP-bound state, Rheb binds the mTOR kinase domain within the mTORC1 complex, stimulating phosphorylation of S6K1 and 4E-BP1 to drive anabolism and suppress [[Autophagy]]. Rheb activity is therefore a key conduit through which growth-factor, energy, and nutrient inputs are translated into the decision between growth and catabolism.

## Structure & Localization

Rheb is a farnesylated Ras-family GTPase that localizes primarily to late endosomal and lysosomal membranes, positioning it adjacent to both [[mTORC1]] (recruited via the [[Ragulator]]–Rag axis) and the upstream TSC (tuberous sclerosis) Rheb-GAP. Its compact G-domain shares the canonical Ras fold but lacks the effector-interaction elaborations of Ras, instead contacting mTOR directly.

## Regulation by TSC1-TSC2

Rheb is held in the inactive GDP-bound state by the tuberous sclerosis complex, a GAP composed of [[TSC1]] (hamartin) and [[TSC2]] (tuberin). TSC2 provides the catalytic GAP domain; TSC1 stabilizes TSC2. Growth factors (via AKT) and amino acids (via the Rag/Gator axis) inhibit TSC2, allowing Rheb to accumulate in the GTP-bound active form. Energy stress (AMPK) and hypoxia conversely activate TSC2, inactivating Rheb and thereby inducing autophagy. Thus Rheb sits downstream of AKT and upstream of mTORC1 in the PI3K–AKT–TSC–Rheb–mTORC1 cascade.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because Rheb is the gatekeeper of mTORC1, its inappropriate activation phenocopies mTORC1 overactivity: in tuberous sclerosis, loss of TSC1/2 relieves Rheb inhibition, driving hamartoma formation and [[Cancer]] proliferation while suppressing autophagic clearance. Pharmacologic inhibition of mTORC1 (rapamycin) or restoration of TSC function re-engages autophagy. Rheb levels also decline with [[Aging]] in some tissues, contributing to dysregulated mTORC1 signaling and impaired proteostasis.
