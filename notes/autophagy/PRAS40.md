# PRAS40

Proline-rich AKT substrate 40 kDa (PRAS40), encoded by AKT1S1, is a stoichiometric subunit and intrinsic negative regulator of [[mTORC1]]. It inhibits the complex by occupying the substrate-binding site on [[Raptor]], blocking access of mTORC1 substrates such as S6K1 and 4E-BP1. Growth-factor signaling through [[AKT]] phosphorylates PRAS40 at threonine 246 (T246), triggering its dissociation from mTORC1 and de-repression of kinase activity, thereby coupling insulin/IGF-1 and survival signals to anabolic growth and suppression of [[Autophagy]].

## Structure & Interaction

PRAS40 is a principally intrinsically disordered protein with an N-terminal proline-rich region (the T246 site lies near the N-terminus) and a C-terminal putative Raptor-binding motif. It is recruited into the mTORC1 holoenzyme by binding Raptor; in the unphosphorylated state it acts as a pseudosubstrate/steric plug at the substrate groove. 14-3-3 proteins bind phosphorylated T246-PRAS40, sequestering it away from mTORC1. PRAS40 thus functions as a molecular brake whose release is gated by AKT and, antagonistically, by AMPK and nutrient/senescence cues.

## Mechanism of Action

Under fasting or energy-deficit conditions, PRAS40 remains hypophosphorylated and bound to mTORC1, restraining substrate phosphorylation and favoring catabolism/[[Autophagy]]. Upon insulin or growth-factor stimulation, AKT phosphorylates T246; 14-3-3 binding displaces PRAS40, allowing mTORC1 to phosphorylate S6K1 and 4E-BP1, drive protein synthesis, and inhibit [[TFEB]]/autophagy. Notably, PRAS40 phosphorylation status also responds to [[AMPK]] (which can promote its inhibitory association) and to amino acid sufficiency, integrating multiple inputs at the mTORC1 core. This positions PRAS40 as a tunable gate rather than a simple off-switch.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because PRAS40 links AKT survival signaling to mTORC1 output, it is implicated in [[Insulin Resistance]] and type 2 diabetes, where altered PRAS40 phosphorylation disturbs metabolic homeostasis. In [[Cancer]], AKT hyperactivation (PI3K/AKT pathway mutations) phosphorylates PRAS40 to sustain mTORC1-driven growth and to suppress autophagic tumor suppression; PRAS40 phosphorylation is used as a biomarker of AKT/mTORC1 activity. Conversely, strategies that preserve PRAS40 association with mTORC1 (or mimic its inhibitory state) can dampen anabolic signaling and promote [[Autophagy]] in [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegeneration]]. PRAS40 therefore exemplifies how a single mTORC1 subunit translates growth-factor tone into the autophagic switch.
