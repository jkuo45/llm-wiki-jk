# TFEC

Transcription Factor EC, a member of the [[MiTF TFE family|MiTF/TFE family]].

## Structure & Domains
TFEC contains the conserved basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-LZ) domain characteristic of the [[MiTF TFE family]], enabling DNA binding to E-box and [[CLEAR Element|CLEAR-box]] motifs. Unlike other family members, TFEC lacks a transactivation domain in its N-terminal region, suggesting it may function primarily as a transcriptional repressor or modulator.

## Mechanism & Regulation
TFEC forms homo- and heterodimers with other MiTF/TFE family members ([[TFEB]], [[TFE3]], [[MITF]]). While TFEB and TFE3 strongly activate CLEAR target genes, TFEC may competitively inhibit their activity by occupying CLEAR-box binding sites or forming non-productive heterodimers. Regulation of TFEC nuclear translocation parallels that of other family members, involving [[mTORC1]]-dependent phosphorylation and [[14-3-3 protein]]-mediated cytosolic retention.

## Physiological Function
TFEC plays a distinct role within the MiTF/TFE family as a potential negative regulator of the lysosomal-autophagic transcriptional program. Its expression is enriched in specific tissues including spleen, lymph nodes, and macrophages, where it may modulate immune cell function. [[TFEC]] knockout studies suggest compensatory upregulation of [[TFEB]] and [[TFE3]], indicating a balancing mechanism within the family.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
TFEC is less studied than other MiTF/TFE family members, but its altered expression has been observed in certain cancers and immune disorders. In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, TFEC expression is suppressed, potentially as part of a broader transcriptional reprogramming favoring [[TFEB]]-mediated adaptation. TFEC gene fusions are rare but have been reported in renal cell carcinoma, analogous to [[TFE3]] and [[TFEB]] translocation-driven cancers.
