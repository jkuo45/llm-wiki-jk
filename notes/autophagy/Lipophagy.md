# Lipophagy

**Lipophagy** is a form of [[Selective Autophagy]] involving the degradation of [[Lipid Droplet]]s within [[Lysosome]]s. It is a critical mechanism for mobilizing fat stores in response to [[Nutrient Availability]].

## Mechanism
- Lipid droplets are sequestered into [[Autophagosome]]s and delivered to lysosomes (or [[Autolysosome]]s).
- Within the acidic environment of the lysosome, [[Lysosomal Lipolysis]] occurs, primarily mediated by [[Lysosomal Acid Lipase]] ([[LAL]] in mammals, [[LIPL-1]] and [[LIPL-3]] in [[C. elegans]]).

## Regulation
- **Nutrient Status**: Lipophagy is activated by [[Fasting]] and [[Starvation]].
- **Transcriptional Control**:
    - In [[C. elegans]], the transcription factors [[MXL-3]] (repressor) and [[HLH-30]] (activator) coordinate the expression of lipophagy-related genes.
    - In mammals, [[TFEB]] acts as a master regulator of the autophagic and lysosomal genes required for lipophagy.
- **Hormonal Signaling**: Insulin signaling (e.g., [[DAF-2]] in worms) and [[mTORC1]] signaling also play regulatory roles.

## Physiological Importance
- **Energy Homeostasis**: Provides fatty acids for β-oxidation during periods of nutrient scarcity.
- **Metabolic Health**: Dysfunction in lipophagy is linked to hepatic steatosis and other [[Metabolic Diseases]].
- **Ageing**: Enhanced lipophagy and lysosomal lipolysis have been shown to extend lifespan in model organisms like [[C. elegans]].
