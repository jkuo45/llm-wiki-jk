# PtdIns(3,5)P2

Phosphatidylinositol 3,5-bisphosphate (PtdIns(3,5)P2) is a low-abundance, dynamically regulated phosphoinositide present predominantly on endolysosomal membranes. Synthesized from [[PtdIns3P]] by the lipid kinase [[PIKfyve]], it orchestrates lysosomal membrane dynamics, ion channel regulation, and organelle reformation, serving as a central lipid signal that links nutrient sensing to [[Autophagy]] and [[Lysosome]] homeostasis.

## Biosynthesis & Turnover

PtdIns(3,5)P2 is produced by PIKfyve (phosphoinositide kinase, FYVE finger-containing), which phosphorylates the 5-position of PtdIns(3)P. Its levels are tightly controlled: the phosphoinositide 5-phosphatase FIG4 (and its yeast homolog Fig4/Sac3) dephosphorylates PtdIns(3,5)P2 back to PtdIns3P, while the 3-phosphatase MTMR (myotubularin) can also consume it. The opposing activities of PIKfyve and FIG4 maintain a low but critical steady-state pool; loss of either enzyme causes profound lysosomal swelling and storage, underscoring the lipid's essential role.

## Mechanism of Action

PtdIns(3,5)P2 acts as a membrane identity mark that recruits effector proteins and activates lysosomal ion channels. Most notably, it directly binds and activates [[TRPML1]] (MCOLN1), the lysosomal Ca2+ release channel; TRPML1 opening elevates cytosolic Ca2+ that drives membrane fission, tubule scission, and the [[Autophagic Lysosome Reformation]]/[[Phagocytic Lysosome Reformation]] processes that rebuild lysosomes after degradation. PtdIns(3,5)P2 also recruits ESCRT and sorting nexin proteins, regulates v-ATPase function and lysosomal acidification, and supports autophagosome-lysosome fusion and [[Autophagic Flux]]. Through these actions it integrates with mTORC1 nutrient sensing at the [[Lysosome]].

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because PtdIns(3,5)P2 is indispensable for lysosomal morphology and reformation, its dysregulation underlies human disease: FIG4 mutations cause Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 4J and Yunis-Varon syndrome, and PIKfyve dysfunction produces fleck cornea with pigmented maculopathy—both manifesting as [[Lysosomal Storage Disorders]]. Defective PtdIns(3,5)P2 signaling collapses autophagic clearance, contributing to [[Neurodegeneration]] and proteostatic failure in [[Aging]]. Pharmacologically, PIKfyve inhibitors such as apilimod raise PtdIns(3)P while depleting PtdIns(3,5)P2, disrupting lysosomal reformation and exhausting lysosomes in [[Cancer]] cells to amplify therapy-induced stress. Thus PtdIns(3,5)P2 is both a disease locus and a therapeutic interface bridging lipid signaling to the autophagy-lysosome network.
