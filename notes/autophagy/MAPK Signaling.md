# MAPK Signaling

Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling comprises evolutionarily conserved three-tier kinase cascades (MAP3K → MAP2K → MAPK) that transduce extracellular cues—growth factors, cytokines, osmotic and oxidative stress, and nutrients—into nuclear and cytoplasmic responses. In the autophagy-lysosome network, the [[p38 MAPK]] and ERK branches are key regulators of the localization and activity of [[TFEB]] and related transcription factors, coupling stress and proliferative signals to [[Autophagy]] and lysosomal biogenesis.

## Core Pathways

The major mammalian MAPK modules are ERK1/2 (RAF → MEK1/2 → ERK1/2), p38 MAPK (MKK3/6 → p38α/β/γ/δ), JNK (MKK4/7 → JNK1/2/3), and ERK5. These cascades phosphorylate transcription factors (ELK1, ATF2, c-FOS, CREB) and cytoplasmic targets, integrating signals at the level of [[mTORC1]] and autophagy initiators. Cross-talk with autophagy is extensive: ERK2 phosphorylates TFEB, while p38 MAPK signaling influences TFEB nuclear shuttling in response to stress, and JNK can activate [[Bcl-2]] phosphorylation to release [[Beclin1]] for autophagosome nucleation.

## Regulation of TFEB & Autophagy

[[p38 MAPK]] activation by stress stimuli promotes TFEB nuclear accumulation and target gene expression, enhancing lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic clearance. Conversely, growth-factor-driven ERK2 phosphorylates TFEB to favor its cytosolic retention, aligning with mTORC1-mediated suppression. This bidirectional control lets MAPK signaling tune the catabolic program to cellular context—pro-survival autophagy under stress versus anabolic growth when nutrients and mitogens dominate. The cascade therefore intersects with [[mTORC1]], [[AMPK]], and the MiT/TFE family to balance metabolism.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

MAPK pathways are central to proliferation, differentiation, inflammation, and stress adaptation, and are frequently dysregulated in [[Cancer]] (e.g., mutant RAS/RAF/MEK driving ERK hyperactivation) and in [[Neurodegeneration]], where chronic stress MAPK activation alters autophagic clearance. Pharmacologic MAPK modulators (MEK/ERK and p38 inhibitors) are used experimentally and clinically, and their effects on TFEB/autophagy are important determinants of therapeutic outcome and resistance. In [[Aging]], declining MAPK-autophagy coordination contributes to proteostatic failure. Understanding MAPK-autophagy crosstalk informs strategies to harness TFEB for clearance of toxic aggregates and storage material.
