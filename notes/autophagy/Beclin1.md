# Beclin1

**Beclin1** (also known as **Atg6**) is a key regulatory protein in the initiation and regulation of [[notes/_link/Autophagy]].

## Function
- It is a component of the class III [[notes/_link/PI3K]]/[[Vps34]] complex, which is essential for the formation of the phagophore (the precursor to the [[Autophagosome]]).
- Beclin1 acts as a scaffold protein, interacting with various regulators to either promote or inhibit autophagic activity.

## Cancer and Tumor Suppression
- Beclin1 is a well-known tumor suppressor. Its suppression or monoallelic deletion is linked to several types of [[notes/_link/Cancer]], including ovarian, breast, and prostate cancer.
- Deletion of BCLN1 in mice leads to the development of hepatic and pulmonary cancers due to a deficient autophagic response.
- **[[BRCA1]]**: The tumor suppressor BRCA1 is physically close to BCLN1, and their co-deletion can significantly impact cancer progression.

## Regulation
- Beclin1 activity is modulated by its interaction with Bcl-2, which can sequester Beclin1 and inhibit autophagy.
