# 14-3-3 protein

14-3-3 proteins are a family of highly conserved dimeric scaffold/adapter proteins found in all eukaryotes. They regulate a vast array of signaling proteins by binding phosphorylated serine/threonine motifs, controlling the subcellular localization, stability, and activity of their partners. In the context of autophagy, 14-3-3 proteins are best known for sequestering the master lysosomal transcription factors [[TFEB]] and [[TFE3]] in the cytosol under nutrient-replete conditions, thereby restraining the [[CLEAR Element]] gene program and coordinated [[Lysosome]] biogenesis.

## Structure & Isoforms

Mammals express seven 14-3-3 isoforms (β, γ, ε, ζ, η, θ/τ, σ), each encoded by a distinct *YWHA* gene. Each monomer folds into an N-terminal helix followed by nine antiparallel β-strands that form a conserved amphipathic ligand-binding groove. 14-3-3 proteins function as constitutive dimers (homo- or heterodimers), and the groove binds mode I (RSXpSXP) and mode II (RXXXpSXP) phosphoserine/phosphothreonine motifs. This plasticity allows a single isoform to engage dozens of phosphorylated clients.

## Mechanism in Autophagy Regulation

When nutrients are abundant, activated [[mTORC1]] phosphorylates [[TFEB]] at Ser211 (and TFE3 at Ser321) in the cytosol. 14-3-3 proteins recognize this phospho-motif and bind the phosphorylated transcription factor, masking its nuclear localization signal and preventing import into the nucleus. Additionally, 14-3-3 binding promotes cytoplasmic retention through association with [[Rag GTPase]]-dependent anchoring at the lysosomal surface. Upon starvation, mTORC1 is inhibited, TFEB is dephosphorylated (in part by [[Calcinesurin]]/phosphatase activity), 14-3-3 dissociates, and TFEB translocates to the nucleus to drive expression of lysosomal hydrolases, [[Autophagosome]] components, and [[Autophagic Flux]] mediators.

Beyond TFEB, 14-3-3 isoforms modulate autophagy by interacting with [[ULK1]], [[BAD]], and components of the apoptotic machinery, positioning them at the interface of survival and death decisions. They also bind phosphorylated [[Atg9]]-trafficking regulators and influence autophagosome biogenesis.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Dysregulation of 14-3-3/TFEB partitioning contributes to [[Neurodegeneration]], where impaired TFEB nuclear entry limits clearance of aggregate-prone proteins. In [[Cancer]], 14-3-3σ loss is associated with genomic instability, while other isoforms support tumor survival under metabolic stress. 14-3-3 proteins are also biomarkers of stroke and prion disease in cerebrospinal fluid, and small-molecule inhibitors (e.g., difopein) are used experimentally to displace clients and interrogate autophagy networks.
