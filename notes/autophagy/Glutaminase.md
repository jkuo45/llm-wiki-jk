# Glutaminase

Glutaminase (GLS) is a mitochondrial enzyme that catalyzes the hydrolytic deamidation of glutamine to glutamate and ammonium, the committed first step of glutaminolysis. In the context of [[Autophagy]] and lysosomal biology, glutaminase links amino acid metabolism to [[TFEB]]-driven transcription and to the induction of cytoprotective autophagy, and it is transcriptionally upregulated by TFEB in [[Pancreatic Cancer]] to fuel tumor metabolism.

## Structure & Isoforms

Two major kidney-type isoforms exist: GLS1 (encoded by GLS, with alternatively spliced variants kidney-type glutaminase KGA and glutaminase C GAC) and GLS2 (liver-type, encoded by GLS2). GLS1 is the predominant isoform in most proliferating cells and is allosterically activated by inorganic phosphate and by the product glutamate at high concentrations, while the broadly conserved tumor suppressor [[TP53]] transcriptionally induces GLS2. Both isoforms are mitochondrial matrix enzymes requiring oligomerization for catalytic activity.

## Mechanism of Action

Glutaminase converts glutamine to glutamate, which is further transaminated to α-ketoglutarate (α-KG), replenishing the TCA cycle (anaplerosis) and supporting biosynthesis, redox balance (via NADPH through malic enzyme), and energy production. Glutamate can also be converted to glutathione, a key antioxidant. Critically, the ammonium generated by glutaminase deamination raises cytosolic pH and acts as a signal that induces [[Autophagy]] as a cytoprotective response to nutrient and therapeutic stress. The [[TFEB]] master regulator directly transactivates GLS (and GLS2), coupling lysosomal-autophagy activation to glutamine catabolism.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Glutaminolysis is a hallmark of rapidly dividing cancer cells, which depend on glutamine ("glutamine addiction") for survival. In [[Pancreatic Cancer]] and other malignancies, TFEB-mediated glutaminase upregulation supports the metabolic flexibility that underlies therapy resistance. Pharmacological inhibition of glutaminase (e.g., with CB-839 / telaglenastat, BPTES, or JHU083) reduces autophagic flux, lowers α-KG, and sensitizes tumor cells to chemotherapy and targeted agents, making GLS a candidate vulnerability in [[Cancer]] metabolism. Conversely, glutaminase-derived glutamate and glutathione tie into [[Oxidative Stress]] buffering, while the ammonia-autophagy axis highlights glutaminase as a bridge between nutrient sensing, [[Autophagy]], and cellular resilience in [[Aging]] and disease.
