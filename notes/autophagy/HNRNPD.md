# HNRNPD

Heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein D (HNRNPD), also known as AUF1 (AU-rich element RNA-binding protein 1), is an RNA-binding protein that post-transcriptionally regulates mRNA stability and processing of autophagy-relevant transcripts. It can promote degradation of [[TFEB]] pre-mRNA following [[METTL3]]-mediated N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modification, and it stabilizes [[ULK1]] and [[Beclin1]] messenger RNAs, placing it at a key post-transcriptional checkpoint of the [[Autophagy]] machinery.

## Structure & Isoforms

HNRNPD belongs to the heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein (hnRNP) family. Alternative splicing of a single gene yields four isoforms (p37, p40, p42, p45 AUF1) that differ in N-terminal sequences but all contain two N-terminal RNA-recognition motifs (RRM1, RRM2) that bind AU-rich elements (AREs) in 3' untranslated regions. RRM1 confers sequence specificity, while RRM2 governs cooperative binding and recruitment of decay or stability complexes.

## Mechanism of Action

AUF1 binds AREs in target mRNAs and modulates their half-life by recruiting either deadenylases/decapping complexes (promoting decay) or stabilization factors (promoting stability), depending on the partner proteins and the methylation state of the transcript. In the TFEB axis, m6A methylation installed by the writer [[METTL3]] (and reader proteins such as YTHDF2) targets TFEB precursor mRNA for AUF1-dependent destabilization, lowering TFEB protein and attenuating lysosomal-autophagy transcription. Conversely, AUF1 stabilizes ULK1 and Beclin1 transcripts, supporting initiation-complex abundance. This bidirectional regulation means HNRNPD integrates epitranscriptomic marks with autophagic capacity.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

AUF1 is a central regulator of mRNA turnover in inflammation, proliferation, and stress responses. Its dysregulation contributes to the age-related decline in autophagy, because reduced stabilization of ULK1/Beclin1 and enhanced TFEB mRNA decay erode the autophagic reserve implicated in [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegeneration]]. In [[Cancer]], AUF1 often acts as an oncogenic promoter of proliferation and metastasis by stabilizing growth-related transcripts, though context-dependent tumor-suppressive roles exist. Because it bridges m6A RNA methylation to [[TFEB]] and core autophagy initiation factors, HNRNPD is an emerging node for interventions aimed at restoring autophagic flux through epitranscriptomic reprogramming.
