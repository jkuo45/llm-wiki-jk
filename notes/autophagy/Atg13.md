# Atg13

Atg13 (autophagy-related protein 13) is a scaffold subunit of the [[ULK1]] (Atg1) initiation complex, central to the earliest steps of [[Macroautophagy|autophagy]]. In yeast, Atg13 bridges Atg1 to the vacuole via Atg17; in mammals, Atg13 coordinates [[ULK1]], [[Atg101]], and [[FIP200]], coupling nutrient sensing to phagophore assembly. Atg13 is the principal substrate through which [[mTORC1]] and [[AMPK]] gate autophagy initiation.

## Structure & Phosphorylation

Mammalian Atg13 contains multiple serine/threonine phosphorylation clusters. mTORC1 phosphorylates Atg13 at several sites (e.g., Ser258, Ser316, Ser320) under fed conditions, maintaining the ULK1 complex in a low-activity state and promoting its association with mTORC1 at the lysosomal surface. Upon starvation, mTORC1 dissociates, AMPK phosphorylates ULK1 and Atg13, and dephosphorylated Atg13 adopts a conformation that enhances ULK1 kinase activation and complex oligomerization. The N-terminal region of Atg13 binds [[Atg101]], which stabilizes Atg13 against proteasomal turnover.

## Mechanism in Initiation

Atg13 functions as the organizational hub that recruits downstream machinery to the nascent [[Autophagosome]] isolation membrane (phagophore). Activated ULK1 phosphorylates [[Atg14]] and components of the [[Vps34]] class III PI3K complex I, directing PtdIns3P production that nucleates the phagophore. Atg13 also helps localize the complex to the phagophore assembly site and supports the recruitment of [[ATG Genes]] products required for LC3 lipidation. Without Atg13, ULK1 complex integrity collapses and autophagosome formation is blocked.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Atg13-dependent initiation is essential for survival during nutrient deprivation, ER stress, and mitochondrial damage. Its regulation by mTORC1/AMPK places it at the core of metabolic control of [[Autophagy]], with implications for [[Aging]], [[Cancer]] (where autophagy supports tumor metabolism or therapy resistance), and [[Neurodegeneration]] (where defective initiation promotes protein aggregation). Atg13 phosphorylation status is increasingly used as a biomarker of autophagic induction in preclinical studies.
