# Lysosomal Storage Disorders

Lysosomal storage disorders (LSDs) are a family of ∼70 inherited metabolic diseases caused by defects in lysosomal enzymes, membrane proteins, or transporters, leading to the intralysosomal accumulation of undegraded macromolecules (glycosphingolipids, glycoproteins, mucopolysaccharides, glycogen, or soluble proteins). Because the [[Lysosome]] is the terminal degradative compartment shared with [[Autophagy]], LSDs secondarily impair autophagic-lysosomal clearance, making them a paradigm for understanding how lysosomal failure drives cellular pathology.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

LSDs arise from monogenic loss of function affecting acid hydrolases (e.g., α-galactosidase A in Fabry disease, glucocerebrosidase in Gaucher disease, α-L-iduronidase in Hurler syndrome), activator proteins, or trafficking/sorting factors (e.g., LAMP-2 in Danon disease, [[TFEB]]-related regulatory defects). The primary block causes substrate buildup within enlarged, dysfunctional lysosomes. This produces a secondary [[Autophagic Flux]] blockade: autophagosomes fuse with impaired lysosomes to form non-degradative autolysosomes, so even otherwise normal cargo (organelles, protein aggregates) accumulates. The resulting proteostasis and [[Oxidative Stress]] collapse underlies the multisystem, often neurodegenerative, phenotypes.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

LSDs present with variable but frequently progressive neurologic decline, hepatosplenomegaly, skeletal dysplasia (dysostosis multiplex), cardiomyopathy, and respiratory involvement. Diagnosis relies on enzyme activity assays in leukocytes/fibroblasts, urinary glycosaminoglycan excretion, and genetic testing; biomarkers include elevated plasma lyso-Gb3 (Fabry), chitotriosidase, and glucosylsphingosine (Gaucher). Many LSDs are screened by newborn tandem mass spectrometry where applicable.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Standard-of-care includes enzyme replacement therapy (ERT), substrate reduction therapy (SRT), chaperone therapy (e.g., migalastat for amenable Gaucher/Fabry mutations), and hematopoietic stem cell transplant. A unifying disease-modifying strategy is pharmacologic activation of [[TFEB]] to enhance lysosomal biogenesis, exocytosis, and clearance of stored material, as well as boosting basal [[Autophagy]] to relieve the secondary autophagic block. Preclinical TFEB activators (e.g., via [[mTORC1]] inhibition or [[Trehalose]]-mimetic small molecules) reduce storage in multiple LSD models. Gene therapy and CRISPR-based correction are emerging for several LSDs. Because lysosomal failure is also central to [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegeneration]], lessons from LSDs inform broader autophagy-lysosome restorative medicine.

## Connections

- [[TFEB]]: Master regulator of lysosomal biogenesis; pharmacologic activation is a leading LSD therapeutic strategy.
- [[Lysosome]]: Defective organelle at the root of all LSD pathology.
- [[Autophagic Flux]]: Secondarily blocked in LSDs, compounding substrate accumulation.
- [[Oxidative Stress]]: Consequence of failed lysosomal clearance in LSDs.
- [[mTORC1]]: Inhibition activates TFEB and relieves lysosomal storage.
- [[Trehalose]]: mTOR-independent TFEB/autophagy activator studied in LSD models.
- [[Neurodegeneration]]: Frequent, severe manifestation of LSDs.
- [[Aging]]: Shared lysosomal decline links LSD mechanisms to senescence.
