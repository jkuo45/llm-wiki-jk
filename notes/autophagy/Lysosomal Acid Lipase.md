# Lysosomal Acid Lipase

**Lysosomal Acid Lipase** (**LAL**; also known as **LipA** in mice) is a critical enzyme that hydrolyzes cholesteryl esters and triglycerides delivered to the [[Lysosome]] via [[Lipophagy]] or endocytosis.

## Mechanism
- LAL operates within the acidic environment of the lysosome to break down lipids into free fatty acids and glycerol.
- In mammals, it is encoded by the *LIPA* gene.

## Regulation
- **Nutrient Status**: Transcriptionally induced by [[TFEB]] during nutrient deprivation or starvation.
- **Transcriptional Control**: In humans and mice, [[TFEB]] links lysosomal function and autophagy to nutrient availability.

## Clinical Significance
- **LAL Deficiency (LALD)**: Mutations in the *LIPA* gene lead to lysosomal storage diseases such as Wolman disease and Cholesteryl Ester Storage Disease (CESD), characterized by massive accumulation of lipids in various tissues.
- **Metabolic Diseases**: Malfunction of the mechanisms coupling LAL to nutritional status may underlie metabolic disorders.

## Orthologues
- In [[C. elegans]], the functional orthologues are the [[LIPL-1]], [[LIPL-2]], [[LIPL-3]], and [[LIPL-5]] proteins, which are regulated by [[MXL-3]] and [[HLH-30]].
