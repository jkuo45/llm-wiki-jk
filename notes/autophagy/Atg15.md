# Atg15

Atg15 is a yeast ([[Saccharomyces cerevisiae]]) autophagy protein with predicted triglyceride lipase/esterase activity. It is delivered via autophagic bodies into the vacuole (the yeast [[Lysosome]] equivalent), where it degrades lipids and the membranes of engulfed cargo. Atg15 is the yeast counterpart of mammalian [[Lysosomal Acid Lipase]] (LIPA) and is central to lipophagy — the selective autophagy of lipid droplets — in fungi.

## Structure & Trafficking

Atg15 is a transmembrane glycoprotein of the vacuolar lumen. It is synthesized at the ER, transits through the secretory pathway, and is sorted into autophagic bodies via the cytoplasm-to-vacuole targeting (Cvt) pathway and nonspecific autophagy. Once the autophagic body fuses with the vacuole and is ruptured by vacuolar hydrolases, Atg15 becomes active in the lumen, where the acidic environment supports its lipolytic function.

## Mechanism in Lipid Degradation

Atg15 hydrolyzes triglycerides and phospholipids, releasing free fatty acids and providing both membrane-building precursors and energy substrates during starvation. During lipophagy, lipid droplets are sequestered into autophagosomes, fused with the vacuole/lysosome, and Atg15 (or its mammalian LIPA homolog) cleaves stored triglycerides. Atg15 also contributes to breakdown of the autophagic body membrane itself, enabling complete turnover of cargo. Loss of Atg15 leads to accumulation of lipid droplets and undegraded autophagic bodies, impairing viability under starvation.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Although Atg15 is yeast-specific, its mammalian homolog [[Lysosomal Acid Lipase]] underlies Wolman disease and cholesteryl ester storage disease when deficient, conditions of failed lysosomal lipid clearance. The lipophagic principle established by Atg15 studies is conserved: autophagy delivers lipid stores to lysosomes for catabolism, linking [[Autophagy]] to [[Aging]], [[Cancer]] metabolic rewiring, and [[Oxidative Stress]]-driven lipid peroxidation. Atg15 remains a model enzyme for understanding how autophagy intersects with cellular lipid homeostasis.
