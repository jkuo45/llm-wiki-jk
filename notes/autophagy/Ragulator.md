# Ragulator

The **Ragulator** complex is a pentameric scaffold (LAMTOR1–LAMTOR5) anchored at the lysosomal membrane that both tethers the heterodimeric [[Rag proteins]] (RagA/B–RagC/D) to the organelle surface and acts as a guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) for RagA/B. By localizing and activating Rag GTPases, Ragulator is the central platform that recruits and activates [[mTORC1]] upon amino acid sensing, placing it at the crossroads of nutrient signaling and [[Autophagy]] suppression.

## Composition & Architecture

Ragulator is built on LAMTOR1, a single-pass membrane anchor whose N-terminal cytosolic region organizes the LAMTOR2–5 subcomplex. LAMTOR2 (p14/MP1) binds LAMTOR1 and bridges LAMTOR3 (p18), LAMTOR4 (C7orf59), and LAMTOR5 (HBXIP). The LAMTOR2–5 assembly forms the GEF-active core. LAMTOR1's myristoylated and palmitoylated N-terminus embeds in the lysosomal membrane, orienting the complex to present Rag GTPases to their upstream amino-acid sensors and to mTORC1.

## Mechanism of mTORC1 Activation

In amino acid-replete cells, lysosomal sensors such as [[SLC38A9]] and the vacuolar ATPase–Ragulator axis promote GTP-loading of RagA/B via Ragulator's GEF activity, while RagC/D become GDP-bound. The active Rag heterodimer binds the Raptor subunit of [[mTORC1]], recruiting the kinase to the lysosomal surface where it is activated by [[Rheb]]. When amino acids are scarce, Ragulator's GAP (for RagC/D) and associated phosphatases inactivate the complex, mTORC1 dissociates, and [[TFEB]]/TFE3 are de-repressed to induce lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Ragulator thus couples lysosomal nutrient status directly to growth-control and catabolic programs. Hyperactive Ragulator–Rag signaling drives mTORC1-dependent proliferation in [[Cancer]] and can suppress autophagic tumor surveillance, while impaired Ragulator assembly disrupts metabolic homeostasis and contributes to [[Aging]]-related decline. Genetic lesions in LAMTOR subunits affect mTORC1 routing and lysosomal positioning, linking the complex to developmental and metabolic disease.
