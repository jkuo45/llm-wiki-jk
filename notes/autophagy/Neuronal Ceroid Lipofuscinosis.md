# Neuronal Ceroid Lipofuscinosis

A group of inherited neurodegenerative disorders known as Batten disease, characterized by the accumulation of lipopigments in [[Lysosome|lysosomes]].

## Etiology & Genetics

NCLs are caused by mutations in at least 13 genes ([[CLN1]]-[[CLN8]], [[CLN10]]-[[CLN14]]), most encoding lysosomal enzymes (e.g., palmitoyl-protein thioesterase 1, tripeptidyl peptidase I) or transmembrane proteins involved in lysosomal function. Inheritance is primarily autosomal recessive.
## Pathophysiology

Defective lysosomal degradation leads to accumulation of autofluorescent lipopigments (ceroid and lipofuscin), consisting of subunit c of mitochondrial ATP synthase and saposin proteins. This storage causes progressive neurodegeneration, especially in the cerebral cortex, cerebellum, and retina.
## Clinical Presentation

Onset ranges from infantile to adult forms. Symptoms include progressive vision loss (retinal degeneration), epilepsy, cognitive and motor decline, and premature death. Diagnosis is confirmed by enzyme assay, genetic sequencing, or electron microscopy showing characteristic fingerprint or granular osmophilic deposits.
## Therapeutic Landscape

No curative treatment exists. [[TFEB]] activation has emerged as a potential therapeutic strategy, as TFEB overexpression enhances lysosomal exocytosis and reduces storage material in NCL models. Enzyme replacement therapy ([[Brineura]] for CLN2), gene therapy, and small-molecule chaperones are under clinical investigation.
