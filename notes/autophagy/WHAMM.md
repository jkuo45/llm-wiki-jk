# WHAMM

**WHAMM** (WASP Homolog Associated with Actin, Membranes, and Microtubules) is a nucleation-promoting factor of the WASP (Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome protein) family that orchestrates branched actin polymerization at membrane surfaces. By recruiting the [[Arp2/3]] complex, WHAMM builds actin networks on ER, Golgi, and autolysosomal membranes that drive membrane tubulation, organelle motility, and membrane remodeling. It plays recognized roles in [[Autophagosome]] biogenesis/trafficking and in [[Autophagic Lysosome Reformation]] (ALR), where actin-dependent tubulation regenerates lysosomes from autolysosomes.

## Structure & Mechanism

WHAMM contains an N-terminal membrane-binding region (recognizing phosphoinositides such as PtdIns(4,5)P2 and PtdIns4P), a central verprolin-cofilin-acidic (VCA) domain that activates Arp2/3, and a C-terminal coiled-coil/microtubule-interacting region. Membrane recruitment positions WHAMM to catalyze local Arp2/3-dependent actin nucleation; the resulting branched networks generate pushing forces for tubule extension and vesicle motility along microtubules. WHAMM thus integrates actin and microtubule cytoskeletons at organelle membranes.

## Role in Autophagy & ALR

During ALR, WHAMM-depedent actin assembly on autolysosomes promotes the scission of proto-lysosomal tubules—together with PtdIns(4,5)P2, clathrin, and [[AP2]]—to restore the lysosomal pool after autophagic degradation. At autophagosome formation, WHAMM contributes actin dynamics at ER–phagophore contact sites, supporting membrane expansion and trafficking of isolation membranes. Loss of WHAMM disrupts both autophagic flux and lysosomal reformation, leading to autolysosomal swelling.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

WHAMM's membrane-trafficking functions implicate it in Golgi integrity, ER homeostasis, and neuronal organelle transport, linking its dysfunction to [[Neurodegeneration]] where autolysosomal recycling fails. Because it couples actin forces to autophagic membrane remodeling, WHAMM is a candidate node modulating autophagic capacity during [[Aging]] and in [[Cancer]] cell migration and secretion.
