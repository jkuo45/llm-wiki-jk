# SUMOylation

**SUMOylation** is a reversible post-translational modification in which Small Ubiquitin-like Modifier (SUMO) proteins are covalently attached to lysine residues of target proteins. Like ubiquitylation, it proceeds through an E1 activating enzyme (SAE1/SAE2), an E2 conjugating enzyme (UBC9), and E3 ligases (e.g., PIAS, RanBP2, and the SENP family in reverse), but SUMO generally modulates protein interactions, subcellular localization, and stability rather than directing degradation. In [[Autophagy]], SUMOylation dynamically regulates key nodes including [[ULK1]], [[TFEB]], and [[GABARAP]], balancing autophagic induction against lysosomal biogenesis.

## Mechanism of Conjugation

Mature SUMO (SUMO1–SUMO3 in humans) is activated by the SAE1/SAE2 heterodimer, transferred to UBC9, and ligated to substrate lysines within ΨKXE consensus motifs (or non-consensus sites via E3 recruitment). SUMO-specific proteases (SENPs) cleave SUMO off, making the mark highly dynamic. Competition and crosstalk with ubiquitylation and phosphorylation integrate SUMOylation into nutrient- and stress-responsive signaling.

## Regulation of Autophagy Components

SUMOylation of [[TFEB]] at K316 attenuates its transcriptional activity, dampening expression of lysosomal and autophagy genes. ULK1 SUMOylation influences its kinase activity and interaction with downstream ATG factors, modulating initiation under stress. GABARAP family members—key LC3 paralogs for autophagosome closure—are SUMO-regulated to control their membrane association and flux. The balance of SUMOylation versus de-SUMOylation thus sets the threshold for autophagic induction and autophagic–lysosomal maturation.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Altered SUMOylation is implicated in [[Neurodegeneration]], where impaired clearance of SUMO-modified aggregates accumulates, and in [[Cancer]], where SUMO pathway enzymes are frequently dysregulated to support proliferation and stress survival. Modulating SENPs or E3 ligases offers a route to tune [[Autophagy]] for therapeutic benefit in proteinopathies and metabolic disease associated with [[Aging]].
