# T Cell

**T cells** are lymphocytes central to the [[Adaptive Immune Response]], mediating cellular immunity through antigen-specific recognition by the T cell receptor. Autophagy is unexpectedly indispensable for T cell biology: it supports thymic development, naive-to-effector activation, clonal expansion, memory formation, and the metabolic reprogramming that fuels proliferation. By clearing damaged mitochondria and recycling nutrients, [[Autophagy]] preserves T cell proteostasis and bioenergetic flexibility under the stringent demands of immune activation.

## Metabolic Dependence on Autophagy

Upon antigen recognition, T cells shift from oxidative phosphorylation to aerobic glycolysis while also requiring mitochondrial integrity. [[Mitophagy]] removes depolarized mitochondria, preventing excess [[Oxidative Stress]] and apoptotic priming, and supports the metabolic switch. Autophagy also supplies amino acids and intermediates during the nutrient-limited phases of clonal expansion, sustaining mTORC1-dependent growth. Loss of Atg proteins in T cells impairs survival, skews cytokine output, and diminishes memory generation.

## Roles in Differentiation & Function

Autophagy modulates T cell subset fate: it restrains excessive [[Inflammation]] by limiting inflammasome activation and mitochondrial DNA release, and it influences regulatory T cell (Treg) suppressive function and effector CD8+ cytotoxic activity. Selective autophagy of signaling molecules tunes TCR and cytokine receptor downstream pathways, integrating environmental cues with immune output. In chronic infection and [[Cancer]], exhausted T cells show defective autophagic flux that compromises antitumor immunity.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

T cell-autophagy intersects with [[Aging]] (immunosenescence) and with neurodegeneration-associated neuroinflammation. Enhancing autophagic capacity in T cells is a strategy to improve vaccine responses and checkpoint immunotherapy, whereas过度 autophagy can trigger T cell death. The autophagy–T cell axis is thus a therapeutic frontier in immunotherapy and inflammatory disease.
