# CLEAR Element

The CLEAR Element (Coordinated Lysosomal Expression and Regulation) is a 10-bp E-box-like consensus motif (5′-GTCACGTGAC-3′) enriched in the promoters and enhancers of lysosomal and autophagic genes. It serves as the cognate binding site for the MiT/TFE transcription factors [[TFEB]] and [[TFE3]], coordinating a genome-wide program of [[Lysosome]] biogenesis, autophagosome component expression, and [[Autophagic Flux]]. The discovery of CLEAR provided the molecular explanation for how a small set of transcription factors can orchestrate hundreds of degradative-organelle genes in a coordinated, nutrient-responsive manner.

## Sequence & Genomic Organization

CLEAR is a variation of the canonical E-box (CANNTG), with the central CACGTG (E-box core) embedded in a CLEAR-specific flanking context. Bioinformatic mapping in mammalian genomes identified thousands of CLEAR sites near lysosomal hydrolase genes (e.g., [[LAMP1]], [[LAMP2]], cathepsins), v-ATPase subunits, and autophagy genes ([[LC3B]], [[Atg]] family members). The number and affinity of CLEAR sites correlate with the strength of TFEB responsiveness of a given target.

## Mechanism of Action

Under starvation or lysosomal stress, [[mTORC1]] is inhibited and TFEB/TFE3 are dephosphorylated, dissociating from [[14-3-3 protein]] and entering the nucleus. There they bind CLEAR elements and recruit co-activators (e.g., [[EBF1]], MEF2, CREB) and chromatin remodelers to drive transcription. Conversely, lysosomal inhibitors (chloroquine) or accumulated undigested cargo also activate TFEB via feedback, increasing CLEAR-driven expression to restore degradative capacity. CLEAR thus couples the functional state of the lysosome to its own biosynthesis.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Enhanced CLEAR activity boosts lysosomal clearance, protecting against [[Neurodegeneration]] and protein aggregation, and is a therapeutic target in lysosomal storage and aging-related decline ([[Aging]]). TFEB/CLEAR activation is being pursued pharmacologically (e.g., via [[mTORC1]] inhibitors or TFEB activators) to treat Alzheimer's and lysosomal diseases. Conversely, dysregulated CLEAR output can support [[Cancer]] cell fitness and [[Oxidative Stress]] resilience. The CLEAR network exemplifies transcriptional coordination of the autophagy–lysosome axis.
