# Nutrient Availability

**Nutrient Availability** refers to the presence and concentration of essential nutrients (e.g., glucose, amino acids, lipids) in the cellular environment, which dictates metabolic priorities and autophagic activity.

## Sensing Mechanisms
- **[[mTORC1]]**: A master nutrient sensor that is active under high nutrient conditions, promoting growth and inhibiting [[Autophagy]].
- **[[AMPK]]**: Activated by low energy (high AMP/ATP ratio), promoting energy-producing pathways and autophagy.
- **[[TFEB]] / [[HLH-30]]**: Transcription factors that coordinate the cellular response to nutrient scarcity by inducing lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy.

## Response to Scarcity
- When nutrients are low ([[Starvation]] or [[Fasting]]), cells activate [[Autophagy]] to recycle internal components and mobilize energy stores.
- **[[Lipophagy]]**: Specifically targets [[Lipid Droplet]]s for degradation via [[Lysosomal Lipolysis]] to provide fatty acids for β-oxidation.
- **Transcriptional Switches**: In [[C. elegans]], [[MXL-3]] (repressor) and [[HLH-30]] (activator) link nutrient status to the mobilization of fat reserves.

## Physiological Impact
- Proper sensing and response to nutrient availability are essential for survival during food deprivation and are linked to [[Aging|lifespan]] regulation and [[Metabolic Diseases]].
