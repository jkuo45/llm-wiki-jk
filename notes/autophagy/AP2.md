# AP2

AP2 (Adaptor Protein complex 2) is a heterotetrameric clathrin adaptor (α, β2, μ2, σ2 subunits) best known for sorting cargo at the plasma membrane during clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Beyond its classical endocytic role, AP2 has been implicated in [[Autophagic Lysosome Reformation]] (ALR) — the process by which [[Lysosome]] tubules bud from autolysosomes to regenerate a functional lysosomal pool after autophagic digestion. During ALR, AP2 is recruited to autolysosomal microdomains enriched in [[PtdIns(4,5)P2]] (PIP2), where it nucleates clathrin coats that drive membrane tubulation.

## Structure & Cargo Recognition

The AP2 complex uses its μ2 subunit to bind YXXΦ tyrosine-based motifs and its σ2 subunit to engage [DE]XXXL[LI] dileucine motifs in cargo cytosolic tails. The α-appendage and β2-soldering domains recognize additional accessory/phosphoinositide-binding partners. AP2's engagement with membranes is lipid-dependent: PIP2 binding and phosphorylation of the μ2 subunit (by AAK1/GAK) cooperatively open the complex into an active, cargo-engaging conformation.

## Mechanism in Autophagic Lysosome Reformation

After autophagosome–lysosome fusion, the resulting autolysosome must be resolved. ALR depends on the [[mTORC1]]-recruited [[PIP4K]]/[[PtdIns(4,5)P2]] axis at the autolysosomal surface. AP2 binds PtdIns(4,5)P2 and recruits clathrin to generate coated tubules that pinch off as proto-lysosomes, replenishing the lysosomal compartment and sustaining [[Autophagic Flux]]. Depletion of AP2 subunits impairs tubule formation and lysosome reformation, leading to swollen, digestion-incompetent vacuoles.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

AP2-mediated ALR is essential for maintaining lysosomal homeostasis in cells with high autophagic turnover, such as neurons and macrophages. Defects in AP2 or its PIP2 cue perturb [[Autophagy]] resolution and contribute to [[Neurodegeneration]] and lysosomal storage phenotypes. AP2 subunits are also mutated in developmental and neurodevelopmental disorders, and AP2's endocytic functions are hijacked by pathogens (e.g., HIV Nef) and exploited in [[Cancer]] invasiveness.
