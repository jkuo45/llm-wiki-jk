# Atg101

Atg101 (autophagy-related protein 101) is a metazoan-specific subunit of the [[ULK1]] initiation complex, essential for the induction of [[Macroautophagy|autophagy]]. It has no yeast ortholog, reflecting the expanded regulatory architecture of autophagy in higher eukaryotes. Atg101's principal role is to stabilize [[Atg13]] against proteasomal degradation and to reinforce the assembly of the Atg1/[[ULK1]]–Atg13–Atg101–[[FIP200]] core complex at the phagophore assembly site.

## Structure & Complex Architecture

Atg101 is a small (~20 kDa) predominantly α-helical protein that binds directly to the N-terminal region of Atg13 through a conserved interaction surface. This binding protects Atg13 from recognition by the proteasome and is required for the high-molecular-weight organization of the ULK1 complex. Atg101 does not possess catalytic activity; rather, it acts as a structural scaffold that nucleates the initiation machinery. Formation of the Atg101–Atg13 subcomplex is stabilized by [[mTORC1]]/[[AMPK]] phosphorylation cues transmitted through ULK1.

## Mechanism in Autophagy Initiation

Under nutrient-replete conditions, [[mTORC1]] phosphorylates Atg13 and ULK1, maintaining the complex in a relatively inactive, dispersed state. Upon starvation, mTORC1 is released from the [[Lysosome]] and AMPK activates ULK1; the Atg101–Atg13 platform then promotes ULK1 kinase activity and recruitment of downstream factors (including the [[Vps34]] class III PI3K complex via [[Atg14]]) to the emerging [[Autophagosome]] isolation membrane. Knockdown of Atg101 destabilizes Atg13, abolishes ULK1 complex integrity, and blocks autophagosome formation.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because Atg101 is required for de novo autophagosome biogenesis, its loss compromises cellular proteostasis and stress survival, linking it to [[Aging]], [[Neurodegeneration]], and [[Cancer]] metabolic adaptation. Atg101 levels are modulated during differentiation and in response to nutrient stress, and the ULK1 complex (including Atg101) is a target of viral hijacking and oncogenic signaling. Atg101 therefore represents a potential node for pharmacologically tuning autophagy induction without broadly inhibiting mTOR.
