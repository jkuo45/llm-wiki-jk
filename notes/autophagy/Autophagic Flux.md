# Autophagic Flux

Autophagic flux is the integrated, dynamic rate at which cellular cargo is sequestered, delivered, and degraded through the full autophagy pathway — from [[Autophagosome]] biogenesis through fusion with the [[Lysosome]] (forming the autolysosome) and terminal catabolism. Measuring flux, rather than merely counting autophagosomes, is essential because accumulated autophagosomes can reflect either increased induction or blocked degradation. Flux thus captures the *throughput* of autophagy as a degradative system.

## Stages of the Flux

Flux comprises: (1) initiation and phagophore nucleation governed by the [[ULK1]] and [[Vps34]] complexes; (2) elongation and closure with [[LC3B]]-II decoration and cargo recruitment via [[p62]]/SQSTM1; (3) autophagosome–lysosome fusion driven by Rab7, [[LAMP2]], and SNARE proteins (e.g., STX17); (4) intralysosomal acidification and hydrolase-mediated breakdown; and (5) [[Autophagic Lysosome Reformation]] to regenerate lysosomes. Each step is rate-limiting and independently regulatable.

## Measurement Strategies

Flux is quantitated by comparing autophagic markers with and without lysosomal inhibitors (e.g., bafilomycin A1, chloroquine, NH4Cl). A true increase in flux is shown by accelerated LC3-II turnover and p62 depletion only in the absence of inhibitor. Tandem mCherry-GFP-LC3 probes exploit pH-sensitive GFP quenching in the autolysosome to discriminate autophagosomes (yellow) from autolysosomes (red). Long-lived protein degradation assays (³H-leucine/¹⁴C-valine release) provide functional confirmation.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Impaired flux — not merely altered autophagosome number — underlies pathology. In [[Neurodegeneration]], defective fusion or lysosomal proteolysis causes toxic aggregate accumulation. In [[Cancer]], flux can be hijacked for survival under hypoxia or chemotherapy. Diminished flux contributes to [[Aging]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] damage, while enhancing flux is a therapeutic goal in proteinopathies and metabolic disease. Drugs such as rapamycin (activating [[mTOR]] inhibition) boost flux, whereas chloroquine blocks it (used in cancer and malaria).
