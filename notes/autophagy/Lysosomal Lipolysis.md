# Lysosomal Lipolysis

**Lysosomal Lipolysis** is the breakdown of lipids (triglycerides and cholesteryl esters) within the acidic environment of the [[Lysosome]]. This process is primarily executed by [[Lysosomal Acid Lipase]] (LAL) and its orthologues.

## Mechanism and Context
- Lysosomal lipolysis is the effector phase of [[Lipophagy]], where lipid-containing cargo (e.g., from [[Lipid Droplet]]s or endocytosis) is degraded.
- It requires an acidic pH (typically ~4.5) for optimal enzymatic activity.

## Regulation
- **Transcriptional Switches**:
    - In [[C. elegans]], the transcription factors [[MXL-3]] (repressor) and [[HLH-30]] (activator) coordinate the response to [[Nutrient Availability]].
    - In mammals, [[TFEB]] induces the expression of lysosomal lipases and autophagic machinery in response to [[Starvation]].
- **Nutrient Sensors**: Activity is modulated by [[mTORC1]] and insulin signaling (e.g., [[DAF-2]]).

## Role in Ageing
- Enhanced lysosomal lipolysis has been shown to extend lifespan in [[C. elegans]], possibly by improving cellular clearance of lipid moieties and re-routing energy reserves for somatic maintenance.
