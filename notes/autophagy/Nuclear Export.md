# Nuclear Export

Nuclear export is the active transport of proteins and RNAs from the nucleus to the cytoplasm through the [[Nuclear Pore Complex]], mediated mainly by the export receptor CRM1 (XPO1, exportin-1) recognizing leucine-rich nuclear export signals (NES). For the autophagy-lysosome network, nuclear export of transcription factors such as [[TFEB]], [[ZKSCAN3]], and [[FOXO]] determines whether catabolic programs are transcriptionally engaged; TFEB nuclear export is accelerated by mTORC1/ERK-mediated phosphorylation at S142 (and S138), trapping it in the cytoplasm and suppressing [[Autophagy]].

## Mechanism

CRM1 binds cargo bearing a hydrophobic NES in a RanGTP-dependent manner and translocates the complex through the nuclear pore. For TFEB, nutrient-rich conditions activate [[mTORC1]] (at the [[Lysosome]]) and ERK2, which phosphorylate TFEB at S142 (and priming S138); these phospho-marks create or expose an NES that recruits CRM1, driving TFEB out of the nucleus and into 14-3-3-bound cytosolic complexes. Under starvation, mTORC1 inhibition dephosphorylates TFEB, allowing it to accumulate in the nucleus and activate CLEAR-network lysosomal and autophagy genes. ZKSCAN3 and FOXO family members are similarly subject to CRM1-dependent shuttling, linking nuclear export broadly to autophagic transcription.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because nuclear export gates the master autophagy regulator TFEB, its pharmacologic modulation is therapeutically attractive. CRM1 inhibitors (e.g., leptomycin B, selinexor/KPT-330, verdinexor) block TFEB export, forcing nuclear accumulation that boosts lysosomal biogenesis and autophagic clearance—beneficial in [[Lysosomal Storage Disorders]], [[Neurodegeneration]], and models of [[Aging]]. However, CRM1 is broadly expressed and exports many oncoproteins and tumor suppressors, so CRM1 inhibitors also affect [[Cancer]] cell survival with narrow therapeutic windows. The balance between nuclear import (via importins/KPNAs) and CRM1 export thus fine-tunes the autophagic transcriptional program in health and disease, and is a key control layer atop mTORC1/TFEB phosphorylation.
