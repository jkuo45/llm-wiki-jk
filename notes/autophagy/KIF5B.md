# KIF5B

KIF5B (Kinesin Family Member 5B), the conventional kinesin-1 heavy chain, is a microtubule-dependent motor protein that transports membrane-bound organelles and vesicles toward the plus ends of microtubules. In the autophagy-lysosome axis, KIF5B drives the extension of lysosomal tubules along microtubules during [[Autophagic Lysosome Reformation]] (ALR) and [[Phagocytic Lysosome Reformation]], orchestrating the recycling of [[Lysosome]] membranes after autolysosomal degradation.

## Structure & Mechanism

KIF5B forms a tetrameric motor with two heavy chains (each containing an N-terminal motor/ATPase domain, a coiled-coil stalk, and a C-terminal cargo-binding tail) and two light chains. The motor domain hydrolyzes ATP to step processively along microtubules toward the cell periphery (plus-end directed). Cargo recruitment is mediated by scaffold proteins such as FYCO1, which links kinesin to autophagosomes via [[Rab7]] and to phosphatidylinositol lipids. KIF5B also interacts with [[PtdIns(4,5)P2]] on autolysosomal membranes, where the phosphoinositide helps anchor and direct tubule extension during reformation.

## Role in Autophagy & Lysosome Reformation

During macroautophagy, KIF5B transports nascent [[Autophagosome]]s and autolysosomes along microtubules toward the perinuclear lysosome-rich region for fusion, enabling efficient degradation. After fusion, the enlarged autolysosome must be rebuilt into functional lysosomes; this is the ALR/PLR reformation step. KIF5B powers the transient tubular protrusions that bud from the autolysosome, a process dependent on [[PIKfyve]]-generated phosphoinositides, [[TRPML1]]-mediated calcium release, and [[PtdIns(4,5)P2]] enrichment at tubule necks. Defective KIF5B motility traps autolysosomes, blocking [[Autophagic Flux]] and causing substrate accumulation.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

KIF5B is essential for polarized transport in neurons, where impaired autolysosome positioning contributes to [[Neurodegeneration]] and axonal pathology. The KIF5B::RET and KIF5B::ALK fusion oncogenes, generated by chromosomal rearrangements, drive papillary thyroid and non-small-cell lung cancers, linking the motor to [[Cancer]]. Because ALR efficiency determines how quickly the lysosome pool is restored after autophagy, KIF5B is a determinant of autophagic capacity in [[Aging]] and stress. Modulating kinesin-lysosome coupling is therefore a candidate strategy to sustain autophagic clearance.
