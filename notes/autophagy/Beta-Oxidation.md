# Beta-Oxidation

Beta-Oxidation is a key metabolic process in which fatty acid molecules are broken down into smaller components, such as Acetyl-CoA, to generate cellular energy. While mitochondria handle the bulk of beta-oxidation for short and medium-chain fatty acids, the [[Peroxisome]] is the primary site for the preliminary beta-oxidation of very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs). This peroxisomal step is essential to shorten these large lipids before they can enter the mitochondria for complete oxidation. Impairment of peroxisomal beta-oxidation, often due to genetic defects affecting organelle biogenesis or transporters like [[ABCD1]], leads to toxic VLCFA accumulation and severe metabolic diseases such as [[Zellweger syndrome]] and [[Adrenoleukodystrophy]].

## Process

Fatty acid β-oxidation occurs in [[Mitochondria]] and [[Peroxisome]]s. Autophagy delivers lipids from [[Lipid Droplet]]s for oxidation.
