# Adrenoleukodystrophy

Adrenoleukodystrophy (ALD) is a severe genetic disorder often caused by mutations in the [[ABCD1]] gene, which encodes a transporter responsible for moving very long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs) into the [[Peroxisome]] for degradation. Defective VLCFA transport impairs peroxisomal lipid turnover and [[Beta-Oxidation]], leading to the toxic accumulation of VLCFAs. This accumulation induces severe oxidative stress and inflammation, primarily damaging the myelin sheath in the brain and affecting the adrenal glands. It represents a significant form of peroxisomal dysfunction that highlights the critical role of these organelles in lipid homeostasis.

## Pathophysiology

ALD is caused by [[ABCD1]] mutations, leading to accumulation of VLCFAs and peroxisomal dysfunction. Impaired [[Autophagy]] contributes to neuroinflammation and demyelination.
