# CASTOR2

CASTOR2 (cellular arginine sensor for mTORC1, subunit 2) is a paralog of [[CASTOR1]] that together with CASTOR1 forms a cytosolic arginine sensor upstream of [[mTORC1]]. The CASTOR1–CASTOR2 complex binds the amino acid [[Arginine]] and, in its arginine-bound state, releases inhibition of the [[GATOR2]] complex, permitting mTORC1 activation at the [[Lysosome]]. CASTOR2 provides redundancy and combinatorial sensitivity, allowing the nutrient-sensing circuit to integrate arginine availability with other amino acid inputs.

## Structure & Complex Assembly

CASTOR proteins are composed of N-terminal Cache domains (responsible for arginine binding) and C-terminal winged-helix domains that mediate dimerization and GATOR2 interaction. CASTOR1 and CASTOR2 can heterodimerize (CASTOR1–CASTOR2) or form homodimers; the heterodimer is the principal arginine-responsive species. In the absence of arginine, the complex binds and inhibits GATOR2; arginine binding induces a conformational change that dissociates CASTOR from GATOR2, relieving repression of mTORC1.

## Mechanism in mTORC1 Regulation

Amino acid sufficiency is relayed to mTORC1 by the GATOR1–GATOR2–CASTOR axis. When arginine is scarce, CASTOR1/2 binds GATOR2, preventing GATOR2 from inhibiting the GAP GATOR1; active GATOR1 keeps [[Rag GTPase]] in the inactive state, so mTORC1 is not recruited to lysosomes and [[Autophagy]] proceeds. When arginine binds CASTOR, GATOR2 is freed, GATOR1 is inhibited, Rag GTPases activate, mTORC1 translocates to the lysosome, and autophagy is suppressed. CASTOR2 thus fine-tunes the threshold of arginine required for mTORC1-driven growth signaling.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

The CASTOR system couples protein intake to growth and autophagy suppression, with implications for [[Cancer]] (where mTORC1 hyperactivation drives proliferation), [[Aging]] (nutrient-sensing decline), and metabolic disease. CASTOR mutations or dysregulation alter cellular arginine sensitivity and autophagic set-point. Because arginine depletion activates autophagy and can synergize with [[Oxidative Stress]] stressors, CASTOR1/2 are relevant to dietary restriction and starvation responses.
