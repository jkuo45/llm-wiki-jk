# PhenoAge

PhenoAge is a widely used epigenetic clock developed by Levine and colleagues that estimates "phenotypic age" from DNA methylation at 513 CpG sites. Unlike first-generation clocks trained purely on chronology, PhenoAge was constructed to track a composite of nine clinical chemistry and hematological biomarkers (including albumin, glucose, and C-reactive protein) strongly associated with all-cause mortality, giving it strong predictive validity for biological aging rather than merely elapsed time.

## Construction & Validation

The clock uses elastic-net regression to link methylation to a mortality-weighted "phenotypic age" estimator. It performs well across tissues and outperforms chronological age for predicting [[Aging|age-related]] morbidity, frailty, and [[Healthspan]] endpoints. Elevated epigenetic age acceleration ("age acceleration") correlates with [[Inflammation]], [[Oxidative Stress]], and [[Senescence]].

## Relation to Other Clocks

PhenoAge complements the pan-tissue [[Horvath Clock]] and the blood-based [[Hannum Clock]], and is often compared with the rate-based [[DunedinPACE]] estimator. Together these clocks operationalize [[Epigenetic Alterations]] as a hallmark of aging.

## Longevity Relevance

PhenoAge is routinely used to test whether interventions — [[Caloric Restriction]], [[Rapamycin]], [[Senolytics|senolytics]] — slow epigenetic aging in humans, serving as a translational biomarker bridging geroscience and clinical trials.

> [!info] Clinical Anchor
> PhenoAge's tie to routine blood biomarkers makes it especially attractive for large human cohort studies without dedicated methylation panels.
