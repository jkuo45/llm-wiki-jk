# Myelin

**Myelin** is a multilamellar lipid-protein sheath that wraps around axons in the [[Nervous System]], produced by [[Oligodendrocytes]] in the central nervous system (CNS) and [[Schwann Cells]] in the peripheral nervous system (PNS). It acts as electrical insulation, enabling rapid saltatory conduction of [[Action Potential|action potentials]] via clustering of [[Voltage-gated sodium channels]] at Nodes of Ranvier.

## Composition

Myelin is ~70-80% [[Lipid]] and ~20-30% [[Protein]] by dry weight. The major lipid components include [[Cholesterol]], [[Galactocerebroside]], and [[Sphingomyelin]]. Key structural proteins are [[Myelin Basic Protein]] (MBP) and [[Proteolipid Protein]] (PLP) in the CNS, and [[P0 protein]] and [[Myelin-associated glycoprotein]] (MAG) in the PNS.

## Function

- **Saltatory Conduction** — myelin increases conduction velocity up to 50× vs. unmyelinated axons without increasing axon diameter
- **Metabolic Support** — oligodendrocytes provide [[Lactate]] to axons via [[MCT1]] transporters
- **Axonal Integrity** — myelin proteins signal axon survival; demyelination leads to axonal degeneration

## Pathology

- [[Multiple Sclerosis]] — autoimmune demyelination in the CNS; [[T Cell]]-mediated attack on myelin antigens (MBP, PLP, [[MOG]])
- [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome]] — acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy in the PNS
- [[Leukodystrophies]] — genetic disorders of myelin metabolism (e.g., [[Metachromatic Leukodystrophy]], [[Krabbe Disease]])
- Decreased myelination occurs with [[Aging]] and correlates with [[Cognitive decline]]
