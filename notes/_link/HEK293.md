## Overview

**HEK293** is a perennial human cell line derived in 1973 by Graham and colleagues through transformation of embryonic kidney cells from a female fetus with sheared adenovirus 5 DNA. The cells express the adenoviral E1A and E1B regions, conferring ease of transfection and rapid growth.

## Origin and Genotype

The parental line is hypotriploid and shows epithelial-like morphology, though transcriptomic analyses suggest partial neuronal/adrenal lineage features. Clonal derivatives include HEK293T (expressing SV40 large T antigen for episomal replication) and HEK293F/HEK293S for suspension and glycosylation engineering.

## Common Research Uses

HEK293 is the workhorse for transient and stable recombinant protein production, including receptors, ion channels, and secreted factors. It is heavily used in [[Mitochondria]]-related and signaling studies, lentiviral packaging, and CRISPR screens. Its high transfectability makes it ideal for reporter assays probing [[mTOR]], [[Apoptosis]], and stress pathways.

## Relevance to Aging, Cancer, and Biomedicine

Although not a cancer line per se, HEK293's transformed phenotype and immortalization limit direct physiological inference. It supports characterization of longevity-associated proteins (e.g., sirtuins) and subcellular trafficking relevant to [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegeneration]] research, but findings require validation in primary cells such as [[MEF]].
