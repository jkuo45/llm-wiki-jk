# VCAM1

**VCAM1 (vascular cell adhesion molecule 1, CD106)** is a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily of cell-surface adhesion glycoproteins, expressed predominantly on activated [[Endothelial Cells|endothelium]]. It mediates the firm adhesion and transmigration of leukocytes across the vessel wall and is a component of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] associated with pro-fibrotic and pro-inflammatory tissue remodeling.

## Structure & Function

- A type-I transmembrane glycoprotein with 6–7 extracellular immunoglobulin-like domains.
- Binds the integrin **VLA-4 (α4β1)** on monocytes, lymphocytes, and eosinophils, mediating leukocyte rolling, firm adhesion, and diapedesis.
- Both a membrane-bound adhesion receptor and, following proteolytic shedding, a soluble circulating form (sVCAM-1) used as an inflammation biomarker.

## Mechanism of Action & Regulation

- Transcriptionally induced by [[NF-κB]] downstream of pro-inflammatory cytokines including [[TNFα]], [[IL-1α]], and [[IL-1β]] — the same axis that drives the pro-inflammatory SASP.
- VCAM1–VLA-4 engagement activates endothelial [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], p38 MAPK, and Rac1 signaling that loosens junctions to permit leukocyte transmigration.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- VCAM1 is upregulated in senescent endothelial cells and is part of the SASP module that propagates cell death, excessive matrix production, and [[Fibrosis|fibrosis]] in normal tissue.
- By recruiting monocytes/macrophages, VCAM1 amplifies local inflammation and contributes to [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]] and vascular ageing.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- Elevated soluble VCAM-1 correlates with [[Atherosclerosis]], [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular disease]], and biological age; it is a candidate circulating SASP/inflammaging biomarker.
- VCAM1-mediated leukocyte recruitment contributes to atherosclerotic plaque formation and chronic inflammatory disease.
