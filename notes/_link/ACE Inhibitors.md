# ACE Inhibitors

[[ACE Inhibitors]] (ACEIs) are a class of medications that block angiotensin-converting enzyme, reducing angiotensin II formation and lowering blood pressure. They are first-line treatments for hypertension, heart failure, and diabetic nephropathy.

## Anti-Aging Relevance

In the longevity literature, ACE inhibitors are highlighted as conventional drugs that extend lifespan in model organisms (e.g., hypertensive and normotensive rats) and prevent or treat more than one age-related disease, qualifying them as "somewhat anti-aging" agents ([Blagosklonny, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6814615/)). They are proposed as components of a combined anti-aging formulation alongside [[Rapamycin]], [[Metformin]], [[Aspirin]], angiotensin receptor blockers, and [[PDE5 Inhibitors]], where agents may complement each other and mitigate respective side effects.

> [!tip] Combination framing
> Because aging is the sum of many age-related diseases, drugs that address multiple conditions (ACE inhibitors, metformin, aspirin, PDE5 inhibitors) may extend life when combined with mTOR inhibition.
