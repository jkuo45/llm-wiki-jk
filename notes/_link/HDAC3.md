# HDAC3

HDAC3 is a class I histone deacetylase (along with HDAC1/2/8) that removes acetyl groups from histone and non-histone substrates, generally repressing transcription. In senescence, HDAC3 forms a functional complex with the catalytically inactive reader [[HDAC4]], which is recruited to [[H3K27ac]]-marked senescence-activated enhancers; together they buffer the [[AP-1]]-driven program that drives [[SASP]] expression.

## Overview

HDAC3 is a ubiquitously expressed NAD+-independent deacetylase requiring corepressor complexes (NCoR/SMRT) for full activity. It deacetylates H3K9ac, H3K14ac, and H4K5ac among other marks, compacting chromatin and silencing target genes. HDAC3 is essential for proliferation, metabolism, and differentiation across tissues.

## Structure & Function

The catalytic domain of HDAC3 harbors a conserved active-site pocket targeted by hydroxamate and benzamide HDAC inhibitors. Unlike class IIa HDACs, HDAC3 is catalytically active and acts in opposition to histone acetyltransferases such as p300. Its recruitment is dictated by scaffolding corepressors rather than by direct DNA binding, allowing context-dependent repression.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review positions HDAC3 downstream of [[HDAC4]] in the H3K27ac axis. HDAC4, acting as an H3K27ac reader, is recruited to senescence-activated super-enhancers where it interacts with HDAC3 to oversee chromatin acetylation status and buffer the AP-1 senescence program (ref. 60). Because HDAC4 is degraded upon senescence entry, this HDAC4–HDAC3 buffering is relieved, permitting enhancer activation and SASP transcription driven by [[BRD4]], [[CEBPα]], and NF-κB at H3K27ac-marked loci. HDAC3 thus contributes to the epigenetic gating of the SASP even though it is not the directly degraded sensor.

## Mechanistic Details

- HDAC3 supplies the catalytic deacetylation activity that complements HDAC4's reader function at SASP enhancers.
- The HDAC4–HDAC3 complex integrates acetyl-lysine sensing with active repression of AP-1-opened enhancers.
- Loss of HDAC4 (and consequent loss of buffering) releases HDAC3-mediated restraint, derepressing SASP genes.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Class I HDAC inhibitors (e.g., entinostat, vorinostat, romidepsin) target HDAC3 among other isoforms and are in oncology trials. Given HDAC3's role in restraining SASP, selective modulation of the HDAC4–HDAC3 axis could inform [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] strategies; however, pan-HDAC inhibition carries broad transcriptional and tolerability liabilities.
