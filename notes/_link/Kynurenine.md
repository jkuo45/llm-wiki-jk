# Kynurenine

**Kynurenine** (Kyn) is the central metabolite of the [[Kynurenine Pathway]] (KP), formed by deformylation of [[N-Formylkynurenine]] (the product of [[IDO1]] or [[TDO]] activity on [[Tryptophan]]).

## Biological Functions

Kynurenine is a multifunctional metabolite with key roles in immune regulation and neurobiology:

1. **Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor activation:** Kynurenine is an endogenous ligand of the [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor]] (AhR). AhR activation drives [[Regulatory T cell|Treg]] differentiation and suppresses pro-inflammatory Th17 and effector T-cell responses, creating an immunosuppressive microenvironment.

2. **Precursor for neuroactive metabolites:** Kynurenine is further metabolized through three branch pathways:
   - Kynurenine aminotransferase (KAT) → [[Kynurenic Acid]] (neuroprotective NMDA antagonist)
   - Kynurenine 3-monooxygenase (KMO) → [[3-Hydroxykynurenine]] → [[Quinolinic Acid]] (neurotoxic NMDA agonist)
   - Kynureninase → anthranilic acid (minor pathway)

3. **Biomarker of IDO1 activity:** The Kyn/Trp ratio (kynurenine-to-tryptophan ratio) is used clinically as a surrogate for [[IDO1]] enzymatic activity and is elevated in chronic [[Inflammation]], [[Cancer]], and [[Insulin Resistance]].

## Clinical Relevance

Elevated kynurenine is a hallmark of the [[Tumor Microenvironment]], where it suppresses antitumor immunity via [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor|AhR]]-mediated [[Regulatory T cell|Treg]] induction. KP dysregulation producing excessive kynurenine is implicated in [[Depression]] (reducing [[Serotonin]] availability) and [[Neurodegeneration]].
