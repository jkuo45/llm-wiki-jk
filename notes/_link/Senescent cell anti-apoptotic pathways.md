# Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways

**Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs)** are a network of pro-survival mechanisms that allow [[Senescent Cells]] to persist and resist [[Apoptosis]] despite high levels of cellular stress and damage.

## Key Components
- **[[Bcl-2]] Family Proteins**: Upregulation of anti-apoptotic members like BCL-2, BCL-XL, and MCL-1.
- **Survival Signaling**: Activation of pathways such as [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], p53 modifications, and [[Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPR).
- **Senescence-Associated Mitochondrial Dysfunction (SAMD)**: Metabolic adaptations that favor survival over death.

## Therapeutic Targeting
SCAPs are the primary targets of [[Senolytics]], drugs that selectively induce [[Apoptosis]] in [[Senescent Cells]] by inhibiting these pro-survival pathways. Examples include:
- [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]] (D+Q): Targets multiple SCAPs.
- [[Navitoclax]]: A BCL-2/BCL-XL inhibitor.
