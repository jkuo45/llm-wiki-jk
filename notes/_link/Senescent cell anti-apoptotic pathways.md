# Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways

**Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs)** are a network of pro-survival mechanisms that allow [[Senescent Cells]] to persist and resist [[Apoptosis]] despite high levels of cellular stress, [[DNA Damage]], and mitochondrial leak (e.g., [[Minority MOMP]]). Senescent cells are "primed to die" yet survive because SCAPs raise the apoptotic threshold—a vulnerability that [[Senolytics]] exploit by tipping the cell back toward death.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **BCL-2 family upregulation**: Senescent cells elevate anti-apoptotic members—BCL-2, BCL-XL, MCL-1, BCL-W—that sequester pro-apoptotic [[BAX]]/[[BAK]] and prevent [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]] (Zhu et al., Nat Med 2015; PMID 25594185).
- **Survival kinase signaling**: [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] and related pro-growth cascades promote survival and metabolic fitness in the arrested state.
- **p53 modulation**: Senescence-associated p53 activity is rewired—often blunted for death execution while retained for arrest—so the cell stays arrested but not eliminated.
- **Unfolded Protein Response (UPR)**: The [[Unfolded Protein Response|UPR]] ([[PERK]], [[ATF6]], [[IRE1]]) and [[ER Stress]] buffering support proteostasis under chronic SASP burdens.
- **Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction (SAMD)**: Metabolic adaptations favoring survival over death.

## Role in Senescence

- SCAPs are the reason senescent cells accumulate rather than self-eliminate, underpinning [[Inflammaging]] and tissue dysfunction.
- They convert transient stress responses into chronic, pro-inflammatory cellular states.
- Because SCAP composition differs between cell types, senolytic selectivity arises from targeting the dominant SCAP in a given tissue.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- SCAPs are the direct targets of [[Senolytics]]: BCL-2/BCL-XL inhibitors ([[Navitoclax]], ABT-737, [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]]) disable survival and trigger apoptosis.
- SCAP modulation is being leveraged in clinical trials for diabetic kidney disease, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and osteoarthritis.
- Understanding SCAP heterogeneity guides next-generation, tissue-specific senolytics.
