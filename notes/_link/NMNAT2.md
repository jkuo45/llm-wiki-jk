# NMNAT2

**NMNAT2** (Nicotinamide Mononucleotide Adenylyl Transferase 2) is a neuronal enzyme that synthesizes [[NAD+]] from [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide|NMN]]. It is the central brake on [[SARM1]]-mediated axonal degeneration.

## Mechanism

NMNAT2 is continuously delivered from the neuronal soma to the axon, where it converts NMN → NAD⁺, maintaining axonal NAD⁺ at ~400 μM and keeping [[SARM1]] in its autoinhibited state. NMNAT2 has a short half-life (~30 min) and is degraded by the proteasome upon axonal injury (transection severs soma-axon transport). Its loss causes NMN accumulation; the rising NMN/NAD⁺ ratio is the primary metabolic trigger that relieves SARM1 autoinhibition and initiates the NAD⁺ catastrophe of [[Wallerian Degeneration]].

## Significance

- NMNAT2 is the most injury-sensitive node in the axonal NAD⁺ economy — its decline is both necessary and sufficient to license SARM1 activation.
- Therapeutic strategies that preserve NMNAT2 (or bypass it with NAD⁺ precursors) protect axons.
- Part of the broader NAD⁺ consumer network alongside [[CD38]], [[PARP1]], and sirtuins.
