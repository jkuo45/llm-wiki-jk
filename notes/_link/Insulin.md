# Insulin

## Overview
[[Insulin]] is a peptide hormone secreted by pancreatic β-cells that promotes glucose disposal, lipogenesis, and protein synthesis. It is the key anabolic signal integrating nutrient availability with growth, and its dysregulation underlies [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] and [[Metabolic Syndrome]].

## Secretion & Signaling
Glucose-stimulated [[Insulin Secretion]] activates the [[Insulin Signaling]] cascade: insulin receptor tyrosine kinase → [[IRS1]] → [[PI3K]] → [[AKT]], which recruits [[GLUT4]] to the membrane for glucose uptake. This pathway converges on [[mTORC1]] and opposes [[AMPK]], linking nutrient sensing to growth and [[Autophagy]].

## Mechanism of Action
Insulin suppresses hepatic gluconeogenesis, enhances glycogen and lipid storage, and promotes [[IGF-1]]-like anabolic responses. Chronic hyperinsulinemia desensitizes receptors, fostering [[Insulin]] resistance and [[Oxidative Stress]] through [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] overproduction in [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].

## Redox, Sirtuins & Longevity
Insulin signaling mirrors the longevity-regulating nutrient-sensing network shared with [[IGF-1]] and [[mTOR]]. Caloric restriction and [[Sirtuins]] activation improve insulin sensitivity, whereas [[Rapamycin]] and [[Metformin]] blunt mTOR/insulin-axis overactivation to extend [[Healthspan]]. Balanced [[Insulin]] tone—akin to [[Mitohormesis]]—is protective, while excess drives inflammaging and senescence. NAD+ precursors such as [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] further support metabolic resilience.
