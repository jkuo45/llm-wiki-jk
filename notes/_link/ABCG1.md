# ABCG1

**Overview:** ABCG1 (ATP-binding cassette subfamily G member 1) is a half-transporter of the ATP-binding cassette (ABC) superfamily that uses the energy of ATP hydrolysis to move sterols and phospholipids across the plasma membrane. It functions as a homodimer and is a central effector of cellular cholesterol efflux, working alongside [[ABCA1]] in the pathway of reverse cholesterol transport.

**Structure and mechanism:** Each ABCG1 monomer contains a cytoplasmic nucleotide-binding domain and a transmembrane domain; two monomers assemble into the functional transporter. ATP binding and hydrolysis drive conformational cycling that translocates cholesterol and oxysterols from the inner to the outer leaflet, making them available for efflux to mature [[HDL]] particles (in contrast to ABCA1, which lipidates lipid-poor apolipoprotein A-I).

**Regulation:** ABCG1 transcription is strongly induced by the liver X receptors (LXRα/β) in response to oxysterol accumulation, coupling intracellular sterol load to efflux capacity. Its expression is prominent in [[Macrophages]], endothelium, and other tissues where lipid handling is critical.

**Physiological and clinical relevance:** By promoting cholesterol export from lipid-laden macrophage foam cells, ABCG1 protects against lipid accumulation in the arterial wall and modulates the progression of [[Atherosclerosis]]. Dysregulated ABCG1 activity is linked to metabolic disease, impaired reverse cholesterol transport, and altered inflammatory responses in vascular tissue. It is studied as a node in HDL-based and LXR-targeting therapeutic strategies.
