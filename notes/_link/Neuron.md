## Overview

A [[Neuron]] is the principal signaling cell of the nervous system, structurally adapted for rapid communication. It comprises a cell body (soma), dendrites that receive inputs, and a single axon that transmits outputs to target cells via synapses.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

Neurons process information through:

- **Membrane excitability** — graded and action potentials driven by voltage-gated ion channels.
- **Synaptic transmission** — release of neurotransmitters at synapses, detected by postsynaptic receptors.
- **Plasticity** — activity-dependent strengthening/weakening of synapses underlies learning and memory.

Neurons rely heavily on mitochondrial ATP and are sensitive to oxidative and metabolic stress. Structural markers such as [[MAP2]] (dendritic), [[Synaptophysin]] (synaptic vesicles), and neurofilaments define neuronal compartments and connectivity.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

Neuronal loss or dysfunction underlies neurodegeneration, stroke, and neuropsychiatric disease. Neurons are vulnerable to senescent and inflammatory microenvironments, including reactive [[Astrocytes]] and [[Microglia]]. In the wiki, neuronal health is a central endpoint for sirtuin- and adrenochrome-related interventions, with markers (MAP2, synaptophysin) used to assess neurotoxicity and protection.
