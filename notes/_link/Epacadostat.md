# Epacadostat

**Epacadostat** (INCB024360) is a selective, orally bioavailable small-molecule inhibitor of [[IDO1]], developed by Incyte Corporation for [[Cancer]] immunotherapy. It was the most advanced IDO1 inhibitor to enter clinical trials.

## Mechanism

Epacadostat competitively inhibits the catalytic activity of [[IDO1]] by binding to the heme iron center in the active site. It is a potent inhibitor (IC₅₀ ~10 nM) and is highly selective for IDO1 over [[TDO]] and [[IDO2]]. By blocking [[Tryptophan]] catabolism and [[Kynurenine]] production, it relieves IDO1-mediated immunosuppression in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]].

## Clinical Development

Epacadostat was evaluated in combination with [[Checkpoint Inhibitor|checkpoint inhibitors]] (e.g., pembrolizumab, nivolumab) in multiple Phase I-III trials for advanced malignancies including melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and bladder cancer. The Phase III ECHO-301/KEYNOTE-252 trial in melanoma did not meet its primary endpoint (progression-free survival), leading to a reassessment of IDO1 as a single target.

## Limitations

- Compensatory upregulation of [[TDO]] and [[IDO2]] may drive resistance
- IDO1 can mediate non-enzymatic (scaffolding) signaling that is not blocked by catalytic inhibition
- Patient selection and biomarker development remain challenges
