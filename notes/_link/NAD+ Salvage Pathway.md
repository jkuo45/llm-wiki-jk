# NAD+ Salvage Pathway

The **NAD+ salvage pathway** is the primary biosynthetic route for maintaining cellular [[NAD+]] levels in most mammalian tissues. It recycles nicotinamide (NAM)—the product of NAD+-consuming enzymes (Sirtuins, PARPs, CD38)—back into NAD+ through a two-step enzymatic process. The rate-limiting enzyme is [[NAMPT]] (nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase), which converts nicotinamide to nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN). NMN is then converted to NAD+ by NMNAT (nicotinamide mononucleotide adenylyltransferase). The salvage pathway is the dominant source of cellular NAD+ in most cell types and is essential for maintaining sirtuin activity, DNA repair, and metabolic homeostasis.

## Pathway Components

### Step 1: NAMPT (Rate-Limiting)
**Nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NAMPT)** catalyses the condensation of nicotinamide with 5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate (PRPP) to form nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN):

```
Nicotinamide + PRPP → NMN + PPi
```

- **Cytoplasmic NAMPT (cNAMPT):** The extracellular/secreted form; circulates as a cytokine-like molecule (visfatin) and may function in intercellular NAD+ transport.
- **Nuclear/cytoplasmic NAMPT:** The intracellular form that maintains local NAD+ pools for sirtuin and PARP activity.

> [!important] Rate-Limiting Enzyme
> NAMPT is the rate-limiting step of the salvage pathway. Its expression and activity directly determine cellular NAD+ levels. NAMPT is transcriptionally regulated by metabolic signals (AMPK, SIRT1, HIF-1α) and declines with age, contributing to the age-related NAD+ decline.

### Step 2: NMNAT
**Nicotinamide mononucleotide adenylyltransferase (NMNAT)** converts NMN to NAD+:

```
NMN + ATP → NAD+ + PPi
```

Three NMNAT isoforms exist:
- **NMNAT1 (nuclear):** Ubiquitously expressed; maintains nuclear NAD+ for DNA repair (PARP) and sirtuin activity
- **NMNAT2 (cytoplasmic/neuronal):** Highly expressed in neurons; critical for neuronal survival; lost early in Alzheimer's disease
- **NMNAT3 (mitochondrial):** Maintains mitochondrial NAD+ pool

## Regulation

### Transcriptional Regulation
- **AMPK:** Activates NAMPT transcription via PGC-1α and FOXO signalling, linking energy stress to NAD+ biosynthesis
- **SIRT1:** Deacetylates and activates PGC-1α, which drives NAMPT transcription—a positive feedback loop (SIRT1 → NAMPT → NAD+ → SIRT1)
- **HIF-1α:** Upregulates NAMPT under hypoxic conditions
- **NF-κB:** Inflammatory signals upregulate NAMPT, linking inflammation to NAD+ metabolism

### Post-Translational Regulation
- **Phosphorylation:** AMPK phosphorylates and activates NAMPT
- **Acetylation:** SIRT1 deacetylates NAMPT, enhancing its activity
- **Ubiquitination:** NAMPT is subject to proteasomal degradation

### Age-Related Decline
NAMPT expression and activity decline with age in multiple tissues, contributing to the age-related decline in NAD+ levels. This decline reduces sirtuin activity, impairs DNA repair, and promotes metabolic dysfunction. NAD+ decline is a hallmark of ageing and is implicated in neurodegeneration, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular disease.

## Tissue Distribution

- **Liver:** High NAMPT expression; the liver is a major site of NAD+ biosynthesis
- **Skeletal muscle:** NAMPT is upregulated by exercise and energy stress
- **Brain:** NMNAT2 is essential for neuronal survival; NAMPT decline contributes to neurodegeneration
- **Adipose tissue:** NAMPT/visfatin was originally identified as an adipokine
- **Immune cells:** NAMPT is upregulated in activated lymphocytes and macrophages

## Physiological Roles

### Sirtuin Activation
NAD+ generated by the salvage pathway is the essential co-substrate for Sirtuins ([[SIRT1]]–[[SIRT7]]). Sirtuins deacetylate proteins and ADP-ribosylate targets using NAD+, linking metabolic state to epigenetic regulation, stress resistance, and longevity.

### DNA Repair
PARPs (PARP1, PARP2) consume NAD+ during DNA damage repair. The salvage pathway replenishes NAD+ to sustain DNA repair capacity. Chronic PARP activation (e.g., in ageing or DNA damage) can deplete NAD+ pools.

### Calcium Signalling
CD38 (a major NAD+ consumer in immune cells) generates cyclic ADP-ribose (cADPR), a calcium-mobilising second messenger. The salvage pathway maintains NAD+ for CD38-mediated calcium signalling.

### Circadian Rhythm
NAMPT expression oscillates with circadian rhythm, creating daily oscillations in NAD+ levels that feed back to regulate SIRT1 activity and circadian gene expression.

## Pathological Roles

### Ageing
The age-related decline in NAMPT and NAD+ is a hallmark of ageing. Reduced NAD+ impairs sirtuin activity, DNA repair, mitochondrial function, and metabolic homeostasis. NAD+ repletion (via NMN, NR, or NAMPT activators) extends lifespan and healthspan in model organisms.

### Neurodegeneration
NMNAT2 is lost early in Alzheimer's disease. NAD+ decline impairs neuronal survival and promotes neuroinflammation. NAD+ repletion strategies are being explored for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and Huntington's diseases.

### Metabolic Disease
NAMPT/NAD+ decline in metabolic tissues contributes to insulin resistance, mitochondrial dysfunction, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). NAMPT overexpression improves metabolic health in obese mice.

### Inflammation
NAMPT is upregulated in activated macrophages and contributes to the pro-inflammatory NAD+ metabolism of immune cells. Extracellular NAMPT (visfatin) has cytokine-like pro-inflammatory activity.

### Cancer
NAMPT is overexpressed in many cancers, supporting the high metabolic and DNA repair demands of proliferating cells. NAMPT inhibitors (e.g., FK866/CHS-828) are being explored as anti-cancer agents.

## Clinical Relevance

- **NAD+ precursors:** Nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) are dietary supplements that bypass NAMPT to directly boost NAD+ levels. Clinical trials are evaluating their efficacy for ageing, metabolic disease, and neurodegeneration.
- **NAMPT inhibitors:** FK866 (APO866) and CHS-828 are NAMPT inhibitors in clinical trials for cancer, leveraging NAD+ depletion to selectively kill tumour cells.
- **Exercise:** Exercise upregulates NAMPT in skeletal muscle, increasing NAD+ biosynthesis and mitochondrial biogenesis.
- **Circadian disruption:** Shift work and jet lag disrupt NAMPT oscillations, contributing to metabolic dysfunction.
