# Amyloid Precursor Protein

[[Amyloid Precursor Protein]] (APP) is a type I transmembrane glycoprotein ubiquitously expressed in neural and non-neural tissues. Its sequential proteolytic cleavage by β-secretase and γ-secretase produces amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides, which are the primary components of amyloid plaques in [[Alzheimer's Disease]].

## Structure

APP is a single-pass transmembrane protein with:
- Large extracellular N-terminal domain with multiple functional domains
- Transmembrane domain containing the Aβ sequence
- Short cytoplasmic C-terminal tail involved in signaling

The most common isoforms are APP695 (neuronal), APP751, and APP770.

## Proteolytic Processing

> [!warning] Amyloidogenic vs. Non-Amyloidogenic Pathways
> APP processing occurs via two pathways:
> 1. **Non-amyloidogenic**: α-secretase cleaves within the Aβ domain, preventing Aβ formation
> 2. **Amyloidogenic**: β-secretase (BACE1) followed by γ-secretase produces Aβ peptides (Aβ40 and Aβ42)
>
> Familial mutations in APP or presenilin genes (PS1/PS2, components of γ-secretase) increase Aβ42/Aβ40 ratio, promoting amyloid plaque formation.

## Biological Functions

APP has several physiological roles:
- Cell adhesion and migration
- Synaptogenesis and synaptic plasticity
- Neurotrophic signaling (via secreted APP fragments)
- Copper binding and redox regulation
- Antimicrobial activity (Aβ peptide)

## Connection to Humanin

> [!info] Shared Mitochondrial Genetic Origin
> [[Humanin]] was originally discovered through functional screening for clones protecting neuronal cells from death induced by mutant [[APP]] associated with familial [[Alzheimer's Disease]]. Both APP and humanin are linked to neurodegeneration through distinct mechanisms: APP mutations produce toxic Aβ peptides, while humanin provides cytoprotection against Aβ toxicity through [[FPRL1]] receptor binding and intracellular [[Bax]]/[[Bid]] inhibition.

## Disease Relevance

- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]: Mutations in APP cause early-onset familial AD; Aβ accumulation is a hallmark of AD pathology
- Cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA): Aβ deposition in cerebral blood vessels
- Down syndrome: Trisomy 21 leads to APP overexpression and early-onset AD
- Type 2 diabetes: Aβ aggregation in pancreatic islets

## Connections

- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]: APP mutations and Aβ accumulation are central to AD pathogenesis.
- [[Humanin]]: Humanin was discovered through functional screening against APP toxicity.
- [[FPRL1]]: Humanin competes with Aβ42 for FPRL1 binding, reducing cytotoxicity.
- [[Bax]]: Aβ-induced apoptosis involves Bax activation; humanin inhibits this pathway.
- [[Apoptosis]]: Aβ toxicity triggers apoptotic cell death pathways.
- [[Oxidative Stress]]: Aβ induces mitochondrial dysfunction and ROS production.
- [[Mitochondria]]: APP mutations impair mitochondrial function; humanin provides mitochondrial protection.
