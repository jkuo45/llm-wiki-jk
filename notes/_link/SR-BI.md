# SR-BI

**Scavenger receptor class B type I (SR-BI)** is a membrane receptor that mediates bidirectional [[Cholesterol]] flux between cells and [[HDL]]. It is one of the three known pathways for free cholesterol efflux from [[Macrophages]].

## Mechanism

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> SR-BI mediates free cholesterol flux in a bidirectional manner. Like the aqueous diffusion mechanism, the net movement of free cholesterol via SR-BI depends on the direction of the cholesterol gradient. SR-BI is one of three pathways for cholesterol efflux from macrophages.

- SR-BI facilitates bidirectional cholesterol transport across the plasma membrane
- Net cholesterol movement depends on the concentration gradient between the cell and the acceptor
- Unlike ABCA1, SR-BI does not preferentially lipidate lipid-poor apoA-1
- SR-BI interacts with mature [[HDL]] particles

## Regulation

- Oxidative stress (Fe/ascorbate) has no effect on SR-BI expression (unlike ABCA1, LXR, and PPAR)
- SR-BI expression is prominent in tissues with high cholesterol flux

## Clinical Relevance

- SR-BI plays a role in [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]] alongside ABCA1 and ABCG1
- SR-BI is involved in hepatic HDL cholesterol uptake
- SR-BI modulation affects [[Atherosclerosis]] progression
