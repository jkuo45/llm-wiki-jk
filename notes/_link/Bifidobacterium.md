# Bifidobacterium

**Overview:** *Bifidobacterium* is a genus of Gram-positive, non-motile, often Y- or V-shaped branched anaerobic bacteria belonging to the phylum [[Actinobacteria]]. They are among the first colonizers of the infant gut and remain prevalent in the adult [[Gut Microbiome]], where they are widely used as [[Probiotics]].

**Metabolism:** Bifidobacteria uniquely ferment carbohydrates via the "bifid shunt" (fructose-6-phosphate phosphoketolase pathway), yielding [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]]—chiefly acetate and lactate—without gas production. This acidifies the lumen, inhibits pathogens, and supplies energy to colonocytes. They metabolize human milk oligosaccharides in infants and dietary fiber in adults.

**Physiological Function:** Beyond nutrient processing, Bifidobacterium strengthens mucosal barrier integrity, competes with pathogens for ecological niches, and educates the developing immune system, promoting regulatory T-cell tolerance and damping inappropriate inflammation. They synthesize B-group vitamins and contribute to bile-acid and amino-acid metabolism.

**Aging and Longevity:** Bifidobacterial abundance declines with age and is consistently enriched in [[Centenarians]] compared with younger adults, supporting a role in [[Longevity]] and healthy aging. Interventions that restore bifidobacterial levels (prebiotics, [[Probiotics]], fiber, exercise) are associated with improved metabolic and immune markers. In the gut–muscle axis, Bifidobacterium abundance tracks favorably with muscle health and may be modulated by compounds such as [[Urolithin A]].

**Pathology:** Generally beneficial, but in rare cases (e.g., in immunocompromised or premature infants) translocation can cause bacteremia ("bifidobacterial sepsis"), which is usually responsive to antibiotics.
