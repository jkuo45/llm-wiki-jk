# Malate Dehydrogenase

**Malate dehydrogenase (MDH)** catalyzes the reversible, NAD+-dependent interconversion of [[Oxaloacetate]] and [[Malate]] (oxaloacetate + NADH + H+ ⇌ malate + NAD+). Two major isoforms exist: cytosolic [[MDH1]] (malate dehydrogenase 1) and mitochondrial [[MDH2]] (malate dehydrogenase 2). Both couple redox state to the [[Citric Acid Cycle|citric acid cycle]] and the [[Malate-Aspartate Shuttle|malate-aspartate shuttle]].

## Structure & Isoforms

- **MDH1 (cytosolic)**: Reduces oxaloacetate to malate using cytosolic NADH, feeding the malate-aspartate shuttle to transfer reducing equivalents into mitochondria.
- **MDH2 (mitochondrial)**: Oxidizes malate back to oxaloacetate, regenerating NADH in the matrix for [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|OXPHOS]].

## Role in Senescence

- In senescent cells, the activity and protein level of cytosolic [[MDH1]] are reduced, contributing to decreased [[NAD+]] levels.
- **Cytosolic NAD+ is senescence-critical**: Knockdown of MDH1 (which lowers the *cytosolic* NAD+/NADH ratio) induces senescence. Counter-intuitively, knockdown of mitochondrial [[MDH2]] — which affects mitochondrial but not cytosolic NAD+ — does *not* affect senescence in young human dermal fibroblasts. This indicates that cytosolic, not mitochondrial, NAD+ pools govern senescence induction.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: States MDH1 activity/protein falls in senescence (lowering NAD+), and that MDH1 knockdown induces senescence whereas mitochondrial MDH2 knockdown does not — implicating cytosolic NAD+ as the senescence-relevant pool.
