# Mucositis

[[Mucositis]] is inflammation and ulceration of the mucous membranes lining the gastrointestinal tract, including the oral cavity. In the context of [[mTOR]] inhibitor therapy it is the broader entity encompassing [[Stomatitis]] (oral mucositis) and gastrointestinal ulceration.

## Rapamycin-Associated Mucositis

At high or chronic doses, [[Rapamycin]] and [[Rapalog|rapalogs]] cause stomatitis and mucositis—ulceration of the oral and digestive tract mucosa ([Peterson et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27334013/)). In off-label longevity surveys, mouth ulcers were the only side effect significantly more common in rapamycin users, although adverse events overall were mild/moderate and reversible on discontinuation ([Kaeberlein et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37191826/); [Roark & Iffland, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12226543/)).

> [!warning] Scheduling mitigation
> For anti-aging use, rapamycin may be given intermittently (e.g., weekly) or at low daily doses and stopped if mucositis or other unpleasant effects occur.
