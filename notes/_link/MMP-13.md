# MMP-13

**MMP-13 (matrix metalloproteinase 13, collagenase-3)** is a zinc-dependent endopeptidase of the [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|matrix metalloproteinase]] family with a strong preference for **type II collagen**, the principal structural collagen of cartilage. It is a component of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] linked to age-related extracellular-matrix degradation.

## Structure & Function

- Secreted as a pro-enzyme (pro-MMP-13) and activated extracellularly by other proteases (e.g., [[MMP-2]], [[MMP-3]], MT1-MMP) and [[Plasmin|plasmin]].
- Cleaves type II collagen (and types I, III), as well as aggrecan, fibronectin, and other ECM components, making it a master remodeler of cartilage and connective tissue.

## Mechanism of Action & Regulation

- Transcriptionally induced by pro-inflammatory cytokines ([[IL-1β]], [[TNFα]], [[IL-6]]) via [[NF-κB]], [[AP-1]], and p38 [[MAPK]]/ERK signaling — overlapping with pro-inflammatory SASP regulation.
- Activity is restrained by tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases ([[TIMP1]], [[TIMP2]]).

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- MMP-13 is upregulated in senescent chondrocytes and fibroblasts and is part of the SASP protease module that drives matrix degradation and tissue dysfunction with ageing.
- Its collagenolytic activity contributes to the ageing-related pathology cluster of the SASP, alongside [[IL-1α]], [[IL-1β]], [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], [[CXCL1]], [[CCL2]], and [[TNFα|TNF]].

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- MMP-13 is a central driver of cartilage destruction in [[Osteoarthritis]] and is a major therapeutic target for disease-modifying osteoarthritis drugs.
- It also participates in tumor invasion, bone remodeling, and chronic wound pathology.
