## Overview

[[Synaptophysin]] (SYP) is a 38 kDa transmembrane glycoprotein of synaptic vesicles, among the most abundant proteins of the presynaptic terminal. It is a widely used immunohistochemical marker of synapses and [[Neuron]]al connectivity.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

Synaptophysin forms a channel-like complex with other vesicular proteins and participates in:

- Synaptic vesicle biogenesis, trafficking, and exocytosis.
- Membrane fusion events during neurotransmitter release.

Although its exact pore function remains debated, it is a reliable quantitative readout of synaptic density. It colocalizes with other synaptic proteins (e.g., synapsin, [[PSD-95]]) and is co-expressed with dendritic markers such as [[MAP2]] to delineate neuronal circuitry.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

Reduced synaptophysin levels indicate synaptic loss, a hallmark of aging and neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's). It is used to quantify neurotoxicity versus synaptic protection. In the wiki, synaptophysin serves as a synaptic integrity endpoint for evaluating adrenochrome/sirtuin neuroprotective strategies.
