# Glutathione Peroxidase
Glutathione peroxidase (GPx) is an enzyme family with peroxidase activity whose main biological role is to protect the organism from oxidative damage.
## Overview
GPx reduces lipid hydroperoxides to their corresponding alcohols and reduces free [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] to water. Its activity can be reduced by [[Glycation]] modifications, leading to increased cellular [[Oxidative Stress]].
## Definition
Glutathione peroxidase (GPx) is the general name of an enzyme family with peroxidase activity whose main biological role is to protect the organism from oxidative damage.
## Function
It reduces free [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] to water and organic hydroperoxides to their corresponding alcohols.
## Isozyme Family and Tissue Distribution
The mammalian GPx family comprises eight members (GPx1–8), distinguished by their tissue distribution, substrate specificity, and active-site architecture. **GPx1** (cytosolic GPx) is ubiquitously expressed and is the most abundant selenoprotein in mammals, using reduced [[Glutathione]] (GSH) as a reducing cofactor to detoxify $H_2O_2$ and small organic hydroperoxides. **GPx2** (gastrointestinal GPx) is expressed in the intestinal epithelium and liver, where it protects against dietary hydroperoxides and luminal oxidative stress. **GPx3** (plasma GPx) is a secreted glycoprotein synthesized primarily in the kidney and found in plasma, where it reduces lipid hydroperoxides associated with lipoproteins. **GPx4** (phospholipid hydroperoxide GPx, PHGPx) is unique in its ability to directly reduce complex lipid hydroperoxides (e.g., phosphatidylcholine hydroperoxides and cholesterol hydroperoxides) embedded in [[Cell Membranes]] and lipoproteins, without prior phospholipase A₂-mediated hydrolysis. GPx4 is also essential for [[Spermatogenesis]] — it constitutes the major structural protein of the mitochondrial capsule in mature spermatozoa, and its absence causes embryonic lethality in mice.
## Selenocysteine and Catalytic Mechanism
All enzymatically active GPx isoforms (GPx1–4 and GPx6) contain a catalytic **selenocysteine** (Sec, U) residue encoded by a UGA codon — normally a stop codon — whose recoding requires a dedicated selenocysteine insertion sequence (SECIS) element in the 3′ untranslated region of the mRNA. Selenium incorporation is rate-limited by dietary selenium availability; selenium deficiency reduces GPx activity in most tissues, impairing antioxidant defenses and linking selenium intake to [[Inflammation]] and [[Cancer]] risk.
The catalytic cycle proceeds in three steps. In the **oxidation** step, the selenolate anion (Sec⁻) of the active site reduces $H_2O_2$ (or ROOH) to water (or ROH), itself being oxidized to selenenic acid (Sec-SeOH). In the **regeneration** step, one molecule of GSH reacts with Sec-SeOH to form a selenenylsulfide adduct (Sec-Se-SG) with mixed disulfide between the enzyme and glutathione. In the **resolution** step, a second GSH molecule attacks the selenenylsulfide, regenerating the active selenolate and releasing oxidized glutathione (GSSG). The overall stoichiometry is: $2GSH + H_2O_2 \rightarrow GSSG + 2H_2O$ (or $2GSH + ROOH \rightarrow GSSG + ROH + H_2O$). The regeneration of GSH from GSSG is catalyzed by [[Glutathione Reductase]] (GR) at the expense of NADPH, coupling GPx activity to the pentose phosphate pathway.
## Substrate Specificity and Role in Lipid Peroxidation
GPx4's ability to directly reduce phospholipid, cholesterol, and cardiolipin hydroperoxides makes it a critical shield against [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and ferroptotic cell death. **[[Ferroptosis]]** — a non-apoptotic form of regulated cell death driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation — is negatively regulated by GPx4. When GPx4 is inhibited (by genetic deletion, selenium deficiency, or pharmacological agents such as RSL3), cellular inability to detoxify lipid hydroperoxides culminates in catastrophic membrane damage and cell death. The discovery of GPx4 as a central ferroptosis suppressor has stimulated therapeutic interest in inducing ferroptosis in therapy-resistant [[Cancer|cancers]] (e.g., [[Breast Cancer]], [[Renal Cell Carcinoma]]) and in protecting against ferroptotic tissue injury in [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]].
## Clinical Significance
**Keshan disease**, an endemic cardiomyopathy named for a region in northeastern China, is caused by combined selenium deficiency and coxsackievirus B infection. The cardiomyopathy arises from insufficient cardiac GPx activity, leading to unchecked $H_2O_2$-mediated damage and viral genome mutations that increase virulence. Selenium supplementation has dramatically reduced Keshan disease incidence.
GPx activity is relevant to [[Diabetes Mellitus]]: GPx1 overexpression in mice paradoxically induces insulin resistance and [[Obesity]], likely through overquenching of $H_2O_2$ that normally serves as a signaling intermediate for insulin receptor desensitization. This exemplifies the "antioxidant paradox" — excessive antioxidant activity can disrupt beneficial redox signaling. In [[Cardiovascular Disease]], GPx1 deficiency in mice accelerates [[Atherosclerosis]], while GPx1 overexpression protects against [[Myocardial infarction]] and [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]]. In [[Aging]], GPx activity declines in many tissues, reducing the capacity to detoxify the accumulating hydroperoxide burden and contributing to the age-related increase in lipid and protein oxidative damage.
