# CD4+ T Cells

**CD4+ T helper cells** are a class of T lymphocytes that express the CD4 co-receptor and serve as central coordinators of adaptive immune responses. They recognize peptide antigens presented by [[MHC type II]] molecules on antigen-presenting cells (APCs) and differentiate into functionally distinct subsets that direct specific immune effector programs through cytokine production.

## Development & Differentiation

CD4+ T cells develop in the thymus, where positive selection ensures MHC class II restriction and negative selection eliminates strongly self-reactive clones. Mature naïve CD4+ T cells exit to the periphery and, upon antigen encounter with appropriate co-stimulation, differentiate into specialized effector subsets depending on the cytokine milieu:

| Subset | Master TF | Key Cytokine | Primary Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Th1]] | T-bet | [[IL-12]], [[IFNγ]] | Intracellular pathogen defense, macrophage activation |
| [[Th2]] | GATA3 | [[IL-4]] | Helminth defense, allergic immunity, IgE production |
| Th17 | RORγt | IL-6, TGF-β, IL-23 | Mucosal defense, extracellular bacteria and fungi |
| Treg | FoxP3 | TGF-β, IL-2 | Immune suppression, tolerance maintenance |
| Tfh | Bcl-6 | IL-6, IL-21 | B cell help in germinal centers |
| Th9 | PU.1 | TGF-β, [[IL-4]] | Anti-helminth, anti-tumor immunity |
| Th22 | AHR | IL-6, TNFα | Skin immunity and tissue repair |

## Mechanism of Action

Upon activation via the TCR and CD28 co-stimulation, CD4+ T cells undergo clonal expansion and differentiate into effector cells. The differentiation pathway is determined by cytokine signals that activate specific STAT transcription factors:

- [[IL-12]] → STAT4 → [[T-bet]] → [[Th1]]
- [[IL-4]] → [[STAT6]] → GATA3 → [[Th2]]
- IL-6 → STAT3 → RORγt → Th17

Effector CD4+ T cells then orchestrate the immune response by:
- Providing **help to B cells** for antibody production and class switching.
- Activating **macrophages** through CD40L–CD40 interactions and [[IFNγ]] production.
- Recruiting and activating **CD8+ T cells** through cytokine and APC licensing.
- Regulating immune responses through production of both pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokines.

## Biological Functions

### Humoral Immunity
CD4+ T cells are essential for T-dependent B cell responses. T follicular helper (Tfh) cells in germinal centers provide critical signals for B cell affinity maturation, class switching, and long-lived plasma cell and memory B cell generation.

### Macrophage Activation
Th1 cells activate macrophages through [[IFNγ]] and CD40L, enhancing their microbicidal activity against intracellular pathogens. This classical macrophage activation pathway is critical for defense against *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* and *Leishmania*.

### Immune Regulation
Regulatory T cells (Tregs), a specialized CD4+ subset expressing FoxP3, suppress immune responses through IL-10, TGF-β, CTLA-4, and IL-2 consumption, maintaining peripheral tolerance and preventing autoimmunity.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **HIV/AIDS**: HIV specifically targets CD4+ T cells via CD4 and CCR5/CXCR4, leading to progressive immune depletion and AIDS.
- **Autoimmune Disease**: Dysregulated CD4+ T cells drive tissue damage in rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, type 1 diabetes, and [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus|systemic lupus erythematosus]].
- **Transplant Rejection**: CD4+ T cells mediate allograft rejection through direct and indirect allorecognition.
- **Cancer**: CD4+ T cells support anti-tumor CD8+ T cell responses and can have direct cytotoxic activity. Tumor-infiltrating CD4+ T cell profiles correlate with prognosis.
- **Therapeutic Targeting**: Anti-CD4 antibodies, cytokine blockade, and Treg-based therapies are under development for autoimmune and inflammatory diseases.
