# ATAC-seq

**ATAC-seq** (Assay for Transposase-Accessible Chromatin using sequencing) is a method for mapping [[Chromatin Accessibility]] genome-wide. It uses hyperactive [[Tn5 Transposase]] to simultaneously fragment and tag open chromatin regions with sequencing adapters, followed by high-throughput sequencing.

## Principle

Tn5 transposase preferentially integrates into regions of open [[Chromatin]] (nucleosome-depleted regions, [[Enhancers]], [[Promoters]], [[Transcription Factor Binding Sites]]). Tagmented DNA fragments are sequenced and aligned to the genome to identify accessible regions. [[ATAC-seq]] also reports [[Nucleosome Positioning]] from fragment length distribution and [[Transcription Factor]] footprinting from insertion patterns.

## Applications

- [[Epigenomics]] — maps regulatory chromatin landscapes
- [[Transcription Factor]] binding site discovery
- [[Single-Cell ATAC-seq]] (scATAC-seq) — chromatin accessibility at single-cell resolution
- [[Enhancer]]-[[Promoter]] interaction inference via co-accessibility
- Comparative [[Epigenetics]] across conditions, tissues, or species
