# Nuclear Envelope

## Overview

The [[Nuclear Envelope|NE]] is the double-membrane boundary separating the [[Nucleus|nucleus]] from the cytoplasm, continuous with the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]] and perforated by nuclear pore complexes. It is a critical hub for cGAS-STING regulation: cGAS localizes to the nuclear envelope via [[Barrier-to-Autointegration Factor 1|BAF1]] (barrier-to-autointegration factor 1), and [[NPC1]] (Niemann-Pick C1) at the NE modulates cGAS activity. Envelope integrity is therefore intimately linked to genomic stability and innate immune activation.

## cGAS Anchoring and STING Localization

Nuclear cGAS is tethered at the NE through BAF1, which dynamically outcompetes cGAS for DNA binding and restricts its activity ([74](#B74)). [[STING]] also localizes to the nuclear envelope — particularly the inner NE — and redistributes to the outer NE (continuous with the ER) upon HSV-1 or poly(I:C) stimulation ([81](#B81), [82](#B82)). NE-localized STING may thus represent a readily mobilizable pool for activation, and the NE's association with [[Heterochromatin|heterochromatin]] organization and RNA processing hints at additional nuclear roles for STING.

## Envelope Breakdown and Genomic Instability

Nuclear envelope breakdown or defects — as occur during mitosis or with laminopathy-like stress — expose chromatin and feed [[DNA Damage]] and [[Genomic Instability|genomic instability]], creating cytosolic DNA that activates cGAS. NPC1 deficiency further supports cGAS activation independent of canonical cytosolic DNA sensing, linking endolysosomal and NE compartmental defects to [[Neuroinflammation]]. Preserving NE integrity is thus a structural determinant of whether cGAS remains quiescent or engaged.
