# Toll-like Receptor 2 (TLR2)

Toll-like Receptor 2 (TLR2, CD282) is an innate immunity sensor that recognizes bacterial lipopeptides, peptidoglycans, and various endogenous damage-associated molecular patterns. It is upregulated in senescent cells and is essential for activating an immunity-regulating SASP during [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]], interacting with the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] pathway.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

TLR2 is a type I transmembrane receptor that heterodimerizes — most often with TLR1 or TLR6 — to recognize diacylated and triacylated bacterial lipopeptides, as well as fungal and endogenous ligands (e.g., hyaluronan fragments, HMGB1). Ligand binding recruits the adaptor [[MyD88]] (and, for some ligands, [[TRIF]]), nucleating a MyD88–IRAK–TRAF6 complex that activates [[IKK complex]] → [[NFKB|NF-κB]], MAPKs (including [[p38 MAPK]]), and IRF signaling. This drives expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, type I interferons, and co-stimulatory molecules, linking microbial and damage sensing to innate immune activation.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

TLR2 is a receptor that activates SASP and links innate immunity to SASP regulation. It is induced during senescence and is essential for the immunity-regulating arm of the SASP during [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]]; its signaling cooperates with the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]]–IRF3 axis to shape an antigen-presentation and immune-recruitment secretome. By engaging NF-κB and IRF pathways, TLR2 helps senescent cells communicate with macrophages, NK cells, and T cells — underpinning both tumor immunosurveillance (senescence as an anti-cancer barrier) and, when chronic, maladaptive inflammation. TLR2 thus determines the immunological character of the SASP in addition to its inflammatory magnitude.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

TLR2 is a therapeutic target in infection, sepsis, and autoimmunity, and TLR2 agonists are developed as vaccine adjuvants. In geroscience, the TLR2–cGAS-STING interplay makes it a candidate modulator of SASP-driven [[Inflammaging]] and of senescence-mediated tumor immune clearance, with both agonist (anti-tumor surveillance) and antagonist (anti-inflammatory) strategies conceivable depending on context.
