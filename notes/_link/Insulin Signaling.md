# Insulin Signaling

**Insulin signaling** is a conserved growth factor pathway that regulates metabolism, growth, and lifespan. It is a central suppressor of [[Autophagy]] via [[PI3K-Akt Signaling]]-dependent activation of [[mTORC1]].

## Pathway Architecture
Insulin binds the [[IGF1R]]/[[InsR]] receptor tyrosine kinase, recruiting [[IRS1]]/[[IRS2]] to activate [[PI3K]] → [[PIP3]] → [[PDK1]] → [[Akt]]. Akt phosphorylates [[TSC2]] (inhibiting the TSC1/TSC2 complex), [[PRAS40]], and [[FOXO]] transcription factors, all converging to activate [[mTORC1]] and suppress autophagy.

## Autophagy Regulation
Insulin signaling suppresses [[Autophagy]] through mTORC1-dependent phosphorylation of [[ULK1]] (inactivating the initiation complex) and [[TFEB]]/[[TFE3]] (promoting cytosolic retention via [[14-3-3 protein]]). Reduced insulin/IGF-1 signaling—through [[Dietary Restriction]], [[Intermittent Fasting]], or genetic reduction of [[DAF-2]] (in [[C. elegans]])—derepresses autophagy and extends lifespan across species.

## Longevity Connection
Reduced IIS is among the most robust lifespan-extending interventions. In [[C. elegans]], mutations in [[DAF-2]] (insulin/IGF-1 receptor) double lifespan via [[DAF-16]]/[[FOXO]] and autophagy activation. In mammals, [[IGF1]] reduction and insulin sensitivity enhancement are associated with longevity.

## Pathological Relevance
Hyperinsulinemia in [[Metabolic Syndrome]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] chronically suppresses autophagy, contributing to proteotoxicity, mitochondrial dysfunction, and accelerated aging. Conversely, autophagy impairment exacerbates insulin resistance.
