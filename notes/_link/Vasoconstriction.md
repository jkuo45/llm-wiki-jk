# Vasoconstriction

[[Vasoconstriction]] is the contraction of vascular smooth muscle that narrows the vessel lumen, increases systemic vascular resistance, and raises arterial blood pressure. It is the physiological opposite of [[Vasodilation]] and is essential for blood-pressure homeostasis, thermoregulation, and the redistribution of blood flow.

## Mechanism

- **Calcium-driven contraction:** agonist binding raises intracellular [[Calcium Ions|calcium]], activating myosin light-chain kinase to drive actin–myosin cross-bridge cycling.
- **Neural/humoral triggers:** [[Norepinephrine]] and [[Epinephrine]] acting on α1-[[Adrenergic receptor|adrenergic receptors]], angiotensin II, endothelin-1, and [[Vasopressors]].
- **Endothelial modulation:** basal [[Nitric Oxide]] (NO) opposes constriction; when the NO → [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]] → [[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate|cGMP]] relaxation pathway is blocked, net vasoconstriction results.

## Clinical Relevance

> [!info] Methylene blue in vasoplegia
> In [[Vasoplegic Shock]] and [[Septic Shock]], pathological NO overproduction causes refractory [[Hypotension]]. [[Methylene blue]] restores vascular tone by inhibiting both nitric oxide synthase and [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]], lowering cGMP and producing therapeutic vasoconstriction — a rescue therapy when catecholamine [[Vasopressors]] fail.
