# MELAS

MELAS (Mitochondrial Encephalomyopathy with Lactic Acidosis and Stroke-like episodes) is a maternally inherited mitochondrial disorder caused by mutations in [[Mitochondrial DNA]], most commonly the A3243G mutation in the [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] tRNA-Leu(UUR) gene.

## Clinical Features

The cardinal features of MELAS include:
- **Stroke-like episodes**: Recurrent episodes of focal neurological deficits, typically before age 40
- **Lactic acidosis**: Elevated blood and cerebrospinal fluid lactate
- **Encephalopathy**: Seizures, cognitive decline, dementia
- **Myopathy**: Progressive muscle weakness
- **Other**: Short stature, hearing loss, diabetes mellitus, cardiomyopathy

## Pathophysiology

> [!warning] Mitochondrial Energy Failure
> MELAS results from impaired mitochondrial protein synthesis due to defective tRNA-Leu(UUR), leading to impaired [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] and ATP depletion. The A3243G mutation is located immediately downstream of the 16S rRNA locus — the site of the [[Humanin]] open reading frame — creating a direct genetic link between MELAS and humanin biology.

The A3243G mutation:
- Disrupts mitochondrial translation of the 13 protein-coding genes
- Causes defective assembly of [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain|electron transport chain]] complexes (especially Complex I and IV)
- Results in energy failure in tissues with high metabolic demands (brain, muscle)
- Leads to secondary [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] generation and [[Oxidative Stress]]

## Humanin Connection

> [!tip] Humanin as a Potential Therapeutic Agent
> [[Humanin]] levels are significantly elevated in skeletal muscles of MELAS patients, suggesting a compensatory stress response. Humanin has been shown to restore ATP levels in lymphocytes from MELAS patients, preventing energy deficiency. This observation has led to investigation of humanin and its analogs as potential therapeutic agents for MELAS.

## Treatment

Current management is supportive:
- Coenzyme Q10 and L-carnitine supplementation
- Dichloroacetate (controversial)
- Avoidance of mitochondrial toxicants
- Seizure management
- Emerging: [[Humanin]]-based therapies under investigation
