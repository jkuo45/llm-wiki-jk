# Central Nervous System

## Overview

The [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] comprises the brain and spinal cord and houses [[Neuron|neurons]] alongside diverse glial populations — [[Microglia]], [[Astrocytes]], oligodendrocytes, and [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]] — that together maintain homeostasis. The [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] is active across all these resident cell types, and its dysregulation is a unifying feature of [[Neuroinflammation]] in [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative diseases]]. The CNS is also uniquely insulated by the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] and surveilled by peripheral immune compartments via meningeal lymphatics.

## Cellular Effectors of cGAS-STING in the CNS

Within the CNS, cGAS-STING activation in [[Microglia]] and [[Astrocytes]] drives robust induction of [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISGs]] and inflammatory cytokines that act in paracrine fashion on neurons, amplifying [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Neurodegeneration]] ([89](#B89), [95](#B95)–[97](#B97)). Neuronal [[STING]] independently regulates excitability and regeneration, while endothelial cGAS-STING activation by damaged [[mtDNA]] disrupts BBB integrity. Peripheral immune cells — [[Monocytes]], [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]], [[B Cells|B cells]], and [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|HSCs]] — interface with the CNS through the BBB and meningeal lymphatic system, adding another layer of regulation.

## Disease Spectrum

cGAS-STING signaling has been implicated across a broad spectrum of CNS disorders reviewed in the literature: [[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]], [[Parkinson's Disease|PD]], [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]], [[Huntington's Disease|HD]], prion disease, [[Frontotemporal Dementia|FTD]], glaucoma, neuropathic pain, and viral encephalitis (including HSV-1 and Zika). The conserved engagement of this pathway across etiologically distinct diseases supports its role as a shared downstream integrator of proteotoxic, mitochondrial, and infectious stress.
