# cGAS Inhibitors

## Overview

cGAS inhibitors are a class of experimental small molecules that block the nucleotidyltransferase activity of [[cGAS]], preventing synthesis of the cyclic dinucleotide [[cGAMP]] and thereby suppressing downstream [[STING]] activation and [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] production. Representative compounds include RU.521, TDI, PF-06928215, G150, and SU-cH. These agents are research/experimental tools and are under investigation for therapeutic modulation of chronic [[Neuroinflammation]].

## Mechanism and Therapeutic Rationale

By inhibiting cGAS, these molecules interrupt the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] at its upstream enzymatic step, reducing [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISG]] expression, [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]]–driven cytokines, and maladaptive glial activation. Critically, cGAS inhibition selectively suppresses neurotoxic inflammatory programs while preserving beneficial microglial functions such as phagocytosis and recruitment to pathological aggregates. In tauopathy models, pharmacological cGAS inhibition phenocopies the protective [[APOE3 Christchurch]] (R136S) resilience allele, validating that suppression of cGAS-STING is sufficient to recapitulate genetic protection against tau toxicity.

## Context-Dependent Considerations

Because STING signaling also exerts homeostatic, protective roles — for example, maintaining [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] integrity in microglia during aging — broad pathway inhibition must be approached with cell-type specificity. cGAS inhibitors offer a potential advantage by targeting the proximal sensor, yet timing, dose, and delivery remain key variables for translating efficacy into safe therapies for [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]], and other proteinopathies.
