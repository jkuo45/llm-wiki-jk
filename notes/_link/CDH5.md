# CDH5

## Overview

CDH5 (Vascular Endothelial Cadherin, VE-cadherin, CD144) is a classical cadherin member of the cadherin superfamily, expressed exclusively in endothelial cells. It is the principal adhesion molecule at endothelial adherens junctions, forming Ca²⁺-dependent homophilic interactions that maintain vascular integrity and barrier function.

## Structure & Domains

- Five extracellular cadherin domains (EC1–EC5)
- Single transmembrane domain
- Cytoplasmic tail that interacts with p120-catenin, β-catenin, and α-catenin
- Phosphorylation of cytoplasmic tail regulates junctional stability and turnover

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- Forms homophilic interactions with CDH5 on adjacent endothelial cells to establish adherens junctions
- Connects to the actin cytoskeleton via catenin complex (p120-catenin → β-catenin → α-catenin → [[Actin]])
- Regulates vascular permeability through junctional remodeling
- Signaling through VEGFR2 modulates CDH5 phosphorylation and junctional dynamics
- Loss of CDH5 expression is a hallmark of [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EndoMT)

> [!info] Source: CXCL12 → Endothelial Dysfunction Mechanistic Trace
> CDH5 is downregulated 45% during CXCL12-driven EndoMT, marking the loss of endothelial identity and junctional integrity. This downregulation is part of the transdifferentiation program driven by [[TGFβ]] signaling.

## Physiological Function

- Maintains endothelial barrier integrity in blood vessels
- Regulates leukocyte transmigration during inflammation
- Essential for vascular development and angiogenesis
- Contributes to vascular homeostasis by sensing hemodynamic forces

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- CDH5 downregulation correlates with vascular leak, inflammation, and tumor angiogenesis
- Mutations cause hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia type 2 (HHT2)
- Loss of CDH5 expression during [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] contributes to age-related vascular dysfunction, cardiac fibrosis, and atherosclerosis
- CDH5 is a marker of endothelial identity; its loss indicates endothelial dedifferentiation
