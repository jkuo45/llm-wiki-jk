# PML

PML (Promyelocytic Leukemia) is a tumor-suppressor scaffold protein best known as the organizer of PML nuclear bodies (PML-NBs or ND10), discrete nuclear foci 0.2–1.0 µm in diameter that concentrate PML together with many partner proteins (p53, DAXX, [[RB1]], [[BRD4]], and others). In senescence, PML nuclear bodies are essential platforms for assembly of [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]]: the histone chaperone [[HIRA]] must translocate to PML bodies for SAHF to form, as described in the npj Aging review of the senescent epigenetic landscape (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Overview

The PML gene encodes a RING-finger protein that oligomerizes via its RBCC/TRIM motif to seed nuclear bodies. PML-NBs are dynamic, multiprotein hubs involved in DNA repair, apoptosis, antiviral responses, and chromatin regulation. Their number and size increase during senescence and in response to stress.

## Structure and Function

PML contains an N-terminal RING finger, two B-boxes, and a coiled-coil (RBCC/TRIM) domain that mediates SUMOylation-dependent oligomerization. This self-assembly creates the scaffold for nuclear bodies that recruit SUMOylated partners and chromatin regulators.

## Role in Senescence and SAHF

PML nuclear bodies are required for SAHF formation. Preventing HIRA translocation to PML-NBs inhibits SAHF assembly (Zhang et al., 2005, PMID 15680342), and disrupting PML bodies themselves likewise blocks SAHF (Ye et al., 2007, PMID 17339329). PML therefore sits genetically upstream of the HIRA/ASF1a/UBN1 heterochromatin program. PML also cooperates with [[HIRA]] and p62/[[SQSTM1]] to regulate inflammation during senescence (Dasgupta et al., 2024, Mol. Cell 84:3271–3287).

## Mechanistic Details

PML-NBs concentrate the machinery (HIRA complex, histone-modifying enzymes) needed to deposit repressive chromatin at senescent loci. By acting as a phase-separated nuclear compartment, PML bodies concentrate factors that write [[H3K9me3]] and [[H3K27me3]] and promote chromosome-wide condensation into SAHF. The p53 and Rb tumor-suppressor pathways, both stabilized/enhanced at PML bodies, reinforce the senescence arrest.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

PML is a classic tumor suppressor whose loss characterizes acute promyelocytic leukemia (targeted by arsenic trioxide). In aging, its role in SAHF/heterochromatin positions PML within the epigenetic control of [[SASP]] and senescence. Modulating PML-body function could inform [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] strategies, albeit with care given its tumor-suppressive role (see [[KDM4]], [[BRD4]]).
