# Caspase 5

Caspase 5 is a human inflammatory (non-canonical) caspase, paralogous to [[Caspase 11]] (mouse) and [[Caspase 4]] (human). It is best known for its role in the non-canonical inflammasome: in response to cytosolic danger signals it oligomerizes and cleaves [[Gasdermin D]], generating plasma-membrane pores that drive [[Pyroptosis]] and release of inflammatory mediators. In the context of senescence, caspase 5 (and its orthologs) also contributes to [[IL-1α]] processing, a regulatory step embedded in the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]].

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Non-canonical inflammasome**: Caspase 5 is activated by cytosolic stimuli (e.g., microbial products, potassium efflux) that promote its oligomerization and auto-cleavage.
- **Gasdermin D cleavage**: Active caspase 5 cleaves [[Gasdermin D]] at its linker, releasing the N-terminal pore-forming domain, which oligomerizes in the plasma membrane to form lytic pores.
- **IL-1α processing**: Caspase 5 cleaves [[IL-1α]], an alarmin that can transit from the cell surface to the nucleus in a copper/calcium-dependent manner, modulating SASP composition and IL-1–biased signaling.
- **Caspase-1 cooperation**: Pore formation and K+ efflux downstream of caspase 5 can license canonical [[Caspase-1]] activation, amplifying maturation of IL-1β/IL-18.

## Role in Senescence

- Caspase 5 is essential for certain IL-1α activation events within the SASP, linking inflammasome signaling to SASP regulation.
- Through gasdermin-D pores it can enforce an inflammatory, lytic cell state that overlaps functionally with senescence-associated inflammation.
- Caspase 5-mediated IL-1α cleavage feeds forward into NF-κB– and IL-1–driven SASP factor expression.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- Caspase 5 is a candidate target in inflammatory and autoinflammatory diseases where non-canonical inflammasome signaling dominates.
- Inhibitors of caspase 5/gasdermin-D may modulate the SASP and attenuate [[Inflammaging]].
- It is implicated in sepsis and age-related inflammation.
