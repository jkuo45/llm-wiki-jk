# BMP

**Bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs)** are a large subfamily of secreted growth factors within the [[TGFβ|TGFβ superfamily]]. Originally identified for their capacity to induce ectopic bone and cartilage formation, BMPs are pleiotropic morphogens governing embryonic patterning, organogenesis, and adult tissue homeostasis. They act as beneficial, developmental components of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].

## Structure & Function

- Synthesized as precursors, cleaved to release C-terminal mature dimers stabilized by an intramolecular cystine knot.
- Signal through heterotetrameric complexes of type I and type II serine/threonine kinase receptors, forming morphogen gradients that specify cell fate in a concentration-dependent manner.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- BMP–receptor engagement phosphorylates the receptor-regulated SMADs **SMAD1/5/8**, which complex with [[SMAD4]] and translocate to the nucleus to regulate transcription (canonical pathway).
- Also signals through non-canonical [[MAPK]] (p38, ERK) routes; antagonized extracellularly by noggin, chordin, and gremlin to shape gradients.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- BMPs are beneficial developmental SASP factors: programmed senescence during embryogenesis produces SASP factors — including BMPs, [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|MMPs]], and [[IGFBP5]] — that contribute to patterning and growth.
- BMP signaling cooperates with senescence programs during morphogenesis (e.g., limb and neural tube development) and tissue remodeling.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- Recombinant BMP-2 and BMP-7 are used clinically to promote bone regeneration and spinal fusion.
- Dysregulated BMP signaling contributes to fibrodysplasia ossificans progressiva, pulmonary arterial hypertension, fibrosis, and cancer.
