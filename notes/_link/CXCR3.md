# CXCR3

CXCR3 (C-X-C chemokine receptor 3), also known as CD183, is a G-protein-coupled receptor that binds the ELR− interferon-inducible C-X-C chemokines [[CXCL10]] and [[CXCL11]] (and CXCL9). It is the principal receptor directing Th1/CD8+ T-cell and NK-cell trafficking, giving it dual roles in SASP-mediated immune surveillance and in cancer progression (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Ligand repertoire**: CXCR3 binds CXCL9, CXCL10 (IP-10), and CXCL11 (I-TAC), the last with highest affinity; these are induced by type I/II interferons via JAK/[[STAT1]] and by [[NF-κB]], the same pathways active in the interferon-SASP branch.
- **Signal transduction**: Gαi coupling activates PI3K/Akt, [[p38 MAPK]]/ERK, and Ca2+ flux, driving polarized chemotaxis and inside-out integrin activation for firm adhesion.
- **Expression**: Constitutively high on Th1 and CD8+ T cells, NK cells, and some plasma cells; in pathology it is also expressed on endothelial cells and on subsets of cancer cells.

## Role in SASP / Senescence

- CXCR3 is the receptor arm that translates the interferon-responsive SASP into immune recruitment: senescent cells release CXCL10/11 that lure NK and T cells to survey and clear them (immune surveillance).
- In therapy-induced senescence (TIS), however, CXCR3 ligands secreted by senescent endothelial cells can instead recruit CXCR3+ cells that foster a pro-tumor microenvironment, exemplifying the SASP's dual nature (see [[CXCL11]] in breast cancer).
- Persistent CXCR3 signaling in aged tissue can convert protective surveillance into chronic [[Inflammaging]] and autoimmunity.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CXCR3 is a therapeutic target in autoimmune disease, atherosclerosis, and cancer; its ligands are readouts in immuno-oncology and in interferon-active inflammation.
- CXCR3 expression on tumors correlates with T-cell infiltration and, depending on context, with better or worse prognosis.
- CXCR3-antagonist approaches are under study to limit SASP-driven inflammation and TIS-associated tumor promotion.
