# Polyphenols

Polyphenols are a category of phytochemicals that are naturally found in plants.

## Overview

Polyphenols possess high antioxidant capacity and are effective inhibitors of [[Glycation]] and [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs) formation. They are categorized into several classes, including [[Flavonoids]], phenolic acids, and stilbenes.

## Role in Aging

By neutralizing [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and trapping reactive carbonyls, polyphenols help prevent age-related organ dysfunction induced by glycotoxins.

## Structural Diversity and Classification

Polyphenols encompass over 8,000 identified compounds broadly classified into four main groups based on carbon skeleton:

### 1. Phenolic Acids
- **Hydroxybenzoic acids** (C6-C1): gallic acid, vanillic acid, syringic acid—present in Tea, Red Wine, and Berries
- **Hydroxycinnamic acids** (C6-C3): caffeic acid, ferulic acid, p-coumaric acid, sinapic acid—abundant in coffee, fruits, and cereals; often esterified as **chlorogenic acid** (caffeoylquinic acid)

### 2. Flavonoids
The largest subclass (see [[Flavonoids]]), including flavonols, flavones, isoflavones, flavanones, flavanols, and anthocyanidins, sharing the diphenylpropane (C6-C3-C6) scaffold.

### 3. Stilbenes
Characterized by the C6-C2-C6 framework:
- **[[Resveratrol]]** (3,5,4′-trihydroxy-trans-stilbene): The most studied stilbene, found in red wine, grapes, and [[Polygonum cuspidatum]]; activates [[Sirtuin 1|SIRT1]] and [[AMPK]]
- **Piceatannol** and **pterostilbene**: Methoxylated analogues with improved bioavailability

### 4. Lignans
Dimeric phenylpropanoid compounds (C6-C3 dimer):
- **Secoisolariciresinol** and **matairesinol**: Found in flaxseed, sesame seeds, and whole grains; metabolized by [[Gut Microbiome]] to enterolactone and enterodiol

## Structure-Antioxidant Activity Relationships

The radical-scavenging capacity of polyphenols depends on:

### Essential Structural Features
- **Ortho-dihydroxyl (catechol) arrangement** on the B-ring: Provides the most stable radical resonance
- **2,3-double bond conjugated with 4-oxo group**: Delocalizes unpaired electrons across the C-ring
- **3- and 5-hydroxyl groups**: Enable electron donation and metal chelation
- **Multiple hydroxyl groups**: Increase hydrogen-donating capacity

### O-Methylation Effects
Methylation of hydroxyl groups (e.g., in metabolized flavonoids) reduces antioxidant capacity by 2–10 fold but enhances membrane partitioning and metabolic stability.

### Galloylation
The galloyl ester moiety (present in [[EGCG]]) provides additional hydroxyl groups, making galloylated catechins among the most potent natural antioxidants (TEAC values 4–5× greater than Trolox).

## Molecular Mechanisms of Antioxidant Action

### Direct Radical Scavenging

Polyphenols donate hydrogen atoms (HAT) or electrons (SET) to terminate radical chain reactions:

  Polyphenol-OH + R• → Polyphenol-O• + RH

The resulting **phenoxyl radical** is stabilized by:
- **Delocalization** across the aromatic ring system
- **Intramolecular hydrogen bonding** between the phenoxyl oxygen and adjacent hydroxyl groups
- **Dimerization** to form biphenyl compounds (e.g., theaflavins in black tea)

Rate constants for polyphenol reactions with peroxyl radicals (ROO•) and [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] approach 10⁸–10⁹ M⁻¹s⁻¹.

### Metal Chelation

Polyphenols chelate Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ and Cu⁺/Cu²⁺ through catechol (o-dihydroxyl) and galloyl (trihydroxyl) groups, forming stable complexes that:
- **Prevent [[Fenton Reaction]]**: Suppressing •OH generation
- **Inhibit [[Lipid Peroxidation]]**: Removing catalytic iron from membranes
- **Modulate iron absorption**: Dietary polyphenols reduce non-heme iron bioavailability

### NRF2/KEAP1 Pathway Activation

Polyphenols are **electrophilic** or are metabolized to electrophilic quinones that modify specific **KEAP1 cysteine residues** (Cys151, Cys273, Cys288), leading to:
1. KEAP1 conformational change
2. NRF2 release from KEAP1-mediated ubiquitination
3. NRF2 nuclear translocation and ARE binding
4. Induction of >200 cytoprotective genes including:
   - [[Glutathione Peroxidase]]
   - [[Catalase]]
   - Heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1)
   - [[NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1]] (NQO1)
   - [[Superoxide Dismutase]]
   - [[Glutathione]] S-transferases (GSTs)

## The Biphasic Hormetic Response

Polyphenols exhibit **hormesis**—low-dose beneficial, high-dose potentially harmful effects:

### Low-Dose Protective Effects (µM range)
- Activation of NRF2, SIRT1, [[AMPK]]
- Enhancement of [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]]
- Improved [[Glutathione]] levels
- Suppression of [[NF-kappa B]] and [[Inflammation]]

### High-Dose Pro-Oxidant Effects (≥100 µM)
- **Autoxidation**: Polyphenol → semiquinone → quinone + O₂⁻•
- **Reduction of Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺**: Promoting Fenton chemistry
- **Quinone-mediated toxicity**: Covalent modification of proteins and [[Glutathione]] depletion
- **DNA damage**: Topoisomerase-mediated DNA cleavage (especially relevant for dietary carcinogenesis studies)

## Gut Microbiota Metabolism

The majority of dietary polyphenols reach the colon intact where they are metabolized by [[Gut Microbiome]]:

- **Ring fission**: Conversion of flavonoid C-ring to phenolic acids (e.g., 3-hydroxyphenylacetic acid, 4-hydroxyphenylpropionic acid)
- **Dehydroxylation**: Loss of hydroxyl groups (e.g., daidzein → equol by [[Lactobacillus]] and [[Bifidobacterium]])
- **Demethylation**: Removal of methoxy groups
- **Decarboxylation**: Short-chain fatty acid production

These microbial metabolites are absorbed systemically and may contribute significantly to the overall health effects of polyphenols, often at higher circulating concentrations than parent compounds.

## Clinical and Pathological Significance

### Cardiovascular Protection
Polyphenols reduce [[Cardiovascular Disease]] risk through:
- **Improved [[Endothelial Dysfunction]]**: Enhancing eNOS activity and NO bioavailability
- **LDL oxidation inhibition**: [[Quercetin]] and resveratrol inhibit Cu²⁺-mediated and cell-mediated LDL oxidation
- **Blood pressure reduction**: Anthocyanins and flavanols lower BP by 2–5 mmHg in meta-analyses
- **Platelet aggregation inhibition**: Resveratrol suppresses thromboxane A₂ synthesis

### Neuroprotection
Polyphenols cross the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] and protect against [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]]:
- **Amyloid-β aggregation inhibition**: [[EGCG]] remodels Aβ fibrils into non-toxic oligomers
- **Metal chelation in [[Alzheimer's Disease]]**: Removing Cu²⁺/Zn²⁺ from Aβ aggregates
- **Dopaminergic neuron protection**: [[Quercetin]] and resveratrol attenuate MPTP/MPP⁺ toxicity in [[Parkinson's Disease]] models

### Cancer Chemoprevention
- **Phase I enzyme modulation**: Inhibition of CYP1A1 by flavonoids
- **Phase II enzyme induction**: NRF2-driven upregulation of detoxifying enzymes
- **Angiogenesis inhibition**: Resveratrol suppresses [[VEGF]] signaling
- **Apoptosis induction**: Pro-oxidant effects selectively eliminate cancer cells

### Metabolic Benefits
- [[Diabetes Mellitus]]: Improved insulin sensitivity through [[AMPK]] activation
- [[Obesity]]: Anthocyanins reduce adipocyte differentiation
- [[Metabolic Syndromes]]: Reduced oxidative stress and inflammation
