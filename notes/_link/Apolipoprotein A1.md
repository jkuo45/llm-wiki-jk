# Apolipoprotein A1

**Apolipoprotein A-1 (apoA-1)** is the primary protein component of [[HDL]] and the specific acceptor for [[ABCA1]]-mediated [[Cholesterol Efflux]]. It is the most abundant apolipoprotein in plasma and plays a central role in [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]].

## Function

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> ABCA1 promotes the transfer of cholesterol and phospholipids to lipid-poor apoA-1. ApoA-1-mediated cholesterol efflux is enhanced by resveratrol in a dose-dependent manner (r² = 0.907, p < 0.05) via ABCA1 upregulation. ApoA-1 is the specific acceptor for ABCA1-mediated cholesterol efflux from macrophages.

- ApoA-1 is the primary protein acceptor for cholesterol efflux via ABCA1
- Lipid-free or lipid-poor apoA-1 is the preferred substrate for ABCA1
- ApoA-1 binding to ABCA1 is enhanced by ABCA1-mediated changes to plasma membrane
- ApoA-1 initiates HDL biogenesis when lipidated by ABCA1

## Regulation

- **[[ABCA1]]**: Mediates cholesterol and phospholipid transfer to apoA-1
- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Enhances apoA-1-mediated cholesterol efflux by upregulating ABCA1
- **cAMP**: Used experimentally to induce ABCA1 and enhance apoA-1-mediated efflux

## Clinical Relevance

- Low apoA-1 levels are associated with increased [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Cardiovascular Disease]] risk
- ApoA-1 is a therapeutic target for HDL-based cardiovascular therapies
- Recombinant apoA-1 and apoA-1 mimetics are being developed to enhance [[Cholesterol Efflux]]
