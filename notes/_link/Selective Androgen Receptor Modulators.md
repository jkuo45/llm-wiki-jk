# Selective Androgen Receptor Modulators

**Selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs)** are non-steroidal ligands that activate the [[Androgen Receptor]] in a tissue-selective manner, producing anabolic effects in muscle and bone with fewer virilizing or hepatotoxic liabilities than exogenous testosterone.

## Structure and Mechanism
SARMs are structurally diverse (arylpropionamide, quinolinone, and tricyclic classes) but share the ability to bind the ligand-binding domain of the androgen receptor, recruiting coactivators that drive transcription of anabolic genes. In [[Skeletal Muscle]] they promote protein synthesis via [[mTORC1]]/translation pathways and oppose [[Muscle Atrophy]] and [[Cachexia]]; in androgenic tissues (prostate, skin, hair) their activity is weaker, accounting for the improved side-effect profile.

## Applications and Investigations
SARMs are studied for [[Sarcopenia]], muscular dystrophy, cancer-cachexia, and osteoporosis-related wasting. Enobosarm (ostarine), andarine, and ligandrol are among agents evaluated in clinical trials, though none are broadly approved for these indications outside research. They are distinct from [[Myostatin]] inhibitors but share anabolic intent.

## Risks and Controversies
Off-label and athletic use is widespread. Concerns include lipid abnormalities, suppression of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis, hepatotoxicity, and uncertain cardiovascular risk. Long-term safety data are limited.

> [!warning] Safety
> SARMs can suppress endogenous testosterone and alter lipids; they are not approved as dietary supplements and carry unknown long-term risks.
