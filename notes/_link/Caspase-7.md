# Caspase-7

## Overview
[[Caspase-7]] is an executioner (effector) caspase that, once activated, cleaves cellular substrates to dismantle the cell during apoptosis. It is activated by initiator caspases ([[Caspase-8]], [[Caspase-9]]) and shares functional redundancy with [[Caspase-3]] but has distinct substrate preferences and regulatory mechanisms.

## Structure & Activation
Caspase-7 is synthesized as an inactive zymogen (procaspase-7) containing a large (p20) and small (p10) catalytic subunit. Activation requires proteolytic cleavage by initiator caspases at specific Asp residues. Once cleaved, the active heterotetramer (p20)₂(p10)₂ can cleave apoptotic substrates including ICAD (inhibitor of caspase-activated DNase), PARP, and lamin B1.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways
Active Caspase-7 cleaves structural and regulatory proteins to execute the apoptotic program. Its substrates include DNA repair enzymes (PARP), nuclear lamins, and cytoskeletal components. While functionally overlapping with [[Caspase-3]], Caspase-7 has a preference for certain substrates and is regulated by distinct upstream signals.

> [!important] PAK2-Mediated Inhibition in Breast Cancer
> **[[PAK2]]** (p21-activated kinase 2) directly binds and phosphorylates Caspase-7 at three residues: **Ser30**, **Thr173**, and **Ser239**. Ser30 phosphorylation in the prodomain obstructs interaction with [[Caspase-9]], preventing activation. Ser239 phosphorylation near the active site sterically hinders substrate binding, neutralizing even "active" molecules. PAK2 is frequently overexpressed in invasive ductal carcinoma, making this axis a major driver of chemotherapy resistance.

## Physiological Function
Caspase-7 plays a non-redundant role in specific apoptotic contexts, particularly in response to endoplasmic reticulum stress and in certain tissue types. It contributes to the clearance of damaged or redundant cells during development and tissue homeostasis.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer**: The PAK2–Caspase-7 axis is a significant mechanism of chemoresistance in breast cancer, particularly invasive ductal carcinoma. Tumors overexpressing PAK2 resist DNA-damaging chemotherapeutics (e.g., staurosporine) by maintaining Caspase-7 in a phosphorylated, inactive state.
- **Therapeutic Targeting**: Disrupting the PAK2–Caspase-7 interaction may restore chemosensitivity in resistant tumors.
