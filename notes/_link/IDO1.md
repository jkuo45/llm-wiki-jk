# IDO1

**IDO1** (Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1) is a heme-containing enzyme that catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step in the [[Kynurenine Pathway]] (KP) of [[Tryptophan]] (Trp) catabolism. It oxidizes L-tryptophan to [[N-Formylkynurenine]] in an O₂-dependent reaction.

## Structure & Domains

IDO1 is a monomeric cytosolic protein (~45 kDa, 403 amino acids) encoded by the *IDO1* gene on human chromosome 8p11.21. It belongs to the heme-dependent dioxygenase family (EC 1.13.11.52). Crystal structures (PDB: 2D0T, 5EK2, 6E40) reveal:
- A large **catalytic domain** with a deep heme-binding pocket containing a ferrous iron center that coordinates O₂
- A smaller **immunoglobulin-like domain** that contributes to substrate recognition and active-site plasticity
- A flexible active-site loop that accommodates multiple indoleamine substrates

## Mechanism

The catalytic cycle requires reduction of the inactive ferric (Fe³⁺) heme to the active ferrous (Fe²⁺) form, typically coupled to [[NADH]] or ascorbate-dependent reductases. The ferrous-oxy complex performs oxidative cleavage of the indole ring of [[Tryptophan]] at the 2,3-position, producing [[N-Formylkynurenine]], which is rapidly deformylated to [[Kynurenine]] by kynurenine formamidase.

IDO1 can act on multiple substrates including L-Trp, D-Trp, 5-hydroxy-Trp, tryptamine, and [[Serotonin]], though L-Trp is the preferred physiological substrate.

## Regulation

**Transcriptional induction:** Strongly induced by pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly [[Interferon-gamma]] (IFNγ) via the [[JAK]]/[[STAT1]] signaling pathway. [[TNF-α]], [[IL-1β]], [[LPS]], and glucocorticoids can synergize in certain cellular contexts.

**Post-translational control:** Phosphorylation of ITIM (immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif) domains recruits [[SOCS3]], promoting ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation — a negative feedback mechanism. IDO1 can also transduce non-enzymatic signaling through ITIM-dependent [[PI3K]] activation, exerting immunomodulatory effects independently of catalytic activity.

**Expression pattern:** Constitutively high in placenta, lymph nodes, lungs, and small intestine. Inducible in [[Dendritic Cells|dendritic cells]], [[Macrophage|macrophages]], monocytes, endothelial cells, and fibroblasts during [[Inflammation]].

## Immunological Functions

IDO1 is a central immunoregulatory enzyme operating through two synergistic mechanisms:

1. **Local tryptophan depletion:** IDO1-mediated Trp consumption creates a Trp-deficient microenvironment. Effector [[T cell|T cells]] (which require Trp for proliferation) activate the [[GCN2]] stress kinase in response to amino acid starvation, leading to cell-cycle arrest, anergy, or [[Apoptosis]].

2. **Kynurenine-dependent signaling:** Accumulated [[Kynurenine]] and downstream KP metabolites ([[Kynurenic Acid]], [[Quinolinic Acid]], [[3-Hydroxykynurenine]]) activate the [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor]] (AhR). AhR signaling promotes [[Regulatory T cell|regulatory T cell]] (Treg) differentiation, suppresses Th17 responses, and inhibits NK-cell and CD8⁺ T-cell effector functions.

This dual mechanism creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment exploited by [[Cancer|cancers]] and critical for maternal-fetal tolerance.

## Clinical Relevance

**Immuno-oncology:** IDO1 is frequently upregulated in [[Tumor Microenvironment|tumors]] and tumor-draining lymph nodes, where it suppresses antitumor immunity. [[Epacadostat]], navoximod, and linrodostat (BMS-986205) are IDO1 inhibitors tested in clinical trials (often combined with [[Checkpoint Inhibitor|checkpoint inhibitors]]), though results have been mixed due to compensatory pathways ([[TDO]], [[IDO2]]).

**Diabetes:** [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|Type 1 diabetes]] involves altered Trp-Kyn profiles linked to IDO1 genotype and reduced IFNγ responsiveness. [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|T2D]] is associated with elevated KP activity in the context of chronic inflammatory [[Insulin Resistance]].

**Neurodegeneration:** KP metabolites (particularly [[Quinolinic Acid]]) are neurotoxic and implicated in [[Neurodegeneration]], [[Depression]], and HIV-associated neurocognitive disorders.
