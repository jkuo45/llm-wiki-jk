# CLOCK

[[CLOCK]] (Circadian Locomotor Output Cycles Kaput) is a core component of the mammalian [[Circadian Rhythm|circadian clock]]. It heterodimerizes with [[BMAL1]] to bind E-box elements and drive transcription of clock-controlled genes, including [[NAMPT]], the rate-limiting enzyme of the [[NAD+|NAD⁺ salvage pathway]].

## SIRT1 Regulation

SIRT1 deacetylates and activates [[CLOCK]], coupling metabolic state to circadian output. CLOCK and [[BMAL1]] are also amplified transcriptionally by SIRT1 via [[PGC-1α]] in the suprachiasmatic nucleus.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - SIRT1 Protein roles at the nexus of health, disease, and therapeutics|SIRT1: Protein Roles at the Nexus (Chaqour, 2026)]]
> In the liver, SIRT1 deacetylates [[PGC-1α]] and amplifies the expression of the circadian transcription factors [[BMAL1]] and [[CLOCK]] in the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus. A key transcriptional target of BMAL1 and CLOCK is [[NAMPT]], the rate-limiting enzyme in the NAD⁺ salvage pathway, establishing a rhythmic pattern of SIRT1 activation aligned with the circadian cycle.

## Physiological Significance

The CLOCK–BMAL1–NAMPT axis generates circadian [[NAD+|NAD⁺]] oscillations that gate SIRT1 activity. Age-related [[SIRT1]] decline disrupts this loop and is linked to metabolic and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]].
