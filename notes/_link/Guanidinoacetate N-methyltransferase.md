**Guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase (GAMT; EC 2.1.1.2)** is a methyltransferase enzyme that catalyzes the irreversible methylation of [[Guanidinoacetate (GAA)|guanidinoacetate (GAA)]] to form [[Creatine]], using [[S-adenosyl Methionine|S-adenosyl methionine (SAMe)]] as the methyl donor. It is encoded by the _GAMT_ gene on chromosome 19p13.3.

## Biochemical Function

GAMT is the second and terminal enzyme in the endogenous creatine biosynthesis pathway:

1. **AGAT** ([[Arginine Glycine Amidinotransferase]]) converts [[Arginine]] and [[Glycine]] to [[Guanidinoacetate (GAA)]] and [[Ornithine]].
2. **GAMT** then methylates GAA → creatine, consuming SAMe and producing [[S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH)|S-adenosylhomocysteine]].

GAMT is expressed primarily in the [[Liver]], [[Kidney]], [[Pancreas]], and to a lesser extent in the [[Brain]]. Most GAA produced in the kidney is transported to the liver for GAMT-mediated methylation.

## Clinical Significance

### GAMT Deficiency (GAMT-d)

- An autosomal recessive inborn error of creatine metabolism (OMIM #612736) caused by mutations in the _GAMT_ gene.
- **Prevalence:** Estimated at ~1:250,000; accounts for a subset of [[Creatine Synthesis Deficiency|creatine synthesis deficiencies]].
- **Pathophysiology:** Accumulation of [[Guanidinoacetate (GAA)]] with depletion of cerebral [[Creatine]] and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|PCr]], impairing [[Brain Bioenergetics|brain bioenergetics]].
- **Clinical features:** Global developmental delay / [[Intellectual Disability|intellectual disability]], [[Epilepsy]] (often intractable), [[Movement Disorders|movement disorders]] (extrapyramidal signs), [[Speech Development|speech/language delay]], and [[Autism|autistic-like]] behaviors.
- **Diagnosis:** Elevated GAA in plasma/urine; reduced creatine on [[Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy|MRS]]; molecular genetic testing.
- **Treatment:** High-dose [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine monohydrate]] (0.3–0.8 g/kg/day), often combined with [[Ornithine]] or [[Arginine]] restriction and [[Sodium Benzoate]] to reduce GAA accumulation. Early treatment (before 9 months) can normalize neurodevelopmental outcomes.

- [[Creatine]]: Product of the GAMT reaction; central to cellular energy metabolism.
- [[S-adenosyl Methionine]]: Methyl donor required for GAMT activity.
