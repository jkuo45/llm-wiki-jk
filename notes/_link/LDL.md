# LDL
**Low-density lipoprotein (LDL)** is a lipoprotein particle that transports [[Cholesterol]] and triglycerides from the liver to peripheral tissues. Elevated LDL is a major risk factor for [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Cardiovascular Disease]].

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> LDL can be taken up and oxidized by macrophages, resulting in significant cholesterol accumulation and foam cell formation — hallmarks of early atherogenesis. Copper- and radical-induced LDL oxidation produces conjugated dienes and alters the apoB protein moiety. Resveratrol (0–1 µM) inhibits LDL oxidation by extending the lag phase and reducing V_max of conjugated diene formation.

## Oxidation and Atherogenesis

- LDL oxidation is characterized by [[Lipid Peroxidation]] of polyunsaturated fatty acids and alteration of the [[Apolipoprotein B|apoB]] protein moiety
- [[Oxidized LDL]] is present in atherosclerotic lesions and correlates with coronary atherosclerosis severity
- Oxidation can be induced by copper ions, γ-radiolysis (free radicals), or endothelial cells
- [[Resveratrol]] protects LDL against oxidation by chelating free copper ions and scavenging free radicals
- [[Vitamin E]] content of LDL is preserved by resveratrol during oxidative stress
