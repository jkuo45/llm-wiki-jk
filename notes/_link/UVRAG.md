# UVRAG

## Overview

[[UVRAG]] (UV radiation resistance-associated gene) is a conserved autophagy regulator originally identified as a gene whose expression confers resistance to UV-induced cell death. It functions as a scaffolding protein that modulates two distinct membrane-trafficking pathways: macroautophagy and endocytic maturation. As a tumor suppressor, reduced UVRAG expression is observed in several human cancers, where its loss correlates with defective autophagic flux and enhanced proliferation.

## Structure & Domains

UVRAG is a coiled-coil domain-containing protein. Its N-terminal region binds the autophagy initiator [[ULK1]] complex, while a central coiled-coil segment mediates interaction with the class III phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase ([[PI3K]] / [[Vps34]]) complex and with [[Beclin1]] (mammalian [[Beclin-1]]). A C-terminal domain connects UVRAG to endosomal tethering factors, linking it to [[Rab7]]-positive late endosomes.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

UVRAG acts as a positive regulator of the [[Vps34]] complex by displacing the inhibitory subunit and stabilizing Vps34–Beclin 1 association, increasing production of the lipid [[PtdIns3P]] at the phagophore. This promotes nucleation of the isolation membrane and progression to the [[Autophagosome]]. UVRAG is therefore a component of the autophagy-activation branch of the [[Beclin1]] complex, contrasting with [[Rubicon]], which inhibits the same complex. Independently, UVRAG promotes autophagosome–lysosome fusion and endosomal maturation through its interaction with the C-Vps/HOPS tethering machinery, supporting [[Lysosome]] biogenesis and cargo degradation.

## Physiological Function

By sustaining basal and induced [[Autophagy]], UVRAG maintains organelle quality control, suppresses the accumulation of damaged proteins and mitochondria ([[Mitophagy]]), and limits [[Oxidative Stress]]. Its endocytic role additionally supports nutrient sensing downstream of [[mTOR]] inhibition.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

UVRAG downregulation occurs in colorectal, gastric, and hepatocellular carcinomas and contributes to impaired autophagic tumor suppression. Restoring UVRAG enhances autophagic flux and sensitizes cancer cells to stress, making it a candidate axis for [[Cancer]] therapeutics aimed at reinstating autophagy.
