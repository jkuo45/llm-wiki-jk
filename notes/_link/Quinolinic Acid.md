# Quinolinic Acid

**Quinolinic acid** (QA) is a dicarboxylic acid intermediate of the [[Kynurenine Pathway]] (KP) formed from 3-hydroxyanthranilic acid by the enzyme 3-hydroxyanthranilate 3,4-dioxygenase (HAAO). It is a precursor for de novo [[NAD+]] biosynthesis and also acts as a selective [[NMDA receptor]] agonist with potent excitotoxic properties.

## Dual Role

- **[[NAD+]] precursor:** QA is converted to nicotinic acid mononucleotide (NaMN) by quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase (QPRT), entering the Preiss-Handler pathway for [[NAD+]] synthesis.
- **Excitotoxin:** QA is an endogenous NMDA receptor agonist with approximately 100-fold greater potency than glutamate. It promotes calcium influx, [[Oxidative Stress]], mitochondrial dysfunction, and [[Apoptosis]] in neurons.

## Clinical Relevance

Elevated QA levels are implicated in [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegenerative conditions]] including:
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]
- [[Parkinson Disease|Parkinson's disease]]
- [[Depression|Major depressive disorder]]
- HIV-associated neurocognitive disorder (HAND)
- [[Huntington's disease]]

QA also contributes to the immunosuppressive [[Tumor Microenvironment]] by activating the [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor]] and promoting [[Regulatory T cell|Treg]] differentiation.
