## Overview

The Urokinase Plasminogen Activator Receptor (uPAR, also known as CD87) is a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored membrane protein that serves as the cellular receptor for [[uPA|urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA)]]. By localizing [[uPA]] activity to the cell surface, uPAR concentrates proteolysis at sites of cell migration and invasion, making it a critical mediator of ECM remodeling and cancer cell dissemination.

## Structure & Domains

- **Molecular weight**: ~35-55 kDa (glycosylation-dependent)
- **GPI anchor**: Tethered to the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane
- **Three-domain structure**:
  - Domain I (D1): Binds [[uPA]]
  - Domain II (D2): Binds vitronectin
  - Domain III (D3): GPI anchor attachment site
- **No transmembrane domain**: Signals through co-receptors (integrins, formyl peptide receptors)

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

### uPA-uPAR Interaction
- **uPA binding**: uPAR binds the growth factor domain of single-chain uPA (pro-uPA)
- **Plasminogen activation**: Localizes [[Plasminogen]] → [[Plasmin]] conversion to the cell surface
- **MMP activation**: Plasmin activates pro-MMPs, amplifying proteolytic cascades
- **PAI-1 regulation**: [[PAI-1]] inhibits uPA-uPAR complex, releasing it from the cell surface

### Downstream Signaling
- **Integrin co-signaling**: uPAR interacts with integrins (αvβ3, α5β1) to promote:
  - Cell migration and adhesion
  - Focal adhesion kinase (FAK) activation
  - Rac1/RhoA cytoskeletal remodeling
- **Formyl peptide receptors**: uPAR peptides (uPAR[84-95]) activate FPRL1 chemotactic signaling
- **ERK/MAPK**: Promotes cell proliferation and survival
- **PI3K/Akt**: Enhances cell survival and anti-apoptotic signaling

## Physiological Function

### Tissue Remodeling
- Concentrates proteolysis at sites of tissue repair
- Facilitates cell migration during wound healing
- Regulates ECM turnover and remodeling

### Immune Function
- Expressed on monocytes, macrophages, and neutrophils
- Facilitates immune cell migration to sites of inflammation
- Modulates phagocytic activity

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

### Cancer
- **Invasion and metastasis**: uPAR promotes cancer cell invasion through ECM degradation
- **Breast cancer**: High uPAR expression correlates with poor prognosis in [[Breast Cancer]]
- **Prostate cancer**: uPAR promotes [[Prostate Cancer|prostate cancer]] invasion and bone metastasis
- **Glioblastoma**: uPAR drives glioma cell migration and invasion

### SASP-Mediated Invasion
- uPAR is expressed by cells responding to SASP factors
- SASP proteases ([[uPA]], [[MMP-3]], [[MMP-9]]) are localized to the cell surface via uPAR
- Promotes [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine]] invasion of neighboring cancer cells
- Contributes to [[Tumor Microenvironment|tumor microenvironment]] remodeling

### Clinical Biomarker
- Soluble uPAR (suPAR) in serum is a biomarker for cancer prognosis
- uPAR expression predicts metastatic potential in multiple cancer types
- Therapeutic target: uPAR-targeted agents (e.g., ATN-658, a monoclonal antibody)

## Key Studies

- **SASP characterization**: uPAR expression is induced by SASP signals in senescent cells
- **Cancer prognosis**: High uPAR expression correlates with poor outcome in breast, colon, and lung cancers
- **Therapeutic targeting**: uPAR-targeted antibodies and peptides show anti-metastatic effects in preclinical models
