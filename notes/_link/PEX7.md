# PEX7

[[PEX7]] encodes the **Peroxisomal Targeting Signal 2 (PTS2) receptor**, a cytosolic shuttling receptor responsible for importing a smaller subset of peroxisomal matrix proteins bearing the N-terminal nonapeptide PTS2 signal (consensus: -R[LVI][LVI][LVI][STG][STG][STG][STG][STG][STG]-). Unlike [[PEX5]], which handles the vast majority of peroxisomal imports, PEX7 imports only a handful of enzymes, but these are critical for [[Fatty acid]] metabolism and bile acid synthesis. PEX7 also cooperates with PEX5L (the long isoform of [[PEX5]]) to import certain PTS2 substrates, providing functional redundancy.

## Structure & Domains

PEX7 is a 38-kDa protein containing **7WD40 (tryptophan-aspartate) repeats** arranged in a seven-bladed β-propeller structure. Key structural features include:

- **WD40 β-propeller domain** — forms the central cargo-binding pocket that recognizes the PTS2 nonapeptide. Each blade consists of a 4-stranded antiparallel β-sheet.
- **C-terminal extension** — mediates interaction with [[PEX5]] (for cooperative import) and with the peroxisomal membrane docking complex ([[PEX13]]/[[PEX14]]).
- **N-terminal region** — contains nuclear localization signals; PEX7 can shuttle between the nucleus and cytosol, though its primary function is cytosolic cargo recognition.

Unlike PEX5, PEX7 is not monoubiquitinated for recycling. Instead, it appears to be recycled through a distinct, less well-characterized mechanism that may involve direct dissociation from the membrane after cargo release.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

PEX7 operates through a catalytic cycle analogous to PEX5 but with distinct features:

1. **Cargo Recognition** — PEX7 binds PTS2-containing precursors in the [[Cytosol]]. The binding affinity is generally lower than PEX5-PTS1 interactions (~100 nM–1 μM range).
2. **Membrane Docking** — The PEX7-cargo complex docks at the peroxisomal membrane, primarily through [[PEX13]] and [[PEX14]], with additional support from the PEX5L isoform.
3. **Translocation & Cargo Release** — PTS2 signals are cleaved by [[Signal Peptidase]] inside the peroxisomal matrix, releasing the mature enzyme.
4. **Recycling** — PEX7 is recycled to the cytosol without ubiquitination, though the exact mechanism remains under investigation.

In humans, PEX7 functionally cooperates with PEX5L for importing several PTS2 substrates. This means that in some cell types, PEX5L can partially compensate for PEX7 loss, and vice versa — but this redundancy is tissue-specific and incomplete.

## Physiological Function

PEX7 imports a select but essential set of peroxisomal enzymes:

- **3-Ketoacyl-CoA Thiolase** (peroxisomal thiolase) — the terminal enzyme in peroxisomal [[Beta-Oxidation]] of branched-chain and very-long-chain fatty acids.
- **Alkyl-DHAP Synthase** — required for [[Plasmalogen]] biosynthesis (ether phospholipids essential for myelin).
- **Phytanoyl-CoA Hydroxylase (PHYH)** — catalyzes α-oxidation of [[Phytanic acid]], preventing its toxic accumulation.
- **Methylmalonyl-CoA Epimerase** — involved in propionate metabolism.

Loss of PEX7 function leads to accumulation of phytanic acid, very-long-chain fatty acids, and reduced plasmalogens — biochemically overlapping with but distinct from PEX5 deficiency.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Mutations in *PEX7* cause **PEX7-related Zellweger Spectrum Disorder (ZSD)**, typically manifesting as:

- **Rhizomelic Chondrodysplasia Punctata (RCDP) type 1** — the classic phenotype, characterized by shortening of proximal limbs (rhizomes), stippled epiphyses (chondrodysplasia punctata), cataracts, intellectual disability, and ichthyosis. RCDP type 1 is the most severe PEX7-related condition.
- **RCDP type 2 and 3** — allelic variants with milder phenotypes.
- **Atypical ZSD** — some PEX7 mutations cause Zellweger-like presentations with hepatic dysfunction and neurological impairment.

PEX7 mutations account for ~5-10% of ZSD cases. Common mutations include the founder allele c.727G>A (p.Gly243Arg) in European populations, which partially impairs PTS2 binding. Biallelic loss in mice causes severe growth retardation, cataracts, and early lethality.
