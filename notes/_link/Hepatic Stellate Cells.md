# Hepatic Stellate Cells

**Hepatic stellate cells (HSC)** are liver-specific pericytes located in the space of Disse between sinusoidal endothelial cells and hepatocytes. In their quiescent state, they store vitamin A (retinoid) droplets and regulate [[Extracellular Matrix|extracellular matrix]] turnover. Upon liver injury, HSC activate into proliferative, fibrogenic myofibroblasts that deposit collagen and contribute to [[Fibrosis|fibrosis]] (Schnabl et al., 2003, *Hepatology* 37:653-664; PMID 12601363).

## Senescence in HSC

Activated HSC can undergo [[Senescence|cellular senescence]], which serves as a self-limiting brake on fibrosis:

- Senescent HSC cease proliferation and reduce collagen production, limiting fibrotic scar formation (Krizhanovsky et al., 2008, *Cell* 134:657-667; PMID 18724938).
- However, senescent HSC also secrete a robust [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] — inflammatory cytokines ([[IL-1β]], [[IL-6]]), chemokines ([[CXCL1]]), growth factors, and matrix metalloproteinases ([[MMP]]) — that remodel the liver microenvironment.

## Role in Obesity-Associated HCC

Senescent HSC are a key driver of obesity-associated [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] through the gut microbiota–SASP–[[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis:

### Gut Microbiota-DCA Axis
[[Obesity]] alters the [[Gut Microbiome|gut microbiota]], increasing production of [[Deoxycholic Acid|deoxycholic acid (DCA)]], a secondary bile acid. DCA reaches the liver via the portal circulation and induces senescence in HSC (Yoshimoto et al., 2013, *Nature* 499:97-101; PMID 23803760).

### SASP-Driven Inflammation
Senescent HSC produce SASP factors that create a pro-inflammatory, pro-tumorigenic niche:
- [[IL-1β]]: Promotes inflammation and hepatocyte proliferation
- [[COX-2]] / [[Prostaglandin E2|PGE2]]: Suppresses anti-tumor immunity, enabling immune evasion (Loo et al., 2017, *Cancer Discov* 7:522-538; PMID 28202625)
- [[MMP]]: Remodels extracellular matrix, facilitating tumor invasion

### cGAS-STING Signaling
In senescent HSC, [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments|cytoplasmic DNA]] accumulates due to downregulation of [[DNASE2]] and [[TREX1]]. This activates the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], driving SASP factor transcription via [[NFκB]] and [[IRF3]]. Knocking out *[[STING]]* in mouse models blunts SASP production in HSC and attenuates obesity-associated HCC (Takahashi et al., 2018, *Nat Commun* 9:1249; PMID 29593264).

### TLR2 and Innate Sensing
[[Toll-like Receptor 2|TLR2]] on HSC recognizes [[Lipoteichoic Acid|lipoteichoic acid (LTA)]] from gram-positive bacteria (translocated due to obesity-related gut permeability) and [[DAMP|DAMPs]] such as serum amyloid A, further amplifying NF-κB-dependent SASP (Loo et al., 2017).

## Dual Role in Liver Disease

| Context | HSC State | Effect |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Acute injury | Transient senescence | Limits fibrosis, promotes tissue repair |
| Chronic obesity | Persistent senescence | SASP-driven inflammation, HCC promotion |
| Cirrhosis | Activation without senescence | Progressive fibrosis, liver failure |

## Therapeutic Targeting

- **Senolytics**: Drugs like [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]] and [[ABT-737]] can selectively eliminate senescent HSC, reducing SASP burden and HCC risk.
- **STING inhibitors**: Blocking cGAS-STING signaling in HSC may suppress SASP-driven inflammation.
- **Gut microbiota modulation**: Reducing DCA production (e.g., via diet, antibiotics, or bile acid sequestrants) may prevent HSC senescence.
