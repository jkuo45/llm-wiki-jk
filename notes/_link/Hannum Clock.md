# Hannum Clock

The Hannum clock is an epigenetic age estimator built by Hannum and colleagues that predicts chronological age from DNA [[Methylation]] at 71 CpG sites, trained predominantly on human whole-blood samples. It was among the first clocks shown to track age with high accuracy in blood and remains a standard reference for hematological and immune-aging studies.

## Construction & Properties

Constructed via penalized regression on blood methylation profiles, the Hannum clock reports a chronological-age estimate whose deviation ("age acceleration") correlates with [[Aging]] and [[Epigenetic Alterations]]. Its blood-specific training makes it sensitive to [[Immunosenescence]] and inflammatory states such as [[Inflammaging]].

## Relation to Other Clocks

The Hannum clock is commonly compared with the pan-tissue [[Horvath Clock]], the morbidity-linked [[PhenoAge]], and the rate-based [[DunedinPACE]]. Together these clocks operationalize [[Epigenome|epigenetic]] drift as a quantifiable hallmark of aging.

## Longevity Relevance

The Hannum clock is used to probe whether longevity strategies — [[Caloric Restriction]], [[Rapamycin]], [[Senolytics|senolytics]] — modulate blood epigenetic age, and to study links between [[Senescence]], [[Telomere]] attrition, and immune aging.

> [!note] Blood Bias
> Because it is trained on whole blood, the Hannum clock is less generalizable to solid tissues than the Horvath clock, but excels in immune and cohort studies.
