# MAPKAPK2

**Mitogen-activated protein kinase-activated protein kinase 2 (MAPKAPK2)**, also known as MK2, is a serine/threonine kinase and a direct substrate of [[p38 MAPK]]. It is the principal effector through which p38 controls the stability of labile inflammatory mRNAs.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

p38 MAPK phosphorylates MK2 on Thr222 (human), activating the kinase and relieving an intramolecular inhibitory interaction. Active MK2 is a master regulator of mRNA stability: it phosphorylates RNA-binding proteins that govern adenylate/uridylate-rich element (ARE)-containing transcripts. Key substrates include [[ZFP36L1]] (also known as TIS11B) and ZFP36/TTP, which normally bind AREs in the 3′-UTR of SASP mRNAs and recruit the deadenylase/decapping machinery to promote decay; MK2 phosphorylation inactivates these decay factors. MK2 also phosphorylates [[HSP27]] (HSPB1), AUF1 ([[AUF1]]/hnRNP D), and HuR, collectively shifting the equilibrium toward transcript stabilization. By suppressing decay, MK2 prolongs the half-life of cytokines, chemokines, and matrix remodelers — a post-transcriptional control layer complementary to NF-κB/STAT transcriptional activation.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

MK2 is a critical regulator of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] through its control of mRNA stability (Freund et al., 2011, PMID 21130033). In senescent cells, persistent [[p38 MAPK]] signaling keeps MK2 active, which stabilizes transcripts for [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], CXCL1/2, and [[MCP-1]], maintaining the inflammatory secretome of [[Senescent Cells]] even after transcriptional initiation wanes. Pharmacological inhibition of p38 or MK2, or MK2 silencing, sharply reduces SASP factor secretion without reversing the DNA-damage-induced cell-cycle arrest — hallmarks of a senomorphic mechanism operating at the post-transcriptional level. The p38–MK2 axis therefore acts as a translational/decay rheostat that determines the magnitude and duration of the SASP.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Because MK2 governs ARE-mRNA stability in innumerable inflammatory settings, p38–MK2 inhibitors have long been pursued for rheumatoid arthritis, atherosclerosis, and sepsis, and are now repurposed as senomorphics to dampen chronic SASP-driven [[Inflammation|inflammation]] and [[Inflammaging]]. Their selectivity for secretome modulation (versus cytotoxic senolysis) makes them attractive for treating senescence-associated pathologies such as fibrosis and osteoarthritis.
