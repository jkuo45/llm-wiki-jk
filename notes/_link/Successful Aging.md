# Successful Aging

**Successful aging** is a multidimensional concept describing the maintenance of health and function into old age. The influential Rowe & Kahn model (1997) defines it by three interacting components: (1) low probability of disease and disease-related disability, (2) high physical and cognitive functional capacity, and (3) active engagement with life, including social and productive activities.

## Conceptual Framework

- **Distinct from usual aging:** it separates the trajectory of high-functioning older adults from "usual" aging marked by accumulating [[Age-Related Diseases]] and decline.
- **Related constructs:** overlaps with healthy aging, active aging, and the biology-focused field of [[Geroscience]], which seeks to compress [[Morbidity]] by targeting the [[Hallmarks of Aging]].
- **Extremes:** [[Centenarians]] and supercentenarians are frequently studied as human models of successful aging, often showing delayed onset of chronic disease.

## Biological & Lifestyle Correlates

> [!info] Determinants
> Successful aging reflects the interplay of genetics, epigenetics, and modifiable factors — physical activity, diet ([[Caloric Restriction]]-like patterns), and social connection. A youthful, diverse [[Gut Microbiome]] and preserved [[Cognition]] are associated markers. It is a key outcome measure in [[Longevity]] research, shifting focus from lifespan alone toward [[Healthspan]] — years lived in good health and independence.
