# Rotenone

**Rotenone** is a naturally occurring isoflavonoid derived from the roots of *Derris* and *Lonchocarpus* plants, used historically as a broad-spectrum pesticide, insecticide, and piscicide.

## Chemical Properties & Mechanism of Action

- Highly lipophilic, allowing it to cross the blood–brain barrier and biological membranes readily.
- **Complex I inhibitor:** it binds the ubiquinone-reduction site of [[Respiratory Chain Complex I]] (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase), blocking electron transfer from iron–sulfur clusters to [[Ubiquinone]].
- **Consequences:** impaired [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] and ATP production, backed-up electrons that generate [[Superoxide]] and [[Mitochondrial ROS]], and resulting [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].

## Research Applications

> [!info] Parkinson's disease model
> Chronic low-dose rotenone reproduces key features of [[Parkinson's Disease]] — selective degeneration of nigral [[Dopaminergic Neurons]], [[Alpha-synuclein]] aggregation, and motor deficits — making it a standard model for studying environmental/mitochondrial contributions to neurodegeneration. It is also a routine laboratory tool to probe [[Electron Transport Chain]] function and ROS biology. Agents such as [[Methylene blue]] that reroute electrons around Complex I can partially rescue rotenone toxicity.

> [!warning] Toxicity
> Rotenone is acutely toxic; epidemiological studies associate occupational rotenone exposure with increased Parkinson's disease risk.
