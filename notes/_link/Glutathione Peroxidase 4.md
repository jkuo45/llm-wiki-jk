# Glutathione Peroxidase 4

**Overview:** [[Glutathione Peroxidase 4]] (GPX4) is a selenium-dependent [[Enzyme]] of the glutathione peroxidase family distinguished by its ability to reduce phospholipid hydroperoxides within cellular and organelle membranes—unlike most GPX isoforms, which act on soluble hydroperoxides.

**Structure and mechanism:** GPX4 is a monomeric selenoenzyme whose catalytic [[Selenium|selenocysteine]] (Sec) residue at the active site enables efficient peroxidase activity. It uses reduced [[Glutathione]] (GSH) as the electron donor to convert lipid hydroperoxides to harmless alcohols, thereby terminating the chain reaction of [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

**Physiological function:** By suppressing iron-dependent membrane peroxidation, GPX4 is the principal guardian against [[Ferroptosis]]—an iron-dependent, non-apoptotic cell death. Three isoforms localize to the cytoplasm/nucleus, mitochondria, and testis, supporting gametogenesis and tissue homeostasis.

**Pathological relevance:** Inactivation or depletion of GSH (e.g., via cystine–glutamate antiporter blockade by erastin) collapses GPX4 activity and triggers ferroptosis. This is leveraged therapeutically in some cancers, while GPX4 deficiency contributes to neurodegeneration, acute kidney injury, and ischemia–reperfusion damage. [[Oxidative Stress]] and iron overload synergize to overwhelm GPX4.
