# Bortezomib

**Chemical properties:** Bortezomib is a dipeptidyl boronic acid and the first proteasome inhibitor approved for clinical use. Its boron atom binds with high affinity to the catalytic threonine hydroxyl in the active site of the proteasome, forming a reversible tetrahedral adduct.

**Mechanism of action:** Bortezomib selectively and reversibly inhibits the chymotrypsin-like (β5) activity of the 26S [[Proteasome]], the multiprotein complex responsible for degrading ubiquitin-tagged proteins. Blocking proteasomal degradation causes accumulation of misfolded and regulatory proteins, triggering endoplasmic reticulum stress and the [[Unfolded Protein Response]]. In malignant plasma cells—which have exceptionally high protein synthesis and secretory load—this proteotoxic stress is lethal. Bortezomib also stabilizes the inhibitor IκB, thereby suppressing [[NF-κB]] pro-survival signaling, and shifts the balance of pro- and anti-apoptotic factors to promote [[Apoptosis]].

**Applications:** Bortezomib is a standard-of-care agent for [[Multiple Myeloma]] and mantle cell lymphoma, used in induction, consolidation, and maintenance regimens, frequently combined with immunomodulatory drugs and corticosteroids. It validated the proteasome as a druggable target and led to next-generation inhibitors such as carfilzomib and ixazomib.

**Safety and toxicity:** The most characteristic dose-limiting toxicity is peripheral neuropathy, which is reduced by subcutaneous rather than intravenous administration and weekly dosing. Other adverse effects include thrombocytopenia, gastrointestinal disturbance, fatigue, and herpes zoster reactivation. Resistance can arise through proteasome subunit mutations and upregulated stress-response pathways.
