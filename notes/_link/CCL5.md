# CCL5 (RANTES)

CCL5 (C-C motif chemokine ligand 5), also known as RANTES, is a CC chemokine that binds the receptors [[CCR5]], CCR1, and CCR3. It recruits T cells, eosinophils, monocytes, and NK cells and, as a SASP factor, links cellular senescence to both cancer progression and reproductive aging (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptor binding**: CCL5 engages [[CCR5]] (principal), CCR1, and CCR3 — all Gαi-coupled GPCRs that activate PI3K/Akt, [[p38 MAPK]]/ERK, and Ca2+ signaling for chemotaxis and adhesion.
- **Cell sources**: Secreted by T cells, macrophages, platelets, and, in senescence contexts, by therapy-induced senescent (TIS) cancer cells and senescent ovarian stromal cells.
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Induced by [[NF-κB]], [[AP-1]], and interferons (JAK/STAT), overlapping the inflammatory and interferon SASP branches.
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Recruits and activates leukocytes; in cancer cells it can trigger proliferative signaling through MYC–cyclin D pathways.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CCL5 is secreted by TIS cancer cells, where it activates MYC–cyclin D signaling in neighboring cancer cells to promote proliferation — contributing to non-small-cell lung cancer progression despite the senescence arrest.
- In reproductive aging, CCL5 released by senescent theca and interstitial cells of the ovary impairs preantral follicle development, linking the SASP to declining fertility.
- Thus CCL5 exemplifies the SASP as a double-edged signal: a senescence program that can stimulate tumorigenesis and accelerate ovarian aging via paracrine disruption.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CCL5/CCR5 is a therapeutic axis in HIV (maraviroc is a CCR5 antagonist) and is being targeted in cancer and inflammatory disease.
- CCR5 blockade is under study to blunt TIS-driven tumor promotion and senescence-associated reproductive decline.
- CCL5 is a candidate biomarker of senescence in reproductive tissues and of SASP burden in cancer.
