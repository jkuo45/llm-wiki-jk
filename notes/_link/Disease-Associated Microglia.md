# Disease-Associated Microglia

## Overview

[[Disease-Associated Microglia]] ([[Disease-Associated Microglia]]) denotes a stereotyped transcriptional and functional microglial activation state that emerges in the context of [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative disease]] and [[Neuroinflammation]]. DAM microglia are characterized by altered expression of homeostatic markers and engagement of phagocytic and lipid-metabolism programs, representing the brain's attempt to contain proteotoxic burden. The signature is regulated by cues such as [[TREM2]] and is widely used as a reference state for comparing disease-associated glial changes.

## Relationship to cGAS-STING Signaling

A critical refinement is that cGAS-STING activation drives a *separate* IFN-I–enriched microglial subpopulation that is distinct from the canonical DAM program. In tauopathy models, cGAS selectively expands an [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISG]]-high microglial population that correlates with synapse loss and cognitive impairment while leaving the core DAM signature largely unchanged. An analogous IFN-responsive microglial subset, distinct from DAM, has been identified in models of aberrant cGAS activation and in [[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]]. Thus, the cGAS-driven state and DAM are overlapping yet separable axes of microglial remodeling.

In [[Parkinson's Disease|PD]] models, cGAS promotes expansion of neurotoxic microglial populations, and the DAM signature itself is reduced by cGAS deletion — illustrating that the relationship is disease dependent. Major AD risk alleles [[APOE4]] and [[TREM2]] R47H synergistically amplify cGAS-STING/IFN-I signaling, driving a senescence-like microglial state marked by mitochondrial stress, [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle]] arrest, and a [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]].

## Functional Implications

While activated [[Microglia]] can engulf pathological aggregates and limit proteotoxicity, chronic activation is a major source of neurotoxicity through [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], excitotoxicity, and sustained cytokine release. Selective inhibition of [[cGAS]] or [[STING]] suppresses the neurotoxic inflammatory program while preserving — or even augmenting — phagocytic clearance, functionally uncoupling toxicity from beneficial clearance.
