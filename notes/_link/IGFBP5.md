# IGFBP5

**IGFBP5 (insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 5)** is a member of the IGFBP family that binds circulating and tissue [[IGF-1|insulin-like growth factors]] with high affinity, modulating their bioavailability and receptor engagement. It is a component of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] with roles in both senescence reinforcement and developmental patterning.

## Structure & Function

- Conserved N-terminal and C-terminal cysteine-rich domains bind IGF-1 and IGF-2, prolonging their half-life while regulating their access to the [[IGF1R|IGF-1 receptor]].
- Exerts both IGF-dependent and IGF-independent actions, the latter through interaction with the ECM, nuclear localization, and modulation of cell survival and differentiation.

## Mechanism of Action & Regulation

- By sequestering IGFs, IGFBP5 can dampen pro-survival [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] signaling, reinforcing senescence-associated growth arrest.
- IGFBP5 is induced during [[Senescence|senescence]] and can act in an autocrine/paracrine manner to promote and propagate the senescent state.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- IGFBP5 is a beneficial developmental SASP factor: during embryogenesis, programmed senescence produces SASP factors — including [[TGFβ]] and IGFBP5 — that contribute to neural tube and forelimb patterning and growth.
- In adult tissue, IGFBP5 participates in senescence reinforcement and matrix remodeling.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- IGFBP5 dysregulation is implicated in fibrosis (promoting ECM deposition), and it shows context-dependent tumor-suppressive or tumor-promoting roles across cancers.
- As an IGF-axis modulator, IGFBP5 intersects with the broader role of reduced IGF/insulin signaling in longevity.
