## Definition
Respiratory burst (also known as oxidative burst) is the rapid release of [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS), specifically [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]], from different cell types, primarily [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] and macrophages.


## Mechanism
The process is driven by the activation of [[NADPH Oxidase]] (NOX), which transfers electrons from NADPH to molecular oxygen to produce superoxide. In neutrophils, the enzyme [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO) further converts hydrogen peroxide into [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl), a potent antimicrobial agent.


## Role in Health and Disease
- **[[Immunity]]**: Essential for the killing of phagocytosed bacteria and fungi.
- **[[notes/_link/Inflammation]]**: Excessive or prolonged respiratory burst can lead to collateral tissue damage and chronic [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]].


## Connections
- [[NADPH Oxidase]]: The primary enzyme responsible for the burst.
- [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]]: The main cell type performing the burst during infection.
- [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]]: The enzyme that amplifies the toxicity of the burst.
- [[Hypochlorous Acid]]: A major product of the burst in neutrophils.
