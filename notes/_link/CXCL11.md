# CXCL11

CXCL11 (C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 11), also known as I-TAC (interferon-inducible T-cell alpha chemoattractant), is an ELR− C-X-C chemokine that binds [[CXCR3]] (and CXCR7) with the highest affinity of the CXCR3 ligands. It is an interferon-inducible T-cell and NK-cell chemoattractant and, in the context of therapy-induced senescence (TIS), acts as a pro-tumorigenic SASP factor that increases breast cancer aggressiveness (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptor binding**: CXCL11 is the most potent agonist of [[CXCR3]] and also binds CXCR7 (an atypical scavenger/decoy receptor). CXCR3 coupling to Gαi activates PI3K/Akt, [[p38 MAPK]]/ERK, and Ca2+ signaling to drive chemotaxis.
- **Cell sources**: Secreted by endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells under IFN (type I/II) and inflammatory stimulation; notably produced by TIS endothelial cells in the tumor vasculature.
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Induced via JAK/[[STAT1]] downstream of interferons, with contribution from [[NF-κB]]; the same DNA-damage/senescence programs that activate the interferon-SASP branch drive its expression.
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Recruits Th1/CD8+ T cells and NK cells, but in TIS it paradoxically remodels the tumor microenvironment to favor cancer cell migration and invasion.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CXCL11 is a component of the interferon-responsive SASP branch, co-induced with [[CXCL10]] (and CXCL9), but its net effect in TIS is pro-tumorigenic rather than protective.
- Secreted by TIS endothelial cells, CXCL11 increases breast cancer aggressiveness by stimulating paracrine signaling that promotes tumor cell motility and metastatic competence.
- This exemplifies the "dark side" of the SASP: a senescence program intended for tumor suppression can, through secreted chemokines, facilitate recurrence and therapy resistance.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CXCL11/CXCR3 signaling influences cancer immunotherapy outcomes and is linked to poor prognosis in breast and other cancers.
- It is a potential therapeutic target to prevent TIS-driven tumor promotion during chemotherapy/radiotherapy.
- Elevated CXCL11 is also seen in autoimmune and infectious diseases where Th1/NK trafficking is enhanced.
