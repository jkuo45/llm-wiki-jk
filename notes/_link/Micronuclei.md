# Micronuclei

## Overview

[[Micronuclei]] (singular: [[Micronuclei]]) are small, aberrant nuclear bodies that arise when chromosomes or chromosome fragments fail to segregate properly during mitosis and become enclosed in their own nuclear envelope. They are a visible manifestation of [[Genomic Instability]] and are commonly used as a biomarker of chromosomal missegregation and genotoxic stress. Because their envelope is often defective, micronuclei are prone to rupturing and exposing DNA to the cytoplasm, making them potent endogenous triggers of innate immune signaling.

## cGAS Sensing and Micronucleophagy

[[cGAS]] localizes to micronuclei to sense the exposed dsDNA within them. Upon engagement, cGAS recruits [[LC3]] to micronuclei, initiating a selective autophagic clearance process termed micronucleophagy, in which the micronucleus is targeted to autophagosomes for degradation. This intersection with [[Autophagy]] provides a containment mechanism for genomically stressed cells, linking DNA damage surveillance to lysosomal turnover. STING-enriched [[ERGIC|ERGICs]] supply membrane for LC3 lipidation during this process.

## Intercellular Transfer and Neuroinflammation

In the CNS, micronuclei can be transferred between [[Neuron|neurons]] and [[Microglia|microglia]], carrying DNA cargo that activates the recipient cell's [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] pathway. This intercellular propagation represents a mechanism by which neuronal [[DNA Damage|genomic stress]] is communicated to glial cells, reshaping microglial states and amplifying [[Neuroinflammation]]. Alongside [[mtDNA]] and [[cGAMP]], micronuclei rank among the nucleic acid cargos that couple neuronal stress to microglial innate immune activation in brain disorders.
