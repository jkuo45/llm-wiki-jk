# Myeloperoxidase

**Myeloperoxidase** (MPO) is a heme-containing enzyme primarily found in the azurophilic granules of [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]]. It plays a critical role in the innate immune system's ability to kill pathogens.


Myeloperoxidase (MPO) is a peroxidase enzyme that in humans is encoded by the MPO gene and is most abundantly expressed in [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]], where it produces [[Hypochlorous Acid]] from hydrogen peroxide and chloride ions.


## Role in [[Adrenochrome]] Formation
Myeloperoxidase can catalyze the [[Oxidation]] of [[notes/_link/Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. During the respiratory burst, [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] release MPO and produce [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS), creating a highly oxidative environment that favors the formation of [[Adrenochrome]] and other aminochromes.


## Function
MPO uses [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] to oxidize halide ions (like chloride) into potent oxidants such as hypochlorous acid (HOCl, the active ingredient in bleach), which are used to destroy bacteria and viruses.


## Clinical Relevance
- **[[notes/_link/Inflammation]]:** Elevated MPO levels are markers of neutrophil activation and systemic inflammation.
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** MPO-derived oxidants contribute to atherosclerosis and tissue damage during myocardial infarction.
- **[[Oxidative Stress]]:** Excessive MPO activity can lead to collateral damage to host tissues.
