# AICAR

**Chemical properties:** AICAR (5-aminoimidazole-4-carboxamide-1-β-D-ribofuranoside) is a cell-permeable nucleoside and an intermediate in the de novo purine biosynthesis pathway. Once inside cells it is phosphorylated by adenosine kinase to its monophosphate form, ZMP, which mimics the structure of AMP.

**Mechanism of action:** ZMP is an allosteric activator of [[AMPK]] (AMP-activated protein kinase), the central cellular energy sensor. By imitating a rise in the AMP:ATP ratio, AICAR switches on AMPK signaling without depleting cellular ATP. Activated AMPK stimulates catabolic, energy-generating pathways—including [[Glucose Uptake]] via GLUT4 translocation and [[Fatty Acid Oxidation]]—while suppressing anabolic processes and mTOR signaling. Downstream, AMPK activation promotes [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] partly through [[PGC-1α]].

**Research and therapeutic applications:** AICAR is a widely used pharmacological tool to probe AMPK-dependent metabolism and has been characterized as an "exercise mimetic" because it reproduces some endurance-training adaptations in skeletal muscle. Clinically (as acadesine) it has been investigated for cardioprotection during ischemia-reperfusion and in hematological malignancies. It intersects with NAD+/sirtuin biology through shared control of mitochondrial and metabolic programs.

**Safety and toxicity:** Because AICAR perturbs energy metabolism and purine handling, effects are dose- and context-dependent; it can alter glucose homeostasis and has been placed on anti-doping prohibited lists owing to its performance-related metabolic actions. Its safety profile depends on dose, duration, and tissue context.
