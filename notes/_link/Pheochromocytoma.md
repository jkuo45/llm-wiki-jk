# Pheochromocytoma

**Pheochromocytoma** is a rare neuroendocrine tumor of the [[Adrenal medulla|adrenal medulla]] that secretes excessive amounts of [[Catecholamines|catecholamines]] (primarily [[Epinephrine]] and [[Norepinephrine]]), leading to episodic hypertension, palpitations, headache, and diaphoresis.

## Pathophysiology

The tumor arises from chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla and can produce catecholamines autonomously or in response to physiological stimuli. The resulting catecholamine excess drives sustained or paroxysmal hypertension, cardiac arrhythmias, and [[Catecholamine-induced cardiomyopathy]]. The high local catecholamine concentration in the tumor microenvironment favors oxidation to [[Aminochromes]] including [[Adrenochrome]], contributing to oxidative damage within the tumor and surrounding tissue.

## Clinical Relevance

- Characteristic triad: episodic headache, palpitations, diaphoresis
- Associated with [[Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy|stress cardiomyopathy]] during catecholamine surges
- Surgical resection (laparoscopic adrenalectomy) is the definitive treatment
- Preoperative management with α-blockers (phenoxybenzamine) and β-blockers to prevent hypertensive crisis
- 10% rule: ~10% bilateral, ~10% extra-adrenal (paraganglioma), ~10% malignant, ~10% familial (MEN2, VHL, NF1)
- Elevated plasma metanephrines and normetanephrines are the gold-standard diagnostic biomarkers
