# PTEN

**[[PTEN]]** (Phosphatase and Tensin homolog) is a powerful tumor suppressor protein that is found in almost all tissues in the body. It acts as a "brake" on cellular growth and division.

### 1. The PI3K/Akt/[[notes/_link/mTOR]] Pathway
PTEN's primary function is to antagonize the PI3K/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway. By doing so, it:
*   **Inhibits Growth**: Prevents excessive cell proliferation.
*   **Promotes [[notes/_link/Autophagy]]**: By inhibiting mTOR, PTEN allows the cell to perform essential "cleanup" processes.

### 2. Role in [[notes/_link/Aging]] and Senescence
The activity and expression of PTEN are critical for a healthy lifespan:
*   **Tumor Suppression**: PTEN is one of the most frequently mutated genes in human [[notes/_link/Cancer]]. Its loss allows cells to divide uncontrollably.
*   **[[Cellular Senescence]]**: PTEN is a key regulator of the senescence response. While senescence helps prevent cancer, the accumulation of senescent cells (driven by chronic PTEN signaling in some contexts) contributes to aging.

### 3. Links to Longevity
Mice with an extra copy of the PTEN gene ("Super-PTEN" mice) are more resistant to cancer and show signs of delayed aging, highlighting its potential as a longevity-promoting factor.

### Connections
- **[[notes/_link/mTOR]]**: The primary pathway regulated by PTEN.
- **[[notes/_link/Aging]]**: PTEN is a central player in the balance between cancer prevention and biological decline.
- **[[notes/_link/Cancer]]**: PTEN is a major tumor suppressor.
- **[[notes/_link/MAPK]]**: PTEN can also cross-talk with the MAPK pathway to regulate cell survival.
