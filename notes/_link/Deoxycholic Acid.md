# Deoxycholic Acid

**Deoxycholic acid (DCA)** is a secondary [[Bile Acid|bile acid]] produced by gut bacterial 7α-dehydroxylation of cholic acid in the colon. It is a hydrophobic, detergent-like molecule that plays critical roles in dietary fat absorption and cholesterol homeostasis. Elevated DCA levels in the context of [[Obesity|obesity]] and high-fat diet have been strongly implicated in [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] pathogenesis through the induction of [[Senescence|cellular senescence]] in [[Hepatic Stellate Cells|hepatic stellate cells (HSC)]] (Yoshimoto et al., 2013, *Nature* 499:97-101; PMID 23803760).

## Biosynthesis and Regulation

DCA is formed from cholic acid by the action of bacterial 7α-dehydroxylase enzymes, primarily from the genus *Clostridium* (cluster XIVa) in the [[Gut Microbiome|gut microbiota]]. Its production is regulated by:
- **Diet**: High-fat diets increase bile acid secretion and DCA production
- **Gut microbiota composition**: Obesity alters microbial ecology, favoring DCA-producing bacteria
- **Enterohepatic circulation**: DCA is reabsorbed in the ileum and returned to the liver via the portal vein

## Role in HSC Senescence and HCC

DCA is the key molecular link between obesity-associated [[Gut Microbiome|gut dysbiosis]] and HCC development:

1. **Obesity** → altered [[Gut Microbiome|gut microbiota]] → increased DCA production
2. **DCA reaches the liver** via portal circulation
3. **DCA induces [[DNA Damage]]** and senescence in HSC
4. **Senescent HSC** secrete [[SASP|SASP factors]] ([[IL-1β]], [[COX-2]]/[[Prostaglandin E2|PGE2]]) via the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING signaling pathway]]
5. **SASP-driven inflammation** creates a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment promoting HCC

## Mechanism of Senescence Induction

DCA causes DNA damage in HSC through:
- Generation of [[Reactive Oxygen Species|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]]
- Direct genotoxic effects on nuclear DNA
- Activation of the [[DNA Damage Response]]

The resulting persistent [[DNA Damage]] signaling triggers HSC senescence, characterized by upregulation of [[p16 INK4a|p16]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21]], acquisition of [[SA-β-Gal|SA-β-gal]] activity, and secretion of SASP factors.

## Clinical Relevance

- Elevated serum DCA levels are observed in obese individuals and correlate with HCC risk.
- DCA levels can be reduced through dietary intervention, [[Antibiotics|antibiotics]], or [[Bile Acid Sequestrants|bile acid sequestrants]], all of which reduce HCC incidence in mouse models.
- Targeting the gut microbiota-DCA-HSC senescence axis is a promising strategy for preventing obesity-associated liver cancer.
