# Ribonuclease III

**Ribonuclease III (RNase III)** is a family of [[Enzyme|enzymes]] that specifically cleave double-stranded RNA (dsRNA). In animals, the two best-known RNase III enzymes are **[[Drosha]]** (nuclear, initiates [[MicroRNA|microRNA]] biogenesis) and **[[Dicer]]** (cytoplasmic, matures miRNAs and siRNAs).

## Family Features

- Contain one or two **RNase III domains** that dimerize to form a single catalytic processing center.
- Typically paired with a **[[Double-stranded RNA-binding domain|dsRNA-binding domain (dsRBD)]]** that positions the substrate.
- Class 2 RNase III enzymes (e.g., human Drosha) process structured RNA hairpins.

## Roles

- **[[Drosha]]** — with [[DGCR8]], forms the [[Microprocessor complex|Microprocessor]]; cleaves pri-miRNA → pre-miRNA in the nucleus.
- **[[Dicer]]** — with partner proteins (e.g., TRBP), cleaves pre-miRNA → ~22-nt mature miRNA duplex and long dsRNA → siRNA in the cytoplasm.

> [!note] Beyond miRNA
> Drosha also participates in ribosomal RNA processing (12S pre-rRNA → 5.8S rRNA), but this activity is independent of [[DGCR8]] and the Microprocessor.
