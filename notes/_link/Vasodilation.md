# Vasodilation

[[Vasodilation]] is the relaxation of vascular smooth muscle that widens blood vessels, lowers systemic vascular resistance, and increases local blood flow. It is the physiological opposite of [[Vasoconstriction]] and is essential for tissue perfusion, thermoregulation, and blood-pressure regulation.

## Mechanism

- **Endothelium-dependent (NO) pathway:** shear stress and agonists activate endothelial nitric-oxide synthase ([[eNOS]]) to produce [[Nitric Oxide]], which diffuses to smooth muscle and activates [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]], raising [[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate|cGMP]]. cGMP lowers intracellular [[Calcium Ions|calcium]] and drives relaxation.
- **Other mediators:** prostacyclin (cAMP-mediated), adenosine, bradykinin, and endothelium-derived hyperpolarizing factor.
- **Pharmacology:** nitrates, [[PDE5 Inhibitors]], and calcium-channel blockers exploit this pathway therapeutically.

## Clinical Relevance

> [!warning] Vasoplegia and shock
> Excessive, cytokine- and NO-driven vasodilation produces refractory [[Hypotension]] in [[Vasoplegic Shock]] and [[Septic Shock]]. Inducible nitric-oxide synthase (iNOS) generates massive [[Nitric Oxide]], overactivating the guanylyl-cyclase pathway. Interventions that interrupt this cascade — [[Methylene blue]] (inhibits NOS and [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]]) and [[Aminoguanidine]] (selective iNOS inhibitor) — restore vascular tone.
