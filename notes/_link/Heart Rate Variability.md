# Heart Rate Variability

[[Heart Rate Variability|Heart rate variability (HRV)]] is the variation in time (measured in milliseconds) between consecutive heartbeats. Although a nominal heart rate of 60 beats per minute implies one beat per second, the actual interbeat intervals fluctuate (e.g., 0.9 s, 1.1 s, 0.85 s). That fluctuation is HRV. Counterintuitively, a more variable heartbeat is a sign of robust health and fitness, whereas a metronome-like, low-variability rhythm signals chronic stress, fatigue, or illness.

## Physiology and the Autonomic Basis

HRV is controlled by the [[Autonomic Nervous System|autonomic nervous system (ANS)]], which balances two branches:

- The **[[Sympathetic Nervous System|sympathetic "fight, flight, or freeze" branch]]** releases adrenaline and cortisol, forcing the heart to beat faster and more rhythmically, which *lowers* HRV.
- The **[[Parasympathetic Nervous System|parasympathetic "rest, digest, and repair" branch]]**, governed largely by the [[Vagus Nerve|vagus nerve]], allows greater beat-to-beat fluctuation, which *raises* HRV.

A high HRV therefore reflects strong vagal (parasympathetic) tone and a nervous system that can rapidly brake stress, conferring cardiovascular flexibility and resilience.

## HRV as a Mitohormesis Dashboard

[[Mitohormesis]] depends on a dose-response relationship to stress: acute stressors (exercise, fasting, heat/cold) trigger [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial adaptation]], but adaptation occurs during the deep recovery that follows — not during the stressor itself.

- **Adaptive (Green) zone:** HRV at or above the personal baseline; the system is successfully adapting and can absorb another hormetic stressor.
- **Acute strain (Yellow) zone:** HRV 15–20% below baseline for 1–2 days; acute stress response, prioritize passive recovery.
- **Chronic under-recovery (Red) zone:** 7-day average HRV below baseline for 3+ consecutive days; the hormetic limit is exceeded and mitohormesis degrades into [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] and systemic [[Inflammation|inflammation]].

A sustained drop of ≥20% in average HRV is a far more insightful signal than any single raw value, because absolute HRV is highly individual and declines with age.

## Practical Tracking

Because HRV fluctuates with thoughts, breathing, and posture, a single daytime reading is largely useless.

- **Overnight tracking (recommended):** Wearables (Oura Ring, Whoop, Garmin) average HRV across the night, reflecting recovery from the prior day.
- **Morning snapshot:** Apple Watch or chest strap (e.g., Polar H10 + EliteHRV) taken supine, breathing normally for 2–3 min immediately on waking.

Plot a 14-day rolling average (baseline band) against daily waking HRV; occasional dips below the band are expected (the stressor), but persistent sub-baseline streaks signal the need to scale back.

> [!tip] Use HRV to time stressors
> Push fasted HIIT, lifting PRs, or extended cold exposure when HRV is at/above baseline. When significantly below baseline, swap intense work for Zone 2 cardio, walking, earlier feeding, sauna, or extra sleep to protect recovery.
