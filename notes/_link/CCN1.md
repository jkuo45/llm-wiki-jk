# CCN1

**CCN1 (cellular communication network factor 1)**, historically known as **CYR61 (cysteine-rich angiogenic inducer 61)**, is a secreted matricellular protein of the CCN family. Rather than acting as a structural matrix component, it modulates cell–matrix interactions by binding integrins and heparan sulfate proteoglycans. CCN1 is a beneficial, regenerative component of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].

## Structure & Function

- A cysteine-rich protein with four conserved modules (IGFBP-like, VWC, TSP1, and CT domains) that engage multiple integrins (αvβ3, αvβ5, α6β1) and heparan sulfate proteoglycans.
- Functions as an adhesion and signaling ligand that regulates cell proliferation, migration, differentiation, angiogenesis, and senescence.

## Mechanism of Action & Regulation

- CCN1 engagement of integrin α6β1 and heparan sulfate proteoglycans can induce fibroblast senescence by triggering [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] accumulation that activates the [[p53]] and [[p16INK4A|p16]]/RB pathways (Jun & Lau, 2010, *Nat Cell Biol*; PMID 20526329).
- Also activates integrin-linked kinase, FAK, and downstream MAPK/NF-κB signaling to regulate ECM remodeling and inflammation.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CCN1 is a beneficial SASP factor deployed in the early response to tissue damage: it recruits immune cells and stimulates the proliferation and differentiation of stromal cells to promote repair.
- By inducing senescence in activated myofibroblasts, CCN1 acts as a **feedback brake on fibrosis**, limiting excessive scar formation during wound healing.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CCN1-induced myofibroblast senescence promotes resolution of granulation tissue and limits fibrosis in skin and liver wound healing.
- Dysregulated CCN1 signaling is implicated in fibrotic disease, inflammation, and tumor progression, where context determines pro- or anti-tumorigenic effects.
