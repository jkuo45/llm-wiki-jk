# Circadian Rhythm

[[Circadian Rhythm|Circadian rhythms]] are endogenous ~24-hour oscillations in physiology and gene expression driven by a transcriptional-translational feedback loop. The core loop centers on the [[BMAL1]]/[[CLOCK]] heterodimer, which is tightly coupled to cellular energy state via [[SIRT1]] and [[NAD+|NAD⁺]].

## SIRT1 Coupling

SIRT1 deacetylates and activates [[BMAL1]] and [[CLOCK]], while [[NAMPT]]—a clock-controlled, BMAL1/CLOCK-transcriptional target and the rate-limiting [[NAD+|NAD⁺ salvage]] enzyme—generates circadian [[NAD+|NAD⁺]] oscillations that rhythmically gate SIRT1 activity.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - SIRT1 Protein roles at the nexus of health, disease, and therapeutics|SIRT1: Protein Roles at the Nexus (Chaqour, 2026)]]
> SIRT1 deacetylates [[PGC-1α]] and amplifies [[BMAL1]]/[[CLOCK]] expression; BMAL1/CLOCK drive [[NAMPT]], establishing a rhythmic NAD⁺ pattern that aligns SIRT1 activation with the circadian cycle. Age-related SIRT1 decline disrupts this loop.

## Significance

Disruption of the SIRT1–circadian axis is an early hallmark of premature aging, implicated in metabolic and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]].

## Connections

- [[BMAL1]] — Core clock TF; deacetylated/activated by SIRT1.
- [[CLOCK]] — Core clock TF; heterodimerizes with BMAL1, gated by SIRT1.
- [[SIRT1]] — Couples NAD⁺/energy state to circadian output via BMAL1/CLOCK deacetylation.
- [[NAMPT]] — Clock-controlled NAD⁺ salvage enzyme closing the SIRT1 circadian loop.
- [[NAD+]] — Oscillates circadianly to rhythmically activate SIRT1.
