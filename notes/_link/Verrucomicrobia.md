# Verrucomicrobia

**Verrucomicrobia** is a phylum of bacteria that are ubiquitous in soil, freshwater, and marine environments, and are also a consistent, low-abundance component of the human [[Gut Microbiome]], where they are represented primarily by the genus [[Akkermansia]].

## Taxonomy and Ecology
Verrucomicrobia are a candidate phylum of the domain Bacteria, phylogenetically distinct from Proteobacteria and Firmicutes. They are abundant in soils, sediments, and wetlands, where many members participate in carbon and methane cycling. In the gut, the predominant culturable representative is *Akkermansia muciniphila*, a mucin-degrading organism that colonizes the intestinal mucus layer.

## Physiology and Gut Role
*Akkermansia muciniphila* consumes host-derived mucus glycoproteins and is supported by [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] and other microbial metabolites. By occupying and partially degrading the inner mucus layer, it helps regulate mucus thickness and preserves barrier integrity. Higher abundance of *A. muciniphila* is consistently associated with metabolic health, whereas depletion is observed in [[Obesity]], [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome]].

## Relevance to Aging and Longevity
Aging is accompanied by microbial shifts (dysbiosis) that correlate with low-grade [[Inflammation]] ("inflammaging"). *Akkermansia* abundance tends to decline with age, and interventions that increase it — polyphenol-rich foods, [[Urolithin A]], and [[Spermidine]] — are studied for metabolic and [[Longevity]] benefits. Preclinical work reports improved glucose tolerance, reduced adiposity, and attenuated systemic inflammation.

> [!note] Relationships
> Verrucomicrobia are microbial organisms; their relevance to human health is mediated mainly through *Akkermansia muciniphila* and its effects on the gut barrier and host metabolism.
