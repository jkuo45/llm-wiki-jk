# Interferon-gamma

**Interferon-gamma** (IFNγ) is the sole member of the type II interferon family, a pro-inflammatory cytokine produced primarily by [[CD8+ T cell|CD8⁺ T cells]], CD4⁺ Th1 cells, and [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]]. It plays a central role in both innate and adaptive immunity against intracellular pathogens and in tumor immune surveillance.

## Signaling Pathway

IFNγ signals through the IFNγ receptor (IFNGR), which consists of two ligand-binding IFNGR1 chains and two signal-transducing IFNGR2 chains. Engagement activates the [[JAK]] kinases (JAK1, JAK2), which phosphorylate [[STAT1]] (Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 1). Phosphorylated STAT1 homodimers (gamma-activated factor, GAF) translocate to the nucleus and bind gamma-activated sequences (GAS) in the promoters of hundreds of interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs).

## Key Functions

- **Macrophage activation:** IFNγ is the classical M1 [[Macrophage]] activator, inducing [[Respiratory Burst]], MHC class II expression, and production of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and [[Nitric Oxide]].
- **[[IDO1]] induction:** IFNγ is the most potent transcriptional inducer of [[IDO1]] via the [[JAK]]/[[STAT1]] pathway, linking immune activation to [[Tryptophan]] depletion and [[Kynurenine Pathway]] flux.
- **Antiviral and antimicrobial defense:** Induces a broad range of antiviral effectors including [[IFITM3]], [[ISG15]], and [[CXCL10]].
- **Antigen presentation:** Upregulates MHC class I and class II molecules, enhancing antigen presentation.
- **Immune regulation:** Drives Th1 polarization and suppresses Th2 responses.

## Clinical Relevance

IFNγ is central to defense against intracellular bacteria (e.g., *Mycobacterium*), viruses, and tumors. IFNγ-[[IDO1]]-[[Kynurenine]]-[[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor|AhR]] axis is a major immunosuppressive pathway in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]]. IFNγ therapy (e.g., Actimmune) is used in chronic granulomatous disease and severe malignant osteopetrosis.
