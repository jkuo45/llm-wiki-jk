# Respiratory Chain Complex I

**Complex I** (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) is the first and largest enzyme of the [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] [[Electron Transport Chain]], embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

## Structure & Mechanism

- **L-shaped assembly** of ~45 subunits (14 core, conserved from bacteria) with a hydrophilic peripheral arm and a membrane arm.
- **Redox chemistry:** oxidizes [[NADH]] at a flavin mononucleotide (FMN) site, passing electrons through a chain of iron–sulfur clusters to reduce [[Ubiquinone]] (coenzyme Q) to ubiquinol.
- **Proton pumping:** the exergonic electron transfer drives translocation of ~4 H⁺ across the membrane, contributing to the [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] that powers [[ATP Synthase]].
- **ROS production:** electron leak at the FMN and Q sites makes Complex I a principal source of [[Superoxide|superoxide]] and [[Mitochondrial ROS]].

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

> [!info] Aging, disease, and inhibitors
> Complex I activity declines with age and contributes to [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Oxidative Stress]]. Genetic deficiency causes Leigh syndrome and mitochondrial encephalopathies, and Complex I inhibition is central to [[Parkinson's Disease]] models. The pesticide [[Rotenone]] and the biguanide [[Metformin]] both inhibit Complex I, while [[Methylene blue]] can act as an alternative electron carrier bypassing a defective complex.
