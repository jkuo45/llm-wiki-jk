# TNFα

**Tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα)** is a pleiotropic pro-inflammatory cytokine produced primarily by activated [[Macrophages]], [[T Cells]], and [[Natural Killer Cells]]. It is a master regulator of the inflammatory response and a central mediator of both acute and chronic inflammation.

## Structure

TNFα is synthesized as a 233-amino acid [[Type II Transmembrane Protein]] (26 kDa) forming homotrimers. The transmembrane form (mTNF) is cleaved by [[TACE]]/[[ADAM17]] to release soluble TNFα (sTNF, 17 kDa), which also forms bioactive homotrimers. Both forms signal through two distinct [[Receptors]]:

- **[[TNFR1]] (p55)** — expressed on nearly all cell types; signals primarily through [[TRADD]] to activate [[NFκB]] and [[MAPK]] pathways and, under certain conditions, [[Apoptosis]] via [[Caspase-8]]
- **[[TNFR2]] (p75)** — expressed mainly on [[Endothelial cells]], [[T Cells]], and [[Neurons]]; signals through [[TRAF2]] to activate [[NFκB]] and [[PI3K]]-[[Akt]]

## Signaling Pathways

### NF-κB Activation
TNFα binding to [[TNFR1]] recruits [[TRADD]], [[RIPK1]], and [[TRAF2]]. [[TRAF2]] activates the [[IKK complex]] ([[IKKα]], [[IKKbeta]], [[IKKγ]]/[[NEMO]]), which phosphorylates [[IkappaBalpha]], targeting it for [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System|proteasomal degradation]]. Released [[NFκB]] (p50-[[p65]] heterodimer) translocates to the [[Nucleus]] and transcriptionally activates hundreds of target genes including pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and anti-apoptotic factors.

### MAPK Signaling
TNFα activates the [[JNK]], [[p38 MAPK]], and [[ERK]] cascades via [[TRAF2]]-[[ASK1]]-[[MKK4/7]] signaling, regulating [[AP-1]] transcription factor activity.

### Apoptosis
Under conditions of [[NFκB]] blockade or [[Protein Synthesis]] inhibition, TNFα signaling switches from survival to death. [[RIPK1]] deubiquitination promotes formation of Complex II ([[FADD]]-[[Caspase-8]]), triggering [[Apoptosis]] via [[Caspase-3]] activation. If caspases are inhibited, [[RIPK3]]-dependent [[Necroptosis]] can occur.

## Biological Functions

- **[[Inflammation]]** — TNFα induces [[Interleukin 1β]], [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], [[COX-2]], and adhesion molecule ([[ICAM-1]], [[VCAM-1]]) expression
- **Immune Activation** — promotes [[Dendritic Cell]] maturation, [[Macrophage]] activation, [[T Cell]] proliferation, and [[Natural Killer Cell]] cytotoxicity
- **[[Acute Phase Response]]** — stimulates [[Liver]] to produce [[CRP]], [[Serum Amyloid A]], and [[Fibrinogen]]
- **[[Apoptosis]]** — kills tumor cells and virus-infected cells
- **[[Cachexia]]** — chronic TNFα causes muscle wasting and [[Sarcopenia]]
- **[[Insulin Resistance]]** — TNFα inhibits [[IRS1]] signaling via [[JNK]]-mediated serine phosphorylation

## Clinical Relevance

### TNFα Inhibitors (Biologics)
Therapeutic blockade of TNFα has revolutionized the treatment of autoimmune and inflammatory diseases:

| Drug | Type | Targets |
|---|---|---|
| [[Infliximab]] | Chimeric mAb | sTNF + mTNF |
| [[Adalimumab]] | Human mAb | sTNF + mTNF |
| [[Etanercept]] | TNFR2-Fc fusion | sTNF |
| [[Certolizumab]] | Pegylated Fab | sTNF + mTNF |
| [[Golimumab]] | Human mAb | sTNF + mTNF |

### Indications
- [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]]
- [[Psoriasis]] and [[Psoriatic Arthritis]]
- [[Ankylosing Spondylitis]]
- [[Crohn's Disease]] and [[Ulcerative Colitis]]
- [[Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis]]

### Adverse Effects
Anti-TNF therapy increases risk of [[Tuberculosis]] reactivation, opportunistic infections, and malignancies. Paradoxically, it can induce [[Lupus]]-like syndrome and [[Demyelinating Disease]].

### Elevated TNFα in Disease
- [[Sepsis]] — TNFα is a primary mediator of septic shock
- [[Metabolic Syndrome]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] — TNFα drives [[Insulin Resistance]]
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — TNFα promotes [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] and [[Foam Cell]] formation
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]] — TNFα contributes to neuroinflammation and [[Tau]] pathology
- [[Cachexia]] in [[Cancer]] — TNFα is a key [[Cachectin]]
