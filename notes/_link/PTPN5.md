# PTPN5

## Overview
[[PTPN5]] (protein tyrosine phosphatase non-receptor type 5), also known as STEP (striatal-enriched protein tyrosine phosphatase), is a non-receptor tyrosine phosphatase that dephosphorylates and activates [[BAK]] by removing the inhibitory phosphate at Tyr108. This dephosphorylation "licenses" BAK for activation, representing a critical control point in mitochondrial apoptosis.

## Structure & Activation
PTPN5 contains an N-terminal KIM (kinase interaction motif) domain that targets it to specific kinases (e.g., ERK1/2, p38), and a C-terminal catalytic phosphatase domain. PTPN5 is regulated by:
- **Phosphorylation at Ser221 by ERK1/2**: Inactivates PTPN5, preventing it from dephosphorylating BAK
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Downregulated in many cancers
- **Proteasomal degradation**: PTPN5 is targeted for degradation in some tumor types

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **BAK Licensing**: PTPN5 dephosphorylates BAK at Tyr108, removing the inhibitory phosphate and allowing BAK to undergo conformational activation. This is the "licensing" step that permits BAK to respond to BH3-only activators (e.g., Bid, Bim).

> [!warning] K-RAS/ERK Blockade of BAK Activation
> In K-RAS mutated cancers (e.g., colon cancer), hyperactive ERK1/2 phosphorylates and **inactivates PTPN5**. This locks BAK in its inactive, phosphorylated state at Tyr108, providing a profound resistance to apoptosis. This mechanism directly links RAS pathway mutations to apoptotic resistance.

- **Neuronal Function**: In the brain, PTPN5 regulates synaptic plasticity by dephosphorylating ERK1/2 and other substrates in striatal neurons.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer**: Genetic loss or downregulation of PTPN5 (and related PTPN2, PTPN23) is common in T-cell leukemias and epithelial cancers, raising the threshold for BAK activation and contributing to apoptotic resistance.
- **Neurodegeneration**: PTPN5 dysregulation is implicated in Huntington's disease and Alzheimer's disease, where aberrant ERK signaling contributes to neuronal dysfunction.
- **Therapeutic Potential**: Restoring PTPN5 activity or directly dephosphorylating BAK Tyr108 could sensitize resistant tumors to apoptosis.
