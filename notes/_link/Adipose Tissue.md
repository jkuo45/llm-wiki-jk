# Adipose Tissue

**Overview:** Adipose tissue is a specialized loose connective tissue composed predominantly of lipid-laden adipocytes, together with preadipocytes, vascular cells, and resident immune cells such as [[Macrophages]]. Beyond energy storage, it is now recognized as a major endocrine organ that coordinates whole-body metabolism, appetite, and inflammation.

**Types and organization:** White adipose tissue (WAT) stores energy as triglycerides in a single large lipid droplet and secretes adipokines. [[Brown Adipose Tissue]] (BAT) is rich in mitochondria and uncoupling protein 1 (UCP1), dissipating energy as heat through non-shivering thermogenesis. "Beige" adipocytes can emerge within WAT depots in response to cold or exercise, acquiring thermogenic properties.

**Endocrine and metabolic function:** Adipocytes secrete [[Leptin]] (signaling satiety and energy stores) and [[Adiponectin]] (enhancing insulin sensitivity and fatty-acid oxidation), as well as free fatty acids and numerous cytokines. Adipose tissue buffers postprandial lipid flux, and its lipolytic and lipogenic balance is governed by insulin, catecholamines, and nutrient-sensing pathways including [[AMPK]].

**Pathological relevance:** Excess or dysfunctional adipose tissue drives [[Obesity]], chronic low-grade inflammation, [[Insulin Resistance]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome]]. Hypertrophic adipocytes recruit inflammatory macrophages and secrete pro-inflammatory adipokines, linking adiposity to [[Diabetes Mellitus]] and cardiovascular disease. Age-related redistribution and dysfunction of adipose depots also contribute to metabolic decline, making it a target for longevity and metabolic interventions.
