# EPR spectroscopy

**Electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy**, also known as electron spin resonance (ESR), is a technique that detects species with unpaired [[Electron|electrons]] (paramagnetic species), including [[Free Radicals]], [[Transition Metal Ions]], and [[Reactive Oxygen Species]].

## Principle

EPR measures the resonant absorption of [[Microwave]] radiation by unpaired electrons in an external [[Magnetic Field]]. The energy difference between electron spin states (m_s = ±½) is proportional to the magnetic field ([[Zeeman Effect]]). The [[g-factor]] and [[Hyperfine Coupling]] to nearby magnetic nuclei provide structural information about the radical species.

## Applications

- [[Spin Trapping]] — detection of short-lived radicals ([[Hydroxyl radical]], [[Superoxide]]) using [[DMPO]], [[POBN]], or [[DEPMPO]]
- [[Redox Biology]] — quantification of [[ROS]] and antioxidant capacity in biological samples
- [[Metalloprotein]] analysis — oxidation states of [[Iron-sulfur clusters]], [[Copper centers]], [[Manganese]]
- [[Oximetry]] — measurement of [[Oxygen]] concentration via line broadening
- [[Aging Research]] — direct detection of free radical production in mitochondria
