# Apigenin

**Apigenin** is a naturally occurring flavone (4′,5,7-trihydroxyflavone) abundant in parsley, celery, chamomile, and many citrus fruits. It has potent anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and senotherapeutic properties.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

Apigenin is a planar polyphenolic compound that acts as an ATP-competitive inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinases. It binds the ATP pocket of [[CDK2]], [[CDK4]], and [[CDK6]], blocking their association with cyclins and arresting cells at the G1/S or G2/M checkpoint. This inhibition upregulates the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor [[p21|p21/CDKN1A]] and stabilizes [[p53]], engaging p53-dependent transcription and growth arrest (Yousefzadeh & Niedernhofer; flavonoid CDK inhibition, PMID 22037824). Apigenin additionally dampens PI3K/Akt and several receptor tyrosine kinases, blunting proliferative and survival signaling, and suppresses the [[NF-κB]] and [[p38 MAPK]] cascades that drive inflammatory gene expression.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

Apigenin displays dual senotherapeutic activity. As a [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic]] it can selectively eliminate senescent cells — an activity shared by several flavonoids (fisetin, quercetin). More consistently, apigenin behaves as a [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] agent: it suppresses the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] by inhibiting NF-κB–dependent expression of [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], and other cytokines without killing the cell, making it useful for damping chronic sterile inflammation ([[Inflammaging]]) in aging.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Apigenin has low oral bioavailability but a favorable tolerability profile, and is under investigation for chemoprevention, neurodegeneration, and as a senomorphic adjunct. Its CDK-inhibitory and p53-stabilizing actions parallel pharmacological [[CDK]] inhibitors in clinical development, supporting its repositioning as a natural senotherapeutic.
