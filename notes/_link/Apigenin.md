# Apigenin

**Apigenin** is a naturally occurring [[Flavones|flavone]] (4′,5,7-trihydroxyflavone; C~15~H~10~O~5~, MW 270.24 Da) abundant in parsley, celery, [[Chamomile]], and many citrus fruits. It has potent anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anticancer, and senotherapeutic properties.

## Sources & Bioavailability

Apigenin occurs naturally as apigenin-7-*O*-glucoside and acylated derivatives; the richest dried source is parsley (≈45,035 μg/g), followed by chamomile flowers (3,000–5,000 μg/g) and celery seeds (≈787 μg/g). It is poorly water-soluble and, after oral intake, is rapidly metabolized by [[UDP-glucuronosyltransferase|UGT1A1]] into glucuronide/sulfate conjugates with limited and often unmeasurable plasma levels. Nonetheless, its long blood half-life (~91.8 h in rats) supports slow tissue accumulation, and nano-formulations (lipidic, gold, polymeric) are under investigation to improve delivery.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

Apigenin is a planar polyphenolic compound that acts as an ATP-competitive inhibitor of cyclin-dependent kinases. It binds the ATP pocket of [[CDK2]], [[CDK4]], and [[CDK6]], blocking their association with cyclins and arresting cells at the G1/S or G2/M checkpoint. This inhibition upregulates the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor [[p21|p21/CDKN1A]] and stabilizes [[p53]], engaging p53-dependent transcription and growth arrest. Apigenin additionally dampens [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] and several receptor tyrosine kinases, blunting proliferative and survival signaling, and suppresses the [[NF-κB]] and [[p38 MAPK|p38]]/[[MAPK]] cascades that drive inflammatory gene expression.

Beyond CDKs, apigenin is a broad multikinase/ signaling modulator. It:
- Blocks [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], [[NF-κB]], [[STAT3]], [[ERK]], [[JNK]], and [[Src]] survival/inflammatory signaling.
- Inhibits [[CK2]] (casein kinase 2), reducing Ikaros degradation and cancer stem-cell self-renewal.
- Induces proteasomal degradation of [[HER2|HER2/neu]] in HER2-overexpressing breast cancer.
- Acts as a dual epigenetic modulator — a [[Histone Deacetylase|HDAC inhibitor]] (targets [[HDAC1]]/[[HDAC3]]) and DNA-methyltransferase inhibitor — increasing p21/Waf1 promoter acetylation.
- Inhibits [[Fatty Acid Synthase]], [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|MMPs]] ([[MMP-2]], [[MMP-9]]), [[VEGF]]/[[HIF-1α]]-driven angiogenesis, and topoisomerase II.
- Binds the glycine-rich domain of hnRNPA2, disrupting RNA splicing.

## Anticancer Activity Across Malignancies

A comprehensive 2017 review ([^1]) catalogs apigenin targets across >18 human cancers. Representative actions:

- **[[Breast Cancer]]**: Proteasomal degradation of [[HER2|HER2/neu]], PI3K/Akt blockade, STAT3 inhibition, autophagy-coupled apoptosis, PD-L1 suppression, and synergy with [[Chrysin]].
- **[[Prostate Cancer]]**: G1 arrest via cyclin D1/CDK downregulation, AR suppression, NF-κB and IGF-I/[[IGFBP-3]] pathway inhibition, and Ku70-mediated Bax release.
- **[[Colon Cancer]]**: G2/M arrest, p21/Waf1 induction (p53-dependent and -independent), TRAIL/DR5 death-receptor activation, and APC involvement.
- **[[Lung Cancer]]**: HIF-1α/VEGF suppression, radiosensitization, GLUT1 metabolic stress, and TRAIL synergy.
- **[[Pancreatic Cancer]]**: Gemcitabine/cisplatin sensitization via Akt/NF-κB inhibition; Ikaros stabilization through CK2 downregulation.
- **[[Gastric Cancer]]**, **[[Liver Cancer]]**, **[[Bladder Cancer]]**, **[[Cervical Cancer]]**, **[[Ovarian Cancer]]**, **[[Skin Cancer]]**, **[[Thyroid Cancer]]**, **[[Neuroblastoma]]**, **[[Leukemia]]**, **[[Lymphoma]]**, **[[Mesothelioma]]**, **[[Osteosarcoma]]**, and others — uniformly showing growth inhibition, cell-cycle arrest, and apoptosis through the pathways above.

> [!tip] Chemosensitizer
> Apigenin potentiates [[Gemcitabine]], [[Cisplatin]], [[Doxorubicin]], [[Paclitaxel]], [[Curcumin]], and [[TRAIL]] by disabling survival signaling and resensitizing resistant/stem-cell populations.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

Apigenin displays dual senotherapeutic activity. As a [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic]] it can selectively eliminate senescent cells — an activity shared by several flavonoids (fisetin, quercetin). More consistently, apigenin behaves as a [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] agent: it suppresses the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] by inhibiting NF-κB–dependent expression of [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], and other cytokines without killing the cell, making it useful for damping chronic sterile inflammation ([[Inflammaging]]) in aging.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Epidemiological and case-control studies link higher flavonoid intake (including apigenin) with reduced risk of gastric, colorectal, breast, ovarian, and endometrial cancers. Small human trials show chamomile-extract (apigenin-standardized) benefits for anxiety/depression and reduced colorectal adenoma recurrence with a flavonoid mixture; however, human pharmacokinetic and safety data remain limited, precluding broad clinical use. Its CDK-inhibitory, p53-stabilizing, and epigenetic actions parallel pharmacological agents in clinical development.
