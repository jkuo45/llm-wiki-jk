# Nucleus Accumbens

**Nucleus Accumbens (NAc)** is a limbic–motor interface region of the ventral [[Striatum]], forming the heart of the brain's reward circuitry. It integrates motivational, emotional, and cognitive signals to direct goal-directed and habitual behavior.

## Structure

The NAc comprises a core and shell, each with distinct inputs and outputs. Its principal cells are GABAergic medium spiny neurons, segregated into D1-receptor "direct pathway" and D2-receptor "indirect pathway" populations that oppositely gate action selection. It receives massive glutamatergic input from the prefrontal cortex (via cortical–limbic afferents), hippocampus, and amygdala, and its defining [[Dopamine]] input arrives from the [[Ventral Tegmental Area|ventral tegmental area (VTA)]] as the mesolimbic dopamine pathway.

## Function

The NAc translates motivational value into action: dopaminergic signaling there encodes reward prediction, salience, and reinforcement, driving learning and approach behavior. It is indispensible for natural rewards (food, social contact) and is hijacked in addiction, where drugs produce supraphysiological dopamine surges that over-train cue–reward associations. Dysfunction contributes to [[Depression]] (anhedonia, reduced motivation), compulsive behaviors, and mood disorders.

## Relevance

As a hub linking emotion, cognition, and movement, the NAc is a key target for understanding motivation, addiction, and affective disease, and for interventions ranging from deep-brain stimulation to pharmacotherapy.
