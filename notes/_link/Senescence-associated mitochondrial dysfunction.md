# Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction

**Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction (SAMD)** refers to the impairment of mitochondrial structure and function that is a hallmark of [[Senescent Cells]].

## Key Features
- **Reduced Oxidative Phosphorylation (OXPHOS)**: Decreased efficiency of the electron transport chain.
- **Increased Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) Production**: Leading to further cellular damage and [[DNA Damage]].
- **Impaired Mitophagy**: Failure to clear damaged mitochondria.
- **Minority MOMP (miMOMP)**: Sublethal mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization that releases [[mtDNA]] into the cytosol without triggering [[Apoptosis]].

## Role in Senescence
SAMD is not just a consequence of senescence but a driver of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]]:
- Released [[mtDNA]] activates the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] pathway.
- Mitochondrial ROS can activate [[NFKB|NF-κB]] and other stress-sensitive signaling pathways.
- Metabolic rewiring in senescent cells supports the high secretory demand of the SASP.

## Therapeutic Targeting
Targeting mitochondrial dysfunction and miMOMP is an emerging strategy to suppress SASP and improve healthspan.
