# Redox Homeostasis

[[Redox Homeostasis]] is the tightly regulated steady state between the production of reactive oxygen/nitrogen species and the capacity of [[Antioxidant]] defenses to neutralize them. Far from mere damage control, the redox balance is a **signaling setpoint** that tunes proliferation, metabolism, and stress adaptation.

## Components

- **Pro-oxidant sources**: [[Mitochondrial ROS]] from the electron transport chain, [[NADPH]] oxidase, peroxisomes, and [[Lipopolysaccharide|LPS]]-driven immune bursts.
- **Antioxidant systems**: [[Glutathione]] (GSH/GSSG), thioredoxin, superoxide dismutase, catalase, and vitamins C/E; redox buffered by the [[NAD+]]/NADH and NADP⁺/NADPH couples.
- **Thiol redox**: Protein cysteine thiols act as redox sensors; their oxidation state reports cellular redox potential.

## Mechanism & Regulation

- **Signaling vs damage**: Low/moderate ROS act as second messengers (e.g., activating [[AMPK]], [[FOXO]], [[NF-κB]]); excess overwhelms buffers → [[Oxidative Stress]] and macromolecular damage.
- **Hormetic tuning**: Mild oxidant exposure induces adaptive antioxidant responses (mitohormesis) — a cornerstone of [[Aging]] intervention strategies.
- **NAD+ coupling**: The [[NAD+]]/NADH ratio links redox state to sirtuin-mediated [[Deacetylation]] and metabolic regulation.

> [!tip] Homeostasis, not zero ROS
> Optimal health requires a *set point*, not elimination of ROS. Complete antioxidant suppression blunts adaptive hormesis; the goal is balanced redox tone.

## Physiological & Clinical Relevance

- **Aging**: Progressive redox imbalance ("redox stress") is a hallmark of [[Aging]] and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].
- **Disease**: Imbalance contributes to neurodegeneration, cancer, and metabolic disease; restoring redox homeostasis is a therapeutic aim.
