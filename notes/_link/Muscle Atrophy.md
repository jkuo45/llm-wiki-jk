# Muscle Atrophy

## Overview

**Muscle atrophy** is the progressive loss of [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] mass, cross-sectional area, and strength. It arises whenever net muscle protein balance becomes negative—i.e., protein degradation outpaces [[Muscle Hypertrophy|protein synthesis]]. Atrophy is a hallmark of [[Sarcopenia|sarcopenia]] (age-related), cachexia, disuse (immobilization/bed rest), denervation, and numerous chronic diseases.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Atrophy reflects coordinated molecular reprogramming:

- **Suppressed synthesis:** Inhibition of the [[mTORC1|mTORC1]] / [[PI3K]]–[[Akt]] axis reduces translational initiation and ribosome biogenesis.
- **Activated degradation:** The [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System|ubiquitin–proteasome system]] is upregulated via transcription factors of the [[FOXO|FoxO]] family, inducing the E3 ligases [[Atrogin-1|Atrogin-1 (MAFbx)]] and [[MuRF1|MuRF1 (Muscle RING-finger 1)]].
- **Autophagy/mitophagy:** Excessive [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] and lysosomal flux can remove [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] and contractile components.
- **Inflammation & oxidative stress:** [[TNF-alpha|TNF-α]], [[NFκB|NF-κB]] signaling, and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] amplify catabolism.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

Manifestations include reduced muscle volume, weakness, fatigability, and impaired mobility. Molecular biomarkers include elevated circulating [[Atrogin-1]]/[[MuRF1]] expression, myofiber atrophy, and shifts toward fast-to-slow or pathological fiber typing. MRI/DEXA quantify lean mass loss.

## Therapeutic Landscape

- **Exercise / loading:** Resistance training is the most effective anabolic stimulus, activating [[mTORC1]] and suppressing atrophy programs.
- **Nutritional support:** Adequate protein/[[Leucine|leucine]] and [[Creatine|creatine]] maintain synthesis.
- **Pharmacological & nutraceutical:** [[Urolithin A]] has shown potential to stimulate muscle protein synthesis while inhibiting degradation pathways ([[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]], [[FOXO]], [[Atrogin-1]]/[[MuRF1]]), helping preserve mass and mitigate wasting in atrophy models. Other candidates include [[Myostatin|myostatin]] inhibitors, [[Selective Androgen Receptor Modulators|SARMs]], and [[Rapamycin|mTOR]] modulators (context-dependent).

## Role in the Urolithin A Review

The review *Pharmacological Effects of Urolithin A and Its Role in Muscle Health and Performance* identifies muscle atrophy—e.g., in neuromuscular disease or prolonged immobility—as a pathology where [[Urolithin A]] may preserve mass by simultaneously promoting protein synthesis and inhibiting degradation, while improving [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] function and reducing [[Inflammation|inflammation]] and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]].
