# MEF2C

## Overview

Myocyte enhancer factor 2C (MEF2C) is a transcription factor that orchestrates a neuronal gene network essential for axon morphology, synaptic function, and overall neuronal homeostasis. Beyond development, MEF2C has emerged as a marker and effector of cognitive resilience: its program is upregulated in neurons from individuals who harbor Alzheimer's pathology yet retain cognitive function, and it is suppressed in disease.

## Mechanism

MEF2C drives expression of genes governing dendritic arborization, synaptic plasticity, and neuronal survival. In tauopathy, MEF2C is downregulated in neurons, an effect linked to chronic microglial cGAS-STING and [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling. Single-nucleus sequencing shows that microglial cGAS deletion reverses the neuronal MEF2C downregulation (Udeochu et al., Nat Neurosci 2023, PMID 37095396), indicating that microglial innate immune activation restrains this protective transcriptional program in a non-cell-autonomous manner. Thus, cGAS-STING/IFN-I signaling dismantles the brain's intrinsic resilience by suppressing MEF2C.

## Pathology

Reduced MEF2C expression correlates with synapse loss and cognitive impairment in [[Tauopathy|tauopathies]] and [[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]]. Conversely, restoration or preservation of MEF2C-associated gene networks confers resilience against tau and amyloid toxicity. The finding that microglial cGAS deletion — or pharmacological cGAS inhibition — rescues MEF2C expression provides a mechanistic rationale for targeting the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis to preserve neuronal resilience, paralleling the protective effects of resilience alleles such as [[APOE3 Christchurch]]. MEF2C therefore represents both a biomarker of cognitive resilience and a downstream node through which innate immune suppression translates into neuronal protection.
