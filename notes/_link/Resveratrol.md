# Resveratrol

**[[notes/_link/Resveratrol]]** is a natural polyphenol compound found in the skin of grapes, blueberries, raspberries, and mulberries. It is perhaps the most famous "anti-aging" molecule in popular culture due to its presence in red wine and its purported ability to extend lifespan.


**[[notes/_link/Resveratrol]]** is a plant compound that acts like an antioxidant. The top food sources include red wine, grapes, and some berries.


## 1. Activation of [[notes/_link/SIRT1]]
The primary mechanism by which resveratrol is thought to promote longevity is through the activation of **[[notes/_link/SIRT1]]**, an NAD+-dependent histone deacetylase. SIRT1 regulates a variety of processes, including metabolism, DNA repair, and mitochondrial function.


## 2. Mimicking [[notes/_link/Caloric Restriction]]
Resveratrol is often called a "caloric restriction mimetic" because it triggers many of the same biological pathways as CR without the need for actual fasting. It promotes:
*   **Mitochondrial Biogenesis**: Improving cellular energy production.
*   **Reduced Inflammation**: Inhibiting the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines.
*   **Improved Insulin Sensitivity**: Helping to prevent [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus]] and [[Metabolic Syndromes]].


## 3. Clinical Research
While resveratrol has shown dramatic results in extending the lifespan of yeast, worms, and flies, its effects in humans are more complex. It is widely used as a supplement in the field of [[Geroscience]] to promote cardiovascular health and metabolic resilience.

## Alternative for Slow COMT
[[notes/_link/Resveratrol]] is considered a safe [[Anti-inflammatory]] and antioxidant option for people with slow [[notes/_link/COMT]] variants. Since it lacks a catechol structure, it does not compete for the [[notes/_link/COMT]] enzyme or cause its inhibition.
