## Overview

[[Atrial natriuretic peptide]] (ANP) is a 28-amino-acid peptide hormone synthesized and stored in atrial cardiomyocytes as pro-ANP. Stretch of the atrial wall (volume/pressure overload) triggers its release, initiating the body's counter-regulatory response to hypervolemia and hypertension.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

ANP binds natriuretic peptide receptor-A (NPR-A/GC-A), a membrane guanylyl cyclase, raising intracellular [[cGMP]]:

- Promotes renal natriuresis and diuresis (inhibits renin, aldosterone, and vasopressin).
- Causes smooth-muscle vasodilation, lowering systemic vascular resistance.
- Suppresses sympathetic tone and myocardial hypertrophy.

ANP is cleared by neutral endopeptidase (NEP) and corin-mediated processing; its actions oppose the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

ANP deficiency or resistance contributes to salt-sensitive hypertension and heart failure, where circulating ANP and [[B-type natriuretic peptide]] rise as compensatory biomarkers. ANP-based and NEP-inhibitor therapies are used clinically. In the wiki, ANP is relevant to cardiovascular endpoints and fluid/oxidant balance within adrenochrome and sirtuin research contexts.
