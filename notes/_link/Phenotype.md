# Phenotype

A **phenotype** is the set of observable characteristics or traits of an organism — its morphology, development, biochemical and physiological properties, behavior, and the products of behavior. The phenotype arises from the interaction of the [[Genotype]] with environmental, stochastic, and epigenetic influences, rather than from genes alone.

## Levels & Types

Phenotypes operate across scales: molecular (e.g., a protein's abundance), cellular (e.g., the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] of a [[Cellular Senescence|senescent cell]]), tissue, organismal, and population. Traits may be morphological, physiological, biochemical, or behavioral, and can be quantitative (height, blood pressure) or qualitative (eye color, presence of a disease).

## Phenotypic Plasticity & Aging

Organisms show **phenotypic plasticity** — the same genotype producing different phenotypes under differing conditions (e.g., muscle hypertrophy with training). In [[Aging]] research, the "aging phenotype" describes the composite decline in physiological reserve, increased vulnerability to disease, and loss of homeostasis. Geroscience reframes aging as a unifying phenotype driven by the [[Hallmarks of Aging]], motivating interventions that improve the overall aging phenotype rather than single diseases.

## Measurement & Relevance

Phenotypes are the endpoint readouts of most biomedical study — from clinical trials to high-content screening — and are increasingly captured as multi-dimensional "phenotypes" via omics and imaging. Understanding phenotype–genotype–environment relationships is central to [[Geroscience]], [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] reprogramming, and personalized longevity medicine.
