# Nanoparticles

**Nanoparticles (NPs)** are colloidal carriers with diameters of ~10–1000 nm, constructed from polymers, lipids, proteins (e.g., [[Human Serum Albumin|HSA]]), or dendrimers. Their high surface-area-to-volume ratio increases the dissolution rate and apparent solubility of encapsulated poorly-soluble drugs (per the Noyes–Whitney equation), improving [[Bioavailability|bioavailability]] and enabling controlled, sustained release.

## Classes Relevant to Fisetin

- **Polymeric NPs** — [[PLA]], [[PLGA]], [[PVP]], MPEG-PCL, HSA. Fisetin-loaded PLA NPs achieved ~2.3-fold AUC gain (i.v.) and 64.7% tumor-volume reduction in 4T1 breast cancer.
- **Solid Lipid Nanoparticles (SLNs)** — physiological lipids, controlled release; fisetin-IR-780 SLNs for combined photodynamic/chemo therapy of colon cancer.
- **Lipid-based** — [[Liposomes]], spherulites, nanocochleates (see [[Liposomes]]).

> [!info] Why nanoparticles help fisetin
> Fisetin's poor aqueous solubility and rapid phase II metabolism are bypassed by NP encapsulation, which protects the drug in the GI tract, slows release, and increases intestinal permeability (up to 4.9× in duodenum for PLGA/PVA NPs).
