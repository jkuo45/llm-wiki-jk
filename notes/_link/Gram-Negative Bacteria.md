# Gram-Negative Bacteria

[[Gram-Negative Bacteria]] are a major class of bacteria defined by a thin peptidoglycan layer sandwiched between an inner and an **outer membrane** rich in [[Lipopolysaccharide|lipopolysaccharide (LPS)]]. The outer membrane makes them resistant to many antibiotics and hosts the endotoxin that potently activates the innate immune system.

## Structure

- **Outer membrane**: Asymmetric lipid bilayer; outer leaflet dominated by LPS (lipid A + core + O-antigen).
- **Periplasm & peptidoglycan**: Thin wall between membranes.
- **Inner membrane**: Standard phospholipid bilayer.

## Mechanism of Action & Host Interaction

- **Endotoxin signaling**: Released LPS ([[LPS]]) engages [[TLR4]] (candidate note) on immune cells, triggering [[NF-κB]] and driving [[Inflammation]], fever, and sepsis.
- **Mitochondrial stress**: LPS and the ensuing cytokine storm increase [[Mitochondrial ROS]] and [[Oxidative Stress]], linking infection to [[Aging]]-associated inflammatory load (inflammaging).
- **Microbiome & translocation**: Gut gram-negative overgrowth and LPS translocation ("metabolic endotoxemia") contribute to chronic low-grade inflammation and metabolic disease.

> [!warning] Endotoxin potency
> Lipid A is one of the most immunostimulatory molecules known; tiny quantities of circulating LPS can sustain systemic inflammation, a bridge between infection, microbiome dysfunction, and aging.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

- **Infection/sepsis**: A leading cause of Gram-negative sepsis via endotoxic shock.
- **Aging & metabolism**: Microbial LPS translocation is implicated in [[Inflammaging]], insulin resistance, and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].
