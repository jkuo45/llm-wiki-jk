# Adipogenesis

## Overview

Adipogenesis is the developmental program by which multipotent mesenchymal stem cells commit to the adipocyte lineage and terminally differentiate into white or brown adipocytes. It expands the body's capacity for energy storage and endocrine regulation through [[Adipose Tissue]]. The process is essential for healthy adipose tissue remodeling and is dysregulated in [[Obesity]] and [[Insulin Resistance]].

## Mechanism of Function

Adipogenesis proceeds in two phases. Early commitment is driven by the C/EBPβ and C/EBPδ transcription factors and the master regulator [[PPARγ]], whose induction is sufficient to convert fibroblasts into fat cells. PPARγ heterodimerizes with RXR and activates adipocyte genes, including those for fatty acid binding protein (aP2/FABP4) and GLUT4. A later phase is orchestrated by C/EBPα, which stabilizes the differentiated state and cooperates with PPARγ. Insulin and IGF-1 stimulate the process via PI3K/Akt and MAPK signaling, while glucocorticoids and cAMP boost it. Mature adipocytes execute [[Lipid Synthesis]] and storage, express leptin and adiponectin, and modulate systemic insulin sensitivity.

## Pathological and Clinical Relevance

Impaired adipogenesis ("adipose tissue dysfunction") forces lipid spillover into liver and muscle, promoting ectopic fat deposition and [[Insulin Resistance]], a core feature of [[Metabolic Syndrome]]. Conversely, excessive adipogenesis and hyperplasia drive obesity. Pharmacologic modulation of PPARγ (thiazolidinediones) improves insulin sensitivity by enhancing adipogenesis and lipid buffering. Brown adipogenesis (via PRDM16/PGC-1α) is a target for thermogenic anti-obesity therapy. Understanding adipogenesis informs strategies to expand healthy, metabolically protective fat.
