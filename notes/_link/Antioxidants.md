# Antioxidants
Antioxidants is a concept related to [[Adrenochrome]] and catecholamine metabolism.
**Antioxidants** are molecules capable of inhibiting the oxidation of other molecules. In biological systems, they play a vital role in protecting cells from the damaging effects of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) and maintaining redox homeostasis.
## Classification
Antioxidants can be broadly categorized into enzymatic and non-enzymatic systems.
## Enzymatic Antioxidants
These are proteins produced by the body that catalyze the neutralization of ROS:
- **[[Superoxide Dismutase]] (SOD)**: Converts [[Superoxide Radicals]] into [[Hydrogen Peroxide]].
- **[[Catalase]]**: Breaks down [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] into water and oxygen.
- **[[Glutathione Peroxidase]] (GPx)**: Reduces lipid hydroperoxides and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] using [[Glutathione]].
## Non-Enzymatic Antioxidants
These include small molecules, many of which are obtained through diet:
- **[[Glutathione]]**: A tripeptide that is the most abundant endogenous antioxidant.
- **[[Vitamin E]] (Alpha-tocopherol)**: Protects cell membranes from [[Lipid Peroxidation]].
- **[[Vitamin C]] (Ascorbic Acid)**: A water-soluble antioxidant that also helps regenerate Vitamin E.
- **[[Coenzyme Q10]]**: A lipophilic antioxidant involved in mitochondrial electron transport.
- **[[Polyphenols]] and [[Flavonoids]]**: Plant-derived compounds with potent antioxidant activities.
## Mechanism of Action
Antioxidants work by:
1. **Scavenging**: Directly reacting with and neutralizing free radicals.
2. **Chelation**: Binding to transition metals (like iron or copper) that can catalyze the formation of ROS via the Fenton reaction.
3. **Regeneration**: One antioxidant can restore another to its active state (e.g., Vitamin C regenerating Vitamin E).
## Clinical Importance
An imbalance between ROS production and antioxidant defenses leads to [[Oxidative Stress]], which is implicated in the pathogenesis of numerous conditions, including [[Cancer]], [[Cardiovascular Disease]], and [[Neurodegenerative Disease]].
