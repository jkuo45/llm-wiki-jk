# ISG15

ISG15 (Interferon-stimulated gene 15) is an interferon-inducible ubiquitin-like modifier. In senescence it is induced by [[p16 INK4a|p16]] overexpression and is part of a distinct, p53-dependent SASP profile enriched for interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), contributing an antiviral/innate-immune character to the secretory program.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

ISG15 is transcribed downstream of type-I/II interferon receptor signaling via JAK–STAT1/STAT2–ISGF3 and IRF7/9. The ~15-kDa precursor is processed to expose a C-terminal LRLRGG motif and conjugated to lysine residues on target proteins (ISGylation) through the E1 UBE1L, E2 UBCM8, and various E3 ligases; it can also act in a free, secreted form as a cytokine. In senescence, ISG15 arises within the interferon-type SASP branch that is driven by cytosolic DNA sensing (cGAS–STING) and by p53, distinguishing it from the canonical NF-κB–IL-6/IL-8 arm. ISGylation modulates protein trafficking, antiviral restriction, and interferon amplification.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

ISG15 is an interferon-pathway SASP factor:
- Induced in p16-driven and p53-associated senescence, marking the ISG-rich SASP subset.
- Reinforces a type-I interferon transcriptional state that can promote [[Paracrine Senescence]] and immune surveillance of senescent cells.
- Contributes to the senescence-associated antiviral mimicry that engages [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] signaling.

Its presence indicates a senescence program skewed toward innate immunity rather than pure inflammatory secretion, relevant to clearance vs. persistence of [[Senescent Cells]].

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- ISG15 is a biomarker of interferon activity in autoimmunity, viral infection, and type-I interferonopathies.
- ISGylation defects underlie susceptibility to intracellular pathogens.
- The ISG-rich SASP may influence immune editing of senescent cells and age-related [[Inflammaging]]; modulating it could reshape senescent-cell clearance.
