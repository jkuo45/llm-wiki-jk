# HIRA

HIRA (Histone cell cycle regulator homolog A) is an evolutionarily conserved histone chaperone best known for replication-independent deposition of the [[H3.3]] histone variant. In the context of cellular senescence, HIRA has emerged as a central coordinator of the facultative heterochromatin structures termed [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|senescence-associated heterochromatin foci (SAHF)]], as reviewed in the npj Aging review on the dynamic epigenetic landscape of [[Senescence]] (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2). HIRA therefore sits at the interface of [[Histone Variant|histone variant]] biology and large-scale [[Chromatin Remodeling|chromatin remodeling]] that accompanies permanent growth arrest.

## Overview

HIRA is the vertebrate ortholog of the yeast Hir1p/Hir2p proteins and contains multiple WD40-repeat domains that mediate protein–protein interactions. Unlike canonical histones, which are synthesized during S phase, the variant H3.3 is deposited throughout the cell cycle in a replication-independent manner; HIRA is the principal chaperone responsible for this deposition at active genes, enhancers, and telomeres. Because senescent and post-mitotic cells largely exit the cell cycle, H3.3 deposition becomes increasingly reliant on HIRA, making the chaperone disproportionately important in aging and senescence.

## Structure and Function

HIRA functions as part of a conserved chaperone complex that includes [[ASF1a]] (anti-silencing function 1a) and [[UBN1]] (ubinuclein 1). ASF1a hands off H3.3–H4 tetramers to HIRA, which together with UBN1 and the CABIN1 subunit forms the HIRA–UBN1–CABIN1–ASF1a (HUCA) complex. This complex mediates nucleosome assembly independent of the replication fork and cooperates with [[PML]] (promyelocytic leukemia) nuclear bodies, to which HIRA relocalizes during senescence.

## Role in Senescence and SAHF Formation

Dissection of SAHF revealed that each chromosome condenses into a single heterochromatin focus, marked by repressive histone modifications such as [[H3K9me3]], H3K9me2, [[H3K27me3]], H4K20me3 and [[DNA Methylation|DNA methylation]] (Zhang et al., 2007, PMID 17339329; Chandra et al., 2012, PMID 22817894). Several studies established that HIRA, together with [[ASF1a]] and [[UBN1]], is essential for SAHF formation (Zhang et al., 2005, Dev. Cell 8:19–30; Banumathy et al., 2009, PMID 19103796; Ye et al., 2007, PMID 17339329). Preventing HIRA translocation to [[PML]] nuclear bodies blocks SAHF formation (Zhang et al., 2005). Rai et al. (2014, PMID 25401360) showed that HIRA orchestrates a dynamic chromatin landscape in senescence and is required for suppression of neoplasia.

Although SAHF are not essential for the cell-cycle arrest itself, their disruption correlates with reduced [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] expression — for example, HIRA depletion lowers SASP (Dasgupta et al., 2024). Whether SAHF or HIRA are strictly necessary for SASP remains unresolved, and robust punctate SAHF are absent in many in vivo and mouse contexts (Kennedy et al., 2010, PMID 20543843). HIRA also cooperates with p62/[[SQSTM1]] and [[PML]] to regulate inflammation during senescence (Dasgupta et al., 2024, Mol. Cell 84:3271–3287).

## Mechanistic Details

HIRA-dependent deposition of H3.3 helps maintain chromatin dynamics in senescent cells (Rai et al., 2014). H3.3 itself can be cleaved by [[Cathepsin L1]] into a form (H3.3cs1) that accumulates in senescent cells and can trigger senescence when ectopically expressed (Duarte et al., 2014, PMID 25319811). Loss of the reader [[BRD4]] can deregulate H3.3 clipping, enhancing chromatin accessibility at senescence genes (Yang et al., 2023, PMID 36800343). Thus HIRA sits upstream of a variant-deposition program that shapes both the repressive (SAHF) and activating (SASP) faces of the senescent epigenome.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

Because epigenetic regulators such as HIRA modulate SAHF and SASP without necessarily affecting growth arrest, HIRA-complex components are candidate targets for [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] intervention — agents that dampen the damaging secretory phenotype while preserving the tumor-suppressive arrest. Repurposing of chromatin-modifying drugs is an active area of aging research (see [[KDM4]], [[BRD4]], [[DOT1L]]).
