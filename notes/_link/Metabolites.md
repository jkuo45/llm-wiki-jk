# Metabolites

**Metabolites** are the small molecules (typically <1.5 kDa) that participate in, or are produced by, [[Metabolism|metabolic]] reactions catalyzed by enzymes. They include substrates, intermediates, and end products of the biochemical networks that sustain life.

## Classification

- **Primary metabolites** — directly involved in growth, energy, and reproduction: amino acids, nucleotides, [[Fatty acid|fatty acids]], sugars, and central-carbon intermediates (e.g. [[Acetyl-CoA]], [[Lactate]], [[NAD+]]).
- **Secondary metabolites** — not essential for basic viability but conferring ecological/physiological advantage (e.g. [[Polyphenols]], alkaloids).
- **Endogenous vs. exogenous/xenobiotic** — synthesized by host cells versus derived from diet, drugs, or the microbiota.

## Physiological & Research Relevance

> [!info] Metabolome, aging, and the microbiome
> The complete set of metabolites in a system is the **[[Metabolome]]**, profiled by metabolomics ([[Mass Spectrometry]], NMR). Metabolites serve as functional readouts of physiology and as [[Biomarkers]] of health and disease. The [[Gut Microbiome]] is a major metabolite factory, producing [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] (butyrate, propionate, acetate), secondary bile acids, and neuroactive compounds that influence host [[Inflammation]], immunity, and the [[Gut-Brain Axis]]. Microbial metabolite diversity increases in the oldest-old, linking the metabolome to healthy aging.
