# IC50

**IC50** (half-maximal inhibitory concentration) is the drug concentration at which a response is inhibited by 50% relative to the maximal effect, defined for a **monotonic sigmoidal** dose-response curve as its inflection point. It is a standard requirement for FDA approval of targeted small-molecule therapies, where a reliable, predictable dosage–activity relationship is expected [^1].

## Contrast with Hormetic Curves

A key limitation of IC50 is its inapplicability to [[Biphasic Dose-Response Curve|non-monotonic (hormetic) curves]]:
- In a sigmoidal curve, response increases monotonically with concentration up to a plateau; IC50 is well defined.
- In a hormetic curve, the slope changes sign, producing an inverted-U; there is **no single inflection point**, and a drug may be most effective at an *intermediate* concentration rather than the highest tolerated dose [^1].

> [!warning] Hormesis is a red flag in development
> Nontypical (biphasic) dose-response curves are often interpreted as indicating side effects or problems, prompting automatic rejection of candidate drugs. Cerrillo, Vidakovic & Míguez (2026) argue instead that such behavior is predictable from network topology (an [[Incoherent Bivalent Motif]]) and should not be grounds for automatic rejection [^1].
