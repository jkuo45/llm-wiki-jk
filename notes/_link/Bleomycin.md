# Bleomycin

**Bleomycin** is a glycopeptide antibiotic that causes DNA double-strand breaks via iron-mediated free-radical generation. Clinically it is used as a chemotherapeutic (e.g., in lymphoma and testicular cancer regimens).

## Use as a Senescence Inducer

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - repurposing_apigen_senomorphic.09.09.611999v1.full|Repurposing apigenin for senomorphic effect in antiaging pipelines]]
> In the apigenin study, PSC27 prostate stromal cells were treated with a sub-lethal dose of bleomycin (50 μg/mL; "BLEO"), producing a canonical senescence phenotype after 8–10 days — elevated SA-β-Gal, reduced BrdU incorporation, and increased [[DNA Damage Response|DDR]] foci. BLEO-induced senescence was used to screen and characterize apigenin's [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] suppression of the [[SASP|SASP]].

Bleomycin is a standard tool for generating [[Therapy-Induced Senescence|therapy-induced senescence (TIS)]] in vitro, alongside ionizing radiation and topoisomerase II inhibitors such as [[Mitoxantrone]].
