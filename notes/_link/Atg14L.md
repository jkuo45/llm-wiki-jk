# Atg14L

## Overview

[[Atg14L]] (autophagy-related 14-like, also called Barkor) is a mammalian-specific subunit of the class III [[PI3K]] complex that confers autophagic specificity on the [[Vps34]]–[[Beclin1]] machinery. By anchoring the complex to the developing phagophore, Atg14L restricts PtdIns3P production to autophagic membranes and is essential for initiation of [[Autophagy]].

## Structure & Domains

Atg14L contains an N-terminal coiled-coil domain that mediates binding to [[Beclin1]] and to the Vps34–Vps15 catalytic core, and a C-terminal coiled-coil/BATS (Barkor/Atg14L-autophagosome-targeting sequence) domain. The BATS domain senses curved membranes and inserts into the highly curved edge of the phagophore, ensuring localization to the site of autophagosome biogenesis.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Atg14L assembles the autophagy-specific Vps34 complex I together with Vps34, Vps15, and Beclin 1. Recruited downstream of the [[ULK1]] complex upon nutrient starvation or [[mTOR]] inhibition, the complex generates [[PtdIns3P]] on the phagophore, which recruits WIPI proteins and [[LC3]]-conjugation machinery via the [[LIR Motif]] network. Atg14L also promotes the fusion of mature [[Autophagosome]] with the [[Lysosome]] by binding the tethering factor Syntaxin 17, completing autophagic flux. It is opposed by [[Rubicon]] and overlaps functionally with [[UVRAG]], both of which modulate the same core complex.

## Physiological Function

Atg14L sustains basal and induced autophagy required for clearance of protein aggregates and damaged organelles, thereby protecting against [[Oxidative Stress]] and supporting cellular homeostasis. It contributes to quality control in post-mitotic cells such as neurons.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Dysregulation of Atg14L impairs autophagic flux and is linked to neurodegeneration and [[Cancer]] progression, where defective cargo clearance promotes genomic instability. Atg14L is a candidate target for modulating therapeutic autophagy.
