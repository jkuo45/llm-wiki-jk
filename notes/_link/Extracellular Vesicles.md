# Extracellular Vesicles

## Overview

[[Extracellular Vesicles]] (EVs) are lipid-bilayer–enclosed particles, including exosomes and microvesicles, released by cells to mediate intercellular communication. In the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]], EVs transport proteins, RNA, and organelles between [[Neuron|neurons]], [[Microglia]], and [[Astrocytes]], and have emerged as a key vehicle for propagating innate immune activation.

## EVs as Carriers of cGAS-STING Alarm Signals

EVs can be loaded with [[cGAMP]] and [[mtDNA]], enabling the transfer of [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] activation signals from a sender cell to a naive receiver. Neurons stressed by activity-induced [[DNA Damage]] or proteotoxic insult package nucleic acid cargo into EVs that activate microglial cGAS-STING, and microglia likewise broadcast signals back to neurons. In this way, EVs function as intercellular alarm signals that convert localized cellular stress into network-wide [[Neuroinflammation]].

## Mitochondrial Transfer and Glia–Neuron Crosstalk

Beyond soluble cargo, whole [[Mitochondria]] can be transferred between neurons and neighboring cells via EVs or [[Tunneling Nanotubes|tunneling nanotubes]], providing a broader mechanism for mtDNA dissemination. This vesicular transfer reshapes microglial states and amplifies maladaptive glial responses in neurodegeneration, making EV-mediated signaling a candidate target for interrupting pathological cGAS-STING spread.
