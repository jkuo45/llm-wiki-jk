# Bacteroidetes

**Overview:** Bacteroidetes is a major phylum of Gram-negative bacteria, typically non-spore-forming rods that are obligate or facultative anaerobes. In humans they are among the dominant constituents of the intestinal [[Gut Microbiome]], where the genus *Bacteroides* and *Prevotella* ferment complex carbohydrates that host enzymes cannot digest.

**Structure and Taxonomy:** Bacteroidetes possess an outer membrane rich in lipopolysaccharide (LPS), characteristic of [[Gram-Negative Bacteria]]. They are distinct from the other dominant gut phylum, [[Firmicutes]], in cell-wall architecture and metabolic output. The phylum also includes environmental genera (e.g., *Cytophaga*, *Flavobacterium*) important in aquatic and soil biogeochemistry.

**Physiological Function:** Gut Bacteroidetes degrade dietary fiber, resistant starch, and host glycans into [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] (principally acetate and propionate) and succinate. These metabolites nourish colonocytes, modulate hepatic lipid metabolism, and engage enteroendocrine and immune cells. Bacteroidetes also synthesize vitamins (e.g., vitamin K, B12) and help maintain the intestinal barrier against pathogens through competitive colonization resistance.

**Aging and Longevity:** The Bacteroidetes-to-[[Firmicutes]] (B/F) ratio is a widely used, if debated, marker of gut community structure in aging. Shifts in this ratio associate with metabolic and inflammatory phenotypes, and Bacteroidetes taxa are recurrently profiled in studies linking the microbiome to [[Longevity]]. Dietary [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] and related [[NAD+]] precursors have been reported to modulate Bacteroidetes abundance, suggesting crosstalk between host metabolic state and microbial composition.

**Pathology:** Although usually commensal, members such as *Bacteroides fragilis* can cause intra-abdominal abscesses and sepsis when translocated, and some produce enterotoxins implicated in inflammatory bowel disease and colorectal carcinogenesis.
