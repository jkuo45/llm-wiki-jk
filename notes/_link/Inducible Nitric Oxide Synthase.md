# Inducible Nitric Oxide Synthase

[[Inducible Nitric Oxide Synthase]] ([[iNOS]], NOS2) is the cytokine-inducible isoform of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]]. Unlike the constitutive endothelial ([[Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase|eNOS]]) and neuronal isoforms, iNOS is expressed in macrophages and many cells after inflammatory stimulation and generates sustained, high-output [[Nitric Oxide]].

## Role in Pathology

Excess iNOS-derived NO drives pathological [[Vasodilation]] and [[Hypotension]] in [[Septic Shock]] and [[Vasoplegic Shock]], impairs myocardial contractility in acute heart-transplant rejection, and contributes to vascular hyperpermeability after burns.

## Aminoguanidine (Selective Inhibitor)

[[Aminoguanidine]] is a relatively selective iNOS inhibitor — it blocks NO *production* upstream while sparing eNOS, theoretically halting inflammatory vasodilation without abolishing normal blood-pressure regulation. This contrasts with [[Methylene blue]], which blocks NO signaling *downstream* at [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]].

> [!info] Upstream vs downstream
> Aminoguanidine (iNOS inhibition) and methylene blue (sGC inhibition) target opposite ends of the same NO–[[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate]] cascade and have been studied as complementary agents.
