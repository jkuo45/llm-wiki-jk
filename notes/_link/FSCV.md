# FSCV

**Fast-scan cyclic voltammetry (FSCV)** is an electrochemical technique used to detect rapid changes in [[Neurotransmitter]] concentration in real time, typically in living brain tissue. It employs a carbon-fiber microelectrode and a rapid voltage ramp (typically 400 V/s, −0.4 to +1.3 V) to oxidize and reduce electroactive species.

## Principle

A triangular voltage waveform is applied to a carbon-fiber [[Microelectrode]] at 10 Hz. Electroactive [[Neurotransmitters]] ([[Dopamine]], [[Serotonin]], [[Norepinephrine]], [[Adenosine]]) are oxidized at characteristic potentials, producing a [[Faradaic Current]] proportional to concentration. Background subtraction isolates the signal from the rapid voltage scan. The resulting [[Cyclic Voltammogram]] provides a chemical fingerprint for analyte identification.

## Applications

FSCV measures sub-second [[Dopamine]] release in awake, behaving animals (rodents). It is used to study [[Reward System]] function, [[Addiction]], [[Motivation]], and the effects of [[Psychostimulants]] ([[Cocaine]], [[Amphetamine]]). Recent advances enable [[Serotonin]] detection with modified waveforms.
