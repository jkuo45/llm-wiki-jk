# IDO2

**IDO2** (Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 2) is a heme-containing enzyme paralogous to [[IDO1]], encoded by the *IDO2* gene in the same chromosomal region (8p11.21 in humans). It catalyzes the same reaction as IDO1 — conversion of [[Tryptophan]] to [[N-Formylkynurenine]] — but with substantially reduced catalytic efficiency.

## Properties

- **Gene:** *IDO2* on chromosome 8p11.21 (adjacent to *IDO1*)
- **Structure:** ~45 kDa monomeric heme protein
- **Catalytic activity:** ~100-fold lower than IDO1 for L-Trp
- **Expression:** Restricted to kidney, liver, and antigen-presenting cells; more tissue-limited than IDO1

## Functional Role

IDO2 is thought to function primarily as a **negative regulator** of [[IDO1]] rather than as a major Trp-catabolizing enzyme. Proposed mechanisms include:

- Competing with IDO1 for heme availability
- Forming heteromeric complexes that modulate IDO1 activity
- Mediating non-enzymatic signaling functions

IDO2 has been implicated in autoimmune [[Inflammation]], particularly in murine models of rheumatoid arthritis and contact hypersensitivity.

## Clinical Relevance

IDO2 contributes to resistance against [[IDO1]]-targeted therapies in [[Cancer]]. IDO2 polymorphisms are associated with susceptibility to autoimmune diseases. Selective IDO2 inhibitors are being explored as immunotherapeutic agents.
