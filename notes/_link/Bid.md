# Bid

[[Bid]] (BH3 interacting-domain death agonist) is a pro-apoptotic BH3-only member of the [[Bcl-2 family]] of proteins. It serves as a critical link between the extrinsic (death receptor) and intrinsic (mitochondrial) apoptotic pathways.

## Structure and Activation

Bid is a 22 kDa protein that exists in an inactive cytosolic conformation. Upon cleavage by [[Caspases|caspase-8]] (downstream of death receptor engagement), the N-terminal fragment (tBid, truncated Bid) translocates to the mitochondria, where it activates [[Bax]] and [[Bak]] to trigger mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP).

> [!important] Bid as an Apoptosis Amplifier
> tBid (truncated Bid) is a potent activator of [[Bax]], directly triggering Bax conformational change and oligomerization at the mitochondrial outer membrane. This mechanism amplifies apoptotic signals from death receptors by engaging the mitochondrial apoptotic machinery.

## Regulation

Bid activity is regulated by:
- **Caspase-8 cleavage**: The primary activating event, converting full-length Bid to tBid
- **[[Humanin]]**: Directly binds and nullifies tBid activity, blocking its ability to activate [[Bax]] and [[Bak]]
- **Phosphorylation**: JNK-mediated phosphorylation at Ser61 and Ser78 enhances Bid pro-apoptotic activity
- **Deamidation**: Asparagine deamidation at positions 60 and 85 activates Bid independently of caspase cleavage

## Biological Significance

Bid is essential for:
- Amplification of death receptor-mediated apoptosis
- Developmental tissue remodeling
- Immune system homeostasis (Bid knockout mice show lymphocyte accumulation)
- Chemotherapy-induced tumor cell death
