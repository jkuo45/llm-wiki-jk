# Glutathione S-Transferase

## Overview

**Glutathione S-transferases (GSTs)** are a superfamily of phase II metabolic enzymes that catalyze the conjugation of the tripeptide [[Glutathione]] (GSH) to a wide range of electrophilic endogenous and xenobiotic substrates, facilitating their detoxification and excretion. Beyond classical detoxification, GSTs are central to cellular [[Antioxidants|antioxidant]] defense and redox homeostasis, and several isoforms directly neutralize lipid peroxidation products such as 4-hydroxynonenal.

## Structure & Isoforms

GSTs are grouped into several classes based on sequence and structure, including cytosolic (Alpha, Mu, Pi, Theta, Omega, Zeta), mitochondrial (Kappa), and microsomal (MAPEG) families. Most cytosolic GSTs function as homo- or hetero-dimers, each subunit containing a catalytic G-site that binds glutathione and an H-site that binds the hydrophobic electrophile.

## Mechanism of Action & Regulation

- **Detoxification:** GSTs lower the pKa of the glutathione thiol, generating the reactive thiolate anion (GS⁻) that attacks electrophilic centers, yielding stable GS-conjugates exported via [[ABCG1|MRP/ABCC]] transporters.
- **NRF2–ARE axis:** GST expression is transcriptionally induced through the [[NRF2]]–[[Antioxidant Response Element|ARE]] (antioxidant response element) pathway. Under basal conditions NRF2 is sequestered by Keap1 and degraded; oxidative/electrophilic stress liberates NRF2, which drives transcription of GSTs, [[Glutathione Peroxidase|glutathione peroxidases]], and [[Superoxide Dismutase|superoxide dismutases]].
- **Redox buffering:** By consuming/regenerating glutathione pools, GSTs modulate cellular redox state and protect proteins from oxidative damage.

## Physiological Function

GSTs protect tissues—particularly liver, intestine, and muscle—from oxidative and electrophilic insults. In muscle, GST activity supports resilience to exercise-induced [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] and maintains [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] integrity.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- Altered GST expression is implicated in carcinogenesis, [[Insulin Resistance|insulin resistance]], and [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] disease.
- Polymorphisms in GST genes (e.g., *GSTM1*, *GSTP1*) modify individual detox capacity and disease susceptibility.
- GSTs are emerging targets for redox-modulating therapeutics.

## Role of Urolithin A

The review *Pharmacological Effects of Urolithin A and Its Role in Muscle Health and Performance* reports that [[Urolithin A]] enhances [[Mitophagy|mitochondrial autophagy]] (mitophagy) by activating GST expression through the NRF2–ARE signaling pathway. This upregulation strengthens cellular autophagy and [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] quality control, contributing to UA's antioxidant and muscle-protective effects.
