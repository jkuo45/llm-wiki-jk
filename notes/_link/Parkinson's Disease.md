# Parkinson's Disease

Parkinson's disease is a long-term degenerative disorder of the central nervous system that mainly affects the motor system. It is characterized by the loss of [[Dopamine]]-producing neurons in the [[notes/_link/Substantia nigra]]. Chronic [[notes/_link/Oxidative Stress]] and mitochondrial dysfunction (often involving [[Mitochondria]]) are key contributors to its progression.


[[Parkinson's Disease]] is a progressive [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative disorder]] primarily characterized by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra, leading to classic motor impairments such as tremors, rigidity, and bradykinesia.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

[[Parkinson's Disease]] has been a primary target for [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSC) technology since its inception. It serves as a flagship model for both [[Disease Modeling]] and [[Regenerative Medicine]]. iPSCs derived from patients with [[Parkinson's Disease]] have been used to create in vitro models that recapitulate hallmark features of the disease, allowing researchers to study the molecular mechanisms of neuronal degeneration.

In terms of cell replacement therapy, iPSC-derived dopaminergic progenitors have shown great promise. Preclinical studies in primate models demonstrated that these cells could survive, integrate, and restore dopamine production, leading to improved motor function. This has paved the way for first-in-human clinical trials, where autologous and allogeneic iPSC-derived grafts are being evaluated for safety and efficacy. However, challenges remain, such as the potential for uncontrolled growth and the development of dyskinesias in some transplanted subjects.

## Symptoms
The cardinal motor symptoms of PD include:
- **Tremor:** Often a "pill-rolling" tremor at rest.
- **Rigidity:** Stiffness of the limbs and trunk.
- **Bradykinesia:** Slowness of movement.
- **Postural Instability:** Impaired balance and coordination.

Non-motor symptoms can also occur, including cognitive impairment, depression, and sleep disturbances.


## Pathology
The primary pathological feature of PD is the selective degeneration of neuromelanin-containing neurons. The disease is closely linked to [[Aging]], which is the main risk factor. Progressive intracellular accumulation of [[Neuromelanin]] above a [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]] is hypothesized to trigger neuronal dysfunction and neurodegeneration.


## Treatments
While there is currently no cure, treatments focus on managing symptoms, primarily by increasing dopamine levels in the brain using [[L-DOPA]] (levodopa) and other dopamine agonists.


## Pathogenesis
- Characterized by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra and the accumulation of alpha-synuclein aggregates (Lewy bodies).
- Mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired [[Mitophagy]] (selective [[Autophagy]] of mitochondria) are key features.


## Autophagy Modulation
- Defective [[Autophagic Flux]] contributes to the buildup of toxic aggregates and neuronal death.
- Inducing autophagy via dietary interventions such as [[Intermittent Fasting]] or [[Ketogenic Diet]] is investigated for its neuroprotective potential in PD.


## COMT and Treatment
In the management of [[Parkinson's Disease]], [[COMT Inhibitors]] are used to increase the effectiveness of [[Levodopa]]. By slowing the degradation of dopamine, these inhibitors help maintain higher levels of the neurotransmitter in the brain.
