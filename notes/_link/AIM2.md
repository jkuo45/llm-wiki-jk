# AIM2

## Overview

AIM2 (Absent in Melanoma 2) is a cytosolic double-stranded DNA sensor belonging to the PYHIN (PYRIN and HIN domain-containing) family. Upon binding cytosolic dsDNA, AIM2 oligomerizes with the adaptor ASC to form an [[Inflammasome|inflammasome]], which activates caspase-1 and drives [[Pyroptosis]] and the release of [[Pro-inflammatory Cytokines|pro-inflammatory cytokines]] such as IL-1beta and IL-18. As a DNA-sensing pattern recognition receptor, AIM2 represents a parallel innate immune arm to [[cGAS]], both detecting cytosolic dsDNA but engaging distinct downstream machinery.

## AIM2 vs. cGAS as Parallel DNA-Sensing Arms

Unlike [[cGAS]], which synthesizes [[cGAMP]] to activate the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] and [[Type I Interferon|type I IFN]] responses via [[STING]] and [[IRF3]], AIM2 triggers an inflammatory cell-death program rather than an interferon program. This division of labor means that cytosolic dsDNA — whether of viral, mitochondrial ([[mtDNA]]), or nuclear ([[DNA Damage]]) origin — can activate either axis depending on cellular context. In the brain, AIM2-mediated inflammasome activation in [[Microglia]] and other CNS cells can exacerbate [[Neuroinflammation]] and contribute to [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]], paralleling the pathological consequences of chronic cGAS-STING activation.

## Cell Death and CNS Pathology

AIM2 inflammasome assembly leads to Gasdermin-D pores and lytic [[Pyroptosis]], a form of inflammatory cell death that amplifies local tissue injury. Given that cGAS and AIM2 both surveil the cytosol for DNA, they form complementary but separable sentinels: cGAS broadcasts an interferon alarm, whereas AIM2 executes an inflammatory death sentence. Dysregulation of either pathway promotes chronic [[Inflammation]] and neuronal damage, making both attractive but context-dependent targets in neurological disease.
