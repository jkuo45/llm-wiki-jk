# Aminoguanidine

[[Aminoguanidine]] (developmental name [[Pimagedine]]) is a chemical compound with the formula $CH_6N_4$. Mechanistically it is best known as the first identified inhibitor of [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs), acting as a "carbonyl trap" that intercepts reactive carbonyl species and prevents protein cross-linking. It is also a selective inhibitor of [[Inducible Nitric Oxide Synthase]] (iNOS) and of [[Diamine Oxidase]] (DAO).

In the adrenochrome literature, aminoguanidine is used as a reagent to stabilize [[Adrenochrome]] by forming [[Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine]].

## Nitric Oxide Pathway (vs. Methylene Blue)

Aminoguanidine and [[Methylene blue]] are frequently compared because they act at different steps of the same **NO–[[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate]] (cGMP) vasodilation cascade**:

- **Aminoguanidine acts upstream** — it selectively inhibits [[Inducible Nitric Oxide Synthase]], blocking overproduction of [[Nitric Oxide]] during inflammation.
- **Methylene blue acts downstream** — it inhibits [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]], blocking cGMP generation even when NO is already present.

> [!info] Selective iNOS inhibition
> Because aminoguanidine spares the endothelial isoform ([[Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase|eNOS]]), it is theorized to halt inflammatory vasodilation without abolishing normal blood-pressure regulation — unlike non-selective NOS blockade.

This complementarity has been explored in [[Septic Shock]], refractory [[Vasoplegic Shock]], acute heart-transplant rejection (restoring myocyte contractility via lowered cGMP), and thermal injury (reducing vascular hyperpermeability).

## Therapeutic Roles and Safety

Aminoguanidine prevented diabetic renal, retinal, and neural complications in experimental models, and reached phase III trials for [[Diabetic Nephropathy]] (as Pimagedine) but was not approved due to safety concerns:

- Pro-oxidant activities.
- Inhibition of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]].
- Potential DNA damage by free radical production.
- Risk of autoimmune reactions and [[Vitamin B6]] depletion.

It is today primarily an experimental/laboratory tool, not an approved broad clinical therapy.

> [!warning] Histamine intolerance (oral)
> Oral aminoguanidine potently inhibits gut [[Diamine Oxidase]], the enzyme that degrades dietary histamine. This can cause severe acute [[Histamine Intolerance]] — headaches, flushing, hives, and tachycardia after histamine-rich foods (aged cheese, cured meats, fermented foods). IV administration bypasses gut DAO but still affects systemic DAO.

## Oral vs. Intravenous

- **Oral:** well absorbed; historically studied (Pimagedine tablets) for chronic AGE inhibition in diabetes.
- **IV / intraperitoneal:** used in experimental animal models of septic shock and ischemia–reperfusion injury for rapid iNOS blockade.
