# JNK

## Overview
[[JNK]] (c-Jun N-terminal kinase) is a stress-activated member of the MAPK family that plays a central role in cellular responses to stress, including apoptosis, inflammation, and metabolism. JNK has context-dependent roles in apoptosis: it can promote cell death by phosphorylating pro-apoptotic substrates like [[BAX]], or promote survival through NF-κB activation. The outcome depends on the duration, intensity, and subcellular localization of JNK signaling.

## Structure & Activation
JNK exists as three genes (JNK1, JNK2, JNK3) with multiple splice variants. JNK is activated by the MAPK cascade: MAP3Ks (e.g., ASK1, MEKK1) → MAP2Ks (MKK4, MKK7) → JNK. Activation requires dual phosphorylation on Thr183 and Tyr185 in the activation loop. JNK is activated by UV radiation, oxidative stress, cytokines, and growth factor withdrawal.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **BAX Phosphorylation**: Under stress conditions (e.g., UV, oxidative stress, growth factor withdrawal), JNK phosphorylates [[BAX]] at Thr167. Unlike the ERK-mediated phosphorylation at the same site (which creates a pro-survival Pin1 binding motif), JNK-mediated phosphorylation promotes BAX activation, conformational change, and mitochondrial translocation, leading to apoptosis.

> [!info] Context-Dependent Phosphorylation at Thr167
> The same residue on [[BAX]] (Thr167) is targeted by both survival and death kinases: [[ERK2]] phosphorylation promotes survival via [[Pin1]] binding, while JNK or [[p38 MAPK]] phosphorylation promotes apoptosis. This represents a molecular coincidence detector where the cellular context (survival vs. stress signals) determines the outcome of a single phosphorylation event.

- **Transcriptional Regulation**: JNK phosphorylates c-Jun, enhancing AP-1 transcriptional activity to induce pro-apoptotic genes (e.g., FasL, Bim).
- **Mitochondrial Pathway**: JNK can phosphorylate Bcl-2 family members (Bim, Bcl-2, Bcl-xL) to modulate the balance between pro- and anti-apoptotic signals.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer**: JNK has dual roles — tumor suppressive in early stages (promoting apoptosis) and tumor promoting in advanced disease (promoting inflammation and invasion).
- **Neurodegeneration**: JNK-mediated apoptosis contributes to neuronal death in Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and stroke.
- **Metabolic Disease**: JNK activation in obesity contributes to insulin resistance and hepatic steatosis.
