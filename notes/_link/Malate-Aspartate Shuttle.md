# Malate-Aspartate Shuttle

The **Malate-Aspartate Shuttle (MAS)** is the major system transporting reducing equivalents from cytosolic NADH (generated during [[Glycolysis|glycolysis]]) into the mitochondrial matrix for [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|OXPHOS]]. It couples cytosolic and mitochondrial [[NAD+]]/[[NADH]] pools via the reciprocal action of [[Malate Dehydrogenase|malate dehydrogenases]] (cytosolic [[MDH1]], mitochondrial [[MDH2]]) and transaminases (GOT1/GOT2), moving malate inward and aspartate outward across the inner membrane.

## Mechanism

- Cytosolic NADH reduces [[Oxaloacetate]] to [[Malate]] via MDH1; malate enters the matrix and is re-oxidized to oxaloacetate by MDH2, regenerating matrix NADH.
- Aspartate/glutamate exchangers return carbon skeletons to the cytosol, completing the cycle without net consumption of TCA intermediates.

## Role in Senescence

- Inhibiting NADH shuttling from glycolysis into mitochondria with [[Aminooxyacetate]] (an aminotransferase inhibitor that blocks the MAS) lowers the *cytosolic* NAD+/NADH ratio — but not the mitochondrial one — and **causes senescence**.
- This, together with MDH1-knockdown data, demonstrates that it is the cytosolic NAD+/NADH ratio, maintained by the malate-aspartate shuttle, that governs senescence induction rather than mitochondrial NAD+ pools.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: Shows aminooxyacetate inhibition of the malate-aspartate shuttle lowers cytosolic NAD+/NADH and induces senescence, supporting a cytosolic (not mitochondrial) NAD+ requirement for senescence suppression.
