# Dopamine Transporter

The **[[Dopamine Transporter]]** (also known as **[[DAT]]**) is a membrane protein responsible for the reuptake of [[Dopamine]] from the synaptic cleft back into the presynaptic neuron, terminating the signal and recycling the neurotransmitter.

## Regional Distribution

The [[Dopamine Transporter]] is highly abundant in the [[Sirtuins]], where it is the primary mechanism for terminating dopamine signaling via rapid uptake into dopaminergic terminals. In contrast, DAT is much less abundant and located farther from synaptic sites in the [[Prefrontal Cortex]], where [[COMT]] and the [[Norepinephrine transporter]] ([[NET]]) play more significant roles in dopamine clearance.

## Interaction with COMT

The regional complementarity between DAT and COMT is a key principle of catecholamine biology:
- **Striatum:** DAT is the dominant dopamine clearance mechanism; COMT plays a secondary role.
- **Prefrontal Cortex:** DAT is sparse; COMT and NET are the primary regulators of extracellular dopamine.
