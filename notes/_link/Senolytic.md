# Senolytic

A **senolytic** is a compound or intervention that selectively kills [[Senescence|senescent cells]] ([[Senescent Cells]]) while sparing proliferating and quiescent healthy cells. The term was coined by Kirkland and colleagues to describe drugs that target the pro-survival networks — Senescent-Cell Anti-Apoptotic Pathways (SCAPs) — that make senescent cells resistant to [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]].

## Mechanism of Action

[[Senescent Cells|Senescent cells]] upregulate SCAPs (e.g., [[Bcl-2|BCL-2 family]] members, [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], [[p21]], [[HSP90|HSP90]]) that defend them against their own pro-apoptotic [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]]. Senolytics disable these pathways, triggering selective apoptosis.

> [!info] Hit-and-run kinetics
> Senolytics need not be present continuously. A short pulse can clear senescent cells whose removal persists for weeks, because new senescent cells form slowly — enabling intermittent ("quarterly or annual") dosing that minimizes side effects.

## Major Senolytics

- **Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q)** — the first widely used combination; [[Dasatinib]] (a tyrosine kinase inhibitor) targets some cell types, [[Quercetin]] (a flavonoid) targets others.
- **Fisetin** — a [[Flavonol|flavonol]] flavonoid; the most potent senolytic among a 10-flavonoid screen, extending median and maximal [[Lifespan|lifespan]] in aged mice (Yousefzadeh et al., 2018, PMID 30279143).
- **Navitoclax / A1331852** — [[Bcl-xL]]/BCL-2 inhibitors; effective against some but not all senescent cell types, with thrombocytopenia toxicity.
- **Other flavonoids** — [[Luteolin]], [[Curcumin]], [[Myricetin]], [[Apigenin]] show weaker activity.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Senolytics alleviate frailty, [[Osteoporosis]], [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular disease]], [[Osteoarthritis]], pulmonary fibrosis, and [[Inflammation|sterile inflammation]] in murine models. Late-life initiation is sufficient to extend [[Healthspan|healthspan]] and [[Lifespan|lifespan]], supporting translation to elderly humans.
