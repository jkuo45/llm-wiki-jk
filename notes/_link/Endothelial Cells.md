# Endothelial Cells

## Overview

Endothelial cells (ECs) are the specialized cells that line blood vessels, forming the innermost layer of the vasculature. In the brain, cerebrovascular endothelial cells are the principal cellular constituent of the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]], joined by tight junctions and working together with [[Astrocytes]] and [[Pericytes]] to restrict paracellular permeability. ECs are not merely passive barriers; they are active participants in [[Inflammation]], hemostasis, and immune surveillance, and they express the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] components [[cGAS]] and [[STING]].

## cGAS-STING Activation Disrupts the BBB

In endothelial cells, cGAS-STING signaling is activated by damaged [[mtDNA]] and other cytosolic DNA triggers. Across models of irradiation, infection, and intracerebral hemorrhage, endothelial cGAS-STING activation induces [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling and [[Pyroptosis|pyroptotic]] cell-death pathways, leading to [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] disruption and increased leukocyte infiltration into the brain. Activated ECs also adopt a pro-inflammatory and pro-thrombotic phenotype, amplifying [[Neuroinflammation]] and promoting vascular dysfunction within the [[Central Nervous System]].

## Contrast with Microglial STING

Importantly, the role of STING in barrier integrity is context dependent and cell type specific. Whereas endothelial STING activation is deleterious to the BBB, [[Microglia|microglial]] STING signaling appears protective, preserving hippocampal BBB integrity during aging. This divergence — endothelial activation disrupting the barrier while microglial activation maintains it — exemplifies the dual, context-dependent nature of cGAS-STING signaling in the neurovascular unit and complicates blanket pharmacological inhibition of the pathway.
