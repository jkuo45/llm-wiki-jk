# Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)

**Traumatic brain injury (TBI)** is an acquired injury to the [[Brain]] from external mechanical force (impact, blast, acceleration-deceleration), producing a cascade of metabolic, inflammatory, and excitotoxic damage. Severity ranges from [[Concussion|mild (concussion)]] to severe.

## Pathophysiology

Primary injury is followed by secondary injury:

- Energy failure and [[Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)|ATP]] depletion
- [[Excitotoxicity]] (glutamate surge)
- [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Inflammation]]
- [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Edema]]

The metabolic crisis outlasts the mechanical event, making energy-supportive interventions relevant.

## Creatine and TBI

[[Creatine in Health and Disease]] summarizes strong preclinical evidence for [[Neuroprotection]]:

- Sullivan et al.: dietary creatine for 5 days before TBI reduced cortical damage by 36% in rats and 50% in mice, attributed to improved [[Energy Availability|energy availability]].
- Proposed mechanisms include sustaining the [[Creatine Phosphate Shuttle]] and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|PCr]] buffering during ischemia-like secondary injury.
- Evidence was compelling enough that an International consensus body (per the review) acknowledged creatine's potential in mild concussion/TBI.

## Management

ABCs, intracranial pressure control, rehabilitation; neuroprotective pharmacotherapies remain limited.
