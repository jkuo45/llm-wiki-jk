# Retinal Ganglion Cells

## Overview

[[Retinal Ganglion Cells]] (RGCs) are the output neurons of the retina, whose axons form the optic nerve to relay visual information to the brain. As CNS neurons, RGCs are particularly vulnerable to axonal injury and chronic stress, and their progressive degeneration underlies vision loss in [[Retinal Ganglion Cells|glaucoma]] and other optic neuropathies.

## cGAS-STING in RGC Degeneration

cGAS-STING signaling is activated within RGCs and the adjacent retinal pigment epithelium, where it drives RGC death and contributes to vision loss. Neuronal [[STING]] activity modulates both axonal regeneration and vulnerability, and interplay with [[TLR9|cGAS/TLR9]] further shapes the retinal innate immune response. Given that RGCs experience activity-induced [[DNA Damage]] and mitochondrial stress, cGAS-STING represents a plausible link between retinal stress and neurodegeneration.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Because STING activation promotes RGC loss, pharmacological inhibition of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis is being investigated as a strategy to preserve RGC viability and visual function. Defining the cell-autonomous versus glial contributions to retinal cGAS-STING activation will be important for targeted intervention.
