# Chlorpromazine

[[Chlorpromazine]] (CPZ, Thorazine) is a phenothiazine derivative bearing a bulky side chain on the position-10 nitrogen. It was the first antipsychotic drug, acting primarily as a [[D2 receptor]] (dopamine D2) antagonist, and pioneered the pharmacological treatment of schizophrenia and acute psychosis. In the methylene blue derivative study it served as the prototype of the "10-nitrogen side-chain substituted" group, which loses methylene blue-like mitochondrial protection [^1].

## Structure & Chemistry

The defining modification versus [[Methylene blue]]/[[Phenothiazine]] is substitution at the 10-nitrogen with a 3-chloropropyl-dimethylaminoethyl side chain. This blocks the free heterocyclic nitrogen required for alternative electron transfer. Chlorpromazine's redox potential is strongly positive (E<sub>1/2</sub> ≈ 1.059 V vs NHE), precluding oxygen-consumption enhancement [^1].

## Mechanism of Action

### Classical antipsychotic action

Chlorpromazine blocks central [[D2 receptor]] and, at higher occupancy, other monoamine receptors (histamine H1, muscarinic, α-adrenergic), producing antipsychotic, sedating, antiemetic, and mild [[Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor|MAO-inhibitory]] effects.

### Loss of mitochondrial electron-shunt activity

In the MB-derivative study, chlorpromazine:

- Did **not** increase [[Cytochrome c]] reduction in the [[Respiratory Chain Complex I|Complex I]]→[[Complex III]] assay.
- Did **not** increase cellular oxygen consumption rate (OCR) or affect ECAR.
- Was the **only** compound that failed to protect against [[Rotenone]] neurotoxicity [^1].
- In the cell-free mitochondria-lysate oxidation assay, **increased** DCF fluorescence (pro-oxidative) at 100 nM–10 µM both with and without mitochondria/[[NADH]] [^1].

## Neuroprotection (structure–activity context)

Against glutamate insult in HT-22 cells, chlorpromazine showed weak protection:

- Viability EC50 ≈ **2192 nM**
- ROS EC50 ≈ **2148 nM**
- Mitochondrial membrane potential EC50 ≈ **3270 nM**

This represents a ~1000-fold loss of potency versus [[Methylene blue]] and [[Toluidine Blue O]], attributable to the 10-nitrogen side chain. Earlier literature reported only minor micromolar protective actions, consistent with these results [^36], [^38]. Like all compounds in the study, it gave no protection against direct H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> from [[Glucose Oxidase]] [^1].

> [!warning] Side chain at 10-N abolishes MB-like protection
> Adding a side chain to the phenothiazine 10-nitrogen (chlorpromazine, [[Promethazine]]) produced a ~1000-fold drop in neuroprotective potency, no electron shunt, no OCR effect, and — uniquely for chlorpromazine — a pro-oxidative signature in cell-free assays [^1].
