# Cathepsin L1

Cathepsin L1 (CTSL, commonly called cathepsin L) is a lysosomal cysteine protease of the papain family. In the context of senescence, it is notable for cleaving the N-terminal tail of the histone H3 variant [[H3.3]], removing post-translational modifications and generating a truncated product, H3.3cs1, that accumulates in senescent cells and can itself induce senescence.

## Overview

Cathepsin L is a ubiquitously expressed endolysosomal protease that degrades intracellular and endocytosed proteins. It is synthesized as an inactive proenzyme and matured in acidic compartments. Beyond bulk proteolysis, cathepsin L has gained recognition for non-lysosomal, nucleus-associated functions, including processing of transcription factors and, remarkably, histones.

## Structure & Function

The mature enzyme is a bilobed papain-fold cysteine protease with a catalytic Cys/His dyad in an active-site cleft. Substrate specificity is directed toward hydrophobic/aromatic residues. Within the nucleus, cathepsin L can access histone substrates during chromatin remodeling and DNA-damage responses, exerting precise proteolytic editing rather than bulk degradation.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review highlights that histone H3.3, a replication-independent H3 variant that accumulates with age and in senescence, can undergo N-terminal tail cleavage by cathepsin L1 (ref. 93). This cleavage removes tail modifications controlling cell-cycle regulators and produces H3.3cs1. Both H3.3 and H3.3cs1 are increased across senescent cell types and can trigger senescence when ectopically expressed. Moreover, the reader [[BRD4]] normally protects against H3.3 clipping at senescence-related loci; its absence triggers clipping and the appearance of activating marks ([[H3K27ac]], [[H3K122ac]], [[H3K4me3]]), enhancing chromatin accessibility and transcription of senescence-specific genes including SASP factors (ref. 95). Cathepsin L1 therefore sits at the intersection of histone-variant metabolism and SASP induction.

## Mechanistic Details

- Cathepsin L1 clipping of H3.3 tail erases regulatory modifications, altering the chromatin landscape at senescence loci.
- H3.3cs1 accumulation correlates with senescence establishment across multiple cell types.
- BRD4 loss → increased clipping → SASP locus activation, linking a BET reader to protease-mediated histone editing.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Cathepsin L inhibitors (e.g., RO5461111, KGP94) are explored in oncology and fibrosis. Modulating cathepsin L1 to limit H3.3 clipping could represent a [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] route to curb SASP, though cathepsin L has broad substrates requiring selectivity. The H3.3/H3.3cs1 axis also provides a potential biomarker of senescence.
