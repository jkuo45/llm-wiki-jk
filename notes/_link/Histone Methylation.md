# Histone Methylation

**Histone methylation** is the covalent addition of methyl groups to lysine or arginine residues on histone tails. Unlike acetylation, it does not alter histone charge; instead it creates docking sites for "reader" proteins that recruit downstream chromatin machinery. Its transcriptional consequence depends on the exact residue and the number of methyl groups added (mono-, di-, or tri-methylation).

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Writers:** [[Histone Methyltransferase]] enzymes deposit marks using [[S-adenosylmethionine]] (SAM) as the methyl donor. Lysine methyltransferases (e.g. [[SUV39H1]], [[EZH2]], [[DOT1L]]) and PRMTs (arginine) show high residue specificity.
- **Erasers:** demethylases such as [[KDM4]] and [[JMJD3]] remove marks, making methylation dynamic and reversible.
- **Activating vs. repressive marks:** [[H3K4me3]] and [[H3K36me3]] associate with active transcription, whereas [[H3K27me3]] (Polycomb, via [[EZH2]]) and H3K9me3 mark repressed [[Heterochromatin]].

## Role in Aging & Senescence

> [!info] Epigenetic drift
> Aging is accompanied by a global loss of repressive H3K9me3/H3K27me3, erosion of [[Heterochromatin]], derepression of [[Transposable Elements]], and redistribution of chromatin modifiers — a core component of the [[Epigenetic Alterations]] hallmark of aging.
