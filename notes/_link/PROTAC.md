# PROTAC

## Overview

A proteolysis-targeting chimera (PROTAC) is a bifunctional molecule composed of a target-binding warhead linked to an E3 ubiquitin ligase recruiter, which induces proximity-dependent ubiquitination and degradation of the target protein via the [[Ubiquitin Proteasome System]]. PROTACs offer a catalytic, occupancy-independent mechanism distinct from conventional enzyme inhibition, enabling clearance of otherwise "undruggable" proteins.

## Application to cGAS-STING

In the context of chronic [[Neuroinflammation]], PROTAC technology has been proposed as an emerging strategy to degrade [[cGAS]] or [[STING]] therapeutically, thereby durably dampening the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]]. Rather than merely blocking enzymatic activity (as with [[cGAS Inhibitors]] or [[STING Inhibitors]]), targeted degradation could eliminate the upstream sensors, potentially providing more sustained suppression of [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling and maladaptive glial activation in [[Neurodegeneration]].

## Therapeutic Outlook

PROTAC-mediated degradation of cGAS/STING remains an exploratory approach, but it aligns with the broader goal of uncoupling neurotoxic inflammation from beneficial cellular functions. Its success will depend on achieving cell-type selectivity — particularly sparing the barrier-protective roles of microglial STING — and on brain penetrance, making it a complementary frontier to small-molecule inhibition for diseases such as [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]].
