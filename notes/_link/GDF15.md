# GDF15

Growth differentiation factor 15 (GDF15) is a distant member of the [[Transforming Growth Factor-beta|TGF-β]] superfamily. Unlike classical TGF-β ligands, GDF15 does not signal through canonical SMAD2/3 pathways; instead it is a [[Cytokines|stress-responsive secretory factor]] induced by the [[Integrated Stress Response]] (ISR) and acts as a [[Mitokines|mitokine]] that communicates local mitochondrial dysfunction to distal tissues. In the context of aging, GDF15 has emerged as one of the most robust circulating biomarkers of [[Senescence|senescence burden]] and [[Cellular Senescence]], linking mitochondrial stress, the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]], and systemic [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]] to age-related functional decline.

> [!info] Core senescence-relevant facts
> - GDF15 is consistently elevated in plasma with age and is a component of multi-protein senescent-cell and frailty biomarker panels.
> - It is induced by the same stress axes that drive senescence ([[DNA Damage Response]], [[p53]], mitochondrial ISR via [[ATF4]]/[[CHOP]]).
> - It is secreted by senescent cells and is itself listed among canonical [[SASP]] factors.
> - Circulating GDF15 is predictive of mortality, frailty, and multiple age-related diseases, independent of classical risk factors.

## Overview

GDF15 is a 34-kDa secreted protein synthesized as a precursor, cleaved in the Golgi, and released as a mature dimer. Under basal conditions its expression is low in most adult tissues, with higher constitutive production in the placenta, prostate, and certain epithelial barriers. Expression is dramatically induced by diverse cellular stresses — mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum stress, oxidative stress, genotoxic damage, and oncogene activation — positioning GDF15 as a generalized "stress alarm" cytokine. Because mitochondrial dysfunction and unresolved cellular stress are hallmarks of [[Aging]] and drivers of [[Cellular Senescence]], GDF15 levels rise as organisms age and serve as a peripheral readout of accumulated senescent burden.

## Structure & Domains

- Belongs to the TGF-β superfamily based on a conserved cysteine-knot motif, but is phylogenetically distant from the BMP/GDF subfamily and lacks standard bioactive pro-domain processing typical of other family members.
- Synthesized as a 308-aa pre-proprotein; the N-terminal signal peptide and propeptide are cleaved to yield the mature ~12–15 kDa C-terminal dimer.
- Does not engage the conventional TGF-β receptor/SMAD2/3 axis. Its high-affinity receptor is the glial-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF) family receptor α-like protein [[GFRAL]], which heterodimerizes with the RET co-receptor in the [[Brainstem]] and selected peripheral neurons to mediate anorexia and weight loss.
- The orphan status of GFRAL in many peripheral tissues implies additional, less-characterized receptors or non-canonical signaling modalities relevant to its metabolic and inflammatory effects.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

### ISR / ATF4 / CHOP induction
GDF15 transcription is a canonical downstream target of the [[Integrated Stress Response]]. Mitochondrial perturbation (e.g., impaired [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|biogenesis]], ETC dysfunction, [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|fusion/fission]] imbalance) activates the OMA1–DELE1–HRI axis, driving phosphorylation of [[eIF2α]] and selective translation of [[ATF4]]. ATF4 then induces [[CHOP]] and directly transactivates *GDF15* (alongside [[FGF21]], another mitokine). This places GDF15 at the apex of the retrograde mitochondrial-to-nuclear stress communication circuit described in [[Mitohormesis]].

### Senescence and DNA damage axes
- **p53-dependent induction**: GDF15 is induced by [[p53]] downstream of [[DNA Damage Response|DNA damage]] and oncogenic stress, and exhibits pro-apoptotic activity in transformed cells. This is the same p53 axis that enforces senescence arrest, explaining why senescent cells upregulate GDF15.
- **DDR / cGAS–STING overlap**: Persistent DNA damage and cytosolic [[mtDNA]] leakage activate the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], a potent SASP inducer. Cells under these conditions upregulate GDF15, coupling mitochondrial-ISR signaling to the senescence program.
- **NF-κB / inflammasome**: SASP amplification via [[NF-κB]] and the [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] reinforces GDF15 secretion, integrating it into the inflammatory secretome of senescent cells.

### Receptor signaling (GFRAL/RET)
Mature GDF15 binds [[GFRAL]] in the area postrema and nucleus of the solitary tract of the [[Brainstem]], activating RET and downstream ERK/PI3K-Akt pathways to suppress [[Appetite]] and increase [[Energy expenditure]]. This explains the anorexia and weight loss seen in mitochondrial disease, cancer cachexia, and during [[Metformin]] therapy — contexts where circulating GDF15 is markedly elevated.

## GDF15 and Cellular Senescence

GDF15 is now recognized as both a *product of* and a *contributor to* the senescent phenotype:

- **SASP component**: GDF15 is catalogued among canonical [[SASP]] factors. The SASP Atlas and related proteomic studies (Basisty et al., 2020; Tanaka et al., 2018) identify GDF15 together with stanniocalcin-1 ([[STC1]]), [[MMP1]], and [[SERPIN|SERPINs]] as secreted senescence-associated proteins. Five SASP factors — GDF15, [[RAGE]], [[VEGFA|VEGF-A]], [[PARC|PARC/CCL18]], and [[MMP-2]] — are strongly associated with mortality in human cohorts.
- **Senescence biomarker panel**: Plasma GDF15 is a leading blood-based surrogate for [[Senescence|senescence burden]]. Epigenetic "GrimAge" clocks incorporate DNA-methylation surrogates for GDF15 alongside [[ADM]], [[B2M]], [[Cystatin C]], and [[PAI-1]], translating its protein level into an accelerated-aging signal. GDF15, [[IL-6|IL-6]], [[IL-8]], and other SASP factors are proposed as minimally invasive markers to monitor senolytic and senomorphic interventions.
- **Senescent-cell origin in aging tissues**: With age, accumulation of senescent cells in liver, adipose, skeletal muscle, and vasculature raises systemic GDF15, which in turn promotes [[Sarcopenia]], insulin resistance, and [[Inflammaging]] through paracrine/endocrine relays — a form of [[Paracrine Senescence]].
- **Frailty and prognosis**: Serum GDF15 is strongly correlated with age, sex, and [[Frailty index|Frailty Index]] scores in individuals with age-related frailty, and independently predicts cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. It therefore sits at the intersection of cellular senescence, organ-level dysfunction, and clinical outcomes.

## GDF15 and Senotherapeutics

Because GDF15 is both a senescence marker and a downstream effector of stress-response pathways targeted by longevity interventions, it is a useful pharmacodynamic readout:

- **Metformin**: Metformin-induced mild ETC inhibition raises circulating GDF15, which mediates the drug's weight-loss and possibly some metabolic benefits; GDF15 levels track metformin exposure and may reflect its senomorphic/mitohormetic activity.
- **Rapamycin / mTOR inhibition**: [[Rapamycin]] and [[mTOR]] modulation, alongside [[AMPK]] activation, reshape the ISR and SASP; changes in GDF15 can report on mitigation of senescent burden.
- **Exercise & mitohormesis**: [[Exercise]] and [[Caloric Restriction]] stimulate [[Mitohormesis]] and raise GDF15 acutely, but chronic training is associated with lower basal senescence burden — illustrating that transient GDF15 spikes (adaptive hormesis) differ from the sustained elevation characteristic of pathological senescence.
- **Senolytics / senomorphics**: [[Senolytic Therapy|Senolytics]] (e.g., [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]], [[Navitoclax]]) and [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphics]] that blunt the SASP are expected to lower circulating GDF15, supporting its use as a treatment-response biomarker in trials such as the [[TAME Trial]].

> [!warning] Interpretive caveat
> An elevated GDF15 is not specific to senescence — it rises in acute mitochondrial disease, cancer, kidney injury, and inflammation. A sustained age-related increase, especially within a multi-SASP biomarker panel, is the signal most consistent with accumulated cellular senescence.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Age-related disease**: Elevated GDF15 associates with [[Cardiovascular Disease]], [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Chronic Kidney Disease]], [[Sarcopenia]], and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegeneration]] — conditions in which senescent-cell burden is implicated.
- **Cancer cachexia & anorexia**: GFRAL-mediated GDF15 signaling drives the anorexia/cachexia of advanced malignancy, a context where tumor and stromal senescence also contribute to the secretome.
- **Diagnostic utility**: Plasma GDF15 is a practical, scalable marker of systemic stress and senescence load, complementing [[Senescence-Associated Beta-Galactosidase|SA-β-gal]], [[CDKN1A|p21]], and [[p53|p16/p53]] assessments in research and clinical-aging studies.
