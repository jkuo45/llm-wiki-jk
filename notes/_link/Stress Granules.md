# Stress Granules

## Overview

Stress granules are cytoplasmic ribonucleoprotein (RNP) condensates that assemble rapidly in response to cellular stress, including oxidative stress, heat shock, and viral infection. They are membraneless organelles formed by [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|liquid-liquid phase separation]] and are commonly marked by G3BP1. Stress granules transiently sequester messenger RNAs and [[RNA-Binding Proteins]] to halt translation and protect the transcriptome, then dissolve once the stress resolves. Their dysregulation is increasingly linked to [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]].

## Sequestration of RNA-Binding Proteins

A defining feature of stress granules is their capacity to concentrate and immobilize [[RNA-Binding Proteins]], including [[TDP-43]]. Aberrant or persistent stress granules trap TDP-43 and other RBPs, promoting their aggregation and depletion from the nucleus — a hallmark of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and frontotemporal dementia. This sequestration disrupts RNA processing and contributes to [[Neuroinflammation]] and neuronal vulnerability. The G3BP1 core component of stress granules also directly binds the [[cGAS]] intrinsically disordered region, priming cGAS for DNA binding and suggesting that stress-granule biology intersects with cytosolic DNA sensing.

## Interface with cGAS-STING Signaling

Although stress granules are cytoplasmic, they may interface with the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] through dsRNA and RNA-sensing mechanisms. G3BP1, a stress-granule marker and RBP, is a recurrent cGAS interactor identified in [[cGAS]] interactome studies, implying that RNA metabolism and DNA sensing converge at phase-separated assemblies. This convergence raises the possibility that stress-granule dynamics modulate innate immune activation, providing a mechanistic bridge between RNA-processing defects and the chronic inflammation seen in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and related disorders.
