# Geroprotectors

Geroprotectors are interventions — small molecules, peptides, or behavioral regimens — that slow the rate of aging and delay the onset of multiple [[Age-Related Diseases]] rather than treating any single disease. The term emphasizes a geroscience strategy: modulate the conserved [[Hallmarks of Aging]] to compress morbidity and extend [[Healthspan]].

## Classes & Examples

Classic pharmacological geroprotectors include the mTOR inhibitor [[Rapamycin]] (and [[Rapalogs]]), the AMPK activator [[Metformin]], and [[Senolytics|senolytics]] that clear [[Senescent Cells]]. Dietary and behavioral prototypes include [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Fasting]]. Many act through overlapping nutrient-sensing axes — [[mTOR]], [[AMPK]], [[Sirtuins]], [[NAD+]] — and engage [[Autophagy]].

## Mechanism & Pharmacology

Effective geroprotectors typically converge on a small set of pathways: inhibition of growth signaling, enhancement of stress resistance and proteostasis, and reduction of [[Inflammaging]] and [[Oxidative Stress]]. Their pleiotropic benefit distinguishes them from single-target drugs.

## Longevity Relevance

Geroprotectors are the translational output of geroscience and are increasingly tested in human [[Biomarkers|biomarker]]-guided trials using [[Epigenetic Alterations|epigenetic clocks]] such as [[PhenoAge]] to quantify biological-age postponement.

> [!info] Pleiotropy Principle
> A genuine geroprotector should delay *multiple* age-related pathologies simultaneously; benefit against a single disease suggests a conventional drug instead.
