# DNA-SCARS

DNA-SCARS (DNA segments with chromatin alterations reinforcing senescence) are persistent, spatially distinct nuclear structures that form at sites of unresolved DNA damage in senescent cells. First characterized by Rodier et al. (2011, PMID 21138974), DNA-SCARS sustain the DNA-damage response (DDR) that maintains the senescence growth arrest and promote secretion of inflammatory cytokines that constitute the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]. They are discussed in the npj Aging review as persistent DDR foci that may evict histones from nucleosomes, increasing chromatin flexibility for transcriptional reprogramming (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Definition

DNA-SCARS are long-lived nuclear foci marked by DNA damage and repair proteins (γ-H2A.X/[[H2A.X]], 53BP1, MDC1) together with chromatin alterations (loss of histones, altered marks). They persist for months in arrested cells and are functionally distinct from transient, repairable damage foci.

## Formation and Mechanism

DNA-SCARS arise when DDR signaling is chronically activated — by telomere dysfunction, oncogene activation, or genotoxic stress — and repair is incomplete. They are enriched in persistent [[H2A.X|γ-H2A.X]] and retain DDR kinases (ATM/ATR). Mechanistically, DNA-SCARS are thought to evict histones from nucleosomes, making damaged chromatin more mobile and flexible and permissive for transcriptional reprogramming (including SASP genes). They may also serve as precursors to [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments|cytoplasmic chromatin fragments (CCF)]] that trigger inflammation via cGAS–STING.

## Role in SASP

DNA-SCARS are central to both arms of the senescence phenotype:

- **Growth arrest:** They sustain DDR/ATM–p53–p21 and p16^INK4a signaling that locks cells in arrest.
- **SASP:** By evicting histones and increasing chromatin flexibility, DNA-SCARS facilitate the epigenetic reprogramming that drives SASP. Persistent DDR at SCARS activates [[NFKB|NF-κB]] and cytokines such as [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]] (Rodier et al., 2011). CCF derived from SCARS are enriched in heterochromatin marks and activate cGAS–STING–NF-κB.

## Mechanistic Details

The transcriptional changes of senescence are underlain by the balance of active and repressive marks at SCARS-adjacent chromatin. Histone loss at these foci increases nucleosome decompaction, enabling pioneer factors and readers to engage SASP loci. The exact mechanistic link between histone loss and CCF remains undefined, but SCARS are upstream of the CCF/inflammasome axis.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

Because SCARS sustain SASP, resolving persistent DDR (e.g., enhancing repair or clearing SCARS) is a potential [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] strategy. Biomarkers such as persistent γ-H2A.X/53BP1 foci report SCARS burden in aging tissues (see [[KDM4]], [[BRD4]], [[DOT1L]]).
