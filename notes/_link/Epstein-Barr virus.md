# Epstein-Barr virus

**Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)** is a double-stranded [[DNA virus]] of the [[Herpesviridae]] family (subfamily Gammaherpesvirinae). It infects >90% of the adult human population worldwide and establishes lifelong [[Latency]] in [[B Cells]].

## Virology

EBV has a ~172 kb genome encoding ~80 proteins. The virion is ~150-200 nm with an icosahedral capsid surrounded by a [[Tegument]] and [[Lipid Envelope]] containing [[Glycoproteins]] (gp350, gB, gH/gL) required for [[B Cell]] entry via [[CD21]] (CR2) and [[HLA Class II]].

## Infection and Latency

Primary infection occurs via [[Saliva]] (infectious mononucleosis), infecting [[Oropharyngeal Epithelial Cells]] and then [[B Cells]]. EBV establishes three latency programs (Latency 0/III) expressing [[EBNA1]], [[EBNA2]], [[LMP1]], [[LMP2A]], and [[EBERs]]. [[LMP1]] constitutively activates [[NFκB]], [[JNK]], and [[PI3K]]-[[Akt]] signaling, mimicking a constitutively active [[CD40 receptor]].

## Associated Diseases

- [[Infectious Mononucleosis]] — acute primary infection
- [[Burkitt Lymphoma]] — endemic form in malaria-endemic regions
- [[Hodgkin Lymphoma]] — EBV present in ~40% of cases
- [[Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma]] — strongly associated with Latency II infection
- [[Post-transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder]] — in immunosuppressed patients
- [[Multiple Sclerosis]] — EBV infection is a necessary risk factor; molecular mimicry between [[EBNA1]] and [[Myelin Basic Protein]]
