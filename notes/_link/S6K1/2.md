# S6K1/2

**S6K1/2** (ribosomal protein S6 kinase 1 and 2; p70S6K/p85S6K) are AGC-family serine/threonine kinases that function as primary downstream effectors of [[mTORC1]]. They phosphorylate ribosomal protein S6 and a battery of translational regulators to promote protein synthesis, and they provide **negative feedback** that dampens upstream [[PI3K]]-[[Akt]]-[[IRS1]] signaling.

## Activation

[[mTORC1]] phosphorylates and activates S6K1 at Thr389 (Thr389 in S6K1; analogous site in S6K2) [^1]. Active S6K1/2 then phosphorylates:
- **Ribosomal protein S6** — enhances translation of 5'-TOP mRNAs.
- **[[IRS1]]** (e.g., Ser1101) — negative feedback that desensitizes [[Insulin Signaling]] and PI3K-Akt signaling [^2].
- **eIF4B, PDCD4** — translational regulators.

## Role in Rapamycin Hormesis (mTOR-PI3K Incoherent Bivalent Motif)

In the mass-action model of Cerrillo, Vidakovic & Míguez (2026), S6K1/2 is central to the rapamycin [[Incoherent Bivalent Motif]]:
- Active S6K1/2 **inactivates** [[mTORC2]] (via mSIN1 phosphorylation) [^3] — one branch of the incoherent loop.
- S6K1/2-mediated [[IRS1]] phosphorylation is the downstream-controlled node of the *backward* (mTORC1→IRS1) link.
- The Michaelis-Menten constant for mTORC1→S6K1/2 activation is significantly **lower** than other interactions (narrow distribution, low median), placing this link in the [[Saturated Enzymatic Regime]] that maximizes [[Biphasic Dose-Response Curve|hormesis probability]] [^4].

Thus S6K1/2 is the kinetic "hinge" whose saturation makes the backward reaction thresholded, enabling rapamycin's inverted-U dose response (peak ~1 nM).

## Physiological and Pathological Roles

- **Cell growth / hypertrophy**: Drives ribosomal biogenesis and protein synthesis.
- **Metabolic disease**: S6K1-mediated IRS1 phosphorylation creates negative feedback contributing to [[Insulin Resistance]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]].
- **Aging**: Part of the mTOR-driven aging axis; inhibition mirrors benefits of [[Rapamycin]] and [[Caloric Restriction]].
