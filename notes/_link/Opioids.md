# Opioids

**Opioids** are a class of analgesic compounds — including opium-derived alkaloids (morphine, codeine), semi-synthetics (oxycodone, heroin), and fully synthetic agents (fentanyl, methadone, [[Tramadol]], meperidine) — that activate opioid receptors (μ, κ, δ) to relieve pain. Their therapeutic effects (analgesia, cough suppression, antidiarrheal) are countered by well-known adverse effects: respiratory depression, constipation, tolerance, dependence, and opioid-use disorder.

## Serotonergic Risk & Drug Interactions

While most opioids act predominantly through opioid receptors, several possess clinically relevant serotonergic activity. [[Tramadol]], meperidine, methadone, and dextromethorphan inhibit serotonin reuptake or release, raising synaptic [[Serotonin]]. When combined with serotonotonergic drugs — [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors]], [[Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor|MAO inhibitors]], or the MAO-A inhibitor [[Methylene blue]] — they can precipitate [[Serotonin Syndrome]], a potentially life-threatening toxidrome of hyperthermia, autonomic instability, and neuromuscular hyperactivity.

## Relevance in This Vault

The vault's focus on [[Methylene blue]] as a phenothiazine redox agent is directly relevant: methylene blue's reversible MAO-A inhibition creates a contraindication with serotonergic opioids. The FDA boxed warning for serious CNS reactions with methylene blue extends to such co-administration, a key safety consideration in perioperative and psychiatric contexts.
