# Senescence

[[notes/_link/Senescence]], or cellular senescence, is a state of permanent cell cycle arrest that occurs when cells are subjected to various stressors, including [[notes/_link/DNA Damage]], oxidative stress, or oncogene activation.


Cellular senescence is a state of permanent cell cycle arrest that occurs in response to various stressors, characterized by the secretion of pro-inflammatory factors known as the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].


## Roadblock to Reprogramming
Senescence is considered a major roadblock during the induction of pluripotency.
- The process of [[Cellular Reprogramming]] using [[OSKM]] factors, especially via viral delivery, can trigger a senescence-like response in [[Fibroblasts]].
- This is often mediated by the activation of [[notes/_link/p53]] and its downstream targets in response to [[Innate Immunity|innate immune]] signaling and [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS).
- Activation of senescence prevents the cells from continuing the division cycles necessary to complete the reprogramming process.


## Markers
Genes like *GDF15* and *SERPINE1* are often associated with the senescence response observed during early reprogramming events.
