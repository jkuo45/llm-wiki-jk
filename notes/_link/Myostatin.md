# Myostatin

## Overview

**Myostatin** (GDF-8) is a transforming growth factor-β superfamily cytokine produced predominantly in [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] (also heart and adipose tissue) that restrains muscle growth. Loss-of-function mutations produce a striking "double-muscling" phenotype — seen in Belgian Blue cattle and in rare humans — confirming its role as a brake on muscle accretion.

## Mechanism of Action & Physiology

Myostatin is synthesized as a precursor, cleaved to the active C-terminal dimer, and circulates bound to latency-associated peptide and follistatin. It binds activin type IIB receptors (ACVR2B), recruiting ALK4/5 to phosphorylate [[SMAD|SMAD2/3]], which repress myogenic genes and suppress myoblast proliferation and differentiation. In parallel, myostatin antagonizes anabolic [[Akt|Akt]]/[[mTORC1]] signaling and promotes protein catabolism, thereby limiting [[Muscle Hypertrophy|hypertrophy]] and favoring [[Muscle Atrophy]].

## Therapeutic Relevance

Because blocking myostatin increases muscle mass, strength, and often reduces adiposity, it is a candidate therapy for [[Sarcopenia]], [[Cachexia]], [[Muscular Dystrophies (MD)|muscular dystrophy]], and frailty of aging. Approaches include anti-myostatin antibodies, ACVR2B ligand traps, follistatin overexpression, and the [[Selective Androgen Receptor Modulators|SARMs]] combinations used to amplify anabolism. Myostatin inhibition also improves bone density, linking muscle and skeletal health in [[Aging]].
