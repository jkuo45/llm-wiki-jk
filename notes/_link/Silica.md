# Silica

**Silica (SiO₂)** is a crystalline mineral that, when inhaled as fine particulate matter, is a potent activator of the [[NLRP3]] inflammasome. Phagocytosed silica crystals cause lysosomal membrane rupture, releasing [[Cathepsin B]] into the cytosol and triggering [[IL-1β]] maturation. Chronic silica exposure causes silicosis, an incurable fibrotic lung disease, and increases risk for autoimmune diseases and lung cancer.

## Mechanism of NLRP3 Activation

Silica activates the NLRP3 inflammasome through the lysosomal damage pathway:

1. **Phagocytosis** — Alveolar [[Macrophages]] engulf silica particles
2. **Lysosomal Rupture** — Sharp, non-degradable silica crystals physically damage lysosomal membranes, causing lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP)
3. **Cathepsin B Release** — Lysosomal proteases including [[Cathepsin B]] spill into the cytosol
4. **NLRP3 Activation** — Cytosolic Cathepsin B and other danger signals trigger NLRP3 oligomerization
5. **Inflammasome Assembly** — NLRP3 recruits [[ASC]] speck and [[Caspase-1]]
6. **IL-1β Maturation** — Caspase-1 cleaves pro-IL-1β into the mature secreted form

Lysosomal membrane permeabilization by silica is potentiated by [[Lipid Peroxidation]] of lysosomal membranes, creating a feedforward loop with [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Pathological Consequences

- **Silicosis** — Chronic occupational silica inhalation (mining, sandblasting, stone cutting) causes progressive fibrotic lung disease characterized by persistent NLRP3 activation, [[IL-1β]] secretion, and granulomatous inflammation
- **Autoimmune Disease** — Silica exposure increases risk of [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]] (Caplan syndrome), [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]], and scleroderma, likely through chronic inflammasome activation
- **Lung Cancer** — IARC classifies crystalline silica as a Group 1 carcinogen
- **Tuberculosis** — Silica-exposed workers have increased susceptibility to TB due to impaired macrophage function

## Occupational Exposure

Major at-risk occupations include mining, quarrying, sandblasting, foundry work, ceramic manufacturing, and hydraulic fracturing. Regulatory limits (OSHA PEL: 50 μg/m³ respirable crystalline silica) aim to reduce chronic exposure.
