# E2F1

E2F transcription factor 1 (E2F1) is a protein that regulates the expression of genes involved in the cell cycle and [[Apoptosis]].

[[E2F1]] is a transcription factor that plays a crucial role in the control of [[Cell Cycle]] and action of tumor suppressor proteins and is also a target of the transforming proteins of small DNA tumor viruses.

**E2F1** (E2F Transcription Factor 1) is a member of the E2F family of transcription factors. It plays a dual role in cell biology, acting as a master regulator of cell cycle progression (facilitating the G1/S transition) and as a potent driver of apoptosis in response to severe DNA damage or oncogenic stress.

## Feedback Loop with SIRT1

E2F1 and the sirtuin [[SIRT1]] form a reciprocal negative regulatory feedback loop that controls apoptotic cell death:

- **E2F1 Induces SIRT1**: E2F1 binds directly to the *SIRT1* promoter and induces its transcription, leading to a rise in SIRT1 protein levels.
- **SIRT1 Inhibits E2F1**: E2F1 physically associates with [[SIRT1]], and SIRT1 acts as a highly efficient inhibitor of E2F1's pro-apoptotic transcriptional functions. SIRT1-mediated suppression of E2F1 protects cancer cells (such as U2OS osteosarcoma and H1299 lung cancer lines) against DNA damage-induced apoptosis, favoring cell survival and recovery.
