# Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)

**Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)** is a progressive [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative disorder]] marked by selective degeneration of both upper and lower [[Motor Neurons|motor neurons]], resulting in [[Muscle Atrophy|muscle wasting]], weakness, paralysis, and ultimately respiratory failure. Most cases are sporadic; ~10% are familial (e.g., *SOD1*, *C9orf72*, *TARDBP*, *FUS* mutations).

## Pathophysiology

Mechanisms include:

- Excitotoxicity and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]]
- Mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired [[Energy Metabolism|cellular energy metabolism]]
- Protein aggregation and defective [[Autophagy]]
- Neuroinflammation and [[Glial Cells|glial]] activation

Because motor neurons are among the most metabolically demanding cells, energy-buffering systems such as the [[Creatine Phosphate Shuttle]] are of interest therapeutically.

## Creatine and ALS

[[Creatine in Health and Disease]] notes that animal and early human studies of [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine supplementation]] in [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]] yielded mixed results. A large multicenter trial (Bender et al.) in patients with Parkinson's, Huntington's, and **ALS** (creatine 9.5 g/day up to 5 years; n=1687) found no statistically significant improvement in ALS patient outcomes. The limited human efficacy may reflect that symptoms appear only after substantial motor-neuron loss.

## Management

Current disease-modifying therapies (riluzole, edaravone, tofersen for *SOD1*) modestly slow progression; care is supportive.
