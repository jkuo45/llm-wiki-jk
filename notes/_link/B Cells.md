# B Cells

## Overview

[[B Cells]] are adaptive immune lymphocytes that originate in the bone marrow and differentiate into antibody-secreting plasma cells upon antigen encounter. Although historically understudied in brain disorders, B cells are now recognized as infiltrating passengers in the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] during neurodegeneration and neuroinflammation, where they modulate both humoral and cellular immune responses.

## B Cells in CNS Inflammation and cGAS-STING

Periphery-derived B cells enter the CNS in neurodegenerative disease and contribute to [[Neuroinflammation]] through cytokine secretion and autoantibody production. The [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis shapes B-cell responses: STING activation influences B-cell priming and can promote regulatory B-cell subsets with anti-inflammatory potential, while dysregulated cGAS-STING–driven autoimmunity favors pathogenic autoantibody generation. The balance between protective and pathogenic B-cell activity is therefore a key determinant of CNS outcome.

## Therapeutic Considerations

Given their dual role, B cells represent both a biomarker and a therapeutic target in neurodegeneration. Strategies that tilt cGAS-STING–shaped B-cell responses toward regulation could dampen chronic CNS inflammation, whereas unchecked B-cell autoimmunity may require suppression.
