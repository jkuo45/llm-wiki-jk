# PDGFAA

**PDGFAA (platelet-derived growth factor AA)** is a PDGF-A homodimer secreted by senescent fibroblasts and endothelial cells at sites of cutaneous injury. It induces myofibroblast differentiation and is required for optimal tissue repair, illustrating a beneficial role of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] (Demaria et al., PMID 24702405).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

PDGF-AA binds preferentially the PDGF receptor α ([[PDGFR]]α/CD140a), activating receptor dimerization and tyrosine kinase signaling through [[PI3K]], [[MAPK]]/ERK, and PLCγ pathways that drive cell migration, proliferation, and differentiation into α-SMA–positive myofibroblasts. In wounded skin, senescent cells accumulate transiently at the wound edge and release PDGF-AA (together with osteopontin) to recruit and differentiate fibroblasts into matrix-producing myofibroblasts, supporting re-epithelialization, vascularization, and extracellular-matrix deposition.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- PDGF-AA is a beneficial, context-specific SASP factor deployed during acute injury repair.
- It promotes tissue regeneration through myofibroblast differentiation and matrix production.
- Genetic or pharmacological removal of senescent cells (e.g., 24 h after injury) decreases myofibroblast differentiation and impairs wound closure, demonstrating that senescence is temporarily required for healing.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance
- PDGF-AA exemplifies why indiscriminate senolysis can be harmful in healing/regenerative contexts.
- PDGF pathways are targeted in fibrotic and atherosclerotic disease (where chronic PDGF signaling is maladaptive).
- Timing senolytic interventions to avoid acute repair windows is a key translational consideration.
