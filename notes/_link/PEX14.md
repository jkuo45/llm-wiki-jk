# PEX14

[[PEX14]] encodes an **integral peroxisomal membrane protein (PMP)** that serves as the **primary docking site** for the [[PEX5]] (PTS1 receptor) at the peroxisomal membrane. Together with [[PEX13]], PEX14 forms the core docking complex that initiates translocation of peroxisomal matrix proteins. PEX14 is unique among peroxins: it directly binds both [[PEX5]] and [[PEX7]], interacts with the translocation machinery, and participates in receptor recycling. PEX14 also plays a moonlighting role as a transcriptional regulator in the nucleus, linking peroxisome biogenesis to cellular signaling pathways.

## Structure & Domains

PEX14 is a ~38 kDa multipass transmembrane protein with three distinct functional regions:

- **N-terminal cytosolic domain** — contains an acidic cluster (Asp/Glu-rich region) that directly binds the PTS1 signal-binding domain of [[PEX5]]. This is the primary receptor docking site. The acidic domain also binds [[PEX7]], enabling PTS2 receptor docking.
- **Central transmembrane region** — contains two transmembrane segments that anchor PEX14 in the peroxisomal membrane. The intermembrane loop contains a conserved motif required for [[PEX13]] interaction.
- **C-terminal peroxisomal matrix-exposed domain** — contains a coiled-coil region that mediates homo-oligomerization and interacts with [[PEX5]] after cargo release. This domain also binds the AAA-ATPase [[PEX1]] for receptor extraction.

PEX14 is the most abundant peroxisomal membrane protein, with estimated copy numbers of ~10,000 per peroxisome in rat hepatocytes. It exists in a 1:1 stoichiometric complex with [[PEX13]], and the complex is further associated with [[PEX2]], [[PEX10]], and [[PEX12]] to form the complete import machinery.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

PEX14 is the central hub of the peroxisomal import machinery:

1. **Receptor Docking** — [[PEX5]] binds PEX14's N-terminal acidic domain with high affinity (Kd ~50 nM). This binding positions PEX5 for membrane insertion and cargo translocation. PEX14 also directly binds [[PEX7]], though with lower affinity.
2. **Translocation Pore** — PEX14 homo-oligomerizes to form a protein-conducting channel. PEX5 inserts its C-terminal region through this pore, and cargo is released into the matrix.
3. **Receptor Release & Recycling** — After cargo release, PEX5 remains bound to PEX14 via its C-terminal domain. The AAA-ATPase [[PEX1]]/[[PEX6]] extracts PEX5 from the PEX14 complex, with monoubiquitination by [[PEX2]]/[[PEX10]]/[[PEX12]] serving as the signal for extraction.
4. **Pexophagy Signal** — When PEX5 recycling is impaired, excess ubiquitinated PEX5 accumulates on PEX14, triggering [[Pexophagy]]-mediated degradation of entire peroxisomes.

PEX14 also functions outside peroxisomes:

- **Nuclear role** — PEX14 translocates to the nucleus under certain conditions, where it interacts with the transcription factor [[TFEB]] (Transcription Factor EB) to regulate lysosomal and peroxisomal gene expression.
- **Signaling scaffold** — PEX14 interacts with [[AMPK]] and [[mTORC1]] at the peroxisomal surface, linking peroxisome metabolism to nutrient sensing pathways.

## Physiological Function

PEX14 is essential for importing virtually all peroxisomal matrix proteins. Its physiological roles include:

- **Fatty acid metabolism** — imports β-oxidation enzymes for [[Fatty acid]] degradation (very-long-chain, branched-chain, and dicarboxylic acids).
- **Plasmalogen synthesis** — imports enzymes for ether phospholipid biosynthesis, essential for myelin sheath integrity and cardiac function.
- **Bile acid synthesis** — imports peroxisomal enzymes that convert C27 bile acid intermediates to mature C24 bile acids (cholic acid, chenodeoxycholic acid).
- **Detoxification** — imports catalase, urate oxidase, and D-amino acid oxidase for reactive oxygen species and xenobiotic metabolism.
- **Ether lipid signaling** — PEX14-imported enzymes produce platelet-activating factor (PAF) and related ether lipid mediators.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Mutations in *PEX14* cause **PEX14-deficient Zellweger Spectrum Disorder (ZSD)**, a severe autosomal recessive condition. PEX14 mutations are rare (~1-2% of ZSD cases) but cause the full spectrum:

- **Zellweger syndrome** (severe) — craniofacial dysmorphism, hepatomegaly, renal cysts, severe hypotonia, seizures, death in infancy.
- **Neonatal Adrenoleukodystrophy** (intermediate) — progressive demyelination, adrenal insufficiency.
- **Infantile Refsum Disease** (mild) — retinitis pigmentosa, hepatic fibrosis, skeletal anomalies.

Missense mutations in the acidic docking domain (e.g., p.Asp298Gly) partially impair [[PEX5]] binding, causing milder phenotypes. Truncating mutations abolish PEX14 function entirely, causing lethal Zellweger syndrome.

Biallelic PEX14 knockout in mice is embryonic lethal. Conditional knockout in hepatocytes causes severe hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and early death, reproducing key features of human ZSD.
