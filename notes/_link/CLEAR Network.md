# CLEAR Network

## Overview

The [[CLEAR Network]] (Coordinated Lysosomal Expression and Regulation) is a gene-regulatory network that coordinates the expression of lysosomal and autophagy-related genes. It is defined by a shared promoter motif, the CLEAR element ([[CLEAR Element]]), recognized by the MiT/TFE family of basic helix-loop-helix transcription factors, principally [[TFEB]] and [[TFE3]]. Activation of the CLEAR network drives a coordinated "lysosomal–autophagic" transcriptional program.

## Structure & Composition

The network comprises hundreds of target genes whose promoters contain CLEAR elements (GTCACGTGAC). These targets include lysosomal hydrolases, v-ATPase subunits, transmembrane transporters, and autophagy effectors such as those involved in [[Autophagosome]] biogenesis. The master regulators, TFEB and TFE3, belong to the [[MiT-TFE family]] of bHLH-LZ transcription factors.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Under nutrient-replete conditions, TFEB is phosphorylated by active [[mTOR]] complex 1 ([[mTORC1]]) at the lysosomal surface and retained in the cytoplasm through interaction with [[Rag GTPase|Rag GTPases]] and [[14-3-3 protein]]. Upon starvation, [[mTOR]] inhibition releases TFEB, which translocates to the nucleus and binds CLEAR elements, upregulating lysosomal biogenesis and [[Autophagy]]. This integrates the network with upstream nutrient sensors including [[AMPK]] and the [[ULK1]] initiation complex. [[Sirtuins|SIRT1]] can deacetylate and activate TFEB, coupling the CLEAR network to NAD+-dependent stress responses.

## Physiological Function

The CLEAR network enables cells to expand lysosomal capacity and autophagic throughput during stress, supporting proteostasis, lipid catabolism ([[Lysosomal Lipolysis]]), and clearance of damaged organelles. It is central to the adaptive response to fasting and exercise.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Dysregulated CLEAR signaling contributes to [[Lysosomal Storage Diseases]], neurodegeneration, and [[Cancer]] metabolic rewiring. Pharmacologic TFEB activation is being explored to boost [[Lysosome]] function in aging and proteinopathies.
