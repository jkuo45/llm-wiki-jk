# Dorsal Root Ganglion

## Overview

The [[Dorsal Root Ganglion]] (DRG) is a collection of sensory neuron cell bodies located along the spinal dorsal roots. DRG neurons, including [[Nociceptor|nociceptors]], transmit somatosensory and pain information from the periphery to the central nervous system and are a primary site of pathological change in peripheral neuropathies.

## DRG and cGAS-STING–Mediated Pain

cGAS-STING signaling within DRG sensory neurons contributes to neuropathic pain. Nerve injury or chemotherapeutic insult can activate the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis in DRG neurons, amplifying nociceptive signaling and allodynia. Because DRG neurons are anatomically positioned at the interface of periphery and CNS, their cGAS-STING activation also interfaces with infiltrating immune cells and local [[Inflammation]], further sustaining pain states.

## Clinical Implications

Targeting DRG-localized cGAS-STING activation offers a peripheral strategy to alleviate chronic pain without broadly suppressing central immune function. Understanding how DNA damage and [[mtDNA]] leakage engage cGAS within DRG neurons may reveal mechanistic entry points for neuropathic pain therapeutics.
