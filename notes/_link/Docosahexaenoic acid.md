# Docosahexaenoic acid

**Docosahexaenoic acid** (DHA, 22:6(n-3)) is an omega-3 [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|polyunsaturated fatty acid]] with six cis double bonds, making it one of the most oxidatively labile lipids in biology. It is highly enriched in neuronal membranes, retinal photoreceptors, and spermatozoa.

## Susceptibility to Lipid Peroxidation

Each bis-allylic methylene group in DHA is a target for hydrogen abstraction by [[Hydroxyl radical]] (•OH) and other radical species. With six double bonds, DHA has approximately 10-fold higher oxidizability than linoleic acid (18:2) and ~3-fold higher than [[Arachidonic acid]] (20:4). The resulting lipid peroxidation products, including 4-HHE (4-hydroxyhexenal, the DHA analog of [[4-Hydroxynonenal]]), [[Malondialdehyde]], and neuroprostanes, contribute to neuronal injury in [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]].

## Physiological Functions

- Essential component of neuronal membranes, modulating fluidity and ion channel function
- Precursor for neuroprotectin D1 (NPD1), a specialized pro-resolving mediator
- Critical for visual function (enriched in retinal rod outer segments)
- Supports synaptic plasticity and cognitive function
