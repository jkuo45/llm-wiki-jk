# HMGB2

HMGB2 (High Mobility Group Box 2) is an abundant, non-histone nuclear protein belonging to the high-mobility-group (HMG) family of chromatin architectural factors. Characterized by two HMG-box DNA-binding domains, HMGB2 binds and sharply bends/loops DNA without sequence specificity, thereby modulating nucleosome positioning and higher-order [[Chromatin Remodeling|chromatin organization]]. In the senescence literature, HMGB2 has a dual, context-dependent role: it is progressively lost as cells enter senescence, yet when chromatin-bound it actively shields [[SASP]] gene loci from the spread of repressive heterochromatin, as summarized in the npj Aging review on the epigenetic landscape of [[Senescence]] (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Overview

The HMGB family comprises HMGB1, HMGB2, HMGB3 and HMGB4. HMGB2 is broadly expressed in development and in many somatic tissues, where it acts as a "DNA chaperone" that destabilizes nucleosome packaging and promotes transcription factor access. Like its paralog [[HMGB1]], HMGB2 can be passively released from damaged cells and function as a damage-associated molecular pattern (alarmin), but its nuclear, chromatin-bound function is the focus of senescence research.

## Structure and Function

The two HMG boxes (A and B) confer the ability to recognize and kink distorted DNA, including cisplatin-modified and bent structures, while an acidic C-terminal tail modulates interactions. By inducing DNA bending and loop formation, HMGB2 influences [[Topologically Associating Domain|TAD]] boundaries, CTCF clustering, and the three-dimensional folding of the genome.

## Role in Senescence and SASP

HMGB2 is progressively depleted in senescent cells and aging tissues (Zirkel et al., 2018, PMID 29779939; Aird et al., 2016, PMID 27893459). This depletion triggers a shift between constitutive and facultative [[Heterochromatin|heterochromatin]] and coincides with elevated expression of [[SASP]] genes in replicative senescence. Strikingly, the function of HMGB2 is model-dependent:

- In **replicative senescence (RS)**, HMGB2-loss remodels binding genome-wide but does not directly impact SASP loci (Zirkel et al., 2018).
- In **oncogene-induced senescence (OIS)**, chromatin-bound HMGB2 binds directly to SASP gene loci, protecting them from the encroachment of repressive heterochromatin and preventing their sequestration into [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]], thereby keeping SASP genes accessible and expressed (Aird et al., 2016). Loss of HMGB2 in OIS thus *silences* SASP.

Notably, HMGB2 depletion does not affect SAHF formation in either RS or OIS, distinguishing its SASP-protective role from the HIRA/SAHF axis. HMGB2 loss also disrupts genomic organization and induces ectopic CTCF clustering across cell types (Zirkel et al., 2018).

## Mechanistic Details

At SASP loci, HMGB2 acts as a physical barrier that blocks the spreading of H3K9me3/H3K27me3-marked heterochromatin. Because HMGB2 is a boundary/anti-silencing factor, its decline in senescence permits large-scale compartment switching (A-to-B and B-to-A transitions, so-called BA-switching) that rewires the transcriptome. HMGB2 therefore operates in parallel with the [[METTL3]]/METTL14 m6A complex and other architectural proteins that reshape the 3D genome to drive SASP.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

Knockdown of HMGB2 prevents emergence of the SASP while leaving cell-cycle arrest intact, marking it as a candidate [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] target (Aird et al., 2016). Because HMGB2 loss correlates with chromatin decompaction and inflammaging, preserving or mimicking its architectural function is of interest for interventions against [[Inflammaging|age-related inflammation]]. Epigenetic modulators that spare growth arrest while curtailing SASP — the senomorphic strategy — could leverage the HMGB2 axis.

Like its paralog [[HMGB1]], extracellular HMGB2 acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern (alarmin) that can amplify inflammatory signaling through innate immune receptors, linking nuclear chromatin regulation to systemic inflammation.

## Role in Cancer

Altered HMGB2 expression is observed across multiple malignancies, including hepatocellular, gastric, breast, and hematopoietic cancers, where it can promote metastatic dissemination and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition. Its dual chromatin-architectural and alarmin functions make HMGB2 a candidate biomarker and therapeutic node at the senescence–inflammation–cancer interface.
