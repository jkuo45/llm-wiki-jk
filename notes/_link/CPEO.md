# CPEO

CPEO (Chronic Progressive External Ophthalmoplegia) is a mitochondrial myopathy characterized by progressive ptosis (drooping eyelids) and ophthalmoplegia (paralysis of the eye muscles), often accompanied by skeletal muscle weakness and exercise intolerance.

## Clinical Features

- **Ptosis**: Progressive drooping of the eyelids, typically bilateral
- **Ophthalmoplegia**: Limited eye movement, particularly horizontal gaze
- **Proximal myopathy**: Weakness of limb and trunk muscles
- **Exercise intolerance**: Fatigue and weakness with exertion
- **Retinal pigmentary degeneration**: In some cases (Kearns-Sayre syndrome variant)

## Genetics

CPEO is associated with various [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] abnormalities including:
- Large-scale single deletions (most common)
- Point mutations (e.g., in tRNA genes)
- Multiple deletions due to nuclear gene mutations (e.g., POLG, TWINKLE)
- These mutations impair [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] and [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain|electron transport chain]] function

## Humanin Connection

> [!tip] Humanin as a Biomarker and Potential Therapy
> [[Humanin]] expression is elevated in skeletal muscles of CPEO patients, similar to observations in MELAS. This upregulation likely represents a compensatory stress response to mitochondrial dysfunction. The correlation between humanin levels and mitochondrial disease severity suggests humanin may serve as a biomarker and potential therapeutic target.
