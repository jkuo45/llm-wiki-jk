## Overview

[[TMG]] (trimethylglycine), commonly called betaine, is a zwitterionic osmolyte and methyl donor. It participates in one-carbon metabolism by donating a methyl group to homocysteine to regenerate methionine via the enzyme betaine–homocysteine S-methyltransferase (BHMT), an alternative to the folate-dependent remethylation pathway.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

TMG serves two principal functions:

- **Methyl donation** — in the liver and kidney, BHMT uses TMG to convert homocysteine to methionine, which is then activated to [[SAM]] (S-adenosylmethionine), the universal methyl donor.
- **Osmoprotection** — as a compatible solute, TMG stabilizes proteins and cells under osmotic, thermal, and oxidative stress.

By lowering homocysteine and supporting [[SAM]] supply, TMG indirectly influences [[SAH]] levels and global methylation (DNA, histone). It also supports [[Phosphatidylcholine]] synthesis via the PEMT pathway.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

Betaine supplementation reduces fasting homocysteine, supports liver health, and may protect against non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. It is relevant to epigenetic and metabolic resilience in aging.
