# Glutamate

## Overview
[[Glutamate]] is the principal excitatory neurotransmitter in the central nervous system, essential for learning and synaptic plasticity. However, uncontrolled extracellular accumulation produces [[Excitotoxicity]], a core mechanism in [[Neurodegeneration]] and aging.

## Synthesis & Clearance
Glutamate is derived from glutamine via glutaminase and from the tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediate α-ketoglutarate. Termination of signaling depends on Na+-dependent excitatory amino-acid transporters (EAATs) on astrocytes and neurons, which clear synaptic glutamate and sustain low extracellular tone.

## Mechanism of Action & Excitotoxicity
Excess glutamate overactivates ionotropic receptors, causing sustained Ca2+ influx. Calcium overload stresses [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], drives [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] generation, and activates proteases that damage neurons. This cascade contributes to [[Parkinson's Disease]], [[Alzheimer's Disease]], and stroke injury. Glutamate also feeds into [[Glutathione]] biosynthesis via cysteine availability, coupling excitotoxic redox cost to antioxidant capacity.

## Redox, Sirtuins & Longevity
Excitotoxic injury elevates [[Oxidative Stress]] and depletes [[NAD+]]-dependent resilience, whereas [[Sirtuins]] and [[Methylene blue]] can buffer downstream mitochondrial failure. [[Creatine]] supports neuronal bioenergetics against glutamate challenge. Maintaining glutamate homeostasis is therefore a longevity-relevant neuroprotective target; senotherapeutic and mitohormetic strategies indirectly limit excitotoxic burden.
