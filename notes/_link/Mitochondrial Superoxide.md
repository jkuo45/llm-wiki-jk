# Mitochondrial Superoxide

**Mitochondrial superoxide** is the upstream [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] produced when electrons leak from the [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain]] — chiefly at [[Complex I]] and [[Complex III|Complex III]] — and one-electron reduce molecular oxygen to the superoxide radical (O2•−).

## Generation & Detoxification

Leak is greatest under high [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] and during reverse electron transport at Complex I. Once formed, matrix superoxide is rapidly dismutated by [[Superoxide Dismutase|SOD2 (MnSOD)]] to hydrogen peroxide, which is then removed by [[Glutathione]] and [[Peroxiredoxin|Prx]] systems. Failure of these defenses lets superoxide damage cardiolipin, proteins, and mtDNA, driving [[Oxidative Stress]], [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], and [[Apoptosis]].

## Signaling Versus Damage

Like other [[Mitochondrial ROS]], superoxide has a dose-dependent duality: transient low-level production supports adaptive signaling and [[Mitohormesis]], whereas sustained excess is pathological. Superoxide (and its diffusion-limited conversion products) can trigger mitochondrial permeability transition and cytochrome c release.

## Pharmacological Modulation

[[Methylene blue]] reduces mitochondrial superoxide by acting as a redox cycler that accepts electrons from NADH and donates them downstream, effectively bypassing impaired Complex I/III and diminishing reverse electron transport. This antioxidant/neuroprotective mechanism underlies interest in methylene blue for neurodegenerative and ischemic injury.
