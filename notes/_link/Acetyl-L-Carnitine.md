# Acetyl-L-Carnitine

Acetyl-L-Carnitine (ALC) is the acetylated form of [[L-Carnitine]] (the naturally occurring L-enantiomer). It facilitates the transport of long-chain fatty acids into [[Mitochondria]] for β-oxidation and supplies an acetyl group for energy metabolism and acetylcholine synthesis.

## Relevance to Aging and Glycation

ALC is grouped with agents such as [[Carnosine]] and [[Aminoguanidine]] as a compound shown to help prevent protein [[Cross-linking]] associated with aging. Its proposed anti-aging actions include:

- **Mitochondrial support**: Maintaining mitochondrial function and reducing age-related declines in cellular energy production.
- **Antioxidant and neuroprotective effects**: Buffering oxidative stress that otherwise accelerates [[Glycation]] and AGE formation.
- **Metabolic and cognitive benefits**: Studied for diabetic neuropathy, depression, and age-related cognitive decline.

> [!note]
> In the aminoguanidine literature, ALC is cited alongside [[Carnosine]] and aminoguanidine as a preventative of cross-linking, whereas [[Alagebrium|ALT-711]] is positioned as a breaker of existing links.
