# HDAC4

HDAC4 is a class IIa histone deacetylase that, unlike most HDACs, is catalytically inactive as a deacetylase and instead functions as an epigenetic reader. It is rapidly recruited to [[H3K27ac]]-marked loci to monitor the acetylation status of H3K27 at senescence-activated super-enhancers, where it normally "buffers" the [[AP-1]]-driven transcriptional program. Upon senescence entry, HDAC4 is degraded, releasing the AP-1-mediated senescence program — including SASP expression.

## Overview

HDAC4 belongs to the class IIa HDACs (HDAC4/5/7/9), characterized by a large N-terminal regulatory domain and a C-terminal catalytic domain that, in class IIa members, has lost robust deacetylase activity and instead binds acetylated lysine. HDAC4 shuttles between nucleus and cytoplasm in a 14-3-3– and phosphorylation-dependent manner and is best known for repressing transcription factor programs (e.g., MEF2, RUNX) in muscle and bone.

## Structure & Function

The C-terminal domain of HDAC4 retains a deacetylase-like fold that binds acetyl-lysine motifs (functioning as a reader) but lacks the catalytic residues for hydrolysis. Through its N-terminal domain, HDAC4 interacts with class I HDACs such as [[HDAC3]], bridging reader function to deacetylase activity. This HDAC4–HDAC3 partnership governs chromatin status at AP-1-regulated enhancers.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review details a pivotal role for HDAC4 in the H3K27ac axis. The pioneer factor [[AP-1]] opens enhancers at SASP genes; the reader [[BRD4]] and transcription factor [[CEBPα]] then bind [[H3K27ac]] at these senescence-activated super-enhancers to drive NF-κB-regulated SASP genes. HDAC4 is swiftly recruited to H3K27ac loci to oversee their acetylation status and, through interaction with HDAC3, buffers this program. Upon entry into senescence, HDAC4 degradation diminishes this buffering, allowing the AP-1-mediated senescence transcriptional program — including the SASP — to be unleashed. Consistent with this, depletion of HDAC4 (like AP-1 depletion) enhances expression of NF-κB-regulated SASP genes (ref. 60).

## Mechanistic Details

- HDAC4 recruitment to H3K27ac acts as a surveillance/brake on enhancer activation at SASP loci.
- Its degradation is a senescence-entry event that licenses SASP expression, mechanistically distinct from direct catalytic repression.
- The HDAC4–HDAC3 complex links an acetyl-lysine reader to a catalytically active deacetylase, integrating the H3K27ac landscape with SASP control.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Because HDAC4 normally restrains SASP, its loss promotes inflammaging, whereas stabilizing or mimicking HDAC4 function could be [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]]. Conversely, class I/II HDAC inhibitors (e.g., vorinostat, romidepsin) broadly affect these complexes and are being examined for senescence modulation, though off-target effects are significant. The HDAC4–HDAC3 axis is a candidate node for selective SASP suppression.
