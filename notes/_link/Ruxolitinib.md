# Ruxolitinib

Ruxolitinib (INCB018424) is a selective ATP-competitive inhibitor of Janus kinase 1 ([[JAK1]]) and JAK2, and the first JAK inhibitor approved by the FDA (2011 for myelofibrosis; 2014 for polycythemia vera). It is a senomorphic therapy that targets the JAK–[[STAT3]] pathway to suppress the pro-inflammatory SASP.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

Ruxolitinib inhibits JAK1 and JAK2, the tyrosine kinases that transduce signals from interferon, hematopoietic, and many cytokine receptors. By blocking JAK catalytic activity it prevents phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of downstream [[STAT3]] (and STAT5), shutting off transcription of inflammatory target genes. In senescent cells, JAK–STAT is a major amplifier of the SASP; ruxolitinib abrogates secretion of [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]] and blunts the broader pro-fibrotic and immunomodulatory secretome without killing the cells.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

Ruxolitinib is a prototypic [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] agent: it does not eliminate senescent cells but suppresses their inflammatory output via JAK–STAT blockade. By quelling the SASP it reduces the senescence-associated chronic inflammation that drives [[Inflammaging]] and age-related tissue dysfunction, and it has been used experimentally to "rejuvenate" aspects of the aged phenotype in several models.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

FDA-approved for myelofibrosis, polycythemia vera, and steroid-refractory acute graft-versus-host disease; a topical formulation (ruxolitinib cream, Opzelura) is approved for atopic dermatitis and vitiligo. Repurposing trials are exploring its senomorphic potential in age-related and fibrotic diseases (e.g., pulmonary fibrosis), leveraging its established safety and JAK–STAT suppression.
