# PEX19

[[PEX19]] encodes a **predominantly cytosolic chaperone and import receptor** for peroxisomal membrane proteins (PMPs). PEX19 is the first factor in the PMP import pathway: it recognizes and binds newly synthesized PMPs in the [[Cytosol]], prevents their aggregation, and delivers them to the peroxisomal membrane receptor [[PEX3]] for insertion. PEX19 is essential for *de novo* peroxisome formation from the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] and for the insertion of new PMPs during peroxisome growth and division. Without PEX19, peroxisomal membrane proteins cannot be inserted, and peroxisomes fail to form — causing severe [[Zellweger Spectrum Disorders]].

## Structure & Domains

PEX19 is a ~33 kDa protein with a distinctive domain architecture:

- **N-terminal farnesylation motif** — contains a C-terminal CaaX box (Cys-Val-Ile-Leu) that is post-translationally modified by farnesylation. Farnesylation anchors a fraction of PEX19 to the peroxisomal membrane, but is not absolutely required for function.
- **Central hydrophobic PMP-binding domain** — an α-helical domain that binds the transmembrane segments of PMPs. This domain is intrinsically disordered in the cytosolic form and becomes structured upon PMP binding. The binding is promiscuous: PEX19 can bind virtually any PMP transmembrane segment.
- **C-terminal PEX3-binding domain** — a short peptide motif that binds [[PEX3]] at the peroxisomal membrane. This interaction is essential for PMP delivery and insertion.

PEX19 exists in two pools: a **cytosolic pool** (~85% of total PEX19) that chaperones newly synthesized PMPs, and a **peroxisome-bound pool** (~15%) that is farnesylated and anchored to the membrane. The cytosolic pool is in dynamic equilibrium with the membrane-bound pool.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

PEX19 operates through a defined pathway for PMP import:

1. **PMP Recognition** — PEX19 binds the transmembrane segments of newly synthesized PMPs in the [[Cytosol]] immediately after their synthesis on [[Ribosomes]]. This prevents aggregation and misfolding of hydrophobic membrane proteins in the aqueous cytosol.
2. **Delivery to PEX3** — The PEX19-PMP complex is delivered to [[PEX3]] at the peroxisomal membrane. PEX19's C-terminal domain binds PEX3's cytosolic loop, positioning the PMP's transmembrane segment for insertion.
3. **Membrane Insertion** — PEX3 mediates the actual insertion of the PMP transmembrane segment into the lipid bilayer. PEX19 is released and recycled to the cytosol for another round of import.
4. **De Novo Peroxisome Formation** — PEX19 delivers PEX3 itself to the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] membrane, where PEX3 initiates the formation of new peroxisomal precursor vesicles that bud off and mature into functional peroxisomes.

PEX19 also participates in **peroxisome inheritance**: during cell division, PEX19 delivers PMPs to daughter peroxisomes to ensure equitable distribution of the peroxisomal membrane.

## Physiological Function

PEX19 is essential for importing all peroxisomal membrane proteins, including:

- **Import machinery components** — [[PEX3]], [[PEX13]], [[PEX14]], [[PEX2]], [[PEX10]], [[PEX12]] — the entire peroxisomal import apparatus depends on PEX19 for membrane insertion.
- **Metabolic enzymes** — peroxisomal acyl-CoA oxidase, D-bifunctional protein, and [[Beta-Oxidation]] enzymes that are partially membrane-associated.
- **Transporters** — ABC transporters (ABCD1/ALDP, ABCD2, ABCD3) that export very-long-chain fatty acyl-CoAs from peroxisomes.
- **Proliferation factors** — [[PEX11β]], which mediates peroxisome fission and proliferation.

Without PEX19, no PMPs can be inserted into membranes, and peroxisomes cannot form *de novo* or maintain their membrane integrity. This causes complete loss of peroxisomal function.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Mutations in *PEX19* cause **PEX19-deficient Zellweger Spectrum Disorder (ZSD)**, the most severe form of peroxisomal biogenesis disorders:

- **Zellweger syndrome** — all PEX19 mutations reported to date cause the severe Zellweger phenotype: craniofacial dysmorphism, hepatomegaly, renal cysts, severe hypotonia, seizures, chondrodysplasia punctata, and death within the first year.
- No milder PEX19-associated phenotypes have been described, suggesting that any loss of PEX19 function is catastrophic for peroxisome biogenesis.

PEX19 mutations account for <1% of ZSD cases. Most reported mutations are truncating alleles or missense mutations in the PEX3-binding domain that abolish PMP delivery.

Biallelic PEX19 knockout in mice is embryonic lethal at E9.5-E11.5, with no detectable peroxisomes in any tissue. Conditional knockout in hepatocytes causes complete absence of peroxisomes, severe hepatic dysfunction, and death within weeks.
