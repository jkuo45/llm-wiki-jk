# Bioavailability

**Bioavailability** is the proportion of an administered dose of a compound that reaches the systemic circulation intact and is accessible at its site of action to elicit a pharmacological response. It is a prerequisite for *bioefficacy* — the magnitude of the intended biological effect. Bioavailability is commonly quantified by AUC (area under the plasma concentration–time curve), C<sub>max</sub> (peak concentration), T<sub>max</sub> (time to peak), and half-life (t<sub>1/2</sub>).

## Determinants and Limitations

Poor bioavailability arises from:

- Low aqueous solubility and high lipophilicity (e.g., logP 3.2 for [[Fisetin]]).
- Limited absorption across the gastrointestinal tract.
- Rapid first-pass and phase II metabolism (sulfation, glucuronidation).
- Fast systemic clearance and short half-life.

> [!warning] Fisetin's bioavailability bottleneck
> [[Fisetin]] has only ~44.1% oral [[Bioavailability|bioavailability]], poor water solubility, and undergoes rapid phase II conjugation, making nanodelivery essential to realize its therapeutic potential (Szymczak & Cielecka-Piontek, 2023, PMID 37762460).

## Strategies to Improve Bioavailability

[[Nanotechnology|Nanodelivery]] and structural modification raise bioavailability dramatically — e.g., AUC gains for [[Fisetin]] of ~1.5-fold (oral [[SNEDDS]]), 24-fold (i.p. [[Nanoemulsions|nanoemulsion]]), 47-fold (i.p. [[Liposomes|liposomes]]), and 141-fold (i.p. nanocochleates). Approaches include [[Nanoparticles]], [[Liposomes]], [[Nanoemulsions]], [[Cyclodextrins]], [[Nanocrystals]], and biotechnological carriers such as [[Inulin|inulin]] and probiotic vesicles.
