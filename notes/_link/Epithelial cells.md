# Epithelial cells

**Overview:** [[Epithelial cells]] are the principal cellular constituents of epithelia, avascular sheets that cover external surfaces and line internal cavities and glands. They sit on a basement membrane and display apical–basal polarity, with distinct membrane domains and cytoskeletal organization.

**Structure and organization:** Cells are joined by tight junctions, adherens junctions, desmosomes, and gap junctions that seal the barrier and coordinate signaling. Apical microvilli or cilia increase surface area, while the basal surface interacts with the underlying extracellular matrix. Stem and progenitor cells at crypts or basal layers sustain lifelong renewal.

**Physiological function:** Epithelia mediate barrier protection, selective absorption and secretion, ion and water transport, and sensory reception. They interface with the [[Immune System]] and the [[Gut Microbiome]] at mucosal surfaces, shaping tolerance and [[Inflammation]].

**Pathological relevance:** Loss of polarity, accrual of DNA damage, and senescence drive [[Aging]]-related Barrier decline. Epithelial cells are the origin of the majority of human cancers; acquisition of migratory, invasive behavior through [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition]] (EMT) underpins metastasis. Defective epithelial turnover and [[Cellular Senescence]] contribute to fibrosis and chronic disease.
