# Preiss-Handler Pathway

The Preiss-Handler pathway is the de novo biosynthesis route to [[NAD+]], the essential redox and signaling cofactor. It converts the amino acid [[Tryptophan]] (or dietary [[Niacin|niacin/vitamin B3]]) into NAD+ through a sequence culminating in the ATP-dependent condensation of quinolinate with phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate to form nicotinic acid mononucleotide — the defining "Preiss-Handler reaction."

## Steps & Intermediates

Tryptophan is degraded via the [[Kynurenine Pathway|kynurenine route]] to quinolinate (the committed precursor), whereas niacin enters from the opposite side as nicotinic acid. Quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase then produces nicotinic acid mononucleotide, which is converted to [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide|NMN]] and finally adenylylated to NAD+. This contrasts with the [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]], which recycles [[Nicotinamide Riboside|nicotinamide riboside]]/nicotinamide back to NAD+ via [[NAMPT]].

## Regulation & Physiology

De novo synthesis is comparatively low-capacity and subordinate to salvage in most adult tissues, but it remains essential when salvage precursors are limiting. Because cellular [[NAD+]] declines with [[Aging]], understanding both de novo and salvage routes informs [[Longevity]] strategies using NAD+ precursors.

## Longevity Relevance

The pathway underpins why [[Vitamin B3]] and tryptophan status influence NAD+ availability, linking nutrition to sirtuin and [[PARP]] activity and to the broader sirtuin–NAD+–autophagy longevity network.

> [!info] De Novo vs. Salvage
> Salvage (NAMPT-driven) supplies most steady-state NAD+, but the Preiss-Handler pathway is the only route that builds NAD+ from scratch.
