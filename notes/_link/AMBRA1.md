# AMBRA1

## Overview

[[AMBRA1]] (activating molecule in Beclin 1-regulated autophagy) is a positive regulator of [[Autophagy]] that activates the [[Beclin1]]–[[Vps34]] complex and also functions as a substrate receptor for the cullin-4 E3 ligase, targeting D-type cyclins for degradation. It thus integrates autophagic initiation with cell-cycle control. AMBRA1 is essential during embryogenesis and neuronal development.

## Structure & Domains

AMBRA1 is a large multidomain protein containing an N-terminal region that binds the Beclin 1–Vps34 complex and a central WD40-repeat domain implicated in protein–protein interactions and substrate recruitment. It is regulated by phosphorylation and by interaction with the motor protein dynein, which restricts its activity to the endoplasmic reticulum under fed conditions.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Upon nutrient starvation or [[mTOR]] inhibition, AMBRA1 disengages from dynein, translocates with the [[Beclin1]] complex to the [[Phagophore Assembly Site]], and enhances Vps34-driven [[PtdIns3P]] production, promoting phagophore expansion. Upstream [[ULK1]] phosphorylates AMBRA1 to reinforce its activation. In parallel, AMBRA1 binds the CUL4–DDB1 ligase to degrade cyclin D1, linking autophagy to G1 progression. AMBRA1 intersects the [[Sirtuins|SIRT1]] network and the [[Lysosome]]-directed trafficking machinery, and is opposed by apoptotic cues that cleave it.

## Physiological Function

AMBRA1 sustains basal and induced autophagy required for neural tube closure, axon guidance, and clearance of damaged organelles, protecting cells against [[Oxidative Stress]]. Its cyclin targeting couples autophagic capacity to proliferation.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

AMBRA1 haploinsufficiency causes neurological defects, and its deregulation contributes to [[Cancer]] by simultaneously impairing autophagy and stabilizing oncogenic cyclin D. AMBRA1 is a candidate tumor suppressor and modulator of therapeutic autophagy.
