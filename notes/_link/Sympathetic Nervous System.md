# Sympathetic Nervous System

The **[[Sympathetic Nervous System|sympathetic nervous system (SNS)]]** is the "fight, flight, or freeze" branch of the [[Autonomic Nervous System|autonomic nervous system]]. It mobilizes the body for action by releasing [[Epinephrine]] and [[Norepinephrine]] from the adrenal medulla and postganglionic terminals, alongside [[Cortisol]] from the adrenal cortex. Its cardiovascular effect is to increase heart rate and rhythmicity, which *lowers* [[Heart Rate Variability|HRV]].

## Role in Hormetic Stress

Acute engagement of the SNS is the intended response to mitohormetic stressors — HIIT, cold exposure, fasting, and heat. The stressor is only adaptive, however, if the [[Parasympathetic Nervous System|parasympathetic branch]] subsequently rebounds. When SNS activation is sustained (concurrent severe caloric restriction, daily high-intensity training, and sleep loss), HRV stays suppressed, [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] stalls, [[Mitophagy]] is impaired, and [[Mitohormesis|mitohormesis]] degrades into [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] and [[Inflammation|inflammation]].
