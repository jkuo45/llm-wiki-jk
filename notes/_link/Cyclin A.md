# Cyclin A

**Cyclin A** is a regulatory subunit of the cell-cycle machinery that, in complex with CDK2 (and CDK1), drives S-phase entry and progression, and later promotes G2/M transition. Two paralogs exist: Cyclin A1 (CCNA1, germline) and Cyclin A2 (CCNA2, somatic). Its periodic accumulation and degradation orchestrate DNA replication.

## Structure & Regulation

- **CDK partnering**: Cyclin A–CDK2 initiates and executes S phase; Cyclin A–CDK1 later contributes to mitotic entry.
- **Destruction**: Degraded via the APC/C at the metaphase–anaphase transition, enforcing irreversible cell-cycle progression.

## Role in Senescence

- Cyclin A is among the proliferative genes whose mRNA is bound and stabilized by the RNA-binding protein [[HuR]] (human antigen R). Activated [[AMPK]] in senescent/metabolically stressed cells prevents HuR translocation from the nucleus to the cytosol; with less cytosolic HuR, translation of [[c-Fos]], Cyclin A, and [[Cyclin B]] mRNA falls, contributing to proliferative arrest.
- Thus Cyclin A downregulation is a downstream effector of the metabolic (AMPK/HuR) axis that enforces the [[Senescence|senescent]] cell-cycle exit.
