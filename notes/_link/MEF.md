## Overview

**MEF** (mouse embryonic fibroblast) refers to primary fibroblast cultures derived from day 12.5–14.5 mouse embryos. They are among the most common primary cells in mammalian cell biology, valued for retaining near-normal physiology compared with immortalized lines.

## Origin and Preparation

MEFs are obtained by dissecting and mincing embryos, followed by enzymatic dissociation. They are frequently used as feeder layers for maintaining pluripotent stem cells and as a genotype-validated background (e.g., wild-type vs. [[Knockout mouse]] embryonic fibroblasts). Early-passage MEFs are diploid and contact-inhibited.

## Common Research Uses

MEFs are standard for studies of [[Senescence]], proliferation, [[Apoptosis]], and [[Mitochondria]] function. They are central to assays of DNA damage, oxidative stress, and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype, and serve as controls for transformed lines such as [[HeLa]] and [[HEK293]].

## Relevance to Aging, Cancer, and Longevity

Primary MEFs undergo replicative [[Senescence]] driven by telomere shortening and stress, making them a classic model for [[Aging]] mechanisms and [[Cancer]] suppression (via oncogene-induced senescence). They are used to test senolytics, mitochondrial antioxidants (e.g., MnSOD modulation), and longevity pathways including sirtuins and [[mTOR]].
