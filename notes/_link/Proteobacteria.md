# Proteobacteria

**Proteobacteria** is one of the largest and most physiologically diverse phyla of [[Gram-Negative Bacteria]], encompassing classes such as Alpha-, Beta-, Gamma-, Delta-, and Epsilonproteobacteria. It includes many familiar genera — *Escherichia*, *Salmonella*, *Pseudomonas*, *Helicobacter*, *Campylobacter* — and a substantial fraction of known [[Pathogens]].

## Gut Ecology & Dysbiosis

In the human [[Gut Microbiome]], Proteobacteria normally occupy a minor niche, but their relative abundance often expands during [[Dysbiosis]] — a state linked to antibiotic use, inflammation, and metabolic disruption. Because Proteobacteria are Gram-negative, they bear lipopolysaccharide ([[LPS]], endotoxin) on their outer membrane; heightened Proteobacteria burden can raise circulating LPS, activating innate immunity and promoting low-grade [[Inflammation]] via TLR4 signaling.

## Aging & Disease Associations

Elevated Proteobacteria and reduced overall microbial diversity are recurrent features of aging and age-related disease, contributing to [[Inflammaging]] and associating with [[Obesity]], [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|type 2 diabetes]], and inflammatory bowel disease. Conversely, [[Centenarians]] and healthy-aging cohorts frequently show lower Proteobacteria loads and richer beneficial taxa (e.g., Akkermansia, Bifidobacterium), underscoring the phylum's utility as a dysbiosis marker. Modulating Proteobacteria abundance is an emerging target for [[Longevity]] and metabolic-health interventions.
