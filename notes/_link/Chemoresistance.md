# Chemoresistance

**Chemoresistance** is the ability of cancer cells to survive and proliferate despite exposure to cytotoxic chemotherapy. A major, underappreciated driver is the **senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP)** of therapy-damaged stromal cells in the tumor microenvironment.

## SASP-Driven Chemoresistance and Apigenin Reversal

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - repurposing_apigen_senomorphic.09.09.611999v1.full|Repurposing apigenin for senomorphic effect in antiaging pipelines]]
> Senescent stromal cells (induced by [[Bleomycin]] or [[Mitoxantrone]]) secrete SASP factors — including [[WNT16B]], [[Amphiregulin|AREG]], and [[EREG]] — that confer marked chemoresistance on adjacent cancer cells (PC3, DU145, M12, LNCaP). Conditioned media from senescent stroma shifted dose–response curves to higher viability; apigenin treatment of the stroma reversed this, depriving cancer cells of acquired resistance. In vivo, combining apigenin with mitoxantrone gave an additional 51.1% tumor reduction (74.9% total vs placebo) with enhanced DNA damage and apoptosis.

This positions [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] SASP suppression (e.g., by [[Apigenin]]) as a strategy to improve conventional anticancer regimens.
