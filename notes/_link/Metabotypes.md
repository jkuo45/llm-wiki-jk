# Metabotypes

## Overview

Metabotypes are reproducible, data-driven subgroups of individuals sharing a characteristic metabolic phenotype, identified through clustering of metabolomic, genomic, and clinical data. Rather than a single disease label, a metabotype captures the integrated state of an individual's metabolism at a given time. Examples include NAD+ metabotypes (individuals stratified by endogenous [[Biomarker]] NAD+ flux capacity) and diet-response metabotypes that predict differential glycemic response to identical meals. The concept parallels the "endotype" in inflammatory disease and supports [[Personalized Medicine]].

## Mechanism of Function

Metabotype derivation begins with high-throughput profiling of the [[Metabolome]] (LC-MS, NMR) combined with covariates such as genetics, microbiota, and lifestyle. Unsupervised clustering (k-means, hierarchical, network models) reveals subgroups with distinct mediator concentrations and pathway activities. A metabotype is dynamic: it can shift with intervention, aging, or disease. Biologically, metabotypes reflect differences in enzyme activity, nutrient sensing (e.g., [[PPARγ]] and AMPK tone), mitochondrial capacity, and substrate preference (carbohydrate versus lipid oxidation), linking molecular variation to phenotype.

## Pathological and Clinical Relevance

Metabotypes refine risk stratification beyond body-mass-index or single biomarkers in [[Metabolic Syndrome]] and cardiometabolic disease, predicting who will benefit from specific diets, exercise, or pharmacotherapy. They enable biomarker-guided stratification in clinical trials and may explain inter-individual variability in drug response and adverse effects. Challenges include standardization of collection, analytical validation, and prospective demonstration of improved outcomes. Metabotyping is emerging as a translational bridge between the [[Metabolome]] and actionable [[Personalized Medicine]].
