# Gut Microbiome

**Overview:** The [[Gut Microbiome]] comprises the trillions of microorganisms—bacteria, archaea, viruses, and fungi—residing in the [[Gastrointestinal Tract]]. Dominated by the phyla [[Bacteroidetes]] and [[Firmicutes]], it functions as a metabolic and immune organ in its own right.

**Physiological function:** The microbiome ferments dietary fiber into [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] (notably [[Butyrate]]), synthesizes vitamins, trains the [[Immune System]], maintains barrier integrity, and modulates [[Metabolism]] and [[Inflammation]]. It communicates with the brain via the [[Gut-Brain Axis]] using neural, endocrine, and immune signals.

**Relevance to aging and disease:** With [[Aging]], microbial diversity often declines and [[Dysbiosis]] emerges, correlating with inflammaging, metabolic dysfunction, and neurodegeneration. Dietary patterns, [[Fasting]], and exercise shift composition toward beneficial taxa. Senotherapeutics such as fisetin have been reported to remodel the microbiome alongside healthspan benefits.

**Research context:** The microbiome is a target for interventions—probiotics, prebiotics, fermented foods, and fecal microbiota transplantation—aimed at extending healthspan and mitigating age-related disease.
