# Very Low Density Lipoprotein

Very Low Density Lipoprotein (VLDL) is a triglyceride-rich lipoprotein particle synthesized by the liver and secreted into the bloodstream to transport endogenous lipids to peripheral tissues. It is frequently characterized as the "bad" form of cholesterol precursor because metabolic remodeling of VLDL gives rise to atherogenic [[LDL]] and remnant particles.

## Role in Vascular Pathology

VLDL and its remnants contribute to [[Atherosclerosis]] through several mechanisms:

- **Vessel-wall adhesion**: Glycated and [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGE]]-modified VLDL shows enhanced binding to the proteins of blood-vessel walls, promoting subendothelial retention.
- **Platelet activation**: By increasing VLDL adhesion, the propensity of blood platelets to coagulate and form dangerous clots is amplified.
- **Progression to LDL**: Sequential lipolysis of VLDL yields intermediate-density and low-density lipoproteins, the latter being directly implicated in plaque formation.

## Interaction with AGE Inhibitors

Experimental work summarized in the aminoguanidine literature reports that [[Aminoguanidine]] reduces the ability of VLDL to bind to blood-vessel walls in animal models, an effect that would be expected to lower clot risk and improve vascular function.
