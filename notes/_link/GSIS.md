# GSIS

**Definition:** [[GSIS]] (glucose-stimulated insulin secretion) is the glucose-dependent release of [[Insulin]] from pancreatic beta cells, the central effector of postprandial glucose disposal.

**Mechanism:** After a meal, glucose enters beta cells via GLUT2/GLUT1 and is metabolized through [[Glycolysis]] and [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] oxidation, raising the ATP/ADP ratio. This closes ATP-sensitive K+ (KATP) channels, depolarizing the membrane and opening voltage-gated Ca2+ channels. The Ca2+ surge triggers fusion of insulin granules with the plasma membrane (exocytosis). Amplifying pathways (glucagon-like peptide-1, cAMP) potentiate secretion.

**Physiological importance:** GSIS couples nutrient intake to [[Glucose]] lowering, lipid synthesis, and anabolic storage, preserving systemic metabolic homeostasis.

**Regulation and dysregulation:** Nutrient sensors and longevity pathways modulate GSIS: [[AMPK]] and [[mTOR]] tune beta-cell mass and function, while [[Sirtuins]] and [[NAD+]] influence insulin secretion and beta-cell survival. Impairment—driven by [[Insulin Resistance]], lipotoxicity, and [[Oxidative Stress]]—produces [[Glucose Intolerance]] and type 2 diabetes. Preserving GSIS is a major therapeutic goal (e.g., GLP-1 receptor agonists, sulfonylureas).
