# Tunneling Nanotubes

## Overview

[[Tunneling Nanotubes]] (TNTs) are thin, actin-rich membrane protrusions that establish direct cytoplasmic continuity between distant cells. First described in cultured cells, TNTs enable the transfer of organelles, vesicles, and signaling molecules, and are increasingly appreciated as conduits for intercellular communication in the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]].

## TNTs in Intercellular cGAS-STING Spread

TNTs provide a contact-dependent route for transferring [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] products—including [[cGAMP]] and [[mtDNA]]—and for propagating activation signals from a stressed sender cell to a naive receiver. Alongside [[Extracellular Vesicles|extracellular vesicles]], TNTs represent a mechanism by which localized [[DNA Damage]] or mitochondrial stress in one cell ignites cGAS-STING signaling in neighboring [[Neuron|neurons]] and [[Microglia]], scaling local stress into network-wide [[Neuroinflammation]].

## Relevance to Neurodegeneration

By permitting the exchange of mitochondria and nucleic acid cargo, TNTs may accelerate the spread of pathological signaling in neurodegeneration, contributing to glial activation and neuronal injury. Disrupting TNT-mediated transfer is a conceptually attractive, though technically challenging, approach to limiting the intercellular dissemination of cGAS-STING–driven inflammation.
