# Astrocyte Senescence

## Overview

[[Astrocyte Senescence]] is the acquisition by [[Astrocytes]] of a stable growth-arrested, pro-inflammatory state akin to [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]]. Senescent astrocytes contribute to [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]] by adopting neurotoxic phenotypes and secreting a [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]] rich in cytokines and proteases. In the context of [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative disease]], cGAS-STING signaling has been identified as a key contributor to astrocyte senescence, particularly in mouse models of [[Parkinson's Disease|PD]].

## cGAS-STING Contribution

Sustained activation of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] pathway promotes astrocyte senescence downstream of proteotoxic and mitochondrial stress. Astrocyte-specific deletion of [[cGAS]] robustly attenuates neurotoxicity, demonstrating that astrocytic cGAS is non–cell-autonomously harmful to neurons. Pharmacological inhibition of cGAS or [[STING]] reduces the neurotoxic A1 astrocyte subtype and promotes the neuroprotective A2 subtype, functionally uncoupling toxicity from beneficial astrocyte functions. This phenotypic switch mirrors findings in AD models, where cGAS-STING inhibition likewise reduces A1 astrocytes.

## Astrocyte Phenotype Switching

Astrocytes exist along a continuum from homeostatic to reactive states. Under cGAS-STING activation, they shift toward the pro-inflammatory A1 phenotype, which is deleterious to neurons through [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], excitotoxicity, and cytokine release. Suppressing the pathway favors the A2 (neuroprotective) state, highlighting astrocyte phenotype as a tractable therapeutic axis. Astrocyte senescence also overlaps with broader [[Inflammaging]] and age-related glial dysfunction.
