# Intrinsically Disordered Region

**Intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs)** are protein segments that do not adopt a stable three-dimensional fold under physiological conditions. Despite lacking fixed structure, IDRs mediate critical biological functions including protein–protein interactions, phase separation, post-translational modification, and signaling[31].

## Role in SIRT6-Vimentin Interaction

[[SIRT6]] harbors a C-terminal IDR (amino acids 298–355) that encompasses the centenarian mutations N308K and A313S. [[Vimentin]] contains two IDRs at its N-terminus (amino acids 1–89) and C-terminus (amino acids 409–422). The interaction between these IDRs regulates SIRT6 protein stability: the centenarian N308K mutation within SIRT6's IDR reduces binding affinity to vimentin's N-terminal IDR, elevating SIRT6 protein levels.

## Functions

- **Mediating Protein Interactions**: IDRs enable transient, specific interactions with multiple partners — critical for signaling hub proteins.
- **Phase Separation**: IDRs drive [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS)]], forming membraneless organelles and condensates.
- **Post-Translational Modification**: IDRs are enriched in sites for phosphorylation, acetylation, and other modifications.
