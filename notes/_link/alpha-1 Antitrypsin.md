# alpha-1 Antitrypsin

**alpha-1 Antitrypsin (AAT)**, also known as alpha-1 protease inhibitor or SERPINA1, is the major circulating serine protease inhibitor (serpin) in human plasma. Its primary function is to protect tissues—particularly the lungs—from excessive proteolysis by neutrophil elastase. AAT is synthesised in the liver and secreted into the bloodstream, where it maintains the protease–antiprotease balance essential for lung homeostasis. Genetic deficiency of AAT (alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, AATD) is the most common inherited cause of early-onset emphysema and can also cause liver disease.

## Structure

- **Gene:** *SERPINA1* is located on chromosome 14q32.13, within the serpin gene cluster. Over 100 allelic variants have been identified.
- **Protein:** AAT is a 52 kDa (394 amino acid) single-chain glycoprotein with three N-linked glycosylation sites. It is an acute-phase protein; plasma levels rise 2–5-fold during inflammation.
- **Mechanism of inhibition:** AAT acts as a "suicide substrate" for target proteases. It presents a reactive centre loop (RCL) that mimics the protease's natural substrate. Upon cleavage by the target protease (e.g., neutrophil elastase), AAT undergoes a dramatic conformational change (RCL insertion into the A-sheet), trapping the protease in a covalent, irreversible complex. This stoichiometric 1:1 inhibition mechanism is characteristic of the serpin superfamily.
- **Conformational disease:** The Z variant (Glu342Lys, the most common deficiency allele) causes polymerisation of AAT molecules within hepatocyte endoplasmic reticulum (ER), leading to ER stress, liver damage, and reduced circulating AAT levels.

## Regulation

- **Hepatic synthesis:** AAT is primarily synthesised in hepatocytes and secreted into plasma (normal plasma concentration: 1.0–2.0 g/L).
- **Acute-phase response:** AAT is an acute-phase protein; plasma levels rise during inflammation in response to IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α.
- **Transcriptional regulation:** Glucocorticoids, oestrogen, and inflammatory cytokines upregulate AAT transcription. The promoter contains glucocorticoid response elements and CCAAT/enhancer-binding protein (C/EBP) binding sites.
- **Other sources:** Monocytes, alveolar macrophages, and bronchial epithelial cells produce smaller amounts of AAT locally in the lung.

## Physiological Functions

### Lung Protection
AAT's primary function is to inhibit neutrophil elastase (NE) released during inflammation. NE degrades elastin, collagen, and proteoglycans in the alveolar walls. Without sufficient AAT, unopposed NE activity destroys alveolar tissue, leading to emphysema. The protease–antiprotease balance (AAT vs. NE) is the central concept in lung homeostasis.

### Anti-inflammatory Properties
Beyond protease inhibition, AAT has direct anti-inflammatory effects:
- Inhibits pro-inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6)
- Promotes anti-inflammatory macrophage polarisation
- Inhibits apoptosis of neutrophils and hepatocytes
- Modulates NF-κB signalling

### Immune Modulation
AAT modulates immune responses by:
- Inhibiting complement activation (C3 convertase)
- Reducing neutrophil chemotaxis and adhesion
- Modulating dendritic cell function

> [!important] Protease-Antiprotease Balance
> The balance between neutrophil elastase (NE) and alpha-1 antitrypsin (AAT) is the central concept in lung homeostasis. AAT deficiency tips this balance toward unopposed proteolysis, causing emphysema. Cigarette smoke further tips the balance by oxidising AAT (reducing its inhibitory activity) and recruiting neutrophils that release NE.

## Pathological Roles

### alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency (AATD)
AATD is the most common inherited cause of emphysema and liver disease:

- **Genetics:** The normal allele is *Pi*M. The most common deficiency alleles are *Pi*Z (Glu342Lys) and *Pi*S (Glu288Val). *Pi*ZZ homozygotes have plasma AAT levels of 3.5–7 µM (15–30% of normal), well below the protective threshold.
- **Pulmonary disease:** *Pi*ZZ individuals develop early-onset (typically 30s–40s) panacinar emphysema, particularly in the lower lobes. Smoking accelerates disease dramatically (20–30 pack-year history often leads to emphysema by age 40). Clinical features include dyspnoea, decreased exercise tolerance, and airflow obstruction.
- **Liver disease:** Polymerised AAT accumulates in hepatocyte ER, causing ER stress and liver damage. *Pi*ZZ individuals may develop neonatal hepatitis, cirrhosis, or hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **Panniculitis:** A rare manifestation; subcutaneous fat necrosis due to unopposed NE activity.

### Cigarette Smoke
Cigarette smoke compounds the protease–antiprotease imbalance:
- Oxidises the Met358 residue in AAT's RCL, reducing its inhibitory activity by 2,000-fold
- Recruits neutrophils to the lung, increasing NE release
- Generates ROS that directly damage elastin and other ECM components
- *Pi*ZZ smokers develop emphysema 10–20 years earlier than *Pi*ZZ non-smokers

### COPD
AAT deficiency accounts for 1–3% of COPD cases. Even in non-deficient individuals, reduced AAT levels (due to smoking, inflammation, or other factors) contribute to COPD pathogenesis.

### Liver Disease
In AATD, polymerised AAT accumulates in hepatocyte ER, causing:
- Neonatal hepatitis (10–15% of *Pi*ZZ infants)
- Chronic hepatitis
- Cirrhosis (2–12% of *Pi*ZZ adults)
- Hepatocellular carcinoma (increased risk)

## Clinical Relevance

- **Augmentation therapy:** Weekly intravenous infusions of pooled human AAT (Prolastin, Aralast, Zemaira) for *Pi*ZZ patients with established emphysema. Aims to maintain plasma AAT above the protective threshold (11 µM). Evidence for clinical benefit is limited but ongoing trials may clarify.
- **Alpha-1 antitrypsin genotyping:** Recommended for all patients with COPD, emphysema, or unexplained liver disease. Early identification enables smoking cessation and monitoring.
- **Smoking cessation:** The most impactful intervention for *Pi*ZZ individuals; smoking cessation slows emphysema progression dramatically.
- **Liver transplantation:** Curative for liver disease; also corrects the emphysema (hepatic-derived AAT resumes production).
- **Gene therapy:** Experimental approaches using AAV vectors to deliver functional *SERPINA1* gene to hepatocytes.
- **Small molecule correctors:** Compounds (e.g., correctors, enhancers) that prevent AAT polymerisation or promote secretion from hepatocytes.
