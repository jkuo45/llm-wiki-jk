# Cerebrospinal Fluid

## Overview

[[Cerebrospinal Fluid]] (CSF) is the clear, watery fluid that circulates through the cerebral ventricles and subarachnoid space, bathing the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] and providing mechanical protection, buoyancy, and solute exchange. Beyond its homeostatic roles, CSF acts as a dynamic communication conduit, carrying signaling molecules, antigens, and immune cells between the brain parenchyma, the [[Meningeal Lymphatic System|meningeal lymphatics]], and the skull bone marrow.

## CSF as a cGAS-STING Signal Conduit

CSF serves as a medium for intercellular propagation of [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] activation. Both [[cGAMP]]—the second messenger synthesized by [[cGAS]]—and downstream inflammatory cytokines can partition into CSF, allowing activation signals to spread beyond the local site of nucleic acid sensing. CSF levels of cGAMP and pro-inflammatory mediators are therefore candidate biomarkers reflecting CNS [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] activation in conditions such as viral encephalitis and neurodegeneration.

## Clinical and Pathological Relevance

In viral encephalitis, elevated CSF cytokines and cGAMP indicate robust intrathecal [[Innate Immune System|innate immune]] activation. In chronic neurodegeneration, CSF-borne inflammatory mediators contribute to the sustenance of [[Neuroinflammation]] and may reflect ongoing [[mtDNA]] leakage and glial cGAS-STING hyperactivity. Monitoring CSF composition offers a minimally invasive window into brain immune state and cGAS-STING pathway engagement.
