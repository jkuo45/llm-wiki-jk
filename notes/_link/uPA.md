# uPA

**Urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA)** is a serine protease that is a component of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]]. By converting plasminogen to plasmin at the cell surface, it initiates a proteolytic cascade that remodels the [[Extracellular Matrix]] and promotes cell migration and invasion.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

uPA is synthesized as a single-chain pro-enzyme (pro-uPA, ~54 kDa) that is converted to the active two-chain serine protease by limited proteolysis. It binds the GPI-anchored urokinase receptor (uPAR/CD87), localizing plasminogen activation to the cell surface, where it cleaves [[Plasminogen]] to generate the broad-spectrum serine protease [[Plasmin]]. Plasmin in turn activates [[MMP-3]], MMP-9 and other matrix metalloproteinases, linking the uPA system to collagenolysis and basement-membrane breach. uPA transcription is induced by SASP-driving signals, including [[NF-κB]] and growth-factor pathways, and its activity is tightly controlled by its fast-acting inhibitor [[PAI-1]] (plasminogen activator inhibitor-1), itself a prominent SASP factor. This uPA↔PAI-1 axis is a key rheostat of the proteolytic SASP.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

uPA is secreted by [[Senescent Cells]] and contributes to the matrix-degrading, pro-migratory arm of the SASP. Through plasmin generation it facilitates ECM degradation and remodeling, promotes cell migration and invasion, and supports the dissemination of neighboring tumor cells. By activating latent MMPs and releasing matrix-bound growth factors, uPA helps sustain [[Paracrine Senescence]] and tissue remodeling in the aged or damaged microenvironment. Its coupling to PAI-1 also feeds the pro-fibrotic SASP output.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

High tumor uPA and low PAI-1 levels are established prognostic markers of poor outcome and metastasis in breast and other cancers, and guide adjuvant therapy decisions. The uPA/plasminogen system is a validated drug target (e.g., the uPA inhibitor upamostat). Given its SASP contribution, modulating uPA offers a route to limit senescence-driven invasion and fibrosis without broad MMP inhibition.
