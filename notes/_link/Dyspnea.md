# Dyspnea

**Overview:** [[Dyspnea]] is the subjective experience of difficult, labored, or uncomfortable breathing. It is a cardinal respiratory symptom rather than a disease, reflecting a mismatch between the sensed need to breathe and the actual effort or outcome of ventilation.

**Pathophysiology:** Dyspnea arises from afferent inputs integrated in the cortex and brainstem: mechanoreceptors and chemoreceptors signal lung stretch, airway resistance, and arterial [[Hypoxia]]/hypercapnia; efferent motor command ("effort of breathing") is also perceived. When ventilatory demand outstrips capacity, the sensation intensifies.

**Clinical Presentation:** It ranges from exertional breathlessness to rest dyspnea and can be acute (pulmonary embolism, asthma, pneumonia, [[Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome]]) or chronic (COPD, heart failure). Associated signs include tachypnea, use of accessory muscles, and audible wheeze.

**Association with Methemoglobinemia:** Dyspnea is a key presenting symptom of [[Methemoglobinemia]], where oxidized [[Methemoglobin]] cannot carry oxygen efficiently, producing functional hypoxia. It appears alongside [[Cyanosis]] (classically gray-blue, "chocolate" blood) and fatigue. Because the hypoxia is due to impaired oxygen *carriage* rather than low arterial oxygen content, standard pulse oximetry may be misleadingly normal, and co-oximetry is diagnostic.

**Therapeutic Relevance:** In symptomatic methemoglobinemia, [[Methylene blue]] reduces methemoglobin back to functional [[Hemoglobin]], relieving dyspnea and cyanosis. Management otherwise targets the underlying cause.
