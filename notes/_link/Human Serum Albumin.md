# Human Serum Albumin

**Human serum albumin (HSA)** is the most abundant protein in blood plasma (~35–50 g/L), a 66.5 kDa, 585-residue globular protein synthesized by hepatocytes.

## Structure & Physiological Function

- **Heart-shaped, three-domain fold** stabilized by 17 disulfide bonds, with two principal hydrophobic ligand-binding pockets (Sudlow sites I and II).
- **Core roles:** maintains plasma colloid oncotic pressure, buffers pH, and serves as the body's major transport protein for fatty acids, [[Bilirubin]], hormones, metal ions, and a wide range of drugs.
- **Redox contribution:** its free Cys34 thiol is a significant extracellular antioxidant, scavenging [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and nitrogen species.

## Applications in Drug Delivery

> [!info] Albumin nanoparticles
> HSA is an ideal [[Nanoparticles|nanocarrier]] because it is endogenous, non-immunogenic, biodegradable, and readily binds hydrophobic drugs — improving [[Bioavailability]] of poorly soluble compounds. Albumin-bound nanoparticle technology is clinically validated (e.g. nab-paclitaxel) and exploits albumin uptake pathways (gp60/SPARC) enriched in tumors.

Fisetin-loaded HSA nanoparticles prepared by desolvation (220 ± 8 nm; ~84% encapsulation efficiency) showed sustained biphasic release (~75% over 72 h) and greater cytotoxicity against MCF-7 [[Breast Cancer]] cells than free [[Fisetin]], illustrating how HSA overcomes the flavonoid's poor solubility and rapid clearance.
