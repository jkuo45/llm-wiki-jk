## Overview

[[Troponin]] is a heterotrimeric complex (troponin C, I, and T) that regulates striated muscle contraction by conferring calcium sensitivity to the actin–myosin interaction. [[Cardiac troponin I]] (cTnI) and [[Cardiac troponin T]] (cTnT) are highly cardiac-specific and are the gold-standard biomarkers of myocardial injury.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

In the thin filament, troponin sits at intervals along actin:

- **TnC** binds Ca2+, initiating conformational change.
- **TnI** inhibits actin–myosin ATPase in the absence of Ca2+ (its inhibitory domain is released upon Ca2+ binding).
- **TnT** anchors the complex to tropomyosin.

Rising intracellular Ca2+ shifts tropomyosin, permitting cross-bridge cycling. During cardiomyocyte necrosis, cTnI/cTnT are released into the bloodstream and detected by high-sensitivity assays.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

Elevated cardiac troponin indicates myocardial damage in acute coronary syndrome, myocarditis, and stress/toxic injury. It is central to the diagnosis of myocardial infarction. In the wiki, troponin is used as a safety/cardiotoxicity biomarker in adrenochrome and sirtuin experimental protocols, including neutrophil and cardiovascular challenge studies.
