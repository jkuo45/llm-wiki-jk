# Euchromatin
**[[Euchromatin]]** is the lightly packed, transcriptionally active form of [[Chromatin]]. It is characterized by high levels of [[Histone Acetylation]] and low levels of DNA methylation, which keep the DNA accessible to the machinery responsible for [[Transcription]].
### 1. Contrast with [[Heterochromatin]]
In a healthy cell, there is a careful balance between euchromatin (the "open" genome) and [[Heterochromatin]] (the "closed" genome). Euchromatin contains the genes that a cell needs to function on a day-to-day basis, while heterochromatin silences repetitive elements and genes that are not needed for that specific cell type.
### 2. Changes During [[Aging]]
The "Heterochromatin Loss Model of Aging" suggests that as we age, regions that were once tightly packed as heterochromatin begin to "relax" and take on the characteristics of [[Euchromatin]].
*   **Aberrant Gene Expression**: This relaxation can lead to the inappropriate activation of genes that promote inflammation or cell cycle arrest (like those in the [[SASP]]).
*   **Loss of Silencing**: The conversion of heterochromatin to euchromatin can also lead to the activation of [[Transposable Elements]], contributing to [[Genomic Instability]].
### 3. Epigenetic Regulators
The formation and maintenance of euchromatin are regulated by enzymes like [[Histone Acetyltransferase]] (HATs), which add acetyl groups to histones, and [[Histone Deacetylase]] (HDACs), which remove them.
