# Nucleosome
The **[[Nucleosome]]** is the fundamental structural unit of [[Chromatin]]. It consists of a segment of DNA (approximately 147 base pairs) wound around a core of eight histone proteins (two each of H2A, H2B, H3, and H4).
### 1. Structural Significance
Nucleosomes act like "spools" around which DNA is wrapped. The density and positioning of nucleosomes determine the accessibility of the genome:
*   **[[Euchromatin]]**: Nucleosomes are loosely packed, allowing for active [[Transcription]].
*   **[[Heterochromatin]]**: Nucleosomes are tightly packed, leading to gene silencing.
### 2. Changes During [[Aging]]
One of the most profound, yet often overlooked, changes in the aging cell is the alteration of nucleosome dynamics:
*   **Loss of Core Histones**: Aged cells often show a global reduction in the total number of nucleosomes, leading to a more "open" and unstable genome.
*   **Fuzzy Positioning**: In young cells, nucleosomes are precisely positioned. In old cells, this positioning becomes "fuzzy" or erratic, leading to [[Genomic Instability]] and aberrant gene expression.
### 3. Epigenetic "Tags"
The tails of the histones within the nucleosome are subject to various [[Histone Modification|histone modifications]] (acetylation, methylation, etc.), which serve as chemical signals to the cell's machinery.
