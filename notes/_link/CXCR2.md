# CXCR2

CXCR2 (C-X-C chemokine receptor 2), also known as IL-8RB or CD182, is a G-protein-coupled receptor that binds the ELR+ C-X-C chemokines [[CXCL1]], [[CXCL2]], [[CXCL3]], [[CXCL5]], and [[IL-8]] (and CXCL6/7). It is the dominant receptor through which the neutrophil-directed arm of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] acts, and it both mediates and reinforces senescence (Acosta et al., 2013, PMID 23746840).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Ligand repertoire**: CXCR2 recognizes all ELR+ CXC chemokines; CXCL8 (IL-8) is a particularly potent agonist, whereas CXCR1 preferentially binds IL-8/CXCL6.
- **Signal transduction**: Ligand binding activates Gαi, triggering PLCβ/IP3/Ca2+, PI3K/Akt, and the [[p38 MAPK]]/ERK cascades that drive actin remodeling, chemotaxis, and survival.
- **Cell sources / expression**: Expressed on neutrophils, endothelial and epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and many tumor cells, positioning it at the interface of inflammation and senescence.
- **Regulation**: Receptor abundance and signaling are controlled by [[NF-κB]], the C/EBP family ([[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]]), and [[p38 MAPK]]; upstream it sits downstream of [[p53]] and the [[DNA Damage Response|DDR]].

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CXCR2 is a reinforcing node of the senescence program: senescent cells secrete IL-8/CXCL1-3 that act back through CXCR2 (autocrine/paracrine) to sustain the senescent growth-arrest and amplify the SASP — a feed-forward loop (Acosta et al., 2013).
- It drives "bystander"/paracrine senescence: factors released from one senescent cell engage CXCR2 on neighbors, spreading the phenotype through tissue.
- Through neutrophil and myeloid recruitment it both supports acute clearance of senescent cells and, chronically, sustains [[Inflammaging]].

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CXCR2 antagonism (e.g., navarixin, AZD5069, reparixin) is in trials for cancer, COPD, and other inflammatory diseases to blunt SASP and neutrophil-driven damage.
- Elevated CXCR2 signaling tracks with age-related inflammation, fibrosis, and poor cancer outcomes.
- It is a candidate senotherapeutic node for limiting senescence propagation without killing senescent cells.
