## Overview

[[CD206]], also called the mannose receptor (MRC1), is a C-type lectin transmembrane receptor predominantly expressed on alternatively activated (M2) [[Macrophages]]. It recognizes terminal mannose, fucose, and N-acetylglucosamine residues on glycoproteins and pathogens, mediating endocytosis and phagocytosis.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

CD206 functions as a pattern-recognition receptor with multiple domains (cysteine-rich, fibronectin type II, and C-type lectin domains) that bind and internalize:

- Pathogen-associated glycans (e.g., on fungi, bacteria, viruses).
- Endogenous ligands such as lysosomal hydrolases and advanced glycation end products.

It trafficks between the plasma membrane and endosomes, delivering cargo to lysosomes. CD206 expression is induced by [[IL-4]] and [[IL-13]] and suppressed by inflammatory stimuli ([[LPS]], [[IFN-γ]]), making it a canonical M2 polarization marker.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

CD206-mediated clearance is important for host defense and tissue homeostasis. It also contributes to antigen processing and cross-presentation. Aberrant CD206 activity is linked to fibrotic disease, allergic inflammation, and tumor-associated macrophage phenotypes that support immune evasion. In the wiki, CD206 is used to phenotype M2 [[Macrophages]] within studies of [[Inflammation]] and senescent-cell associated tissue remodeling.
