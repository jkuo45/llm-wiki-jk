# Vimentin

**Vimentin** (VIM) is a type III intermediate filament protein expressed in mesenchymal cells that provides structural support to the cytoskeleton and maintains cell integrity. Beyond its classical structural role, vimentin is implicated in [[Autophagy]][53], vesicle trafficking[54,55], [[Proteostasis]], and liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS)[30]. It is a canonical marker of [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT)]] and is upregulated in various cancers.

## Structure & Domains

Vimentin contains three major domains: an N-terminal head domain, a central α-helical rod domain, and a C-terminal tail domain. Both the N-terminal (amino acids 1–89) and C-terminal (amino acids 409–422) regions contain [[Intrinsically Disordered Region|intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs)]] that mediate protein–protein interactions and phase separation behavior.

## Mechanism & Interactions

- **SIRT6 Interaction**: Vimentin binds the C-terminal IDR of [[SIRT6]] (amino acids 298–355). The centenarian SIRT6 N308K mutation weakens this interaction, leading to increased SIRT6 protein levels — a mechanism distinct from the [[USP10]]-mediated stabilization pathway.
- **Phase Separation**: Vimentin undergoes liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS) to form droplets that wet and stabilize actin fibers[30], a process dependent on its IDR.
- **Proteostasis**: Vimentin has been implicated in autophagy and vesicle trafficking, regulating organelle distribution and intracellular cargo transport[53–55].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Vimentin is a hallmark of EMT and is overexpressed in aggressive cancers, where it correlates with invasiveness and metastasis. It is also a target of citrullination in [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], where citrullinated vimentin serves as a [[Biomarker|biomarker]] for anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA).
