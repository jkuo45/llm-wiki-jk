# Lipotoxicity

**Definition:** [[Lipotoxicity]] refers to cellular and tissue injury arising when lipid intermediates accumulate ectopically in non-adipose tissues—liver, skeletal muscle, pancreatic beta cells, heart, and kidney—exceeding the capacity for safe storage and oxidation.

**Mechanism:** Excess delivery or incomplete oxidation of fatty acids produces deleterious intermediates such as diacylglycerol, ceramides, and long-chain acyl-CoAs. These activate protein kinase C and [[mTOR]], inhibit insulin signaling, and provoke [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER stress]] and [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] dysfunction with [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] overproduction. The resulting [[Inflammation]] (often via TLR4/NF-κB and NLRP3) and impaired [[Autophagy]] amplify damage.

**Physiological importance and dysregulation:** Lipotoxicity is a key mechanism linking [[Obesity]] and ectopic fat to [[Insulin Resistance]], type 2 diabetes, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and cardiomyopathy. Failure to expand subcutaneous [[Adipose Tissue]] safely forces lipid spillover into other organs.

**Therapeutic angle:** Interventions that enhance lipid oxidation ([[Exercise]], AMPK activation), improve adipocyte storage, or inhibit [[mTOR]] (e.g., [[Rapamycin|rapamycin]]) can blunt lipotoxic injury; caloric restriction and [[Fasting]] reduce ectopic lipid load.
