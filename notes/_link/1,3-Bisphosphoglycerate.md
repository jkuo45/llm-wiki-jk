# 1,3-Bisphosphoglycerate

**1,3-Bisphosphoglycerate (1,3-BPG)** is a high-energy glycolytic intermediate formed from [[Glyceraldehyde 3-Phosphate|glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate]] by glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase ([[GAPDH]]) in a [[NAD+]]-consuming, NADH-generating step. It is subsequently converted to 3-phosphoglycerate by phosphoglycerate kinase, yielding the first ATP of glycolysis via substrate-level phosphorylation.

## Mechanism

- **GAPDH step**: Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate + Pi + NAD+ → 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate + NADH + H+.
- **ATP generation**: 1,3-BPG + ADP → 3-phosphoglycerate + ATP (phosphoglycerate kinase).

## Relevance to Senescence

- Formation of 1,3-BPG depends on [[NAD+]], which is limiting in senescent cells because the NAD+/NADH ratio falls (reduced [[Citric Acid Cycle|cycle]]/[[Oxidative Phosphorylation|ETC]] efficiency plus NAD+ consumption by repair pathways).
- Inhibited production of 1,3-BPG curtails glycolytic ATP, raising the AMP+ADP/[[ATP]] ratio and activating [[AMPK]], which reinforces the senescent arrest and triggers compensatory mitochondrial biogenesis.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: Notes GAPDH converts glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate to 1,3-bisphosphoglyceric acid using NAD+, and that reduced NAD+/NADH in senescence inhibits glycolysis and ATP production, elevating AMP/ATP and activating AMPK.
