# Cataract

**Overview:** [[Cataract]] is the opacification of the crystalline lens of the eye, producing blurred vision, glare, and eventually blindness if untreated. It is the leading cause of preventable blindness worldwide and its prevalence rises steeply with [[Aging]].

**Etiology and Pathophysiology:** The lens is uniquely reliant on tightly packed, long-lived [[Crystallins]] that must remain soluble and transparent for decades. With age, cumulative [[Oxidative Stress]] (fromUV light, metabolic reactive oxygen species) damages lens proteins: thiol groups are oxidized, proteins undergo [[Protein Carbonylation]], and non-enzymatic glycation forms advanced glycation end-products (AGEs). Damaged crystallins unfold, aggregate, and cross-link, scattering light and producing the characteristic cloudiness. The lens has limited protein turnover and almost no regenerative capacity, so damage accumulates irreversibly.

**Risk Factors:** [[UV-induced photoaging|UV exposure]], diabetes, smoking, corticosteroid use, and nutritional antioxidant deficiency all accelerate cataract formation. Dietary advanced glycation end-products likewise promote lens protein glycation.

**Clinical Presentation and Biomarkers:** Symptoms include gradual vision loss, reduced contrast sensitivity, and night-glare. Diagnosis is clinical (slit-lamp); no circulating biomarker is established, though lens autofluorescence reflects AGE accumulation.

**Therapeutic Landscape:** Definitive treatment is surgical extraction with intraocular lens implantation. Pharmacologic prevention centers on antioxidant protection and AGE inhibition—e.g., [[Aminoguanidine]] (an AGE-crosslink inhibitor) and [[Rapamycin]]-related modulation of proteostasis are under study. Lifestyle measures (UV protection, glycemic control) reduce risk.
