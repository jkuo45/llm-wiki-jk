# LIT-927

## Overview

LIT-927 is a bispecific neutraligand (dual-neutralizing antibody) that simultaneously targets [[CXCL12]] through both its receptors, [[CXCR4]] and [[ACKR3]]. By blocking CXCL12 signaling at both receptor nodes, LIT-927 comprehensively neutralizes the downstream pathological effects of elevated CXCL12, including endothelial dysfunction, mitochondrial ROS production, and EndoMT.

## Mechanism of Action

- Binds and neutralizes [[CXCL12]], preventing receptor engagement
- Blocks signaling through both [[CXCR4]] and [[ACKR3]] simultaneously
- Reduces CXCL12-mediated eNOS uncoupling → restores [[Nitric Oxide]] bioavailability
- Attenuates CXCL12-driven mitochondrial superoxide production
- Prevents CXCL12-induced [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]]
- Complements [[Fisetin]] by targeting the mediator (CXCL12) while fisetin targets the source (senescent cells)

> [!info] Source: CXCL12 → Endothelial Dysfunction Mechanistic Trace
> LIT-927 restores endothelial function in aged mice by neutralizing circulating CXCL12. The combination of fisetin + LIT-927 reduces mitochondrial superoxide further than either alone, confirming that targeting both source and mediator is synergistic.

## Evidence from Mahoney et al. 2025

- LIT-927 restores NO production by +10% in aged mice
- LIT-927 reduces CXCL12-mediated mitochondrial superoxide
- Combination with fisetin produces additive effects greater than either alone
- Validates CXCL12 as a causal mediator of age-related endothelial dysfunction

## Therapeutic Context

- Targets the "humoral senescence" axis — circulating SASP factors driving remote tissue dysfunction
- Complements senolytic therapy ([[Fisetin]]) by addressing the downstream mediator
- Potential biomarker-guided therapy: elevated circulating CXCL12 could identify patients most likely to benefit
- The combination of senolytic (source elimination) + neutraligand (mediator blockade) represents a dual-hit strategy
