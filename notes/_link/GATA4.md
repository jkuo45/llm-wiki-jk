# GATA4

GATA4 is a zinc-finger transcription factor of the GATA family, best known for governing cardiac and endodermal development. In the context of cellular senescence, GATA4 is a stress-inducible factor that functions as a regulator of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]], acting alongside [[NFKB|NF-κB]], [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]], and DNA-damage signaling to sustain inflammatory secretome expression.

## Overview

GATA4 binds the consensus motif (A/T)GATA(A/G) via two zinc fingers and activates or represses target genes involved in differentiation, stress response, and survival. It is stabilized under cellular stress and is a nodal mediator linking tissue injury and inflammatory gene programs.

## Structure & Function

The two C4 zinc fingers of GATA4 mediate DNA binding and protein interactions; an N-terminal transcriptional activation domain and regulatory serines govern its activity. GATA4 can be degraded by autophagy (e.g., upon nutrient stress via FOXO/autophagy) and stabilized by stress, making its protein level a sensor of cellular damage.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review lists GATA4 among the transcription factors and upstream regulators — together with DNA-damage signaling, [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]], [[p38 MAPK|p38 MAPK]], [[mTOR]], [[JAK|JAK-STAT]], and [[Notch]] — that control SASP expression. GATA4 contributes to the NF-κB- and C/EBPβ-driven inflammatory program, helping maintain expression of SASP cytokines and chemokines. Its induction under genotoxic and oncogenic stress positions GATA4 as a stress-integrating transcription factor that converts DDR and inflammatory signaling into sustained SASP output, complementing the master regulator NF-κB.

## Mechanistic Details

- GATA4 integrates stress signals (DNA damage, oxidative stress) with inflammatory transcription factors to enforce SASP.
- It cooperates with C/EBPβ and NF-κB at SASP enhancers, reinforcing the feed-forward inflammatory loop.
- GATA4 stabilization/accumulation in senescent cells parallels the enhancer activation ([[H3K27ac]] gain) at SASP loci described in the review.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Because GATA4 sits upstream of SASP, its modulation is a candidate [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] approach. Strategies that lower GATA4 stabilization (e.g., restoring autophagic clearance) may dampen SASP without eliminating senescent cells. GATA4 is also a biomarker of senescence in several tissues.
