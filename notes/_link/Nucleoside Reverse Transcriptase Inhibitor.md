# Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitor

Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) are antiretroviral compounds developed for HIV that act as chain-terminating analogs of natural nucleosides, competitively inhibiting retroviral reverse transcriptase and terminating viral DNA synthesis. In the senescence field, NRTIs are repurposed to block the reverse transcription of LINE-1 ([[LINE-1]]) and human endogenous retrovirus ([[Human Endogenous Retrovirus|HERV]]) cDNA, thereby alleviating the cGAS–STING-mediated interferon inflammation and [[SASP]] that arise from retrotransposon derepression in aging.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

NRTIs are nucleoside analogs (e.g., zidovudine/AZT, lamivudine/3TC, emtricitabine/FTC, tenofovir) phosphorylated intracellularly to active triphosphates. They lack a 3′-OH, so once incorporated into nascent DNA by reverse transcriptase, chain elongation halts. They are selective for viral RT over host DNA polymerases at therapeutic doses, though mitochondrial toxicity can occur with prolonged use.

## Mechanism of Action

Retrotransposon activation in senescence generates cytoplasmic LINE-1/HERV cDNA, which is sensed by [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] to induce type I interferon and SASP. NRTIs inhibit the LINE-1/HERV reverse transcriptase that produces this cDNA, interrupting the upstream trigger of inflammaging. This acts transcriptionally upstream of the SASP rather than at the SASP promoter itself.

## Applications

The review notes that inhibition of LINE-1 or HERV reverse transcription by NRTIs alleviates interferon-mediated inflammation in multiple mouse tissues and SASP in cells (refs. 135, 136, 139). Repurposing HIV drugs is proposed as a strategy to improve health in old age by targeting retrotransposon-associated inflammation. In a clinical trial, emtricitabine is being evaluated for Alzheimer's disease (NCT04500847, Phase 1, ongoing) — a prescription medicine already used in HIV-1 infection. This positions NRTIs as readily available, clinically validated candidates for [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] (anti-inflammaging) repurposing.

## Therapeutic Relevance

NRTIs offer a uniquely tractable route to dampen senescence-associated inflammation because they are approved, dosed chronically, and target a conserved driver (retrotransposon cDNA). Challenges include off-target mitochondrial toxicity and the need to preserve beneficial acute-SASP functions. Their use exemplifies the review's thesis that epigenetic/retrotransposon modulators can be senomorphics.
