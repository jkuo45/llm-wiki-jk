# DOT1L

DOT1L (disruptor of telomeric silencing 1-like), also known as KMT4, is the only known methyltransferase for histone H3 lysine 79 (H3K79). It deposits H3K79me1/2/3 across gene bodies of active genes. In senescence, DOT1L knockdown prevents emergence of the [[SASP]] while leaving the senescence growth arrest unaffected, making it a candidate [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] target.

## Overview

DOT1L is an evolutionarily conserved, SAM-dependent methyltransferase unique in lacking a SET domain; instead it adopts a class I SAM-dependent methyltransferase fold. It mono-, di-, and trimethylates H3K79, a mark found within gene bodies of transcriptionally engaged genes and at telomeres, where it antagonizes silent chromatin.

## Structure & Function

DOT1L binds nucleosomal H3 at the floor of the nucleosome near K79, requiring an unmodified H4 tail for optimal activity. H3K79 methylation is read by proteins such as the tumor suppressor 53BP1 (via Tudor domain) at DNA double-strand breaks, linking DOT1L to DNA-damage signaling. DOT1L is also recruited by the MLL-fusion oncoproteins in leukemia to aberrantly activate HOXA gene expression.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review lists DOT1L among the epigenetic regulators whose knockdown prevents SASP emergence without affecting cell-cycle arrest (ref. 70). Along with [[MLL1]], [[KDM4]], and [[BRD4]], DOT1L therefore belongs to the class of senescence-epigenetic modulators that are selectively required for the secretome but dispensable for the arrest. H3K79 methylation by DOT1L is thought to maintain an open, transcriptionally permissive chromatin state at SASP loci, and its loss likely restricts SASP transcription without reversing growth arrest.

## Mechanistic Details

- H3K79me is a gene-body mark associated with active elongation; DOT1L sustains chromatin accessibility at SASP genes.
- DOT1L's role in DDR (via 53BP1 recruitment at breaks) may couple it to the DNA-damage signaling that drives SASP.
- As with other histone-modifying enzymes in the review, DOT1L inhibition is senomorphic rather than senolytic.

## Therapeutic Relevance

The DOT1L inhibitor EPZ-5676 (pinometostat) is in Phase 1 clinical trials for MLL-rearranged and other leukemias (NCT02141828, NCT01684150), described as "well tolerated." The review highlights EPZ-5676 as a clinical-stage agent that could be repurposed as a senomorphic to dampen SASP-associated inflammation once safety is established.
