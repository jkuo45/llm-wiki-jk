# mTOR

**[[notes/_link/mTOR]]** (Mechanistic Target of Rapamycin) is a protein kinase that serves as a central "master regulator" of cellular metabolism. It integrates signals from nutrients, growth factors, and energy status to control cell growth, protein synthesis, and [[notes/_link/Autophagy]].


**Mammalian target of rapamycin** (mTOR) is a kinase that serves as a central regulator of cell growth, proliferation, and survival in response to nutrient and hormonal signals.


The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) is a central regulator of cell growth, proliferation, and survival, existing in two distinct complexes: [[mTORC1]] and [[mTORC2]].


## 1. The Growth vs. Survival Axis
The mTOR pathway is optimized for growth and reproduction in early life, but its chronic activation in adulthood is a major driver of [[notes/_link/Aging]]:
*   **Growth Promotion**: When nutrients are abundant, mTOR is active, promoting the building of proteins and lipids.
*   **Suppression of [[notes/_link/Autophagy]]**: Active mTOR inhibits autophagy, the process by which cells clean out damaged components.
*   **Longevity through Inhibition**: Reducing mTOR activity (through drugs like Rapamycin or via [[notes/_link/Caloric Restriction]]) is one of the most effective ways to extend lifespan in nearly every species studied.


## 2. Interaction with Longevity Pathways
mTOR works in a dynamic balance with other key energy sensors:
*   **[[notes/_link/AMPK]]**: High energy stress activates AMPK, which in turn inhibits mTOR to conserve resources.
*   **[[IGF-1]]**: High levels of IGF-1 signaling activate mTOR, linking systemic growth signals to cellular metabolism.


## 3. Impact on Age-Related Disease
Chronic mTOR activation contributes to:
*   **[[notes/_link/Cancer]]**: By promoting uncontrolled cell growth and division.
*   **[[Metabolic Syndromes]]**: Linked to insulin resistance and obesity.
*   **[[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]]**: By inhibiting the clearance of toxic protein aggregates through [[notes/_link/Autophagy]]. Conversely, mTOR inhibition (e.g., by [[Rapamycin]]) is a key strategy for enhancing autophagic clearance of these aggregates.

## Role in Cancer
- **Signaling Axis**: A key component of the [[notes/_link/PI3K]]/[[notes/_link/Akt]]/mTOR pathway, which is often dysregulated in aggressive [[notes/_link/Breast Cancer]] subtypes.
- **Inhibition**:
    - [[melittin]] inhibits the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway in breast cancer cells.
    - [[Ivermectin]] blocks the Akt/mTOR axis to induce [[notes/_link/Autophagy]].


## Connections
- **[[notes/_link/Aging]]**: mTOR is a primary hallmark and regulator of the aging process.
- **[[notes/_link/Autophagy]]**: The process most directly suppressed by mTOR activity.
- **[[notes/_link/AMPK]]**: The cellular energy sensor that counteracts mTOR.
- **[[IGF-1]]**: An upstream activator of the mTOR pathway.


- [[notes/_link/Akt]]: An upstream regulator of mTOR.
- [[notes/_link/PI3K]]: Part of the same signaling axis.
- [[melittin]]: Suppresses mTOR activity.
- [[Ivermectin]]: Inhibits the mTOR pathway, leading to programmed cell death.
