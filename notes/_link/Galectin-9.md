## Overview

[[Galectin-9]] (Gal-9) is a member of the galectin family of β-galactoside-binding lectins, characterized by two tandem carbohydrate recognition domains (CRDs) connected by a flexible peptide linker. Galectin-9 is secreted by various cell types and plays diverse roles in immune regulation, inflammation, and cancer. It has been identified as a component of the [[SASP]] secreted by senescent cells.

## Structure and Receptors

- Two carbohydrate recognition domains (CRDs) connected by a flexible linker
- Primary receptor: TIM-3 (T-cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain-3) on T cells and other immune cells
- Also interacts with Dectin-1 on macrophages
- Binds N-glycans on multiple cell surface glycoproteins

## Immunosuppressive Functions

Galectin-9 exerts broad immunosuppressive effects in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]]:

- Promotes apoptosis of T cells and monocytes via TIM-3 engagement
- Increases regulatory T cell (Treg) differentiation and function
- Promotes M2 macrophage polarization
- Impairs anti-tumor immune responses
- Activates Dectin-1 on macrophages, promoting pancreatic carcinoma and peritumoral immune tolerance

> [!info] Source: Dong et al. 2024
> Senescent cells secrete Galectin-9 in melanoma, contributing to immunosuppressive effects in the TME—promoting T cell apoptosis, increasing Treg and M2 macrophage polarization, and impairing anti-tumor immunity.

## Role in Senescence

Galectin-9 was first reported as a SASP component secreted by senescent melanoma cells (Tarallo et al. 2024). Its presence in SASP contributes to the immunosuppressive arm of the senescent secretome, potentially facilitating immune evasion by tumors that harbor senescent cell populations.

> [!tip] Therapeutic Implication
> Galectin-9 may serve as both a biomarker for senescent cell burden in tumors and a therapeutic target. Blocking Galectin-9 could potentially restore anti-tumor immunity in the senescent TME.

## Dual Roles in Cancer

Interestingly, Galectin-9 exhibits context-dependent effects in cancer:

- **Protumor**: Immunosuppressive effects facilitate tumor immune evasion, particularly in melanoma and pancreatic cancer
- **Antitumor**: Can inhibit melanoma metastasis by blocking adhesion to endothelium and extracellular matrices
