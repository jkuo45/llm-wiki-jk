# E-cadherin

**E-cadherin (epithelial cadherin, CDH1)** is a calcium-dependent transmembrane glycoprotein that mediates homophilic cell–cell adhesion at epithelial adherens junctions. It is the prototypical epithelial marker whose downregulation is a defining event of the [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT)]]. It appears in the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] tumorigenesis module as an adhesion determinant modulated during senescence-associated tumor progression.

## Structure & Function

- Extracellular cadherin (EC) repeats mediate Ca2+-dependent homophilic binding between adjacent cells; the cytoplasmic tail links to β-catenin and α-catenin, anchoring junctions to the actin cytoskeleton.
- Maintains epithelial integrity, apicobasal polarity, and contact inhibition of proliferation.

## Mechanism of Action & Regulation

- E-cadherin loss releases sequestered **β-catenin**, which can translocate to the nucleus and activate [[Wnt signaling|Wnt/β-catenin]] target genes promoting proliferation and stemness.
- Transcriptionally repressed by the EMT transcription factors SNAIL, SLUG, ZEB1, and TWIST, which are induced by SASP factors such as [[TGFβ]], [[IL-6]], and [[IL-8]].

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- In the [[Tumor Microenvironment|tumour microenvironment]], SASP-driven EMT downregulates E-cadherin, enabling cancer cell migration, [[Metastasis|metastasis]], and invasion.
- The E-cadherin-to-N-cadherin switch is a hallmark of the SASP's pro-tumorigenic capacity to alter epithelial cell identity (see [[Paracrine Reprogramming]]).

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- Loss of E-cadherin (via CDH1 mutation, promoter hypermethylation, or transcriptional repression) is associated with invasive and metastatic carcinomas; germline CDH1 mutations cause hereditary diffuse gastric cancer.
- E-cadherin status is a prognostic marker of tumor aggressiveness and EMT activation.
