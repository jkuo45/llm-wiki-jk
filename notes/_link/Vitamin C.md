# Vitamin C
**Vitamin C** ([[Ascorbic Acid|ascorbic acid]]) is a water-soluble vitamin and essential nutrient with potent antioxidant properties. It serves as a cofactor for numerous enzymatic reactions and is a critical chain-breaking antioxidant in aqueous cellular compartments.
## Biochemical Role
Vitamin C acts as an electron donor (reducing agent), protecting cells from [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative damage]]. It regenerates [[Vitamin E|α-tocopherol]] from its radical form at membrane interfaces and participates in the synthesis of [[Collagen]], [[Carnitine]], and [[Norepinephrine]].
## Antioxidant Network
- Recycles [[Vitamin E|α-tocopherol]] from the α-tocopheroxyl radical
- Scavenges [[Superoxide Radicals|superoxide]], [[Hydroxyl Radicals|hydroxyl radicals]], and [[Singlet Oxygen]]
- Regenerates [[Glutathione]] from glutathione disulfide (GSSG) via dehydroascorbate reductase
## Dual Pro-Oxidant Role
At pharmacological concentrations, Vitamin C can act as a pro-oxidant via the [[Fenton Reaction]] (Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ → Fe³⁺ + •OH + OH⁻), generating [[Hydroxyl Radicals|hydroxyl radicals]] that selectively kill [[Cancer]] cells in preclinical models.
