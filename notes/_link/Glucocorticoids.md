# Glucocorticoids

Glucocorticoids (cortisol, dexamethasone, prednisolone) are adrenal steroid hormones with potent anti-inflammatory effects. They block pro-inflammatory [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] by impairing [[IL-1α]]-mediated [[NF-κB]] activity via the glucocorticoid receptor, making them a class of [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] therapy.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

Glucocorticoids bind the intracellular [[Glucocorticoid Receptor]] (GR, NR3C1), a ligand-activated nuclear receptor. The hormone–GR complex translocates to the nucleus, where it both transactivates anti-inflammatory genes and transrepresses pro-inflammatory transcription: it recruits histone deacetylases, competes with [[NF-κB]] and AP-1 for coactivators, and directly inhibits NF-κB transcriptional activity. In senescent cells, glucocorticoids specifically blunt the IL-1α–driven arm of the SASP — suppressing [[IL-1α]] maturation and the NF-κB–dependent expression of downstream SASP factors (Coppé et al.; SASP review, PMID 20365073).

## Role in Senescence / SASP

Glucocorticoids are classic senomorphic agents: they suppress the pro-inflammatory component of the SASP without eliminating senescent cells. They preferentially dampen the IL-1α/NF-κB limb, leaving some growth-factor SASP components relatively intact. By contrast, chronic endogenous glucocorticoid excess (Cushing phenotype) is associated with accelerated aging features, illustrating that context and dose determine whether GR signaling is protective or detrimental to tissue homeostasis.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Glucocorticoids are among the most prescribed anti-inflammatory drugs (asthma, rheumatology, oncology). Long-term use carries metabolic, skeletal, and infectious risks. Their senomorphic action is incidental to their primary immunosuppressive use, but it highlights GR as an endogenous modulator of [[Inflammaging]] and a candidate node for senomorphic intervention.
