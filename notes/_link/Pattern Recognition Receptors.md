# Pattern Recognition Receptors

## Overview

Pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) are germline-encoded molecular sentries of the [[Innate Immune System]] that detect conserved molecular patterns — including pathogen-derived and endogenous danger-associated nucleic acids — and launch immune and inflammatory programs. Within the [[Central Nervous System]], PRR activation in [[Microglia]], [[Astrocytes]], and [[Endothelial Cells]] underpins [[Neuroinflammation]] across [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]], and other [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative diseases]]. The PRR family spans membrane, endosomal, and cytosolic compartments and includes Toll-like receptors (TLRs), RIG-I–like receptors (RLRs), NOD-like receptors (NLRs), and several DNA sensors.

## Major DNA-Sensing PRRs

Among DNA sensors, [[cGAS]] occupies a unique position: it directly and sequence-independently detects dsDNA in both the [[Cytosol]] and the [[Nucleus]], activating the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] and driving [[Type I Interferon|type I IFN]] production via [[STING]] and [[IRF3]]. In parallel DNA-sensing arms, [[AIM2]] forms an [[Inflammasome|inflammasome]] in response to cytosolic dsDNA, inducing [[Pyroptosis]]; [[TLR9]] senses DNA within endosomes and lysosomes; and [[ZBP1]] recognizes the noncanonical left-handed [[Z-DNA]] (and Z-RNA) conformation. Each of these sensors feeds distinct downstream effectors, but cGAS is distinguished by its ability to bridge cytosolic DNA detection with both canonical interferon signaling and noncanonical nuclear functions in chromatin and RNA metabolism.

## Canonical vs. Noncanonical Roles

The classical PRR paradigm positions these sensors as detectors of foreign or damaged nucleic acids that trigger protective immunity. However, the expanding noncanonical functions of [[cGAS]] blur this boundary: nuclear cGAS acts as a chromatin- and splicing-associated scaffold rather than a conventional DNA sensor, regulating [[Genomic Instability|genome stability]], [[DNA Damage]] repair, and transcription through [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|phase-separated]] assemblies with [[RNA-Binding Proteins]], the [[SWI-SNF Complex]], and the [[Spliceosome]]. Understanding PRRs therefore requires distinguishing their inflammatory signaling roles from emerging homeostatic and regulatory functions, particularly in the brain.
