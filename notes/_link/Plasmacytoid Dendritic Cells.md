# Plasmacytoid Dendritic Cells

**Plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs)** are a specialized dendritic cell subset, notable for being the major producers of [[Type I Interferon|type I interferons]] upon viral sensing. They express [[TLR9]] in endosomes and are key innate sentinels of nucleic-acid danger signals.

## Mechanism & DAMP Recognition

- Extracellular [[TFAM]] (mitochondrial transcription factor A) is pro-inflammatory. When bound to [[mtDNA]], the TFAM–mtDNA complex engages the [[RAGE]] (receptor for advanced glycation end-products) on pDC surfaces, facilitating internalization of mtDNA.
- Once internalized, the mtDNA (with unmethylated [[CpG Islands]]) is recognized by endosomal [[TLR9]], initiating signaling that drives expression of inflammatory factors — linking mitochondrial [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMPs]] to innate activation.

## Role in Senescence

- In senescence, mitochondrial DAMP release (including TFAM–mtDNA) can recruit/activate pDCs, contributing to the chronic, low-grade inflammation (inflammaging) associated with senescent-cell accumulation. This is one of several pathways (alongside cGAS-STING and NLRP3) by which senescence-derived mitochondrial damage engages immunity.
