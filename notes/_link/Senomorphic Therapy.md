# Senomorphic Therapy

**Senomorphic therapies** focus on modulating senescence-associated properties — particularly the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] — without directly inducing cytotoxicity. This contrasts with [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytics]], which selectively kill senescent cells. Senomorphics are valuable where senescent cells contribute to beneficial tissue structure or where acute clearance is unsafe, and they avoid the "senolytic cliff" of sudden debris release. Conceptually, senomorphics aim to convert a harmful senescent cell into a quiescent, metabolically benign one (Kirkland & Tchkonia, 2020, PMID 32529839).

## Mechanism of Action

Senomorphic therapies primarily target the pro-inflammatory and pro-tumorigenic SASP factors downstream of [[NF-κB]], [[p38 MAPK]], [[mTORC1|mTOR]], and [[JAK]]–[[STAT3]] signalling. They reduce SASP *output* rather than senescent cell *number*, generally preserving cell-cycle arrest while blunting paracrine toxicity ([[Paracrine Senescence]]) and [[Inflammaging]].

## Key Senomorphic Agents

### Targeting NF-κB and IL-1α
- **Glucocorticoids**: Potent suppressors of chronic inflammation; block pro-inflammatory SASP by impairing IL-1α-mediated NF-κB activity.
- **[[Metformin]]**: Inhibits NF-κB signalling in fibroblasts, macrophages, and senescent endothelial cells; also activates [[AMPK]].
- **Olive phenols (oleuropein, hydroxytyrosol)**: Reduce cGAS–STING–NF-κB pro-inflammatory SASP; preserve [[Lamin B1]] expression.

### Targeting p53 Stabilization
- **[[Nutlin 3a]]**: MDM2 inhibitor that stabilizes [[p53]]; suppresses the pro-inflammatory SASP arm.

### Targeting p38 MAPK–MK2
- **p38 MAPK inhibitors (SB203580)**: Inhibit SASP from fibroblasts of older donors.
- **MK2 inhibitors**: Rescue chemotherapy-induced bone loss in mice.

### Targeting mTOR
- **[[Rapamycin]]** and rapalogs: Suppress SASP by interfering with mTOR and the p38 MAPK–IL-1α–NF-κB pathways; also show STAT3-dependent SASP-suppressing activity in Nrf2-null models.
- **Hypoxia-mimetic compounds**: Block mTOR through [[AMPK]] activation; suppress pro-inflammatory SASP; improve health span of therapy-induced or naturally aged animals.

### Targeting cGAS–STING
- **Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs)**: Lamivudine (3TC) inhibits [[LINE-1]] propagation, reducing cGAS–STING–NF-κB SASP (de Magalhães et al.).

### Targeting JAK–STAT
- **[[Ruxolitinib]]**: JAK1/2 inhibitor; reduces SASP cytokines (IL-6, IL-8) in human senescent fibroblasts and in aged mice.
- **Tofacitinib**: JAK inhibitor with analogous SASP-suppressive effects.

### Targeting Lipid SASP
- **Zileuton**: ALOX5 inhibitor targeting leukotriene synthesis.
- **Colchicine**: Modulates lipid inflammatory mediators.

### Natural Compounds
- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Inhibits pro-inflammatory SASP cytokines via [[Sirtuins|SIRT1]]/NF-κB.
- **Apigenin**: Reduces breast-cancer aggressiveness by inhibiting NF-κB-dependent SASP factors and CXCL10.
- **Avenanthramide C**, **Astaxanthin**: Anti-inflammatory / antioxidant SASP modulators.

### Other Approaches
- **HSP90 inhibitors**: Blunt chemotherapy-induced IL-8, enhancing chemosensitivity.
- **EXOC7 targeting**: Compromises pro-tumorigenic SASP in inflammation-driven cancer.
- **Simvastatin / Haritaki**: Target IL-6 secretion.

## cAMP Modulators

Emerging senomorphics raise intracellular [[cAMP]] (e.g., PDE4 inhibitors such as rolipram) to suppress NF-κB–driven SASP without affecting the arrest, illustrating that the SASP and the cell-cycle block are dissociable (Laberge et al., 2013, PMID 23640595).

## Clinical Applications

- [[Cancer]] — suppressing pro-tumorigenic SASP to enhance immunotherapy and reduce therapy-induced relapse.
- Age-related conditions — reducing chronic [[Inflammation]] and [[Inflammaging]].
- [[Osteoarthritis]] — modulating SASP-driven joint inflammation.
- [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]] — reducing pro-fibrotic SASP factors.
- [[Cardiovascular Disease]] — mitigating vascular SASP.

## Lifestyle Interventions with Senomorphic Effects

- **Dietary restriction / caloric restriction**: ~30% caloric decrease reduces DNA damage, NF-κB, and SASP.
- **Exercise**: Reduces senescent cell burden and circulating SASP factors.
- **Intermittent fasting**: May reduce [[p21 CIP1|p21]] expression and SASP.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Unlike senolytics, senomorphics do not require a specific senescent-cell-clearance window and can be given chronically; they are being evaluated in trials for IPF (e.g., tricyclic senomorphics) and for mitigating chemotherapy toxicity. A key limitation is that the SASP returns when the drug is withdrawn, whereas senolytics produce a more durable reduction in senescent-cell number.
