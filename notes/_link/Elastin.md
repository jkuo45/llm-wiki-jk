# Elastin

**Elastin** is a highly elastic structural protein of the [[Extracellular Matrix|ECM]] that provides resilience, recoil, and compliance to tissues subject to repeated mechanical stress—including skin, lungs, arteries, ligaments, and vocal cords. Elastin is one of the longest-lived proteins in the human body (half-life of decades), and its progressive degradation and crosslinking with age are fundamental drivers of tissue stiffening, skin ageing, and cardiovascular disease.

## Structure & Biosynthesis

- **Gene:** *ELN* is located on chromosome 7q11.23. Deletions or mutations cause Williams syndrome (supravalvular aortic stenosis, connective tissue abnormalities).
- **Protein:** Elastin is a ~72 kDa soluble precursor (tropoelastin) that is secreted by fibroblasts, smooth muscle cells, and chondrocytes. Tropoelastin monomers are cross-linked by [[Lysyl Oxidase]] (LOX) on microfibrillar scaffolds (composed of fibrillin-1 and fibrillin-2) to form insoluble elastin polymers.
- **Crosslinking:** Elastin crosslinks include desmosine and isodesmosine, unique amino acids formed by LOX-mediated oxidative deamination of lysine residues. These crosslinks give elastin its extraordinary elasticity and durability.
- **Turnover:** Elastin has an extremely slow turnover rate in adult tissues. In healthy human arteries, elastin half-life is estimated at 70+ years, making it one of the most stable proteins in the body. This longevity makes elastin particularly vulnerable to cumulative damage from glycation, oxidation, and proteolysis.

> [!important] Longest-Lived Structural Protein
> Elastin's extreme longevity (decades) means that damage from glycation, oxidation, and proteolysis accumulates over a lifetime without efficient repair, making it a central player in ageing-related tissue deterioration.

## Tissue Distribution & Function

- **Skin:** Elastin fibres in the dermis provide elasticity and resilience. Loss of elastin integrity manifests as sagging, wrinkles, and loss of skin recoil.
- **Lungs:** Elastin in alveolar walls enables lung expansion and recoil during breathing. Elastin degradation is the hallmark of emphysema.
- **Arteries:** Elastic lamellae in large arteries (aorta, carotids) enable pulsatile recoil and maintain blood pressure during diastole. Elastin fragmentation drives arterial stiffening.
- **Ligaments & tendons:** Elastin provides elasticity and recoil, particularly in ligaments subject to repeated stretching (e.g., ligamentum flava of the spine).
- **Other:** Vocal cords, bladder, uterus, and cornea all contain elastin essential for their mechanical function.

## Degradation & Proteases

Elastin is resistant to most proteases but is degraded by a specialised set of elastinolytic enzymes:

- **[[MMP-12]]:** The most potent elastinolytic MMP. MMP-12 is the only MMP that can effectively degrade insoluble elastin at physiological concentrations, making it the principal elastase in macrophage-mediated tissue remodelling.
- **[[MMP-2]]:** Gelatinase A has moderate elastinolytic activity.
- **[[MMP-9]]:** Gelatinase B degrades elastin, particularly in inflammatory lung disease.
- **[[MMP-3]]:** Stromelysin-1 degrades elastin as part of its broad substrate repertoire.
- **Neutrophil elastase:** A serine protease from neutrophil granules; potent elastinolytic enzyme prominent in pulmonary inflammation.
- **Cathepsins:** Cathepsin S and cathepsin L contribute to elastin degradation in the vasculature and skin.

## Elastin Fragmentation & Elastokines

Degradation of elastin generates bioactive fragments called **elastin-derived peptides (EDPs)** or **elastokines**, which have potent signalling activities:

- **EBP receptor:** EDPs bind the elastin-binding protein (EBP), a complex of spliced Gal-3, elastin-binding protein, and Neu-1 sialidase on the cell surface.
- **MMP amplification:** EDP–EBP signalling upregulates MMP expression (including MMP-2, MMP-9, MMP-12), creating a positive feedback loop of elastin degradation.
- **Inflammation:** EDPs are chemotactic for monocytes and macrophages, promoting inflammatory cell recruitment to degraded elastic tissues.
- **Senescence:** EDP-mediated MMP upregulation in dermal fibroblasts contributes to the aged fibroblast phenotype and paracrine senescence.

> [!warning] Positive Feedback Loop
> Elastin fragmentation → EDP generation → EBP signalling → MMP upregulation → further elastin degradation. This feed-forward loop accelerates tissue deterioration in ageing, COPD, and atherosclerosis.

## Age-Related Changes

### Glycation & AGEs
Elastin accumulates advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) over the lifespan. AGE crosslinks stiffen elastin fibres, reduce elasticity, and make elastin resistant to normal remodelling. The Maillard reaction generates these crosslinks progressively, contributing to arterial stiffening and loss of skin compliance.

### Oxidative Damage
Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) from UV radiation, cigarette smoke, and inflammatory cells damage elastin through direct oxidation and by upregulating elastinolytic MMPs. UVA-induced singlet oxygen is a major driver of solar elastosis (photoaging).

### Calcification
Degraded or glycated elastin serves as a nucleation site for calcium phosphate deposition, contributing to vascular calcification in ageing and chronic kidney disease.

## Pathological Roles

### Skin Ageing
MMP-12-mediated elastin degradation is a central mechanism of both intrinsic and extrinsic skin ageing. UV irradiation and ROS upregulate MMP-12 in dermal fibroblasts and macrophages, generating EDPs that amplify the degradation cascade. Accumulation of MMP-12-expressing senescent fibroblasts correlates with wrinkle formation and loss of skin elasticity.

### COPD & Emphysema
Elastin degradation in alveolar walls by MMP-12 (and neutrophil elastase) is the defining pathological feature of emphysema. MMP-12-deficient mice are protected from cigarette smoke-induced emphysema. The protease–antiprotease imbalance (excess elastinolytic activity overwhelming alpha-1 antitrypsin capacity) drives airspace enlargement.

### Arterial Stiffness & Cardiovascular Disease
Elastin fragmentation by MMP-2, MMP-9, and MMP-12 reduces arterial elastic recoil, contributing to systolic hypertension, left ventricular hypertrophy, and cardiovascular risk. Elastin fragmentation is one of the earliest structural changes in atherosclerotic vessel remodelling.

### Atherosclerosis
Elastin degradation in the arterial wall by macrophage-derived MMP-12 contributes to plaque instability, vessel wall weakening, and aneurysm formation.

### Cancer
Elastin degradation facilitates tumour invasion through elastic tissue barriers (e.g., pleura, basement membranes). Elastin-derived peptides also promote tumour angiogenesis via EBP signalling.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Biomarker:** Elastin-derived peptides (EDPs) in serum and urine serve as biomarkers of elastin degradation in COPD, atherosclerosis, and skin ageing.
- **Therapeutic target:** MMP-12 inhibitors may slow age-related elastin degradation. Anti-elastokine strategies (blocking EBP signalling) could interrupt the positive feedback loop.
- **Williams syndrome:** *ELN* mutations cause defective elastin assembly, leading to supravalvular aortic stenosis, connective tissue abnormalities, and cognitive deficits.
- **Cutis laxa:** Rare connective tissue disorders caused by defects in elastin biogenesis or crosslinking, resulting in loose, sagging skin and systemic complications.
