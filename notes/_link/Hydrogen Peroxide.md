# Hydrogen Peroxide

**Hydrogen Peroxide** ($H_2O_2$) is a non-radical but highly reactive oxygen species (ROS). It is a key signaling molecule in cells but can also contribute to oxidative damage.

## Biological Role
- **Source:**
    - **Enzymatic:** Produced by the dismutation of [[Superoxide anion]] (via [[notes/_link/Superoxide Dismutase]]) or by enzymes such as [[MAO]] (during catecholamine degradation), [[Xanthine Oxidase]], and [[NADPH Oxidase]].
    - **Immune Cells:** Generated during the respiratory burst in [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]].
- **Signaling:** A critical "second messenger" in cell signaling because it is relatively stable and can diffuse across biological membranes. It regulates cell growth, differentiation, and [[notes/_link/Apoptosis]].
- **Defense:** Used by the immune system in the killing of pathogens.

## Role in [[Adrenochrome]] Formation
Hydrogen peroxide, along with [[Superoxide anion]], serves as an oxidant that can drive the non-enzymatic conversion of [[notes/_link/Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. This process is especially prominent under conditions of [[notes/_link/Oxidative Stress]].

## Impact and Toxicity
- **Precursor to Damage:** In the presence of transition metals (e.g., $Fe^{2+}$, $Cu^+$), it undergoes the Fenton reaction to produce the highly destructive [[Hydroxyl radical]].
- **Enzyme Inactivation:** Can oxidize cysteine residues in proteins, altering their function.

## Regulation
- [[Catalase]]: One of the primary enzymes responsible for decomposing $H_2O_2$ into water and oxygen.
- [[Glutathione Peroxidase]]: Neutralizes $H_2O_2$ using [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] as an electron donor.

## Connections
- [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] ↔ Hydrogen peroxide is a major member of the ROS family.
- [[Superoxide anion]] ↔ Precursor to hydrogen peroxide via dismutation.
- [[MAO]] ↔ Enzyme that produces H₂O₂ as a metabolic byproduct.
- [[Adrenochrome]] ↔ Oxidative byproduct whose formation is facilitated by H₂O₂.
- [[notes/_link/Oxidative Stress]] ↔ Central player in the ROS cascade.
