# Adaptive Immune System

## Overview

The [[Adaptive Immune System]] mediates antigen-specific immunity through [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]] and [[B Cells|B cells]], characterized by clonal selection, immunological memory, and antibody production. Although classically peripheral, adaptive immune cells infiltrate the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] in neurodegeneration and injury, where they interact with resident [[Microglia]] and the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis.

## cGAS-STING Priming of Adaptive Immunity

cGAS-STING activation shapes adaptive responses by enhancing antigen presentation and promoting T- and B-cell priming. STING-driven [[Type I Interferon|type I IFN]] and chemokine production can recruit adaptive lymphocytes to the CNS, while cGAS-STING activity also fosters regulatory T-cell and regulatory B-cell responses that may be protective in [[Neuroinflammation]]. Conversely, dysregulated cGAS-STING promotes autoimmunity through excessive self-antigen presentation and autoantibody generation.

## Role in CNS Disease

In neurodegenerative disease, STING activation can increase T/B-cell infiltration via chemokines, contributing to chronic CNS inflammation. The net effect is context dependent: adaptive immune engagement may be deleterious when driven by autoimmunity yet beneficial when it tempers innate inflammation, underscoring the dual pro- and anti-inflammatory roles of cGAS-STING across immune compartments.
