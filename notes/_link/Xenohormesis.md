# Xenohormesis
Xenohormesis is a biological hypothesis proposing that animals have evolved to sense chemical cues from stressed plants to prepare for worsening environmental conditions. For example, plant molecules synthesized during a prolonged drought signal to recipient animals to prepare for a presumed famine.
## Mechanism
Plants synthesize molecules like [[Resveratrol]], [[Quercetin]], and other [[Polyphenols]] in response to environmental stress. When animals ingest these "xenohormetic" compounds, they activate conserved stress-response pathways (e.g., [[Sirtuins]] activation), which confer health benefits and stress resistance.
## Relation to Aging
Xenohormesis provides an evolutionary explanation for why certain plant compounds have health-promoting and longevity-extending effects in humans and other animals.

## Definition and Evolutionary Rationale
The term **xenohormesis** (from Greek *xenos* = "foreign" + *hormesis*) was formally proposed by Howitz and Sinclair (2008, *Cell* 133(3):387–391) to describe the evolutionary logic by which heterotrophs exploit stress-induced plant metabolites as early warning signals. The core premise: because plants and animals share conserved stress-response pathways ([[Sirtuins]], [[NRF2]], [[AMPK]]), the same molecules that plants synthesize to cope with environmental hardship can activate analogous protective programs in animal consumers.
This logic provides an adaptive rationale for the health benefits of dietary phytochemicals: rather than acting as direct antioxidants (which many are too low in concentration to effect systemically), they function as **signaling molecules** that prepare the recipient organism for anticipated adversity — impending food scarcity, oxidative challenge, or pathogenic threat.
## Specific Examples
### [[Resveratrol]]
Resveratrol (3,5,4′-trihydroxy-*trans*-stilbene) is a stilbenoid polyphenol produced by Grapes, Peanuts, and [[Japanese knotweed]] in response to fungal infection, UV radiation, and mechanical stress. At low micromolar concentrations, resveratrol:
- Activates [[SIRT1]] indirectly (via [[AMPK]] and [[cAMP]]/[[Epac1]]/[[CaMKKβ]] signaling), enhancing [[PGC1-α]] deacetylation.
- Induces the [[NRF2]]/[[ARE]] pathway, upregulating [[NQO1]] and [[HO-1]].
- Inhibits [[mTORC1]] via [[Sestrin2]]/[[GATOR2]]/[[TSC2]] signaling.
- Enhances [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] and [[Fatty acid oxidation]].
### [[Sulforaphane]]
Sulforaphane, an isothiocyanate derived from glucoraphanin in cruciferous vegetables (Broccoli, Brussels sprouts), is released upon plant tissue damage (chewing, cutting). It is the most potent known dietary activator of [[NRF2]], achieving this via covalent modification of [[Keap1]] cysteine residues (C151, C273, C288). Sulforaphane also inhibits [[Histone Deacetylase]] (HDAC) activity, linking xenohormetic signaling to [[Epigenetics]] regulation.
### [[Curcumin]]
Curcumin, the lipophilic polyphenol from [[Turmeric (Curcuma longa)]], exemplifies a hormetic phytochemical with a narrow therapeutic window. It:
- Activates [[NRF2]], [[AMPK]], and [[p38 MAPK]] at low doses.
- Inhibits [[NFκB]], [[COX-2]], and [[mTOR]] at moderate doses.
- Induces [[Apoptosis]] and [[ER Stress]] at high doses.
Its low oral [[Bioavailability]] (due to rapid glucuronidation) actually supports a xenohormetic mode of action: transient, low-grade gut and hepatic stress triggers systemic adaptive responses.

## Implications for Dietary Supplementation
The xenohormesis framework has important practical consequences:
- **Dose matters**: Low, intermittent doses are likely more effective than continuous high dosing, which can desensitize stress pathways or cause toxicity.
- **Context matters**: The same compound may have different effects in well-nourished vs. calorie-restricted or sedentary vs. exercised individuals.
- **Pulsing regimens**: Periodic, short-term supplementation (e.g., 1–2 days on, 5–6 days off) may better mimic the natural intermittent exposure to plant stress compounds.
- **Combination effects**: Synergy between xenohormetic compounds acting on different targets (Nrf2 + AMPK + SIRT1) may enhance hormetic signaling at lower individual doses.
## Connection to the [[Glyco-Oxidative Proteostasis Shield]] Strategy
The [[Glyco-Oxidative Proteostasis Shield]] (GOPS) framework explicitly incorporates xenohormetic principles. The GOPS approach uses controlled exposure to electrophilic and redox-active compounds (including [[Carbazochrome]] and [[Methylene blue]]) to activate the same stress-response pathways engaged by dietary phytochemicals. In this sense, xenohormetic compounds form the nutritional arm of a broader strategy to maintain proteostasis and mitochondrial function during aging, with synthetic mitohormetic agents serving as a pharmacological complement.
