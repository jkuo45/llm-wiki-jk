# Redox Potential

The **redox potential** (E<sub>1/2</sub>) is the reduction potential of a half-reaction, reported versus a reference electrode (here normalized to the normal hydrogen electrode, NHE). It indicates how readily a molecule accepts electrons (more negative = better electron acceptor/shuttle). In the methylene blue derivative study, cyclic voltammetry was used to classify the compounds [^1].

## Findings in the methylene blue study

| Compound | E<sub>1/2</sub> (V vs NHE) | OCR effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| [[Neutral Red]] | 0.450, −0.269 | ↑ |
| [[Methylene blue]] | 0.500, −0.108 | ↑ |
| [[Toluidine Blue O]] | 0.488, −0.012 | ↑ |
| [[Phenothiazine]] | 1.342, 0.779 | none |
| [[2-Chlorophenothiazine]] | 0.942 | none |
| [[Chlorpromazine]] | 1.059 | none |
| [[Promethazine]] | 1.069 | none |

The three compounds that enhanced cellular oxygen consumption (MB, TB, NR) share **negative** redox potentials, whereas the electron-shunt-incompetent phenothiazines have strongly positive potentials [^1]. The amine side chains at positions 3,7 are proposed to drive the negative potential and hence the OCR/electron-transfer phenotype [^1].
