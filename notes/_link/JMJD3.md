# JMJD3

JMJD3 (Jumonji domain-containing protein 3), also known as KDM6B, is a JmjC-domain histone demethylase that removes the repressive trimethyl mark on histone H3 lysine 27 ([[H3K27me3]]), thereby promoting transcription. In senescence, JMJD3 specifically demethylates H3K27me3 at the promoters of the key SASP cytokines [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]], elevating their expression and that of other SASP factors.

## Overview

JMJD3 is a Fe(II)- and α-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase in the KDM6 subfamily (with UTX/KDM6A). It catalyzes demethylation of H3K27me3 and H3K27me2, counterbalancing the methyltransferase activity of [[EZH2]] (PRC2). JMJD3 is induced by developmental and inflammatory cues and is a central executor of transcriptional activation at lineage and stress-response genes.

## Structure & Function

The catalytic JmjC domain uses Fe(II) and α-KG to oxidatively demethylate tri- and dimethyl lysine. JMJD3 is recruited to target loci by transcription factors such as NF-κB, STATs, and pioneer factors, where it erases H3K27me3 to open chromatin. Because H3K27me3 is a major Polycomb repressive mark, JMJD3 activity is a key switch from repressed to active transcriptional states.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review notes that overexpression of JMJD3 specifically reduces H3K27me3 levels at the IL-6 and IL-8 gene loci, elevating expression of these and other SASP factors (ref. 65). This places JMJD3 on the "active vs. repressive mark" axis that also includes [[MLL1]] (H3K4me3) and EZH2 (H3K27me3). Whereas EZH2 inhibition and MLL1 depletion act more globally, JMJD3 provides a focal mechanism — direct removal of the Polycomb mark from the two prototypical pro-inflammatory SASP genes — coupling chromatin demethylation to the inflammatory secretome.

## Mechanistic Details

- H3K27me3 is a repressive mark enriched in senescence-associated heterochromatin ([[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]]) and at silenced SASP loci; JMJD3 erosion of this mark at IL-6/IL-8 is a local derepression event.
- JMJD3 cooperates with the DDR and [[NFKB|NF-κB]] signaling that drive SASP, since NF-κB can recruit H3K27 demethylases to inflammatory loci.
- Loss of H3K27me3 at SASP loci is consistent with the broader enhancer activation ("canyon" formation) seen in replicative senescence.

## Therapeutic Relevance

KDM6B/JMJD3 inhibitors (e.g., GSK-J4, CPI-0209 class) are under investigation in oncology and inflammatory disease. Because JMJD3 directly controls IL-6/IL-8 SASP expression, selective KDM6B inhibition is a plausible [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] approach to attenuate senescence-associated inflammation, though specificity versus KDM6A must be managed.
