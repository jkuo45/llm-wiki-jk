# ACKR3 (CXCR7)

**ACKR3** (atypical chemokine receptor 3, formerly [[CXCR7]]) is a scavenger/decoy receptor for [[CXCL12]] (SDF-1) that, unlike classical [[GPCR|G protein-coupled receptors]], signals primarily through [[β-arrestin]]-dependent pathways rather than G proteins. It functions as a chemokine scavenger, shaping [[CXCL12]] gradients and modulating [[CXCR4]]-mediated signaling.

## Role in Senescence and Vascular Aging

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Senolytic Treatment With Fisetin Reverses Age‐Related Endothelial Dysfunction Partially Mediated by SASP Factor CXCL12|Mahoney et al. 2025]]
> In senescent [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells (ECs)]], *ACKR3* [[MRNA|mRNA]] levels were lower with aging compared to young controls, but *[[CXCL12]]-[[ACKR3]]* interaction strength was increased — indicating that despite lower expression, the receptor's signaling output was amplified, potentially driving autocrine senescence reinforcement and immune cell recruitment.
>
> CellChat analysis revealed:
> - **Increased** *Cxcl12-Ackr3* interactions in old vehicle mice: senescent ECs communicating with other senescent ECs (autocrine), non-senescent ECs, [[B Cell|B cells]], [[Macrophages|macrophages]], and [[T Cell|T cells]]
> - **Decreased** *Cxcl12-Cxcr4* interactions in the same animals: defective communication with [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]] and mesothelial cells
> - Both patterns reversed by [[Fisetin]] ([[Senolytic|senolytic]]) treatment, restoring young-like signaling patterns
>
> The shift from [[CXCR4]]-dominant to [[ACKR3]]-dominant signaling in senescent ECs may represent a molecular switch that reinforces the senescent phenotype and promotes [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]].
