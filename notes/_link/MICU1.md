# MICU1

MICU1 (mitochondrial calcium uptake 1) is a regulatory subunit of the [[Mitochondria]]l calcium uniporter (MCU) complex that controls Ca²⁺ entry into the mitochondrial matrix. MICU1 contains two EF-hand domains that bind Ca²⁺ and endow the uniporter with a steep threshold response: at low cytosolic Ca²⁺ it keeps the pore closed, whereas a rise in Ca²⁺ opens the channel, allowing rapid mitochondrial Ca²⁺ uptake that stimulates rate-limiting dehydrogenases of the [[Electron Transport Chain]] and boosts ATP production.

MICU1 is essential for preventing pathological matrix Ca²⁺ overload, which can trigger [Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore] opening and [[Apoptosis]]. It acts partly cooperatively with [[MICU2]], which reinforces pore closure at rest. Loss of MICU1 causes excessive, uncontrolled Ca²⁺ uptake, mitochondrial swelling, and sensitization to cell death, underscoring its gatekeeper role.

> [!info] MICU1 couples cytosolic Ca²⁺ signals to mitochondrial metabolism while protecting against Ca²⁺-driven oxidative and permeability catastrophes.

Because mitochondrial Ca²⁺ influences redox state and bioenergetics, MICU1 indirectly modulates [[ROS]] generation and intersects with longevity and stress-adaptation pathways that depend on balanced mitochondrial calcium and [[Oxidative Stress]] control.
