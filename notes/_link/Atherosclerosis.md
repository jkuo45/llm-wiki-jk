# Atherosclerosis
Atherosclerosis is a disease in which the inside of an artery narrows due to the build up of plaque. [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Lipid Peroxidation]] are key drivers of plaque formation. It often involves [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] and the uptake of lipids via receptors like the [[CD36 Receptor]], while [[ABCA1]] helps in cholesterol efflux.
**Atherosclerosis** is a chronic inflammatory disease characterized by the buildup of plaques within arterial walls. While traditionally viewed through the lens of lipid metabolism and inflammation, recent research has identified **[[Epigenetics]]** as a critical bridge between environmental risk factors and the biological process of **[[Aging]]**, which remains the primary non-modifiable risk factor for cardiovascular disease.
## 1. The Epigenetic Landscape of Atherosclerosis
Epigenetic mechanisms regulate how vascular cells (endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, and macrophages) respond to pathological stimuli like high cholesterol, hypertension, and disturbed blood flow.
*   **[[DNA Methylation]]:** Atherosclerotic tissues often exhibit **global hypomethylation** alongside **gene-specific hypermethylation**. For example, hypermethylation of estrogen receptor genes can reduce their protective effects in the vascular wall.
*   **[[Histone Modification]]s:** An imbalance in histone acetylation and methylation can lead to the persistent activation of pro-inflammatory and pro-oxidant genes.
*   **[[Non-coding RNA]] (ncRNAs):** [[MicroRNA]] (miRNAs) and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) act as post-transcriptional regulators. For instance, miR-155 and miR-126 are known to modulate endothelial function and plaque stability.
## 2. Epigenetic Aging and the [[Epigenetic Clock]]
Aging is characterized by a progressive decline in cellular function, often driven by epigenetic drift. Researchers use epigenetic clocks (e.g., the [[DNAmGrimAge]] clock) to measure "biological age" based on DNA methylation patterns.
*   **Epigenetic Age Acceleration (EAA):** When a person's biological age exceeds their chronological age, they are said to have accelerated epigenetic aging. Individuals with subclinical atherosclerosis often exhibit higher EAA.
*   **Vascular Wall Mosaicism:** Atherosclerotic plaques often show accelerated biological aging compared to healthy arterial segments in the same individual.
## 3. [[Clonal Hematopoiesis]] (CHIP)
A major discovery linking aging, epigenetics, and atherosclerosis is **Clonal Hematopoiesis of Indeterminate Potential (CHIP)**. As people age, hematopoietic stem cells can acquire mutations in epigenetic regulators like *TET2* and [[DNA Methyltransferase]] 3A (*DNMT3A*). These mutated cells produce hyper-reactive inflammatory macrophages that significantly accelerate plaque inflammation.
## 4. Therapeutic Potential
Unlike genetic mutations, epigenetic marks are potentially reversible:
*   **Epigenetic Drugs:** Inhibitors of DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs) and [[Histone Deacetylase]]s ([[HDAC]]s) are being explored to "reset" the vascular epigenome.
*   **Lifestyle:** [[Caloric Restriction]] and exercise have been shown to delay vascular aging, likely through epigenetic reprogramming of longevity-related pathways like [[Sirtuins]]s.
