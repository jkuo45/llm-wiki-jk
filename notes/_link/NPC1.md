# NPC1

## Overview

Niemann-Pick C1 (NPC1) is a late-endosomal/lysosomal membrane protein essential for intracellular cholesterol trafficking. Loss-of-function mutations in *NPC1* cause Niemann-Pick disease type C, a lethal lysosomal storage disorder characterized by cholesterol accumulation, progressive neurodegeneration, and chronic [[Neuroinflammation]]. Beyond its metabolic role, NPC1 has emerged as a regulator of innate immune signaling: impaired NPC1 function drives constitutive activation of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] through endolysosomal stress, linking lysosomal dysfunction to chronic interferon responses.

## Regulation of cGAS-STING at the Nuclear Envelope

NPC1 deficiency (NPC1-/-) disrupts endolysosomal trafficking and triggers STING overactivation. This activation can occur independently of canonical [[cGAS]] in some contexts, as impaired STING degradation and vesicular trafficking defects prolong signaling at the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] and [[Nuclear Envelope]]. The resulting sustained [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] production and [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISG]] expression mirror the maladaptive innate immune activation seen across proteinopathies, implicating NPC1 as a checkpoint that restrains cGAS-STING under homeostatic conditions.

## Relevance to Neurodegeneration

Because lysosomal dysfunction is a shared feature of many neurodegenerative diseases, NPC1-dependent STING activation provides a mechanistic bridge between endolysosomal stress and [[Neuroinflammation]]. In the cGAS-STING framework, NPC1 loss supports the concept that [[STING]] acts as a broader integrator of cellular stress beyond cytosolic DNA, contributing to interferon activation in neurodegeneration alongside triggers such as [[mtDNA]] leakage and [[DNA Damage]].
