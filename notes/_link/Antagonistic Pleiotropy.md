# Antagonistic Pleiotropy

**Antagonistic Pleiotropy** is an evolutionary concept (notably developed by George C. Williams) in which a genetic trait or physiological program that enhances fitness early in life exerts deleterious effects later in life, after the period of peak reproduction. It explains why selection does not eliminate aging-causing mechanisms.

## Application to the RCM Hypothesis

Within the [[Relocalization of Chromatin Modifiers (RCM) Hypothesis]], antagonistic pleiotropy frames chromatin-modifier relocalization as a beneficial short-term response: recruiting modifiers to [[DNA Damage|damage sites]] helps cells survive acute threats (rapid replication in microbes/embryos or injury) by prioritizing repair over normal gene regulation. This is advantageous in youth; across a lifetime, the cumulative "failure to reset" becomes detrimental and drives aging.
