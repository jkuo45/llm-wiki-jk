# Iodoacetic Acid

[[Iodoacetic Acid]] (IAA, iodoacetate) is a reactive halogenated acetic acid derivative that irreversibly inhibits glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) by alkylating its catalytic cysteine thiol. This blocks glycolysis, depletes [[ATP]], and — through disrupted disulfide handling and impaired NADH reoxidation — raises [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and promotes lipid peroxidation [^23], [^24], [^25], [^26].

## Role as a neurotoxicity insult

In the methylene blue derivative study, IAA was one of three HT-22 hippocampal-neuron insults used to resolve structure–activity relationships for neuroprotection [^1]. All tested phenothiazine-related compounds protected against IAA neurotoxicity; [[Methylene blue]] and [[2-Chlorophenothiazine]] showed greater efficacy than [[Chlorpromazine]] and [[Neutral Red]] [^1]. Because IAA kills cells via energy failure and ROS rather than by direct extracellular oxidant exposure, it is sensitive to both the electron-carrier action of MB/TB and the direct scavenging action of the naked phenothiazine nucleus.

## Mechanistic contrast

IAA injury is distinct from [[Glucose Oxidase]] injury: glucose oxidase generates extracellular H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> that classical antioxidants (e.g., pyruvate) neutralize but MB does **not**, whereas IAA injury is mitigated by MB's mitochondrial mechanisms [^1].
