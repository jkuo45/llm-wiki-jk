# Condensin

Condensin is a structural maintenance of chromosomes (SMC) complex that compacts and organizes chromatin. In senescence, condensin (the SMC2–SMC4 core, often condensin I/II) shows increased binding at B-to-A compartment-switching regions, regions that are enriched for SASP genes and coincide with their upregulation — placing condensin within the 3D-genome rewiring that governs the [[SASP]].

## Overview

Eukaryotic condensins are pentameric complexes built around an SMC2–SMC4 heterodimer plus three non-SMC subunits (CAP-D2/E/G for condensin I; CAP-D3/E2/G2 for condensin II). ATP-dependent conformational changes in the SMC "hinge–head" allow condensin to extrude DNA loops, compact chromosomes during mitosis, and shape interphase chromatin architecture.

## Structure & Function

The SMC2–SMC4 ATPase heads dimerize at the "head," while the hinge provides the DNA-entry gate. Non-SMC subunits regulate loading, target specificity, and interaction with other chromatin factors. Condensin participates in chromosome condensation, resolution of sister chromatids, and loop extrusion that organizes topologically associating domains.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review (Iwasaki et al., 2019; PMID 31729328) reports that in both oncogene-induced and replicative senescence, B compartments frequently merge with adjacent A compartments, forming B-to-A-switching (BA-switching) regions. Binding of the condensin complex is notably increased at these BA-switching regions and coincides with upregulation of SASP genes (ref. 22). These regions are enriched for SASP-related gene groups, and the master SASP regulator [[NFKB|NF-κB]] locus itself resides in an active A compartment, remaining transcriptionally engaged. Condensin thus contributes to the large-scale compartment reorganization that licenses SASP transcription, operating alongside enhancer–promoter looping and boundary proteins such as [[HMGB2]].

## Mechanistic Details

- Condensin enrichment at BA-switching regions couples compartment repositioning to SASP locus activation.
- Loop-extrusion activity may stabilize the contacts that bring SASP promoters into active A-compartment environments.
- Condensin's role complements the METTL3–METTL14-driven enhancer–promoter looping and HMGB2 boundary function in 3D-genome SASP control.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Condensin itself is challenging to target selectively, but its placement within the senescence 3D-genome axis suggests that modulating chromosome architecture (e.g., via loop-extrusion or TAD-boundary factors) may influence SASP. Such approaches remain exploratory relative to established [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] agents like BET or KDM4 inhibitors.
