# Ryanodine Receptor

The ryanodine receptor (RyR) is a large tetrameric intracellular Ca²⁺ release channel embedded in the membrane of the sarcoplasmic/endoplasmic reticulum. Named for its high-affinity binding of the plant alkaloid ryanodine, RyR releases Ca²⁺ from intracellular stores in response to cytosolic Ca²⁺ itself (calcium-induced calcium release), voltage, or chemical ligands. In excitable cells, RyR-mediated release generates the cytosolic Ca²⁺ transients that are then taken up by [[Mitochondria]] through the MCU complex, coupling ER/ SR Ca²⁺ signaling to mitochondrial metabolism.

Three vertebrate isoforms exist (RyR1 in skeletal muscle, RyR2 in heart, RyR3 in brain and other tissues). Chronic RyR leak—driven by oxidative modification, post-translational modifications, or aging—elevates cytosolic Ca²⁺ and can drive mitochondrial Ca²⁺ overload, [[ROS]] production, and opening of the [Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore], culminating in [[Apoptosis]].

> [!warning] RyR channels are redox-sensitive; oxidation of RyR cysteine residues promotes "leaky" channels that fuel mitochondrial Ca²⁺ stress and [[Oxidative Stress]].

RyR thus sits upstream of mitochondrial calcium and redox balance, linking excitation–contraction coupling and intracellular signaling to mitochondrial quality and longevity.
