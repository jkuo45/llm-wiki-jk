# Serotonin Receptor

[[Serotonin Receptor]]s (5-HT receptors) are the family of membrane proteins through which [[Serotonin]] (5-hydroxytryptamine) transmits its signals in the central and peripheral nervous systems, gut, and vasculature.

## Chemical/Molecular Classification

- **Seven families (5-HT1–5-HT7):** all are G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) **except** 5-HT3, which is a ligand-gated cation channel.
- **Signaling:** 5-HT1 couples to Gi/o (lowering cAMP, autoreceptor feedback); 5-HT2 couples to Gq (activating PLC and intracellular calcium); 5-HT4/6/7 couple to Gs (raising cAMP).
- **Distribution:** dense expression in raphe nuclei, cortex, limbic system, enteric neurons, platelets, and smooth muscle — explaining serotonin's broad effects on mood, cognition, GI motility, and vascular tone.

## Pharmacology & Clinical Relevance

> [!warning] Serotonin syndrome
> Excessive agonism at 5-HT receptors — particularly 5-HT2A — produces [[Serotonin Syndrome]], a potentially life-threatening triad of neuromuscular excitation, autonomic instability, and altered mental status. Risk rises when serotonergic drugs are combined, e.g. a [[Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor]] (or the MAO-A-inhibiting dye [[Methylene blue]]) plus a [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors|SSRI]].

Serotonin receptors are major drug targets: triptans (5-HT1B/1D) for migraine, atypical antipsychotics (5-HT2A antagonism), ondansetron (5-HT3 antagonist antiemetic), and hallucinogens (5-HT2A agonists).
