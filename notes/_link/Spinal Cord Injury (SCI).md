# Spinal Cord Injury (SCI)

**Spinal cord injury (SCI)** is damage to the [[Spinal Cord]] — typically from trauma (vertebral fracture, compression) or ischemia — producing loss of [[Motor Neurons|motor]], sensory, and autonomic function below the level of lesion. Secondary injury involves ischemia, [[Inflammation]], [[Oxidative Stress]], and [[Apoptosis]] of neurons and glia.

## Clinical Consequences

- Paralysis and [[Muscle Atrophy|muscle wasting]] below lesion
- [[Neuropathic Pain|neuropathic pain]]
- Autonomic dysreflexia, bladder/bowel dysfunction
- Reduced aerobic capacity and [[Functional Capacity|functional limitation]]

## Creatine and SCI

[[Creatine in Health and Disease]] reviews evidence that [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine]] supports recovery and adaptation after SCI:

- **Neuroprotection (animal)**: Hausmann et al. and Rabchevsky et al. found pre-/post-injury creatine reduced scar tissue, preserved gray matter, and improved locomotor function.
- **Human rehabilitation**: Jacobs et al. reported creatine (20 g/day × 7 days) enhanced aerobic capacity and ventilatory anaerobic threshold in cervical SCI. Amorim et al. found creatine (3 g/day × 8 weeks) + [[Vitamin D]] + resistance training improved arm muscle area, strength, and function. Some studies showed no benefit.

## Management

Acute decompression/stabilization, methylprednisolone (controversial), rehabilitation, and emerging neuroregenerative approaches.
