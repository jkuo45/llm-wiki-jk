# Autonomic Nervous System

The [[Autonomic Nervous System|autonomic nervous system (ANS)]] is the involuntary division of the peripheral nervous system controlling cardiac muscle, smooth muscle, and glands. It operates without conscious input and is the physiological substrate read out by [[Heart Rate Variability|HRV]]. The ANS has three branches — sympathetic, parasympathetic, and enteric — but the sympathetic/parasympathetic balance is what dominates HRV and recovery signaling.

## Sympathetic Branch ("Fight, Flight, or Freeze")

The **[[Sympathetic Nervous System|sympathetic nervous system]]** mobilizes energy for action. It releases catecholamines ([[Epinephrine]], [[Norepinephrine]]) and cortisol, increasing heart rate, bronchodilation, and glucose availability while *suppressing* HRV. It is engaged by acute mitohormetic stressors such as HIIT, cold exposure, and fasting.

## Parasympathetic Branch ("Rest, Digest, and Repair")

The **[[Parasympathetic Nervous System|parasympathetic nervous system]]** is dominated by the [[Vagus Nerve|vagus nerve]] (cranial nerve X) and the sacral outflow. It slows the heart, stimulates digestion, and promotes cellular repair, *raising* HRV. High vagal tone is the hallmark of a well-recovered, adaptable system.

## Relevance to Mitohormesis

[[Mitohormesis]] applies acute stressors that transiently engage the sympathetic branch, then relies on parasympathetic rebound during recovery. When stressors are stacked without recovery (severe caloric restriction + daily HIIT + poor sleep), the sympathetic branch gets "stuck on," HRV chronically drops, [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] stalls, and [[Mitophagy]] is impaired — converting hormesis into [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] and [[Inflammation|inflammation]].
