# AUF1

**AU-rich element RNA-binding protein 1 (AUF1)**, also known as HNRNPD, is an RNA-binding protein that targets mRNAs containing AU-rich elements (AREs) in their 3' untranslated regions for degradation. It is a key post-transcriptional brake on the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]].

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

AUF1 (hnRNP D) exists as four isoforms (p37, p40, p42, p45) generated by alternative splicing, each bearing two RNA-recognition motifs that bind AREs of the AUUUA type. Once bound, AUF1 recruits the exonuclease XRN1 and the deadenylase PARN, and collaborates with the exosome, to drive rapid transcript decay. Its function is regulated by phosphorylation: the [[p38 MAPK]]–[[MAPKAPK2|MK2]] pathway phosphorylates AUF1, promoting its cytoplasmic relocalization away from target mRNAs and/or reducing decay competence. This relieves ARE-mRNA turnover. AUF1 also interacts with heat-shock proteins (Hsp27/Hsp70) and with the translation machinery, integrating stress responses with mRNA stability.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

AUF1 is a negative regulator of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]]:
- It normally promotes the decay of transcripts for many pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines.
- In [[Senescent Cells]], the [[MAPKAPK2|MK2]] pathway (downstream of [[p38 MAPK]]) phosphorylates AUF1, leading to its inactivation or redistribution.
- This allows for the stabilization and increased production of SASP factors like [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]].

The p38–MK2–AUF1 axis thus converts chronic stress signaling into sustained SASP output at the level of mRNA half-life, acting in parallel with the [[NF-κB]] transcriptional program. Restoring AUF1 activity (or inhibiting MK2) can blunt SASP intensity — a senomorphic strategy.

## Significance

The regulation of AUF1 activity is a critical control point for determining the intensity of the SASP, with relevance to [[Inflammaging]] and therapy-induced senescence.
