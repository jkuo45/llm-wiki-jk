# CCL2 (MCP-1)

CCL2 (C-C motif chemokine ligand 2), also known as monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), is a chemokine that plays a central role in the recruitment of monocytes and macrophages to sites of inflammation. It is a key component of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]], regulated by [[IL-1α]] and [[Interleukin 1β|IL-1β]].

## Mechanism of Action

- CCL2 is secreted by senescent cells as a SASP factor
- It acts as a strong chemoattractant for monocytes and macrophages through binding to its receptor CCR2
- CCL2 expression is regulated by [[IL-1α]] and [[IL-1β]] signaling
- [[BMI1]] can negatively regulate CCL2 through an IL-1α-mediated mechanism in senescent mesenchymal stromal cells

## Role in SASP

- CCL2 is one of the most well-characterized SASP chemokines
- It mediates immune cell recruitment to senescent cells, which can be beneficial (immune surveillance) or detrimental (pro-inflammatory)
- CCL2 promotes immune evasion by attracting CCR2+ myeloid cells in cancer contexts
- Urine levels of CCL2 are used to assess SASP in patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

## Clinical Relevance

- CCL2 is elevated in age-related inflammatory conditions
- CCL2/CCR2 signaling is a therapeutic target in inflammatory diseases and cancer
- CCL2 levels correlate with senescence burden in human studies
