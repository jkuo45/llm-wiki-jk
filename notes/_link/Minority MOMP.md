# Minority MOMP

**Minority MOMP (miMOMP)** refers to sublethal [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]] events that occur in a fraction of the mitochondria within a cell, without triggering full-scale [[Apoptosis]]. Unlike uniform, all-or-nothing MOMP that commits a cell to death, miMOMP is stochastic and reversible at the population level: only a minority of mitochondria undergo permeabilization at any moment, so sufficient [[Cytochrome c]] and [[mtDNA]] are released to signal stress, but not enough to activate executioner caspases.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Pore regulation**: miMOMP is mediated by pro-apoptotic [[Bcl-2]] family proteins, especially [[BAX]] and [[BAK]], whose activity is held in check by anti-apoptotic members (BCL-2, BCL-XL, MCL-1) and by [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways|SCAPs]].
- **mtDNA release**: High-order BAX/BAK macropores permit herniation of the inner membrane and ejection of intact [[mtDNA]] and other mitochondrial DAMPs into the cytosol.
- **cGAS–STING activation**: Cytosolic mtDNA is sensed by the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] innate immune axis, engaging [[TBK1]]/IRF and NF-κB signaling.
- **Limited apoptosis**: Because only a minority of mitochondria are permeabilized, caspase activation stays below the threshold for demolition, preserving cell viability.

## Role in Senescence

- miMOMP is a critical link between [[Apoptosis]] and [[Cellular Senescence]]: damaged cells survive and instead broadcast their stressed state through the SASP.
- Released mtDNA drives a chronic, type I interferon–biased SASP that sustains [[Inflammaging]].
- By keeping the cell alive but inflammatory, miMOMP explains how senescent cells accumulate despite possessing active apoptotic machinery.
- Interrupting miMOMP–cGAS–STING signaling is a strategy to blunt the senescence phenotype without killing the cell (senomorphic) or to sensitize it to [[Senolytics]].

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- miMOMP-derived mtDNA sensing contributes to age-related inflammation and to SASP-driven pathologies (fibrosis, metabolic dysfunction).
- It provides a mechanistic rationale for combining [[Senolytics]] (which kill SCAP-protected cells) with cGAS–STING inhibitors in age-related disease.
- Dysregulated miMOMP is implicated in sterile inflammation and therapy resistance.
