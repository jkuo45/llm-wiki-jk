# PARP2

**PARP2** (poly[ADP-ribose] polymerase 2) is a member of the PARP enzyme family that, together with [[PARP1]], accounts for the majority of DNA-damage-induced poly(ADP-ribosyl)ation (PARylation) in the nucleus.

## Structure & Mechanism of Action

- Contains a catalytic ADP-ribosyltransferase domain and a WGR/DNA-binding region that recognizes DNA strand breaks.
- **Reaction:** upon binding DNA damage, PARP2 consumes [[NAD+]] to transfer ADP-ribose units onto target proteins (and itself), forming branched PAR chains that recruit [[DNA Repair]] machinery — particularly for [[Base Excision Repair]] and single-strand break repair.
- PARP2 is especially important at the later stages of the DNA-damage response and in maintaining genome and telomere stability.

## Physiological Role & NAD+/Sirtuin Crosstalk

> [!important] Competition with sirtuins
> Because both PARPs and [[Sirtuins|sirtuins]] use [[NAD+]] as substrate, chronic PARP activation depletes the cellular NAD⁺ pool and thereby restrains NAD⁺-dependent deacetylases. Elevated PARP2 activity specifically lowers [[SIRT1]] activity both by substrate competition and by binding directly to the *Sirt1* promoter to repress its transcription. This positions PARP2 at the intersection of [[Genomic Instability]], NAD⁺ metabolism, and the metabolic/longevity functions of SIRT1 — a rationale for NAD⁺-boosting strategies ([[Nicotinamide Riboside]], [[NMN]]) and PARP inhibitors in aging research.
