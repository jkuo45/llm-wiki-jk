# SERT

## Overview
[[SERT]] (serotonin transporter, SLC6A4) is a Na+/Cl−-dependent membrane transporter that reuptakes extracellular [[Serotonin]] (5-HT) into presynaptic neurons and platelets, terminating serotonergic signaling. It is the primary target of [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors]] and a key node in [[Depression]] and [[Aging]].

## Structure & Function
SERT belongs to the SLC6 family, alongside [[Dopamine Transporter]] ([[DAT]]) and norepinephrine transporters. It couples serotonin influx to the Na+ gradient and exports H+, tightly regulating synaptic 5-HT concentration and tone.

## Mechanism of Action & Pharmacology
Inhibition of SERT by [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors]] or [[Tricyclic Antidepressant]] agents raises synaptic serotonin, relieving mood disorders. Combining serotonergic agents risks [[Serotonin Syndrome]]. SERT function is also shaped by [[Monoamine oxidase]] catabolism and by [[Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors]]. Polymorphisms in SLC6A4 modulate stress resilience and [[Psychiatric Disorders]] risk.

## Redox, Excitotoxicity & Longevity
Serotonin signaling interacts with [[Glutamate]]-mediated [[Excitotoxicity]] and with [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] homeostasis in the brain. Altered SERT contributes to [[Neurodegeneration]] and age-related cognitive change. Balanced serotonergic tone supports [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] resilience, linking SERT to redox and [[Healthspan]]-relevant neuroprotection.
