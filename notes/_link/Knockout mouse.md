## Overview

A **knockout mouse** is a genetically modified mouse strain in which one or more specific genes have been rendered non-functional ("knocked out") through targeted recombination, typically exploiting embryonic stem (ES) cell-mediated homologous recombination or, more recently, CRISPR/Cas9 editing. The resulting animals allow investigators to infer the physiological role of a gene by comparing knockout phenotypes against wild-type littermates.

## Origin and Methodology

The classical workflow uses [[C57BL-6]] or 129/Sv-derived ES cells, introducing a targeting construct that disrupts the coding sequence or exon of interest. Chimeric offspring are bred to germline transmission, and heterozygous progeny are intercrossed to yield homozygous knockouts. Conditional knockouts (e.g., Cre-loxP systems) permit tissue- or time-specific gene ablation, reducing developmental lethality.

## Common Research Uses

Knockout mice are foundational for defining gene function across [[Aging]], [[Cancer]], immunology, metabolism, and neuroscience. They validate druggability of targets, model human monogenic disease, and dissect pathways such as [[mTOR]], [[Mitochondria]]-mediated stress responses, and [[Apoptosis]]. Within aging and longevity research, knockouts of candidate longevity genes (e.g., sirtuins, insulin/IGF-1 signaling components) test causal roles in lifespan and healthspan.

## Relevance to Longevity, Cancer, and Neuroscience

Knockout models reveal whether a gene promotes or suppresses tumorigenesis and are used to study [[Neurodegeneration]] when combined with neuronal-specific Cre drivers. Limits include strain background effects, compensatory developmental remodeling, and incomplete correspondence to human physiology.
