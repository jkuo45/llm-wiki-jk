# Bax

[[Bax]] (BCL2-associated X protein) is a pro-apoptotic member of the [[Bcl-2 family]] of proteins. It is a critical effector of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway, functioning at the mitochondrial outer membrane to trigger [[Cytochrome C]] release and subsequent activation of the [[Caspases|caspase]] cascade.

## Structure and Domains

Bax is a 20 kDa protein containing three functional domains:
- **BH3 domain**: Mediates binding to anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2]] and [[Bcl-xL]] family members
- **BH1 domain**: Required for homo-oligomerization and channel formation
- **C-terminal transmembrane domain (TM)**: Anchors Bax to the mitochondrial outer membrane

In healthy cells, Bax exists in an inactive cytosolic conformation with its TM domain buried. Upon apoptotic stimuli, Bax undergoes conformational change, exposing the TM domain and enabling mitochondrial translocation.

## Mechanism of Action

> [!important] Bax Activation and Oligomerization
> Upon activation by BH3-only proteins (e.g., [[Bid]], Bim, PUMA), Bax translocates to the mitochondrial outer membrane, undergoes conformational change, and oligomerizes to form large pores. These pores disrupt [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] and permit release of [[Cytochrome C]] and other intermembrane space proteins into the cytoplasm, triggering [[Intrinsic Apoptosis]].

## Regulation

Bax activity is tightly regulated by the balance between:
- **Anti-apoptotic proteins**: [[Bcl-2]], [[Bcl-xL]], Mcl-1, which sequester Bax and prevent oligomerization
- **Pro-apoptotic BH3-only proteins**: [[Bid]], Bim, PUMA, which directly activate Bax or displace it from anti-apoptotic complexes
- **[[Humanin]]**: Directly binds Bax in the cytosol (amino acids 8–12), preventing translocation to the mitochondrial membrane

## Biological Significance

Bax plays essential roles in:
- Developmental programmed cell death
- Immune system homeostasis
- Tumor suppression (Bax knockout mice develop lymphomas)
- Neuronal death in neurodegenerative diseases including [[Alzheimer's Disease]]
