# BAX

BAX (BCL-2-associated X protein, gene *BCL2L4*) is a pro-apoptotic effector of the [[Bcl-2]] family that mediates [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]] and commitment to [[Apoptosis]]. In healthy cells BAX is largely cytosolic or loosely tethered to mitochondria; upon apoptotic or senescent stress it undergoes conformational activation, inserts into the outer membrane, and oligomerizes with [[BAK]] to form pores. High-order BAX/BAK "macropores" can permit herniation of the mitochondrial inner membrane into the cytosol and release of intact [[mtDNA]], a process increasingly linked to chronic [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] activation (see [[Minority MOMP]]).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Activation**: BH3-only proteins (e.g., [[Bim]], [[PUMA]], [[NOXA]], tBID) displace BAX from sequestration by anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2]] family members (BCL-2, BCL-XL, MCL-1). Activated BAX exposes its N-terminal helix, translocates to the outer membrane, and dimerizes.
- **Pore formation**: BAX and BAK cooperatively assemble lipidic pores. Low-order pores release soluble intermembrane proteins such as [[Cytochrome c]], whereas high-order macropores cause inner-membrane protrusion and ejection of cytosolic [[mtDNA]].
- **Apoptotic execution**: Released cytochrome c binds APAF-1 to nucleate the [[Apoptosome]], activating [[Caspase-9]] and executioner [[Caspase-3]], driving cellular demolition.
- **Senescence crosstalk**: In cells experiencing sublethal stress, partial (minority) MOMP leaves BAX/BAK pore activity sufficient to leak mtDNA without triggering full apoptosis, engaging the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] axis.

## Role in Senescence

- BAX-mediated mtDNA release is a proximal trigger of type I interferon–biased SASP via cGAS–STING and [[TBK1]]/IRF signaling.
- Because senescent cells upregulate [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways|SCAPs]], many BAX pores remain sub-lethal, converting a death signal into a chronic inflammatory signal.
- Loss of BAX (or BAK) blunts mtDNA release and SASP in several models, supporting its role as a senescence effector node.
- BAX/BAK macropores are therefore a mechanistic bridge between [[Apoptosis]] and [[Cellular Senescence]].

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- BAX is a long-standing pro-apoptotic target in oncology (BH3 mimetics neutralize its inhibitors rather than BAX directly).
- Its role in senescent-cell mtDNA leakage informs [[Senolytics]] and anti-SASP strategies aimed at interrupting the cGAS–STING loop.
- Age-related [[Inflammaging]] is partly sustained by BAX-driven chronic mtDNA sensing.
