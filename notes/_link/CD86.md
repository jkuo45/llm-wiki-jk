## Overview

[[CD86]], also known as B7-2, is a type I transmembrane protein of the B7 immunoglobulin superfamily expressed on [[Antigen-Presenting Cells]] such as [[Macrophages]], dendritic cells, and activated B cells. It is a principal co-stimulatory ligand that delivers the secondary signal required for full T-cell activation.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

CD86 binds two receptors on T cells:

- **CD28** — provides activating co-stimulation (signal 2), promoting [[T-cell]] proliferation, [[IL-2]] production, and survival.
- **CTLA-4 (CD152)** — delivers an inhibitory signal that attenuates T-cell responses and maintains tolerance.

CD86 is rapidly upregulated following APC activation and works in concert with its homolog [[CD80]] (B7-1). The balance of CD86/CD80 engagement of CD28 versus CTLA-4 finely tunes immune activation versus suppression, linking innate sensing to adaptive immunity.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

Dysregulated CD86 expression contributes to autoimmune disease, chronic inflammation, and anti-tumor immunity. CD86 is a target of co-stimulatory blockade therapies (e.g., CTLA-4Ig/abatacept) used in autoimmune conditions. In the wiki's research on [[Inflammation]] and senescent cell immunosurveillance, CD86 marks activated antigen-presenting cells and modulates the inflammatory milieu associated with the [[SASP]].
