# CXCL10

CXCL10 (C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 10), also known as IP-10 (interferon gamma-induced protein 10), is an ELR− (non-ELR) C-X-C chemokine that binds [[CXCR3]] (and CXCR3B splice variant). Unlike ELR+ CXC chemokines it is not angiogenic/neutrophil-directed; instead it recruits T cells and natural killer cells and is a key immunosurveillance arm of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptor binding**: CXCL10 is the principal ligand of [[CXCR3]] (also binds CXCR3B). Receptor activation couples to Gαi → PI3K/Akt, [[p38 MAPK]]/ERK, and Ca2+ flux, driving polarized migration.
- **Cell sources**: Secreted by endothelial cells, macrophages, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells; in senescence it is prominently released by ROS-induced senescent hepatocytes.
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Strongly induced by type I/II interferons through JAK/[[STAT1]] and the [[NF-κB]] and [[p38 MAPK]] pathways; in senescent tumor cells it is restrained by the Polycomb methyltransferase [[EZH2]], which represses its promoter. The [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] DNA-sensing pathway is an upstream driver linking genotoxic stress to CXCL10 release.
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Chemoattracts activated Th1/CD8+ T cells, NK cells, and monocytes; enhances NK cytotoxicity and promotes interferon-responsive, anti-tumor immunity.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CXCL10 is an immunomodulatory SASP factor that converts senescent cells into beacons for immune effector cells, supporting immune surveillance and clearance of senescent hepatocytes and other damaged cells.
- It is a core component of the interferon-responsive SASP branch and is co-induced with other CXCR3 ligands ([[CXCL11]], CXCL9).
- By recruiting NK and T cells, CXCL10 helps resolve senescence acutely, but persistent expression in aged tissue can sustain [[Inflammaging]] and contribute to autoimmune pathology.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- Elevated CXCL10/CXCR3 signaling is found in viral hepatitis, SLE, rheumatoid arthritis, atherosclerosis, and multiple sclerosis.
- CXCL10 is a candidate blood biomarker of senescence burden in liver disease and of interferon-active inflammatory states.
- It is being evaluated as a target/partner in cancer immunotherapy and as a senescence-clearance readout in senolytic trials.
