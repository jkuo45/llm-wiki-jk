# Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors

**Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs)** are antidepressants that block reuptake of both [[Serotonin]] and [[Norepinephrine]], raising their synaptic availability. Major agents include venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine, duloxetine, and levomilnacipram.

## Pharmacodynamics
By inhibiting the serotonin transporter and the norepinephrine transporter, SNRIs enhance both serotonergic and noradrenergic neurotransmission. This dual action is exploited not only in major depression but also in generalized anxiety disorder, diabetic peripheral neuropathic pain (duloxetine), fibromyalgia, and certain stress urinary incontinence indications. At higher doses, venlafaxine also weakly inhibits dopamine reuptake.

## Adverse Interactions
Like [[Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors]], SNRIs are contraindicated with [[Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor]]s because the combined serotonergic and noradrenergic load can precipitate [[Serotonin Syndrome]]. [[Methylene blue]] exhibits MAO-A–inhibitory activity and therefore should not be combined with SNRIs. Discontinuation of SNRIs can produce a withdrawal syndrome (dizziness, dysphoria, "brain zaps") because of their relatively short half-lives compared with SSRIs.

> [!warning] Drug Interaction
> Methylene blue's MAO-A inhibition makes co-administration with SNRIs dangerous; observe a washout period when switching classes.
