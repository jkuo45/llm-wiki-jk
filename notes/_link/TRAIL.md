# TRAIL

## Overview
[[TRAIL]] (TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand) is a type II transmembrane protein and member of the TNF superfamily that induces apoptosis preferentially in transformed and tumor cells while sparing most normal cells. TRAIL signals through death receptors DR4 (TRAIL-R1) and DR5 (TRAIL-R2) to activate the extrinsic apoptotic pathway via [[Caspase-8]].

## Structure & Activation
TRAIL is expressed as a type II membrane protein that can be cleaved by proteases (e.g., metalloproteinases, caspases) to release a soluble extracellular domain. The soluble TRAIL trimer binds DR4 and DR5, inducing receptor trimerization and recruitment of the adaptor protein FADD and procaspase-8 to form the death-inducing signaling complex (DISC).

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Extrinsic Apoptosis**: TRAIL binding to DR4/DR5 recruits FADD and [[Caspase-8]] to the DISC. Caspase-8 undergoes proximity-induced auto-activation, then directly cleaves executioner caspases ([[Caspase-3]], [[Caspase-7]]) or cleaves Bid to engage the mitochondrial amplification loop.
- **Decoy Receptors**: TRAIL also binds decoy receptors DcR1 and DcR2, which lack functional death domains and compete with DR4/DR5 for TRAIL binding, modulating apoptotic sensitivity.

> [!info] SHP1 Controls TRAIL Sensitivity
> The efficacy of TRAIL depends on the phosphorylation state of [[Caspase-8]]. [[SHP1]]-mediated dephosphorylation of Caspase-8 at Tyr380 restores apoptotic sensitivity to TRAIL. Tumors that silence SHP1 maintain Caspase-8 in its phosphorylated, non-functional state, rendering them resistant to TRAIL.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer Therapeutics**: Recombinant TRAIL (dulanermin) and TRAIL receptor agonist antibodies (mapatumumab, conatumumab) have been tested in clinical trials, with mixed results. Resistance mechanisms include decoy receptor overexpression, FLIP upregulation, and Caspase-8 silencing/phosphorylation.
- **Immune Surveillance**: TRAIL is expressed on NK cells, T cells, and macrophages, contributing to immune surveillance against tumors and virus-infected cells.
