# Selenium

**Selenium** (Se) is an essential trace element that is incorporated into selenoproteins as the 21st amino acid, selenocysteine (Sec, U), where it confers unique redox properties critical for antioxidant defense, thyroid hormone metabolism, and redox signaling.

## Selenoproteins

The human selenoproteome comprises 25 selenoproteins, including:
- **[[Glutathione Peroxidase|GPx]] family** (GPx1–4, GPx6): Reduce H₂O₂ and lipid hydroperoxides
- **[[Thioredoxin reductase]] family** (TXNRD1–3): Regenerate reduced thioredoxin
- **[[Iodothyronine deiodinases]]** (DIO1–3): Activate/inactivate thyroid hormones
- **[[Selenoprotein P]]** (SELENOP): Selenium transport from liver to peripheral tissues
- **[[Selenoprotein S]]** (SELENOS, VIMP): ER stress and unfolded protein response
- **[[Methionine sulfoxide reductase B1]]** (MSRB1): Repair of oxidized methionine residues

## Catalytic Mechanism

Selenocysteine (pKa ~5.2) is fully ionized at physiological pH, making it a more potent nucleophile than cysteine (pKa ~8.3). The selenolate (Se⁻) is rapidly oxidized to selenenic acid (SeOH) by peroxides, then recycled via glutathionylation and reduction, conferring catalytic rates 100–1,000× higher than equivalent cysteine-dependent enzymes and resistance to irreversible overoxidation.

## Dietary Sources & Requirements

Major dietary sources include Brazil nuts, seafood, organ meats, eggs, and grains grown in selenium-rich soil. The recommended dietary allowance (RDA) is 55 μg/d for adults; the tolerable upper intake level (UL) is 400 μg/d. Selenium content in soil varies dramatically by geography (e.g., North American plains are selenium-rich; parts of China, Europe, and New Zealand are selenium-poor).

## Deficiency & Toxicity

- **Selenium Deficiency**: Impairs GPx and TXNRD activity → increased oxidative stress. Endemic deficiencies cause [[Keshan disease]] (cardiomyopathy, primarily in children and women of childbearing age in selenium-poor regions of China) and [[Kashin-Beck disease]] (osteoarthropathy).
- **Selenosis**: Symptoms include garlic breath odor, hair loss, brittle nails, skin lesions, and neurotoxicity at intakes >900 μg/d.

## Clinical Relevance

- [[notes/oxidative_stress/Cancer|Cancer Prevention]]: Epidemiological studies associate higher selenium status with reduced risk of prostate, lung, colorectal, and bladder cancers (SELECT trial found no benefit with selenomethionine alone, but secondary analyses suggest benefit in men with low baseline selenium).
- [[notes/oxidative_stress/Inflammation|Inflammatory Diseases]]: Selenium supplementation reduces inflammatory markers in [[notes/oxidative_stress/Rheumatoid Arthritis|rheumatoid arthritis]], [[Asthma|asthma]], and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease|COPD]].
- [[notes/oxidative_stress/Neurodegeneration|Neurodegeneration]]: Selenoprotein P and GPx4 protect against ferroptosis in neurons; selenium deficiency increases susceptibility.
