# HSP27

**Heat shock protein 27 (HSP27)**, also known as HSPB1, is a small heat-shock protein (~27 kDa) that functions as an ATP-independent molecular chaperone and a key node of the senescence-associated secretory program.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

HSP27 exists as large oligomers that disassemble into monomers upon phosphorylation by [[MAPKAPK2|MK2]] (a direct substrate of [[p38 MAPK]]) at Ser15, Ser78, and Ser82. This phosphorylation is induced by stress, cytokines, and the senescence program. Phosphorylated HSP27 binds and stabilizes labile mRNAs — including SASP transcripts — and also modulates actin cytoskeleton dynamics, redox homeostasis, and [[Apoptosis]] (via interaction with cytochrome c and inhibition of caspase-3). It thereby couples stress signaling to protein-folding and transcript-stability control.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

HSP27 sits on the p38 MAPK–[[MAPKAPK2|MK2]]–HSP27 axis that is indispensable for a full SASP. MK2-phosphorylated HSP27 stabilizes [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], and other SASP mRNAs, sustaining the high cytokine/chemokine output of [[Senescent Cells]] (Freund et al., 2010, PMID 20357755). HSP27 is itself upregulated in senescence and serves as a senescence-associated marker. Through its anti-apoptotic activity it helps senescent cells resist immune clearance, promoting their persistence and chronic SASP.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

HSP27 overexpression is linked to chemotherapy resistance and poor prognosis in several cancers, and it is being explored as a biomarker and therapeutic target. In aging, the MK2–HSP27 axis is a candidate senomorphic node whose inhibition could blunt the SASP without eliminating senescent cells.
