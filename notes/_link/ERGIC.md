# ERGIC

## Overview

The [[ERGIC]] (ER–Golgi intermediate compartment) is a tubulovesicular membrane system situated between the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|endoplasmic reticulum]] and the Golgi apparatus. It serves as a sorting and trafficking hub for newly synthesized secretory and membrane proteins and, critically, as the platform where activated [[STING]] assembles its signaling machinery.

## Role as a STING Trafficking Hub

Upon binding of [[cGAMP]], ER-resident STING translocates via COPII-coated vesicles and ARF GTPases to the [[ERGIC]], where it oligomerizes and recruits TANK binding kinase 1 ([[TBK1]]). TBK1 phosphorylates STING and then [[IRF3]], driving nuclear translocation of IRF3 and expression of [[Type I Interferon|type I IFNs]] and [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISGs]]. STING-enriched ERGIC membranes also supply a source for LC3 lipidation, linking cGAS-STING activation to [[Autophagy]] induction.

## Interface with ER Stress and ER-Phagy

Because ERGIC originates from the ER, it sits at the interface of [[Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress|ER stress]] and autophagic clearance (ER-phagy). Organelle stress—including vesicular trafficking defects—can promote STING overactivation at the ERGIC independently of [[cGAS]], and impaired STING degradation at this compartment prolongs signaling. The ERGIC thus integrates both canonical DNA sensing and cellular stress inputs into cGAS-STING output.
