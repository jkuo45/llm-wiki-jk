# Endothelial Dysfunction

**Endothelial dysfunction** is a systemic pathological state of the vascular endothelium characterized by reduced bioavailability of [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]], impaired vasodilation, increased [[notes/_link/Inflammation|inflammatory]] adhesion molecule expression, and a pro-thrombotic phenotype. It is an early hallmark of [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]] and [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]].

## Mechanisms

- Reduced [[eNOS]] activity and [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] production
- Increased [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] from [[NADPH Oxidase]] and [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] ROS
- [[notes/_link/Inflammation|Inflammatory]] cytokine activation ([[TNF-α]], [[IL-1β]])
- Impaired [[notes/_link/Insulin signaling]]

## Clinical Assessment

Measured by flow-mediated dilation (FMD), [[Pulse Wave Velocity]], and circulating biomarkers ([[VCAM-1]], [[ICAM-1]], [[E-selectin]]).

# Endothelial Dysfunction
Endothelial dysfunction is a systemic pathological state of the endothelium and can be broadly defined as an imbalance between vasodilating and vasoconstricting substances. It is often characterized by reduced bioavailability of [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] and increased levels of [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]], serving as an early marker for [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]] and other types of [[Oxidative Stress]] related vascular diseases.

## eNOS Uncoupling: The Central Molecular Mechanism

The primary driver of endothelial dysfunction is reduced bioavailable [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] (NO), which occurs through multiple convergent mechanisms. The endothelial isoform of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] (eNOS, NOS3) normally dimerizes to catalyze the NADPH-dependent oxidation of [[L-arginine]] to citrulline and NO, requiring the cofactors [[tetrahydrobiopterin]] (BH4), FAD, and FMN. Under conditions of elevated [[oxidative_stress|oxidative stress]], BH4 is oxidized to dihydrobiopterin (BH2) by [[Peroxynitrite]], a process termed "BH4 deficiency." In the absence of sufficient BH4, eNOS undergoes "uncoupling" — the enzyme's electron transfer becomes uncoupled from L-arginine oxidation, and the oxygenase domain instead reduces molecular oxygen to [[Superoxide Radicals]] rather than producing NO. This converts eNOS from a protective NO-generating enzyme into a pro-oxidant source of [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]], creating a feed-forward loop: superoxide from uncoupled eNOS rapidly reacts with any remaining NO (diffusion-limited rate constant of ~6.7 × 10^9 M^−1·s^−1) to form [[Peroxynitrite]], which further oxidizes BH4 and perpetuates uncoupling.

## Endothelial ROS Sources Beyond eNOS

While uncoupled eNOS is a major source, endothelial dysfunction involves a network of interconnected [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]-producing systems. [[NADPH Oxidase]] (particularly the NOX1 and NOX4 isoforms in endothelial cells) generates superoxide in response to [[Angiotensin II]], [[TNF-alpha]], and [[shear stress|mechanical shear stress]]. [[Xanthine Oxidase]], which is highly expressed in the [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease|vascular endothelium]] and circulates bound to endothelial glycosaminoglycans, produces both [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] during purine catabolism. [[notes/_link/Mitochondria|Mitochondrial]] electron transport chain complexes I and III also contribute significantly; in the dysfunctional endothelium, elevated [[mitochondrial membrane potential]] favors superoxide production at complex III (the Q-cycle), particularly when [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide|NO]] levels are low and cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) is not NO-inhibited. The interplay between these sources is non-additive — [[Xanthine Oxidase|XO]]-derived superoxide activates ROS-sensitive signaling pathways that upregulate [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] expression, while [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]]-derived [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] can stabilize HIF-1α and induce [[Hypoxia|hypoxic]] gene programs that remodel endothelial function.

## Asymmetric Dimethylarginine (ADMA) and Endogenous NOS Inhibition

The endogenous NOS inhibitor [[ADMA|asymmetric dimethylarginine]] (ADMA) accumulates in [[oxidative_stress|oxidative stress]] states because ROS directly inhibit the enzyme [[DDAH|dimethylarginine dimethylaminohydrolase]] (DDAH), which degrades ADMA. DDAH contains a critical cysteine residue (Cys-249) in its active site that is susceptible to [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidation]] and [[S-Nitrosylation|S-nitrosylation]], rendering the enzyme inactive. Elevated ADMA competitively inhibits eNOS, promoting uncoupling and exacerbating endothelial dysfunction. Plasma ADMA levels are a robust independent predictor of [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular events]] and all-cause mortality in patients with [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis|coronary artery disease]], [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus|type 2 diabetes]], and [[Renal Failure|chronic kidney disease]].

## Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Markers

Endothelial dysfunction is most commonly quantified non-invasively by [[Flow-Mediated Dilation]] (FMD) of the brachial artery, a technique where reactive hyperemia-induced shear stress triggers NO-mediated vasodilation that is measured by ultrasound. Reduced FMD (<5–7% dilation) is an independent predictor of future [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular events]]. Circulating biomarkers include elevated levels of [[von Willebrand Factor]] (vWF), [[sICAM-1|soluble ICAM-1]], [[sVCAM-1|soluble VCAM-1]] (both products of [[NF-kappa B]] activation), [[E-Selectin]], and [[ADMA|ADMA]]. In the microcirculation, [[reactive hyperemia index]] (RHI) measured by peripheral arterial tonometry (PAT) provides an alternative assessment of small-vessel endothelial function.

## Therapeutic Interventions

Restoring endothelial function requires simultaneously enhancing NO bioavailability and reducing ROS production. [[notes/_link/Melatonin|Statins]] (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) improve eNOS expression and activity while reducing [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] expression, independent of lipid-lowering (pleiotropic effects). [[Angiotensin II|Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors]] reduce [[Angiotensin II]]-mediated [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] activation. [[tetrahydrobiopterin|BH4]] supplementation (sapropterin) has shown mixed results but supports eNOS recoupling. Lifestyle interventions including [[exercise|aerobic exercise]], [[Caloric Restriction]], and [[Mediterranean diet]] (rich in [[Flavonoids]] and [[Polyphenols]]) improve FMD through combined [[notes/_link/Nrf2|Nrf2]] activation, [[notes/_link/Antioxidants|antioxidant]] enzyme induction, and reduced [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] expression. [[notes/_link/Metformin]] improves endothelial function in [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus|diabetic]] patients via [[AMPK]]-mediated eNOS phosphorylation at Ser-1177 and reduced [[Mitochondrial ROS|mitochondrial ROS]].
