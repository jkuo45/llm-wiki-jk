# Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells

## Overview

Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) are somatic cells reprogrammed to a pluripotent state, capable of differentiating into any cell type. In neurodegeneration research, iPSCs — particularly patient-derived lines carrying disease-associated genotypes — are differentiated into human [[Neuron|neurons]], [[Microglia]], [[Astrocytes]], and motor neurons to model cell-autonomous mechanisms of [[Neurodegeneration]] in a human genetic context.

## Modeling cGAS-STING in Human Cells

iPSC-derived cultures provide a physiologically relevant platform to interrogate the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] outside the limitations of overexpression mouse models such as [[5xFAD]]. Notably, STING inhibition reduces cell death in TDP-43–treated, iPSC-derived motor neurons, demonstrating a cell-autonomous neuroprotective effect of pathway suppression. This human-cell evidence complements in vivo studies and supports the translational relevance of [[STING Inhibitors]] and [[cGAS Inhibitors]] for [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]] and other proteinopathies.

## Advantages and Limitations

iPSC models capture human-specific biology and genetic risk (e.g., [[APOE4]], [[TREM2]] R47H) that are underutilized in animal studies, while avoiding species differences in innate immunity. However, they lack the full multicellular CNS milieu and peripheral immune interactions, so findings require validation in intact systems. Nonetheless, they remain essential for dissecting neuronal versus glial contributions to [[Neuroinflammation]] and for screening degraders such as [[PROTAC]] approaches targeting cGAS/STING.
