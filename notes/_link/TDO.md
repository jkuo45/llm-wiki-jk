# TDO

**TDO** (tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase, also known as tryptophan pyrrolase) is a heme-containing enzyme that catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step of the [[Kynurenine Pathway]], converting [[Tryptophan]] to [[N-Formylkynurenine]]. It is functionally parallel to [[IDO1]] but exhibits distinct regulation, tissue distribution, and substrate specificity.

## Properties

- **Gene:** *TDO2* (human chromosome 4q32.1)
- **Structure:** Tetrameric heme protein (~48 kDa per subunit)
- **Tissue distribution:** Primarily hepatic (liver); also expressed in brain, placenta, and immune cells
- **Substrate specificity:** Selective for L-Trp (unlike [[IDO1]], which accepts multiple indoleamines)

## Regulation

- **Substrate regulation:** TDO is induced by its own substrate, [[Tryptophan]] — rising Trp levels increase TDO activity and expression.
- **Hormonal regulation:** Glucocorticoids (cortisol) induce TDO expression; this is the mechanism linking stress to altered Trp metabolism.
- **Not significantly induced by [[Interferon-gamma]]** — this distinguishes TDO from [[IDO1]].

## Clinical Relevance

TDO represents a compensatory route for Trp catabolism in tumors when [[IDO1]] is inhibited, contributing to resistance to [[Epacadostat]] and other IDO1-targeted therapies. TDO expression in [[Cancer]] correlates with immunosuppression and poor prognosis. TDO inhibitors (e.g., LM10) are in preclinical development for cancer immunotherapy.
