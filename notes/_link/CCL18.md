# CCL18 (PARC)

CCL18 (C-C motif chemokine ligand 18), also known as PARC (pulmonary and activation-regulated chemokine), DC-CK1, or MIP-4, is a CC chemokine constitutively produced by dendritic cells and alternatively activated macrophages. It is notable as a SASP component that, together with a small panel of secreted proteins, strongly predicts mortality and is used as a senescence biomarker (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptor**: CCL18 lacks a classical high-affinity GPCR; proposed receptors include PITPNM3 (NIR1) and CCRL2, and it may act partly via glycosaminoglycan presentation. Signaling is less well defined than for CCR-binding chemokines.
- **Cell sources**: Produced by plasmacytoid/dendritic cells and M2-like macrophages; in aging it appears in the secretome of senescent stromal and immune cells.
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Induced by IL-4/IL-13 (Th2/alternative activation) and, in senescence, by the broader SASP network rather than by a single master transcription factor; co-regulated with [[GDF15]], [[VEGF]]A, RAGE, and [[MMP-2]].
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Recruits naive T cells and immature dendritic cells and modulates dendritic-cell trafficking and T-cell polarization, contributing to immune modulation in aged tissue.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CCL18 is one of five SASP factors (with [[GDF15]], RAGE, [[VEGF]]A, and [[MMP-2]]) most strongly associated with human mortality, forming the basis of a circulating "senescence panel."
- As such it is a quantitative readout of senescent-cell burden and of [[Inflammaging]], rather than a direct driver of the inflammatory chemotactic wave.
- Its elevation in senescence may contribute to age-related immune dysfunction and to fibrotic remodeling (e.g., in IPF).

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CCL18 is a leading blood biomarker candidate for senescence burden and for predicting aging-related mortality.
- Elevated CCL18 tracks with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, atopic/allergic disease, and several cancers.
- It is being evaluated as an endpoint in senolytic/senomorphic trials to confirm reduction of the SASP.
