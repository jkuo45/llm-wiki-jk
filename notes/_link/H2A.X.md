# H2A.X

H2A.X is an H2A histone variant present in all eukaryotes that is distinguished from canonical H2A by a C-terminal tail containing a conserved Ser–Gln–Glu motif. In response to DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs), the serine (Ser139 in humans) is rapidly phosphorylated to generate γ-H2A.X, which acts as a recruitment platform for DNA repair and damage-signaling proteins. In the context of [[Senescence]], H2A.X/γ-H2A.X is a recognized hallmark of persistent DNA damage and is required for DNA-damage-induced senescence and [[SASP]], as described in the npj Aging review of the senescent epigenetic landscape (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Overview

H2A.X is ~95% identical to canonical H2A but carries the extended C-terminal tail. Phosphorylation of Ser139 is catalyzed mainly by the PI3K-like kinases ATM, ATR and DNA-PKcs at sites of DSBs, producing megabase-scale γ-H2A.X foci that visualize as nuclear puncta by immunofluorescence. Dephosphorylation by phosphatases (e.g., PP4C/PP2A) resolves foci once repair is complete.

## Structure and Function

The SQ(E/D)φ motif at the C-terminus is the sole distinction from H2A and is both necessary and sufficient for damage focus formation. γ-H2A.X recruits MDC1, which in turn recruits the MRN complex and ubiquitin ligases, amplifying the repair signal and promoting chromatin compaction around the break.

## Role in Senescence and SASP

H2A.X belongs to the H2A variant family most implicated in senescence. Alongside [[H2A.J]], it associates with sites of [[DNA Damage|DNA damage]] and contributes to SASP activation (Rodier et al., 2011, PMID 21138974; Contrepois et al., 2017, PMID 28368001). Key points:

- **Persistent damage signaling:** γ-H2A.X increases during aging in mouse and human tissues, largely as a consequence of persistent DNA damage and [[DNA-SCARS|DNA-SCARS]] as well as deprotected telomeres (Sedelnikova et al., 2004, PMID 14745497; Rodier et al., 2011).
- **Cytoplasmic chromatin fragments:** [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments|CCF]] — chromatin ejected into the cytoplasm that drives SASP via cGAS–STING — are enriched in γ-H2A.X (Ivanov et al., 2013, PMID 24000932).
- **Required for senescence/SASP:** Depletion of H2A.X impairs both senescence and SASP in human fibroblasts following irradiation (Rodier et al., 2011). γ-H2A.X also induces [[p53]] expression and activates senescence signaling pathways (Rogakou et al., 1998, PMID 9488723).

Thus DNA damage and H2A.X are established hallmarks of senescence, with γ-H2A.X linking the damage response to the secretory phenotype through CCF and NF-κB activation.

## Mechanistic Details

γ-H2A.X foci mark the persistent DNA-damage response (DDR) that sustains the SASP. Persistent DDR foci (including at telomeres) are upstream of inflammatory cytokine secretion; their resolution or deficiency changes the senescence outcome. H2A.X therefore bridges the "genomic instability" and "cellular senescence" hallmarks of aging.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

Because H2A.X/γ-H2A.X underlies damage-induced SASP, it is both a biomarker of senescence (persistent γ-H2A.X foci) and a node in DDR-targeted [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] strategies. Reducing persistent DDR signaling can blunt SASP without necessarily rescuing arrest, paralleling approaches targeting [[KDM4]], [[BRD4]] and [[DOT1L]].
