# Cachexia

## Overview

**Cachexia** is a complex metabolic syndrome characterized by involuntary loss of skeletal muscle and adipose tissue that cannot be fully reversed by nutritional support. It is driven by a catabolic state in which systemic [[Inflammation|inflammation]] (e.g., [[TNF-alpha|TNF-α]], [[IL-6]]) and neurohormonal signals suppress anabolism and accelerate protein degradation via the [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]] and [[FOXO]]–[[Atrogin-1]]/[[MuRF1]] axis—overlapping mechanistically with [[Muscle Atrophy]].

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Cachexia accompanies malignancies, chronic heart failure, COPD, renal failure, and advanced [[Sarcopenia|sarcopenia]]. Inflammatory cytokines activate [[NFκB|NF-κB]] and [[STAT1|STAT]] pathways, while [[mTORC1]] signaling is impaired, tipping net protein balance negative.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

Progressive weakness, fatigue, anorexia, and hypoalbuminemia. Biomarkers include elevated acute-phase proteins, reduced lean mass (DEXA/MRI), and upregulated muscle E3 ligases.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Management targets the underlying disease plus appetite/anabolic agents (e.g., ghrelin analogs, [[Selective Androgen Receptor Modulators|SARMs]]) and anti-inflammatory strategies. [[Urolithin A]] is under investigation for preserving muscle mass by balancing synthesis/degradation and improving [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] health.
