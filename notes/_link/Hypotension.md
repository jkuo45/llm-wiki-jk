# Hypotension

**Overview:** [[Hypotension]] is arterial blood pressure low enough to compromise organ perfusion. It ranges from benign orthostatic drops to life-threatening shock.

**Etiology and mechanisms:** Causes include hypovolemia (hemorrhage, dehydration), cardiogenic failure, and excessive [[Vasodilation]]. In distributive shock—[[Septic Shock]] and [[Vasoplegic Shock]]—massive [[Nitric Oxide]] release activates soluble [[Guanylate Cyclase]], raising cGMP and producing profound, catecholamine-refractory vasoplegia.

**Clinical presentation:** Symptoms reflect hypoperfusion: dizziness, syncope, oliguria, confusion, and, in shock, altered mental status and lactic acidosis. Measurement guides resuscitation targets (e.g., mean arterial pressure).

**Therapeutic landscape:** First-line therapy is volume resuscitation and [[Vasopressors]] (norepinephrine). Refractory vasoplegia unresponsive to pressors prompts rescue with [[Methylene blue]], which inhibits guanylate cyclase and blocks the NO→cGMP vasodilatory cascade, restoring vascular tone. Aminoguanidine (an iNOS inhibitor) has been studied similarly.
