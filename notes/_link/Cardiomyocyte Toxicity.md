# Cardiomyocyte Toxicity

**Cardiomyocyte toxicity** (cardiotoxicity) refers to damage or dysfunction of [[Cardiomyocyte|heart muscle cells]] caused by drugs, environmental toxins, or disease states. It is a major dose-limiting [[Adverse Effect]] of [[Chemotherapy]], particularly [[Anthracyclines]] ([[Doxorubicin]], [[Daunorubicin]]).

## Mechanisms

- **[[Oxidative Stress]]** — [[Doxorubicin]] undergoes redox cycling at [[Mitochondrial Complex I]], generating [[Superoxide]] and [[Hydroxyl radical]] that damage [[Cardiomyocyte]] [[DNA]], [[Proteins]], and [[Lipids]]
- **[[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]** — cardiotoxic agents impair [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]], opening the [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore]] (mPTP) and depleting [[ATP]]
- **[[Apoptosis]] and [[Autophagy]] Dysregulation** — [[Topoisomerase 2β]] (TOP2B) poisoning by anthracyclines activates [[p53]]-dependent apoptosis in cardiomyocytes
- **[[Calcium Overload]]** — disrupted [[Ca²⁺]] handling via [[SERCA]] and [[Ryanodine Receptor]] dysfunction
- **[[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] Activation** — cardiotoxic drugs trigger [[Interleukin 1β]]-mediated inflammatory damage

## Clinical Presentation

Cardiotoxicity can present as:
- Acute: [[Arrhythmia]], [[QT prolongation]], [[Myocarditis]]
- Chronic: [[Cardiomyopathy]], [[Heart Failure]] with reduced [[Ejection Fraction]] (HFrEF)
- Late-onset: developing years after chemotherapy completion

## Prevention and Management

- [[Dexrazoxane]] — iron chelator; cardioprotective agent for anthracycline-treated patients
- [[Liposomal Doxorubicin]] — alters tissue distribution to reduce cardiac accumulation
- [[ACE Inhibitors]] and [[Beta-Blockers]] — standard heart failure therapy for cardiotoxicity
- [[Statins]] — emerging cardioprotective agents via anti-inflammatory effects
- [[Exercise]] — improves cardiac reserve and reduces anthracycline cardiotoxicity
