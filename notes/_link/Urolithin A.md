# Urolithin A
**Urolithin A** is a postbiotic metabolite derived from ellagitannins (found in pomegranates and nuts) that has been shown to induce mitophagy and improve muscle function and longevity in various models.
## Mitohormetic Redox-Relay (MRR)
In the [[Mitohormetic Redox-Relay]] strategy, Urolithin A serves as the "cleanup" component. It ensures that the increased mitochondrial turnover triggered by the mitohormetic pulse (induced by [[Carbazochrome]]) results in the selective clearance of dysfunctional organelles through enhanced [[Autophagy|mitophagy]].
## Metabolic Pathway and Bioavailability
Urolithin A is produced from dietary [[Ellagitannins]] (found in Pomegranates, Raspberries, Walnuts, and Strawberries) via a multi-step biotransformation by the gut microbiota. Ellagitannins are hydrolyzed to [[Ellagic acid]] in the small intestine, which is then sequentially dehydroxylated by colonic bacteria (primarily [[Gordonibacter urolithinfaciens]] and [[Ellagibacter isourolithinifaciens]]) to form urolithin M5, urolithin D, urolithin C, and finally urolithin A. The conversion efficiency is highly variable between individuals due to differences in gut microbiome composition. Three distinct [[Metabotypes]] have been identified: **Metabotype A** (producing only urolithin A), **Metabotype B** (producing urolithin A plus isourolithin A and urolithin B), and **Metabotype 0** (non-producers, lacking the requisite bacteria). Approximately 40% of the population exhibits Metabotype A, 40% Metabotype B, and 20% Metabotype 0. Following absorption, urolithin A undergoes extensive phase II metabolism, forming glucuronide and sulfate conjugates with a plasma elimination half-life of approximately 8–12 hours. Tissue distribution studies in rodents demonstrate preferential accumulation in [[Skeletal Muscle]], Brain, and [[Adipose Tissue]], relevant to its proposed metabolic effects.
## Mechanism of Mitophagy Induction
Urolithin A is the most potent known natural inducer of [[Mitophagy]] — the selective autophagic clearance of dysfunctional mitochondria. The mechanism involves urolithin A-induced depolarization of the [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] (ΔΨm), which stabilizes [[PTEN-Induced Kinase 1]] ([[PINK1]]) on the outer mitochondrial membrane. PINK1 accumulation recruits the E3 ubiquitin ligase [[Parkin]] ([[PARK2]]), which ubiquitinates outer membrane proteins ([[MFN1]], [[MFN2]], [[VDAC1]]), targeting the mitochondrion for autophagic degradation by the [[LC3]]-[[p62]] machinery. Urolithin A also directly inhibits [[mTORC1]] signaling and activates [[AMP-Activated Protein Kinase]] (AMPK), both of which promote autophagy through [[ULK1]] activation. The EC₅₀ for mitophagy induction in primary myotubes is approximately 2–5 µM, which is achievable in human plasma following oral supplementation (500–1000 mg/day). Importantly, urolithin A selectively eliminates depolarized mitochondria without affecting healthy organelles, maintaining mitochondrial network integrity and preventing the bioenergetic collapse that accompanies non-selective mitochondrial elimination.
## Preclinical Evidence
In [[C. elegans]], urolithin A extends lifespan by approximately 20% and maintains motility during aging through a [[PINK1]]-dependent mechanism. In [[Mus musculus]], urolithin A (50 mg/kg/day) improves exercise capacity (running endurance by 42%), reduces age-associated muscle weakness, and prevents the accumulation of damaged mitochondria in skeletal muscle and heart. In [[Rattus norvegicus]] models of [[Aging]], urolithin A prevents [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] in the brain and improves cognitive performance in the Morris water maze. In cellular models of [[Parkinson's Disease]], urolithin A rescues [[PINK1]] mutant phenotypes and protects against [[MPTP]]-induced dopaminergic neuron loss. In models of [[Atherosclerosis]], urolithin A reduces macrophage foam cell formation and plaque size by enhancing mitophagic clearance of oxidized LDL-damaged mitochondria.
## Clinical Trials and Human Evidence
Phase I and II clinical trials in older adults have established the safety and tolerability of oral urolithin A (Mitopure™). A 28-day Phase I trial in sedentary older adults (60–90 years) demonstrated dose-proportional pharmacokinetics and target engagement, evidenced by reduced plasma [[Acylcarnitines]] (a biomarker of mitochondrial dysfunction) and increased mitochondrial gene expression in skeletal muscle biopsies. A 12-week Phase II trial in 60 older adults demonstrated significant improvements in muscle endurance (6-minute walk test) and reductions in [[TNFα]] and [[Interleukin-6]], consistent with anti-inflammatory and mitophagy-activating effects. A third trial showed improved grip strength and walking speed after 8 weeks of supplementation, with no serious adverse events. Ongoing trials are evaluating urolithin A in combination with exercise training and in the context of [[Sarcopenia]], [[Alzheimer's Disease]], and [[Cardiovascular Aging]].
## Connection to Mitochondrial Homeostasis and Adrenochrome Pathways
Urolithin A's mitophagy-activating mechanism positions it as a complementary agent to mitohormetic strategies that enhance mitochondrial biogenesis (e.g., [[Carbazochrome]] via the [[Mitohormetic Redox-Relay]]). While urolithin A clears damaged mitochondria, [[Carbazochrome]] and [[Adrenochrome]] at ultralow doses stimulate the production of new mitochondria through [[PGC1-α]] activation. This conceptual framework suggests a two-pronged approach: mitophagy induction (urolithin A) coupled with mitochondrial biogenesis stimulation ([[Carbazochrome]]/[[Adrenochrome]]) could synergistically rejuvenate the mitochondrial network. Urolithin A also attenuates mitochondrial ROS production, which could theoretically buffer against the pro-oxidant side effects of the [[Mitohormetic Redox-Relay]] protocol, providing a built-in safety mechanism against excessive oxidative stress. Conversely, the protonophoric activity of urolithin A may modestly depolarize mitochondria, and the combined effect with mild uncouplers (e.g., [[Dinitrophenol]] or [[BAM15]]) warrants investigation for additive mitophagic efficacy.
