# mitoCPR

**mitoCPR** (mitochondrial compromised protein import response) is a conserved surveillance and quality-control pathway, first characterized in budding yeast, that is triggered when the import of nuclear-encoded proteins into mitochondria becomes compromised. Because ~99% of mitochondrial proteins are synthesized in the cytosol and imported, a stall at the outer-membrane translocon threatens organellar and cellular proteostasis.

## Mechanism

- **Sensing:** import stress and accumulation of mitochondrial precursor proteins activate the transcription factor [[PDR3]].
- **Recruitment:** PDR3 induces expression of **[[Cis1]]**, which docks at the [[TOM complex|TOM]] translocon (via Tom70) and recruits the AAA-ATPase **[[Msp1]]** (the yeast orthologue of mammalian [[ATAD1]]).
- **Extraction & degradation:** Msp1 extracts stalled/clogging precursor proteins from the import channel, delivering them to the [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System|ubiquitin–proteasome system]] for degradation.
- **Upregulation of the [[Proteasome]]** enhances clearance capacity during the stress episode.

## Physiological Importance

> [!info] Mitohormesis and quality control
> mitoCPR is one of several import- and translation-linked surveillance systems that protect against **[[mPOS]]** (mitochondrial precursor over-accumulation stress). By clearing clogged translocons it preserves [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] and prevents proteotoxic collapse — a facet of adaptive [[Mitohormesis]] and mitochondrial [[Proteostasis]].

## Related Pathways

- [[mitoTAD]] — constitutive clearance of stalled precursors at translocons.
- [[mPOS]] — precursor over-accumulation stress.
- [[MitoStores]] — storage depots for mislocalized precursors.
- [[mitoRQC]] — ribosome-associated quality control.
- [[UPRam]] — unfolded protein response activated by mistargeting.
