# Naked Mole Rat

## Overview

The naked mole rat (*Heterocephalus glaber*) is an extraordinarily long-lived, eusocial subterranean rodent that displays remarkable resistance to cancer and age-related disease. It has become a premier comparative model for studying [[Senescence]], genomic stability, and innate immunity. Intriguingly, naked mole rats maintain high levels of genomic instability yet exhibit muted inflammatory signaling, including restrained [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] activity — a phenotype that illuminates mechanisms of [[Neuroinflammation]] restraint and resilience.

## Muted cGAS-STING as a Longevity Adaptation

Unlike human cGAS, which is anchored to [[Nucleosome|nucleosomes]] and restrained to prevent aberrant activation, naked mole rat cGAS exhibits prolonged retention on [[Chromatin]] and enhances [[DNA Repair|DNA damage repair]]. This suggests an evolutionary rewiring in which nuclear cGAS is co-opted toward genome maintenance rather than inflammatory signaling. The resulting dampening of chronic [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] and [[Inflammation]] may protect long-lived tissues from the collateral damage of sustained innate immune activation, a theme relevant to [[Neurodegeneration]] and brain aging.

## Relevance as a Model

The naked mole rat offers a natural experiment in cGAS-STING restraint: its ability to tolerate genomic instability without triggering destructive neuroinflammation contrasts sharply with the hyperactive cGAS-STING observed in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]]. Dissecting the molecular basis of its muted response — including differences in cGAS chromatin interactions and downstream [[STING]] tuning — may reveal strategies to recapitulate resilience pharmacologically, akin to the protective [[APOE3 Christchurch]] allele.
