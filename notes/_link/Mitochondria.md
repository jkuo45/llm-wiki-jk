# Mitochondria
Mitochondria are the primary energy-producing organelles in eukaryotic cells, which are selectively degraded via [[Mitophagy]] to maintain cellular health and prevent oxidative stress.
## Definition
Mitochondria are double-membrane-bound organelles found in most eukaryotic organisms. They are often called the "powerhouses of the cell" due to their role in producing ATP, but they are also the primary source and target of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS).
## Role in Oxidative Stress
- **Primary Source:** Superoxide is produced as a byproduct of the electron transport chain, specifically through electron leakage at **Complex I** and **Complex III**.
- **Primary Target:** High ROS production within mitochondria can damage mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA), proteins, and membranes, leading to mitochondrial dysfunction.
- **Sensor for Death:** Damage to mitochondria is the central trigger for the intrinsic pathway of [[Apoptosis]].
## Antioxidant Defense
Mitochondria possess their own defense systems, including:
- **SOD2 (Mn-SOD):** Specifically neutralizes superoxide within the mitochondrial matrix.
- **Glutathione System:** Mitochondria maintain a high concentration of [[Glutathione]] for local peroxide neutralization.
