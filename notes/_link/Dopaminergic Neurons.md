# Dopaminergic Neurons
**Dopaminergic Neurons** are the primary source of the neurotransmitter [[Dopamine]] in the mammalian central nervous system. They play a critical role in the control of metabolic processes, executive functions, and motor control.
## Distribution and Function
- **[[Substantia Nigra]]:** A brain region rich in dopaminergic neurons, specifically in the substantia nigra pars compacta.
- **Function:** Essential for motor control, reward-motivated behavior, and executive functions.
## Role in Disease
- **[[Parkinson's Disease]]:** Characterized by the progressive and selective loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra.
- **[[Neuromelanin]] and Oxidative Stress:** Synthesis of neuromelanin in these neurons involves [[Aminochromes]] as intermediates, such as [[Dopaminochrome]] (formed from the oxidation of [[Dopamine]]). These processes can contribute to oxidative stress if dysregulated.
## Research and Regenerative Medicine
- **[[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs):** Used to generate functional dopaminergic neurons for transplantation and disease modeling.
- **[[Induced Neural Stem Cells]] (iNSCs):** Another source for generating dopaminergic neurons through direct reprogramming.
