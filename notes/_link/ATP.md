# ATP

**Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)** is a nucleotide composed of adenine, ribose, and three phosphate groups that serves as the universal energy currency of living cells. Hydrolysis of ATP to [[ADP]] and [[Pi]] (or [[AMP]] and [[PPi]]) releases [[Gibbs free energy|free energy]] used to drive endergonic biochemical reactions.

## Structure

ATP consists of:

- **[[Adenine]]** — a purine nitrogenous base
- **[[Ribose]]** — a pentose sugar (β-D-ribofuranose)
- **Triphosphate moiety** — three phosphate groups (α, β, γ) linked by phosphoanhydride bonds

The γ-phosphate is the high-energy phosphate most commonly hydrolyzed. The phosphoanhydride bonds between phosphate groups have a high [[ΔG°′]] of approximately −30.5 kJ/mol under standard conditions.

## Production

ATP is generated by three major pathways:

### Glycolysis
[[Glycolysis]] produces 2 ATP per [[Glucose]] molecule via substrate-level phosphorylation ([[Hexokinase]], [[Phosphofructokinase-1]], [[Pyruvate Kinase]]). This occurs in the [[Cytosol]] and does not require [[Oxygen]].

### Oxidative Phosphorylation
The [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] [[Electron Transport Chain]] ([[Complex I]]–[[Complex IV]]) creates a [[Proton Gradient]] across the [[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane]]. [[ATP Synthase]] ([[Complex V]]) uses the proton motive force to generate approximately 32–34 ATP per glucose via chemiosmosis. This is the dominant pathway under aerobic conditions.

### Substrate-Level Phosphorylation
The [[TCA Cycle]] (Krebs cycle) produces 1 [[GTP]] per turn (equivalent to 1 ATP) via [[Succinyl-CoA Synthetase]].

## Cellular Roles

ATP powers nearly every energy-requiring process in the cell:

- **[[Active Transport]]** — [[Sodium Potassium ATPase]], [[Ca²⁺ ATPase]], [[V-ATPase]], [[ABC transporters]]
- **[[Protein Synthesis]]** — ~4 ATP equivalents per peptide bond (aminoacyl-tRNA formation + ribosome translocation)
- **[[DNA Replication]] and [[Transcription]]** — dNTP incorporation and RNA polymerization
- **[[Cytoskeletal Dynamics]]** — [[Actin]] polymerization, [[Myosin]]-[[Actin]] sliding in [[Muscle Contraction]]
- **[[Signal Transduction]]** — ATP-dependent kinases ([[PKA]], [[PKC]], [[MAPK]]), [[Adenylyl Cyclase]] for [[cAMP]] production
- **[[Autophagy]]** — [[V-ATPase]] maintains [[Lysosome]] acidification; [[ULK1]] and [[AMPK]] are ATP-sensing kinases
- **[[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]]** — ubiquitin activation requires ATP

## Regulation

Cellular ATP concentration is typically maintained at 2–10 mM. The [[ATP]]/[[ADP]] and [[ATP]]/[[AMP]] ratios are tightly regulated. [[AMPK]] (AMP-activated protein kinase) serves as the primary cellular energy sensor — it is activated by rising AMP and falling ATP, triggering catabolic pathways and suppressing anabolic processes.

## Relevance to Aging and Disease

[[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] with age impairs ATP production, contributing to [[Sarcopenia]], [[Neurodegeneration]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome]]. [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Exercise]] enhance mitochondrial efficiency and ATP synthesis. Many [[Geroprotectors]] — including [[Metformin]], [[Rapamycin]], and [[NAD+ precursors]] — act partly by improving cellular energy homeostasis.
