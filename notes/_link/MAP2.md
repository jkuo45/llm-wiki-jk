## Overview

[[MAP2]] (microtubule-associated protein 2) is a high-molecular-weight protein abundant in neuronal dendrites. It binds and stabilizes microtubules, regulating cytoskeletal architecture, dendrite outgrowth, and synaptic plasticity.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

MAP2 contains a microtubule-binding domain and a projection domain that cross-links microtubules and interacts with other cytoskeletal elements. It is regulated by phosphorylation: kinases such as [[GSK-3β]] and [[CDK5]] phosphorylate MAP2, modulating its binding affinity and thereby dendritic stability. MAP2 isoforms (a, b, c) differ in expression across development and neuronal compartments, serving as a specific dendritic marker distinct from axonal MAPs (e.g., tau).

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

Loss or abnormal phosphorylation of MAP2 disrupts dendritic integrity and is associated with neurodegeneration, traumatic brain injury, and excitotoxicity. MAP2 immunostaining is widely used to assess neuronal/ dendrite preservation. In the wiki, MAP2 is employed as a neuronal integrity marker to evaluate neuroprotective or neurotoxic effects of adrenochrome/sirtuin interventions.
