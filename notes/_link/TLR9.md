# TLR9

## Overview

TLR9 (Toll-Like Receptor 9) is an endosomal Toll-like receptor that senses unmethylated CpG-rich DNA within the lumen of endosomes and lysosomes. Unlike the cytosolic DNA sensor [[cGAS]], TLR9 is confined to the endolysosomal compartment, where it detects pathogen-derived DNA and, in some contexts, endogenous self-DNA delivered to these vesicles. TLR9 activation triggers [[NF-kappaB]] and interferon regulatory factor signaling, linking endosomal DNA detection to [[Inflammation]] and [[Type I Interferon|type I IFN]] responses.

## TLR9 and cGAS Crosstalk

A notable feature of TLR9 is its capacity to regulate cGAS expression. In the retinal pigment epithelium, TLR9 signaling upregulates [[cGAS]], illustrating a positive feed-forward relationship between an endosomal sensor and the cytosolic DNA-sensing pathway. This crosstalk positions TLR9 as an upstream modulator of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], shaping the magnitude of downstream innate immune activation. Such compartmentalized sensing — endosomal versus cytosolic/nuclear — allows cells to integrate DNA signals from distinct subcellular sources.

## Context-Dependent Roles in the CNS

The function of TLR9 in the brain is strikingly context dependent. In the [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]] CNS, TLR9 is reported to be downregulated, an association interpreted as potentially protective, since excessive endosomal DNA sensing could amplify [[Neuroinflammation]]. This contrasts with its upregulation-driven effects in other tissues. The divergent roles of TLR9 across cell types and disease states echo the broader theme that DNA-sensing pathways, including those centered on [[cGAS]] and [[STING]], exert both deleterious and homeostatic effects depending on cellular context within the [[Central Nervous System]].
