# MMP-12

MMP-12 (Matrix metalloproteinase-12), also known as macrophage metalloelastase (MME), is a zinc-dependent endopeptidase of the matrix metalloproteinase family. It is the most potent elastinolytic MMP, secreted predominantly by tissue macrophages. MMP-12 plays critical roles in extracellular matrix (ECM) remodelling, inflammation resolution, and tissue homeostasis. Its dysregulation is implicated in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), atherosclerosis, skin ageing, and several other pathological conditions.

## Gene & Protein Structure

- **Gene:** *MMP12* is located on chromosome 11q22.2, within a cluster of MMP genes. It spans approximately 12.2 kb and contains 10 exons.
- **Protein:** The mature enzyme is a 54 kDa secreted glycoprotein. Like most MMPs, it is synthesised as an inactive zymogen (pro-MMP-12, ~56 kDa) that requires proteolytic cleavage for activation.
- **Domains:** MMP-12 comprises three functional domains:
  - **Pro-domain:** Maintains latency via a cysteine-switch mechanism (Cys residue coordinates the catalytic zinc ion).
  - **Catalytic domain:** Contains the conserved HExxHxxGxxH zinc-binding motif essential for proteolytic activity.
  - **Hemopexin-like C-terminal domain:** A four-bladed β-propeller structure involved in substrate specificity, protein-protein interactions, and TIMP binding. Crystal structures (e.g., PDB: 3BA0) reveal that the hemopexin and catalytic domains exhibit significant conformational freedom relative to each other, suggesting an active role for the hemopexin domain in substrate recognition.
- **Activation:** Pro-MMP-12 is activated extracellularly by serine proteases such as [[Plasmin]], as well as by other MMPs (e.g., MMP-3, MMP-7). Membrane-type MMPs can also participate in activation at the cell surface.

## Substrates & Catalytic Activity

MMP-12 has broad substrate specificity with particular potency against elastic fibres:

- **Elastin:** MMP-12 is the most efficient MMP for degrading insoluble elastin, the primary structural protein of elastic fibres. This unique capability underlies its central role in pathologies involving elastic tissue destruction.
- **Other ECM components:** Collagen IV, fibronectin, laminin, proteoglycans, and type I/III collagen (at lower efficiency).
- **Bioactive molecules:** MMP-12 processes and inactivates ELR+ CXC chemokines (CXCL1, -2, -3, -5, -8) by cleaving at the critical ELR receptor-binding motif. It also inactivates monocyte chemotactic proteins CCL2, -7, -8, and -13, generating CCR antagonists. This chemokine-processing activity is central to its role in inflammation resolution.
- **Other substrates:** Cleaves plasminogen to generate angiostatin (an endogenous anti-angiogenic fragment), and can process collagen XVIII to generate endostatin.

> [!important] Unique Elastinolytic Capacity
> MMP-12 is the only MMP that can effectively degrade insoluble elastin at physiological concentrations, making it the principal elastase in macrophage-mediated tissue remodelling.

## Cellular Sources & Regulation

- **Primary source:** Tissue macrophages, particularly M2-polarized (alternatively activated) macrophages involved in tissue remodelling and inflammation resolution.
- **Other sources:** Monocytes (upon differentiation to tissue macrophages), osteoclasts, and some tumour cells. Unlike most MMPs, MMP-12 is not expressed by immature monocytes; expression is acquired upon differentiation to tissue macrophages.
- **Inducers:** Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), TNF-α, IL-1β, phorbol esters, and oxidative stress upregulate MMP-12 expression.
- **Transcriptional regulation:** Controlled by AP-1, NF-κB, and SP-1 transcription factor binding sites in the promoter region. MicroRNAs (e.g., miR-125b, miR-199a) can post-transcriptionally silence MMP-12.
- **Inhibition:** Endogenous inhibitors include TIMP-1 (tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase-1) and TIMP-2. The balance between MMP-12 and TIMPs determines net elastinolytic activity in tissues.

## Physiological Functions

### ECM Remodelling & Wound Healing
MMP-12 participates in normal tissue remodelling during wound repair, facilitating macrophage migration through ECM barriers by degrading elastin and other structural components. It is essential for restoring tissue architecture following injury.

### Inflammation Resolution
MMP-12 acts as a molecular switch in acute inflammation. By cleaving ELR+ CXC chemokines at the receptor-binding motif, it terminates neutrophil (PMN) recruitment. Simultaneously, it generates CCR antagonists from CC chemokines, dampening monocyte recruitment. MMP-12-deficient mice show significantly increased PMN and macrophage infiltration at 72 hours post-LPS challenge, demonstrating its critical role in resolving acute inflammatory responses.

### Angiostatin Generation
MMP-12 cleaves plasminogen to generate angiostatin, an endogenous inhibitor of angiogenesis. This positions MMP-12 as a negative regulator of pathological neovascularization in certain contexts.

## Role in SASP

MMP-12 is a recognised component of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), particularly relevant to skin ageing and tissue deterioration:

- **ECM degradation:** Secreted by senescent dermal fibroblasts, MMP-12 degrades elastin and other ECM components, contributing to the structural deterioration characteristic of aged skin.
- **Pro-ageing SASP protease:** MMP-12 collaborates with other SASP proteases ([[MMP1]], [[MMP-3]], [[MMP-9]]) to amplify ECM breakdown beyond what any single MMP achieves alone.
- **Paracrine senescence:** By degrading the ECM, MMP-12 disrupts fibroblast-ECM mechanosignalling, promoting a feed-forward cycle of TGF-β pathway downregulation, further ECM loss, and propagation of the aged fibroblast phenotype to neighbouring cells.
- **Inflammaging:** MMP-12-mediated chemokine processing may contribute to the chronic, low-grade inflammation (inflammaging) characteristic of ageing tissues.

> [!tip] Therapeutic Target
> MMP-12 may be a promising target for anti-ageing interventions. Senolytic strategies that clear senescent cells, or direct MMP-12 inhibitors, could potentially slow age-related ECM deterioration in skin and other tissues.

## Pathological Roles

### Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) & Emphysema
MMP-12 plays a central role in cigarette smoke-induced emphysema. MMP-12-deficient mice are protected from emphysema development following chronic cigarette smoke exposure. MMP-12 degrades alveolar elastin, disrupting the protease-antiprotease balance (overwhelming alpha-1 antitrypsin capacity) and driving airspace enlargement. Polymorphisms in the *MMP12* promoter are associated with lung function decline and COPD susceptibility.

### Atherosclerosis
MMP-12 is highly expressed in atherosclerotic plaques, particularly in macrophage-rich regions. It degrades elastin in the arterial wall, contributing to:
- Vessel wall remodelling and weakening
- Plaque instability and rupture risk
- Foam cell formation and lipid core expansion
- Arterial stiffness (via elastin degradation)
MMP-12 genotyping has been linked to coronary artery luminal dimensions in diabetic patients with coronary artery disease.

### Skin Ageing
MMP-12 is a key mediator of both intrinsic and extrinsic skin ageing:
- UV irradiation and ROS upregulate MMP-12 in dermal fibroblasts and macrophages
- Elastin fragmentation generates bioactive elastokines (Elastin-derived peptides, EDPs) that bind the elastin-binding protein (EBP) receptor, further amplifying MMP expression
- Accumulation of MMP-12-expressing senescent fibroblasts correlates with wrinkle formation and loss of skin elasticity
- MMP-12 is overexpressed in granulomatous skin diseases (sarcoidosis, granuloma annulare) in areas of elastic fibre degradation

### Cancer
MMP-12 has dual and context-dependent roles in cancer:
- **Pro-tumorigenic:** Facilitates tumour invasion and metastasis by degrading ECM barriers; promotes angiogenesis via chemokine processing; upregulated in tumour-associated macrophages.
- **Anti-tumorigenic:** Generates angiostatin and endostatin, which inhibit tumour angiogenesis. Overexpression of MMP-12 in tumour models has shown tumour regression through angiostatin generation.

### Neurological Diseases
MMP-12 is implicated in blood-brain barrier (BBB) damage following focal cerebral ischaemia. MMP-12-deficient mice show attenuated brain damage after ischaemic stroke, and specific knockdown of MMP-12 offers neuroprotection. The interplay between MMP-12 and tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA) contributes to post-stroke pathology.

### Kidney Disease
MMP-12 acts as a major factor for glomerular injury in anti-glomerular basement membrane nephritis, contributing to renal tissue destruction.

### Retinal Disease
MMP-12 contributes to pathological retinal angiogenesis in oxygen-induced retinopathy. MMP-12-deficient mice and mice treated with MMP-408 (a pharmacological MMP-12 inhibitor) show reduced macrophage infiltration, decreased vascular leakage, and attenuated pathological neovascularization.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Biomarker:** Serum and tissue MMP-12 levels serve as biomarkers for COPD severity, atherosclerotic burden, and skin ageing.
- **Therapeutic target:** MMP-12 inhibitors are under development, though historical challenges with MMP inhibitor clinical trials (lack of selectivity, musculoskeletal side effects) necessitate next-generation selective inhibitors.
- **RNA interference:** MMP-12 silencing by siRNA or shRNA is being explored as a therapeutic strategy for COPD and other inflammatory lung diseases, with lung-specific delivery systems under investigation.
- **Genetic associations:** *MMP12* promoter polymorphisms (e.g., -1082 A/G) are associated with COPD risk, lung function trajectories, and cardiovascular disease susceptibility.
