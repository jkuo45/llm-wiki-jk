# 5xFAD

## Overview

5xFAD (5XFAD) is a widely used transgenic mouse model of [[Alzheimer's Disease]] that co-expresses five familial AD mutations in the amyloid precursor protein (APP) and presenilin 1 (PSEN1) transgenes, driving aggressive, early-onset [[Amyloid Beta]] plaque deposition. Although it captures amyloid pathology more faithfully than tau or other features of human disease, 5xFAD exhibits accelerated phenotypes that do not fully recapitulate the slow temporal progression of late-onset human AD.

## Utility in cGAS-STING Research

In the cGAS-STING framework, 5xFAD mice have been instrumental in demonstrating that microglial [[cGAS]] deletion increases recruitment of [[Microglia]] to amyloid plaques and enhances Aβ clearance while simultaneously suppressing [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling. This established the key principle that cGAS-STING inhibition can uncouple neurotoxic inflammation from beneficial phagocytosis. The model thus provides a controlled system to test [[cGAS Inhibitors]] and [[STING Inhibitors]] and to dissect how [[Astrocytes]] and microglia states shift upon pathway suppression.

## Caveats and Limitations

Because 5xFAD overexpressed transgenes produce early and severe pathology, findings must be validated in more physiologically and genetically relevant systems — particularly those incorporating human risk alleles such as [[APOE4]] and [[TREM2]] R47H. Nevertheless, 5xFAD remains a cornerstone for probing the maladaptive innate immune programs downstream of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] in amyloid-driven [[Neurodegeneration]].
