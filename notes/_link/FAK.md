# FAK

## Overview
[[FAK]] (focal adhesion kinase, PTK2) is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase enriched at focal adhesions, where it integrates signals from integrins and growth factor receptors to regulate cell adhesion, migration, proliferation, and survival. FAK is recruited by phosphorylated [[Caspase-8]] as part of the non-canonical, pro-migratory signaling complex that replaces the apoptotic function of Caspase-8.

## Structure & Activation
FAK contains an N-terminal FERM domain (membrane/receptor binding), a central kinase domain, and a C-terminal focal adhesion targeting (FAT) domain. FAK is activated by autophosphorylation at Tyr397 upon integrin clustering, creating a binding site for Src family kinases, which then phosphorylate additional tyrosine residues (Tyr576, Tyr577, Tyr861) for full activation.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Caspase-8 Scaffolding Complex**: When [[Caspase-8]] is phosphorylated by [[Src]] at Tyr380, it loses its apoptotic function and instead acts as a scaffold that recruits FAK, Calpain-2, and [[PI3K]]. This complex promotes:
  - Cell migration and invasion (via FAK → Rac1 → actin remodeling)
  - Focal adhesion dynamics
  - Survival signaling ([[NFKB]], [[mTORC1]])

> [!info] FAK in the Non-Canonical Caspase-8 Complex
> The recruitment of FAK by phosphorylated Caspase-8 represents a gain-of-function switch that couples loss of apoptotic sensitivity with enhanced migratory capacity. This mechanism is particularly relevant in aggressive cancers (colon, glioblastoma) where Caspase-8 phosphorylation is prevalent.

- **Growth Signaling**: FAK activates PI3K/Akt and RAS/ERK pathways to promote proliferation.
- **Survival**: FAK suppresses anoikis (detachment-induced apoptosis) by activating Akt and inhibiting pro-apoptotic signals.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer**: FAK is overexpressed and hyperactivated in many cancers, driving invasion, metastasis, and drug resistance. Its recruitment by phosphorylated Caspase-8 links apoptotic resistance to enhanced tumor motility.
- **Therapeutic Targeting**: FAK inhibitors (e.g., defactinib, GSK2256098) are in clinical trials for mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, and other solid tumors.
