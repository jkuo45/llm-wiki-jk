# Cyclic Dinucleotide

## Overview

[[Cyclic Dinucleotide|Cyclic dinucleotides (CDNs)]] are small cyclic nucleotide signals that include the endogenous second messenger 2′3′-cyclic GMP-AMP ([[cGAMP]]) synthesized by [[cGAS]] from ATP and GTP, as well as structurally related exogenous CDNs produced by bacteria. CDNs are the direct ligands of [[STING]] and constitute the pivotal link between DNA sensing and downstream innate immune activation.

## CDNs as STING Agonists

Endogenous 2′3′-cGAMP binds STING in the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]], triggering its translocation to the [[ERGIC]] and recruitment of [[TBK1]] and [[IRF3]], culminating in [[Type I Interferon|type I IFN]] and [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISG]] expression. Exogenous CDNs—such as bacterial c-di-GMP and c-di-AMP—likewise engage STING as potent agonists. Because CDNs can be transferred between cells via [[Gap Junction|gap junctions]], [[PANX1]] channels, and [[Extracellular Vesicles|extracellular vesicles]], they act as intercellular alarm signals that spread cGAS-STING activation.

## Therapeutic and Vaccine Applications

Synthetic CDNs are exploited as vaccine adjuvants and as STING-activating therapeutics to boost antitumor and antiviral immunity. Conversely, dysregulated CDN accumulation—whether from excessive [[cGAS]] activity or impaired degradation—drives chronic [[Inflammation]] and [[Neuroinflammation]], making CDN sensing a double-edged target in both infectious and neurodegenerative disease.
