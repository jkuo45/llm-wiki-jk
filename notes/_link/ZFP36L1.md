# ZFP36L1

ZFP36L1 (Zinc finger protein 36 C3H1-like 1; also BRF1) is a member of the tristetraprolin (TTP/TIS11) family of RNA-binding proteins. It binds AU-rich elements (AREs) in the 3' UTRs of labile mRNAs and recruits the deadenylase complex to trigger decay. In senescent cells, ZFP36L1 is phosphorylated and inhibited by [[MAPKAPK2|MK2]], stabilizing transcripts of many SASP factors — including [[IL-1α]] — and sustaining the secretory program.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

ZFP36L1 contains two C3H1 zinc fingers that recognize the consensus ARE (UUAUUUAUU). Upon binding, it recruits CCR4–NOT and PARN deadenylases and the exosome, promoting mRNA shortening and degradation. Its activity is controlled post-translationally: the p38 MAPK–[[MAPKAPK2|MK2]] axis phosphorylates ZFP36L1 (and the related ZFP36/TTP), impairing its interaction with the decay machinery and with 14-3-3 scaffolding proteins, thereby relieving ARE-mRNA decay. This couples environmental/DNA-damage stress signaling directly to transcript half-life. ZFP36L1 can also reinforce a negative feedback loop by destabilizing MK2 pathway components, tuning the response.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

ZFP36L1 is a negative regulator of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] under basal conditions, keeping cytokine transcripts short-lived. Upon senescence induction, MK2-mediated inactivation removes this brake, stabilizing ARE-containing SASP mRNAs (IL-1α, IL-6, IL-8, GM-CSF). The p38–MK2–ZFP36L1 axis is therefore a central post-transcriptional control point that converts chronic stress signaling into durable SASP output, operating in parallel with the [[NF-κB]] transcriptional module. Overexpression of ZFP36L1 dampens SASP intensity, whereas its loss amplifies secretion — a handle for senomorphic manipulation.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- ZFP36L1/TTP-family members regulate inflammation in arthritis, cancer, and cardiovascular disease.
- Modulating ARE-mRNA stability (e.g., MK2 inhibitors) is a strategy to suppress SASP-driven inflammation and [[Inflammaging]].
- TTP-family proteins are implicated in mRNA destabilization of oncogenic and immune transcripts, with context-dependent tumor suppressor or promoter roles.
