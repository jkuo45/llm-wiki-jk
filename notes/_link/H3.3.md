# H3.3

H3.3 is a conserved replication-independent variant of canonical histone H3 (H3.1/H3.2). It differs from H3.1 and H3.2 by only four or five amino acids, yet these subtle sequence differences are routed through distinct chaperone pathways and confer distinct genomic localization. In aging and [[Senescence]], H3.3 is of particular interest because it accumulates in post-mitotic cells and is enriched at actively transcribed and regulatory regions, as reviewed in the npj Aging survey of the senescent epigenetic landscape (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Overview

Canonical H3.1/H3.2 are synthesized during S phase and deposited behind the replication fork by CAF-1. By contrast, H3.3 is expressed throughout the cell cycle and deposited in a replication-independent manner by dedicated chaperones — principally [[HIRA]] at gene bodies/enhancers and [[ASF1a]]/[[UBN1]] in the HIRA complex, with ATRX/DAXX handling centromeric/pericentromeric deposition. Because senescent and differentiated cells have exited the cell cycle, replication-coupled deposition falls and H3.3 progressively replaces canonical H3.

## Accumulation in Aging and Senescence

Multiple studies show that H3.3 levels rise with age in post-mitotic cells and that the H3.3-to-total-H3 ratio increases in senescent cells (Tvardovskiy et al., 2017, PMID 28977489; Rai et al., 2014, PMID 25401360). H3.3 accumulation is associated with profound remodeling of the histone methylation landscape with age. In senescent cells, both full-length H3.3 and a proteolytically processed form (H3.3cs1) are increased across several senescent cell types (Duarte et al., 2014, PMID 25319811).

## Role in Senescence and SASP

H3.3 is functionally embedded in the senescent chromatin program in several ways:

- **SAHF and chromatin dynamics:** Its chaperone [[HIRA]] is required for [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]] formation and for maintaining chromatin dynamics in senescence (Rai et al., 2014).
- **Clipping-driven senescence:** The lysosomal protease [[Cathepsin L1]] cleaves the H3.3 N-terminal tail, removing post-translational modifications that control cell-cycle regulators. Ectopic expression of H3.3 or of H3.3cs1 can trigger senescence (Duarte et al., 2014).
- **BRD4 protection:** [[BRD4]] normally protects against H3.3 clipping at senescence-related loci during hematopoiesis. Loss of BRD4 unleashes clipping, which is associated with emergence of [[H3K27ac]], [[H3K122ac]] and [[H3K4me3]] marks, enhanced chromatin accessibility, and transcription of senescence-specific genes including [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] components (Yang et al., 2023, PMID 36800343).

## Mechanistic Details

Because H3.3 has a long half-life (months), it is unusually vulnerable to cumulative, stochastic non-enzymatic damage over time (carbonylation, glycation), which may impair nucleosome function and contribute to epigenetic dysregulation in aging. Its deposition by HIRA at active regulatory elements links the variant to both the repressive (SAHF) and activating (SASP enhancer) arms of senescent chromatin.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

The H3.3–BRD4–clipping axis positions H3.3 as a node connecting chromatin variants to SASP. Because the variant accrues damage with age, it is a candidate biomarker and potential target in [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] strategies that aim to preserve beneficial arrest while damping damaging secretion (see also [[KDM4]], [[DOT1L]]).
