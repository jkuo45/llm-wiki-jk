# Genotoxic Stress

**Genotoxic stress** refers to cellular damage inflicted on [[DNA]] by chemical, physical, or biological agents that directly or indirectly cause DNA lesions. It is a primary trigger of [[Senescence|cellular senescence]], [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]], and — when unresolved — [[Genomic Instability|genomic instability]] and [[Cancer|cancer]]. The cellular response to genotoxic stress is orchestrated by the [[DNA Damage Response|DNA damage response (DDR)]], a highly conserved signaling network that coordinates cell cycle arrest, DNA repair, and cell fate decisions.

## Sources of Genotoxic Stress

### Exogenous Sources
- **[[Chemotherapy|Chemotherapeutic agents]]**: [[Cisplatin]], [[Etoposide]], doxorubicin, cyclophosphamide — cause DNA crosslinks, double-strand breaks, and intercalation
- **[[Ionizing Radiation]]**: Gamma rays and X-rays induce double-strand breaks
- **UV radiation**: Causes pyrimidine dimers and photoproducts
- **Environmental toxins**: [[Aflatoxin]] B1, benzo[a]pyrene, [[Paraquat]]

### Endogenous Sources
- **[[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS)**: Byproducts of [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|oxidative phosphorylation]] cause base modifications (e.g., [[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine|8-oxo-dG]])
- **[[Oncogene Activation]]**: Hyper-replication stress from oncogenic signaling (e.g., [[RAS]], [[MYC]])
- **[[Telomere Attrition]]**: Erosion of telomeres triggers a DDR mimicking double-strand breaks
- **Metabolic byproducts**: [[Methylglyoxal]], lipid peroxidation products

## Cellular Responses to Genotoxic Stress

### DNA Damage Response (DDR)
The DDR is initiated by sensor proteins ([[ATM]], [[ATR]], [[DNA-PK]]) that detect DNA lesions and activate transducer kinases ([[CHK1]], [[CHK2]]), which amplify the signal to effector proteins.

### Outcomes

| Outcome | Description | Key Mediators |
|---------|-------------|---------------|
| **DNA Repair** | Lesions resolved; cell returns to normal | [[Base Excision Repair|BER]], [[Nucleotide Excision Repair|NER]], [[Homologous Recombination|HR]], [[Non-Homologous End Joining|NHEJ]] |
| **Senescence** | Permanent cell cycle arrest; SASP secretion | [[p53]], [[p21 CIP1|p21]], [[p16 INK4a|p16]], [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] |
| **Apoptosis** | Programmed cell death for heavily damaged cells | [[p53]], [[BAX]], [[PUMA]], [[Caspase-9]], [[Caspase-3]] |
| **Genomic Instability** | Mutations, chromosomal rearrangements if repair fails | Impaired [[Homologous Recombination|HR]], [[Non-Homologous End Joining|NHEJ]] |

## Role in SASP Induction

Persistent genotoxic stress is a hallmark of senescent cells. The unresolved DDR signaling maintains stable cell cycle arrest and drives [[SASP|SASP factor]] expression through:
1. **NF-κB activation**: DDR-associated kinases (ATM, IKK) activate [[NFκB]], a master SASP regulator
2. **[[cGAS-STING Pathway]]**: Genotoxic stress generates [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments|cytoplasmic chromatin fragments]] (CCF), [[mtDNA]] release, and [[LINE-1]]-derived [[cDNA]], all of which activate cGAS-STING-dependent SASP transcription
3. **Epigenetic changes**: Persistent DDR triggers degradation of H3K9 methyltransferases (G9a, GLP), de-repressing SASP gene loci

## Pathological Implications

- **[[Therapy-Induced Senescence]]**: Genotoxic chemotherapy induces senescence in tumor cells, with beneficial (arrest) and detrimental (SASP) consequences
- **[[Inflammaging]]**: Age-related accumulation of genotoxic stress drives chronic SASP and sterile inflammation
- **[[Cancer]]**: Mutations arising from genotoxic stress drive oncogenesis; DDR defects predispose to malignancy

## Therapeutic Targeting

- **Senolytics**: Eliminate cells made senescent by genotoxic stress
- **DDR inhibitors**: [[PARP inhibitors]] (olaparib, niraparib) exploit synthetic lethality in BRCA-deficient cancers
- **Antioxidants**: [[N-Acetylcysteine]] and [[Vitamin C]] reduce endogenous ROS-mediated genotoxic stress
