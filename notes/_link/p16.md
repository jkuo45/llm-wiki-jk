## Overview

[[p16]] (also known as p16^INK4a^ or [[CDKN2A]]) is a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor that functions as a critical tumor suppressor and a central mediator of cellular senescence. It binds to and inhibits [[CDK4]] and [[CDK6]], preventing phosphorylation of [[Rb]] and thereby enforcing cell cycle arrest at the G1/S checkpoint. p16 is one of the most widely used biomarkers of cellular senescence and aging.

## Structure and Domains

- Encoded by the *CDKN2A* locus on chromosome 9p21
- Contains four ankyrin repeat domains that mediate binding to CDK4/CDK6
- The same locus alternatively encodes p14^ARF^ (p19^ARF^ in mice), which stabilizes [[p53]] by inhibiting MDM2
- No kinase activity; functions purely as an inhibitor via steric occlusion of the CDK active site

## Mechanism of Action

- Binds CDK4 and CDK6, forming p16-CDK4/6 complexes that cannot phosphorylate [[Rb]]
- Unphosphorylated Rb remains bound to [[E2F1|E2F]] transcription factors, sequestering them and preventing S-phase gene expression
- This enforces irreversible G1 cell cycle arrest, a hallmark of senescence
- Acts independently of the p53/p21 pathway, providing a parallel and complementary senescence enforcement mechanism

> [!info] Source: Dong et al. 2024
> The p16/RB pathway is one of two major signaling axes driving cellular senescence. p16 binds CDK4/CDK6 kinases, forming complexes that block RB phosphorylation, resulting in senescence. This pathway is particularly important in oncogene-induced senescence and age-related senescence accumulation.

## Role in Senescence

p16 expression increases progressively with age and accumulated stress:

- **Biomarker of senescence**: p16^INK4a^ mRNA and protein levels are among the most reliable markers of senescent cell burden in tissues
- **Irreversibility marker**: p16 enforces the irreversible nature of senescence by maintaining Rb in its hypophosphorylated state
- **Age-related accumulation**: p16-expressing cells accumulate with age in virtually all tissues; their clearance extends healthspan in mice (Baker et al. 2011, 2016)
- **Paracrine induction**: SASP factors from senescent cells can induce p16 expression in neighboring cells, spreading senescence

> [!tip] Source: Dong et al. 2024
> In addition to the p53/p21 mechanism, the p16/RB pathway provides an independent mechanism for enforcing cell cycle arrest. Oncogene-induced senescence (OIS) involves both pathways, with RB playing an important role in maintaining OIS by inhibiting E2F expression.

## Pathological Significance

### Cancer
- Homozygous deletion or promoter methylation of *CDKN2A* is one of the most common genetic alterations in human cancers
- Loss of p16 function removes a critical barrier to malignant transformation
- *CDKN2A* mutations are found in melanoma, pancreatic cancer, glioblastoma, and many other tumor types

### Aging
- p16^INK4a^-positive cells accumulate with age and drive age-related tissue dysfunction
- Selective clearance of p16-positive senescent cells delays age-related pathologies in progeroid and naturally aged mice
- p16 expression is used as the basis of the INK-ATTAC and p16-3MR transgenic systems for selective senescent cell clearance
