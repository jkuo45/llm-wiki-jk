# Rapalogs

**Rapalogs** are analogs and derivatives of [[Rapamycin]] (sirolimus) that inhibit [[mTORC1]] by binding the FKBP12-FRB interface of the [[mTOR]] kinase. Clinically they include [[Everolimus]], temsirolimus, ridaforolimus, and deforolimus. They share rapamycin's core pharmacology—mTORC1 inhibition, [[Autophagy]] induction, and lifespan/healthspan effects—and, by the same network logic, can display [[Hormesis|hormetic]] dose responses rooted in an [[Incoherent Bivalent Motif]] around [[mTORC1]].

## Members and Indications

- **Everolimus** — immunosuppression (organ transplant), renal angiomyolipoma, subependymal giant cell astrocytoma, advanced hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer, neuroendocrine tumors.
- **Temsirolimus** — renal cell carcinoma.
- **Ridaforolimus** — sarcoma.
- **Sirolimus (rapamycin)** — the parent compound; also used in vascular-restenosis stents and as an investigational [[Geroprotectors|geroprotector]].

## Mechanism

Rapalogs bind [[FKBP12]], and the rapamycin-FKBP12 complex binds the FRB (rapamycin-binding) domain of mTOR within [[mTORC1]], allosterically inhibiting its kinase activity. Acute exposure spares [[mTORC2]]; chronic exposure can disrupt mTORC2 assembly, the basis of the rapamycin [[Incoherent Bivalent Motif]] [^1].

## Hormesis and Dosing

Because rapalogs act on the same mTOR-PI3K network, their effects follow a [[Biphasic Dose-Response Curve]]—beneficial at intermediate exposure, detrimental at high dose. This motivates intermittent/personalized dosing strategies in longevity applications.
