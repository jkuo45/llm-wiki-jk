# Vasopressors

**Vasopressors** are drugs that elevate arterial blood pressure, used to restore organ perfusion in shock. They act primarily through [[Vasoconstriction]] (increased systemic vascular resistance) or by increasing myocardial contractility (positive inotropy), and many are endogenous [[Catecholamines]] or their analogs.

## Pharmacodynamics
First-line therapy is [[Norepinephrine]], an α1-adrenergic agonist that raises vascular tone with modest β1-mediated inotropy; it is the standard for [[Septic Shock]] and most distributive shocks. [[Dopamine]] was historically used but is now reserved for selected cases because of arrhythmogenic risk. [[Angiotensin II]] is a rescue agent that raises pressure through the renin–angiotensin system. [[Epinephrine]] combines α- and β-effects and is preferred in [[Hypotension]] refractory to other agents and in anaphylactic shock.

## Clinical Applications and Resistance
Vasopressors are titrated to mean arterial pressure targets while monitoring for limb ischemia, arrhythmias, and splanchnic hypoperfusion. In [[Vasoplegic Shock]] — refractory hypotension with low systemic resistance despite high-dose catecholamines — standard agents may fail. This state is driven by nitric-oxide–mediated vascular hyporesponsiveness, and rescue strategies include methylene blue, which inhibits soluble guanylyl cyclase and restores vascular tone (see [[Methylene blue]]).

> [!warning] Safety
> Excessive vasoconstriction can cause distal ischemia and lactic acidosis; dosing should be the minimum required to maintain perfusion.
