# Nanoemulsions

**Nanoemulsions** are colloidal dispersions of two immiscible liquids (oil and water) stabilized by surfactants/co-surfactants, with droplet sizes of ~20–200 nm. They form transparent o/w or w/o systems and, via their large interfacial area, increase dissolution rate and intestinal absorption, thereby improving [[Bioavailability|bioavailability]].

## Self-Nanoemulsifying Drug Delivery Systems (SNEDDS)

[[SNEDDS]] are anhydrous preconcentrates (oil + surfactant + co-surfactant + drug) that self-emulsify into an o/w [[Nanoemulsions|nanoemulsion]] upon aqueous dilution in the stomach. Fisetin-SNEDDS (droplet size 154 nm) showed:

- 11.0-, 3.6-, and 7.9-fold higher dissolution in water, pH 1.2, and pH 6.8 media, respectively.
- 3.7-fold higher C<sub>max</sub> and 1.5-fold AUC after oral dosing (20 mg/kg) in rats.
- Neuroprotection in rotenone-induced [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] rats, reducing [[TNFα]] and [[IL-6]] and raising [[Glutathione|GSH]], [[Superoxide Dismutase|SOD]], and [[Catalase|CAT]].

Conventional fisetin [[Nanoemulsions|nanoemulsions]] (i.p.) produced a 24-fold bioavailability increase and a 53% tumor-size reduction at 36.6 mg/kg vs. 223 mg/kg free fisetin in Lewis lung carcinoma.
