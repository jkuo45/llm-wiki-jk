# Satellite Cell

**Satellite cells** are [[Muscle Stem Cell|tissue-resident stem cells]] of [[Skeletal Muscle]], located between the [[Basal Lamina]] and the [[Sarcolemma]] of mature muscle fibers. They are essential for postnatal [[Muscle Growth]], regeneration after injury, and maintenance of muscle mass throughout life.

## Quiescence and Activation

In adult muscle, satellite cells are maintained in a quiescent state ([[G0 Phase]]), marked by [[Pax7]] expression. Upon [[Muscle Injury]] or [[Exercise]], they exit quiescence through [[Nitric Oxide]]-[[HGF]]-[[c-Met]] signaling, proliferate as myogenic precursor cells, and differentiate into [[Myoblasts]] that fuse to repair or form new myofibers. A subset self-renews to replenish the stem cell pool.

## Myogenic Program

Satellite cell activation follows a defined transcription factor cascade:
- [[Pax7]] — master regulator; maintains stem cell identity
- [[MyoD]] — myogenic commitment factor
- [[Myogenin]] — terminal differentiation
- [[Myf5]] — early myogenic determination

## Aging

Satellite cell number and function decline with age, contributing to [[Sarcopenia]]. This decline involves [[Cellular Senescence]], reduced [[Autophagy]], [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], and [[Notch Signaling]] impairment. [[Exercise]] and [[Caloric Restriction]] partially preserve satellite cell function in aged muscle.
