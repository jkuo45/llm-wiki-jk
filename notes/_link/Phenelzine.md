# Phenelzine

**Phenelzine** is a non-selective, irreversible [[Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor]] (MAOI) used as an antidepressant, particularly for atypical and treatment-resistant [[Depression]]. As a phenethylamine derivative, it covalently inactivates both MAO-A and MAO-B, blocking the oxidative deamination of monoamine neurotransmitters.

## Mechanism & Pharmacodynamics

By inhibiting [[Monoamine oxidase]], phenelzine raises synaptic concentrations of [[Serotonin]], [[Norepinephrine]], and [[Dopamine]] (and tyramine), thereby relieving depressive and anxious symptoms. This same class of action — monoamine preservation — is exhibited at low doses by [[Methylene blue]], a selective MAO-A inhibitor, linking the two agents mechanistically despite different primary indications.

## Clinical Use & Adverse Effects

Benefits include efficacy in anxious depression and social phobia. Common adverse effects are orthostatic hypotension, weight gain, sedation, and sexual dysfunction. Because inhibition is irreversible, enzyme activity recovers only as new protein is synthesized (days), prolonging interaction risk.

## Interactions & Safety

Phenelzine carries two hallmark dangers: the "cheese reaction" (tyramine-induced hypertensive crisis) and [[Serotonin Syndrome]] when combined with serotonergic drugs (SSRIs, SNRIs, certain opioids, or methylene blue). The shared MAO-inhibitory action with methylene blue is why both appear in serotonergic-safety discussions within this vault.
