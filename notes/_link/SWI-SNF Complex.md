# SWI-SNF Complex

## Overview

The SWI/SNF (SWItch/Sucrose Non-Fermentable) complex — also called the BAF complex in mammals — is an ATP-dependent chromatin-remodeling machine that slides, evicts, or restructures nucleosomes to regulate DNA accessibility and transcription. Core catalytic subunits include SNF2H (SMARCA5) and SNF2L (SMARCA1). The SWI/SNF complex was identified as a major cGAS-interacting partner in nuclear cGAS interactome studies, revealing an unexpected link between DNA-sensing proteins and chromatin architecture.

## Recruitment by Noncanonical Nuclear cGAS

Beyond its canonical role in the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], nuclear [[cGAS]] engages the SWI/SNF complex through its [[Intrinsically Disordered Region|intrinsically disordered region]]-mediated [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|phase separation]]. cGAS scaffolds SNF2H/SNF2L-containing SWI/SNF assemblies within nuclear condensates, positioning the remodeler at sites where cGAS is tethered to [[Chromatin]] and [[Nucleosome|nucleosomes]]. This recruitment implies that cGAS can modulate higher-order chromatin architecture and gene expression programs in a STING-independent manner, adding a transcriptional-regulatory dimension to its noncanonical functions.

## Implications for Genome Stability and Disease

Because SWI/SNF controls nucleosome positioning, its coordination with cGAS may influence [[DNA Damage]] repair, replication, and [[Genomic Instability|genome stability]] — processes already known to be shaped by nuclear cGAS through interactions with PARP1. Dysregulation of this axis could therefore contribute to the chromatin stress and [[Neuroinflammation]] observed in [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]]. Defining how phase-separated cGAS directs SWI/SNF activity remains an important frontier for understanding noncanonical cGAS functions in the [[Nucleus]] and their relevance to aging and brain disease.
