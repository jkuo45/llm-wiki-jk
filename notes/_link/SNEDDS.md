# SNEDDS

**Self-nanoemulsifying drug delivery systems (SNEDDS)** are anhydrous liquid or semisolid preconcentrates containing a drug dissolved in a mixture of oil, surfactant, and co-surfactant. Upon dilution with aqueous fluid (e.g., in the stomach), they spontaneously form an oil-in-water [[Nanoemulsions|nanoemulsion]] with nanometer droplets, dramatically increasing surface area for absorption and thus [[Bioavailability|bioavailability]].

## Fisetin SNEDDS

Kumar et al. formulated fisetin-SNEDDS (castor oil / Lauroglycol FCC / Tween 80 / Transcutol P; droplet size 154 nm; 100% drug loading). Key findings:

- Dissolution 11.0×, 3.6×, and 7.9× higher in water, pH 1.2 HCl, and pH 6.8 phosphate buffer vs. free fisetin.
- 3.79-fold higher intestinal permeation and lower excretion in cell-line studies; 89% viable cells vs. 10% for free fisetin (lower toxicity via reduced cell interaction).
- Oral pharmacokinetics (20 mg/kg, rats): 3.7-fold higher C<sub>max</sub>, T<sub>max</sub> 30 min (vs. 15 min free), ~1.5-fold AUC.
- Neuroprotection in rotenone [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] model: reduced [[TNFα]]/[[IL-6]], raised [[Glutathione|GSH]], [[Superoxide Dismutase|SOD]], [[Catalase|CAT]].
