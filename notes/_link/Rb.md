## Overview

[[Rb]] (Retinoblastoma protein) is a critical tumor suppressor and master regulator of the G1/S cell cycle transition. Rb functions primarily by binding and sequestering [[E2F1|E2F]] transcription factors, preventing transcription of genes required for S-phase entry. When hypophosphorylated, Rb enforces cell cycle arrest—a state that is central to both quiescence and [[Senescence|cellular senescence]].

## Structure and Domains

- Encoded by the *RB1* gene on chromosome 13q14
- Contains a globular "pocket" domain (A and B boxes) that mediates binding to E2F transcription factors and viral oncoproteins (e.g., HPV E7, SV40 Large T)
- Also contains an N-terminal domain and a C-terminal domain involved in protein-protein interactions
- Subject to extensive post-translational modifications: phosphorylation by CDKs is the primary regulatory mechanism

## Mechanism of Cell Cycle Regulation

- **Hypophosphorylated Rb** (active state): Binds E2F transcription factors, preventing expression of S-phase genes (cyclin A, cyclin E, DNA polymerase, thymidine kinase)
- **Hyperphosphorylated Rb** (inactive state): Released from E2F; allows cell cycle progression
- Phosphorylation is catalyzed by [[CDK4]]/CDK6-cyclin D (mono-phosphorylation) and CDK2-cyclin E/A (hyper-phosphorylation)
- [[p16]] inhibits CDK4/6, maintaining Rb in its active hypophosphorylated state

> [!info] Source: Dong et al. 2024
> p21 inhibits the phosphorylation of Rb by restraining CDK2 function. p16 binds CDK4/CDK6 to block RB phosphorylation. Both pathways converge on Rb to enforce cell cycle arrest and senescence. Rb's role in maintaining oncogene-induced senescence is through inhibition of E2F transcription factor expression.

## Role in Senescence

Rb plays a pivotal role in enforcing the irreversible nature of senescence:

- Hypophosphorylated Rb maintains E2F in a repressed state, preventing cell cycle re-entry
- Rb recruits chromatin remodeling complexes (e.g., SWI/SNF, histone deacetylases) to E2F target gene promoters, establishing a repressive chromatin state
- This chromatin remodeling makes the arrest irreversible, even if Rb is subsequently degraded
- In OIS, Rb is maintained in its hypophosphorylated state by both p16 and p21 pathways simultaneously
- Loss of Rb function can allow cells to bypass senescence and re-enter the cell cycle

## Pathological Significance

### Cancer
- *RB1* was the first tumor suppressor gene identified (Knudson's two-hit hypothesis)
- Mutations/deletions of *RB1* cause retinoblastoma in children and are found in SCLC, bladder cancer, and osteosarcoma
- HPV E7 oncoprotein inactivates Rb by binding the pocket domain, releasing E2F and driving proliferation
- Loss of Rb function cooperates with loss of p53 in malignant transformation

### Aging and Senescence
- Rb-mediated senescence contributes to age-related tissue degeneration
- Rb loss in senescent cells could potentially contribute to genomic instability and tumor emergence from senescent populations

## Connections

- [[p16]] — p16 inhibits CDK4/6, preventing Rb phosphorylation and maintaining cell cycle arrest
- [[E2F1]] — Rb sequesters E2F, blocking S-phase transcription
- [[CDK2]] — CDK2-cyclin E/A hyperphosphorylates Rb, inactivating its growth-suppressive function
- [[CDK4]] — CDK4-cyclin D mono-phosphorylates Rb as the initial step toward inactivation
- [[Senescence]] — Rb hypophosphorylation is a central enforcer of senescence-associated cell cycle arrest
- [[p53]] — Parallel pathway converging on cell cycle arrest; p53 activates p21 which inhibits CDK2, maintaining Rb hypophosphorylation
- [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence]] — OIS depends on both p53/p21 and p16/RB pathways maintaining Rb in its active state
- [[Therapy-Induced Senescence]] — Chemotherapy activates DDR → p53 → p21 → CDK2 inhibition → Rb hypophosphorylation
