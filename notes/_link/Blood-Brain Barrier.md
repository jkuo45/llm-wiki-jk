# Blood-Brain Barrier

The **blood-brain barrier (BBB)** is a highly selective neurovascular barrier that separates the circulating blood from the brain parenchyma. It is formed by specialised [[Endothelial cells|endothelial cells]] of brain capillaries connected by tight junctions, surrounded by [[Astrocytes|astrocyte]] end-feet and pericytes. The BBB maintains brain homeostasis by restricting the passage of most molecules, pathogens, and immune cells from the bloodstream into the central nervous system (CNS), while permitting selective transport of essential nutrients.

## Structure & Components

The BBB is a multi-cellular structure comprising four main components:

- **Brain capillary endothelial cells:** Unlike peripheral endothelium, BBB endothelial cells have continuous tight junctions (claudins, occludin, ZO-1), minimal pinocytic vesicles, and low rates of transcytosis. They express efflux transporters (P-glycoprotein, BCRP, MRPs) that actively pump out xenobiotics.
- **Basement membrane:** A dense extracellular matrix underlying the endothelium, composed of collagen IV, laminin, fibronectin, and proteoglycans. It provides structural support and signalling cues.
- **Pericytes:** Mural cells embedded within the basement membrane that regulate capillary diameter, blood flow, and BBB permeability. Pericyte coverage is essential for BBB integrity; pericyte loss leads to BBB breakdown.
- **Astrocyte end-feet:** Projections from astrocytes that ensheathe >99% of the capillary surface. They secrete factors (angiopoietin-1, sonic hedgehog, glial-derived neurotrophic factor) that maintain BBB tightness and induce tight junction expression.

## Transport Mechanisms

- **Paracellular pathway:** Effectively sealed by tight junctions. Only small lipophilic molecules (O₂, CO₂, ethanol) and small uncharged molecules can passively diffuse across.
- **Transcellular transport:** Essential nutrients cross via specific transporters:
  - **GLUT1:** Glucose transporter; brain glucose uptake is GLUT1-dependent
  - **LAT1:** Large neutral amino acid transporter
  - **MCT1:** Monocarboxylate transporter for lactate and ketone bodies
  - **CTR1:** Copper transporter
- **Receptor-mediated transcytosis:** Larger molecules (transferrin, insulin, leptin) cross via receptor-mediated endocytosis across endothelial cells.
- **Efflux transporters:** P-glycoprotein (MDR1/ABCB1), BCRP (ABCG2), and MRPs actively export drugs and toxins from brain endothelium back into the blood, creating a formidable pharmacokinetic barrier.

> [!important] CNS Drug Delivery Challenge
> The BBB restricts >98% of small-molecule drugs and nearly all large-molecule therapeutics from reaching the brain, making CNS drug delivery one of the greatest challenges in pharmacology. Strategies to bypass the BBB include intrathecal injection, focused ultrasound, nanoparticles, and receptor-mediated transcytosis "Trojan horse" approaches.

## Physiological Functions

### Neural Homeostasis
The BBB maintains the tightly regulated ionic and molecular milieu required for neuronal signalling. It restricts fluctuations in plasma potassium, amino acids, and neurotransmitters that would disrupt neural function if freely entering the brain.

### Immune Surveillance
The BBB limits peripheral immune cell entry under normal conditions, contributing to the CNS immune-privileged status. Under inflammatory conditions, activated leukocytes can cross the BBB via adhesion molecules (ICAM-1, VCAM-1) and chemokine gradients.

### Neurovascular Coupling
Astrocyte end-feet sense neuronal activity and relay signals to the vasculature, adjusting local cerebral blood flow to match metabolic demand (functional hyperaemia).

## Pathological Roles

### Ischaemic Stroke & Reperfusion Injury
Following [[Ischemic Stroke|ischaemic stroke]], the BBB breaks down within hours. Reperfusion generates [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] that damage tight junctions, basement membrane, and endothelial cells. BBB disruption leads to vasogenic oedema, haemorrhagic transformation, and inflammatory cell infiltration. [[MMP-2]], [[MMP-9]], and [[MMP-12]] degrade basement membrane components (collagen IV, laminin, elastin), exacerbating BBB breakdown.

### Neurodegenerative Diseases
- **Alzheimer's Disease:** Amyloid-beta damages endothelial cells, compromises tight junctions, activates MMPs, and causes BBB breakdown. BBB dysfunction impairs amyloid-beta clearance, creating a vicious cycle.
- **Parkinson's Disease:** BBB dysfunction in the substantia nigra permits entry of neurotoxic molecules and inflammatory cells.
- **Multiple Sclerosis:** BBB breakdown allows autoreactive T cells to enter the CNS and attack myelin.

### Neuroinflammation
Pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6) upregulate adhesion molecules (ICAM-1, VCAM-1) on BBB endothelium, promoting leukocyte transmigration. This is a key mechanism in autoimmune encephalitis, meningitis, and post-infectious neuroinflammation.

### Brain Tumours
Glioblastoma and other brain tumours disrupt the BBB locally, creating a "leaky" barrier that allows contrast agent enhancement on MRI. Tumour-associated angiogenesis produces dysfunctional vessels with defective tight junctions.

### Diabetes & Metabolic Disease
Chronic hyperglycaemia, advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), and oxidative stress damage the BBB. BBB dysfunction in diabetes contributes to cognitive impairment and increased susceptibility to CNS infections.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Thrombolytic therapy:** [[Tissue Plasminogen Activator|tPA]] (alteplase) must cross or work at the BBB to lyse clots in ischaemic stroke. Excessive tPA can cause haemorrhagic transformation by disrupting the BBB.
- **Neuroimaging:** BBB integrity is assessed clinically using gadolinium contrast-enhanced MRI. Contrast enhancement indicates BBB breakdown.
- **Drug delivery:** Strategies to bypass the BBB include intrathecal administration, convection-enhanced delivery, focused ultrasound with microbubbles, nanoparticles, and antibody-mediated transcytosis.
- **Biomarkers:** Serum neurofilament light chain (NfL) and S100β are released when the BBB is disrupted, serving as biomarkers of BBB damage.
