# PEX5

[[PEX5]] encodes the **Peroxisomal Targeting Signal 1 (PTS1) receptor**, the primary cytosolic shuttling receptor responsible for importing the majority of peroxisomal matrix proteins. PEX5 recognizes the C-terminal tripeptide PTS1 signal (typically -SKL or variants) on newly synthesized proteins in the [[Cytosol]], docks at the peroxisomal membrane via interactions with the docking complex ([[PEX13]]/[[PEX14]]), and translocates its cargo into the [[Peroxisome]] lumen. After cargo release, PEX5 is recycled back to the cytosol through a monoubiquitination-dependent mechanism involving the RING finger peroxins ([[PEX2]], [[PEX10]], [[PEX12]]).

## Structure & Domains

PEX5 contains two major functional regions:

- **N-terminal TPR (Tetratricopeptide Repeat) domain** — mediates binding to the PTS1 signal and to [[PEX13]]/[[PEX14]] at the peroxisomal membrane. The TPR domain comprises 7 tetratricopeptide repeats that form a concave surface for PTS1 recognition.
- **C-terminal unstructured region** — contains the monoubiquitination site (Lys209 in human PEX5) essential for receptor recycling. This region is intrinsically disordered and becomes structured upon membrane insertion.

Two isoforms exist: PEX5L (long, ~70 kDa) and PEX5S (short, ~65 kDa). PEX5L contains an additional 37-amino acid insertion that enables recognition of a subset of PTS2-containing proteins in cooperation with [[PEX7]].

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

PEX5 operates through a defined catalytic cycle:

1. **Cargo Recognition** — PEX5 binds PTS1-containing precursors in the [[Cytosol]] with high affinity (Kd ~10-100 nM).
2. **Membrane Docking** — The PEX5-cargo complex docks at the peroxisomal membrane by binding [[PEX14]] (primary docking site) and [[PEX13]] (stabilizing interaction).
3. **Translocation** — PEX5 inserts into the membrane, forming a transient pore. The cargo is released into the peroxisomal matrix, where PTS1 signals are cleaved by [[Signal Peptidase]].
4. **Ubiquitination & Recycling** — PEX5 is monoubiquitinated at Lys209 by the RING E3 ligase complex ([[PEX2]]/[[PEX10]]/[[PEX12]]). The AAA-ATPase [[PEX1]] (in complex with [[PEX6]]) extracts ubiquitinated PEX5 from the membrane, deubiquitinates it, and returns it to the cytosol for another round of import.

PEX5 recycling is energetically demanding: ~50% of peroxisomal matrix import depends on continuous PEX5 cycling. When recycling is disrupted, PEX5 accumulates in ubiquitinated forms on the membrane, triggering [[Pexophagy]]-mediated degradation of entire peroxisomes.

## Physiological Function

PEX5 is essential for importing virtually all PTS1-dependent peroxisomal enzymes, including:

- **Beta-Oxidation enzymes** — acyl-CoA oxidase, D-bifunctional protein, thiolase (critical for [[Fatty acid]] β-oxidation).
- **Plasmalogen biosynthesis enzymes** — dihydroxyacetone phosphate acyltransferase, alkenylDHAP synthase (myelin sheath components).
- **Detoxification enzymes** — catalase, urate oxidase, D-amino acid oxidase.
- **Bile acid synthesis enzymes** — peroxisomal α-methylacyl-CoA racemase.

Loss of PEX5 function causes complete failure of peroxisomal matrix protein import, resulting in lethal accumulation of very-long-chain fatty acids, branched-chain fatty acids, and bile acid intermediates.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Mutations in *PEX5* cause **PEX5-deficient Zellweger Spectrum Disorder (ZSD)**, a severe autosomal recessive condition within the [[Zellweger Spectrum Disorders]]:

- **Zellweger syndrome** (severe) — craniofacial abnormalities, hepatomegaly, renal cysts, severe hypotonia, seizures, death within first year.
- **Neonatal Adrenoleukodystrophy** (intermediate) — progressive demyelination, adrenal insufficiency.
- **Infantile Refsum Disease** (mild) — sensorineural deafness, retinopathy, hepatic dysfunction.

PEX5 mutations account for ~5-10% of ZSD cases. Common mutations include nonsense and frameshift alleles that abolish PTS1 receptor function. Missense mutations in the TPR domain can cause milder phenotypes by partially retaining PTS1 binding.

Biallelic PEX5 loss in mice is embryonic lethal; conditional knockout in liver causes severe hepatic dysfunction and early death, mirroring human ZSD pathology.
