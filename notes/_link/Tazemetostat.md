# Tazemetostat

**Tazemetostat (EPZ-6438; trade name Tazverik)** is a first-in-class, orally bioavailable, selective inhibitor of the histone methyltransferase **[[EZH2]]**, approved by the FDA in 2020.

## Chemical Properties & Mechanism of Action

- A small-molecule **SAM-competitive inhibitor** that binds the catalytic SET domain of [[EZH2]], the enzymatic subunit of Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2).
- **Epigenetic effect:** blocks EZH2-mediated trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 27 ([[H3K27me3]]), a repressive [[Histone Methylation]] mark.
- **Consequence:** loss of aberrant [[H3K27me3]] derepresses silenced tumor-suppressor genes, restoring differentiation and cell-cycle control in cancers addicted to EZH2 activity.

## Applications

> [!info] Precision epigenetic oncology
> EZH2 gain-of-function mutations and overexpression drive several malignancies. Tazemetostat is approved for **epithelioid sarcoma** (INI1/SMARCB1-deficient) and for relapsed/refractory **EZH2-mutant follicular lymphoma**. It exemplifies targeting the "writer" enzymes of the [[Epigenetics|epigenome]], complementing eraser-targeting drugs such as HDAC inhibitors ([[SAHA]]).

> [!warning] Safety
> A notable class risk is secondary malignancy (e.g. T-cell lymphoma) observed in early pediatric studies, alongside cytopenias and fatigue.
