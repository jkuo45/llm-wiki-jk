# BAFF (B-cell activating factor)

BAFF (B-cell activating factor), also known as TNFSF13B, is a cytokine of the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) superfamily that plays a key role in B-cell survival, maturation, and immune regulation. It is secreted by senescent fibroblasts and monocytic leukaemia cells and functions as a SASP factor that reinforces the upregulation of [[IL-6]] and other SASP components.

## Mechanism of Action

- BAFF is secreted by senescent cells and acts in an autocrine/paracrine manner to amplify the SASP
- It reinforces the expression of [[IL-6]] and other pro-inflammatory SASP factors
- BAFF signaling supports B-cell survival and differentiation, linking cellular senescence to adaptive immunity

## Role in SASP

- BAFF is identified as a component of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] that modulates immune responses in senescent tissue environments
- It contributes to the pro-inflammatory milieu characteristic of senescent cells
- BAFF expression may link cellular senescence to autoimmune and age-related inflammatory conditions

## Clinical Relevance

- BAFF is a therapeutic target in autoimmune diseases (e.g., belimumab for systemic lupus erythematosus)
- Elevated BAFF levels are associated with age-related immune dysfunction
- BAFF may serve as a biomarker for senescence burden in certain tissues
