# BAFF (B-cell activating factor)

BAFF (B-cell activating factor), also known as TNFSF13B, BLyS, or TALL-1, is a type II membrane-bound and proteolytically released cytokine of the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) superfamily. It is a SASP factor secreted by senescent fibroblasts and monocytic leukaemia cells that reinforces the secretory program — notably upregulating [[IL-6]] — and connects cellular senescence to B-cell-mediated adaptive immunity (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptors**: BAFF binds three TNF-receptor superfamily members — [[BAFF-R]] (BR3, the principal B-cell survival receptor), [[BCMA]], and [[TACI]] — with distinct affinities that shape B-cell fate.
- **Cell sources**: Produced by monocytes/[[Macrophages]], dendritic cells, neutrophils, T cells, and, in senescence, by senescent fibroblasts and monocytic leukaemia cells.
- **Signal transduction**: BAFF-R engages TRAF3→NIK to activate the non-canonical [[NF-κB]] pathway (plus canonical NF-κB, MAPK, and PI3K/Akt), promoting B-cell survival, maturation, and immunoglobulin class-switching.
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Sustains mature B-cell survival and plasma-cell differentiation; in senescence it acts autocrine/paracrine to amplify pro-inflammatory SASP components.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- BAFF is an identified SASP cytokine whose secretion by senescent cells reinforces and broadens the pro-inflammatory secretome, raising [[IL-6]] and other mediators.
- By supporting B-cell survival and maturation, BAFF links the senescent tissue microenvironment to adaptive (humoral) immunity, implicating the SASP in autoimmune and age-related immune dysregulation.
- Chronic BAFF exposure in aged tissue thus couples [[Inflammaging]] with autoantibody production and loss of B-cell tolerance.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- BAFF is a validated therapeutic target in autoimmunity: belimumab (anti-BAFF) is approved for systemic lupus erythematosus and is studied in other autoimmune diseases.
- Elevated BAFF is associated with age-related immune dysfunction, autoimmune disease, and B-cell malignancies.
- BAFF is a candidate biomarker of senescence-linked humoral autoimmunity and a potential senomorphic intervention point.
