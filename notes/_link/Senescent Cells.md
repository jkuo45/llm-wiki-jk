# Senescent Cells

**Senescent Cells** are cells that have entered a state of stable, irreversible [[Cell Cycle]] arrest in response to various stressors, including [[DNA Damage]], [[Oncogene Activation]], [[Oxidative Stress]], or [[Telomere Attrition]].

They are characterized by:
- **Proliferative Arrest**: Mediated primarily by the [[p53]]/[[p21 CIP1|p21]] and [[p16 INK4a|p16]]/[[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] pathways.
- **Resistance to Apoptosis**: Upregulation of senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs), including [[Bcl-2]] family proteins and survival pathways like [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]].
- **Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype (SASP)**: The secretion of a complex mix of cytokines, chemokines, growth factors, and proteases that influence the tissue microenvironment.

## Physiological Role
Senescence acts as a tumor-suppressive mechanism by preventing the proliferation of damaged cells. It also plays a role in wound healing and tissue repair.

## Pathological Role
The accumulation of senescent cells with age contributes to aging and age-related diseases. The SASP produced by these cells can drive chronic inflammation ([[Inflammaging]]), tissue dysfunction, and paradoxically promote cancer progression in established tumors.

## Therapeutic Targeting
- **[[Senolytics]]**: Drugs that selectively induce apoptosis in senescent cells (e.g., [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]], [[Navitoclax]]).
- **[[Senomorphic Therapy]]**: Drugs that suppress the SASP without killing the cells (e.g., [[Rapamycin]]).
