# Senescent Cells

**Senescent Cells** are cells that have entered a state of stable, essentially irreversible [[Cell Cycle]] arrest in response to various stressors, including [[DNA Damage]], [[Oncogene Activation]], [[Oxidative Stress]], or [[Telomere Attrition]]. First described by Hayflick and Moorhead (1961) as replicative senescence, the state is now recognized as a multifaceted stress response with tumor-suppressive, developmental, and pathological roles.

They are characterized by:
- **Proliferative arrest**: Enforced by the [[p53]]/[[p21 CIP1|p21]] and [[p16 INK4a|p16]]/[[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] pathways (the [[CDKN2A]] locus is central).
- **Apoptosis resistance**: Upregulation of [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways|SCAPs]], including [[Bcl-2]] family proteins and [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] survival signaling.
- **SASP**: The [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]]—a complex secretion of cytokines ([[IL-6]], [[IL-1α]]), chemokines ([[CCL2]]), growth factors, and proteases (MMPs) that reshape the tissue microenvironment.
- **Morphological and metabolic markers**: Enlarged flattened morphology, SA-β-galactosidase activity, senescence-associated heterochromatin foci (SAHF), and mitochondrial dysfunction.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Arrest enforcement**: DNA-damage signaling (ATM/ATR → CHK1/2 → p53 → p21) initiates arrest; sustained arrest is locked in by p16/Rb-mediated repression of E2F targets.
- **SASP induction**: NF-κB, C/EBPβ, and cGAS–STING (fed by [[Minority MOMP]] and cytosolic [[mtDNA]]) drive the secretome; [[IL-1α]] acts as an apical SASP amplifier.
- **Survival wiring**: SCAPs raise the apoptotic threshold, so cells that experience [[Minority MOMP]] survive and signal instead of dying.
- **Dormancy vs. plasticity**: Though "irreversible," arrest can be bypassed under specific oncogenic or reprogramming conditions, a safety concern for senescence-inducing therapies.

## Role in Senescence & Physiology

- **Tumor suppression**: Senescence halts proliferation of damaged/oncogene-stressed cells, preventing transformation.
- **Wound healing and development**: Transient senescence aids tissue repair, fibrosis resolution, and embryonic patterning.
- **Immune clearance**: The SASP recruits immune cells that normally clear senescent cells; age-related decline in this surveillance permits accumulation.

## Pathological Role

- Accumulation of senescent cells with age contributes to [[Aging]] and age-related diseases.
- The SASP drives chronic inflammation ([[Inflammaging]]), tissue dysfunction, and can paradoxically promote [[Cancer]] progression in established tumors.
- Senescent cells contribute to fibrosis, metabolic dysfunction, osteoarthritis, and neurodegeneration.

## Therapeutic Targeting

- **[[Senolytics]]**: Drugs that selectively induce apoptosis in senescent cells by disabling SCAPs (e.g., [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]], [[Navitoclax]], [[Fisetin]]).
- **[[Senomorphic Therapy]]**: Drugs that suppress the SASP without killing the cells (e.g., [[Rapamycin]], JAK inhibitors).
- **Clinical translation**: Senolytic trials are underway in diabetic kidney disease, IPF, and osteoarthritis; SASP factors (IL-6, MMP-9, GM-CSF) serve as pharmacodynamic biomarkers.
