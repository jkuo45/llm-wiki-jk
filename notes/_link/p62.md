# p62

[[p62]] (also known as sequestosome 1, SQSTM1) is a protein that acts as a selective autophagy receptor. It binds to ubiquitinated proteins and organelles and targets them to the [[notes/_link/Autophagy]] pathway for degradation. In [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]], p62 is a common component of [[Lewy Bodies]] and other protein inclusions.

# p62

**p62** (also known as **SQSTM1** or Sequestosome 1) is a multifunctional protein that acts as an adapter for [[Selective Autophagy]].

## Role in Autophagy
- p62 binds to ubiquitinated protein aggregates and damaged organelles.
- It interacts with [[LC3]] on the [[Autophagosome]] membrane, effectively "linking" the cargo to the autophagy machinery.
- As a result, p62 is degraded along with the cargo within the [[Lysosome]].

## Marker for Autophagic Flux
- Because p62 is degraded during autophagy, its accumulation is often used as a marker for impaired [[Autophagic Flux]], while its reduction indicates active autophagy.

## Pathological Significance
- Accumulation of p62 is observed in many [[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]] and is associated with [[Proteotoxicity]].
- Under hyperglycemic conditions, intracellular p62 levels increase, correlating with suppressed autophagy.
