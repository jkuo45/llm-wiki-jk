# Peroxisomes
**Peroxisomes** are single-membrane-bound organelles present in virtually all eukaryotic cells that perform essential functions in lipid metabolism, reactive oxygen species (ROS) metabolism, and signaling.
## Oxidative Functions
Peroxisomes are a major site of cellular oxygen consumption and H₂O₂ production. They contain a high concentration of [[Catalase]] (~15% of total peroxisomal protein), which decomposes H₂O₂ generated by peroxisomal oxidases, including:
- **Acyl-CoA oxidases**: First step of very-long-chain fatty acid β-oxidation, producing H₂O₂
- **D-amino acid oxidase**, **L-α-hydroxyacid oxidase**, **Polyamine oxidase**, **Xanthine oxidase** (converted from XDH)
The co-localization of H₂O₂-producing enzymes and Catalase within the same organelle is a classic example of metabolic compartmentalization. Peroxisomes also contain [[Superoxide Dismutase|SOD1]] (CuZn-SOD) and peroxiredoxin PRDX5, contributing to their ROS detoxification capacity.
## Metabolic Roles
- **β-Oxidation**: Very-long-chain fatty acids (VLCFAs, >C22), pristanic acid, and dicarboxylic acids
- **Bile Acid Synthesis**: Conversion of cholesterol to cholic and chenodeoxycholic acids
- **Ether Phospholipid Synthesis**: Formation of plasmalogens (critical for myelin in the nervous system)
- **α-Oxidation**: Phytanic acid metabolism
- **Glyoxylate Detoxification**: Conversion of glyoxylate to glycine (preventing oxalate stone formation)
## Clinical Relevance
Peroxisomal disorders include **Zellweger Syndrome** (severe peroxisome biogenesis disorder), **X-linked Adrenoleukodystrophy** (defective VLCFA transport), and **Acatalasemia** (Catalase deficiency leading to oral gangrene). Peroxisomes also play roles in [[Aging]], [[Inflammation]], and [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]], as peroxisomal dysfunction increases oxidative stress.
