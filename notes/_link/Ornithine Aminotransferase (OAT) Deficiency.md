**Ornithine Aminotransferase (OAT) deficiency** is a rare autosomal recessive inborn error of metabolism caused by mutations in the _OAT_ gene encoding ornithine aminotransferase, a pyridoxal phosphate-dependent mitochondrial enzyme that converts [[Ornithine]] to [[Glutamate]] γ-semialdehyde.

## Biochemical Basis

- OAT deficiency impairs ornithine catabolism, leading to massive accumulation of [[Ornithine]] in plasma and tissues (plasma ornithine 10–20× normal).
- The elevated ornithine inhibits [[Arginine Glycine Amidinotransferase|AGAT]], the first enzyme in endogenous [[Creatine]] biosynthesis, causing secondary creatine depletion.
- Secondary creatine deficiency contributes to the [[Muscle Atrophy|muscle atrophy]] and [[Weakness]] seen in the disease.

## Clinical Features

- **Gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina** — progressive chorioretinal degeneration beginning in childhood, characterized by circular atrophic lesions in the peripheral retina.
- **Vision loss** — progressive constriction of visual fields and loss of night vision, often leading to legal blindness by age 40–50.
- **Muscle involvement** — [[Muscle Atrophy|muscle atrophy]], [[Weakness]], and tubular aggregate myopathy.
- **CNS** — mild intellectual disability in some patients; [[White Matter|white matter]] changes on [[MRI]].

## Treatment

- **Creatine supplementation** — [[Creatine Supplementation|Creatine monohydrate]] (0.1–0.3 g/kg/day) helps restore muscle creatine levels and improves muscle strength and function \[[^218],[^219],[^220],[^221],[^222]\].
- **Arginine restriction** — dietary restriction of [[Arginine]] (to reduce ornithine production) combined with low-protein diet.
- **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6)** — some OAT mutations are responsive to pharmacological doses of [[Vitamin B6|pyridoxine]].
- **Novel therapies** — [[Gene therapy]] and [[Enzyme replacement therapy|enzyme replacement]] are in preclinical development.
