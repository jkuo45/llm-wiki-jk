# Longevity
Longevity refers to the capacity of an organism to survive beyond the average lifespan, often influenced by a combination of genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors.
Longevity refers to a long duration of individual life. It is influenced by genetic and environmental factors, and research into [[Aging]] often focuses on mechanisms to promote longevity, such as reducing [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]].
## Hallmarks of Aging
The landmark framework by [[Carlos López-Otín]] et al. (2013, *Cell* 153(6):1194–1217; updated 2023, *Cell* 186(2):243–278) categorizes aging into twelve interconnected hallmarks:
1. **Genomic instability**: Accumulation of [[DNA Damage]] and [[Mutations]].
2. **Telomere attrition**: Progressive shortening of [[Telomeres]].
3. **Epigenetic alterations**: Changes in [[DNA Methylation]], [[Histone Modification]], and [[Chromatin Remodeling]].
4. **Loss of proteostasis**: Decline in [[Autophagy]], [[Unfolded Protein Response]], and [[Proteasome]] function.
5. **Disabled macroautophagy**: Impaired [[Autophagy]] contributes to aggregate accumulation.
6. **Mitochondrial dysfunction**: [[ETC]] defects, [[Redox imbalance]], [[Mitochondrial Dynamics]] fragmentation.
7. **Cellular senescence**: Accumulation of [[Senescent cells]] and [[SASP]] propagation.
8. **Stem cell exhaustion**: Decline in regenerative capacity of [[Stem cells]].
9. **Altered intercellular communication**: [[Inflammaging]], [[Immunosenescence]], [[Neuroendocrine dysregulation]].
10. **Deregulated nutrient sensing**: [[IIS]] pathway, [[mTOR]], [[AMPK]], [[Sirtuins]].
11. **Chronic inflammation**: Persistent low-grade inflammation.
12. **Dysbiosis**: Alterations in [[Gut microbiota]] composition.
Each hallmark serves as both a contributor to and a consequence of the aging process, with extensive interconnectivity.
## Mechanisms of Lifespan Extension
### [[TOR]] Inhibition
Reduced [[mTORC1]] signaling and [[IGF1]] pathway extends lifespan in all model organisms tested:
- [[Rapamycin]] extends murine lifespan even when started late in life.
- [[Dietary Restriction]] suppresses mTOR via [[AMPK]] and [[Sestrin]]. [[S6K1]] knockout mice show female-specific lifespan extension.
### [[AMPK]] Activation
AMPK functions as a cellular energy sensor that promotes catabolism and inhibits anabolism:
- [[Metformin]] activates AMPK via mild [[Complex I]] inhibition and [[AMP:ATP ratio]] elevation.
- [[AICAR]] (ZMP) directly activates AMPK; chronic administration extends lifespan in *C. elegans*.
- AMPK phosphorylates [[PGC1-α]], [[ULK1]] (autophagy initiation), [[TSC2]] (mTOR suppression), and [[FOXO]].
### Sirtuin Activation
The [[Sirtuins]] family ([[SIRT1]]–[[SIRT7]]) links [[NAD+]] availability to longevity:
- [[SIRT1]] deacetylates [[PGC1-α]], [[FOXO3a]], [[p53]], and [[NFκB]].
- [[SIRT3]] and [[SIRT4]] regulate mitochondrial metabolism and [[UPRmt]].
- [[NAD+]] precursors ([[Nicotinamide Riboside]], [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide]]) improve sirtuin activity and extend healthspan in rodents.
### [[Mitohormesis]]
Mild mitochondrial stress triggers adaptive responses that extend lifespan:
- [[Mitohormetic]] signaling via [[UPRmt]], [[Retrograde Response]], and [[Mitokines]].
- Lowered ETC activity (e.g., [[isp-1]], [[clk-1]], [[nuo-6]] mutants in *C. elegans*) extends lifespan via ROS signaling.
- [[Mitophagy]] induction by [[Urolithin A]] and [[Actinonin]] maintains mitochondrial quality.

## Interventions
### [[Dietary Restriction]]
The most robust lifespan-extending intervention across taxa:
- **[[Caloric Restriction]]** (20–40% reduction): Extends lifespan in rodents, primates, and likely humans.
- **[[Intermittent Fasting]]**: Periodic fasting without caloric restriction may confer similar benefits.
- **[[Protein restriction]]**: Low protein intake (particularly [[Methionine]] and [[Branched-chain amino acids]]) reduces [[mTORC1]] and [[IIS]] signaling.
- **[[Time-restricted feeding]]**: Circadian alignment of feeding windows.
### Exercise
Physical activity extends healthspan and reduces all-cause mortality:
- Induces [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]], [[Autophagy]], [[Antioxidant]] defense.
- Improves [[Insulin Sensitivity]], [[Cardiovascular function]], [[Cognitive function]].
- [[HIIT]] and [[Endurance training]] engage distinct but overlapping longevity pathways.
### Pharmacological
- **[[Metformin]]**: TAME (Targeting Aging with Metformin) trial; reduces all-cause mortality in diabetics.
- **[[Rapamycin]]**: Extends lifespan in mice; [[Everolimus]] improves immune function in elderly.
- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Extends lifespan in model organisms; mixed results in humans.
- **[[Urolithin A]]**: Improves [[Mitochondrial function]] and muscle strength.
- **[[Senolytics]]**: [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]] clearance of senescent cells rejuvenates aged tissues.
## Connection to Redox Homeostasis and Mitohormesis
Longevity is intimately connected to cellular redox state:
- Moderate elevations in [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) activate adaptive pathways.
- Excessive ROS accelerates telomere attrition, mitochondrial dysfunction, and senescence.
- The [[Glyco-Oxidative Proteostasis Shield]] strategy leverages these principles by using controlled [[Redox Cycling]] (via [[Carbazochrome]]) to maintain the hormetic window while clearing damaged proteins and organelles.
- [[Longevity]] may depend less on minimizing oxidative damage than on optimizing adaptive responses to redox challenges — what might be termed "redox robustness."
