# G3BP1

G3BP1 (Ras-GTPase-activating protein SH3-domain-binding protein 1) is a multifunctional RNA-binding protein best known as the principal scaffold of stress granules. In senescence, G3BP1 assists [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS]] recognition of and binding to cytoplasmic chromatin fragments (CCFs), an event essential for cGAS–STING activation and downstream SASP induction.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

G3BP1 contains an N-terminal nucleic-acid-binding domain, a central RGG RNA-binding region, and a C-terminal RhoGAP domain. Under stress it nucleates stress granules via prion-like low-complexity regions and LLPS. In senescent cells, persistent DNA damage and nuclear envelope rupture release CCFs into the cytosol; G3BP1 binds these DNA-coated structures and acts as a co-factor that promotes cGAS oligomerization and stable engagement with double-stranded DNA, lowering the activation threshold. This synergizes with [[TOP1]] cleavage complexes, which also license cGAS at CCFs. Activated cGAS produces cGAMP, engaging STING to trigger TBK1/IRF3 and NF-κB signaling — the type-I interferon and NF-κB arms of the SASP.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

G3BP1 is required for cytosolic-DNA-driven SASP: loss of G3BP1 attenuates cGAS–STING signaling and the attendant interferon/chemokine secretion, while stress-granule assembly itself can concentrate and protect senescence-associated transcripts. By bridging nuclear dysfunction (CCF formation) to innate immune signaling, G3BP1 positions the cytoplasmic DNA–cGAS axis as a core upstream trigger of the SASP, especially in oncogene- and therapy-induced senescence. It also intersects with p53 and Ras pathways through its GAP and stress-responsive functions.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- G3BP1 is targeted by viruses (e.g., poliovirus, flaviviruses) that cleave it to block stress granules — relevant to antiviral and oncolytic strategies.
- Interfering with G3BP1/cGAS co-activation may dampen SASP in [[Senescent Cells]] and reduce [[Inflammaging]].
- G3BP1 dysregulation is implicated in neurodegeneration (stress-granule pathology) and cancer metastasis.
