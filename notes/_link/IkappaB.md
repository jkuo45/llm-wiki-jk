# IkappaB

**IκB** (inhibitor of NF-κB) proteins are a family of cytoplasmic inhibitors that sequester [[NFκB]] transcription factors in the [[Cytosol]], preventing their nuclear translocation. The best-characterized member is [[IkappaBalpha]] (encoded by [[NFKBIA]]), along with [[IκBβ]], [[IκBε]], and the precursor proteins [[p105]] ([[NFKB1]]) and [[p100]] ([[NFKB2]]).

## Mechanism

IκB proteins contain multiple [[Ankyrin Repeat]] domains that bind the [[Nuclear Localization Signal]] (NLS) of NF-κB [[Rel]] dimers (p50-[[p65]], p50-RelB, p52-RelB), masking them from nuclear import machinery. [[Canonical NF-κB Activation]] proceeds via [[IKKbeta]]-mediated phosphorylation of IκBα at Ser32/Ser36, leading to [[K48-linked ubiquitination]] and [[Proteasomal Degradation]]. Freed NF-κB enters the [[Nucleus]] and activates target gene transcription. [[IkappaBalpha]] itself is transcriptionally induced by NF-κB, creating a negative [[Feedback Loop]] that terminates the response.

## Family Members

- **[[IkappaBalpha]]** — primary regulator of canonical NF-κB; rapid degradation and resynthesis
- **[[IκBβ]]** — slower kinetics; resistant to resynthesis-induced termination
- **[[IκBε]]** — regulates [[p65]]-[[c-Rel]] dimers
- **[[IκBζ]]** — nuclear IκB; regulates [[IL-6]] transcription
- **[[BCL-3]]** — atypical IκB; can act as a transcriptional coactivator
- **[[p105]]/[[p100]]** — precursor proteins; contain C-terminal IκB-like domains; processed to [[p50]] and [[p52]]
