**Gyrate atrophy** (OMIM #258870) is a rare autosomal recessive chorioretinal degeneration caused by mutations in the _OAT_ gene encoding [[Ornithine Aminotransferase|ornithine aminotransferase]]. The disease is characterized by sharply demarcated circular atrophic lesions in the mid-peripheral retina that progressively enlarge and coalesce, leading to tunnel vision and eventual blindness.

## Pathophysiology

- Deficiency of [[Ornithine Aminotransferase (OAT)|ornithine aminotransferase]] causes accumulation of [[Ornithine]] (10–20× normal plasma levels).
- Elevated ornithine inhibits [[Arginine Glycine Amidinotransferase|AGAT]], reducing endogenous [[Creatine]] synthesis.
- The resulting creatine depletion contributes to [[Muscle Atrophy|muscle atrophy]] and [[Weakness]].

## Clinical Course

- **Childhood onset** (age 5–10): myopia and night blindness are often the first symptoms.
- **Adolescence:** development of characteristic circular chorioretinal atrophic lesions visible on fundoscopy.
- **Adulthood:** progressive constriction of visual fields; legal blindness typically by age 40–50.
- **Associated findings:** posterior subcapsular cataracts, [[Vitreous|vitreous]] opacities, and [[Cystoid Macular Edema|cystoid macular edema]].

## Treatment

- [[Creatine Supplementation|Creatine monohydrate supplementation]] (0.1–0.3 g/kg/day) improves muscle creatine stores, strength, and function.
- [[Arginine]] restriction and low-protein diet reduce ornithine levels.
- [[Vitamin B6|Pyridoxine]] supplementation benefits pyridoxine-responsive mutations.
- Regular ophthalmological monitoring is essential.
