# Biphasic Dose-Response Curve

A **Biphasic Dose-Response Curve** is a non-monotonic concentration–effect relationship in which the local slope changes sign at least once—i.e., the response first increases then decreases (inverted-U) or vice versa (U-shaped). In pharmacology and toxicology this is the defining quantitative signature of [[Hormesis]] and is also called a *hormetic* or *biphasic* curve.

## Definition and Detection

In the high-throughput screening framework of Cerrillo, Vidakovic & Míguez (2026), dose-response curves are classified as:
- **Insensitive**: max–min response difference < 0.01.
- **Monotonic**: slope sign never changes.
- **Hormetic / Biphasic**: a change in the sign of the local derivative occurs [^1].

The curve is generated by recording the steady-state *Output* node activity across a logarithmically spaced range of inhibitor concentrations (e.g., 30 points from 0.05 to 1000).

## Shape and Interpretation

- **Inverted-U (bell) shape**: beneficial/stimulatory at low–intermediate dose, detrimental at high dose. Classic for performance/survival endpoints (e.g., [[Rapamycin]] lifespan/viability, peak ~1 nM).
- **J-shaped**: risk reduction at low dose, risk increase at high dose (mortality/tumor incidence).

The inverted-U reflects **two opposing processes** whose relative weights shift with dose—often the two branches of an [[Incoherent Bivalent Motif]] centered on the drug target.

## Prevalence

A systematic literature analysis identified ~9000 dose-response relationships classifiable as hormetic—appearing *more frequently* than the typical sigmoidal (monotonic) shape [^1]. Widely used agents showing clear biphasic responses include [[Aspirin]], [[Heparin]], [[Metformin]], [[Curcumin]], and [[Rapamycin]].

## Mechanistic Basis

Rather than a curve-fitting artifact, biphasic dose responses can arise from **network topology**: an incoherent bivalent motif centered on the inhibited target, especially when the backward (Target→Input) interaction operates in the [[Saturated Enzymatic Regime]]. See [[Incoherent Bivalent Motif]].

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- [[Hormetic Window]] — the dose range over which the beneficial (ascending) arm of the curve operates.
- [[IC50]] — the inflection point of a *monotonic* sigmoidal curve; absent or ambiguous for biphasic curves.
- [[Hormesis]] — the biological phenomenon the curve quantifies.
