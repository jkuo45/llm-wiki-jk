# Parkinson Disease

Parkinson Disease (PD) is a neurodegenerative disorder characterized by selective loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra and intracellular α-synuclein aggregates (Lewy bodies). A growing body of evidence links PD to [[Senescence]] of neurons, glia, and peripheral cells, and to a distinguishable circulating SASP signature.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

PD pathogenesis involves progressive dopaminergic neuron loss and α-synuclein misfolding. Senescent astrocytes and microglia mount a pro-inflammatory SASP that includes [[IL-6]] and [[IL-1α]], exacerbating [[Neuroinflammation]] and promoting [[Alpha-synuclein]] spreading. Persistent DNA damage and mitochondrial dysfunction in aging glia further fuel the senescence program, creating a feed-forward loop between proteinopathy and inflammation.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The SASP profile detectable in urinary extracellular vesicles distinguishes older individuals with and without PD, supporting circulating senescence signatures as a non-invasive diagnostic readout (SASP review, PMID 20365073). Senescent cell accumulation in the aging brain and the broader [[Inflammaging]] milieu are hypothesized to accelerate dopaminergic neuron death, placing PD within the spectrum of senescence-associated neurodegeneration rather than a purely cell-autonomous proteinopathy.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Beyond standard dopaminergic replacement, senolytic and senomorphic approaches are proposed to blunt PD-associated neuroinflammation. Inflammaging and SASP biomarkers may aid early detection and patient stratification, and senescence-modifying strategies are an emerging therapeutic direction for neurodegenerative disease.
