# Parkinson Disease

Parkinson Disease is a neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the loss of dopaminergic neurons. The SASP profile in urinary extracellular vesicles can distinguish between older individuals with or without Parkinson disease, indicating a link between cellular senescence and neurodegeneration.

## Mechanism of Action

- Parkinson Disease involves progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons
- The SASP profile in urinary extracellular vesicles differs between patients and healthy individuals
- This indicates a link between cellular senescence and neurodegeneration
- SASP factors may contribute to neuroinflammation in Parkinson Disease

## Role of SASP

- SASP biomarkers in urinary vesicles can distinguish Parkinson Disease patients
- Senescent cells may accumulate in the brain in Parkinson Disease
- SASP factors may contribute to neuroinflammation and disease progression

## Clinical Relevance

- SASP profiling may be a diagnostic tool for Parkinson Disease
- Senolytic or senomorphic therapies may be beneficial in Parkinson Disease
- Urinary SASP profiling is a non-invasive diagnostic approach
