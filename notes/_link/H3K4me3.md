# H3K4me3

H3K4me3 is the trimethylation of lysine 4 on histone H3, a canonical activating mark concentrated at the promoters of actively transcribed genes. In senescence, H3K4me3 dynamics — together with the repressive [[H3K27me3]] — reshape the promoter landscape and influence both SASP genes and the DNA-damage response, as discussed in the npj Aging review (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Definition

H3K4me3 is deposited by SET1-family methyltransferases (MLL1/KMT2A and paralogs) and is recognized by PHD-finger and other reader domains. It associates with open promoters and, together with H3K27me3 "bivalent"/"mesa" states, defines transcriptional poise. In contrast, H3K4me1 marks active enhancers.

## Writer, Reader, Eraser

- **Writer:** [[MLL1]] (KMT2A) and other SET1-complex methyltransferases deposit H3K4me3.
- **Reader:** PHD-finger–containing proteins (e.g., BPTF, ING proteins) and components of the transcription machinery engage H3K4me3.
- **Eraser:** KDM5 (JARID1) demethylases remove H3K4me3.

## Role in SASP and Senescence

A redistribution of H3K27me3 and H3K4me3 strongly impacts gene expression. The two marks co-localize in "mesas" while H3K27me3 is depleted from "canyons" in replicative senescent cells (Chibaya et al., 2023, PMID 37174594). Promoters of actively transcribed genes are enriched in H3K4me3 and H3/H4 acetylation. In senescent genomes, the most notable chromatin transitions occur at enhancers, with promoters only minimally affected (Guan et al., 2020; Martinez-Zamudio et al., 2020). H3.3 clipping upon [[BRD4]] loss is associated with emergence of H3K4me3 at senescence-specific loci, enhancing accessibility (Yang et al., 2023, PMID 36800343).

Functionally, depletion/inhibition of MLL1 reduces [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] expression — but the overall H3K4me3 reduction at SASP loci is moderate and does not fully track the large drop in SASP transcription. Instead, MLL1 inhibition reduces SASP by preventing activation of the [[DNA Damage Response|DNA-damage response (DDR)]] pathway (Capell et al., 2016, PMID 26853365). Thus H3K4me3 operates partly through DDR licensing rather than direct promoter activation of SASP genes.

## Mechanistic Details

H3K4me3 at SASP promoters is generally maintained (promoters are less remodeled than enhancers), so its primary senescence role is in establishing the activating promoter state and, via MLL1, in sustaining DDR that feeds SASP. EZH2 (writer of H3K27me3) antagonism with MLL1 at "mesa"/"canyon" regions tunes the balance between activation and repression.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

MLL1 is a candidate [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] node; its inhibition lowers SASP without fully abolishing arrest (see [[KDM4]], [[BRD4]], [[DOT1L]]).
