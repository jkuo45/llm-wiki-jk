# GABA_A receptor

## Overview

The **[[GABA_A receptor]]** is a pentameric, ligand-gated **ion channel** that mediates the majority of fast inhibitory neurotransmission in the central nervous system. Binding of the endogenous agonist [[GABA]] opens a chloride channel, hyperpolarizing the postsynaptic membrane. Because of its structural and pharmacological heterogeneity (multiple subunit combinations: α, β, γ, δ, ε, π, θ, ρ), it is the target of numerous therapeutics — benzodiazepines, barbiturates, anesthetics, and neuroactive natural products.

## Structure and Subunit Heterogeneity

- Pentameric assembly from subunit classes (most commonly 2×α, 2×β, 1×γ, or δ in extrasynaptic receptors).
- Subunit composition determines pharmacology, localization, and kinetics (e.g., δ-subunit-containing receptors are largely extrasynaptic and mediate tonic inhibition).
- The **benzodiazepine site** resides at the α–γ interface.

## Modulation by Honokiol and Magnolol

[[Honokiol]] and [[Magnolol]] are **positive allosteric modulators (PAMs)** of both synaptic and extrasynaptic GABA<sub>A</sub> receptors. They increase [[Muscimol|[<sup>3</sup>H]muscimol]] binding-site number ~3-fold by allosterically increasing the affinity of low-affinity sites, and show markedly higher efficacy at **δ-subunit-containing** receptors (900–1100% response) than at αβγ-only receptors (300–500%). Their anxiolytic and sleep-promoting effects are mediated at the benzodiazepine site and blocked by [[Bicuculline]] (a GABA<sub>A</sub> antagonist) and flumazenil.

> [!info] Source: [[Neuro-Modulating Effects of Honokiol A Review]]
> Honokiol exerts anxiolysis with a better side-effect profile than [[Diazepam]] and promotes NREM sleep by exciting neurons in the [[Ventrolateral Preoptic Area]], both via positive allosteric modulation of the GABA<sub>A</sub> receptor at the benzodiazepine site.

## Physiological Function

- Primary mediator of inhibitory tone; regulates anxiety, sleep, muscle relaxation, seizure threshold, and cognition.
- Tonic (extrasynaptic, δ-rich) and phasic (synaptic) inhibition serve distinct computational and behavioral roles.

## Pathology and Clinical Relevance

- Dysregulation implicated in [[Epilepsy]], anxiety disorders, insomnia, and sedation under anesthesia.
- Positive allosteric modulators (benzodiazepines, barbiturates, neurosteroids, and neolignans such as honokiol/magnolol) are used therapeutically but carry risks of dependence, sedation, and cognitive side effects.

## Connections

- [[GABA]] — Endogenous orthosteric agonist opening the chloride channel.
- [[Honokiol]] — Positive allosteric modulator; anxiolytic/sleep-promoting via δ-subunit and benzodiazepine site.
- [[Magnolol]] — Shared positive allosteric modulation, generally slightly less potent than honokiol.
- [[Bicuculline]] — Competitive GABA<sub>A</sub> antagonist used to probe receptor function.
- [[Diazepam]] — Benzodiazepine site agonist; comparator for honokiol's anxiolysis.
- [[Ventrolateral Preoptic Area]] — Sleep-promoting neurons excited by honokiol via GABA<sub>A</sub>.
