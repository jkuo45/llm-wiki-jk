# Metabolome

The **metabolome** is the complete set of small-molecule chemicals — metabolic intermediates, cofactors, hormones, and signaling metabolites — present within a biological sample, cell, tissue, or organism at a specific moment. It is the downstream, most phenotypically proximate "ome," reflecting the integrated output of the [[Genome]] (via the transcriptome and proteome) and the continual influence of diet, the [[Gut Microbiome]], drugs, and the environment.

## Composition & Dynamics

Unlike the relatively stable genome, the metabolome is highly dynamic, shifting on timescales of seconds to hours with feeding, exercise, circadian rhythm, and stress. It spans endogenous compounds such as amino acids, organic acids, lipid mediators, nucleotides, and redox cofactors (e.g., [[NAD+]] and [[Glutathione]]) as well as microbially derived products like [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]].

## Measurement

The metabolome is profiled by untargeted or targeted [[Metabolomics]] using mass spectrometry and nuclear magnetic resonance, producing fingerprints that serve as sensitive [[Biomarkers]] of physiological state and disease risk.

## Relevance to Aging & Longevity

Aging remodels the metabolome: alterations in acylcarnitines, branched-chain amino acids, the [[Kynurenine Pathway]], and NAD+ intermediates track with [[Inflammation]], [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome]]. Comparative metabolomic studies of [[Centenarians]] and long-lived cohorts help identify pro-longevity metabolic signatures, positioning the metabolome as a key readout in [[Longevity]] and geroscience research.
