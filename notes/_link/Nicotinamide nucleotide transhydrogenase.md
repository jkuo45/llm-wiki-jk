## Overview

[[Nicotinamide nucleotide transhydrogenase]] (NNT) is a proton-translocating enzyme of the mitochondrial inner membrane that catalyzes the reversible transfer of a hydride ion between NAD(H) and NADP(H): NADPH + NAD+ ⇌ NADH + NADP+. It links redox state to the proton motive force.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

NNT uses the electrochemical proton gradient (generated by the [[Electron Transport Chain]]) to drive the energy-unfavorable reduction of NADP+ by NADH:

- **Reverse (energized) mode**: proton influx powers NADPH formation from NADP+ and NADH — critical for maintaining the NADPH pool used by antioxidant systems ([[Glutathione]], thioredoxin).
- **Forward mode**: can contribute to proton motive force and ATP generation.

Thus NNT integrates [[Mitochondrial Uncoupling]] and respiratory state with cellular antioxidant capacity, sustaining NADPH for detoxification of reactive oxygen species.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

NNT deficiency (e.g., the C57BL/6J mouse Nnt mutation) impairs redox control and glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, linking to metabolic and oxidative stress phenotypes. Human NNT mutations cause familial glucocorticoid deficiency. In the wiki, NNT is relevant to mitochondrial redox resilience and sirtuin-regulated metabolism, supporting antioxidant defense against adrenochrome-related oxidative stress.
