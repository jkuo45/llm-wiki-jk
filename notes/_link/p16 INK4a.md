# p16 INK4a

**p16^INK4a^** (encoded by the [[CDKN2A]] gene, at the INK4a/ARF locus) is a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor and one of the most established markers of [[Cellular Senescence]]. It enforces permanent G1 arrest and, notably, governs a **DDR-free SASP profile (PASP)** distinct from the classical NF-κB–driven secretome (Kumari et al., 2021; PMID 33660965; theoretical foundation in Campisi/Kuilman SASP literature).

## Mechanism of Action

p16 inhibits [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 4|CDK4]] and [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 6|CDK6]], preventing their association with D-type cyclins. This results in:
1. Hypophosphorylation of [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]], p107, and p130
2. Repression of [[E2F]] target genes required for S-phase entry
3. Stable G1 cell-cycle arrest
4. Reinforcement of Rb-dependent chromatin changes (supporting [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]])

## Role in SASP Regulation (PASP)

Unlike the DDR-dependent NF-κB-associated SASP (NASP), p16 overexpression or long-term CDK4/6 inhibition induces a **DDR-free senescent state** lacking pro-inflammatory SASP factors but expressing a unique **p53-dependent SASP profile (PASP)** that includes:
- [[IGFBP3]] (insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 3) — a direct p53 target
- [[ISG15]] — an interferon-stimulated factor
- [[CXCL14]] — recruits macrophages for immunosurveillance
- Type I interferon pathway factors

This p53-arm SASP is distinct from the NF-κB-driven pro-inflammatory SASP and may be essential for senescence-mediated immune clearance rather than tissue damage.

## p21 Cooperation

- p16 supports an early secretory phenotype (CXCL14, IGFBP3) that places stressed cells under immunosurveillance, mediated by [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] activation.
- p16 is essential for expression of these factors in [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]] and therapy-induced senescence (TIS).
- Both p53–[[p21 CIP1|p21]] and p16 pathways regulate the SASP through distinct mechanisms; CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib) engage the p16 arm.

## Senescence Biomarker

p16 is the most widely used senescence biomarker, rising with age in many tissues. Associations include:
- [[Aging]] — p16-positive cells accumulate exponentially with age
- [[Cancer]] — CDKN2A deletion/loss permits escape from arrest (a hallmark of malignancy)
- [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]], [[Osteoarthritis]], [[Cardiovascular Disease]]

Importantly, p16-driven senescence (DDR-free PASP) appears *less* detrimental than NF-κB-associated SASP, arguing that the secretome, not arrest per se, drives pathology.

## Therapeutic Relevance
- **Senolytic targeting**: p16-positive cells can be selectively eliminated by senolytic drugs (e.g., dasatinib + quercetin clear p16^high^ cells).
- **Senomorphic modulation**: CDK4/6 inhibitors induce p16-dependent senescence without NF-κB-driven SASP.
- **Biomarker**: p16 levels correlate with senescent burden and age-related disease; INK4a locus polymorphisms modify risk.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

The INK4a/ARF locus is the most frequently altered region in human cancer (CDKN2A deletion/mutation in melanoma, glioblastoma, pancreatic cancer). In ageing, p16^INK4a^ accumulation in β-cells, T-cells, and endothelium contributes to tissue dysfunction; its clearance improves function in murine models (Baker et al., 2016).
