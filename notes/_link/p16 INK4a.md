# p16 INK4a

**p16^INK4a^** (encoded by the [[CDKN2A]] gene) is a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor that is one of the most well-known markers of [[Cellular Senescence]]. It is primarily associated with enforcing permanent cell cycle arrest and is upregulated in many pathological conditions.

## Mechanism of Action

p16 inhibits [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 4|CDK4]] and [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 6|CDK6]], preventing their association with D-type cyclins. This results in:

1. Hypophosphorylation of [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]], p107, and p130
2. Downregulation of [[E2F]] target genes essential for cell cycle progression
3. Stable cell cycle arrest in G1 phase
4. Reinforcement of NF-κB activities in later phases of senescence

## Role in SASP Regulation

Unlike the DDR-dependent NF-κB-associated SASP (NASP), p16 overexpression or long-term CDK4/6 inhibition induces a **DDR-free senescent state** without pro-inflammatory SASP factors but with a unique **p53-dependent SASP profile (PASP)** that includes:

- [[IGFBP3]] (insulin-like growth factor-binding protein 3) — a direct p53 target gene
- [[ISG15]] — an interferon pathway factor
- [[CXCL14]] — promotes immunosurveillance by recruiting macrophages
- Type I interferon pathway factors

This p53-arm SASP profile is distinct from the NF-κB-driven pro-inflammatory SASP and may be essential for senescence-mediated immune surveillance.

## p21 Cooperation

- p16 supports an early secretory phenotype that includes CXCL14 and IGFBP3, placing stressed cells under immunosurveillance, mediated by [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] activation
- p16 is essential for expression of these SASP factors in [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]] and therapy-induced senescence (TIS) cells
- Both p53–p21 and p16 pathways directly regulate the SASP through distinct mechanisms

## Senescence Biomarker

p16 is the most well-known senescence biomarker, associated with many pathological conditions including:
- [[Aging]] — p16-positive cells accumulate with age
- [[Cancer]] — loss of p16 allows escape from senescence
- [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]]
- [[Osteoarthritis]]
- [[Cardiovascular Disease]]

Notably, cells induced to senescence through p16 overactivation do not promote disease, suggesting that the p16-dependent SASP profile may be less detrimental than the NF-κB-associated SASP.

## Therapeutic Relevance

- **Senolytic targeting**: p16-positive cells can be selectively eliminated by senolytic drugs
- **Senomorphic modulation**: CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib) induce p16-dependent senescence without NF-κB-driven SASP
- **Biomarker**: p16 expression levels correlate with senescent cell burden and age-related disease
