# GM-CSF

**GM-CSF (granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor, CSF2)** is a hematopoietic cytokine that is part of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]]. It is measured as a circulating biomarker of senescent-cell burden and of senolytic-therapy efficacy in clinical studies.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

GM-CSF binds the heterodimeric GM-CSFR, composed of a specific α-chain (CD116) and the common β-chain (βc/CD131, shared with IL-3 and IL-5). Receptor engagement activates [[JAK]]2 (and to a lesser extent JAK1/TYK2), leading to phosphorylation of [[STAT5]] (and STAT3), plus [[MAPK]] and [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] pathways. GM-CSF is a principal myeloid growth and activation factor: it stimulates dendritic-cell, macrophage, and granulocyte proliferation, differentiation, and functional activation (including ROS production and antigen presentation). In senescent tissues, GM-CSF secreted by [[Senescent Cells]] acts as a chemoattractant and activator that recruits monocytes/macrophages and neutrophils into the microenvironment.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- GM-CSF is a secreted SASP component that drives myeloid recruitment and innate immune infiltration.
- It contributes to the immune-modulating arm of the SASP, helping bridge senescence to inflammation and to immune surveillance.
- Circulating GM-CSF levels fall after [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic]] treatment, making it a pharmacodynamic biomarker in trials (e.g., diabetic kidney disease).

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance
- GM-CSF is a practical blood biomarker of senescence burden and senolytic response.
- Recombinant GM-CSF is used clinically to accelerate neutrophil recovery after chemotherapy.
- Modulating GM-CSF may tune SASP-driven myeloid inflammation without eliminating senescent cells (a [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] approach).
