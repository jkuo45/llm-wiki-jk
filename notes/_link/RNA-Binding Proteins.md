# RNA-Binding Proteins

## Overview

RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) are a large and diverse class of proteins that interact with RNA to govern its processing, stability, localization, and translation. They operate throughout the cell but are especially prominent in the [[Nucleus]] and in [[Stress Granules]]. RBPs are recurrently enriched among the interaction partners of [[cGAS]], reflecting a deep and previously underappreciated connection between DNA-sensing machinery and RNA metabolism that is central to the noncanonical functions of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]].

## cGAS Scaffolding of RBPs

A striking finding from cGAS interactome studies is that nuclear [[cGAS]] scaffolds RBPs via its [[Intrinsically Disordered Region|intrinsically disordered region]], leveraging [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|phase separation]] to maintain nuclear homeostasis. Among the RBPs directly engaging cGAS, G3BP1 — a core component of [[Stress Granules]] — binds the cGAS IDR and primes cGAS for DNA binding, while [[PQBP1]] acts as an adaptor linking monomeric [[Tau]] to cGAS activation in tauopathy. These interactions position cGAS as a conformational hub that organizes RNA-processing proteins within phase-separated nuclear assemblies, suggesting roles in transcription and splicing regulation.

## RBP Mislocalization and Neurodegeneration

When RBPs become mislocalized or aggregated, they drive profound pathology. [[TDP-43]] is a canonical example: under stress it redistributes into cytoplasmic aggregates and [[Stress Granules]], losing nuclear function and gaining toxicity in [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]] and frontotemporal dementia. Many RBPs are thereby linked to [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]], and their sequestration by pathological protein condensates (e.g., tau, polyQ) further disrupts RNA metabolism. Because cGAS scaffolds RBPs to preserve homeostasis, perturbation of this noncanonical axis may contribute to the [[Neuroinflammation]] and neuronal dysfunction that characterize chronic brain disorders.
