# Barrier-to-Autointegration Factor 1

## Overview

Barrier-to-autointegration factor 1 (BAF1, encoded by BANF1) is a small, highly conserved chromatin-associated protein that binds DNA, [[Histone|histones]], and components of the [[Nuclear Envelope]]. It plays essential roles in nuclear architecture, [[Nucleus|nuclear]] envelope integrity, and chromatin organization. In the context of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], BAF1 has emerged as a critical regulator of [[cGAS]] localization and activity at the nuclear periphery.

## Regulation of cGAS Localization and Activity

BAF1 dynamically outcompetes [[cGAS]] for DNA binding and restricts cGAS activity, thereby helping prevent aberrant activation of the DNA-sensing machinery. cGAS binds the [[Nuclear Envelope]] and chromatin in part through interactions with BAF1, which positions cGAS appropriately within the nucleus and constrains its enzymatic output. This competitive relationship means that BAF1 acts as a gatekeeper, ensuring that nuclear cGAS remains tethered and restrained under homeostatic conditions rather than launching spurious [[Type I Interferon|interferon]] responses.

## BAF1 Loss and Senescence-Driven cGAS-STING

A particularly disease-relevant insight is that loss of BAF1 in [[Cellular Senescence|senescent cells]] promotes cGAS-STING activation via micronuclear envelope rupture. When the nuclear envelope is compromised, chromatin and cytosol mix, freeing cGAS to sense DNA and trigger [[STING]]-dependent inflammation. This mechanism links nuclear envelope integrity — governed by BAF1 — to the chronic [[Inflammation]] and [[Barrier-to-Autointegration Factor 1|SASP]] that characterize aging and [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]]. BAF1 thus sits at the interface of nuclear structure and innate immune signaling.
