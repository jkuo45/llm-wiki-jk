# Oxaloacetate

**Oxaloacetate (OAA)** is a four-carbon α-keto dicarboxylic acid intermediate of the [[Citric Acid Cycle|citric acid cycle]]. It condenses with acetyl-CoA to form citrate and is regenerated from [[Malate]] by [[Malate Dehydrogenase|malate dehydrogenase]] (producing NADH). In the [[Malate-Aspartate Shuttle|malate-aspartate shuttle]], oxaloacetate is the cytosolic product regenerated after malate is oxidized in the matrix.

## Metabolic Role

- **TCA entry point**: OAA + acetyl-CoA → citrate initiates each turn of the cycle.
- **Redox couple**: The OAA↔malate equilibrium, mediated by MDH and [[NAD+]]/[[NADH]], ties the cycle to cytosolic-mitochondrial redox balance.

## Relevance to Senescence

- The oxaloacetate–malate node is where cytosolic [[NAD+]] levels are set: lowered [[MDH1]] activity or blocked shuttle flux (via [[Aminooxyacetate]]) depresses the cytosolic NAD+/NADH ratio and promotes senescence.
- OAA is also a substrate for FAH domain-containing protein 1 (FAHD1), an oxaloacetate decarboxylase implicated in metabolic regulation (referenced in the mitochondrial-dysfunction senescence literature).

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: Cites FAHD1 as an oxaloacetate decarboxylase (PMID 25575590) and positions oxaloacetate within the MDH-catalyzed equilibrium governing NAD+/NADH in senescence.
