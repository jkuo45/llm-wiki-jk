# Hematopoiesis

**Overview:** [[Hematopoiesis]] is the process by which all blood cells are generated from [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cells]] (HSCs). It occurs primarily in the bone marrow and continues throughout life to replace short-lived cells.

**Mechanism and regulation:** HSCs self-renew and differentiate along myeloid ([[Macrophages]], [[Neutrophils]], erythrocytes, megakaryocytes) and lymphoid lineages. Lineage commitment is orchestrated by [[Transcription|transcription factors]], cytokines, and [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] modifiers within the [[Stem Cell Exhaustion|stem cell niche]]. Metabolic state and nutrient sensing (mTOR, AMPK) further tune output.

**Physiological function:** Hematopoiesis sustains oxygen carriage, immunity, and hemostasis, and adapts cell output to infection, bleeding, and stress.

**Aging and disease:** With [[Aging]], HSC function declines—a manifestation of [[Stem Cell Exhaustion]]—leading to skewed myelopoiesis, impaired lymphopoiesis, and diminished regenerative capacity. This contributes to immunodeficiency, anemia, and myeloid malignancy risk, and is intertwined with [[Inflammation]] (inflammaging) and genomic instability.
