# Double-Strand Break

A **Double-Strand Break (DSB)** is a form of [[DNA Damage|DNA damage]] in which both phosphodiester backbones of the DNA duplex are cleaved. DSBs are among the most cytotoxic lesions, as unrepaired breaks can cause chromosomal rearrangements, aneuploidy, and cell death.

## Role in the RCM Hypothesis

Within the [[Relocalization of Chromatin Modifiers (RCM) Hypothesis]], DSBs are the principal trigger for the recruitment of [[Chromatin Modifiers|chromatin modifiers]] — including [[SIRT1]], [[SIRT6]], [[HDAC1]], and [[PARP1]] — away from their native genomic loci to damage sites. Repeated cycles of DSB-induced relocalization are proposed to erode the [[Epigenetic Landscape|epigenetic landscape]] over time.

## Repair and Experimental Induction

- DSBs are repaired primarily by homologous recombination and non-homologous end joining.
- The [[ICE Mouse|ICE mouse]] uses the endonuclease [[I-PpoI]] to induce targeted DSBs *without* introducing mutations, isolating the epigenetic consequences of repair from mutational load.
- DSBs advance the [[Epigenetic Clock|epigenetic clock]], plausibly via effects on [[DNA Methylation|DNA methylation]] machinery ([[TET Enzymes|TETs]], [[DNMT1|DNMTs]]).
