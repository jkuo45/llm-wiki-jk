# Stomatitis

[[Stomatitis]] is inflammation and ulceration of the mucous membranes of the mouth (and, more broadly, the oral cavity and digestive tract when termed mycositis/mucositis). It is a characteristic, often dose-limiting adverse event of [[mTOR]] inhibitor therapy.

## Rapamycin-Associated Stomatitis

At high or chronic doses, [[Rapamycin]] and [[Rapalog|rapalogs]] cause [[Stomatitis]] and mucositis—ulceration of the oral and gastrointestinal mucosa ([Peterson et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27334013/)). Stomatitis was among the most common adverse events in the EXIST-3 trial of everolimus for tuberous sclerosis complex–associated seizures, alongside infections, [[Hyperlipidemia]], and cytopenias ([French et al., 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27613521/)). These effects are classified as "manageable" in trial settings but may be less tolerable in healthy individuals using rapamycin for anti-aging or aesthetic purposes ([Roark & Iffland, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12226543/)).

> [!warning] Scheduling mitigation
> For longevity use, rapamycin may be administered intermittently (e.g., weekly) or at low daily doses and discontinued if stomatitis or other unpleasant effects occur ([Blagosklonny, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6814615/)).
