# Heat Shock Proteins

**Heat shock proteins ([[Heat Shock Proteins|HSPs]])** are a conserved family of molecular chaperones induced by proteotoxic, thermal, oxidative, and exercise stress. Major families include small HSPs ([[HSP27]]), HSP60/70 ([[HSP70]]), and HSP90 ([[Hsp90]]), whose expression is coordinated transcriptionally by heat shock factor [[HSF1]].

## Function

HSPs bind exposed hydrophobic regions of nascent or stress-denatured proteins, preventing aggregation and refolding them to their native state. They thereby maintain proteostasis and protect [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] and other organelles from stress-induced damage.

## Mitohormetic Relevance

Sauna use (15–20 min) induces HSPs, which repair misfolded proteins and support mitochondrial function — a hormetic heat stress that, with recovery, enhances resilience. This complements other mitohormetic stimuli such as [[Mitophagy]] and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]].
