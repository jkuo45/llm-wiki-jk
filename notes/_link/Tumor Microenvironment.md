## Overview

The [[Tumor Microenvironment]] (TME) is the complex ecosystem surrounding and interacting with tumor cells, composed of stromal cells (fibroblasts, endothelial cells, pericytes), immune cells (T cells, NK cells, macrophages, dendritic cells, myeloid-derived suppressor cells), extracellular matrix (ECM) components, soluble factors (cytokines, chemokines, growth factors), and extracellular vesicles. The TME is not a passive bystander but an active participant in tumor initiation, progression, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance.

## Composition

- **Immune cells**: [[CD8 T cells]], [[Natural Killer Cells]], [[Macrophages]], [[Dendritic cells]], [[Regulatory T cells]], [[Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells]]
- **Stromal cells**: Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), endothelial cells, pericytes
- **Extracellular matrix**: [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|MMPs]], collagen, laminin, fibronectin
- **Soluble factors**: Cytokines ([[IL-6]], [[IL-1β]], [[TNF-alpha]]), chemokines ([[CXCR2]], [[CCL2]]), growth factors ([[VEGF]])
- **Metabolic milieu**: Adenosine, [[CD73]], lactate, reactive oxygen species

## Senescence and the TME

[[Senescent Cells]] and their [[SASP]] are major modifiers of the TME. SASP factors reshape the TME in context-dependent ways:

### Pro-Tumor Remodeling
- SASP promotes immunosuppressive TME through recruitment of [[Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells]] and M2 macrophages
- IL-6 from senescent cells elevates adenosine via [[CD73]]/JAK/[[STAT3]] signaling, impairing T cell infiltration
- SASP-induced [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|EMT]] promotes tumor cell invasiveness
- [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|MMPs]] remodel ECM to facilitate invasion and metastasis
- SASP-induced angiogenesis provides nutrients for tumor growth

### Anti-Tumor Remodeling
- SASP recruits immune cells for [[Senescence Surveillance]] of damaged and pre-malignant cells
- Cell cycle arrest enforced by SASP factors limits proliferation of precancerous cells
- IFN-γ synergizes with SASP to enhance antigen presentation and immune surveillance

> [!info] Source: Dong et al. 2024
> The impact of SASP on the TME varies significantly depending on the type of cells undergoing senescence and the specific triggers of senescence. In melanoma, SASP-associated cytokines support immune surveillance; in HCC, hepatic SASP promotes tumor progression through macrophage polarization.

## Therapeutic Implications

Targeting the TME through senescence-modulating therapies represents a promising approach:

- **Senolytics** ([[Senolytic]]): Eliminate senescent cells to reverse detrimental TME remodeling
- **Senomorphics** ([[Senomorphic]]): Suppress SASP without killing senescent cells, reducing TME inflammation
- **Immune checkpoint inhibitors**: Anti-[[PD-L1]]/anti-PD-1 therapies combined with senolytics to overcome SASP-mediated immunosuppression
- **CD73 inhibition**: Targets adenosine-mediated immune evasion in the senescent TME

## Connections

- [[SASP]] — Senescence-associated secretory phenotype is the primary driver of TME remodeling by senescent cells
- [[Senescent Cells]] — Sources of SASP factors that reshape the TME
- [[Senescence Surveillance]] — Immune-mediated clearance of senescent cells within the TME
- [[IL-6]] — Key SASP cytokine that reshapes TME through STAT3/adenosine signaling
- [[CD73]] — Ecto-5'-nucleotidase upregulated by SASP; metabolic immune checkpoint in TME
- [[PD-L1]] — Immune checkpoint molecule upregulated in the senescent TME
- [[Macrophages]] — Polarized by SASP; can be pro- or anti-tumorigenic depending on context
- [[Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells]] — Immunosuppressive cells recruited by SASP to the TME
- [[Angiogenesis]] — SASP promotes neo-vascularization in the TME
- [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition]] — SASP-driven EMT within the TME promotes tumor invasiveness
