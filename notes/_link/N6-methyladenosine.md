# N6-methyladenosine

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) is the most abundant internal post-transcriptional modification of eukaryotic messenger RNA, consisting of a methyl group at the N6 position of adenosine. It is deposited co-transcriptionally by the [[METTL3]]–[[METTL14]] writer complex, removed by FTO and ALKBH5 demethylases, and interpreted by YTH-domain reader proteins. Although best known as a regulator of RNA metabolism, the m6A pathway intersects with the epigenetic landscape that orchestrates [[SASP]] expression in senescence.

## Overview

m6A occurs on roughly 0.1–0.4% of adenosines in mammalian mRNA, enriched near stop codons, 3′ UTRs, and long internal exons. The methylation influences mRNA splicing, stability, nuclear export, localization, and translation, thereby shaping the transcriptomic output of cells under stress, differentiation, and aging.

## Biological Roles

The writer complex METTL3–METTL14, with its accessory subunits (WTAP, VIRMA, ZC3H13, RBM15), deposits m6A in a sequence- and structure-dependent manner (consensus DRACH motif). Reader proteins (YTHDF1/2/3, YTHDC1/2) bind m6A and dictate downstream fates — YTHDF2 promotes decay, YTHDC1 influences splicing and export. Dynamic m6A turnover thus acts as a rapid post-transcriptional rheostat for gene expression.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The source review emphasizes a non-canonical, catalytic-independent dimension of the m6A writer machinery: in senescence, [[METTL3]]–METTL14 redistributes genome-wide and accumulates at enhancers of SASP genes, where it promotes enhancer–promoter looping and SASP transcription in an activity-independent manner (ref. 48). This positions the m6A complex as a structural participant in 3D-genome reorganization rather than solely an RNA modifier. Separately, m6A levels on SASP-related transcripts can modulate their stability and translation, adding a post-transcriptional layer to secretome control. The broader RNA-modification machinery therefore contributes to both the transcriptional (3D chromatin) and post-transcriptional regulation of the senescent phenotype.

## Therapeutic Relevance

m6A modulators (METTL3 inhibitors such as STM2457; FTO inhibitors) are pursued in oncology. Given that [[Senomorphic|senomorphics]] aim to dampen SASP without killing senescent cells, the m6A pathway — particularly the EP-looping function of its writer complex — represents a plausible, still unexplored senomorphic lever.
