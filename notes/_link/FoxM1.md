# FoxM1

**Overview:** [[FoxM1]] (Forkhead box protein M1) is a member of the Fox family of transcription factors that is normally expressed in proliferating cells and is largely absent in quiescent and most differentiated adult tissues. It is a master regulator of the cell cycle.

**Structure and domains:** FoxM1 contains an N-terminal repression domain, a central DNA-binding forkhead domain, and a C-terminal transactivation domain whose phosphorylation by CDK–cyclin complexes licenses transcriptional activity.

**Mechanism of action and pathways:** FoxM1 transactivates genes required for G1/S and G2/M progression (e.g., cyclin B1, Aurora B, Plk1, Cdc25), promotes DNA replication and repair, and supports mitotic spindle integrity. It is inhibited by the CDK inhibitor p27Kip1 and antagonized by tumor suppressors. FoxM1 also intersects stress and longevity pathways: silencing FoxM1 promotes [[Apoptosis]] and [[Autophagy]] through the [[SIRT7]]/mTOR/IGF2 axis in gastric cancer cells, linking sirtuin signaling to proliferation control.

**Physiological function:** FoxM1 is essential for tissue regeneration, trophoblast development, and renewal of epithelia and hematopoietic cells.

**Pathology and clinical relevance:** FoxM1 is overexpressed in a broad range of carcinomas and correlates with proliferation, chemoresistance, and poor prognosis. It also promotes cellular senescence bypass and EMT. Because of its oncogenic role, FoxM1 is pursued as a therapeutic target, though its physiological indispensability complicates inhibition.
