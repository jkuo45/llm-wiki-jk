# Traumatic Brain Injury

**Traumatic brain injury (TBI)** is structural or functional brain damage caused by mechanical force — such as falls, blows, or blast exposure. It ranges from mild concussion to severe, life-threatening injury with prolonged unconsciousness.

## Pathophysiology
The primary insult (direct trauma) is followed by a secondary injury cascade: excitotoxicity from glutamate release, ionic imbalance, [[Oxidative Stress]] with [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] generation, neuroinflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and [[Apoptosis]] of neurons. Disruption of the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] permits edema and immune infiltration, worsening outcomes. These processes make the injured brain vulnerable for hours to days after impact.

## Clinical Presentation and Biomarkers
Mild TBI presents with headache, confusion, and transient memory loss; severe TBI shows prolonged altered consciousness, pupillary abnormality, and focal deficits. Diagnosis relies on clinical scales (Glasgow Coma Scale) and neuroimaging (CT/MRI). Serum biomarkers such as glial fibrillary acidic protein and ubiquitin C-terminal hydrolase-L1 are emerging adjuncts.

## Therapeutic Landscape
Acute care targets intracranial pressure control, oxygenation, and seizure prophylaxis; no disease-modifying drug is approved. [[Methylene blue]] is an investigational neuroprotective agent in TBI, attributed to [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain]] bypass, reduced [[Mitochondrial Superoxide]], and inhibition of [[Neuroinflammation]]. [[Creatine]] and [[Urolithin A]] are also explored for mitochondrial support after injury.

> [!info] Research Direction
> Mitochondria-targeted agents that limit the secondary injury cascade are a major focus of TBI neuroprotection research.
