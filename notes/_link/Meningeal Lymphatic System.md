# Meningeal Lymphatic System

## Overview

The [[Meningeal Lymphatic System]] comprises dural lymphatic vessels that line the meninges and provide a dedicated route for drainage of [[Central Nervous System|CNS]] interstitial fluid, antigens, and immune mediators to the cervical lymph nodes. Once considered absent, these vessels are now recognized as essential for brain immune surveillance and for clearing protein aggregates and inflammatory signals from the neuroparenchyma.

## Drainage of cGAS-STING Products

The meningeal lymphatics serve as a conduit for [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]]–derived products, including [[cGAMP]] and cytokines, as well as CNS antigens, transporting them from the brain to peripheral lymphoid tissue. This drainage shapes both innate and [[Adaptive Immune System|adaptive]] immune responses to neurodegeneration. Efficient lymphatic clearance limits the accumulation of inflammatory mediators, whereas impaired drainage permits their buildup within the [[Cerebrospinal Fluid|CSF]] and parenchyma.

## Link to Neurodegeneration and Inflammation

Brain lymphatic dysfunction exacerbates amyloid pathology and worsens [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]]–driven [[Neuroinflammation]], implicating the meningeal lymphatics as a modulator of disease progression in [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]] and related disorders. Supporting meningeal lymphatic function is therefore being explored as a strategy to enhance clearance of pathological proteins and dampen chronic CNS inflammation.
