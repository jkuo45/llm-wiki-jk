# Cross-linking

Cross-linking is the formation of covalent bonds that bridge adjacent protein molecules (or within a single molecule), creating large, inert macromolecular networks. In the context of aging and metabolic disease, the dominant pathological form is **non-enzymatic protein cross-linking** driven by [[Glycation]] and the accumulation of [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs).

## Overview

Cross-linking reactions are familiar in everyday life — they cause cut fruit to toughen and brown, and food proteins to yellow. In the body, the continuous reaction of reducing sugars with the free amino groups of proteins proceeds through Schiff-base and Amadori intermediates to stable cross-links such as [[Glucosepane]] and [[Pentosidine]]. Because sugar–protein adducts persist for months to years, cross-linking accumulates progressively with age.

## Mechanisms and Pathology

- **Extracellular matrix stiffening**: Cross-links between structural proteins such as [[Collagen]] and elastic fibers reduce tissue elasticity. This is a leading mechanism behind [[Arterial Stiffness]], [[Cardiac Hypertrophy]], and senile [[Cataract]].
- **Vascular disease**: AGE cross-linking of vessel-wall matrix, together with glycation of [[LDL]] and [[Very Low Density Lipoprotein]], promotes [[Atherosclerosis]] and impairs blood flow.
- **Diabetic complications**: Cross-linking product levels in diabetics are two to three times those of non-diabetics, implicating cross-linking in nephropathy, retinopathy, and neuropathy.
- **Cancer and immunity**: Cross-linking–mediated damage to the [[Immune System]] and to DNA (via glucose–DNA adducts) is proposed to raise infection susceptibility and malignancy risk.

## Prevention and Reversal

- **Inhibitors** such as [[Aminoguanidine]] and [[Carnosine]] trap reactive carbonyl intermediates and prevent new cross-links from forming.
- **Breakers** such as [[Alagebrium|ALT-711]] (a thiazolium salt) are claimed to cleave pre-existing AGE cross-links, representing a potential "reverse-aging" strategy.
