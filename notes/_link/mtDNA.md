# mtDNA

**Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)** is the ~16.6 kb circular genome encoding subunits of the electron-transport-chain complexes and rRNAs/tRNAs. While essential for oxidative phosphorylation, mtDNA can act as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) when released from mitochondria into the cytosol or extracellular space, triggering innate immune activation.

## Release Mechanisms

- **Major Mitochondrial Outer Membrane Permeabilization (MOMP)**: Typically associated with [[Apoptosis]]; massive [[BAX]]/[[BAK]] pore formation releases mtDNA and apoptogenic factors.
- **[[Minority MOMP]] (miMOMP)**: Sublethal permeabilization by BAX/BAK that releases mtDNA without triggering cell death — the principal route in senescent cells (McArthur et al., 2018; PMID 30257233).
- **Vacuole-like structures / mitophagy defects**: Impaired [[Mitophagy]] allows damaged mitochondria to leak mtDNA.
- **Extracellular export**: mtDNA is found in extracellular vesicles and cell-free in plasma as an ageing biomarker.

## Role in Senescence

In [[Senescent Cells]], [[Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] and miMOMP cause cytosolic mtDNA accumulation. This activates the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] innate-immune sensor (cGAS binds mtDNA, generating cGAMP that activates STING → [[TBK1]]/[[IRF3]] and [[NF-κB]]), driving type I interferons and pro-inflammatory cytokines that are core components of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] (Dougherty et al., 2019/2020; PMID 31624094; Glück et al., 2020).

## Retrograde Signaling

Beyond innate immunity, mtDNA stress engages mitochondrial retrograde signaling (e.g., via [[ATF4]]/CHOP and [[AMPK]]) that remodels nuclear gene expression, contributing to the senescent metabolic state and [[Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction|SAMD]].

## Damage and Depletion in Senescence

Senescent cells accumulate mtDNA mutations, strand breaks, and copy-number alterations. mtDNA depletion or mutation can itself induce a senescence-like arrest, linking mitochondrial genome integrity to the senescence program.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Cytosolic and circulating mtDNA are major drivers of sterile [[Inflammaging]] and age-related diseases (atherosclerosis, [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]], neurodegeneration). Elevated cell-free mtDNA in plasma is a candidate biomarker of senescent burden. Therapeutically, blocking mtDNA release (miMOMP inhibitors) or its sensing (NRTIs such as lamivudine; STING inhibitors) is an active [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] strategy.
