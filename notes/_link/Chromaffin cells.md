# Chromaffin cells

**Overview:** Chromaffin cells are neuroendocrine cells that constitute the principal secretory cell type of the [[Adrenal medulla]], with additional populations in the extra-adrenal paraganglia and sympathetic ganglia of the [[Autonomic Nervous System]]. Derived embryologically from the neural crest, they are effectively modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons that have lost their axons and instead release hormones directly into the circulation, making the adrenal medulla a neuroendocrine transducer.

**Function and secretion:** Chromaffin cells synthesize, store, and release [[Catecholamines]]—principally [[Epinephrine]] (adrenaline) and, to a lesser extent, [[Norepinephrine]]. Catecholamines are packaged with chromogranins and ATP into dense-core secretory granules (the "chromaffin granules"). Upon activation of preganglionic sympathetic fibers releasing acetylcholine, calcium influx triggers exocytosis of granule contents into the bloodstream, mediating the systemic "fight-or-flight" response: increased heart rate, bronchodilation, glycogenolysis, and redistribution of blood flow. The name "chromaffin" derives from the brown staining ("chromaffin reaction") produced when catecholamines are oxidized by chromium salts.

**Biochemistry and adrenochrome relevance:** Within chromaffin granules, the enzyme phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase (PNMT) converts norepinephrine to epinephrine. When epinephrine is oxidized—upon release, exposure to oxidants, or metal-catalyzed reactions—it can form [[Adrenochrome]], an aminochrome oxidation product of catecholamines. This links chromaffin cell biology to oxidative stress chemistry and the adrenochrome hypothesis.

**Pathology:** Neoplastic transformation of chromaffin cells gives rise to pheochromocytomas (adrenal) and paragangliomas (extra-adrenal), tumors that secrete excess catecholamines and cause paroxysmal hypertension, palpitations, headache, and sweating. Some cases are hereditary, linked to mutations in genes such as SDHx, VHL, RET, and NF1.
