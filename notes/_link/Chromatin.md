# Chromatin
**Chromatin** is the complex of DNA and proteins (primarily histones) that packages the genome within the cell nucleus. In young, healthy cells, chromatin is organized into a precise landscape of tightly packed [[Heterochromatin]] (transcriptionally silent) and open [[Euchromatin]] (transcriptionally active). During [[Aging]], this structure undergoes significant remodeling, often referred to as **epigenetic drift**.
### 1. Loss of [[Heterochromatin]]
One of the most consistent hallmarks of aging is the global loss of heterochromatin, known as the **Heterochromatin Loss Model of Aging**.
*   **Mechanism:** Regions of the genome that are normally permanently silenced, such as centromeres and repetitive DNA sequences, become "leaky" and less condensed.
*   **Consequences:** This leads to the activation of [[Transposable Elements]] (jumping genes) and increased [[Genomic Instability]].
*   **[[SAHF]] (Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci):** Paradoxically, while global heterochromatin decreases, senescent cells often form dense, localized clusters of heterochromatin to permanently silence cell-cycle genes.
### 2. Histone Modifications and Loss
Histones are the structural proteins around which DNA is wound to form [[Nucleosome]]s. Aging affects both the quantity and the chemical "tags" on these proteins.
*   **Histone Loss:** The total amount of core histone proteins often decreases with age, leading to a more "open" but disorganized chromatin state.
*   **Altered Marks:** Aging is associated with a reduction in repressive marks (e.g., H3K9me3) and an increase in certain active marks (e.g., H4K16ac) that promote a less stable chromatin structure.
### 3. [[DNA Methylation]] Changes
Predictable shifts in DNA methylation patterns are central to chromatin remodeling during aging.
*   **Global Hypomethylation:** Older cells generally show a decrease in overall DNA methylation, contributing to the activation of repetitive elements.
*   **Focal Hypermethylation:** Conversely, specific promoter regions—often those of tumor suppressor genes—can become hypermethylated and silenced.
*   **[[Epigenetic Clock]]:** These consistent shifts in methylation are used by epigenetic clocks to accurately measure biological age.
### 4. 3D Genome Disorganization
The physical folding of chromatin into loops and domains changes with age.
*   **Loss of Compartmentalization:** The separation between active and inactive regions of the genome blurs.
*   **Structural Defects:** Changes in the proteins that maintain DNA architecture can lead to "miswiring," where enhancers interact with the wrong genes, causing them to turn on or off inappropriately.
