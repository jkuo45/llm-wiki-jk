# Vasopressin

## Overview
[[Vasopressin]] (antidiuretic hormone, AVP) is a posterior-pituitary nonapeptide released from the hypothalamus that acts on renal V2 receptors to conserve water and on V1a receptors for [[Vasoconstriction]]. Beyond fluid balance, it influences [[Cognition]], stress, and [[Neurodegeneration]].

## Synthesis & Release
Synthesized in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei, vasopressin is transported to the posterior pituitary and secreted in response to plasma osmolality and baroreceptor tone. Aging is associated with declining vasopressin neurons and disrupted circadian secretion.

## Mechanism of Action
Via Gq-coupled V1a receptors, vasopressin mobilizes calcium and activates [[MAPK]] and [[PKC]]; via Gs-coupled V2 receptors it raises cAMP and inserts aquaporins. These signals modulate vascular tone, [[Autophagy]], and central arousal. Stress-axis cross-talk links AVP to [[Cortisol]] and sympathetic output.

## Redox, Autophagy & Longevity
Vasopressin signaling interacts with [[mTOR]] and [[Autophagy]] pathways relevant to [[Aging]] and [[Healthspan]]. Its vasoconstrictive action can worsen ischemia–reperfusion and [[Oxidative Stress]] when dysregulated, while balanced tone supports [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] resilience. AVP decline in aging may contribute to [[Neurodegeneration]] and cognitive decline, making it a candidate node for redox and longevity interventions.
