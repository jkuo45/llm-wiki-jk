# PAI-1

**Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1)**, also known as SERPINE1, is a serine protease inhibitor that is a key component of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] and a leading plasma biomarker of the senescent burden.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

PAI-1 is the principal physiological inhibitor of tissue-type (tPA) and urokinase-type (uPA) plasminogen activators, blocking fibrinolysis and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodelling. It is transcriptionally induced by [[TGFβ]], [[p53]], and inflammatory cues, and its expression rises sharply in senescence as part of the SASP program. By inhibiting plasmin generation it also modulates integrin and latent-growth-factor activation, feeding fibrotic and proliferative loops.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

PAI-1 is one of the most robust secreted SASP factors. It promotes senescence in an autocrine/paracrine loop (via plasmin suppression and integrin signalling) and contributes to fibrosis, vascular stiffness, and the pro-coagulant state of aging. In large proteomic meta-analyses of aging cohorts, circulating PAI-1 was identified — together with [[GDF15]], [[RAGE]], [[VEGF]] (VEGF-A), and [[PARC]]/[[CCL18]] — as one of five SASP-related plasma proteins whose levels are strongly associated with all-cause mortality, forming a circulating "senescence signature" (population proteomics studies; SASP review, PMID 20365073).

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Elevated PAI-1 tracks with [[Obesity]], [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Cardiovascular Disease]], and frailty, and predicts mortality. Pharmacologic PAI-1 inhibition (e.g., with TM5441) reduces age-related pathologies in mice, supporting PAI-1 both as a practical senescence biomarker and as a tractable senotherapeutic target.
