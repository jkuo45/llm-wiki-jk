# S100A13

S100A13 is a member of the S100 family of EF-hand calcium-binding proteins that is required for nuclear translocation of cleaved [[IL-1α]] and thereby for initiation of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]].

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

S100A13 forms homodimers/heterodimers that bind Ca2+ and Cu2+. It partners with [[IL-1α]] (and S100A6/A11) to regulate IL-1α localization: it facilitates the copper/calcium-dependent nuclear translocation of cleaved IL-1α, where IL-1α acts as a transcriptional coactivator to induce NF-κB target genes. S100A13 is also released extracellularly — often together with FGF-1/FGF-2 in a Cu2+-dependent complex — where it can function as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) engaging [[Inflammaging]] programs.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

S100A13 is positioned at the apex of the SASP. By driving nuclear IL-1α, it initiates the IL-1α→NF-κB feed-forward loop that induces downstream SASP factors, and extracellular S100A13 adds to the pro-inflammatory milieu (Coppé et al.; SASP review, PMID 20365073). Loss of S100A13 disrupts full SASP development, marking it as a sentinel of the IL-1α–driven senescence program rather than a downstream effector.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

S100A13 is implicated in cancer, wound healing, and inflammatory disease. Its strict dependence on copper/calcium homeostasis suggests that metal chelation or copper modulation could therapeutically reshape the SASP, and S100A13 is a candidate marker of the IL-1α–driven senescence state.
