# IFN-β

**Interferon beta (IFN-β)** is a type I interferon and a central output of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] innate immune pathway in senescent cells. It reinforces the senescent arrest and contributes to the interferon/"senescence-associated" inflammatory secretome.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

Senescent cells accumulate cytosolic DNA—leaked [[mtDNA]] and chromatin fragments—that activates cGAS, which produces cGAMP to engage [[STING]] (TMEM173). STING recruits [[TBK1]], which phosphorylates [[IRF3]] (and IRF7), driving transcription of IFN-β (Dou & Chen, PMID 29211712). Secreted IFN-β acts in autocrine/paracrine fashion through the IFNAR receptor (IFNAR1/IFNAR2), activating [[JAK]]1/TYK2 and the STAT1/STAT2–IRF9 (ISGF3) complex, which induces a broad program of interferon-stimulated genes (e.g., [[ISG15]]). Type I IFN signaling also intersects with NF-κB to amplify the SASP.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- IFN-β is a primary readout of cytosolic DNA–cGAS-STING activation in [[Senescent Cells]].
- It reinforces the senescent state via ISG-mediated arrest and [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]] formation.
- It coordinates innate immune recognition/clearance of senescent cells, but when chronic, the persistent type I IFN signature drives sterile [[Inflammaging]] and tissue dysfunction.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance
- A systemic type I IFN signature is a feature of aging and many age-related diseases.
- STING/IFN-β overactivation is implicated in lupus, atherosclerosis, and senescence-driven pathology.
- Modulating cGAS-STING–IFN-β is a [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] avenue to limit harmful SASP while preserving tumor suppression.
