# Simian Virus 40

**Simian Virus 40 (SV40)** is a non-enveloped [[DNA virus]] belonging to the [[Polyomaviridae]] family. Its small (~5.2 kb) circular double-stranded [[DNA]] genome has been a foundational model in [[Molecular Biology]] for studying [[DNA Replication]], [[Transcription]], [[RNA Splicing]], and cellular transformation.

## Genome and Proteins

SV40 encodes two major proteins:
- **[[Large T Antigen]] (T-ag)** — a multifunctional oncoprotein that drives [[Viral DNA replication]] by binding [[SV40 Origin of Replication]] and recruiting [[DNA Polymerase α-primase]]; inactivates [[p53]] and [[pRb]] family proteins, leading to [[Cellular Transformation]]
- **[[Small T Antigen]] (t-ag)** — binds [[PP2A]], disrupting its regulatory activity and enhancing [[MAPK]] and [[Akt]] signaling

## Historical Significance

SV40 was discovered as a contaminant in [[Polio Vaccine]] (1955–1963). It was instrumental in:
- Discovery of [[DNA Replication]] mechanisms ([[Okazaki Fragments]], [[Replication Fork]])
- Elucidation of [[RNA Splicing]] (SV40 was the system in which splicing was first discovered)
- Identification of [[p53]] (SV40 large T-antigen co-immunoprecipitation led to p53 discovery)
- [[Enhancer]] discovery (SV40 72-bp repeat)
