# Neuroinflammation

## Overview

[[Neuroinflammation]] is the immunological response that occurs within the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]], characterized by activation of brain-resident glial and endothelial cells and infiltration of peripheral immune cells. It is a hallmark of multiple neurological disorders, including [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]] (AD), [[Huntington's Disease|Huntington's disease]] (HD), [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] (PD), and acute brain injuries. While acute, well-contained neuroinflammation supports protection and clearance, sustained and dysregulated signaling has deleterious consequences for [[Neuron|neuronal homeostasis]] and accelerates the progression of [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative disease]]. The [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] pathway has emerged as a central driver of chronic neuroinflammation, bridging proteotoxic and mitochondrial stress to innate immune activation.

## Cellular Effectors

Neuroinflammation is orchestrated by both resident and infiltrating immune populations. Brain-resident cells — [[Microglia]], [[Astrocytes]], and [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]] — undergo pro-inflammatory activation, while peripheral [[Monocytes]], [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]], and [[B Cells|B cells]] infiltrate the parenchyma, particularly when the [[Blood-Brain Barrier|blood-brain barrier]] is compromised. Cytokines and [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs)]] produced by these cells act in a paracrine manner on neighboring neurons, amplifying and propagating inflammatory responses. The phenotype of activated glia is heterogeneous and shaped by upstream cues: sustained [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling and [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]] activation promote neurotoxic states, whereas resolution programs restore homeostatic functions.

## cGAS-STING as a Central Driver

A unifying insight is that many [[Proteinopathy|proteinopathies]] ultimately generate cytosolic dsDNA — predominantly via [[mtDNA]] leakage from [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial stress]] or from [[DNA Damage|genomic DNA damage]] — that activates [[cGAS]] and [[STING]]. This triggers [[IRF3]]- and [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]]-dependent transcription of [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] and pro-inflammatory [[Cytokines]], establishing a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop. Noncanonical activation also occurs: the [[Tau|tau]]–[[PQBP1]] complex engages cGAS through direct protein–protein interaction, bypassing DNA entirely. Although cGAS-STING signaling is predominantly deleterious in chronic disease, it is context dependent — microglial STING preserves hippocampal [[Blood-Brain Barrier|BBB]] integrity during aging, complicating blanket pathway inhibition. Chronic neuroinflammation also intersects with [[Inflammaging]] and [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]], contributing to age-related vulnerability.

## Connections

  - [[cGAS-STING Pathway]]: canonical and noncanonical activation produces IFN-I and cytokines that drive neuroinflammation.
  - [[Microglia]]: primary sensor and effector; chronic activation is a major neurotoxic source.
  - [[Astrocytes]]: adopt neurotoxic A1 states and senesce under cGAS-STING signaling.
  - [[Type I Interferon]]: downstream readout that amplifies glial activation and synapse loss.
  - [[Tauopathy]]: pathogenic tau activates microglial cGAS-STING via PQBP1 and mtDNA.
  - [[Proteinopathy]]: unifying upstream trigger through cytosolic dsDNA generation.
  - [[Blood-Brain Barrier]]: barrier disruption permits leukocyte infiltration, worsening inflammation.
  - [[Inflammaging]]: age-related sterile inflammation that overlaps with neurodegenerative neuroinflammation.
