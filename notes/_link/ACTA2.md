# ACTA2

## Overview

ACTA2 (Smooth Muscle Alpha-Actin, α-SMA) is a cytoskeletal protein and the principal actin isoform in vascular smooth muscle cells. It is a marker of mesenchymal differentiation and myofibroblast activation. ACTA2 expression is upregulated during [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EndoMT), fibrosis, and wound healing, marking the acquisition of a contractile, mesenchymal phenotype.

## Structure & Domains

- 375-amino acid protein with ATP-binding domain
- Monomer that polymerizes into actin filaments
- Incorporates into stress fibers and contractile apparatus
- Interacts with myosin II for contractile function

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- Polymerizes into F-actin filaments that form the contractile apparatus
- Interacts with myosin II to generate contractile force in smooth muscle cells
- Expression regulated by TGF-β/SMAD signaling, serum response factor (SRF), and myocardin
- Upregulated during EndoMT as endothelial cells acquire mesenchymal/myofibroblast phenotype
- Serves as a marker of mesenchymal differentiation in EMT/EndoMT programs

> [!info] Source: CXCL12 → Endothelial Dysfunction Mechanistic Trace
> ACTA2 is upregulated 5.4-fold during CXCL12-driven EndoMT, marking the acquisition of mesenchymal identity. This parallels TGFB1 upregulation and contrasts with CDH5/PECAM1 downregulation, confirming transdifferentiation.

## Physiological Function

- Provides contractile force in vascular smooth muscle cells
- Maintains vascular tone and blood pressure regulation
- Essential for wound healing and tissue repair
- Contributes to cytoskeletal integrity in contractile cell types

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- ACTA2 mutations cause thoracic aortic aneurysms and dissections (TAAD)
- Overexpression marks myofibroblast activation in fibrotic diseases (pulmonary, cardiac, renal fibrosis)
- Upregulation during EndoMT contributes to age-related vascular dysfunction and stiffening
- ACTA2 is a diagnostic marker for smooth muscle cell differentiation in pathology
