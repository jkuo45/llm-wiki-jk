## Overview

[[CD9]] is a member of the tetraspanin superfamily of transmembrane proteins that organize "tetraspanin-enriched microdomains" (TEMs) within the plasma membrane. It associates laterally with other tetraspanins, integrins, and signaling receptors to modulate cell-cell and cell-matrix interactions. CD9 is widely expressed on hematopoietic cells, epithelial cells, and stem cells, and is commonly used as a surface marker for exosomes and certain stem cell populations.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

CD9 functions as a molecular scaffold that concentrates partner proteins into specialized membrane platforms. It participates in:

- **Adhesion and migration** — by regulating integrin affinity and trafficking, CD9 modulates cell motility and platelet aggregation.
- **Fusion and vesicle release** — CD9 facilitates membrane fusion events, including the biogenesis and release of extracellular vesicles (exosomes), and supports sperm-egg fusion.
- **Signaling** — CD9 clustering influences downstream pathways involving [[MAPK]] and [[PI3K-AKT]] signaling, affecting proliferation and survival.

In the context of senescence and immune cell biology, CD9 serves as a marker on myeloid-derived and senescent-associated cell populations, helping distinguish subsets of [[Macrophages]] and other immune cells.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

CD9 dysregulation is implicated in cancer metastasis, where it can act as either a metastasis suppressor or promoter depending on cellular context. It is also relevant to tissue regeneration and stem cell identity. Within the wiki's research on [[SASP]] and senescent cell clearance, CD9 helps define senescent and immune cell phenotypes targeted by ligand-conjugated approaches.
