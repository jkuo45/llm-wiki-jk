## Overview

Mitochondria-ER contact sites (MERCS), also known as mitochondria-associated ER membranes (MAMs), are specialized subdomains where the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] (ER) and [[Mitochondria]] come into close apposition (10–30 nm). These contact sites are not passive tethers but serve as dynamic signaling hubs that regulate [[Calcium]] transfer, [[Lipid Synthesis|lipid synthesis]] and trafficking, [[Autophagy|autophagy]], [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|mitochondrial dynamics]], and inflammatory signaling.

## Role in Cellular Senescence

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease]]
> The number of MERCS increases in [[Cellular Senescence|senescence]]. Artificial linkage of mitochondria to the ER promotes senescence, while the senolytic [[Quercetin]] preserves MERCS to improve mitochondrial morphology and function. MERCS localize fusion/fission machinery, and their increased abundance in senescence may contribute to altered [[Mitochondrial Dynamics]] and Ca^2+^ handling via [[ITPR2]] and [[MCU]].

## Role in Neurodegenerative Disease

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease]]
> In [[Alzheimer's Disease]], MERCS are increased with shorter ER-mitochondria distances, promoting Ca^2+^ accumulation and mPTP activation. Reducing MERCS in the 3xTg-AD mouse model prevents mitochondrial Ca^2+^ overload and reduces Aβ levels. In [[Parkinson's Disease]], α-synuclein dysregulates MERCS, though conflicting evidence exists on whether it promotes or impairs their formation. In [[Huntington's Disease]], increased DRP1-mediated fission in the R6/1 model reduces MERCS and impairs mitochondrial Ca^2+^ handling.

## Connections

- [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] — The partner organelle forming contact sites with mitochondria
- [[Calcium]] — MERCS facilitate efficient Ca^2+^ transfer from ER to mitochondria
- [[ITPR2]] — ER Ca^2+^ release channel localized at MERCS
- [[MCU]] — Mitochondrial Ca^2+^ uptake channel that receives Ca^2+^ at MERCS
- [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore]] — Activated by Ca^2+^ overload from increased MERCS activity
- [[Mitochondrial Dynamics]] — MERCS regulate fission/fusion by localizing DRP1 and other machinery
- [[Cellular Senescence]] — MERCS increase in senescence; artificial linkage promotes senescence
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]] — MERCS are altered in AD, contributing to Ca^2+^ dysregulation
- [[Quercetin]] — Preserves MERCS in senescent cardiomyocytes, improving mitochondrial function
