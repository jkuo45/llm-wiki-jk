# Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor

The **aryl hydrocarbon receptor** (AhR) is a ligand-activated basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) Per-Arnt-Sim (PAS) domain transcription factor belonging to the bHLH-PAS family. It functions as a sensor of both xenobiotic environmental compounds (e.g., dioxins, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons) and endogenous ligands derived from [[Tryptophan]] metabolism.

## Activation and Signaling

In its unliganded state, AhR resides in the cytoplasm in a complex with HSP90, p23, and the immunophilin XAP2/AIP. Upon ligand binding, AhR translocates to the nucleus and heterodimerizes with the aryl hydrocarbon receptor nuclear translocator (ARNT). The AhR-ARNT complex binds xenobiotic response elements (XREs) in target gene promoters.

## Endogenous Ligands

- **[[Kynurenine]]** (Kyn) — a tryptophan metabolite produced by [[IDO1]] and [[TDO]]
- **Kynurenic acid** — downstream [[Kynurenine Pathway]] metabolite
- **6-Formylindolo[3,2-b]carbazole (FICZ)** — a tryptophan photoproduct
- **Indole-3-acetic acid** — a microbial tryptophan metabolite

## Immunological Functions

AhR is a key regulator of immune responses, particularly at mucosal barriers:

- Promotes [[Regulatory T cell|Treg]] differentiation (FoxP3⁺) while suppressing Th17 responses
- Drives [[IDO1]] expression in dendritic cells, creating a feed-forward immunosuppressive loop
- Regulates [[Dendritic Cells|dendritic cell]] function and IL-22 production by innate lymphoid cells (ILC3s)
- Mediates the immunosuppressive effects of [[Kynurenine]] in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]]

## Clinical Relevance

AhR is a drug target for [[Cancer]] immunotherapy (blocking the IDO1-Kyn-AhR axis), autoimmune disease, and chronic [[Inflammation]]. Environmental AhR ligands (dioxins) are potent immunotoxins and carcinogens.
