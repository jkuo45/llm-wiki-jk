# DunedinPACE

DunedinPACE is an epigenetic estimator of the "pace of aging" derived from the longitudinal Dunedin Study birth cohort. Rather than estimating a single biological age, it quantifies how fast a person is aging per calendar year, trained on repeated-measure methylation changes tracked across childhood to midlife. A value of 1.0 indicates average pacing; values above 1.0 denote accelerated aging.

## Construction & Properties

The estimator uses 46 CpG sites whose rate of methylation change best tracks multisystem [[Aging]] decline. Crucially, it predicts later physical and cognitive functioning, facial aging, and incidence of multiple [[Age-Related Diseases]] from a single blood sample, independent of chronological age.

## Relation to Other Clocks

DunedinPACE is complementary to static age clocks such as the [[Horvath Clock]] and [[Hannum Clock]] and to the morbidity-linked [[PhenoAge]]. Where PhenoAge estimates level, DunedinPACE estimates rate — the two together describe both position and trajectory of aging.

## Longevity Relevance

Because it captures rate, DunedinPACE is especially suited to testing whether interventions — [[Caloric Restriction]], [[Rapamycin]], [[Senolytics|senolytics]] — slow the aging process itself, linking directly to [[Healthspan]] and [[Hallmarks of Aging]].

> [!info] Rate vs. Level
> DunedinPACE's unique contribution is measuring *velocity* of aging, enabling shorter intervention trials powered on rate change rather than endpoint morbidity.
