# TIMP1

**TIMP1** (Tissue Inhibitor of Metalloproteinases-1), also known as metalloproteinase inhibitor 1 or erythroid potentiating activity (EPA), is a soluble secreted protein that inhibits matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs). It is a key regulator of [[Extracellular Matrix|ECM]] homeostasis, a pro-fibrotic effector downstream of TGF-β signalling, and a component of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]]. TIMP1 also functions as a plasma protein surrogate in the DNAmGrimAge epigenetic clock, making it a biomarker of biological ageing.

## Structure & Mechanism

- **Protein:** TIMP1 is a ~28 kDa glycoprotein composed of an N-terminal inhibitory domain (~125 residues) and a C-terminal domain. The N-terminal domain binds the catalytic site of target MMPs in a 1:1 stoichiometric complex, blocking substrate access.
- **Solubility:** Unlike [[TIMP3]], which is tethered to the ECM, TIMP1 is soluble and diffusible, allowing it to act on MMPs at distant sites.
- **Primary targets:** TIMP1 preferentially inhibits [[MMP-9]] (gelatinase B) and [[MMP-12]] (macrophage metalloelastase), but also inhibits MMP-1, MMP-2, MMP-3, MMP-7, and MMP-13 with varying affinity. It does not efficiently inhibit membrane-type MMPs (MT1-MMP, MT2-MMP).
- **Additional activities:** Beyond MMP inhibition, TIMP1 has growth-factor-like (anti-apoptotic) and erythropoietic activities. It promotes cell proliferation and survival through signalling via CD63/integrin complexes on the cell surface.

## Regulation

- **TGF-β/SMAD3 axis:** TIMP1 is a transcriptional target of the TGF-β1/SMAD3 pathway. TGF-β1 activates SMAD3-dependent transcription of pro-fibrotic genes including [[COL1A1]], [[COL3A1]], [[FN1]] (fibronectin), [[ACTA2]] (α-SMA), and TIMP1. This positions TIMP1 as a central effector of tissue fibrosis.
- **Inflammatory cytokines:** IL-1β, TNF-α, and [[LPS]] upregulate TIMP1 expression in macrophages and fibroblasts.
- **Epigenetic regulation:** TIMP1 promoter methylation modulates its expression in cancer and ageing tissues.
- **Post-transcriptional:** MicroRNAs (e.g., miR-29 family) can target TIMP1 mRNA, providing an additional layer of regulation.

## Physiological Functions

### ECM Homeostasis
TIMP1 maintains ECM integrity by restraining excessive MMP-mediated proteolysis. The balance between TIMP1 and its target MMPs determines net ECM turnover in tissues. In healthy tissues, this balance preserves structural integrity while allowing necessary remodelling during wound healing and development.

### Erythropoiesis
TIMP1 was originally identified as "erythroid potentiating activity" (EPA) for its ability to support erythroid progenitor growth. It acts as a survival factor for erythroid cells, though the physiological significance of this activity in vivo remains under investigation.

### Wound Healing
TIMP1 is upregulated during wound healing to limit MMP activity and protect newly deposited ECM during the remodelling phase. Premature or excessive TIMP1 activity can contribute to pathological fibrosis.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

TIMP1 is a component of the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]] with complex, context-dependent roles:

- **SASP reprogramming:** In prostate cancer senescence models induced by PTEN loss, deletion of TIMP1 reprogrammes the SASP from a tumour-suppressive to a pro-tumorigenic phenotype. This suggests TIMP1 acts as a molecular switch that determines the functional output of the SASP.
- **Pro-fibrotic SASP:** TIMP1 contributes to the pro-fibrotic arm of the SASP by inhibiting MMP-mediated ECM degradation, favouring net ECM accumulation.
- **Ageing biomarker:** Plasma TIMP1 levels increase with age and correlate with the senescent cell burden.

## Pathological Roles

### Fibrosis
TIMP1 is one of the most consistently upregulated genes in fibrotic diseases (pulmonary fibrosis, liver cirrhosis, cardiac fibrosis). By inhibiting MMPs that degrade newly deposited collagen, TIMP1 promotes net ECM accumulation and scar formation. TIMP1 transgenic mice develop progressive fibrosis in multiple organs.

### Cancer
TIMP1 has dual roles in cancer:
- **Anti-tumorigenic:** Inhibits MMPs that degrade basement membranes, limiting tumour invasion and metastasis.
- **Pro-tumorigenic:** Promotes cell proliferation and survival via CD63/integrin signalling; high TIMP1 in tumour stroma correlates with poor prognosis in breast, colorectal, and other cancers. TIMP1 deletion in senescent prostate cancer cells reprogrammes the SASP toward a pro-tumorigenic profile.

### Cardiovascular Disease
Elevated TIMP1 is associated with cardiac remodelling, heart failure, and atherosclerotic plaque progression. The MMP-9/TIMP1 balance in the vasculature determines ECM turnover and plaque stability.

### Ageing
Plasma TIMP1 is one of 12 protein surrogates used in the DNAmGrimAge epigenetic clock for predicting biological age and mortality risk. Elevated TIMP1 correlates with all-cause mortality in ageing cohorts.
