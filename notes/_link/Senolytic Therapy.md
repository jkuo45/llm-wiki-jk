# Senolytic Therapy

Senolytic Therapy refers to treatments that selectively eliminate [[Senescent Cells]], exploiting the fact that these cells are primed for [[Apoptosis]] but are kept alive by [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways|SCAPs]]. By transiently disabling SCAPs, senolytics push the cell across the apoptotic threshold while sparing proliferating and quiescent healthy cells. The foundational demonstration was the Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q) combination (Zhu et al., Nat Med 2015; PMID 25594185), followed by targeted BCL-2 family inhibitors (Yosef et al., Cell Metab 2016; PMID 27912057).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **SCAP targeting**: Senolytics neutralize the pro-survival networks (BCL-2/BCL-XL/MCL-1, PI3K/Akt, UPR) that sustain senescent cells, permitting [[BAX]]/[[BAK]]-mediated [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]] and caspase activation.
- **BCL-2 family inhibitors**: [[Navitoclax]] (ABT-263) and ABT-737 block BCL-2/BCL-XL/BCL-W; [[Dasatinib]] (a tyrosine-kinase inhibitor) plus [[Quercetin]] (a flavonoid) hit multiple SCAP nodes.
- **Other agents**: [[Fisetin]], piperlongumine, and the FOXO4-DRI peptide each engage distinct SCAP components.
- **Intermittent dosing**: Because clearance is rapid, pulsatile dosing minimizes off-target toxicity to healthy cells.

## Role in Aging

- Reduces senescence burden and the [[Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype|SASP]], ameliorating [[Inflammaging]].
- Improves health span and function in aged rodents and in models of specific age-related diseases (Childs et al., Nat Rev Drug Discov 2017; PMID 28627678).
- Complements [[Senomorphic Therapy]], which suppresses the SASP without killing the cell.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **Diabetic kidney disease**: D+Q tested in a pilot/phase trial (NCT02848131) showing reduced SASP factors.
- **Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)**: D+Q improved physical function in a pilot study (NCT02874989).
- **Osteoarthritis**: Senolytics tested for pain and function (e.g., NCT04210986).
- **Biomarkers**: SASP factors (IL-6, MMP-9, GM-CSF) are used to evaluate senolytic efficacy.
- **Safety caveats**: BCL-2 inhibitors can cause thrombocytopenia; tissue selectivity and intermittent regimens are active design goals.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - The-senescence-associated-secretory-phenotype-and-its-physiological-and-pathological-implications|SASP Review 2024]]: The review frames senolytic therapy as a strategy that transiently disables SCAPs (e.g., BCL-2/BCL-XL, PI3K/Akt) to selectively push senescent cells into apoptosis while sparing healthy cells. By eliminating the source of the SASP rather than only the secretome, it reduces senescence burden and inflammaging, complementing senomorphic approaches.
