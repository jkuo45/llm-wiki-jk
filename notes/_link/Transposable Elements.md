# Transposable Elements

**Transposable elements** (TEs), colloquially "jumping genes," are DNA sequences capable of changing their position within a genome. They comprise roughly 45% of the human genome and are normally kept transcriptionally silent.

## Types & Mechanisms

- **Retrotransposons** (Class I) move via a "copy-and-paste" RNA intermediate that is reverse-transcribed:
  - **LTR elements** — including [[Endogenous Retrovirus|endogenous retroviruses]] ([[Human Endogenous Retrovirus|HERVs]]).
  - **Non-LTR elements** — autonomous [[LINE-1]] (L1) and non-autonomous [[Alu element|Alu]] (SINEs) that depend on L1 machinery.
- **DNA transposons** (Class II) move by a "cut-and-paste" mechanism using a transposase; most are inactive in humans.

## Role in Aging & Disease

> [!important] Derepression during aging
> TEs are silenced by [[Heterochromatin]], DNA methylation, and repressive histone marks (e.g. H3K9me3). Age-related [[Epigenetic Alterations]] and heterochromatin erosion — including declining [[SIRT6]] and loss of Piwi/KRAB-ZNF control — allow TE reactivation. Mobilized [[LINE-1]] elements cause insertional mutagenesis and [[Genomic Instability]], and their cytoplasmic cDNA activates the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], fueling [[Inflammaging]] and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype.

TE reactivation is therefore both a marker and a driver of [[Cellular Senescence]], and nucleoside reverse-transcriptase inhibitors have been explored to suppress it.
