# ATF4
ATF4 (Activating Transcription Factor 4) is a stress-induced transcription factor that serves as a central hub for the [[Integrated Stress Response]] (ISR) in mammals.
## Mechanism of Activation
ATF4 is regulated primarily at the translational level. Under normal conditions, its translation is inhibited. However, during various cellular stresses (e.g., ER stress, amino acid starvation, or mitochondrial stress), the phosphorylation of [[eIF2α]] allows for the selective translation of ATF4 mRNA.
## Role in Mitochondrial Stress
In mammalian cells, ATF4 is a major downstream mediator of the metabolic reprogramming that occurs following mitochondrial dysfunction. It activates the transcription of genes involved in:
- **Amino Acid Metabolism**: Including serine biosynthesis and transsulfuration.
- **One-Carbon Metabolism**: Such as the induction of [[MTHFD2]].
- **Antioxidant Defenses**: To help maintain redox homeostasis.

## Regulation & Activation
ATF4 is a bZIP transcription factor whose translation is paradoxically upregulated during [[ER Stress]] when eIF2α is phosphorylated by [[PERK]] or other eIF2α kinases ([[GCN2]], [[HRI]], [[PKR]]). This phospho-eIF2α-dependent translational bypass allows selective ATF4 synthesis even when global cap-dependent translation is suppressed.
## Transcriptional Targets
ATF4 activates pro-survival genes including [[CHOP]], [[GADD34]], [[BiP]], and amino acid transporters. It also regulates [[Autophagy]]-related genes such as [[LC3B]] and [[Atg5]] during the integrated stress response.
## Role in Autophagy & Metabolism
ATF4 bridges the unfolded protein response (UPR) to [[Autophagy]] by inducing autophagy genes under [[Amino Acid Sensing|amino acid deprivation]]. It interacts with [[TFE3]] to co-regulate lysosomal gene expression and is a downstream effector of the [[GCN2]]-eIF2α pathway.
