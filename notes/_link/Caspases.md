# Caspases
**Caspases** are a family of cysteine proteases that play essential roles in [[Apoptosis|programmed cell death (apoptosis)]], [[Inflammation]], and [[Pyroptosis]]. They are synthesized as inactive zymogens (procaspases) and activated by proteolytic cleavage.

## Post-Translational Regulation

> [!important] Phosphorylation Controls Caspase Activity
> Individual caspases are subject to regulatory phosphorylation that fine-tunes apoptotic sensitivity:
> - **[[Caspase-8]]**: Phosphorylated by [[Src]] at Tyr380 (inhibits activation); dephosphorylated by [[SHP1]] (restores function)
> - **[[Caspase-7]]**: Phosphorylated by [[PAK2]] at Ser30/Thr173/Ser239 (inhibits activation and substrate binding)
> - **[[Caspase-9]]**: Phosphorylated by [[Akt]] at Ser196 (suppresses activity)
> - **[[XIAP]]**: Directly binds and inhibits [[Caspase-3]], [[Caspase-7]], and [[Caspase-9]]; regulated by [[TBK1]] (degradation) and [[Akt]] (stabilization)
