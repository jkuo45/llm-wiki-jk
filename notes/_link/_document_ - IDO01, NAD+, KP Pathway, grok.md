in-depth indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase

Both [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|type 1 diabetes]] (T1D) and [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|T2D]] show disrupted [[NAD+]] homeostasis as comprehensively summarized elsewhere^86^. In T1D, distinct patterns emerge, including increased urinary [[Kynurenine|kynurenine]] excretion and altered serum [[Tryptophan|tryptophan]]–[[Kynurenine|kynurenine]] profiles^87,88^. Pediatric studies involving 165 individuals with T1D have linked these alterations to a specific [[IDO1]] genotype and reduced [[Kynurenine|kynurenine]] production in response to [[Interferon-gamma|interferon-γ]] (IFNγ)^89,90^. By contrast, T2D presents its own characteristic metabolic signature in relation to [[NAD+]] metabolism^91^.

> [!info] IDO1 is the rate-limiting enzyme of the [[Kynurenine Pathway]] (KP), which accounts for ~95% of [[Tryptophan]] degradation outside protein synthesis and [[Serotonin]] production.

[[IDO1]] is a heme-containing enzyme that catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step in the [[Kynurenine Pathway]] (KP) of [[Tryptophan]] (Trp) catabolism. It oxidizes L-tryptophan (and some other indoleamine substrates) to [[N-Formylkynurenine]] in an O₂-dependent reaction. [[N-Formylkynurenine]] is then rapidly converted to [[Kynurenine]] (Kyn).

## Enzymatic Function and Structure

[[IDO1]] is a monomeric cytosolic protein (~45 kDa) encoded by the *IDO1* gene on human chromosome 8p11.21. It belongs to the family of heme-dependent dioxygenases (EC 1.13.11.52), distinct from but functionally related to [[TDO|tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase]] (TDO, mainly hepatic) and [[IDO2]] (a less active paralog).

The catalytic mechanism involves:
- A heme prosthetic group (iron center) that binds O₂.
- Reduction of inactive ferric (Fe³⁺) heme to active ferrous (Fe²⁺) form.
- Oxidative cleavage of the indole ring of [[Tryptophan]] at the 2,3-position, producing [[N-Formylkynurenine]].

Crystal structures (e.g., PDB entries like 2D0T, 5EK2) reveal a large domain with a deep heme-binding pocket and a smaller domain; the active site shows plasticity that allows inhibitor and substrate binding. [[IDO1]] can act on multiple substrates, including D-Trp, 5-hydroxy-Trp, tryptamine, and [[Serotonin]], though L-Trp is primary.

> [!important] The [[Kynurenine Pathway]] leads to metabolites including [[Kynurenic Acid]], [[3-Hydroxykynurenine]], [[Quinolinic Acid]] (a neurotoxin and [[NAD+]] precursor), and ultimately [[NAD+]]. This pathway accounts for ~95% of [[Tryptophan]] degradation outside protein synthesis and [[Serotonin]] production.

Downstream, the KP leads to metabolites like [[Kynurenic Acid]], [[3-Hydroxykynurenine]], [[Quinolinic Acid]] (a neurotoxin and [[NAD+]] precursor), and ultimately [[NAD+]]. This pathway accounts for ~95% of [[Tryptophan]] degradation outside protein synthesis and [[Serotonin]] production.

## Regulation

**Induction:** [[IDO1]] is strongly induced by inflammatory cytokines, especially [[Interferon-gamma|interferon-γ]] (IFNγ) via [[JAK]]/[[STAT1|STAT]] signaling, but also by [[Interferon-gamma|IFNα]]/β, [[TNF-α]], [[IL-1β|IL-1]], [[LPS]], and other signals in immune and other cells. Glucocorticoids can synergize in some contexts.

**Expression:** High in placenta, lymph nodes, lungs, small intestine, and certain immune cells ([[Dendritic Cells|dendritic cells]]/DCs, [[Macrophage|macrophages]], monocytes). It is inducible in many tissues during [[Inflammation]].

**Post-translational control:** Phosphorylation of ITIM motifs recruits [[SOCS3]], leading to ubiquitination and degradation (negative feedback). It can also mediate non-enzymatic signaling (e.g., [[PI3K]] activation).

**Other modulators:** [[IDO2]] may compete for heme and act as a negative regulator in some settings.

## Immunological Roles

[[IDO1]] exerts immunosuppression through two main mechanisms:

1. **Local [[Tryptophan]] depletion:** [[Tryptophan]] is essential for [[T cell|T-cell]] proliferation; depletion activates the [[GCN2]] stress kinase, leading to cell cycle arrest, anergy, or [[Apoptosis]] in effector T cells.
2. **[[Kynurenine]] and metabolite accumulation:** Kyn and derivatives activate the [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor]] (AhR), promoting [[Regulatory T cell|regulatory T cell]] (Treg) differentiation and suppressing effector T/NK cells. This creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment.

Physiological roles include:
- Maternal-fetal tolerance (high placental expression).
- Antimicrobial defense (starving Trp-dependent pathogens like certain bacteria or viruses).
- Regulation of [[Inflammation]] and autoimmunity.
- [[Tumor Microenvironment|Tumor immune escape]] (often upregulated in [[Cancer|cancers]] and the tumor microenvironment).

[[IDO1]] is expressed in antigen-presenting cells (e.g., DCs) and modulates T-cell responses peripherally.

## IDO1 in Diabetes

Both [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|T1D]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|T2D]] involve disrupted [[NAD+]] homeostasis, with the KP (via [[IDO1]]/[[TDO]]) being a major Trp-to-[[NAD+]] route.

**Type 1 Diabetes (T1D):**
- Pediatric studies link altered Trp–Kyn profiles and increased urinary [[Kynurenine]] to specific [[IDO1]] genotypes.
- Reduced [[IDO1]] activity/response to [[Interferon-gamma|IFNγ]] in some patients, potentially impairing immune regulation and contributing to autoimmunity against β-cells.
- Defective [[IDO1]] in DCs (e.g., in NOD mouse models) and loss of [[IDO1]] expression in human pancreatic β-cells observed in T1D. This may fail to maintain tolerance, exacerbating autoimmune destruction.

**Type 2 Diabetes (T2D):**
- Often shows elevated KP activity/[[IDO1]]-related changes, linked to chronic low-grade [[Inflammation]], [[Insulin Resistance]], and complications (e.g., nephropathy, retinopathy).
- Higher Kyn/Trp ratios (proxy for [[IDO1]] activity) sometimes correlate with poor glycemic control or [[Oxidative Stress]].
- KP metabolites may influence [[NAD+]] levels, [[Inflammation]], and vascular complications. [[Metformin]] and other interventions can modulate KP in T2D contexts.

> [!tip] The pathway's role is context-dependent: protective via tolerance in autoimmunity but potentially detrimental when overactive in metabolic [[Inflammation]].

Overall, the pathway's role is context-dependent: protective via tolerance in autoimmunity but potentially detrimental when overactive in metabolic [[Inflammation]].

## Therapeutic Targeting

[[IDO1]] is a major target in immuno-oncology due to its role in [[Tumor Microenvironment|tumor evasion]]. Inhibitors (e.g., [[Epacadostat]], navoximod, linrodostat/BMS-986205) have been tested in trials, often in combination with [[Checkpoint Inhibitor|checkpoint inhibitors]] (PD-1/PD-L1), though results have been mixed. Potential in autoimmunity, transplantation, and neurological conditions (due to neuroactive KP metabolites). Challenges include isoform selectivity, compensatory pathways ([[TDO]], [[IDO2]]), and context-specific effects.

## Additional Notes

- **Polymorphisms:** [[IDO1]] SNPs associate with altered activity, infection risk (e.g., aspergillosis), autoimmunity predisposition, and possibly diabetes-related traits.
- **Broader links:** KP dysregulation implicated in [[Neurodegeneration]], [[Depression]], chronic [[Inflammation]], and infections (e.g., HIV modulates [[IDO1]]).

> [!summary] [[IDO1]] sits at the intersection of [[Metabolism|metabolism]], immunity, and [[Inflammation]]. Its activity integrates nutrient sensing ([[Tryptophan]] availability) with immune modulation and [[NAD+]] biosynthesis, making it relevant to diabetes, [[Cancer]], and beyond.
