# LC3
**LC3** (Microtubule-associated protein 1A/1B light chain 3B) is a central protein in the [[Autophagy]] pathway. It is the mammalian homologue of yeast [[Atg8]] and serves as the most widely used marker for autophagosomes.
## Role in Autophagy
LC3 is conjugated to phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) to form lipidated LC3-II, which resides on the autophagosomal membrane. LC3-II levels correlate with autophagosome number and are used as a standard readout of [[Autophagic Flux]].
## Processing and Lipidation
- **LC3-I**: The cytosolic form of LC3.
- **LC3-II**: The lipidated form of LC3 (conjugated to phosphatidylethanolamine). The conversion of LC3-I to LC3-II is a critical step in the formation of the [[Autophagosome]] membrane.
- This process is mediated by the E1-like enzyme [[Atg7]] and the E2-like enzyme [[Atg3]], along with the [[Atg12]]-[[Atg5]]-[[Atg16]] complex.
## Functions
- LC3-II is incorporated into both the inner and outer membranes of the autophagosome.
- It plays a role in both membrane fusion and cargo selection (via interaction with adapter proteins like [[p62]]).
## Marker for Autophagy
- The detection of LC3-II levels and the visualization of "LC3 puncta" (representing autophagosomes) are standard methods for monitoring [[Autophagic Flux]].
## Sirtuin Regulation
[[SIRT1]] promotes autophagy by deacetylating LC3 (Microtubule-associated protein 1A/1B-light chain 3). SIRT1 deacetylation of LC3 reduces its acetylation, enhancing autophagic flux in endothelial cells and cancer contexts.
