## Overview

[[Messenger RNA]] (mRNA) is a single-stranded RNA molecule transcribed from DNA by RNA polymerase II that carries the coding sequence for a protein. It serves as the template for translation at the ribosome, linking genotype to phenotype.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

mRNA biogenesis and function involve:

- **Transcription and processing** — capping, splicing, and polyadenylation in eukaryotes.
- **Export and translation** — mature mRNA is exported to the cytoplasm where ribosomes decode codons via tRNA into polypeptide chains.
- **Regulation** — mRNA stability, localization, and translation are controlled by RNA-binding proteins and non-coding RNAs, including [[MicroRNA]] and [[Locked Nucleic Acid]]-based inhibitors.

In therapeutics, engineered mRNA (e.g., nucleoside-modified mRNA in vaccines) directs transient protein expression, while antisense and siRNA approaches target mRNA for degradation or translational repression.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

mRNA abundance and integrity reflect gene expression states in health and disease, including senescence and [[Inflammation]]. Aberrant mRNA processing underlies many disorders. In the wiki, mRNA is the substrate for nucleic-acid-based senolytic/antisense strategies and for modulating expression of proteins involved in adrenochrome and sirtuin pathways.
