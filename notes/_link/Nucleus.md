# Nucleus

## Overview

The [[Nucleus]] (cell nucleus) is the membrane-bound [[Nucleus]] that encloses the genome, organizing [[Chromatin]] and regulating transcription, DNA replication, and repair. Although [[cGAS]] is best known for cytosolic DNA sensing, a substantial pool localizes to the [[Nucleus|nucleus]], where it performs noncanonical, STING-independent functions. Nuclear cGAS is tightly tethered to [[Nucleosome|nucleosomes]] via histones H2A/H2B, a configuration that restricts aberrant activation while enabling roles in genome stability and RNA processing.

## Noncanonical Nuclear cGAS Functions

Nuclear cGAS interacts with [[PARP1]], disrupting the PARP1-Timeless complex and thereby suppressing [[Homologous Recombination|homologous recombination]] and influencing [[DNA Damage]] repair ([43](#B43)). Its N-terminal [[Intrinsically Disordered Region|intrinsically disordered region]] promotes [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|LLPS]], scaffolding RNA-binding proteins, the [[Spliceosome|spliceosome]], and the [[SWI-SNF Complex|SWI/SNF]] chromatin-remodeling complex, implicating nuclear cGAS in gene expression regulation ([77](#B77)). Under homeostatic conditions, nucleosome tethering and N-terminal hyperphosphorylation keep nuclear cGAS restrained; however, viral infection or [[DNA Damage]] can license nuclear cGAS to produce [[cGAMP]] and elicit a STING-dependent [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] response.

## Retrograde Signaling and Protective Roles

Nuclear cGAS also engages in Z-DNA sensing and retrograde signaling, and a pool of [[STING]] resides at the adjacent [[Nuclear Envelope]], suggesting coordinated nuclear-envelope signaling. In long-lived species such as the naked mole rat, chromatin-retained cGAS actually enhances DNA repair, indicating that noncanonical nuclear cGAS can be protective. Differentiating inflammatory versus homeostatic nuclear cGAS functions remains a key open question for understanding [[Neurodegeneration]] in postmitotic neurons.
