# Ischemia-reperfusion Injury

Ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI) is tissue damage caused when blood supply returns to ischemic tissue after a period of restriction. The restoration of oxygen and nutrients paradoxically triggers inflammation, oxidative stress, and cell death rather than recovery. This counterintuitive phenomenon is central to many acute clinical settings, including [[Myocardial Infarction]], ischemic stroke, organ transplantation, cardiopulmonary bypass, peripheral vascular disease, and shock with resuscitation. Although reperfusion is essential to salvage ischemic tissue, the biochemical cascade it unleashes can exacerbate tissue injury, expand infarct size, and worsen patient outcomes. Understanding the molecular mechanisms of IRI has driven decades of research into cardioprotective and organ-protective strategies.

## Pathophysiology

### Ischemic Phase

During ischemia, oxygen deprivation forces cells to shift from oxidative phosphorylation to anaerobic [[Glycolysis]], leading to rapid [[ATP]] depletion. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump fails, causing intracellular sodium and calcium accumulation, cellular swelling, and membrane depolarization. Elevated cytosolic Ca²⁺ activates [[Calpain]] proteases, phospholipases, and endonucleases, initiating structural damage. Anaerobic glycolysis generates [[Lactate]], causing intracellular acidosis. If ischemia persists, mitochondrial function collapses and cells progress toward irreversible injury.

### Reperfusion Phase

Reperfusion introduces molecular oxygen into metabolically compromised tissue, triggering a burst of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS). Major sources include xanthine oxidase (converted from xanthine dehydrogenase during ischemia), the mitochondrial electron transport chain, [[NADPH Oxidase]], and activated neutrophils. Superoxide anion (O₂⁻) is rapidly converted to hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and hydroxyl radicals (OH·), causing lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage. Calcium overload persists, activating the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP). mPTP opening dissipates the mitochondrial membrane potential, causes [[Cytochrome c]] release into the cytosol, and activates caspase-dependent apoptosis. The release of mitochondrial [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns]] (DAMPs) further amplifies inflammatory signaling.

### Inflammatory Cascade

Reperfusion activates both innate and adaptive immune responses. Complement activation generates anaphylatoxins C3a and C5a, which recruit and activate neutrophils. Upregulated adhesion molecules — [[P-Selectin]], [[E-Selectin]], and [[ICAM-1]] — on endothelial surfaces mediate neutrophil rolling, firm adhesion, and transmigration. Activated neutrophils release proteases (matrix metalloproteinases), additional ROS, and pro-inflammatory cytokines including [[TNF-α]], [[IL-1β]], and [[IL-6]]. These signals converge on the [[NF-κB]] transcription factor, amplifying the inflammatory loop. Platelet activation and microthrombi formation further impair microvascular flow.

### No-Reflow Phenomenon

The no-reflow phenomenon describes persistent microvascular obstruction despite successful restoration of epicardial or large-vessel patency. Mechanisms include endothelial cell swelling, increased vascular resistance, leukocyte plugging of capillaries, microthrombi, and extravascular compression from edema. In the heart, microvascular obstruction is a strong predictor of adverse remodeling after [[Myocardial Infarction]]. In the brain, it contributes to poor outcomes after thrombolysis for ischemic stroke.

## Cell Death Pathways

IRI engages multiple overlapping cell death modalities:

- **Necrosis**: ATP depletion leads to plasma membrane rupture and uncontrolled release of intracellular contents, amplifying inflammation. Predominates in the ischemic core.
- **Apoptosis**: The intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway involves [[Bcl-2]] family regulation, mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, cytochrome c release, apoptosome assembly, and caspase-9/caspase-3 activation. The extrinsic pathway engages death receptors (Fas, TRAIL receptors) with caspase-8 activation.
- **Necroptosis**: A programmed form of necrosis mediated by the [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] kinase cascade. MLKL phosphorylation drives oligomerization and membrane disruption. Necroptosis is particularly relevant when apoptosis is inhibited (e.g., by caspase-8 deficiency).
- **Ferroptosis**: An iron-dependent form of regulated cell death driven by lipid peroxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids in membrane phospholipids. The system Xc⁻/[[Glutathione]]/[[GPX4]] axis is a key regulator; its failure permits lethal lipid peroxide accumulation. Ferroptosis has been implicated in cardiac and renal IRI.
- **Pyroptosis**: [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] activation leads to caspase-1 cleavage of pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18, and gasdermin D pore formation, causing inflammatory cell death and cytokine release.
- **Parthanatos**: Excessive [[PARP1]] hyperactivation in response to DNA damage consumes NAD⁺ and ATP, triggering metabolic collapse and mitochondrial AIF release.

## Organs Affected

### Heart

Myocardial IRI is the prototypical clinical scenario. Reperfusion arrhythmias (ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation) are common in the first minutes after restoration of flow. [[Myocardial Stunning]] — transient contractile dysfunction despite viable myocytes — reflects reversible oxidative damage to contractile proteins. Microvascular obstruction limits effective reflow and expands infarct size. The extent of IRI determines final infarct size and long-term ventricular function after [[Myocardial Infarction]].

### Brain

In ischemic stroke, a core of irreversibly damaged tissue is surrounded by an ischemic penumbra of metabolically compromised but salvageable neurons. Reperfusion, while necessary, risks [[Hemorrhagic Transformation]] after thrombolytic therapy (e.g., [[tPA]]) and contributes to [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] disruption and vasogenic edema. Microvascular dysfunction in cerebral IRI contributes to poor neurological outcomes.

### Kidney

Renal IRI is a leading cause of acute kidney injury (AKI) after cardiac surgery, renal transplantation, and sepsis. Proximal tubular epithelial cells are particularly vulnerable due to high metabolic demand and mitochondrial density. Injury manifests as loss of brush border, tubular cell detachment, and impaired glomerular filtration rate.

### Liver

Hepatic IRI occurs during partial hepatectomy, liver transplantation, and circulatory shock. Sinusoidal endothelial cells and Kupffer cells are early targets, with hepatocyte death following. The liver's regenerative capacity is impaired by IRI, increasing the risk of primary non-function in transplantation.

### Lung

Pulmonary IRI contributes to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) after cardiopulmonary bypass, lung transplantation, and pulmonary embolism reperfusion. Alveolar-capillary barrier disruption leads to non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema and impaired gas exchange.

## Therapeutic Strategies

### Ischemic Conditioning

Ischemic preconditioning (IPC) — brief, repeated ischemic episodes before a sustained insult — activates endogenous protective pathways (adenosine, [[PKC]], KATP channels, [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], [[ERK1/2]]). Ischemic postconditioning applies similar intermittent occlusion at the onset of reperfusion. Remote ischemic conditioning (RIPC) applies brief ischemia to a distant limb, providing protection via humoral and neural mediators. These approaches have shown promise in cardiac and renal IRI in preclinical models, with mixed but evolving clinical evidence.

### Pharmacological Interventions

- **mPTP inhibitors**: [[Cyclosporin A]] binds cyclophilin D to inhibit mPTP opening, reducing mitochondrial damage. Clinical trials (e.g., CIRCUS, CYCLE) have yielded conflicting results.
- **Antioxidants**: [[N-Acetylcysteine]], [[Edaravone]], and superoxide dismutase mimetics target ROS but clinical translation has been limited by poor bioavailability and timing constraints.
- **Anti-inflammatory agents**: [[Anakinra]] (IL-1 receptor antagonist) and anti-complement therapies aim to dampen the inflammatory cascade.
- **Nitric oxide donors**: NO protects endothelial function, inhibits platelet aggregation, and may reduce neutrophil adhesion.
- **Therapeutic hypothermia**: Cooling to 32–34°C reduces metabolic demand, ROS production, and excitotoxicity. Established in cardiac arrest and neonatal encephalopathy; under investigation in stroke and renal IRI.

### Mechanical and Emerging Approaches

Remote ischemic conditioning protocols are being standardized for clinical use. Stem cell therapy and extracellular vesicle (exosome) administration promote tissue repair and modulate immune responses in preclinical models. Gene therapy approaches targeting [[HIF-1α]] (hypoxia adaptation), [[SOD2]] (mitochondrial antioxidant defense), and anti-apoptotic genes are in early development.
