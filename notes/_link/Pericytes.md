# Pericytes

## Overview

Pericytes are contractile perivascular cells that ensheathe the endothelial lining of capillaries and post-capillary venules, embedded within the basement membrane. Together with [[Endothelial Cells]] and astrocytic end-feet, they constitute the neurovascular unit that maintains the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]]. Pericytes regulate vascular stability, blood flow, and barrier integrity, and their loss or dysfunction is implicated in [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]] and small-vessel disease of the brain.

## cGAS Activation Destabilizes the Neurovascular Unit

Emerging evidence indicates that cGAS activation within [[Pericytes]] contributes to neurovascular breakdown. In tauopathy models, pericyte cGAS activation destabilizes the neurovascular unit and compromises [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] integrity, facilitating immune infiltration and exacerbating [[Neuroinflammation]]. This pericyte-centric mechanism parallels the endothelial cGAS-STING axis, in which [[cGAS]] and [[STING]] activation in vascular cells breaches the barrier. The convergence of pericyte and endothelial cGAS signaling highlights the vascular compartment as a key site where the DNA-sensing pathway drives barrier failure.

## Implications for Neurodegeneration

Because pericytes are intimately positioned at the brain–blood interface, their activation state shapes whether the CNS remains insulated from peripheral immune cells. Chronic pericyte cGAS activation, potentially triggered by [[mtDNA]] leakage or [[DNA Damage]], thus represents a upstream contributor to barrier breakdown and downstream neuronal vulnerability. Distinguishing pericyte versus microglial contributions is essential, since microglial [[STING]] appears to protect rather than disrupt the BBB, underscoring the context-dependent roles of cGAS-STING across cell types in the [[Central Nervous System]].
