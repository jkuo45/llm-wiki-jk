# Milled Wheat

[[Milled Wheat]] refers to wheat kernels that have undergone mechanical reduction and separation to produce various grades of [[Flour]]. The milling process transforms the raw [[Wheat Berries]] into a wide range of culinary products.

## The Milling Process
Modern milling involves several stages to isolate different parts of the wheat kernel:
- **Cleaning and Tempering**: Removing impurities and adjusting moisture levels to toughen the [[Bran]] for easier separation from the [[Endosperm]].
- **Breaking**: Using roller mills to crack the kernel and release the starchy [[Endosperm]].
- **Reduction**: Gradually grinding the endosperm into fine particles.
- **Sifting and Purification**: Separating the ground particles by size and density to isolate pure [[Endosperm]], [[Bran]], and [[Germ]].

## Types of Milled Products
- **[[Whole-wheat flour]]**: Produced by milling the entire [[Wheat Berries]], retaining the [[Bran]] and [[Germ]]. It is nutrient-dense but has a shorter shelf life due to the oils in the germ.
- **[[Refined Flour]]**: Primarily composed of the [[Endosperm]]. This results in a finer texture and longer shelf life, but lower nutritional density unless it becomes [[Enriched Flour]].
- **[[Semolina]]**: Coarse particles of [[Endosperm]] typically derived from [[Durum]] wheat, used extensively in pasta production.

## Culinary Applications
- **Baking**: The primary ingredient for [[Bread]], [[Pastries]], and [[Flatbread]].
- **Pasta and Noodles**: Essential for creating various pasta shapes and noodle types.
- **Thickening Agents**: Used in sauces, gravies, and soups.
