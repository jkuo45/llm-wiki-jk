# Oxidative Stress

## Maintenance

- Last updated: 04_July_2026 08:00 PM PDT
- Total count of entities: 73

This directory contains notes and research on **oxidative stress**, its generation via reactive oxygen species (ROS), mitochondrial function, and enzymes such as [[NADPH Oxidase]] and [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]]. It covers the resulting biochemical effects (lipid peroxidation, DNA damage, protein oxidation) and its **dual role** as both a source of damage and an essential cellular signal.

Special emphasis is placed on lifestyle practices that intentionally engage oxidative stress for resilience, vitality, **elevated resting metabolic rate**, and long-term health:
- **Exercise** (especially intense, varied training) – acute ROS bursts that drive mitochondrial biogenesis, antioxidant upregulation, metabolic adaptation, and higher resting energy expenditure.
- Daily movement, cold exposure, heat (sauna), and other ancestral/environmental stressors that support **elevated resting metabolic rate** through improved mitochondrial function and adaptive hormesis.
- **Breathing practices & meditation** – controlled hypoxia/hyperoxia cycles and stress modulation that influence redox balance, nervous system resilience, and metabolic efficiency.
- **Campfires, wood smoke, and occasional cigars** – low-dose smoke exposure and plant compounds that interact with redox pathways (hormetic stressors).

The focus includes oxidative stress involvement in [[Adrenochrome]] formation, [[Cancer]], [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]], [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]], cardiovascular disease, chronic inflammation, and aging. At the same time, the notes assume a healthy baseline of oxidative stress is not only inevitable but **beneficial and encouraged** in contexts such as:

- Selective cancer cell targeting
- Neuromelanin formation
- Redox signaling
- Hormetic adaptation from exercise, breathwork, fire, cold/heat exposure, and other deliberate lifestyle stressors that elevate resting metabolic rate
