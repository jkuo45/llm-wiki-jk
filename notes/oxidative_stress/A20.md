# A20

**A20 (TNF alpha-induced protein 3, TNFAIP3)** is a dual-function ubiquitin-editing enzyme that functions as a master negative regulator of [[NF-kappa B]] signaling and [[Inflammation]].

## Mechanism

A20 contains an N-terminal ovarian tumor (OTU) domain with deubiquitinase (DUB) activity that removes K63-linked ubiquitin chains from signaling intermediates ([[RIPK1]], [[TRAF6]], [[NEMO]]/IKKγ), and a C-terminal zinc-finger domain with E3 ubiquitin ligase activity that conjugates K48-linked ubiquitin chains targeting these same proteins for proteasomal degradation. This "ubiquitin-editing" mechanism terminates NF-κB signaling. A20 is itself a transcriptional target of NF-κB, creating a powerful negative feedback loop.

## Clinical Significance

A20 is a crucial brake on inflammatory signaling. Single-nucleotide polymorphisms in TNFAIP3 are associated with increased risk of [[Rheumatoid Arthritis|rheumatoid arthritis]], [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease|inflammatory bowel disease]], systemic lupus erythematosus, [[Psoriasis]], and [[Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease|COPD]]. A20 haploinsufficiency causes a rare autoinflammatory syndrome (HA20) characterized by early-onset systemic inflammation. Under [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], A20 activity can be impaired by cysteine oxidation, contributing to sustained NF-κB activation in chronic inflammatory diseases.
