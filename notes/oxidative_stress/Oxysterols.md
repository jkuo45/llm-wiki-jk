# Oxysterols

**Oxysterols** are oxygenated derivatives of [[Cholesterol]] formed either enzymatically by cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP27A1, CYP46A1, CYP7A1) or non-enzymatically by [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], yielding >30 distinct species including [[7-Ketocholesterol]], [[7β-hydroxycholesterol]], and [[25-hydroxycholesterol]]. They function both as physiological signaling molecules and as pathological mediators of oxidative damage.

## Enzymatic Formation and Signaling

Enzymatic oxysterols are central to cholesterol homeostasis. 27-hydroxycholesterol is produced by mitochondrial [[CYP27A1]] (sterol 27-hydroxylase) as the first step of cholesterol catabolism and as the main oxysterol crossing the blood–brain barrier. 24(S)-hydroxycholesterol is formed by CYP46A1 in brain neurons, and 7α-hydroxycholesterol by CYP7A1 initiates bile-acid synthesis. These enzymes couple cholesterol elimination to signaling.

Oxysterols are the endogenous ligands of liver X receptors ([[LXRα]], LXRβ). 22(R)-, 24(S)-, 25-, and 27-hydroxycholesterol bind and activate LXRα, inducing target genes for reverse cholesterol transport ([[ABCA1]], [[ABCG1]], [[ApoE]]) and lipogenesis ([[SREBP-1c]]). They also suppress [[SREBP]] processing, coordinating cholesterol efflux and limiting cellular overload. 25-hydroxycholesterol additionally modulates immunity, suppressing IL-1β and shaping inflammatory responses.

## Non-Enzymatic Formation and Toxicity

Non-enzymatic oxysterols arise when [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] oxidize cholesterol in membranes and lipoproteins. [[7-Ketocholesterol]]—the predominant dietary oxysterol and a major product in oxidized LDL—is especially cytotoxic: it induces [[Apoptosis]] in vascular cells via ROS-dependent mitochondrial permeabilization and [[Caspase-3]] activation, and promotes foam-cell formation. 7β-hydroxycholesterol and 5α,6α-epoxycholesterol similarly drive inflammation and plaque instability.

## Pathological Relevance

Oxidized cholesterol species accumulate in [[Lipid Peroxidation|oxidized LDL]] within atherosclerotic plaques, promoting foam-cell death, inflammation, and vulnerability to rupture—linking oxysterols to [[Atherosclerosis]]. They are elevated in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and other [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]]: 27-hydroxycholesterol correlates with amyloid burden and may modulate [[Amyloid-beta]] generation via LXR and lipid-raft effects; 24(S)-hydroxycholesterol rises with neuronal damage. Oxysterols are also implicated in age-related macular degeneration and in the pathogenesis of Alzheimer's via cholesterol dyshomeostasis. Dietary oxysterols (from heated/fried foods) contribute to systemic oxidative load.
