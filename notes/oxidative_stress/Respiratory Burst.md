# Respiratory Burst

## Definition
Respiratory burst (also known as oxidative burst) is the rapid release of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS), specifically [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]], from different cell types, primarily [[Neutrophils]] and macrophages.

## Mechanism
The process is driven by the activation of [[NADPH Oxidase]] (NOX), which transfers electrons from NADPH to molecular oxygen to produce superoxide. In neutrophils, the enzyme [[Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO) further converts hydrogen peroxide into [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl), a potent antimicrobial agent.

## Role in Health and Disease
- **[[Immunity]]**: Essential for the killing of phagocytosed bacteria and fungi.
- **[[Inflammation]]**: Excessive or prolonged respiratory burst can lead to collateral tissue damage and chronic [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Connections
- [[NADPH Oxidase]]: The primary enzyme responsible for the burst.
- [[Neutrophils]]: The main cell type performing the burst during infection.
- [[Myeloperoxidase]]: The enzyme that amplifies the toxicity of the burst.
- [[Hypochlorous Acid]]: A major product of the burst in neutrophils.
