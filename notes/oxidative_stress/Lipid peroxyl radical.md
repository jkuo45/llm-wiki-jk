# Lipid peroxyl radical

A **lipid peroxyl radical** (LOO•) is formed when a carbon-centered lipid radical (L•) reacts rapidly with molecular oxygen (k ≈ 10⁹ M⁻¹s⁻¹). It is the chain-propagating species in the [[Lipid Peroxidation]] chain reaction, abstracting a hydrogen atom from an adjacent [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA)]] to generate a [[Lipid hydroperoxide]] (LOOH) and a new carbon-centered lipid radical (L•).

## Role in the Peroxidation Chain

Lipid peroxidation proceeds via a free-radical chain mechanism:

1. **Initiation:** A reactive species (e.g., [[Hydroxyl radical]] (•OH) or [[Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻)) abstracts a bis-allylic hydrogen from a PUFA, forming a resonance-stabilized carbon-centered lipid radical (L•).
2. **Oxygen addition:** L• + O₂ → LOO• (near diffusion-limited rate).
3. **Propagation (rate-determining):** LOO• + PUFA–H → LOOH + L• (new chain).
4. **Termination:** 2 LOO• → LOOL + O₂ (Russell mechanism) or LOO• + [[Vitamin E]] (α-tocopherol) → LOOH + α-tocopheroxyl radical.

Each LOO• can propagate dozens to hundreds of oxidation cycles before termination. This autocatalytic amplification means that a single initiating event can damage large membrane areas.

## Reactivity and Targets

Lipid peroxyl radicals are moderately reactive (E°′ ≈ 1.0 V) compared to •OH (E°′ ≈ 2.8 V), but their longer half-life (~seconds) allows diffusion within and between membrane leaflets. They preferentially abstract hydrogen from bis-allylic positions (bond dissociation energy ~75–80 kcal/mol), with reactivity scaling with PUFA unsaturation: docosahexaenoic acid (DHA, 22:6) >> arachidonic acid (20:4) > linoleic acid (18:2). Chain-breaking [[Antioxidants]] such as [[Vitamin E]] intercept LOO• at diffusion-limited rates (k ≈ 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹), donating a phenolic hydrogen to form a stable tocopheroxyl radical that can be recycled by [[Ascorbic Acid]] at the membrane interface.

## Clinical Significance

Lipid peroxyl radicals are the primary propagating species linking initial oxidant attack to downstream pathology in [[Atherosclerosis]] (oxLDL formation), [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]] (membrane damage in neurons), [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], and [[Ferroptosis]] (iron-dependent lipid peroxide accumulation). The termination product LOOL (lipid peroxide dimer) and the fragmentation products [[Malondialdehyde]] and [[4-Hydroxynonenal]] serve as biomarkers of in vivo lipid peroxidation.
