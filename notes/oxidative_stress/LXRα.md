# LXRα
Liver X receptor alpha (LXRα) is a nuclear receptor that plays a central role in the regulation of [[Cholesterol]], fatty acid, and glucose homeostasis. It works in conjunction with other receptors like [[PPARγ]] to regulate the expression of [[ABCA1]], which is vital for cholesterol efflux.

## Oxidative Stress and LXRα Regulation

### Oxysterol Ligands and Redox-Dependent Activation
LXRα is activated by specific [[Oxysterol]] ligands, including 22(R)-hydroxycholesterol, 24(S)-hydroxycholesterol, and 27-hydroxycholesterol — all generated through enzymatic or non-enzymatic [[Cholesterol]] oxidation. The production of these ligands is directly tied to cellular [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]]: increased ROS drive cholesterol oxidation, generating oxysterols that activate LXRα. Upon ligand binding, LXRα forms a heterodimer with [[Retinoid X Receptor]] ([[RXRα]]) and binds to [[LXR response elements]] (LXREs) in the promoter regions of target genes. The transcriptional activity of the LXRα/RXR heterodimer is further modulated by redox-dependent [[Coactivator]] recruitment (e.g., [[SRC-1]], [[PGC-1α]]) and [[Corepressor]] ([[NCoR]], [[SMRT]]) release.

### Regulation of Cholesterol Efflux and Reverse Cholesterol Transport
LXRα transcriptional targets constitute a coordinated cholesterol efflux program. The most well-characterized target — [[notes/oxidative_stress/ABCA1]] — encodes the ATP-binding cassette transporter A1, which mediates the rate-limiting step of [[apolipoprotein A-I]] (apoA-I) lipidation to form nascent [[HDL]]. Additional targets include [[ABCG1]] (promotes efflux to mature HDL), [[ABCG5]]/[[ABCG8]] (in hepatocytes and enterocytes, mediating biliary and fecal sterol excretion), and [[Apolipoprotein E]] ([[ApoE]]). Through this network, LXRα promotes [[reverse cholesterol transport]] (RCT), reducing [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis|atherosclerotic]] plaque lipid burden.

### Transrepression of Inflammatory Genes
LXRα suppresses inflammatory gene expression through a process termed [[transrepression]] — independent of its DNA-binding activity. Ligand-activated LXRα undergoes [[SUMOylation]] at lysine residue K447, which targets the receptor to [[nuclear receptor corepressor]] (NCoR) complexes on the promoters of [[NF-kappa B]] target genes. This sumoylation-dependent mechanism prevents the clearance of NCoR, thereby maintaining repression of inflammatory genes including [[notes/oxidative_stress/IL-1b]], [[notes/oxidative_stress/TNF-alpha]], [[COX-2]], and [[MMP-9]]. This anti-inflammatory function positions LXRα as a key modulator of the [[notes/_link/Inflammation]]-[[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] interface in [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]].

### Crosstalk with Lipo genesis and Metabolic Consequences
LXRα activation also induces [[SREBP-1c]] (sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1c) and its lipogenic targets — [[FASN]] (fatty acid synthase), [[SCD1]] (stearoyl-CoA desaturase), and [[ACC]] (acetyl-CoA carboxylase) — promoting de novo fatty acid and [[Triglyceride]] synthesis in the liver. This dual effect — beneficial cholesterol efflux combined with undesirable hepatic steatosis and hypertriglyceridemia — has been a major obstacle to clinical development of synthetic LXR agonists. The balance between LXRα-dependent cholesterol efflux and lipogenesis is influenced by [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]]: ROS impair SREBP-1c processing, potentially uncoupling lipogenesis from RCT under pro-oxidant conditions.

### LXRα in Macrophage Foam Cells and Atherosclerosis
In atherosclerotic lesions, [[notes/oxidative_stress/CD36 Receptor]]-mediated uptake of [[Oxidized LDL]] elevates cellular cholesterol and oxysterol levels in macrophages, activating LXRα as a protective feedback mechanism. LXRα-driven [[notes/oxidative_stress/ABCA1]] and [[ABCG1]] upregulation promotes cholesterol efflux, counteracting [[Foam cell]] formation. However, chronic exposure to [[notes/oxidative_stress/Lipid Peroxidation]] products (e.g., [[4-Hydroxynonenal]]) can modify LXRα directly — 4-HNE adduction at cysteine residues impairs LXRα DNA-binding activity, creating a vicious cycle where oxidative stress disables the protective LXRα pathway, accelerating [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]].

### Clinical Perspectives: LXR Agonists and Oxidative Modulation
Synthetic LXR agonists (e.g., [[GW3965]], [[T0901317]]) demonstrate potent anti-atherosclerotic and anti-inflammatory effects in animal models but are limited by hepatic lipogenesis. Novel strategies include [[LXRβ-selective agonists]] (to avoid LXRα-driven lipogenesis), [[tissue-selective LXR modulators]], and combination therapy with [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]]-targeted antioxidants. LXRα also interacts with [[Nrf2]] signaling — the LXRα promoter contains an [[ARE|antioxidant response element]], and Nrf2 activation upregulates LXRα expression, suggesting coordinated cytoprotective transcriptional programs.
