# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease** (COPD) is a progressive inflammatory lung disease characterized by long-term breathing problems and poor airflow. The main symptoms are shortness of breath, chronic cough, and sputum production. [[Oxidative Stress]] is now recognized as a central, unifying mechanism that amplifies airway and parenchymal destruction in COPD.

## Pathophysiology

The primary driver is chronic [[Inflammation]] of the airways, most often triggered by long-term exposure to cigarette [[Smoking]] or other noxious particulates. Key processes include small-airway disease (chronic bronchiolitis narrowing airways) and parenchymal destruction ([[Emphysema]]—loss of alveolar surface area for gas exchange). Both are exacerbated by an oxidant–antioxidant imbalance.

## Role of Oxidative Stress

In COPD, oxidative stress is elevated from dual sources: exogenous (cigarette smoke contains high concentrations of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]]) and endogenous (activated inflammatory cells—[[Neutrophils]] and [[Macrophage]]s—generate ROS via [[Respiratory Burst]] through NOX2). Consequences include:

- **Antiprotease inactivation**: ROS oxidize and inactivate α₁-antitrypsin and other antiproteases, permitting uncontrolled elastase-mediated tissue destruction.
- **NF-κB activation**: Oxidants activate [[NF-kappa B]], sustaining cytokine (TNF-α, IL-8) transcription.
- **HDAC2 inactivation**: [[Peroxynitrite]] (formed from NO + O₂•⁻) nitrates and inactivates histone deacetylase 2, blunting the anti-inflammatory response to corticosteroids—a key reason COPD is steroid-resistant.
- **Impaired [[DNA Repair]]** and accelerated [[Cellular Senescence]] in lung epithelium.
- **[[Hypoxia]]**: Exacerbations worsen tissue hypoxia, further increasing [[Mitochondria]]l ROS.

## Biomarkers and End-Organ Effects

Oxidative damage markers—[[Malondialdehyde]], [[F2-Isoprostanes]], 4-HNE, and exhaled NO—are elevated in COPD and correlate with severity. Comorbidities share the oxidative mechanism: [[Pulmonary Hypertension]] arises from endothelial dysfunction, and [[Metabolic Syndromes]] frequently co-occur. COPD also overlaps with [[Asthma]] in the "asthma-COPD overlap" phenotype.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Inhaled bronchodilators and corticosteroids remain mainstays; the latter are limited by HDAC2 inactivation. [[N-Acetylcysteine]] and other [[Glutathione]]-augmenting mucolytics show modest benefit as antioxidant adjuncts. Smoking cessation and pulmonary rehabilitation reduce oxidant load. Targeting Nox enzymes, restoring HDAC2 activity, and NRF2 activation are investigational strategies.
