# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease** (COPD) is a progressive inflammatory lung disease characterized by long-term breathing problems and poor airflow. The main symptoms include shortness of breath and cough with sputum production.

## Pathophysiology
The primary driver of COPD is chronic [[Inflammation]] of the airways, often triggered by long-term exposure to irritating particulate matter or gases, most commonly from tobacco [[Smoking]].

Key pathological processes include:
- **[[Oxidative Stress]]**: An imbalance between [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) and antioxidant defenses leads to tissue damage and amplification of the inflammatory response in the [[Lungs]].
- **Small Airway Disease**: Chronic bronchiolitis results in narrowing of the airways.
- **Parenchymal Destruction**: [[Emphysema]] involves the destruction of the alveoli, reducing the surface area available for gas exchange.

## Role of Oxidative Stress
In COPD, oxidative stress is markedly increased due to both exogenous sources (cigarette smoke contains high concentrations of ROS) and endogenous sources (activated inflammatory cells like [[Neutrophils]] and macrophages). This stress contributes to:
- Inactivation of protective antiproteases.
- Activation of pro-inflammatory transcription factors like [[NF-kappa B]].
- Impaired [[DNA Repair]] mechanisms.
- Accelerated [[Cellular Senescence]] in lung tissue.

## Connections
- **[[Smoking]]**: The leading cause of COPD, providing a massive exogenous source of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]].
- **[[Inflammation]]**: Chronic inflammatory response is central to the disease progression.
- **[[Lungs]]**: The primary organ system affected by COPD.
- **[[Alpha-1 Antitrypsin]]**: Deficiency in this protein is a genetic risk factor for COPD.

## Sources of Oxidative Stress in COPD

### Exogenous Sources
- **[[Cigarette smoke]]**: Contains >10¹⁵ free radicals per puff, including:
  - **Tar-phase radicals**: Semiquinone radicals that reduce O₂ to O₂⁻• via redox cycling
  - **Gas-phase radicals**: [[Hydroxyl Radicals|•OH]], [[Nitric Oxide Synthase|•NO]], alkyl radicals, [[Hydroperoxides]]
  - **Transition metals**: Fe, Cu (catalytic for [[Fenton Reaction|Fenton chemistry]])
  - **Aldehydes**: [[Malondialdehyde|Acrolein]], 4-HNE, formaldehyde
- **Air pollution**: [[Particulate matter]] (PM₂.₅), ozone, diesel exhaust particles
- **Occupational exposures**: Silica, coal dust, cadmium

### Endogenous Sources
- **[[NADPH Oxidase]] (NOX) enzymes**:
  - **NOX2** in alveolar [[Macrophage|macrophages]] and [[Neutrophils]]: Upregulated 2–3 fold in COPD, producing sustained O₂⁻• during the [[Respiratory Burst]]
  - **NOX4** in bronchial [[Epithelial cells]]: Constitutively active; expression correlates with disease severity
  - **DUOX1/DUOX2**: H₂O₂-producing NADPH oxidases in airway epithelium
- **[[Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO)**: Inflammatory cells release MPO, generating [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl); elevated MPO levels are found in COPD sputum (2–5× control)
- **[[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]**:
  - Impaired [[Electron Transport Chain]] complexes I and III in COPD lung cells
  - Increased [[Mitophagy]] and mitochondrial ROS (mtROS) production
  - Reduced [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] (PGC-1α downregulation)
- **[[Xanthine Oxidase]]**: Increased activity in COPD plasma; converts hypoxanthine/xanthine to O₂⁻• and uric acid
- **[[Uncoupled eNOS]]**: Endothelial NOS in pulmonary vessels produces O₂⁻• instead of [[Nitric Oxide]] (NO) when BH₄ is oxidized

## Oxidative Stress-Mediated Pathological Mechanisms

### Protease-Antiprotease Imbalance
The **elastase-antielastase hypothesis** is central to COPD pathogenesis:
- **[[Neutrophil elastase]] (NE)**: Inhaled [[Oxidants]] and endogenous ROS inactivate **[[Alpha-1 Antitrypsin]]** (α₁-AT), the primary inhibitor of NE, by oxidizing Met358 at its active site (Met→Sulfoxide)
- **[[Matrix Metalloproteinases]] (MMPs)**: MMP-9 and MMP-12 are activated by ROS-mediated oxidation of the [[Cysteine switch]] in their pro-domains; levels are elevated 3–5 fold in COPD BAL fluid
- **Cysteine cathepsins**: Cathepsin S, K, L released by [[Macrophage|macrophages]] degrade [[Elastin]] and [[Collagen]]; ROS inactivate their endogenous inhibitors (cystatins)

### Corticosteroid Resistance
A hallmark of severe COPD is **resistance to [[Glucocorticoid]] therapy**:
- **HDAC2 inactivation**: [[H₂O₂]] and [[Peroxynitrite|ONOO⁻]] oxidize and nitrate [[HDAC2]], reducing its deacetylase activity by >50%
- **Impaired glucocorticoid receptor (GR) function**: Oxidative modification of GRα prevents its nuclear translocation and interaction with [[Histone Acetylation|acetylated histones]]
- **PI₃Kδ activation**: ROS-activated [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] signaling further reduces HDAC2 expression
- **Clinical consequence**: Reduced efficacy of inhaled corticosteroids, necessitating higher doses

### Mucus Hypersecretion
ROS stimulate [[MUC5AC]] gene expression in [[Goblet cells]] via:
  ROS → [[NADPH Oxidase]] activation → [[TGF-beta]] release → [[NF-kappa B]]-dependent MUC5AC transcription
HOCl directly induces mucus cross-linking, increasing sputum viscosity.

### DNA Damage and Cellular Senescence
- **Accelerated [[Nucleic Acids|DNA oxidation]]**: 8-oxo-dG levels are elevated 2–3 fold in COPD [[Lungs]] tissue
- **Telomere shortening**: [[Telomere Attrition]] in alveolar epithelial cells correlates with COPD severity (2–3× shorter than age-matched controls)
- **[[Senescence]] associated secretory phenotype (SASP)**: Senescent lung cells secrete [[IL-1b]], [[TNF-alpha]], [[MMP-9]], and [[TGF-beta]], perpetuating inflammation
- **[[p53]] activation**: Oxidative DNA damage stabilizes p53, promoting cell cycle arrest and [[Apoptosis]]

### Autoimmune Components
Oxidative modifications generate **neoepitopes**:
- **Carbonylated proteins**: Recognized as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)
- **3-Nitrotyrosine**: Elevated in COPD lung tissue; anti-3-NT antibodies are elevated in ~30% of COPD patients
- **Elastin fragments**: Oxidized elastin peptides activate [[Toll-like Receptor|TLR4]] and promote [[Matrix Metalloproteinases|MMP-9]] release

## Defective Antioxidant Defenses in COPD

### Impaired NRF2 Pathway
The transcription factor [[NRF2]], master regulator of antioxidant genes, is dysregulated:
- Reduced nuclear NRF2 levels in COPD [[Macrophage|macrophages]] and [[Epithelial cells]]
- Decreased expression of NRF2 target genes: [[Glutathione Peroxidase]], [[Catalase]], [[Superoxide Dismutase]], [[Heme oxygenase-1]], [[NQO1]]
- **KEAP1 oxidation**: Chronic exposure to cigarette smoke modifies KEAP1 cysteines, but paradoxical NRF2 impairment suggests additional epigenetic silencing (promoter methylation of NRF2 target genes)

### Glutathione Depletion
- **[[Glutathione]] (GSH) levels**: Depressed 40–60% in COPD [[Epithelial lining fluid]] (ELF)
- **GSH/GSSG ratio**: Shifted from ~100:1 (healthy) to ~10:1 (COPD), indicating severe oxidative stress
- **γ-Glutamylcysteine ligase (GCL)**: Rate-limiting GSH synthesis enzyme is downregulated in COPD

### Reduced [[Vitamin E]] and [[Ascorbic Acid]] levels
Plasma levels of these chain-breaking antioxidants are 30–50% lower in COPD patients, correlating with lung function (FEV₁).

## Systemic Effects and Comorbidities

COPD is now recognized as a **systemic disease** driven in part by **spillover of oxidative and inflammatory mediators** from the lungs into the circulation:

- **[[Cardiovascular Disease]]**: Oxidized LDL, MPO, and AOPPs contribute to [[Atherosclerosis]], [[Coronary Heart Disease]], and [[Pulmonary Hypertension]]
- **[[Sarcopenia]] and [[Cachexia]]**: ROS-mediated [[Proteasome]] activation drives muscle proteolysis; mitochondrial dysfunction reduces oxidative capacity
- **[[Osteoporosis]]**: ROS activate [[Osteoclast]] and suppress [[Osteoblasts]]
- **[[Diabetes Mellitus]]**: [[TNF-alpha]] and [[IL-1b]] induce insulin resistance
- **[[Depression]]**: Systemic [[Inflammation]] and [[SASP]] contribute to neuroinflammation

## Therapeutic Strategies Targeting Oxidative Stress

### Approved/Ongoing Interventions
- **[[N-Acetylcysteine]] (NAC)**: Mucolytic and GSH precursor; 600 mg BID reduces exacerbation rate by ~25% (BRONCUS trial, PANTHEON trial)
- **[[Carbocysteine]]**: Mucolytic with antioxidant properties
- **[[Glutathione]] supplementation**: Inhaled GSH shown to improve ELF GSH levels
- **[[Vitamin E]]**: Mixed trial results; γ-tocopherol may be more effective than α-tocopherol in COPD

### Novel Therapeutic Targets
- **Nrf2 activators**: [[Sulforaphane]] (broccoli sprout extract), [[Bardoxolone methyl]] (CDDO-Me)
- **NOX inhibitors**: GKT137831 (setanaxib) targeting NOX1/NOX4
- **MPO inhibitors**: AZD3241 under investigation for COPD
- **Mitochondria-targeted antioxidants**: [[MitoQ]] and [[MitoTEMPO]]
- **[[Senolytics]]**: [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]] to clear senescent cells in COPD lung
- **[[Metformin]]**: Activates [[AMPK]], restoring NRF2 activity and reducing airway inflammation

## Pathological Hallmarks and Staging
The Fletcher-Petch curve of FEV₁ decline shows that COPD is progressive, but oxidative stress biomarkers (8-oxo-dG, AOPPs, MPO) can predict **rapid decliners** (FEV₁ loss >60 mL/year).

### Emphysema Predominance
- Increased [[Lipid Peroxidation|lipid peroxidation]] and protein carbonylation
- Elastin degradation and alveolar wall destruction
- [[Alpha-1 Antitrypsin]] deficiency accelerates damage

### Chronic Bronchitis Predominance
- Goblet cell hyperplasia and [[MUC5AC]] overexpression
- Neutrophilic [[Inflammation]] with high MPO activity
- Oxidant-induced epithelial barrier dysfunction
