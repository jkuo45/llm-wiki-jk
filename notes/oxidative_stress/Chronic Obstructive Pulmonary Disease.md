# Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease

**Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease** (COPD) is a progressive inflammatory lung disease characterized by long-term breathing problems and poor airflow. The main symptoms include shortness of breath and cough with sputum production.

## Pathophysiology
The primary driver of COPD is chronic [[notes/_link/Inflammation]] of the airways, often triggered by long-term exposure to irritating particulate matter or gases, most commonly from tobacco [[Smoking]].

Key pathological processes include:
- **[[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]]**: An imbalance between [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) and antioxidant defenses leads to tissue damage and amplification of the inflammatory response in the [[Lungs]].
- **Small Airway Disease**: Chronic bronchiolitis results in narrowing of the airways.
- **Parenchymal Destruction**: [[Emphysema]] involves the destruction of the alveoli, reducing the surface area available for gas exchange.

## Role of Oxidative Stress
In COPD, oxidative stress is markedly increased due to both exogenous sources (cigarette smoke contains high concentrations of ROS) and endogenous sources (activated inflammatory cells like [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] and macrophages). This stress contributes to:
- Inactivation of protective antiproteases.
- Activation of pro-inflammatory transcription factors like [[NF-kappa B]].
- Impaired [[notes/_link/DNA Repair]] mechanisms.
- Accelerated [[Cellular Senescence]] in lung tissue.

## Connections
- **[[Smoking]]**: The leading cause of COPD, providing a massive exogenous source of [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]].
- **[[notes/_link/Inflammation]]**: Chronic inflammatory response is central to the disease progression.
- **[[Lungs]]**: The primary organ system affected by COPD.
- **[[Alpha-1 Antitrypsin]]**: Deficiency in this protein is a genetic risk factor for COPD.
