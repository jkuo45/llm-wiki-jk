# Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids

**Polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs)** are fatty acids containing two or more carbon–carbon double bonds. As essential structural components of [[Cell Membranes]], they are particularly susceptible to [[Lipid Peroxidation]], yet they also serve as precursors for critical signaling lipids. Their oxidation is the biochemical engine of ferroptotic and oxidative cell death.

## Structure and Susceptibility to Oxidation

The bis-allylic methylene groups (–CH₂– flanked by two double bonds) in PUFAs are the primary targets of radical attack because the resulting carbon-centered radical is resonance-stabilized. Relative oxidation rates scale with bis-allylic positions: linoleic acid (18:2, n-6) = 1×, arachidonic acid (20:4, n-6) = 6×, docosahexaenoic acid (22:6, n-3) = 8×. Thus membranes enriched in long-chain n-3 PUFAs are both functionally flexible and oxidatively fragile—a trade-off with direct disease implications.

## Major PUFAs

- **Linoleic acid (LA, 18:2, n-6)**: Essential; precursor to arachidonic acid.
- **Arachidonic acid (AA, 20:4, n-6)**: Substrate for eicosanoids (prostaglandins, leukotrienes, thromboxanes) and for [[F2-Isoprostanes]] formation.
- **Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA, 22:6, n-3)**: Enriched in brain and retina; highly oxidizable.
- **Eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5, n-3)**: Anti-inflammatory eicosanoid precursor (resolvins, protectins).

## Role in Oxidative Stress & Disease

PUFA peroxidation generates reactive aldehydes—[[Malondialdehyde]] and [[4-Hydroxynonenal]]—that adduct proteins and [[DNA]], propagating damage. While PUFA-rich membranes are more oxidation-prone, controlled PUFA oxidation also produces specialized pro-resolving mediators that terminate [[Inflammation]]. Peroxidation of PUFAs in membrane phospholipids—especially arachidonoyl and adrenoyl chains on phosphatidylethanolamines—is the driving force of [[Ferroptosis]], an iron-dependent regulated cell death; inhibition of [[GPX4]] (a [[Glutathione Peroxidase]]) or depletion of [[Glutathione]] unleashes lethal PUFA peroxidation. This pathway is implicated in neurodegeneration, ischemia–reperfusion, and tumor suppression.

Dietary PUFA composition modulates redox tone: high n-3 intake increases peroxidation potential but yields anti-inflammatory resolvins; antioxidant defenses ([[Vitamin E]], [[Coenzyme Q10]]) must scale accordingly. In [[Atherosclerosis]], PUFA peroxidation within LDL initiates oxLDL formation (see [[Cholesterol]]).

## Connections

- [[Lipid Peroxidation]]: Bis-allylic PUFAs are the molecular substrate of LPO.
- [[F2-Isoprostanes]]: Non-enzymatic peroxidation products of arachidonic acid.
- [[Malondialdehyde]]: Aldehyde end-product of PUFA peroxidation.
- [[4-Hydroxynonenal]]: Reactive aldehyde adduct from n-6 PUFA oxidation.
- [[Ferroptosis]]: PUFA peroxidation is the execution step of iron-dependent cell death.
- [[Inflammation]]: PUFA-derived eicosanoids and resolvins govern inflammation.
- [[Glutathione Peroxidase]]: GPX4 restrains PUFA peroxidation and ferroptosis.
- [[Vitamin E]]: Lipophilic chain-breaking antioxidant protecting PUFA membranes.
