# Nucleic Acids

## Definition
Nucleic acids are biopolymers, such as DNA and RNA, composed of nucleotide monomers. They are responsible for the storage and expression of genetic information in all living organisms.

## Role in Oxidative Stress
Nucleic acids are prone to damage by [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]], particularly the [[Hydroxyl Radicals]]. This damage, known as **DNA oxidation**, results in base modifications, strand breaks, and DNA-protein cross-links. The most studied lesion is the formation of [[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine]] (8-OHdG), which is both mutagenic and a biomarker for [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]].

## Mechanisms of Oxidative DNA Damage

### Hydroxyl Radical Attack
The [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hydroxyl Radicals|hydroxyl radical]] (•OH), generated by the [[notes/oxidative_stress/Fenton reaction|Fenton reaction]] (Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ → Fe³⁺ + •OH + OH⁻) and by [[notes/oxidative_stress/Ionizing Radiation|radiolysis of water]], is the most reactive species toward DNA. It reacts with both the **deoxyribose backbone** and the **nitrogenous bases** at near diffusion-limited rates (k ≈ 10⁹–10¹⁰ M⁻¹s⁻¹).

#### Sugar Damage
•OH abstracts hydrogen atoms from each carbon of deoxyribose (C1′–C5′), leading to:
- **C4′-radical formation**: The most frequent lesion, leading to β-elimination and **strand breaks** with 3′-phosphoglycolate and 5′-phosphate termini
- **C1′-radical**: Produces abasic sites via base release (deoxyribonolactone)
- **C5′-radical**: Generates 5′-aldehydes that are blocking lesions for polymerases

#### Base Modifications
- **Guanine** (lowest reduction potential, most easily oxidized):
  - 8-oxo-7,8-dihydroguanine ([[notes/oxidative_stress/8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine|8-oxo-dG]]): The most abundant oxidative lesion (~10³ – 10⁶ lesions/cell/day)
  - 2,6-diamino-4-hydroxy-5-formamidopyrimidine (FapyG)
  - Oxazolone and imidazolone (secondary oxidation products of 8-oxo-dG)
- **Adenine**:
  - 8-oxo-7,8-dihydroadenine (8-oxo-dA)
  - 4,6-diamino-5-formamidopyrimidine (FapyA)
- **Cytosine**:
  - Thymine glycol (5,6-dihydroxy-5,6-dihydrothymine)
  - 5-hydroxycytosine, 5-hydroxyuracil (deamination product)
- **Thymine**:
  - Thymine glycol (cis and trans isomers) — a strong replicative block
  - 5-formyluracil, 5-hydroxymethyluracil
  - 5,6-dihydrothymine

### Singlet Oxygen (¹O₂)
[[notes/oxidative_stress/Singlet Oxygen]] selectively targets guanine, producing 8-oxo-dG as the primary initial product. This reactivity underlies the photodynamic damage to DNA in skin cells exposed to [[Ultraviolet A]] (UVA) radiation.

### Peroxynitrite and Nitrative Stress
[[notes/oxidative_stress/Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻) and [[Nitrogen dioxide]] (•NO₂) mediate **nitrative DNA damage**:
- **8-nitroguanine**: The major nitration product; highly mutagenic and prone to depurination, generating abasic sites
- **Deamination**: ONOO⁻ promotes deamination of cytosine → uracil and 5-methylcytosine → thymine

### One-Electron Oxidation by Carbonate Radical
The [[notes/oxidative_stress/Carbonate radical]] (CO₃⁻•), formed from ONOO⁻ + CO₂, is a selective one-electron oxidant of guanine with longer lifetime than •OH, enabling damage to diffuse across cellular compartments.

## Mitochondrial DNA Oxidation

[[notes/_link/Mitochondria|Mitochondrial]] DNA (mtDNA) is particularly susceptible to oxidative damage due to:
- **Proximity to the electron transport chain** (complex I–IV), the major intracellular source of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals|superoxide]]
- **Lack of protective [[Histone|histones]]** and limited [[notes/_link/Chromatin|chromatin]] structure
- **Limited repair capacity**: mtDNA relies primarily on [[Base Excision Repair]] (BER) and lacks [[Nucleotide Excision Repair]] (NER)
- **High copy number**: mtDNA lesions can accumulate in heteroplasmic fashion

Oxidized mtDNA (e.g., 8-oxo-dG, thymine glycol) impairs transcription of mtDNA-encoded [[Electron Transport Chain|ETC]] subunits (~13 proteins in humans), establishing a vicious cycle:
  ETC dysfunction → more ROS → more mtDNA damage → reduced ETC function → further ROS elevation

This mechanism is central to the [[notes/_link/Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] theory of aging and is implicated in [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]], [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]], and [[notes/_link/Sarcopenia]].

## RNA Oxidation

RNA is more susceptible to oxidation than DNA due to:
- **Single-stranded nature**: Bases are more exposed to oxidants
- **Cytosolic location**: Proximity to ribosomes and metabolic ROS sources
- **Lack of repair**: Oxidized RNA is degraded rather than repaired

Key consequences of RNA oxidation include:
- **Ribosomal RNA (rRNA) oxidation**: Impairs translation fidelity and capacity
- **Messenger RNA (mRNA) oxidation**: 8-oxo-dG in mRNA causes ribosomal stalling and truncated protein products
- **Transfer RNA (tRNA) oxidation**: Disrupts aminoacylation and codon-anticodon pairing
- **Non-coding RNA oxidation**: [[notes/_link/MicroRNA|microRNA]] oxidation deregulates gene expression

oxidation of RNA is elevated in [[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]] ([[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]], [[notes/_link/Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]], and [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]]), often preceding detectable DNA damage.

## DNA Repair Pathways for Oxidative Lesions

### Base Excision Repair (BER)
The primary repair pathway for oxidative base damage:
1. **[[DNA Glycosylase]] recognition**:
   - [[OGG1]] (8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase): Removes 8-oxo-dG, FapyG
   - [[NEIL1/NEL2]]: Removes FapyA, FapyG, thymine glycol
   - [[NTHL1]]: Removes thymine glycol, 5-hydroxycytosine
   - [[MUTYH]]: Removes adenine mispaired with 8-oxo-dG
2. **AP endonuclease** ([[APE1]]): Cleaves the abasic site
3. **Polymerase β**: Inserts correct nucleotide
4. **Ligase III/XRCC1**: Seals the nick

Single nucleotide BER is the most common, but **long-patch BER** (2–12 nucleotides) is used when the 5′-end is blocked by 5′-deoxyribose phosphate.

### Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER)
Removes bulky helix-distorting lesions, including some oxidative products like cyclopurine dinucleotides (guanine or adenine cyclonucleosides formed by •OH attack). Transcription-coupled NER (TC-NER) specifically removes oxidative lesions from transcribed strands, linking oxidative damage to [[Cockayne Syndrome]] pathology.

### Direct Reversal
- **[[MGMT]]** (O⁶-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase): Repairs O⁶-alkylguanine adducts
- **[[ABH]] family** ([[ALKBH2]], [[ALKBH3]]): Demethylate 1-methyladenine and 3-methylcytosine via oxidative dealkylation

### Translesion Synthesis (TLS)
Specialized DNA polymerases (Pol η, Pol ι, Pol κ, Pol ζ, and Rev1) bypass unrepaired oxidative lesions:
- **Pol η**: Inserts A opposite 8-oxo-dG (error-free) or C (error-prone)
- **Pol κ**: Error-free bypass of thymine glycol
- **Pol ζ + Rev1**: Extends past lesions after insertion by another TLS polymerase

## Epigenetic Consequences of Oxidative DNA Damage

Oxidative modifications to DNA intersect with [[notes/_link/Epigenome|epigenetic]] regulation:

- **TET enzyme inhibition**: 8-oxo-dG adjacent to 5-methylcytosine inhibits [[notes/_link/TET Enzymes]], altering [[DNA demethylation]] dynamics
- **OGG1-mediated gene regulation**: OGG1 binding to 8-oxo-dG in gene promoters recruits [[notes/_link/NFKB]] and [[notes/_link/KRAS]], activating transcription of inflammatory genes
- **DNMT inhibition**: Oxidized CpG dinucleotides inhibit [[DNA methyltransferase]] activity, potentially causing [[notes/_link/Epigenetic Drift]]
- **Histone modification crosstalk**: Oxidative DNA damage triggers [[notes/_link/Histone Modification|histone]] H2AX phosphorylation (γH2AX) and local [[notes/_link/Chromatin]] remodeling

## Clinical and Pathological Significance

### Cancer
Oxidative DNA damage drives [[notes/_link/Genomic Instability]], a hallmark of cancer:
- **Mutational signatures**: 8-oxo-dG causes G→T transversions (COSMIC mutational signature SBS18)
- [[notes/_link/p53]] inactivation: C→T transitions at CpG sites in [[TP53]] are linked to oxidative DNA damage
- **ROS-driven clonal evolution**: Chronic [[notes/_link/Inflammation]] creates mutagenic microenvironments in [[Colorectal Cancer]], [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], and [[Breast Cancer]]

### Aging and Neurodegeneration
Accumulation of unrepaired oxidative DNA lesions correlates with organismal aging:
- **Telomere attrition**: Oxidative damage accelerates [[notes/_link/Telomere Attrition]] by inducing single-strand breaks in telomeric sequences (TTAGGG is G-rich and oxidation-prone)
- **Reduced DNA repair capacity**: Aging tissues show decreased OGG1 and APE1 activity
- **Somatic mtDNA mutations**: Accumulation of mtDNA deletions in post-mitotic tissues (muscle, brain)

### Clinical Biomarker
[[notes/oxidative_stress/8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine]] (8-OHdG/8-oxo-dG) measured in urine, plasma, or tissue is the most widely used biomarker of oxidative DNA damage. Elevated levels are reported in:
- [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]]
- [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus]]
- [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]]
- [[notes/_link/Schizophrenia]]
- [[notes/oxidative_stress/Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease]]
