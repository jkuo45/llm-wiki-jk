# Nucleic Acids

Nucleic acids—DNA and RNA—are biopolymers of nucleotide monomers responsible for the storage and expression of genetic information in all living organisms. They are major targets of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], and oxidative damage to nucleic acids is a fundamental mechanism of [[Oxidative Stress]]-driven mutation, aging, and disease.

## Oxidative Damage to DNA

The guanine base is the most oxidation-prone nucleobase because it has the lowest ionization potential. The signature lesion is **[[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine]] (8-OHdG / 8-oxoG)**, formed when [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] (generated via the [[Fenton Reaction]]) add to C8 of guanine. 8-oxoG mispairs with adenine during replication, causing G→T transversion mutations—a major source of [[Genomic Instability]] and [[Cancer]] initiation. Other lesions include thymine glycol, 5-hydroxycytosine, and single- and double-strand breaks. RNA is likewise oxidized (8-oxoG in mRNA), which can alter translation and trigger innate immune sensing.

## Sources of DNA-Damaging Species

The primary DNA-attacking radical is the [[Hydroxyl Radicals]], which arises when [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] meet catalytic Fe²⁺/Cu⁺ via the [[Fenton Reaction]]. [[Peroxynitrite]] (from superoxide + [[Nitric Oxide]]) nitrates guanine to 8-nitroguanine and oxidizes bases. Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is especially vulnerable because it sits near the [[Mitochondria]]l electron transport chain—the main superoxide source—and lacks the robust chromatin packaging of nuclear DNA, though it does benefit from proximity to [[Superoxide Dismutase]] (SOD2).

## Repair and Consequences

Cells counter oxidative DNA damage through multiple pathways: [[Base Excision Repair]] (BER) handles small lesions such as 8-oxoG via OGG1 glycosylase; [[Nucleotide Excision Repair]] addresses bulkier adducts (including MDA-derived M₁dG); and mismatch/strand-break repair fixes replication errors and breaks. Persistent, unrepaired damage drives [[Senescence]] (DNA damage-induced cell-cycle arrest via p53/p21) and apoptosis. Heavy damage activates [[PARP1]], which consumes [[NAD+]] to signal repair; excessive PARP1 activation can deplete NAD⁺ and trigger parthanatos cell death—linking oxidative DNA damage to bioenergetic failure.

## Disease and Biomarker Relevance

Urinary 8-OHdG is a widely used, non-invasive biomarker of systemic oxidative DNA damage, elevated in [[Cancer]], [[Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]], and [[Aging]]. Mitochondrial 8-oxoG accumulation is implicated in [[Parkinson's Disease]] and [[Alzheimer's Disease]]. Targeting DNA repair pathways and lowering ROS (e.g., with [[N-Acetylcysteine]] or [[MitoQ]]) are strategies to reduce oxidative mutagenesis.
