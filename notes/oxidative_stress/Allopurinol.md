# Allopurinol

**Allopurinol** is a hypoxanthine analog that inhibits [[Xanthine Oxidase|Xanthine Oxidase (XO)]], used primarily to lower [[Uric Acid|uric acid]] in [[Gout]] and [[Tumor Lysis Syndrome]], and increasingly recognized for its antioxidant and cardiovascular protective properties. Beyond urate lowering, its ability to suppress XO-derived [[Superoxide Radicals|reactive oxygen species]] has made it a candidate redox-modulating therapy.

## Chemistry & Mechanism

Allopurinol is structurally analogous to hypoxanthine and is oxidized by XO to its active metabolite **oxypurinol** (alloxanthine). Both compounds bind the molybdenum–pterin active site of XO. Allopurinol acts as a suicide substrate: XO converts it to oxypurinol, which then forms a tight, slowly reversible (effectively permanent) complex with the reduced (Mo⁴⁺) form of the enzyme. This blocks the two-step oxidation of hypoxanthine → xanthine → uric acid, lowering both serum and urinary urate.

Critically for redox biology, XO is a major enzymatic source of [[Superoxide Radicals]] during catalysis: each oxidation of hypoxanthine or xanthine transfers electrons to O₂, generating superoxide and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]. Inhibiting XO therefore reduces XO-derived ROS. This antioxidant effect is especially relevant in [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], where XO activity surges as ATP degrades to hypoxanthine and xanthine, and reoxygenation drives a burst of oxidant production that damages endothelium and parenchyma.

## Clinical Applications

- **[[Gout]] and Hyperuricemia**: First-line urate-lowering therapy. Typical starting dose 100 mg/d (50 mg in renal impairment), titrated to serum urate <6 mg/dL. Major adverse effects include hypersensitivity (DRESS, Stevens-Johnson syndrome), strongly associated with the HLA-B*5801 allele—genotyping is recommended in high-prevalence populations.
- **[[Cardiovascular Disease]]**: Multiple trials show allopurinol improves endothelial function (flow-mediated dilation), reduces arterial stiffness, and slows progression of left ventricular hypertrophy and heart failure in chronic kidney disease. These benefits are attributed to reduced oxidative stress and improved [[eNOS]]/NO bioavailability rather than urate lowering alone.
- **[[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]]**: Investigated to attenuate reperfusion injury in myocardial infarction, stroke, and organ transplantation by blocking XO-derived ROS.
- **[[Tumor Lysis Syndrome]]**: Standard prophylaxis (with rasburicase for high-risk cases) during chemotherapy of hematologic malignancies.

## Research Relevance & Limitations

While allopurinol's antioxidant actions are mechanistically attractive, the clinical magnitude of cardiovascular benefit remains debated; large outcome trials (e.g., ALL-HEART) have yielded mixed results. The related agent febuxostat is a non-purine XO inhibitor with similar redox effects. Allopurinol also interacts with [[Azathioprine]] and mercaptopurine (XO-metabolized drugs), requiring dose reduction to avoid bone-marrow toxicity.
