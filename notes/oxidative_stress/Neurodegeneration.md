# Neurodegeneration

## Definition
Neurodegeneration is the progressive loss of structure or function of neurons, including the death of neurons. 

## Role of Oxidative Stress
The brain is particularly vulnerable to [[notes/comt/Oxidative stress]] due to its high oxygen consumption, high lipid content (prone to [[Lipid Peroxidation]]), and relatively low antioxidant defenses. Oxidative damage to [[Proteins]] (leading to aggregation) and [[Mitochondria]] (leading to energy failure) are central drivers of neurodegenerative processes.

## Related Conditions
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]: Characterized by [[Amyloid-beta]] plaques and [[Tau protein]] tangles, both linked to oxidative damage.
- [[Parkinson's Disease]]: Involves the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the [[notes/_link/Substantia nigra]], often driven by mitochondrial dysfunction.
- [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]] (ALS): Linked to mutations in [[Superoxide Dismutase]] (SOD1) and oxidative damage to motor neurons.

## Connections
- [[notes/comt/Oxidative stress]]: A primary cause and consequence of neuronal damage.
- [[Lipid Peroxidation]]: Brain tissue is rich in polyunsaturated fats, making it a major target.
- [[Mitochondria]]: Dysfunction in these organelles is a common hallmark of neurodegenerative diseases.
