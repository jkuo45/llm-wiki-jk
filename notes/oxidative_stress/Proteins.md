# Proteins

Proteins are large biomolecules of one or more amino-acid chains that perform catalysis, structure, transport, and signaling. They are major targets of [[Oxidative Stress]]: radical-mediated damage—termed **protein oxidation**—alters side chains, fragments backbones, and forms cross-links, causing loss of enzymatic activity, mis-folding, and accumulation of aggregates.

## Sites of Oxidative Modification

Different amino acids are attacked preferentially. **Cysteine** and **methionine** are oxidized to sulfenic/sulfinic acid and methionine sulfoxide, often reversibly—these modifications serve redox signaling roles regulated by [[Thioredoxin]] and methionine sulfoxide reductase. **Histidine**, **tyrosine**, **tryptophan**, and **phenylalanine** undergo ring hydroxylation/nitration (e.g., 3-nitrotyrosine from [[Peroxynitrite]]). **Proline**, **arginine**, **lysine**, and **threonine** are vulnerable to backbone cleavage. The most commonly measured marker is **protein carbonylation**—introduction of aldehyde/ketone groups on lysine, arginine, proline, and threonine—a stable, irreversible lesion quantified as a proxy for oxidative protein damage.

## Functional Consequences

Oxidation can be deleterious or regulatory. Low-level, reversible oxidation of cysteines in transcription factors and phosphatases (e.g., PTEN, PTP1B) transmits redox signals. However, extensive oxidation inactivates enzymes (e.g., [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] from oxidized ETC complexes amplifies ROS), promotes hydrophobic exposure and aggregation, and forms advanced oxidation protein products (AOPPs) that engage [[RAGE]] inflammatory signaling. Carbonylated proteins resist degradation and accumulate with [[Aging]] and in [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]] (e.g., oxidized tau, α-synuclein).

## Cellular Responses and Clearance

Cells counteract oxidized proteins through antioxidant enzymes—[[Glutathione Peroxidase]], [[Catalase]], and [[Thioredoxin]] (reducing methionine sulfoxide reductase and peroxiredoxin)—and through degradation. The [[Unfolded Protein Response]] handles oxidized proteins in the ER; globally, [[Autophagy]] (including aggrephagy) and the proteasome clear damaged species. Failure of clearance shifts the proteome toward a damaged, pro-inflammatory state—the broader concept of [[Carbonyl Stress]].

## Disease Relevance

Protein oxidation markers rise in [[Cardiovascular Disease]] (oxLDL-apoB carbonylation), [[Diabetes Mellitus]] (HbA1c and AOPPs), COPD, and neurodegeneration. Interventions lowering oxidative protein damage include [[N-Acetylcysteine]] (GSH repletion), [[Vitamin E]], and NRF2 activators. Mild oxidative modification can also be hormetic, inducing cytoprotective repair pathways.
