# ADMA

**ADMA (Asymmetric dimethylarginine)** is an endogenous inhibitor of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase|nitric oxide synthase (NOS)]] produced by post-translational methylation of arginine residues in proteins by [[Protein arginine methyltransferases|PRMTs]] and released during proteolysis. It is a central mediator linking [[Oxidative Stress]], endothelial dysfunction, and cardiovascular disease through impairment of nitric oxide bioavailability.

## Formation & Metabolism

ADMA is generated when protein arginine methyltransferase enzymes (PRMT1 primarily) methylate arginine side chains on nucleolar and other cellular proteins. During normal protein turnover, methylated arginines are released as free ADMA, symmetric dimethylarginine (SDMA), and monomethylarginine (MMA). ADMA is cleared almost exclusively by dimethylarginine dimethylaminohydrolase ([[DDAH]]), which hydrolyzes it to dimethylamine and L-citrulline; a minor fraction is excreted renally. SDMA, by contrast, is not a NOS inhibitor but competes for cationic amino acid transport, indirectly raising ADMA.

## Mechanism of NOS Inhibition

ADMA competitively inhibits all NOS isoforms ([[eNOS]], nNOS, iNOS) at the L-arginine binding site, with an inhibition constant (Ki) in the low micromolar range. At physiologically elevated concentrations (~0.5–1 μM, versus ~0.3 μM in healthy subjects), ADMA significantly reduces [[eNOS]] activity. Critically, partial eNOS inhibition promotes [[eNOS uncoupling|eNOS uncoupling]]: when tetrahydrobiopterin (BH₄) is limiting, the enzyme shifts from NO synthesis to [[Superoxide Radicals|superoxide]] generation, converting a protective vasodilator enzyme into a pro-oxidant source. This represents a switch from redox-protective to redox-damaging signaling.

## The Oxidative Stress Feed-Forward Loop

ADMA levels rise in conditions of [[Oxidative Stress]] because reactive oxygen species inactivate [[DDAH]] via oxidation of its cysteine residues, reducing ADMA clearance. The resulting ADMA accumulation further suppresses eNOS and promotes uncoupling, generating more superoxide—a self-amplifying cycle: oxidative stress → DDAH inactivation → ADMA accumulation → eNOS uncoupling → more ROS. This loop is central to the progression of endothelial dysfunction.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Elevated ADMA is an independent risk factor for [[Cardiovascular Disease]], [[Endothelial Dysfunction]], [[Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Hypertension]], and chronic kidney disease. The plasma L-arginine:ADMA ratio is a biomarker of NO bioavailability and cardiovascular risk. ADMA predicts adverse outcomes in chronic renal failure, pulmonary hypertension, and preeclampsia. Therapeutic strategies aimed at lowering ADMA include enhancing DDAH activity (e.g., via [[Folic Acid]] or_statins), increasing L-arginine substrate availability, and reducing oxidative stress directly. Allopurinol and [[MitoQ]]-style interventions that lower XO-derived ROS indirectly improve the ADMA–DDAH axis.
