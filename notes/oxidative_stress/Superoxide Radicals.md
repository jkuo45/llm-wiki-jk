# Superoxide Radicals

Superoxide radicals (O₂•⁻) are reactive oxygen species (ROS) formed by the addition of a single electron to molecular oxygen (O₂). They are the "primary" ROS that initiate formation of [[Hydrogen Peroxide]], [[Hydroxyl Radicals]], and [[Peroxynitrite]].

## Generation Mechanisms

Mitochondrial superoxide is produced mainly at Complex I (during reverse electron transport) and Complex III (via semiquinone autoxidation). Under physiological conditions ~1–3% of consumed O₂ escapes as superoxide, rising under [[Hypoxia]] or respiratory-chain impairment. Extramitochondrial sources include [[NADPH Oxidase]] (NOX1–5, DUOX), which actively generates superoxide; [[Xanthine Oxidase]] during purine catabolism; and [[Monoamine oxidase]], whose amine catabolism yields [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]. In phagocytes, the [[Respiratory Burst]] via NOX2 (gp91phox) produces superoxide for microbial killing.

## Disproportionation and SOD

At neutral pH, spontaneous dismutation is slow (~2 × 10⁵ M⁻¹s⁻¹), but [[Superoxide Dismutase]] accelerates it to ~2 × 10⁹ M⁻¹s⁻¹—diffusion-limited. Three isoforms exist: cytosolic CuZnSOD (SOD1), mitochondrial MnSOD (SOD2), and extracellular CuZnSOD (SOD3). The product is always [[Hydrogen Peroxide]], further detoxified by [[Catalase]] or [[Glutathione Peroxidase]].

## Reaction with Metal Centers and Other Radicals

Superoxide preferentially oxidizes iron–sulfur ([4Fe-4S]) dehydratases such as [[Aconitase]] (TCA cycle) and fumarase, releasing Fe²⁺ that fuels the [[Fenton Reaction]]—a feed-forward amplifier of [[Hydroxyl Radicals]]. Its near-diffusion-limited reaction (k ≈ 6.7 × 10⁹ M⁻¹s⁻¹) with [[Nitric Oxide]] forms [[Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻), a potent nitrating species underlying much NO-overproduction toxicity. Superoxide also reduces cytochrome c (Fe³⁺→Fe²⁺), modulating [[Apoptosis]] via the permeability transition pore.

## Pathological Significance

Superoxide overproduction drives [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] (reoxygenation burst from damaged [[Mitochondria]] and [[Xanthine Oxidase]]). In [[Cardiovascular Disease]], NOX-derived superoxide consumes [[Nitric Oxide]] (an "NO sink"), impairing vasodilation and promoting [[Atherosclerosis]]. In [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]] such as [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]], SOD1 mutations cause toxic gain-of-function increasing superoxide and aggregation. Genetic NOX2 defects cause chronic granulomatous disease—recurrent infections from failed [[Respiratory Burst]].

## Connections

- [[Oxidative Stress]]: Superoxide is a major initiator of oxidative damage.
- [[Superoxide Dismutase]]: Primary enzyme neutralizing superoxide to H₂O₂.
- [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]: Invariable dismutation product requiring further detox.
- [[Hydroxyl Radicals]]: Generated via Fenton chemistry fed by superoxide-released iron.
- [[Peroxynitrite]]: Near-instant product with nitric oxide.
- [[Nitric Oxide]]: Consumed by superoxide, impairing vascular signaling.
- [[Aconitase]]: Fe-S enzyme inactivated by superoxide, releasing catalytic iron.
- [[Mitochondria]]: Principal endogenous source of superoxide.
