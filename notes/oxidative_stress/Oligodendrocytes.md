# Oligodendrocytes

**Oligodendrocytes** are the myelinating glial cells of the central nervous system (CNS), producing the myelin sheath that enables rapid saltatory conduction along axons. They are exceptionally vulnerable to [[Oxidative Stress]] due to their unique metabolic, iron-handling, and lipid-rich characteristics—a vulnerability central to demyelinating and neurodegenerative disease.

## Structure and Myelination

A single oligodendrocyte extends multiple processes, each wrapping ~20–50 axonal segments in concentric membrane layers rich in cholesterol and glycolipids. Myelin is ~70% lipid (high in polyunsaturated fatty acids) and ~30% protein (MBP, PLP, MAG). Maintaining this metabolically expensive structure demands high ATP from [[Mitochondria]] and abundant membrane synthesis, both of which generate and interface with reactive oxygen species.

## Vulnerability to Oxidative Stress

Oligodendrocytes are sensitized to ROS/RNS through several mechanisms:

- **High iron content**: They accumulate the highest intracellular iron of any CNS cell type, required by iron-dependent enzymes of lipid synthesis such as [[Aconitase]]. This iron pool can catalyze [[Fenton Reaction]] chemistry, generating [[Hydroxyl Radicals]].
- **Low [[Glutathione]]**: Compared with astrocytes, oligodendrocytes have relatively low GSH and limited capacity to upregulate antioxidant defenses, leaving them poorly buffered against oxidants.
- **High ATP demand**: Intensive mitochondrial activity during myelination produces substantial [[Superoxide Radicals]] as a byproduct.
- **Lipid-rich membranes**: Myelin's high PUFA content makes it a prime substrate for [[Lipid Peroxidation]], yielding toxic aldehydes ([[Malondialdehyde]], 4-HNE) that damage myelin proteins.

## Consequences of Oxidative Damage

Oxidative injury to oligodendrocytes causes myelin breakdown, mitochondrial depolarization, and cell death. Iron-mediated peroxidation of myelin basic protein disrupts the integrity of the sheath, slowing or blocking conduction. Because oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs) also depend on redox-sensitive signaling for differentiation, chronic oxidative stress impairs remyelination—a key reason lesions fail to repair in demyelinating disease.

## Clinical Relevance

Oligodendrocyte loss and oxidative myelin damage are hallmarks of [[Multiple Sclerosis]] (both active lesions and normal-appearing white matter), periventricular leukomalacia ([[Hypoxia-ischemia]]) in preterm infants, and [[Neurodegeneration]] (e.g., in Alzheimer's and ALS white-matter involvement). Protecting oligodendrocytes—via iron chelation, NRF2 activation, or [[MitoQ]]-style mitochondrial antioxidants—is a therapeutic goal in remyelination strategies.
