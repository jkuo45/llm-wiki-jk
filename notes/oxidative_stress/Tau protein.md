# Tau protein
Tau proteins are proteins that perform the function of stabilizing microtubules, and their aggregation is a key pathological feature of [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]].

## Oxidative Stress and Tau Pathology

### Redox-Sensitive Kinases and Tau Hyperphosphorylation
Tau phosphorylation at specific serine/threonine residues (including Ser202, Thr205, Ser396, Ser404) is regulated by a balance between [[Kinase]] and [[Phosphatase]] activities. [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] potently activates kinases that hyperphosphorylate tau, promoting its dissociation from [[Microtubules]] and self-aggregation into paired helical filaments (PHFs). [[notes/_link/GSK3|Glycogen synthase kinase-3β]] (GSK-3β) is the principal tau kinase, and its activity is enhanced by ROS-mediated inhibition of [[Akt]]/[[PKC]] survival signaling. [[CDK5]] — activated by [[p25]] (a cleavage product of [[p35]] by [[Calpain]] — an enzyme activated by Ca²⁺ influx from oxidative [[notes/oxidative_stress/Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] damage) — also contributes to tau hyperphosphorylation. [[notes/oxidative_stress/p38 MAPK]] and [[JNK]], both activated by ROS, additionally target tau at disease-relevant phospho-epitopes.

### Direct Oxidative Modifications of Tau
Tau protein itself is subject to multiple oxidative post-translational modifications that modulate its aggregation propensity:
* **Cysteine oxidation**: Tau contains two cysteine residues (Cys291 and Cys322). Under oxidizing conditions, intermolecular disulfide bridge formation between Cys322 residues promotes tau dimerization — a critical nucleation step for aggregation. In the reducing environment maintained by [[notes/_link/Glutathione]], Cys residues are kept reduced, preventing dimerization.
* **Methionine sulfoxidation**: Tau's methionine residues (Met) are oxidized to [[methionine sulfoxide]] (MetO) by ROS. MetO formation in the microtubule-binding repeat region alters tau conformation and impairs microtubule binding.
* **4-Hydroxynonenal adduction**: The lipid peroxidation product [[4-Hydroxynonenal]] (4-HNE) forms covalent Michael adducts with tau's cysteine and lysine residues, promoting tau oligomerization and inhibiting proteasomal degradation. 4-HNE-modified tau accumulates in [[Neurofibrillary tangles]] in [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]].
* **Tyrosine nitration**: [[notes/oxidative_stress/Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻) nitrates tau at Tyr18, Tyr29, and Tyr394, generating [[3-nitrotyrosine]]. Nitration impairs tau-microtubule interaction and enhances PHF formation.

### Tau and Mitochondrial Dysfunction: A Bidirectional Relationship
Tau pathology and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] dysfunction engage in a mutually reinforcing cycle. In [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]], pathological tau accumulates within mitochondria, where it binds to [[VDAC]] (voltage-dependent anion channel) and impairs the [[mitochondrial permeability transition pore]] (mPTP), increasing ROS leakage and reducing [[notes/_link/ATP]] production. Conversely, mitochondrial ROS activate the kinases that hyperphosphorylate tau. Tau knockout mice are resistant to mitochondrial toxin-induced neurodegeneration, and tau reduction mitigates [[notes/oxidative_stress/Amyloid-beta]]-induced mitochondrial dysfunction — positioning tau as a critical mediator of oxidative damage in [[notes/oxidative_stress/Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]].

### Tau Acetylation and Its Redox Sensitivity
Tau acetylation at Lys residues (particularly Lys174, Lys274, Lys280, Lys281) — mediated by [[p300]]/[[CREB-binding protein]] (CBP) acetyltransferases — is elevated in AD and promotes tau aggregation by neutralizing positive charges in the microtubule-binding region. The deacetylase [[SIRT1]] (a [[NAD⁺]]-dependent enzyme) removes tau acetylation; however, SIRT1 activity is reduced under oxidative conditions due to NAD⁺ depletion and direct ROS-mediated inhibition. Reduced SIRT1 activity leads to increased tau acetylation, providing another mechanism through which [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] drives tau pathology.

### Clinical Correlates and Therapeutic Directions
Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) levels of tau phosphorylated at Thr181 (p-tau181), Thr217 (p-tau217), and total tau are established biomarkers of tau pathology in AD. [[Positron emission tomography]] (PET) with tau-binding radiotracers ([[Florzolotau|18F-florzolotau]] [previously 18F-THK5351], [[Flortaucipir|18F-flortaucipir]] [AV-1451/Tauvid]) enables in vivo visualization of tau burden. Therapeutic strategies targeting tau–oxidative stress interplay include [[Methylene blue]] (inhibits tau aggregation and acts as a redox cycler), [[N-acetylcysteine|N-Acetylcysteine]] (replenishes [[notes/_link/Glutathione]], thereby reducing cysteine oxidation-mediated tau dimerization), and [[GSK-3β inhibitors]] ([[Lithium]], [[Tideglusib]]).
