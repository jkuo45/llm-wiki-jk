# Peroxynitrite

## Definition
Peroxynitrite ($ONOO^-$) is a powerful oxidant and nitrating agent formed by the near-instantaneous reaction between [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]]. It is a key mediator of "nitrative stress."

## Biological Role
- **Source:** Formed in tissues where both superoxide and nitric oxide are present, particularly at sites of [[Immunity|inflammation]].
- **Defense:** Can be used by macrophages to kill intracellular pathogens.

## Impact and Toxicity
- **Nitrative Stress:** Causes the permanent modification of proteins through tyrosine nitration, which often inactivates enzymes.
- **Macromolecular Damage:** Can cross cell membranes and cause DNA strand breaks and lipid peroxidation.
- **Bioavailability of NO:** Depletes [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]], leading to impaired vasodilation and endothelial dysfunction.

## Formation Kinetics and Chemical Fate
The reaction between [[Superoxide Radicals]] ($O_2^{\bullet-}$) and [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] (NO•) proceeds at a near-diffusion-limited rate constant of $k \approx 1.6 \times 10^{10}$ M⁻¹s⁻¹ — approximately 3–4 times faster than the dismutation of superoxide by [[notes/_link/Superoxide Dismutase]] (SOD). This kinetic favorability means that wherever NO• and $O_2^{\bullet-}$ are simultaneously produced (e.g., activated [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]], [[notes/_link/Endothelial Cells]], neurons), peroxynitrite formation is unavoidable. The rate of formation is a function of the product of the steady-state concentrations of both precursors; thus, even modest elevations in either radical can dramatically increase peroxynitrite production.

Peroxynitrite is a relatively stable anion at alkaline pH (t½ ~1 s at pH 7.4) but is protonated to peroxynitrous acid (ONOOH, $pK_a \approx 6.8$) under physiological conditions. ONOOH is highly unstable, undergoing two competing transformations: (1) **homolytic cleavage** to hydroxyl radical (•OH) and nitrogen dioxide (•NO₂), with a ~30% yield — this pathway generates the extremely reactive [[Hydroxyl Radicals]]; and (2) **isomerization** to nitrate (NO₃⁻), a harmless end-product. The •NO₂ generated can diffuse within the cell to nitrate tyrosine residues in proteins, while •OH mediates local [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and [[notes/_link/DNA Damage]]. Peroxynitrite also reacts directly with $CO_2$ (present at ~1–2 mM in biological fluids) to form the carbonate radical anion ($CO_3^{\bullet-}$) and nitrosoperoxycarbonate (ONO₂CO₂⁻), both potent one-electron oxidants that extend the damaging radius beyond that of peroxynitrite itself.

## Protein Modification: Tyrosine Nitration and Cysteine Oxidation
The hallmark of peroxynitrite-mediated damage is **3-nitrotyrosine** (3-NT) formation, an irreversible post-translational modification that serves as a specific biomarker of nitrative stress. The reaction proceeds via two pathways: (1) •NO₂, formed from ONOOH homolysis or CO₂-catalyzed decomposition, attacks the ortho position of tyrosine phenoxyl radicals to yield 3-nitrotyrosine; (2) direct addition of ONOO⁻ to the tyrosine phenolic ring forms an intermediate that rearranges with oxygen retention. Approximately 20–50 proteins are preferentially nitrated in vivo, including [[MnSOD]] (SOD2, inactivated by nitration of Tyr34), [[notes/_link/Cytochrome c Oxidase]] (Complex IV of the respiratory chain), [[Aconitase]], [[notes/_link/Glutamine Synthetase]], and the [[notes/_link/Sarcoplasmic Reticulum Ca²⁺-ATPase]] (SERCA). Tyrosine nitration typically inactivates the target enzyme, causing loss of function and, for mitochondrial proteins, bioenergetic failure.

Peroxynitrite also directly oxidizes cysteine thiols (Cys-SH) to sulfenic acid (Cys-SOH), sulfinic acid (Cys-SO₂H), and, with excess oxidant, the irreversible sulfonic acid (Cys-SO₃H). The reaction with [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] (GSH) dominates the cellular defense against peroxynitrite: $ONOO^- + GSH \rightarrow GSOH + NO_2^-$, followed by GSH disulfide (GSSG) formation. The rate constant ($k \approx 1.3 \times 10^3$ M⁻¹s⁻¹ at pH 7.4) makes GSH a kinetically relevant scavenger only at millimolar concentrations, which are present in most cell types. In [[notes/_link/Mitochondria]], the oxidation of [[notes/_link/Thioredoxin]] 2 (Trx2) and the [[notes/_link/Permeability Transition Pore]] components by peroxynitrite triggers mitochondrial swelling and [[notes/_link/Apoptosis]].

## DNA and Lipid Damage
Peroxynitrite reacts with [[DNA]] primarily through guanine oxidation (see [[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine]]) and the formation of 8-nitroguanine, a specific nitrative lesion that undergoes rapid depurination, generating abasic sites. 8-nitroguanine is also a mutagenic lesion, inducing G→T transversions, and has been detected in [[notes/_link/Helicobacter pylori]]-infected gastric epithelium and in [[Cholangiocarcinoma]] tissues, linking nitrative stress to infection-associated carcinogenesis. In [[Cell membranes]], peroxynitrite initiates [[Lipid Peroxidation]] by abstracting hydrogen atoms from PUFAs; the resulting lipid peroxyl radicals propagate chain reactions and generate [[Malondialdehyde]] and 4-hydroxynonenal. Peroxynitrite also nitrates unsaturated fatty acids (e.g., linoleic acid) to form **nitro-fatty acids** (NO₂-FAs), which can act as anti-inflammatory signaling molecules through [[notes/_link/PPARγ|PPARγ]] activation and [[notes/_link/NFKB]] inhibition — representing a potential negative feedback loop to limit Nitrative Stress.

## Clinical and Pathological Relevance
Peroxynitrite is centrally implicated in diverse pathologies:

- **[[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]]**: Peroxynitrite-mediated depletion of [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] impairs endothelium-dependent vasodilation, a hallmark of endothelial dysfunction and the earliest stage of [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]]. 3-Nitrotyrosine is elevated in human atherosclerotic plaques, and plasma nitrotyrosine levels independently predict cardiovascular events. In [[notes/_link/Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], peroxynitrite produced during early reperfusion causes [[notes/_link/Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[notes/_link/Apoptosis]] of cardiomyocytes.
- **[[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]]**: Peroxynitrite nitrates [[notes/_link/Alpha-synuclein]] in Lewy bodies ([[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]]), [[notes/_link/Tau]] protein in neurofibrillary tangles ([[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]]), and TDP-43 aggregates ([[notes/_link/Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]]). Nitrated alpha-synuclein is more prone to aggregation and is resistant to proteasomal degradation, suggesting a direct role in disease progression. Peroxynitrite-induced [[notes/_link/Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] in [[notes/_link/Dopaminergic Neurons]] is an early and critical event in [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]] pathogenesis.
- **[[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus]]**: Peroxynitrite contributes to β-cell destruction in type 1 diabetes and to insulin resistance in type 2 diabetes through nitration of [[notes/_link/Insulin Receptor Substrate]] (IRS) proteins.
- **[[notes/_link/Inflammation]]**: Inflammatory cytokines ([[TNF-alpha]], [[IL-1b]], [[IFN-γ]]) synergistically upregulate iNOS and NOX2, creating a "perfect storm" for peroxynitrite production in [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]], [[notes/_link/Microglia]], and [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]].

Therapeutic strategies include low-molecular-weight peroxynitrite decomposition catalysts (e.g., **FeTMPyP**, **FeTPPS** — iron porphyrins that catalytically isomerize ONOO⁻ to NO₃⁻), [[notes/_link/Uric Acid]] (a physiological peroxynitrite scavenger whose levels are dramatically elevated in humans compared to other mammals), [[notes/_link/Selenium]]-based compounds (ebselen — a GPx mimetic), and [[notes/_link/Melatonin]].
