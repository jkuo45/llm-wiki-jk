# CD36 Receptor
CD36 is a member of the class B scavenger receptor family and is involved in lipid metabolism and inflammation. It plays a significant role in [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]] by mediating the uptake of [[Oxidized LDL]], which is a product of [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

## Oxidative Stress and Functional Mechanisms

### Oxidized LDL Uptake and Foam Cell Formation
CD36 is the primary scavenger receptor for [[Oxidized LDL]] (oxLDL) on [[notes/_link/Macrophage]] and vascular smooth muscle cells. Unlike native [[Cholesterol|LDL]], oxLDL is generated via [[Lipid Peroxidation]] of polyunsaturated fatty acids within the lipoprotein particle, producing reactive aldehydes (MDA, 4-HNE) that covalently modify apolipoprotein B-100. CD36 recognizes these modified phospholipids through a specific oxidized phospholipid-binding domain. Unregulated uptake of oxLDL via CD36 leads to intracellular lipid accumulation and [[Foam Cells]] transformation — a defining event in [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]]. This process is amplified by [[Oxidative Stress]], as ROS upregulate CD36 expression through [[notes/_link/PPARγ]]-dependent transcriptional mechanisms.

### Thrombospondin-1 and Angiogenesis
CD36 serves as a receptor for [[Thrombospondin-1]] (TSP-1), a matricellular protein that regulates angiogenesis and vascular homeostasis. TSP-1 binding to CD36 activates a signaling cascade involving [[FYN]] kinase and [[p38 MAPK]], leading to [[notes/_link/Apoptosis]] in microvascular endothelial cells. This anti-angiogenic effect is modulated by the redox state — oxidative conditions enhance CD36 palmitoylation and membrane clustering, potentiating TSP-1 signaling.

### Metabolic Signaling and Insulin Resistance
CD36 facilitates long-chain fatty acid translocation across cell membranes in adipose tissue, heart, and skeletal muscle. Under conditions of [[Oxidative Stress]], CD36-mediated fatty acid uptake promotes intracellular [[Lipotoxicity]] and diacylglycerol (DAG) accumulation, activating [[Protein kinase C]] (PKC) isoforms that impair insulin signaling. Elevated CD36 expression correlates with insulin resistance in [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus]] and [[notes/_link/Metabolic Syndromes]].

### Regulation by Oxidative Stress and Inflammatory Pathways
CD36 expression is induced by [[notes/_link/Oxidized LDL]] itself, [[TNF-alpha]], [[IL-1b]], and [[TGF-beta]] in a redox-dependent manner. The CD36 promoter contains binding sites for [[NF-kappa B]] and [[notes/_link/PPARγ]], both of which are activated by ROS. CD36 also acts as a co-receptor for [[Toll-like Receptor]] 2 and 6 ([[notes/_link/Toll-like Receptor|TLR2/6]]), mediating sterile inflammation in response to oxLDL and [[Amyloid-beta]], thereby linking [[Oxidative Stress]] to innate immune activation.

### Clinical Relevance
CD36 deficiency in humans is rare but associated with altered lipid metabolism and myocardial fatty acid uptake defects. In [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]], CD36 blockade reduces [[Foam Cells]] formation and plaque development in murine models. Soluble CD36 (sCD36) is being investigated as a circulating biomarker of [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]] risk.
