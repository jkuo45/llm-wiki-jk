# VCAM-1
Vascular cell adhesion protein 1 (VCAM-1) is a protein that mediates the adhesion of lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils to vascular endothelium. Its expression is upregulated in response to pro-inflammatory stimuli and is a marker for [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] and early [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]] linked to chronic [[notes/_link/Inflammation]].

## Redox Regulation of VCAM-1 Expression and Function

### Transcriptional Regulation by Oxidative Stress
VCAM-1 expression is primarily regulated at the transcriptional level by binding of [[NF-kappa B]] (specifically the [[p65]]/[[p50]] heterodimer) to two κB response elements within the VCAM-1 promoter. [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] potently activates NF-κB through degradation of its inhibitory subunit [[IκBα]] — a process mediated by the [[IκB kinase]] ([[IKK]]) complex, whose activity is enhanced by [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]]. In [[notes/oxidative_stress/Vascular Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]], [[Exogenous H₂O₂]] ([[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]]) and [[Lipid hydroperoxides]] directly induce VCAM-1 transcription, independently of cytokine stimulation. Additionally, [[notes/oxidative_stress/TNF-alpha]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/IL-1b]] — both of which signal through ROS-dependent pathways — synergistically upregulate VCAM-1 via p38 [[notes/oxidative_stress/p38 MAPK|MAPK]] and [[JNK]] activation, which stabilize VCAM-1 mRNA by binding [[HuR]] (an RNA-binding protein) to its 3'-UTR.

### NADPH Oxidase-Dependent VCAM-1 Induction
The endothelial [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] isoforms [[Nox1]] and [[Nox4]] are major sources of ROS that regulate VCAM-1 expression. [[Oxidized LDL]] ([[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidized LDL]]), via binding to lectin-like oxidized LDL receptor-1 ([[LOX-1]]), activates Nox4, producing [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] that diffuses to the nucleus and potentiates [[NF-kappa B]] binding to the VCAM-1 promoter. [[Shear stress]] — the frictional force of blood flow — suppresses endothelial Nox activity and VCAM-1 expression at physiological levels (15–40 dyn/cm²), whereas oscillatory or low shear stress (characteristic of atherosclerotic-prone regions) enhances Nox expression and VCAM-1 upregulation. This mechano-redox coupling explains the focal distribution of early [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis|atherosclerotic]] lesions at arterial bifurcations.

### VCAM-1/VLA-4 Interactions and Leukocyte Recruitment
VCAM-1 binds to the integrin [[VLA-4]] (α₄β₁ integrin, also known as [[CD49d]]/[[CD29]]) expressed on monocytes, lymphocytes, eosinophils, and basophils. This interaction mediates firm adhesion and transendothelial migration of leukocytes into the subendothelial space — a critical step in [[notes/_link/Inflammation]] and [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]]. The VCAM-1/VLA-4 binding triggers [[outside-in signaling]] within leukocytes, activating [[Rac1]] and [[Notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase|macrophage NADPH oxidase]], generating ROS that facilitate matrix degradation by [[MMP-9]] ([[Gelatinase B]]) during diapedesis. The binding affinity of VLA-4 for VCAM-1 is enhanced under oxidizing conditions due to disulfide bond rearrangement in the integrin extracellular domain.

### Soluble VCAM-1 (sVCAM-1) as a Biomarker
Proteolytic cleavage of membrane-bound VCAM-1 by [[ADAM-17]] ([[TACE]] — TNF-α converting enzyme) releases [[soluble VCAM-1]] (sVCAM-1) into the circulation. sVCAM-1 levels are elevated in [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]], [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Hypertension]], and [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], serving as a biomarker of [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] and systemic [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]]. sVCAM-1 also retains functional activity — it promotes monocyte chemotaxis and induces endothelial [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] activation in a feed-forward loop.

### Therapeutic Modulation of VCAM-1 via Redox Pathways
Several clinically used drugs inhibit VCAM-1 expression through antioxidant mechanisms:
* **[[Statins]]** (e.g., [[Atorvastatin]], [[Rosuvastatin]]): Reduce VCAM-1 expression by inhibiting [[Rac1]] geranylgeranylation (blocking Nox activation) and by upregulating endothelial [[nitric oxide synthase]] ([[eNOS]]), whose product [[notes/oxidative_stress/Nitric Oxide|NO]] inhibits NF-κB.
* **[[PPARα agonists]]** ([[Fibrates]]: [[Fenofibrate]], [[Gemfibrozil]]): Suppress VCAM-1 by interfering with NF-κB transactivation in a PPARα-dependent manner.
* **[[PPARγ agonists]]** ([[Thiazolidinediones]]: [[Pioglitazone]]): Downregulate VCAM-1 through transrepression of NF-κB and AP-1 pathways.
* **[[N-acetylcysteine]]** (NAC): Directly inhibits VCAM-1 transcription by scavenging ROS and replenishing [[notes/_link/Glutathione]].
* **[[Resveratrol]]** and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Polyphenols|dietary polyphenols]]: Suppress VCAM-1 via [[SIRT1]] activation and [[Nrf2]]-dependent [[notes/oxidative_stress/HO-1|HO-1]] induction, which degrades the pro-oxidant heme required for Nox function.

### VCAM-1 in Neuroinflammation and Multiple Sclerosis
In the central nervous system, VCAM-1 is expressed on cerebral [[Endothelial cells]] and facilitates entry of [[encephalitogenic T cells]] across the [[Blood-brain barrier]] (BBB) into the CNS parenchyma in [[Multiple Sclerosis]]. [[Natalizumab]] ([[Tysabri]]) — a humanized monoclonal antibody against α₄ integrin (the VLA-4 subunit) — blocks VCAM-1/VLA-4 interactions, reducing T-cell trafficking into the CNS. The therapeutic efficacy of natalizumab underscores the critical role of VCAM-1 in neuroinflammatory disease. [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] at the BBB upregulates VCAM-1 through local [[TNF-alpha]] and [[IL-1b]] production by activated [[Microglia]], further promoting immune cell infiltration.
