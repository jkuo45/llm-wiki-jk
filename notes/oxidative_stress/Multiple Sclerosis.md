# Multiple Sclerosis
Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a demyelinating disease in which the insulating covers of nerve cells in the brain and spinal cord are damaged. Chronic [[notes/_link/Inflammation]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] contribute to the process of [[Demyelination]], leading to progressive neurological impairment.

## Oxidative Stress Mechanisms in MS Pathology

### Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Demyelinated Axons
Demyelinated axons face a massive energetic crisis. Saltatory conduction — which normally confines ion fluxes to [[Nodes of Ranvier]] — is lost, requiring ATP-dependent Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase to expel Na⁺ entering along the entire axolemma. This demand is met by axonal [[notes/_link/Mitochondria]], which become overwhelmed in chronic lesions. [[Complex I]] (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) and [[notes/_link/Complex IV]] (cytochrome c oxidase) activities are significantly reduced in MS motor cortex and spinal cord mitochondria, leading to electron leakage and [[Superoxide Radicals]] generation. Mitochondrial DNA ([[mtDNA]]) deletions — caused by oxidative damage — accumulate in neuronal [[Substantia nigra]] and motor cortex of MS patients, creating a self-amplifying cycle of bioenergetic failure and ROS production.

### Iron Accumulation and Fenton Chemistry
Iron accumulates in the basal ganglia, thalamus, and periventricular white matter of MS brains, detectable by [[susceptibility-weighted MRI]] ([[SWI]]). Within active MS lesions, iron is released from [[oligodendrocytes]] (the most iron-rich CNS cells) and taken up by activated [[Microglia]] and [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]]. Free ferrous iron (Fe²⁺) catalyzes the [[Fenton reaction]], converting [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] to the highly reactive [[Hydroxyl Radicals|•OH radical]]. [[Lipid Peroxidation]] products — particularly [[4-Hydroxynonenal]] and [[Malondialdehyde]] — are elevated in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and colocalize with damaged [[Myelin]] in MS plaques.

### Myeloperoxidase and Neutrophil Involvement
[[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO) — an enzyme traditionally associated with [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] — is expressed by activated [[Microglia]] and [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]] within MS lesions. MPO utilizes [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] and chloride to generate [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl), a potent oxidant that chlorinates tyrosine residues on myelin proteins, disrupting the [[Myelin basic protein]] (MBP) and [[Proteolipid protein]] (PLP). MPO levels in CSF correlate with disease activity and disability progression. [[Nitrative Stress]] — mediated by [[Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻) formed from the reaction of [[Superoxide Radicals]] with [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] — further nitrates myelin proteins on tyrosine residues ([[3-nitrotyrosine]]), acting as a biomarker and pathogenic mediator.

### Oligodendrocyte Vulnerability to Oxidative Injury
[[Oligodendrocytes]] — the myelin-producing cells of the CNS — are particularly susceptible to [[Oxidative Stress]] due to their high iron content (required for [[myelin]] synthesis), low [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] levels, and elevated [[ATP]] demand. [[Glutathione S-transferase π]] (GSTP1), an oligodendrocyte-specific antioxidant enzyme, is depleted in MS lesions. The resulting oxidative damage triggers [[Ferroptosis]] — iron-dependent, non-apoptotic cell death characterized by [[Lipid Peroxidation]] — in oligodendrocytes. [[GPX4]] overexpression protects oligodendrocytes from ferroptosis in experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis ([[EAE]]) models.

### Therapeutic Targeting of Oxidative Stress in MS
[[Dimethyl fumarate]] (DMF, [[Tecfidera]]) — a first-line MS therapy — exerts its therapeutic effect largely through activation of the [[Nrf2]] pathway. DMF covalently modifies [[Keap1]] (Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1), releasing Nrf2 to translocate to the nucleus and induce antioxidant genes including [[Heme oxygenase-1]], [[NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1]], and [[Glutathione Peroxidase]]. Additional antioxidants under investigation include [[Lipoic acid]] (an eight-carbon dithiol compound that chelates redox-active metals and scavenges ROS), [[N-acetylcysteine|N-Acetylcysteine]] (NAC), and [[Uric acid]] (a peroxynitrite scavenger). The [[mitochondria-targeted antioxidant MitoQ]] has shown neuroprotective effects in EAE models by reducing [[notes/_link/Mitochondrial ROS]] within the inner mitochondrial membrane.
