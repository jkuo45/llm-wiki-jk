# Alpha-Lipoic Acid

## Definition
Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA) is a sulfur-containing compound that acts as a potent "universal" antioxidant due to its ability to function in both water-soluble and fat-soluble environments.

## Biological Role
- **Antioxidant Recycling:** Its reduced form, dihydrolipoic acid (DHLA), can "recharge" other antioxidants like [[Vitamin C]], [[notes/_link/Vitamin E]], and [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] after they have been oxidized.
- **GSH Synthesis:** Increases the availability of cysteine (the rate-limiting precursor) for [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] synthesis.
- **Metabolism:** Serves as a critical cofactor for mitochondrial enzymes involved in energy production (e.g., pyruvate dehydrogenase).

## Impact on Health
- **Protective Effects:** Scavenges [[Hydroxyl Radicals]], [[Singlet Oxygen]], and [[Hypochlorous Acid]].
- **Chelation:** Can bind to toxic metal ions, preventing them from generating ROS.

## Connections
- [[notes/_link/Glutathione]]: ALA is a major restorer of glutathione levels.
- [[notes/_link/Vitamin E]]: ALA helps recycle vitamin E in lipid membranes.
- [[notes/_link/Mitochondria]]: Vital for energy metabolism and managing mitochondrial ROS.
