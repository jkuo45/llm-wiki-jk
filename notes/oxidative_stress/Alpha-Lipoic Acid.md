# Alpha-Lipoic Acid

## Definition
Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA) is a sulfur-containing compound that acts as a potent "universal" antioxidant due to its ability to function in both water-soluble and fat-soluble environments.

## Biological Role
- **Antioxidant Recycling:** Its reduced form, dihydrolipoic acid (DHLA), can "recharge" other antioxidants like [[Vitamin C]], [[Vitamin E]], and [[Glutathione]] after they have been oxidized.
- **GSH Synthesis:** Increases the availability of cysteine (the rate-limiting precursor) for [[Glutathione]] synthesis.
- **Metabolism:** Serves as a critical cofactor for mitochondrial enzymes involved in energy production (e.g., pyruvate dehydrogenase).

## Impact on Health
- **Protective Effects:** Scavenges [[Hydroxyl Radicals]], [[Singlet Oxygen]], and [[Hypochlorous Acid]].
- **Chelation:** Can bind to toxic metal ions, preventing them from generating ROS.

## Biosynthesis and Dietary Sources

Mammals synthesize ALA endogenously in the [[Mitochondria]] via the [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] fatty acid synthesis pathway, where octanoic acid is conjugated to a carrier protein and sulfur atoms are inserted by the enzyme lipoyl synthase (LIAS). This process requires S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and iron-sulfur cluster cofactors, linking ALA biosynthesis to [[Metabolism|one-carbon metabolism]] and iron homeostasis. Despite endogenous production, dietary sources (red meat, organ meats, spinach, broccoli) significantly contribute to tissue levels, and oral supplementation achieves pharmacological concentrations in plasma and tissues.

## Molecular Mechanism of Antioxidant Action

The ALA/DHLA redox couple has a highly negative reduction potential (−0.32 V for the dihydrolipoate/lipoate couple), enabling it to reduce oxidized [[Glutathione]] (GSSG), [[Ascorbic Acid|dehydroascorbate]], and [[Vitamin E|α-tocopheroxyl radicals]] thermodynamically. DHLA directly scavenges [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] including [[Superoxide Radicals]], [[Hydroxyl Radicals]], and [[Hypochlorous Acid]], as well as [[Reactive Nitrogen Species]] such as [[Peroxynitrite]]. Importantly, ALA operates at the membrane interface: the dithiolane ring resides in the aqueous phase while the octanoic acid tail anchors into lipid bilayers, allowing simultaneous protection of membrane lipids and cytosolic components.

## Signaling and Gene Regulation

Beyond direct antioxidant chemistry, ALA modulates redox-sensitive transcription factors. It activates the [[NRF2]]/ARE pathway by modifying reactive cysteine residues on [[Keap1]], leading to nuclear translocation of Nrf2 and induction of phase II detoxification enzymes (glutathione S-transferases, NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1, heme oxygenase-1). Simultaneously, ALA inhibits [[NF-kappa B]] activation by preventing IκB phosphorylation and degradation, suppressing [[Pro-inflammatory Cytokines]] such as [[TNF-alpha]] and [[IL-1β]]. This dual Nrf2 activation and NF-κB suppression underlies ALA's anti-inflammatory properties. Additionally, ALA [[AMPK|activates AMPK]] in skeletal muscle and liver, enhancing [[Glutathione|glucose uptake]] and [[Fatty acid oxidation|fatty acid oxidation]], suggesting therapeutic utility in [[Diabetes Mellitus|insulin resistance]] and [[Metabolic Syndromes]].

## Clinical Applications

The most extensively studied clinical application of ALA is [[Diabetic Neuropathy]]. The ALADIN (Alpha-Lipoic Acid in Diabetic Neuropathy) trials demonstrated that intravenous ALA (600 mg/day) significantly improved neuropathic symptoms, including pain, paresthesia, and numbness, likely through combined antioxidant and [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] support mechanisms. A meta-analysis of four randomized controlled trials confirmed that ALA improves [[Flow-Mediated Dilation|endothelial function]] and reduces biomarkers of [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative damage]] (F2-isoprostanes, [[Malondialdehyde]]) in patients with [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular risk factors]]. Emerging evidence also supports ALA in [[Diabetes Mellitus|gestational diabetes]], [[Age-Related Macular Degeneration]], and as an adjunct in [[Metformin|metformin]] therapy by reducing [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative]] side effects.
