# Asthma
Asthma is a common long-term inflammatory disease of the airways of the lungs. It is characterized by variable and recurring symptoms including reversible airflow obstruction and bronchospasm. [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] and [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] play significant roles in the [[notes/_link/Inflammation]] of the airways. Antioxidants like [[notes/_link/Vitamin E]] have been studied for their potential protective effects.

## Oxidative Mechanisms in Asthma Pathophysiology

### Environmental Oxidant Exposure and Airway Epithelium
The airway epithelium is directly exposed to environmental oxidants, including [[Ozone]] (O₃), particulate matter (PM2.5), and [[cigarette smoke]], which deplete airway surface liquid antioxidants and trigger an inflammatory cascade. These exogenous ROS activate the [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] system in epithelial cells, leading to production of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals]] and [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]]. The transcription factor [[Nrf2]] (nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2) — a master regulator of antioxidant response element (ARE)-driven gene expression — is dysregulated in asthmatic airways, resulting in insufficient upregulation of antioxidant enzymes such as [[notes/oxidative_stress/Catalase]], [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Dismutase]], and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Glutathione Peroxidase]].

### Eosinophil and Neutrophil Contributions
In eosinophilic asthma, activated eosinophils release [[Eosinophil Peroxidase]] (EPO), which utilizes Br⁻ and SCN⁻ to produce hypobromous acid (HOBr) and hypothiocyanite (OSCN⁻). In neutrophilic asthma, [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] recruited to the airways generate copious ROS via [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] and release [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO), producing [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl). These potent oxidants damage airway epithelial cells, increase mucus secretion, and amplify airway hyperresponsiveness.

### Glutathione Depletion and Lipid Peroxidation in Bronchoalveolar Lavage Fluid
The epithelial lining fluid (ELF) normally contains high concentrations of [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] (approximately 100-fold higher than plasma). In asthma, the ratio of reduced (GSH) to oxidized (GSSG) glutathione in [[bronchoalveolar lavage fluid]] is significantly decreased, reflecting severe depletion of antioxidant capacity. Concurrent [[notes/oxidative_stress/Lipid Peroxidation]] generates elevated levels of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Malondialdehyde]] (MDA) and [[8-Isoprostane]] in exhaled breath condensate (EBC), both of which serve as non-invasive biomarkers of airway oxidative injury.

### Clinical and Therapeutic Implications
Oxidative stress in asthma correlates with disease severity, frequency of exacerbations, and corticosteroid resistance. [[notes/_link/N-Acetylcysteine]] (NAC) replenishes intracellular [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] and has been studied as an adjunctive mucolytic and antioxidant. Dietary antioxidant supplementation with [[notes/_link/Vitamin C]] ([[notes/_link/Ascorbic Acid]]), [[notes/_link/Vitamin D]], and [[Polyphenols]] (e.g., [[notes/oxidative_stress/Flavonoids]] from [[quercetin]]) has shown modest benefit in epidemiological studies. The novel therapeutic [[Sulforaphane]] — an Nrf2 activator found in cruciferous vegetables — is under investigation for restoring antioxidant gene expression in asthmatic airway epithelium.
