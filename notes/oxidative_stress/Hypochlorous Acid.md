# Hypochlorous Acid
Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) is a weak acid that forms when chlorine dissolves in water. In biology, it is a highly reactive species generated by activated [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] via the enzyme [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]]. It is a potent component of [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] and contributes significantly to tissue damage during chronic [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]].

## Biochemical Generation: The MPO-H₂O₂-Cl⁻ System

[[notes/oxidative_stress/Hypochlorous Acid]] is generated by [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO), a heme-containing enzyme stored in the azurophilic granules of [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] and, to a lesser extent, in [[notes/_link/Macrophage|monocytes/macrophages]]. The two-electron oxidation reaction is:

  H₂O₂ + Cl⁻ + H⁺ → HOCl + H₂O

The [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] (H₂O₂) substrate is primarily provided by the [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] (NOX2)-generated [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals]] (O₂⁻•) via spontaneous or [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Dismutase]]-catalyzed dismutation. During the [[notes/_link/Respiratory Burst]], activated [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] consume oxygen at rates 10–20 times above basal levels, producing micromolar concentrations of HOCl locally at sites of [[notes/_link/Inflammation]].

## Chemical Reactivity and Targets

HOCl is a potent two-electron oxidant (E°′ = 1.28 V for HOCl/Cl⁻) that reacts preferentially with sulfur-containing amino acids and nitrogen centers.

### Reaction with Thiols and Thioethers
- **Cysteine residues**: HOCl oxidizes cysteine thiols (−SH) to sulfenic acid (−SOH), then to sulfinic (−SO₂H) and sulfonic acid (−SO₃H) derivatives. Sulfenic acid intermediates can form disulfide cross-links, inactivating enzymes such as [[notes/oxidative_stress/Glutathione Peroxidase]] and [[notes/_link/Protein Tyrosine Phosphatases]]
- **Methionine residues**: Oxidized to methionine sulfoxide (MetSO), which can be repaired by methionine sulfoxide reductases (MsrA/MsrB)
- **[[notes/_link/Glutathione]]**: Rapidly consumed by HOCl (k ≈ 10⁷ M⁻¹s⁻¹), depleting the major intracellular antioxidant

### Reaction with Amines and Chloramines
HOCl reacts with primary amines to form **chloramines** (RNHCl):
  RNH₂ + HOCl → RNHCl + H₂O

These chloramines retain oxidizing capacity and serve as **longer-lived secondary oxidants** that propagate tissue damage. The major chloramine species include:
- **Taurine chloramine**: A relatively stable mediator that modulates [[notes/_link/Macrophage]] function
- **Ammonia chloramine (monochloramine, NH₂Cl)**: Membrane-permeable and highly cytotoxic
- **Protein chloramines**: Formed on lysine side chains, leading to protein cross-linking and dysfunction

### Reaction with Aromatic Amino Acids
- **Tyrosine**: HOCl chlorinates tyrosine at the ortho-position to form **3-chlorotyrosine**, a specific biomarker of MPO activity detected in [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis|atherosclerotic plaques]]
- **Tryptophan**: Oxidized to N-chlorotryptophan and further to kynurenine derivatives

### Lipid and Nucleic Acid Oxidation
- **Chlorinated lipids**: HOCl generates chlorohydrins from unsaturated fatty acids, disrupting [[Cell Membrane|membrane]] integrity
- **Cholesterol**: Forms cholesterol chlorohydrins, implicated in [[notes/oxidative_stress/Lipid Peroxidation]] within [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis|atherosclerotic lesions]]
- **[[notes/oxidative_stress/Nucleic Acids]]**: HOCl chlorinates DNA bases (particularly guanine to 8-chloroguanine) and induces strand breaks

## Physiological Role: Pathogen Killing

HOCl is the primary microbicidal oxidant of the innate immune system. It kills pathogens through:
- **Membrane permeabilization**: Chlorination of bacterial lipid bilayers
- **Inactivation of bacterial [[notes/_link/Chaperone|chaperones]]**: Preventing protein folding
- **Oxidation of bacterial [[notes/_link/Iron-sulfur cluster]] proteins**: Disrupting respiration
- **DNA damage**: Inducing lethal chlorinated base adducts
- **Flagellar and motility protein oxidation**: Impairing bacterial invasion

Pathogens have evolved countermeasures, including **AhpC** (alkyl hydroperoxide reductase) and **Ohr** (organic hydroperoxide resistance) proteins, as well as MPO-inhibitory virulence factors in organisms like [[Pseudomonas aeruginosa]].

## Pathological Significance: Host Tissue Damage

When MPO activity is excessive or dysregulated, HOCl contributes to inflammatory tissue pathology:

### Cardiovascular Disease
HOCl-modified [[notes/oxidative_stress/Apolipoprotein B]] in LDL enhances foam cell formation, and 3-chlorotyrosine levels are elevated 6–10 fold in [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis|atherosclerotic plaques]] compared to healthy arteries. HOCl also impairs [[notes/oxidative_stress/ABCA1]]-mediated cholesterol efflux, promoting lipid accumulation.

### Pulmonary Disease
In [[notes/oxidative_stress/Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Asthma]], MPO activity in airway [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] generates HOCl that:
- **Inactivates [[Alpha-1 Antitrypsin]]**: Destroying the major antiprotease shield, leading to [[Emphysema]]
- **Impairs [[notes/oxidative_stress/Catalase]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Glutathione Peroxidase]]**: Depleting airway antioxidant defenses
- **Induces [[Mucus]] hypersecretion**: Through direct irritation and [[notes/oxidative_stress/NF-kappa B]] activation

### Renal Disease
In chronic kidney disease, HOCl-modified proteins (advanced oxidation protein products, AOPPs) accumulate and serve as biomarkers of oxidative damage and predictors of cardiovascular mortality.

### Arthritis
In [[notes/oxidative_stress/Rheumatoid Arthritis]], HOCl modifies cartilage [[notes/oxidative_stress/Proteins]] and [[Proteoglycans]], contributing to joint destruction, and activates [[notes/_link/MMP]] (matrix metalloproteinases) via oxidative activation of their pro-enzymes.

## Therapeutic Implications

- **MPO inhibitors** (e.g., AZD3241, PF-06282999) are in clinical development for neurodegenerative and cardiovascular diseases
- **[[notes/_link/N-Acetylcysteine]]** replenishes [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] and scavenges HOCl
- **[[notes/_link/Ascorbic Acid|Vitamin C]]** and **[[notes/_link/Melatonin]]** directly neutralize HOCl
- **Taurine** supplementation buffers HOCl by forming the more stable taurine chloramine
