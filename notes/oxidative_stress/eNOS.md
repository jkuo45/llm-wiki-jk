# eNOS

**eNOS (Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase, NOS3)** is a constitutively expressed, calcium/calmodulin-dependent isoform of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] that produces low, pulsatile fluxes of [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] critical for vascular homeostasis.

## Structure & Regulation

eNOS is a multidomain enzyme with an N-terminal oxygenase domain (containing heme, tetrahydrobiopterin/BH4, and L-arginine binding sites), a C-terminal reductase domain (binding NADPH, FAD, and FMN), and a calmodulin-binding hinge region. Its activity is tightly regulated by:
- **Ca²⁺/Calmodulin**: Transient increases in intracellular Ca²⁺ trigger calmodulin binding, enabling electron flow from the reductase to oxygenase domain.
- **Phosphorylation**: Activating phosphorylation at Ser1177 (by [[Akt]]/PKB, [[AMPK]], [[PKA]]) increases electron flux; inhibitory phosphorylation at Thr495 decreases activity.
- **Subcellular Localization**: eNOS is targeted to plasma membrane caveolae via N-myristoylation and palmitoylation, where interaction with [[Caveolin-1]] maintains it in an inactive state; Ca²⁺/calmodulin binding displaces caveolin.
- **Protein Interactions**: [[Hsp90]] and [[Dynamin-2]] enhance activity.

## Physiological Functions

- **Vasodilation**: eNOS produces NO that activates soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC) in vascular smooth muscle, raising cGMP → vasorelaxation.
- **Platelet Inhibition**: NO inhibits platelet adhesion and aggregation.
- **Leukocyte Adhesion**: NO suppresses [[VCAM-1]] and [[MCP-1]] expression, reducing leukocyte recruitment.
- **Vascular Barrier**: NO maintains endothelial barrier integrity.

## eNOS Uncoupling

Under conditions of [[BH4]] deficiency or [[L-arginine]] depletion, eNOS becomes "uncoupled" — the reductase domain transfers electrons to molecular oxygen instead of L-arginine, producing [[Superoxide Radicals]] rather than NO. Major causes of uncoupling include:
- [[Peroxynitrite]]-mediated oxidation of BH4 to BH2
- [[ADMA]] (asymmetric dimethylarginine) competitively inhibiting L-arginine binding
- [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] disrupting the zinc-thiolate complex

Uncoupled eNOS is a major contributor to [[notes/oxidative_stress/Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] in [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular disease]], [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]], and [[notes/_link/Hypertension|hypertension]].
