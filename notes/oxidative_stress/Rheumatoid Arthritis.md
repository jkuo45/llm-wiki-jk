# Rheumatoid Arthritis
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a long-term autoimmune disorder that primarily affects joints. It involves persistent synovial [[notes/_link/Inflammation]] and is often exacerbated by [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]], with pro-inflammatory cytokines like [[TNF-alpha]] playing a major role in tissue damage.

## Oxidative Stress Mechanisms in RA Pathology

### Hypoxia-Reoxygenation in the Inflamed Synovium
The rheumatoid synovium is characterized by severe [[Hypoxia]] (pO₂ as low as 1–2%) due to rapid synovial proliferation outpacing vascular supply, combined with compression of microvessels by joint effusion. During movement or joint decompression, reoxygenation of the ischemic synovium produces a burst of [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] analogous to [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]]. This cycle — termed "hypoxia-reperfusion injury" — activates [[notes/oxidative_stress/Xanthine Oxidase]] (converted from [[xanthine dehydrogenase]] under hypoxic conditions), which generates [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals]] and [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] during the reoxygenation phase. [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hypoxia]]-inducible factor 1α ([[HIF-1α]]) is stabilized, driving [[VEGF]] expression and angiogenesis, which paradoxically worsens hypoxia via formation of leaky, dysfunctional vessels.

### NADPH Oxidase Isoforms in RA Synovial Fibroblasts and Immune Cells
Both [[Phagocytic]] and [[non-phagocytic]] [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] (Nox) isoforms are upregulated in RA. [[Nox2]] (gp91ᵖʰᵒˣ) is highly active in infiltrating [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] and [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]] within the synovial fluid, producing copious [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals]] during the [[notes/_link/Respiratory Burst]]. RA [[Synovial fibroblasts]] express [[Nox4]], which constitutively generates [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] and drives matrix metalloproteinase ([[MMP-1]], [[MMP-3]], [[MMP-13]]) production via [[notes/oxidative_stress/p38 MAPK]] and [[JNK]] activation. Nox4-derived H₂O₂ also promotes [[osteoclastogenesis]] by enhancing [[RANKL]] signaling, contributing to periarticular bone erosion.

### Myeloperoxidase and HOCl-Mediated Tissue Damage
[[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO) levels in RA synovial fluid and serum correlate with disease activity and joint destruction score. MPO from activated [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]] catalyzes the production of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl), which oxidizes and chlorinates [[Proteoglycans]] and [[Collagen type II]] in the articular cartilage. HOCl also inactivates [[protease inhibitors]] such as [[α1-antitrypsin]] (α1-AT), shifting the protease–antiprotease balance toward matrix degradation. [[3-Chlorotyrosine]] — a specific biomarker of MPO activity — is elevated in RA synovial tissue and correlates with radiographic joint damage.

### Citrullination and Oxidative Stress: The PAD Connection
[[Protein arginine deiminase]] ([[PAD]]2 and [[PAD4]]) enzymes, which convert arginine to [[citrulline]] in target proteins (e.g., [[Fibrinogen]], [[Vimentin]], [[Collagen type II]]), require supraphysiological Ca²⁺ concentrations for activation. [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] promotes calcium influx into RA synovial cells through oxidation of [[Ca²⁺-ATPase]] pumps and [[TRP channels]], creating conditions permissive for PAD activation. The resulting citrullinated proteins are targets of [[anti-citrullinated protein antibodies]] ([[ACPA]]), the most specific serological marker for RA. This oxidative–citrullination nexus may represent the mechanistic link between environmental triggers ([[Smoking]] — a potent oxidant source) and RA autoimmunity.

### Therapeutic Implications and Redox-Modulating Therapies
Conventional disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs ([[DMARD]]s) target oxidative stress in RA. [[Methotrexate]] (MTX) — a first-line therapy — increases extracellular [[adenosine]], which suppresses [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] activity in neutrophils and inhibits TNF-induced ROS production in synovial fibroblasts. [[Sulfasalazine]] scavenges •OH and HOCl and inhibits [[NF-kappa B]] activation. [[Gold compounds]] ([[Auranofin]]) inhibit [[Thioredoxin reductase]] ([[TrxR]]), modulating the thioredoxin antioxidant system. [[Anti-TNF therapy]] ([[Infliximab]], [[Adalimumab]]) reduces circulating [[notes/oxidative_stress/Malondialdehyde]] and enhances [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] levels in RA patients, partly by breaking the TNF–ROS amplification loop.
