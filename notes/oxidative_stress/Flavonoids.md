# Flavonoids

## Definition
Flavonoids are a class of polyphenolic secondary metabolites found in plants, often responsible for pigments.

## Function
They exhibit antioxidant activity by scavenging free radicals and chelating metal ions.

## Structural Classification and Redox Chemistry

Flavonoids share a common diphenylpropane (C6-C3-C6) backbone consisting of two aromatic rings (A and B) linked by a three-carbon heterocyclic ring (C). The major subclasses include:
- **[[notes/_link/Flavonols]]** (e.g., [[notes/_link/Quercetin]], [[notes/_link/Kaempferol]]): 3-hydroxyflavone backbone, abundant in onions and tea
- **Flavones** (e.g., luteolin, apigenin): No 3-hydroxyl, found in parsley and celery
- **Isoflavones** (e.g., [[notes/oxidative_stress/Genistein]], daidzein): B-ring attached at C3, found in soy
- **Flavanones** (e.g., naringenin, hesperetin): Saturated C-ring, abundant in citrus
- **Flavanols** (e.g., [[notes/_link/EGCG|catechins]], epicatechins): Present in [[Green Tea]] and cocoa
- **Anthocyanidins** (e.g., cyanidin, delphinidin): Pigments responsible for red-blue colors in berries

### Structure-Antioxidant Activity Relationships

The antioxidant potency of flavonoids is governed by specific structural features:
- The **catechol group** (ortho-dihydroxyl) on the B-ring enables efficient electron delocalization and stable semiquinone radical formation
- The **2,3-double bond** conjugated with the 4-oxo group in the C-ring facilitates electron donation
- The **3-hydroxyl group** on the C-ring enhances radical scavenging
- The **5,7-dihydroxyl pattern** on the A-ring contributes to [[Metal Chelation]] capacity

These features allow flavonoids to scavenge [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] including [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals]] (O₂⁻•), [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] (H₂O₂), [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hydroxyl Radicals]] (•OH), and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻) with rate constants approaching diffusion-limited kinetics.

## Molecular Mechanisms of Antioxidant Action

### Direct Radical Scavenging

Flavonoids neutralize free radicals by donating a hydrogen atom (H⁺) or an electron (e⁻), forming a relatively stable flavonoid radical (flavonoid-O•) that can be regenerated by other antioxidants like [[notes/_link/Ascorbic Acid]] and [[notes/_link/Glutathione]]. The reaction proceeds via:
- **Hydrogen atom transfer (HAT)**: Prevalent in non-polar environments
- **Single electron transfer (SET)**: Favored in aqueous media
- **Sequential proton-loss electron transfer (SPLET)**: Dominant at physiological pH

### Metal Chelation

Flavonoids chelate redox-active transition metals (Fe²⁺, Cu⁺) through their catechol and keto-enol moieties, preventing the [[notes/oxidative_stress/Fenton reaction]]:
  Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ → Fe³⁺ + •OH + OH⁻
By sequestering iron and copper, flavonoids suppress hydroxyl radical generation, which is central to [[notes/oxidative_stress/Lipid Peroxidation]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/DNA Damage]].

### Enzyme Modulation

Flavonoids regulate endogenous antioxidant and pro-oxidant enzymes:
- **Inhibition of [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]]**: Reducing superoxide production in endothelial cells and [[notes/_link/Neutrophils]]
- **Inhibition of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Xanthine Oxidase]]**: Blocking uric acid and superoxide generation
- **Activation of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Catalase]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Dismutase]]**: Enhancing enzymatic antioxidant defenses
- **Activation of NRF2/KEAP1 pathway**: Flavonoids like [[notes/_link/Quercetin]] and [[notes/_link/EGCG]] modify KEAP1 cysteine residues, releasing NRF2 to translocate to the nucleus and induce phase II detoxifying enzymes including [[notes/oxidative_stress/Glutathione Peroxidase]], [[notes/oxidative_stress/Catalase]], heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), and NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase 1 (NQO1)

### Anti-Inflammatory Signaling

Flavonoids suppress [[notes/_link/Inflammation]] through:
- **Inhibition of [[notes/oxidative_stress/NF-kappa B]]**: Blocking IκB phosphorylation and p65 nuclear translocation
- **Suppression of [[notes/oxidative_stress/TNF-alpha]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/IL-1b]] production**: Reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine release
- **Modulation of [[notes/_link/MAPK]] signaling**: Attenuating p38 and JNK phosphorylation
- **Activation of [[notes/_link/FOXO]] transcription factors**: Promoting expression of antioxidant genes like [[notes/oxidative_stress/Catalase]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Dismutase]]

## Bioavailability and Phase II Metabolism

A critical limitation is that flavonoids undergo extensive first-pass [[Metabolism]] in the liver and gut, including:
- **Glucuronidation**: Conjugation with glucuronic acid by UDP-glucuronosyltransferases
- **Sulfation**: By sulfotransferases
- **Methylation**: By catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT)

These conjugates reach micromolar plasma concentrations and retain partial biological activity. Gut microbiota further degrades flavonoids to simple phenolic acids (e.g., phenylacetic, phenylpropionic acids) that may themselves contribute to systemic antioxidant effects.

## Pro-oxidant Effects and Hormesis

At high concentrations or in the presence of excess transition metals, flavonoids can act as **pro-oxidants**:
- **Autoxidation**: Generation of superoxide and semiquinone radicals
- [[notes/oxidative_stress/Fenton reaction]] amplification: Reducing Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺, promoting •OH generation
- **Depletion of [[notes/_link/Glutathione]]**: Formation of flavonoid-quinone conjugates
- **[[notes/oxidative_stress/Signaling Molecules]]**: Pro-oxidant effects activate adaptive stress responses (hormesis) that ultimately enhance cellular resilience

## Clinical and Pathological Relevance

Epidemiological studies associate high flavonoid intake with reduced risk of [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]], [[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]], and certain cancers. Flavonoids protect LDL from oxidation—a critical step in [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]]—and improve [[notes/oxidative_stress/Endothelial Dysfunction]] by enhancing [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] bioavailability. In the context of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]], flavonoids attenuate [[notes/oxidative_stress/Lipid Peroxidation]], preserve [[notes/oxidative_stress/Proteins]] from carbonylation, and reduce [[notes/oxidative_stress/Nucleic Acids|Nucleic Acid]] oxidation as measured by [[notes/oxidative_stress/8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine]] levels.
