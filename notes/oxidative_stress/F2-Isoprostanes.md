# F2-Isoprostanes

**F2-Isoprostanes (F2-IsoPs)** are prostaglandin-like compounds formed non-enzymatically by free radical-mediated [[Lipid Peroxidation|peroxidation]] of [[Arachidonic acid]], widely considered the gold-standard biomarker for endogenous [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]].

## Formation

F2-IsoPs are produced in situ on phospholipids by free radical attack on arachidonic acid (20:4, n-6). The mechanism involves hydrogen abstraction from the bis-allylic C7, C10, or C13 positions, followed by oxygen insertion, cyclization, and reduction to the F-ring prostane structure. Unlike cyclooxygenase-derived prostaglandins, F2-IsoPs are formed independently of COX enzymes, directly on esterified phospholipids within cell membranes, and are then released in free form by phospholipases (PLA₂).

## Analytical Detection

F2-IsoPs are measured by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) or liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS). Urinary 8-iso-PGF₂α (iPF₂α-III, 15-F₂t-IsoP) is the most commonly measured isoform. Measurement requires rigorous sample handling to prevent ex vivo autoxidation.

## Clinical Relevance

Elevated F2-IsoPs are found in virtually every disease involving oxidative stress, including [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular disease]] (plaque formation, reperfusion injury), [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]] ([[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]]), [[Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease|COPD]], [[Rheumatoid Arthritis|rheumatoid arthritis]], and [[Aging]]. They also possess biological activity as weak vasoconstrictors and can modulate platelet activation through thromboxane receptor (TP) binding.
