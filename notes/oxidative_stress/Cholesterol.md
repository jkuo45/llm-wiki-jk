# Cholesterol
Cholesterol is a type of lipid molecule that is essential for cell membrane structure and as a precursor for steroid hormones, but its oxidation leads to [[Oxidized LDL]] and contributes to [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]].

## Mechanisms of Cholesterol Oxidation and Oxidative Signaling

### Oxysterol Formation and Biological Activity
Cholesterol is susceptible to non-enzymatic oxidation by [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]], yielding a family of >30 [[Oxysterols]] including 7α-hydroxycholesterol, 7β-hydroxycholesterol, 5α,6α-epoxycholesterol, and 7-ketocholesterol. The most abundant circulating oxysterol — 27-hydroxycholesterol (27-OHC) — is produced enzymatically by [[Mitochondrial]] [[CYP27A1]] (sterol 27-hydroxylase). Oxysterols are potent signaling molecules that regulate [[notes/oxidative_stress/LXRα]] and [[LXRβ]] nuclear receptor activity, cholesterol efflux via [[notes/oxidative_stress/ABCA1]], and inflammatory gene expression. 7-Ketocholesterol, the predominant dietary oxysterol, induces [[notes/_link/Apoptosis]] in vascular cells through ROS-dependent [[notes/oxidative_stress/Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] permeabilization and activation of [[caspase-3]].

### LDL Oxidation and Atherogenesis
The oxidation of LDL-associated cholesterol and phospholipids is a cornerstone of the [[notes/oxidative_stress/Lipid Peroxidation]] hypothesis of [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]]. Within the subendothelial space, [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]]-derived [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hypochlorous Acid]] and [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] from activated [[notes/_link/Macrophage]] oxidize LDL cholesterol. Oxidized LDL ([[Oxidized LDL|oxLDL]]) is taken up unregulated by scavenger receptors including [[notes/oxidative_stress/CD36 Receptor]] and [[SR-A]], driving [[Foam cell]] formation. Cholesterol hydroperoxides (ChOOH) — specifically 7α-OOH and 7β-OOH — accumulate in oxLDL and serve as propagating species in the [[notes/oxidative_stress/Lipid Peroxidation]] chain reaction.

### HDL and Paraoxonase 1 (PON1) as Antioxidant Defense
High-density lipoprotein ([[HDL]]) carries [[Paraoxonase 1]] (PON1), an esterase that hydrolyzes oxidized phospholipids and cholesterol ester hydroperoxides, preventing their accumulation on LDL. PON1 activity is reduced under conditions of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]], contributing to the transition from protective HDL to dysfunctional, pro-inflammatory HDL. Polymorphisms in the [[PON1 gene]] (e.g., Q192R) influence antioxidant capacity and [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]] risk.

### Cholesterol in Membrane Rafts and Redox Signaling
Cholesterol is a critical structural component of [[Lipid rafts]] — ordered membrane microdomains that compartmentalize signaling proteins. Raft-associated cholesterol modulates the activity of [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase]] (Nox) enzymes by regulating membrane fluidity and the localization of [[Rac GTPase]]. Depletion of membrane cholesterol suppresses [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals]] production, while enrichment enhances it. In [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]], membrane cholesterol influences [[notes/oxidative_stress/Amyloid-beta]] generation by modulating [[BACE1]] and [[γ-secretase]] activity within raft domains.

### Clinical and Therapeutic Implications
The interplay between [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] and cholesterol metabolism is targeted by [[Statin]] therapy beyond LDL reduction. [[Statins]] exert pleiotropic antioxidant effects by inhibiting [[Rac1]] prenylation, reducing Nox-dependent ROS production, and upregulating [[notes/oxidative_stress/Catalase]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Dismutase]]. [[notes/_link/Vitamin E]] (α-tocopherol) traps lipid peroxyl radicals within LDL particles, though large clinical trials have shown mixed cardiovascular outcomes.
