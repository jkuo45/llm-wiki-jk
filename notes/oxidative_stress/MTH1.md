# MTH1

**MTH1 (MutT Homolog 1, NUDT1)** is a nucleotide pool sanitization enzyme that hydrolyzes oxidized purine nucleoside triphosphates — principally [[8-oxo-dGTP]] and [[2-OH-dATP]] — to their monophosphate forms, preventing their incorporation into DNA during replication.

## Mechanism

MTH1 catalyzes the hydrolysis of the pyrophosphate bond in oxidized dNTPs: 8-oxo-dGTP → 8-oxo-dGMP + PPi. It exhibits broad substrate specificity, acting on 8-oxo-dGTP, 8-oxo-GTP, 2-OH-dATP, and 2-OH-ATP, with a strong preference for oxidized versus normal nucleotides. By sanitizing the nucleotide pool, MTH1 prevents the incorporation of oxidized bases into DNA, which would otherwise cause mismatches during replication (8-oxo-G·A pairing leading to G→T transversions) and generate mutagenic and cytotoxic lesions.

## Relationship to Other Repair Systems

MTH1 functions upstream of [[Base Excision Repair|BER]]: while [[OGG1]] repairs 8-oxoG already incorporated in DNA, MTH1 prevents incorporation of the oxidized nucleotide in the first place. Together, these two pathways provide overlapping protection against oxidative DNA damage — OGG1 repairs DNA, MTH1 sanitizes the precursor pool. [[MTH2]] (NUDT15) and [[NUDT5]] provide partially redundant activities.

## Clinical Relevance

- **[[Cancer]]**: MTH1 is overexpressed in many cancer types (lung, breast, colorectal, bladder, melanoma), reflecting the elevated oxidative stress in cancer cells and their increased reliance on nucleotide pool sanitization. MTH1 knockdown in cancer cells leads to DNA damage, cell cycle arrest, and cell death, while normal cells with lower oxidative burdens are less affected — making MTH1 a potential therapeutic target (MTH1 inhibitors: [[TH287]], [[TH588]], [[IACS-4759]]).
- **[[Neurodegeneration]]**: MTH1 protects post-mitotic neurons from oxidative DNA damage accumulation; MTH1⁻/⁻ mice show elevated 8-oxoG in brain and accelerated age-related behavioral decline.
