# L-arginine
L-arginine is an amino acid that serves as a primary precursor for the biosynthesis of [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] via the enzyme [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]]. Availability of L-arginine is a critical factor in maintaining vascular health and preventing [[Endothelial Dysfunction]].

## The L-Arginine-NO Pathway

L-arginine is converted to [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] (NO) and L-citrulline by [[notes/oxidative_stress/Nitric Oxide Synthase]] (NOS) in a two-step monooxygenase reaction requiring molecular oxygen and [[NADPH]]:

  1. NOS hydroxylates L-arginine to Nᴳ-hydroxy-L-arginine (NOHLA)
  2. NOHLA is further oxidized to L-citrulline + NO

The reaction consumes five electrons, uses [[FAD]], [[FMN]], heme, [[Tetrahydrobiopterin]] (BH₄), and calmodulin as cofactors, and produces L-citrulline as a stoichiometric coproduct.

## Competing Pathways and the Arginine Paradox

### Arginase Competition
L-arginine is also metabolized by **arginase** (isoforms arginase I and II), which hydrolyzes arginine to L-ornithine and urea, shunting arginine away from NO synthesis. Key characteristics:
- **Arginase I**: Cytosolic, highly expressed in liver (urea cycle)
- **Arginase II**: Mitochondrial, expressed in endothelial cells, [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]], and kidney

Under inflammatory conditions, [[notes/oxidative_stress/TNF-alpha]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/IL-1b]] upregulate arginase expression, reducing L-arginine availability for NOS. This creates the **arginine paradox**: despite intracellular L-arginine concentrations (100–800 µM) being well above the NOS Kₘ (2–10 µM), exogenous L-arginine supplementation paradoxically increases NO production—suggesting compartmentalized arginine pools or endogenous inhibitors.

### Asymmetric Dimethylarginine (ADMA)
**ADMA** is an endogenous competitive inhibitor of NOS that accumulates in [[notes/oxidative_stress/Endothelial Dysfunction]], [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]], and [[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus]]. ADMA is generated by protein arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs) and degraded by [[Dimethylarginine dimethylaminohydrolase]] (DDAH). [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] inactivates DDAH, causing ADMA accumulation and further NOS inhibition.

## NOS Uncoupling and Superoxide Generation

When L-arginine or BH₄ availability is limited, [[notes/oxidative_stress/Nitric Oxide Synthase|eNOS]] becomes **uncoupled**: instead of transferring electrons to L-arginine, the reduced heme transfers electrons to molecular oxygen, producing [[notes/oxidative_stress/Superoxide Radicals]] (O₂⁻•) rather than NO. This phenomenon is a hallmark of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Endothelial Dysfunction]].

Causes of eNOS uncoupling include:
- **L-arginine depletion** (due to arginase upregulation or ADMA competition)
- **BH₄ oxidation**: [[notes/oxidative_stress/Peroxynitrite]] rapidly oxidizes BH₄ to BH₂/dihydrobiopterin, which cannot support NOS coupling
- **Thiol oxidation**: Oxidative modification of the zinc-thiolate cluster in eNOS disrupts dimerization

Uncoupled eNOS transforms a NO-producing enzyme into an O₂⁻•-generating enzyme, creating a vicious cycle: O₂⁻• scavenges any remaining NO to form [[notes/oxidative_stress/Peroxynitrite]], which further oxidizes BH₄, sustaining uncoupling.

## The L-Arginine / ADMA Ratio

The plasma **L-arginine:ADMA ratio** is a clinically relevant biomarker of NO bioavailability and cardiovascular risk. A low ratio correlates with:
- [[notes/_link/Coronary Heart Disease]] progression
- [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease|Cardiovascular]] mortality in patients with [[notes/_link/Metabolic Syndromes|metabolic syndrome]]
- [[notes/oxidative_stress/Endothelial Dysfunction]] in [[notes/oxidative_stress/Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease]]
- Worsening of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Renal Function]] in chronic kidney disease

## Therapeutic Supplementation and Controversies

Oral L-arginine supplementation (3–9 g/day) has been investigated for:
- **[[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]]**: Improving flow-mediated dilation and reducing [[Notes/_link/Arterial Stiffness|arterial stiffness]]
- **[[notes/oxidative_stress/Raynaud's Phenomenon]]**: Enhancing NO-dependent vasodilation
- **[[Erectile Dysfunction]]**: Enhancing NO-dependent corpus cavernosum relaxation
- **[[notes/oxidative_stress/Wound Healing]]**: Promoting NO-dependent angiogenesis

However, results are inconsistent. High-dose L-arginine (≥10 g/day) can cause gastrointestinal distress, and in some contexts, excessive NO may promote [[notes/oxidative_stress/Nitrative Stress]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Peroxynitrite]] formation. Additionally, oral L-arginine undergoes extensive **first-pass metabolism** by arginase in the intestinal epithelium and liver, limiting systemic bioavailability.

**L-Citrulline** supplementation has emerged as an alternative: it is converted to L-arginine via the argininosuccinate pathway with better bioavailability, avoiding arginase-mediated degradation and paradoxically raising plasma arginine more effectively than equimolar arginine.

## Connections to Oxidative Stress

L-arginine metabolism intersects with [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]] at multiple nodes:
- **Arginase induction** by [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hydrogen Peroxide]] and [[notes/oxidative_stress/Hypochlorous Acid]] diverts arginine from NO synthesis
- **eNOS uncoupling** is both a consequence and cause of [[notes/oxidative_stress/Oxidative Stress]]
- **ADMA** accumulation under oxidative conditions reinforces endothelial dysfunction
- **Peroxynitrite** from NO + O₂⁻• reactions causes nitration of proteins and lipids
- **Arginine delminase** in [[Mycoplasma]] infections depletes local arginine, contributing to host tissue damage
