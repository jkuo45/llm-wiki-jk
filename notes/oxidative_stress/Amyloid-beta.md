# Amyloid-beta

Amyloid-beta (Aβ) denotes peptides of 36–43 amino acids that are the main component of the amyloid plaques found in the brains of people with [[Alzheimer's Disease]]. Beyond its role as a pathological aggregate, Aβ is a potent local generator of [[Oxidative Stress]] through redox-metal catalysis, mitochondrial toxicity, and depletion of antioxidant defenses—processes now considered early, causal contributors to neuronal loss.

## Generation and Redox Metal Binding

Aβ is produced by sequential proteolytic cleavage of the [[Amyloid Precursor Protein]] (APP) by β-secretase ([[BACE1]]) and γ-secretase. The 42-residue isoform (Aβ42) is especially aggregation-prone and binds redox-active metal ions—Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, and Zn²⁺—with high affinity at its N-terminal histidine and tyrosine residues. Cu²⁺-bound Aβ catalyzes the reduction of O₂ to [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] via the [[Fenton Reaction]], and the peptide itself can oscillate between oxidative and reductive states, establishing a direct biochemical link between Aβ and [[Oxidative Stress]]. This metal-catalyzed [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] generation creates a localized pro-oxidant microenvironment around plaques that damages nearby neurites and synapses.

## Mitochondrial Toxicity and Lipid Peroxidation

Aβ accumulates within [[Mitochondria]], particularly in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] brains, where it impairs [[Cytochrome c oxidase]] ([[Complex IV]]) and disrupts electron transport chain function. This elevates mitochondrial ROS and depresses [[ATP]] synthesis. The resulting [[Lipid Peroxidation]] generates reactive aldehydes—[[Malondialdehyde]] (MDA) and [[4-Hydroxynonenal]] (4-HNE)—that form covalent adducts on proteins, propagating oxidative damage. Because neuronal membranes are rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids (see [[Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids]]), they are especially vulnerable to this cascade.

## Glutathione Depletion and Protein Carbonylation

Intracellular [[Glutathione]] is markedly reduced in neurons exposed to Aβ oligomers, compromising cellular antioxidant capacity. This promotes protein [[Carbonylation]] and advanced glycation end-product (AGE) formation, contributing to proteotoxic stress. Aβ further impairs [[Glutathione Peroxidase]] and [[Sirtuins]] family deacetylases, exacerbating redox imbalance.

## Interplay with Tau and Neurofibrillary Tangles

[[Tau Protein]] hyperphosphorylation—the hallmark of neurofibrillary tangles—is promoted by oxidative stress. ROS activate kinases such as [[GSK3|GSK-3β]] and [[CDK5]], which phosphorylate tau. Tau pathology in turn impairs axonal transport, reducing delivery of mitochondria and antioxidants to distal synapses, creating a feed-forward amplification of oxidative injury that drives [[Neurodegeneration]].

## Clinical Significance

PET imaging with Aβ-binding tracers (Pittsburgh compound B, florbetapir) quantifies amyloid burden in vivo. Disease-modifying therapies include anti-Aβ monoclonal antibodies ([[Aducanumab]], [[Lecanemab]]) that clear plaques. Adjunctive antioxidant strategies—[[Vitamin E]], [[N-Acetylcysteine]], and metal chelators such as [[Clioquinol]]—are under investigation to mitigate Aβ-driven oxidative damage, though clinical benefit remains unproven.
