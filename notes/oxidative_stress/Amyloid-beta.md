# Amyloid-beta
Amyloid-beta (Aβ) denotes peptides of 36–43 amino acids that are the main component of the amyloid plaques found in the brains of people with [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]].

## Molecular Mechanisms in Oxidative Stress

### Aβ Generation and Redox Metal Binding
Aβ is produced via sequential proteolytic cleavage of the [[notes/_link/APP|Amyloid Precursor Protein]] by β-secretase ([[BACE1]]) and γ-secretase. The 42-residue isoform (Aβ42) is particularly prone to aggregation and exhibits high-affinity binding to redox-active metal ions, including Cu²⁺, Fe²⁺, and Zn²⁺. Cu²⁺-bound Aβ catalyzes the reduction of O₂ to [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] via the [[Fenton Reaction]], establishing a direct biochemical link between Aβ and [[Oxidative Stress]]. This metal-catalyzed [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] generation creates a localized pro-oxidant microenvironment around amyloid plaques.

### Mitochondrial Toxicity and Lipid Peroxidation
Aβ accumulates within [[notes/_link/Mitochondria]], particularly in [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]] brains, where it impairs [[Cytochrome c oxidase]] ([[notes/_link/Complex IV]]) activity and disrupts electron transport chain function. This leads to increased mitochondrial ROS production and decreased [[notes/_link/ATP]] synthesis. Consequent [[Lipid Peroxidation]] generates reactive aldehydes such as [[Malondialdehyde]] (MDA) and [[4-Hydroxynonenal]] (4-HNE), which form covalent adducts on proteins, further propagating oxidative damage. Aβ-induced lipid peroxidation is especially pronounced in [[Lipids]] rich in polyunsaturated fatty acids within neuronal membranes.

### Glutathione Depletion and Protein Carbonylation
Intracellular [[notes/_link/Glutathione]] levels are significantly reduced in neurons exposed to Aβ oligomers, compromising the cellular antioxidant capacity. The resulting protein [[notes/_link/Carbonylation]] and formation of advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) contribute to proteotoxic stress and neuronal dysfunction. Aβ also impairs the activity of [[Glutathione Peroxidase]] and [[Sirtuins]] family deacetylases, further exacerbating the redox imbalance.

### Interplay with Tau and Neurofibrillary Tangles
[[Tau Protein]] hyperphosphorylation — a hallmark of neurofibrillary tangle formation — is promoted by oxidative stress. ROS activate kinases such as [[notes/_link/GSK3|Glycogen synthase kinase-3β]] and [[notes/_link/CDK5]], which directly phosphorylate tau. Conversely, tau pathology impairs axonal transport, reducing delivery of antioxidants and mitochondria to distal synapses, creating a feed-forward amplification of oxidative injury that drives [[Neurodegeneration]].

### Clinical Significance
Positron emission tomography (PET) imaging with Aβ-binding radiotracers (e.g., Pittsburgh compound B, florbetapir) enables in vivo quantification of amyloid burden. Therapeutic strategies targeting Aβ include [[β-secretase inhibitors]], [[γ-secretase modulators]], and anti-Aβ monoclonal antibodies (e.g., [[Aducanumab]], [[Lecanemab]]). Adjunctive antioxidant approaches — including [[notes/_link/Vitamin E]], [[notes/_link/N-Acetylcysteine]], and metal chelators like [[Clioquinol]] — are under investigation to mitigate Aβ-driven oxidative damage in [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]].
