# Chronic Granulomatous Disease

**Chronic Granulomatous Disease (CGD)** is a primary immunodeficiency caused by genetic defects in the [[NADPH Oxidase]] complex (particularly [[NOX2]]/gp91ᵖʰᵒˣ), resulting in the inability of [[Phagocytes]] to generate the [[Respiratory Burst|respiratory burst]] and produce [[Superoxide Radicals|superoxide]] required for killing bacterial and fungal pathogens.

## Genetics

Most cases (65–70%) are X-linked recessive, caused by mutations in *CYBB* (encoding NOX2). Autosomal recessive forms involve [[p47ᵖʰᵒˣ]] (NCF1, ~25%), [[p67ᵖʰᵒˣ]] (NCF2), [[p22ᵖʰᵒˣ]] (CYBA), and [[p40ᵖʰᵒˣ]] (NCF4). Over 700 distinct mutations have been identified.

## Clinical Manifestations

- **Recurrent Infections**: Particularly with catalase-positive organisms — *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Serratia marcescens*, *Burkholderia cepacia*, *Nocardia*, and *Aspergillus* species
- **Dysregulated Inflammation**: Sterile granuloma formation in hollow viscera (GI tract, urinary tract) causing obstruction
- **Inflammatory Complications**: Colitis resembling [[Crohn's disease]], pneumonitis, and chorioretinitis

## Diagnosis

Flow cytometry using dihydrorhodamine (DHR) oxidation assay demonstrates absent or severely reduced NADPH oxidase activity. The nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) test is also used.

## Treatment

- **Prophylaxis**: Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole + itraconazole + interferon-γ
- **Curative Therapy**: Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and experimental gene therapy targeting *CYBB*
