# Free Radicals

## Definition
Free radicals are atoms, molecules, or ions that have at least one unpaired valence electron. This unpaired electron makes them highly chemically reactive toward other substances, or even toward themselves.

## Role in Oxidative Stress
In biological systems, free radicals are frequently derived from oxygen ([[Reactive Oxygen Species]]) or nitrogen ([[Reactive Nitrogen Species]]). They can initiate chain reactions, such as [[Lipid Peroxidation]], where a single radical can cause the oxidation of many lipid molecules. 

## Key Examples
- **[[Superoxide Radicals]]** (O2•−)
- **[[Hydroxyl Radicals]]** (•OH)
- **[[Nitric Oxide]]** (NO•)

## Connections
- [[Oxidative Stress]]: The pathological state resulting from an excess of free radicals.
- [[Antioxidants]]: Molecules that neutralize free radicals by donating an electron.
- [[Mitochondria]]: The primary site of endogenous free radical production.
