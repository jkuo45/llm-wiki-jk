# Reactive Nitrogen Species

## Definition
Reactive Nitrogen Species (RNS) are a family of antimicrobial molecules derived from [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] (•NO) and superoxide (O2•−) produced via the enzymatic activity of inducible [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] (iNOS) and [[NADPH Oxidase]], respectively.

## Key Species
- **[[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]]** (NO•): A free radical and signaling molecule.
- **[[Peroxynitrite]]** (ONOO−): A potent oxidant formed by the reaction of nitric oxide and superoxide.
- **Nitrogen dioxide** (•NO2): Formed from the decomposition of peroxynitrite.

## Role in Oxidative Stress
RNS act in coordination with [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) to cause **[[Nitrative Stress]]**. They can damage a wide range of molecules, including [[Proteins]] (via [[Tyrosine Nitration]]), [[Lipids]], and [[Nucleic Acids]].

## Connections
- [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]]: The primary precursor for most RNS.
- [[Peroxynitrite]]: The most damaging and reactive species in the RNS family.
- [[Nitrative Stress]]: The pathological state associated with excess RNS.
- [[Tyrosine Nitration]]: A key biomarker of RNS-mediated damage to proteins.
