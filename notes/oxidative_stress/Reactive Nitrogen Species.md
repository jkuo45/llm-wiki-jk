# Reactive Nitrogen Species

## Definition
Reactive Nitrogen Species (RNS) are a family of antimicrobial molecules derived from [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] (•NO) and superoxide (O2•−) produced via the enzymatic activity of inducible [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] (iNOS) and [[NADPH Oxidase]], respectively.

## Key Species
- **[[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]]** (NO•): A free radical and signaling molecule.
- **[[Peroxynitrite]]** (ONOO−): A potent oxidant formed by the reaction of nitric oxide and superoxide.
- **Nitrogen dioxide** (•NO2): Formed from the decomposition of peroxynitrite.

## Role in Oxidative Stress
RNS act in coordination with [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) to cause **[[Nitrative Stress]]**. They can damage a wide range of molecules, including [[Proteins]] (via [[Tyrosine Nitration]]), [[Lipids]], and [[Nucleic Acids]].

## Biochemistry of RNS Generation
The RNS family encompasses both radical and non-radical species derived from nitrogen-containing intermediates. The primary source of all RNS is [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]] (NO•), a gaseous free radical synthesized from [[L-arginine]] and molecular oxygen by the [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] (NOS) family — comprising neuronal (nNOS/NOS1), inducible (iNOS/NOS2), and endothelial (eNOS/NOS3) isoforms. iNOS is of particular relevance to [[Nitrative Stress]] because it is transcriptionally induced by pro-inflammatory cytokines ([[TNF-alpha]], [[IL-1b]], [[IFN-γ]]) and [[notes/_link/LPS]], producing high, sustained fluxes of NO• (up to micromolar steady-state concentrations) for hours to days, in contrast to the low picomolar-to-nanomolar pulses generated by constitutive nNOS and eNOS.

The major propagation pathways are as follows:

1. **[[Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻) formation**: The near-diffusion-limited reaction ($k \approx 1.6 \times 10^{10}$ M⁻¹s⁻¹) between NO• and [[Superoxide Radicals]] ($O_2^{\bullet-}$) produces peroxynitrite, the most reactive and damaging RNS. This reaction outcompetes [[notes/_link/Superoxide Dismutase]] for superoxide, making peroxynitrite formation unavoidable in any microenvironment where both radicals are generated simultaneously.

2. **Nitrogen dioxide (•NO₂) and dinitrogen trioxide (N₂O₃)**: •NO₂ is generated from peroxynitrite decomposition ($ONOO^- + H^+ \rightarrow \bullet OH + \bullet NO_2$), from the peroxidase activity of [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]] and lactoperoxidase ($NO_2^- + H_2O_2 \rightarrow \bullet NO_2 + H_2O$), and from autoxidation of NO• in the presence of O₂ ($2NO + O_2 \rightarrow 2NO_2$). N₂O₃ is the anhydride of nitrous acid ($HNO_2$) and forms by $NO + NO_2 \rightleftharpoons N_2O_3$. Both •NO₂ and N₂O₃ mediate **S-nitrosylation** (transfer of NO⁺ equivalents to cysteine thiols) and **N-nitrosation** (formation of nitrosamines from secondary amines).

3. **Nitroxyl (HNO)**: The one-electron reduced form of NO•, generated from the decomposition of S-nitrosothiols or from NOS under certain conditions (substrate or cofactor deficiency). HNO has distinct biological targets compared to NO•, including inhibition of [[notes/_link/Aldehyde Dehydrogenase]] and activation of [[notes/_link/TRPA1]] channels, and exhibits cardiotropic effects (positive inotropy, vasodilation).

4. **Higher nitrogen oxides**: Dinitrogen tetroxide (N₂O₄), nitrite ($NO_2^-$), and nitrate ($NO_3^-$) are downstream metabolites that can be recycled back to NO• via the enterosalivary nitrate–nitrite–NO pathway, mediated by commensal [[notes/_link/Gut Microbiome]] bacteria containing nitrate reductases.

## Immunological Functions
RNS constitute a core component of the innate immune arsenal. In [[notes/_link/Macrophage|macrophages]], the coordinate induction of iNOS and [[NADPH Oxidase]] (NOX2) during classical (M1) activation creates a high-flux RNS/ROS environment within the phagolysosome that is potently antimicrobial. The mechanisms of pathogen killing include:

- **Inactivation of iron-sulfur proteins**: RNS attack [4Fe-4S] and [2Fe-2S] clusters in bacterial respiratory complexes and dehydratases (e.g., aconitase, fumarase), impairing pathogen metabolism.
- **DNA damage**: Nitric oxide and its derivatives (particularly peroxynitrite and N₂O₃) cause bacterial [[notes/_link/DNA Damage]], including deamination of cytosine (→ uracil) and 5-methylcytosine (→ thymine) via nitrosative deamination reactions.
- **Lipid peroxidation**: Peroxynitrite and •NO₂ initiate lipid peroxidation in bacterial membranes.
- **S-nitrosylation**: S-nitrosylation of key bacterial enzymes (including those involved in respiration and transcription) inhibits pathogen growth.

RNS are effective against a wide range of pathogens, including [[notes/_link/Salmonella]], [[notes/_link/Mycobacterium tuberculosis]], [[notes/_link/Leishmania]], [[notes/_link/Plasmodium]] (malaria), and [[notes/_link/Fungal]] pathogens. The importance of this pathway is underscored by the susceptibility of iNOS knockout mice to these infections.

## Pathological Roles and Nitrative Stress
When RNS production exceeds the detoxification capacity of endogenous scavengers ([[notes/_link/Glutathione]], [[notes/_link/Thioredoxin]], [[notes/_link/Uric Acid]]), a state of **[[Nitrative Stress]]** emerges, characterized by widespread protein tyrosine nitration, S-nitrosylation of signaling proteins, [[Lipid Peroxidation]], and [[notes/_link/DNA Damage]]. The pathological footprint of RNS has been documented in:

- **[[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease]]**: eNOS uncoupling (loss of eNOS activity due to BH₄ deficiency or oxidation) converts eNOS from an NO• producer to a superoxide producer, shifting its output toward peroxynitrite and contributing to [[notes/_link/Endothelial Dysfunction]]. 3-Nitrotyrosine levels in plasma and vascular tissue correlate with [[notes/_link/Atherosclerosis]] burden and predict [[notes/_link/Major Adverse Cardiovascular Events]].
- **[[notes/_link/Neurodegenerative Diseases]]**: Protein nitration is a consistent feature of [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]] (nitrated [[notes/_link/Alpha-synuclein]] in Lewy bodies), [[notes/_link/Alzheimer's Disease]] (nitrated [[notes/_link/Tau]] and amyloid-β), and [[notes/_link/Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]] (nitrated TDP-43 and neurofilament proteins). RNS-mediated [[notes/_link/Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] in [[notes/_link/Dopaminergic Neurons]] is mechanistically linked to [[notes/_link/Parkinson's Disease]].
- **[[notes/_link/Diabetes Mellitus]]**: iNOS and NOX2 are upregulated in pancreatic islets of type 1 diabetes, and peroxynitrite-mediated damage contributes to β-cell destruction. In type 2 diabetes, RNS impair insulin signaling through [[notes/_link/IRS-1]] nitration and [[notes/_link/Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase 1B]] (PTP1B) oxidation.
- **[[Cancer]]**: Chronic inflammation-driven RNS production promotes mutagenesis (8-nitroguanine formation, G→T transversions), activation of pro-survival signaling ([[notes/_link/NFKB]], [[notes/_link/STAT3]]), and inhibition of [[notes/_link/DNA Repair]] enzymes (through S-nitrosylation of [[notes/_link/OGG1]] and [[notes/_link/APE1]]). RNS can also promote tumor [[notes/_link/Angiogenesis]] through stabilization of [[notes/_link/HIF-1α]].
- **[[notes/_link/Rheumatoid Arthritis]]**: Elevated 3-nitrotyrosine in synovial fluid and tissues correlates with disease activity and joint destruction.
- **[[notes/_link/Septic Shock]]**: Excessive iNOS-derived NO• causes profound vasodilation and hypotension refractory to vasopressors, with peroxynitrite contributing to myocardial depression and [[notes/_link/Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].

## Connections
- [[notes/_link/Nitric Oxide]]: The primary precursor for most RNS.
- [[Peroxynitrite]]: The most damaging and reactive species in the RNS family.
- [[Nitrative Stress]]: The pathological state associated with excess RNS.
- [[Tyrosine Nitration]]: A key biomarker of RNS-mediated damage to proteins.
- [[notes/_link/Myeloperoxidase]]: Contributes to RNS formation via nitrite oxidation.
- [[notes/_link/Immune System]]: RNS are essential components of macrophage-mediated antimicrobial defense.
