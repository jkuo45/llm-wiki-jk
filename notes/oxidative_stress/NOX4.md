# NOX4

**NOX4** (NADPH oxidase 4) is a constitutively active NADPH oxidase isoform that primarily produces [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide|H₂O₂]] rather than [[Superoxide Radicals]]. It is broadly expressed in the kidney, vasculature, liver, lung, and thyroid, where it functions in cell differentiation, senescence, angiogenesis, and metabolic regulation.

## Distinctive Features

Unlike NOX1 and NOX2, NOX4 does not require p47ᵖʰᵒˣ or p67ᵖʰᵒˣ for activity; it is constitutively active and regulated primarily by its expression level and binding to [[p22ᵖʰᵒˣ]] and [[Polymerase delta-interacting protein 2|Poldip2]]. NOX4 generates H₂O₂ directly by a non-canonical mechanism involving a conserved histidine in the E-loop that promotes rapid dismutation of the superoxide intermediate within the active site.

## Physiological Functions

- **Cellular Senescence**: NOX4-derived H₂O₂ drives replicative and stress-induced senescence by activating [[p53]]/[[p21]] and [[p16]]/[[Rb]] pathways, contributing to organismal [[notes/_link/Aging]].
- **Differentiation**: NOX4 promotes myofibroblast differentiation in response to [[TGF-beta]] and is required for [[TGF-beta]]-induced epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT).
- **Oxygen Sensing**: In the kidney, NOX4-derived H₂O₂ contributes to [[HIF-1α]] stabilization and erythropoietin production.
- **Angiogenesis**: NOX4-derived H₂O₂ promotes endothelial cell migration and tube formation through [[VEGF receptor]] signaling.

## Pathological Roles

NOX4 is upregulated in [[notes/oxidative_stress/Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease|COPD]] (airway epithelial cells), [[notes/oxidative_stress/NADPH Oxidase|pulmonary fibrosis]] (myofibroblasts), [[notes/_link/Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular disease]] (endothelial dysfunction), diabetic nephropathy, and [[Cancer|various cancers]] (including [[notes/_link/Breast Cancer]], [[notes/_link/Renal Cell Carcinoma]], and [[notes/_link/Colorectal Cancer]]) where it promotes proliferation, migration, and resistance to [[notes/oxidative_stress/Ferroptosis|ferroptosis]].
