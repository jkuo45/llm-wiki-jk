# Intraperitoneal

**Intraperitoneal (IP)** refers to something situated within or administered into the peritoneal cavity — the sterile space between the visceral and parietal peritoneum that contains the abdominal organs and is lined by a semi-permeable serosal membrane. Intraperitoneal administration is a widely used route for delivering drugs, fluids, and diagnostic agents in both laboratory animal research and certain clinical settings.

## Absorption & Pharmacokinetics

The peritoneum is highly vascularized, with blood draining via the portal vein (to the liver) and systemic veins (to the systemic circulation). Molecules administered intraperitoneally are absorbed across the peritoneal membrane into the portal and systemic circulations, producing pharmacokinetic behavior intermediate between intravenous (immediate systemic) and subcutaneous/intramuscular (slower, depot) routes. Small, lipophilic, and low-molecular-weight molecules are absorbed most rapidly. The liver's first-pass extraction can metabolize some compounds before they reach systemic circulation, an important consideration for drugs such as [[Adrenochrome]] or other catecholamine-derived compounds studied in experimental models.

## Research Applications

In basic and translational research, intraperitoneal injection is the default parenteral route in rodents because it accommodates larger volumes than intravenous injection, is technically simpler, and yields reproducible absorption. It is used to administer anesthetics, saline, test compounds, and reference standards in pharmacokinetic and toxicology studies. In the context of adrenochrome and related aminochrome research, IP dosing has been employed to evaluate systemic distribution, metabolite formation, and toxicity.

## Clinical Relevance

Clinically, intraperitoneal routes appear in peritoneal dialysis (dialysate exchanged into the peritoneal cavity), intraperitoneal chemotherapy for certain cancers (e.g., ovarian carcinoma), and intraperitoneal insulin in experimental closed-loop systems. Intraperitoneal administration of antidotes or contrast agents is occasionally used where rapid abdominal absorption is advantageous.
