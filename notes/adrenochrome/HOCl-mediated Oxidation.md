# HOCl-mediated Oxidation

HOCl-mediated Oxidation refers to the chemical process where hypochlorous acid (HOCl), generated by the [[Myeloperoxidase]] system, acts as a powerful electrophile to oxidize substrates such as [[Epinephrine]] into [[Aminochromes]] like [[Adrenochrome]].

## Myeloperoxidase-Dependent HOCl Production

[[Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO), a heme-containing oxidoreductase abundantly expressed in [[Neutrophils]], [[Monocytes]], and tissue [[Macrophages]], catalyzes the two-electron oxidation of [[Chloride]] (Cl⁻) by [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] (H₂O₂) to produce [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl): H₂O₂ + Cl⁻ + H⁺ → HOCl + H₂O. The reaction proceeds through a two-step catalytic cycle involving Compound I (an oxoferryl π-cation radical intermediate), which is reduced back to the resting ferric state by Cl⁻. The steady-state concentration of HOCl in the [[Phagolysosome]] can reach 50–100 μM, and local tissue concentrations during active inflammation may approach similar levels. MPO accounts for ~5% of total neutrophil protein mass, underscoring its quantitative importance.

## Selectivity for Electron-Rich Substrates

HOCl (pKa = 7.53; E₀′ = +1.08 V at pH 7.0) is a potent two-electron oxidant with marked selectivity for electron-rich aromatic compounds. The catechol ring of [[Epinephrine]], with its two electron-donating hydroxyl groups, is rapidly chlorinated and oxidized by HOCl. The second-order rate constant for the HOCl–epinephrine reaction exceeds 10⁴ M⁻¹s⁻¹, making it among the fastest non-enzymatic routes to [[Adrenochrome]]. HOCl preferentially targets the C2 and C5 positions of the catechol ring via electrophilic aromatic substitution, generating chlorocatechol intermediates that rapidly hydrolyze to the [[o-quinone]].

## Reaction Kinetics and Product Profile

At equimolar HOCl:epinephrine ratios, the predominant product is [[Adrenochrome]], formed within milliseconds under physiological pH and temperature. Excess HOCl drives further oxidation to ring-opened pyrrole-2,3-dicarboxylic acid derivatives and [[Chlorinated melanins]]. The reaction is pH-dependent: at acidic pH (<5, typical of the phagolysosome), HOCl exists as the neutral species and exhibits enhanced membrane permeability, oxidizing epinephrine both within and beyond the phagosome. The presence of [[Albumin]] or other scavengers modulates product distribution by competing for HOCl, though the high reaction rate with catecholamines ensures that adrenochrome formation persists even in complex biological matrices.

## Role in Neutrophil-Mediated Adrenochrome Formation

Activated [[Neutrophils]] at sites of inflammation release MPO into the extracellular space, where the H₂O₂–Cl⁻–MPO system generates HOCl in the immediate pericellular environment. [[Epinephrine]] released from sympathetic nerve terminals or the [[Adrenal medulla]] during the acute-phase response is rapidly converted to [[Adrenochrome]] in this milieu. The resulting adrenochrome can be detected in inflammatory exudates and has been proposed as a biomarker of [[MPO]] activity in conditions such as [[Sepsis]], [[Acute respiratory distress syndrome]], and [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]].

## Tissue Damage in Inflammatory Diseases

MPO-derived HOCl contributes to tissue injury through several mechanisms: (1) oxidation of catecholamines to redox-active aminochromes that deplete [[Glutathione]] and inactivate [[Thiol]]-dependent enzymes; (2) chlorination of [[Tyrosine]] residues in proteins to form [[3-Chlorotyrosine]], a specific HOCl footprint; (3) activation of matrix [[Metalloproteinases]] via oxidative modification of their cysteine switch; and (4) promotion of [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] through consumption of [[Nitric Oxide]]. Elevated MPO and adrenochrome levels are documented in [[Atherosclerotic plaques]], [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], and [[Chronic Kidney Disease]], linking HOCl-mediated catecholamine oxidation to inflammatory pathology.
