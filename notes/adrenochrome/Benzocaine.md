# Benzocaine

**Benzocaine** is an ester-type local anesthetic commonly formulated in over-the-counter teething gels, topical sprays (including some throat and urethral preparations), and sunburn remedies. It blocks voltage-gated sodium channels to produce surface anesthesia. Despite its local-action profile, benzocaine is a well-recognized cause of **acquired [[Methemoglobinemia]]**.

The mechanism is metabolic: benzocaine is bioactivated (largely in erythrocytes) to an oxidizing metabolite that converts ferroheme (Fe²⁺) of [[Hemoglobin]] to ferriheme (Fe³⁺), forming [[Methemoglobin]], which cannot carry oxygen and shifts the oxygen-dissociation curve leftward. Even modest systemic absorption — especially in infants, with mucosal application, or at high doses — can produce cyanosis, chocolate-colored blood, and hypoxia. Severe cases are treated with [[Methylene blue]], which reductively regenerates functional hemoglobin via NADPH-methemoglobin reductase.

> [!warning] Age and route
> Infant teething gels containing benzocaine carry particular risk; mucosal and large-surface application increases absorption and methemoglobin burden.

Benzocaine shares this adverse profile with other topical anesthetics such as [[Lidocaine]] and [[Prilocaine]], though the incidence and potency differ.
