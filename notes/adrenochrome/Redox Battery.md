# Redox Battery

The **Redox Battery** is a conceptual construct in the [[Adrenochrome]] handling protocol representing the reservable store of cellular reducing equivalents that absorbs oxidative load generated during catecholamine autoxidation. Analogous to an electrical battery, it is "charged" by reducing capacity and "discharged" when [[Adrenochrome]] and its quinone derivatives ([[Aminochrome]], [[o-quinone]]) drain electrons through redox cycling.

The battery's two principal cells are the [[NAD+]]/NADH and [[Glutathione]] (GSH/GSSG) couples. Sustained adrenochrome flux oxidizes GSH to GSSG and shifts NAD+/NADH toward NAD+, depleting the reserve. Recharge depends on NADPH-generating metabolism and precursor availability (e.g., [[Nicotinamide]]). A depleted redox battery permits aminochrome electrophiles to alkylate proteins, triggering [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] activation and [[Senescence]]. Protocol design therefore monitors battery state and replenishes it in step with adrenochrome load.

> [!warning] A collapsed redox battery converts transient catecholamine oxidation into persistent oxidative damage — the boundary where acute handling fails and chronic senescence signaling begins.
