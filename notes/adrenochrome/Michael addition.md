# Michael addition

The **Michael addition** (or Michael reaction) is a nucleophilic addition of a nucleophile to an alpha, beta-unsaturated carbonyl compound.

## Role in Adrenochrome Formation
In the conversion of [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]], the intramolecular Michael addition occurs when the nitrogen atom on the amine "tail" attacks the reactive [[o-quinone]] ring, causing it to "fold" and form a closed loop.

## Mechanism and Chemoselectivity

The Michael addition proceeds via a two-step mechanism. The nucleophile (a carbanion, enolate, or heteroatom-centered nucleophile) attacks the β-carbon of an [[α,β-unsaturated carbonyl]] system, generating an enolate intermediate that is subsequently protonated to yield the saturated adduct. In the catecholamine cascade, oxidation of the catechol ring to the [[o-quinone]] creates an electrophilic α,β-unsaturated diketone system. The pendant primary or secondary amine of the ethylamine side chain serves as the intramolecular nucleophile, undergoing a 1,4-conjugate (Michael) addition at the C6 position of the quinone ring. This cyclization is stereoelectronically favored by the formation of a five-membered indole ring, yielding [[Leuco-adrenochrome]], which rapidly oxidizes to the intensely colored [[Adrenochrome]].

## Aminochrome Reactivity with Cellular Nucleophiles

The α,β-unsaturated carbonyl system of aminochromes (including [[Adrenochrome]] and [[Dopaminochrome]]) retains electrophilic character, rendering them reactive toward abundant cellular thiols. [[Glutathione]] (GSH), the predominant intracellular low-molecular-weight thiol, undergoes Michael addition to aminochromes at position C7 of the indole ring, forming glutathionyl adducts. [[Cysteine]] residues in proteins are similarly susceptible; adduction of aminochromes to critical [[Cysteine]] thiols in enzymes such as [[ALDH2]] (aldehyde dehydrogenase 2) and [[Complex I]] of the mitochondrial electron transport chain results in irreversible inhibition and functional impairment.

## Implications for Detoxification

The conjugation of aminochromes with [[Glutathione]] via Michael addition represents a major detoxification pathway. GSH adducts are more water-soluble and can be exported from cells via [[MRP transporters]] or further metabolized via the [[Mercapturic acid pathway]]. However, glutathione depletion — as occurs under conditions of [[Oxidative Stress]] — saturates this protective mechanism, leaving proteins vulnerable to electrophilic modification. The resulting protein-carbonyl adducts can trigger [[Proteotoxic stress]], [[Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPR) activation, and ultimately [[Apoptosis]]. Pharmacological strategies to enhance aminochrome detoxification include upregulation of [[GCL]] (glutamate-cysteine ligase) to boost GSH synthesis and use of exogenous thiol donors such as [[N-Acetylcysteine]].
