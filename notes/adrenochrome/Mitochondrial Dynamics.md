# Mitochondrial Dynamics

Mitochondrial dynamics refers to the continuous processes of **mitochondrial fusion** and **mitochondrial fission** that regulate mitochondrial morphology, number, and function within a cell.

## Fusion and Fission

- **Mitochondrial Fusion**: The joining of two mitochondria into a single, elongated organelle. This process helps maintain mitochondrial health by allowing the exchange of components (e.g., mtDNA, proteins, metabolites) and diluting damaged components.
- **Mitochondrial Fission**: The division of a single mitochondrion into two or more smaller organelles. Fission is necessary for mitochondrial inheritance during cell division, the removal of damaged mitochondria via [[Mitophagy]], and responding to metabolic demands.

## Role in Aging and Mitohormesis

The balance between fusion and fission (mitochondrial morphology) is a critical determinant of cellular homeostasis. 
- **Aging**: Aging is often associated with a shift in this balance, typically leading to fragmented or abnormally enlarged mitochondria, which contributes to [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]].
- **Mitohormesis**: Sublethal mitochondrial stress can trigger changes in mitochondrial dynamics as part of an adaptive response. For example, mild stress might promote fusion to preserve function or fission to facilitate the removal of damaged organelles.

## Molecular Machinery of Fusion

Mitochondrial fusion is a multi-step process mediated by dynamin-related GTPases. **[[MFN1]]** and **[[MFN2]]** (mitofusins 1 and 2) reside on the outer mitochondrial membrane and mediate tethering and fusion of the outer membranes between adjacent mitochondria. [[MFN2]] also tethers mitochondria to the endoplasmic reticulum at mitochondria-associated membranes (MAMs), coupling calcium transfer and lipid synthesis to organellar dynamics. The inner mitochondrial membrane fusion is executed by **[[OPA1]]** (optic atrophy 1), another dynamin-family GTPase. OPA1 exists as a mixture of long (L-OPA1) and short (S-OPA1) isoforms generated by proteolytic cleavage via **[[OMA1]]** and **[[YME1L]]** proteases. L-OPA1 tethered to the inner membrane promotes fusion, while S-OPA1 in the intermembrane space regulates cristae morphology and apoptosis sensitivity. Fusion requires the coordinated action of all three GTPases: MFN1/2 on opposing mitochondria physically dimerize and hydrolyze GTP to drive lipid bilayer mixing, followed by OPA1-dependent inner membrane fusion.

## Molecular Machinery of Fission

Fission is primarily executed by **[[DRP1]]** (dynamin-related protein 1, also known as DNM1L), a cytosolic GTPase that translocates to the OMM upon activation. DRP1 is recruited to future fission sites by receptor/adaptor proteins:
- **[[FIS1]]** (fission protein 1): An OMM-anchored protein that recruits DRP1, though its role in mammals is less prominent than in yeast.
- **[[MFF]]** (mitochondrial fission factor): A key DRP1 receptor whose overexpression induces fragmentation and whose loss causes hyperfusion.
- **[[MiD49]]** and **[[MiD51]]** (mitochondrial dynamics proteins of 49 and 51 kDa): Alternative DRP1 receptors that also regulate recruitment and assembly.
Once docked, DRP1 oligomerizes into ring-like structures around the mitochondrial tubule and, upon GTP hydrolysis, constricts to sever both outer and inner membranes. The endoplasmic reticulum and actin cytoskeleton cooperate with DRP1 by pre-constricting mitochondria at ER-mitochondria contact sites and providing mechanical force.

## Regulation by Post-Translational Modifications

Mitochondrial dynamics are acutely regulated by post-translational modifications that modulate GTPase activity, localization, and protein stability:
- **Phosphorylation**: DRP1 Ser616 phosphorylation (by CDK1/cyclin B, CaMKII, or ERK2) promotes fission, while Ser637 phosphorylation (by PKA) inhibits DRP1 translocation and promotes fusion. MFN1/2 are phosphorylated by [[Akt]] and JNK, influencing their stability and turnover.
- **Ubiquitination**: [[Parkin]] ubiquitinates MFN1/2 and DRP1, targeting them for proteasomal degradation during mitophagy. Deubiquitinases such as USP30 counteract this to maintain fusion capacity.
- **Acetylation**: SIRT3 deacetylates OPA1, enhancing its GTPase activity and promoting fusion under caloric restriction.
- **Proteolytic cleavage**: [[OMA1]] cleaves OPA1 during mitochondrial stress, shifting the balance toward fission and facilitating mitophagy.

## Connection to Mitophagy

Fission is mechanistically coupled to [[Mitophagy]] as a prerequisite for autophagic engulfment. Mitochondrial depolarization triggers DRP1-mediated fragmentation, separating damaged segments from the reticular network. The resulting daughter mitochondria with low membrane potential are selectively recognized by the [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] pathway, ubiquitinated, and delivered to autophagosomes. Blocking fission by DRP1 knockdown or dominant-negative mutants impairs mitophagy, causing accumulation of damaged mitochondrial components. Conversely, excessive fission — without compensatory fusion — generates an overabundance of small, dysfunctional mitochondria that overwhelm the mitophagic system and trigger apoptosis.

## Role in Mitohormesis

Mild mitochondrial stress induces adaptive changes in dynamics that contribute to [[Mitohormesis]]. Sublethal ROS production or mild electron transport chain inhibition (e.g., low-dose [[Menadione]], [[2-deoxy-D-glucose]]) promotes transient fission, activating [[PGC1-α]] and [[NRF2]]-dependent transcriptional programs that enhance mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant capacity. The sequential fusion-fission cycle also enables content mixing, diluting transient damage across the mitochondrial network. This dynamic flexibility is essential for cellular adaptation — rigid networks (either hyperfused or hyperfragmented) show impaired stress resistance and accelerated aging.

## Dysfunction in Aging and Disease

Aging is consistently associated with a shift toward mitochondrial fragmentation (increased fission, decreased fusion), driven by declining OPA1 and MFN2 expression, increased OMA1 activity, and DRP1 hyperactivation. This fragmented phenotype correlates with reduced respiration, increased ROS, impaired calcium buffering, and elevated mitophagy burden. In [[Neurodegeneration]], mutations in OPA1 cause dominant optic atrophy, while MFN2 mutations cause Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A. In [[Parkinson's Disease]], Parkin-mediated ubiquitination of MFN1/2 links mitophagy failure directly to dynamics dysregulation. Cardiac ischemia-reperfusion injury triggers DRP1-dependent fragmentation that exacerbates [[Cardiotoxicity]], and inhibiting DRP1 with [[Mdivi-1]] is cardioprotective in preclinical models.
