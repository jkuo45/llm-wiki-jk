# aak-2

**aak-2** is the principal catalytic (α) subunit of the *C. elegans* [[AMPK]] (AMP-activated protein kinase) complex, the conserved cellular energy sensor that monitors the AMP/ATP ratio. When bioenergetic stress (low glucose, exercise, mitochondrial dysfunction) raises AMP, AMPK phosphorylates targets that suppress anabolic processes (protein/lipid synthesis) and stimulate catabolic, repair, and recycling pathways ([[Autophagy]], mitochondrial biogenesis).

Aak-2 is required for several longevity paradigms. Genetic loss of *aak-2* abolishes the lifespan extension produced by glucose restriction and by mitochondrial stress-induced [[Mitohormesis]], placing AMPK downstream of the energy-surfeit signal that triggers adaptive reprogramming. Aak-2 also cooperates with [[Sirtuins]] (e.g., sir-2.1) and the insulin/IGF-1 signaling axis, integrating nutrient and redox cues into a coordinated longevity response.

> [!info] Conserved geroscience node
> Mammalian AMPK is a major target of metformin and exercise mimetics; *aak-2* therefore models the same energy-sensing rheostat that links calorie restriction to healthspan in humans.

Because AMPK activation improves glucose disposal, aak-2 biology is directly relevant to [[Insulin Resistance]] and diabetic vascular aging.
