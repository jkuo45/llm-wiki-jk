# Adrenochromatization

**Adrenochromatization** denotes the process or state of elevating systemic or local levels of [[Adrenochrome]] and its reactive intermediates — whether by controlled experimental administration or by stress-induced endogenous production from [[Epinephrine]] (and [[Norepinephrine]]) autoxidation and enzymatic oxidation. The term captures the shift of catecholamine redox chemistry toward the aminochrome/quinone species that define adrenochrome biology.

At its core, adrenochromatization is a **redox event**: epinephrine is oxidized (spontaneously, by trace metals, or via monoamine oxidase/cytochrome pathways) to adrenochrome, its semiquinone radical, and downstream adrenolutin, generating reactive oxygen species and depleting reducing equivalents ([[Glutathione]], ascorbate). The process is self-amplifying through [[Redox Cycling]] of the semiquinone.

> [!info] Mitohormetic framing
> In the proposed research paradigm, calibrated adrenochromatization is intended to induce a "mitohormetic" state — a transient, adaptive redox stress that engages antioxidant and mitochondrial quality-control pathways (the [[Mitohormesis]] response) rather than causing outright toxicity.

The therapeutic window is narrow: insufficient redox signal yields no adaptation, whereas excessive adrenochrome load drives oxidative damage, methemoglobin-like perturbations, and neuronal stress.
