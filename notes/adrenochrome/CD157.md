# CD157

**CD157** (also known as **BST-1**, bone marrow stromal cell antigen 1) is a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)-anchored ectoenzyme encoded by the *BST1* gene on chromosome 4p15. It is a member of the [[ADP-ribosyl cyclase]] family, alongside [[CD38]], and catalyzes the conversion of [[NAD+]] to [[cADPR|cyclic ADP-ribose (cADPR)]] and [[ADP-ribose]], both of which are calcium-mobilizing second messengers. The enzyme can also produce [[Nicotinamide]] as a byproduct of its NAD+ glycohydrolase activity.

## Structure & Domains

CD157 is a GPI-anchored protein of approximately 33 kDa (unglycosylated), tethered to the outer leaflet of the plasma membrane via a [[GPI anchor]]. Its single catalytic domain is structurally homologous to [[CD38]], sharing a conserved (β/α)₈ TIM barrel fold characteristic of the ADP-ribosyl cyclase family. The active site contains a catalytic triad comprising Glu179, Trp184, and Thr188, which coordinates substrate binding and catalysis. The enzyme possesses 3–4 N-glycosylation sites that contribute to proper folding, stability, and surface expression. Unlike CD38, which is a type II transmembrane protein, CD157 lacks a transmembrane domain and relies entirely on its GPI anchor for membrane attachment, conferring distinct lateral mobility and membrane microdomain localization.

## Mechanism of Action

CD157 exhibits three distinct enzymatic activities, with the relative ratios varying across species and cell types:

1. **NAD+ glycohydrolase** — Hydrolyzes [[NAD+]] to [[ADP-ribose]] and [[Nicotinamide]], representing the predominant activity in most contexts.
2. **ADP-ribosyl cyclase** — Cyclizes [[NAD+]] to form [[cADPR]], a cyclic nucleotide second messenger.
3. **cADPR hydrolase** — Hydrolyzes [[cADPR]] to [[ADP-ribose]], terminating the calcium-mobilizing signal.

[[cADPR]] is a potent calcium second messenger that activates ryanodine receptors on the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]], triggering Ca²⁺ release into the cytosol. This calcium signal is critical for diverse cellular processes, including muscle contraction, secretion, gene expression, and cell migration. The balance between CD157's cyclase and hydrolase activities determines the amplitude and duration of cADPR-mediated calcium signals.

## Physiological Function

### Immune System

CD157 is expressed on [[Monocytes]], [[Macrophages]], [[Neutrophils]], and bone marrow stromal cells. It promotes [[B Cell]] growth and differentiation, partly through its enzymatic production of [[cADPR]] and consequent calcium signaling. CD157 also functions as a cell adhesion molecule, facilitating interactions with [[Integrins]] on adjacent cells. In [[Neutrophils]], CD157 regulates migration and transendothelial extravasation, contributing to leukocyte trafficking during [[Inflammation]].

### Neuronal Function

CD157 is expressed in the brain, where its [[cADPR]]-producing activity contributes to neuronal calcium homeostasis. Given that [[CD38]]-mediated cADPR signaling regulates oxytocin release and social behavior, CD157 may play complementary roles in the central nervous system. The *BST1* locus has been linked to [[Parkinson's Disease]] susceptibility, suggesting a role in [[Dopaminergic Neurons]] survival and function.

### Bone Marrow Stromal Support

As its name suggests, CD157 is highly expressed on bone marrow stromal cells, where it supports hematopoiesis by providing trophic signals to developing hematopoietic progenitors. The enzymatic production of [[cADPR]] and [[ADP-ribose]] within the bone marrow [[Microenvironment]] influences progenitor cell proliferation and differentiation.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

### Parkinson's Disease

The *BST1* gene is a well-established [[GWAS]] risk locus for sporadic [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Multiple independent studies have identified single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) at this locus, including rs11724635 and rs4698412, that confer increased disease risk. The mechanism is thought to involve altered CD157 expression or enzymatic activity, which may affect [[Dopaminergic Neurons]] vulnerability to age-related stressors. Reduced cADPR production could impair calcium buffering and mitochondrial function in substantia nigra neurons, while excess NAD+ consumption may deplete cellular [[NAD+]] reserves, compromising [[Sirtuins]] and other NAD+-dependent enzymes.

### Autoimmune Disease

CD157 is overexpressed in synovial fibroblasts from patients with [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], where it may contribute to the hyperproliferative and invasive phenotype of the synovial lining. The *BST1* locus has also been associated with [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]], implicating CD157 in intestinal immune homeostasis.

### Cancer

CD157 expression has been documented in certain cancers, where it may influence the [[Tumor Microenvironment]] through modulation of [[NAD+]] metabolism and calcium signaling. In some contexts, CD157 overexpression on tumor-associated stromal cells could suppress antitumor immunity by depleting local [[NAD+]] or by generating immunosuppressive adenosine downstream of [[ADP-ribose]] metabolism.
