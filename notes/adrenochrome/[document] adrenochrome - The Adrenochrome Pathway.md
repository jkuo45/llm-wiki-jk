INTRODUCTION Since the conversion of [[Epinephrine|adrenaline]] to its pink oxidation product [[Adrenochrome]] was first observed in 18S61 many varied biological activities have been ascribed to this compound. Indeed in the 1930's and 40's this pathway was thought by many to be the major catabolic route for [[Epinephrine|adrenaline]] in vivo: More recently, [[Adrenochrome]] has been shown to be [[Cardiotoxicity|cardiotoxic]], causing [[cell necrosis]]3, [[arrhythmias]]4 and [[coronary artery constriction]]S. Despite this, the clinical significance of [[Adrenochrome]] remains controversial because there is no established method for [[Adrenochrome]] formation in vivo, and it has never been detected in biological fluids. The conversion of [[Epinephrine|adrenaline]] to [[Adrenochrome]] is an oxidation reaction, mediated by [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxygen radicals]]6• One major mechanism by which [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxygen radicals]] are produced in vivo is by stimulated [[neutrophils]]/, and these cells infiltrate [[myocardium]] in large numbers after [[myocardial infarction]]. The question therefore, was, could [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxygen radicals]] produced by [[neutrophils]] oxidize [[Epinephrine|adrenaline]] through the [[adrenochrome pathway]], especially those [[neutrophils]] at the site of a [[myocardial infarction]]?
