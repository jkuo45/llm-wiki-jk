# Cathepsin D

Cathepsin D is an aspartic lysosomal protease that plays a central role in the degradation of intracellular proteins, including those damaged by glycation.

## Degradation of AGEs

Intracellular proteins modified by [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs), such as [[N-(carboxymethyl)-lysine]] (CML), are targeted to the [[Lysosome]] for clearance. Cathepsin D, along with the cysteine protease [[Cathepsin B]], is a major driver of this proteolytic degradation.

A decrease in Cathepsin D activity or expression—often observed during [[Aging]] or after UV exposure—correlates directly with the cellular accumulation of AGEs.
