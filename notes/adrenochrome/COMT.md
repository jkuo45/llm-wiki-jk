# COMT (Catechol-O-methyltransferase)

**COMT** (Catechol-O-methyltransferase) is one of the primary enzymes responsible for the degradation of [[Catecholamines]], including [[Dopamine]], Norepinephrine, and [[Epinephrine]].

## Function
COMT transfers a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to the catechol ring, neutralizing the neurotransmitter's activity and preparing it for excretion.

## Role in [[Adrenochrome]] Pathway
Under normal conditions, COMT and [[MAO]] are the dominant routes for adrenaline metabolism. If COMT is inhibited (e.g., by drugs like entacapone used in Parkinson's disease), levels of [[Epinephrine]] can rise. This increased substrate availability, combined with [[comt/Oxidative stress]], may shift more adrenaline toward the minor [[Adrenochrome]] oxidative pathway.

## Connections
- [[Catecholamines]] ↔ Primary substrate for COMT.
- [[Epinephrine]] ↔ Metabolized by COMT.
- [[MAO]] ↔ The other major enzymatic pathway for catecholamine degradation.
- [[Adrenochrome]] ↔ Pathway that becomes more prominent when enzymatic degradation is blocked.
