# Peptide deformylase

Peptide deformylase (PDF) is a metal-dependent hydrolase that removes the *N*-formyl group from the initiator *N*-formylmethionine of newly synthesized bacterial and organellar polypeptides. In prokaryotes and in the plant/chloroplast lineage it acts immediately after translation, preceding methionine aminopeptidase clipping of the now-exposed methionine. The enzyme uses a dinuclear or mononuclear metal center (typically Fe²⁺ or Ni²⁺) positioned in a conserved active site to hydrolyze the formyl amide bond.

Within the [[Mitochondria|mitochondrion]], remnants of the endosymbiotic bacterium retain a formylmethionine-based translation system, and PDF activity is required for maturation of a subset of matrix- and inner-membrane-localized proteins. Because mitochondrial translation products include core respiratory-chain subunits, PDF function is coupled to the assembly and quality control of oxidative-phosphorylation complexes. Agents that inhibit PDF — most notably [[Actinonin]], a natural hydroxamate bacterial siderophore — block deformylation and cause the accumulation of formylated, misfolding-prone precursors. This creates protein-folding stress that can engage the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPRmt]] and perturb electron-transport efficiency, indirectly modulating reactive oxygen species output.

The relevance to [[Adrenochrome]] and redox longevity biology is the general principle that mitochondrial translation and proteostasis determine the leak of [[Superoxide anion]] and other ROS from the respiratory chain. Compounds such as actinonin are therefore studied both as antimicrobials (exploiting the absence of PDF from mammalian cytosolic translation) and as tools to probe mitonuclear signaling, with downstream consequences for [[Oxidative Stress]] buffering and the autoxidation of catecholamines such as [[Epinephrine]].

> [!tip] Selectivity
> Mammalian cytosolic translation uses unformylated methionine, so PDF inhibitors are relatively selective for bacterial and organellar systems.
