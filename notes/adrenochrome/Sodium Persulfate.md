# Sodium Persulfate

**Sodium Persulfate** (Na₂S₂O₈, molecular weight 238.10 g/mol) is an inorganic persulfate salt that appears as a white crystalline powder. It is a strong [[Oxidation|oxidizing]] agent characterized by a high standard potential (E° = 2.01 V) and is highly soluble in water. In aqueous solution, it decomposes slowly via hydrolysis, a process accelerated by heat, transition metal ions, and alkaline pH. Sodium persulfate belongs to the [[Persulfate]] family alongside [[Ammonium Persulfate]] and [[Potassium Persulfate]], sharing their characteristic two-electron oxidation chemistry.

## Properties

The persulfate anion (S₂O₈²⁻) undergoes a two-electron reduction to sulfate: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2e⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ (E° = 2.01 V), placing it among the strongest common peroxygen oxidants, comparable to ozone and exceeding [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] (E° = 1.78 V). Under thermal, photolytic ([[UV]]), or transition-metal-catalyzed activation, the symmetrical O–O bond undergoes homolytic cleavage to generate two sulfate [[Free Radicals|radical]] anions (SO₄⁻•), which are even more powerful oxidants with reduction potentials of E° = 2.4–3.1 V. This radical-generating capability makes sodium persulfate an effective initiator for free radical chain reactions.

## Uses

Sodium persulfate has broad industrial and environmental applications. In **groundwater remediation**, it is employed for in situ chemical oxidation (ISCO) of organic pollutants such as chlorinated solvents, petroleum hydrocarbons, and pesticides; the activated persulfate system generates highly reactive sulfate radicals that non-selectively mineralize contaminants. In **polymer chemistry**, it serves as a free radical initiator in emulsion [[Polymerization]] for the production of styrene-butadiene latex, acrylic adhesives, and other water-based polymers. In **electronics manufacturing**, it is used for microetching of copper in printed circuit board (PCB) fabrication, providing controlled surface roughening prior to lamination or plating. In **consumer products**, it functions as a bleaching agent and stain remover in laundry detergents, dishwasher formulations, and hair-color lightening products.

## Adrenochrome Relevance

In biochemical research, sodium persulfate can be used as [[In vitro|in vitro]] oxidizing agent to convert [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. The reaction follows the same two-electron oxidation mechanism as [[Ammonium Persulfate]] and [[Potassium Persulfate]]: the persulfate anion oxidizes the catechol ring of epinephrine to the corresponding [[o-quinone]] intermediate ([[adrenaline-quinone]]), which undergoes rapid intramolecular cyclization to form the indoline-like adrenochrome chromophore. Adrenochrome formation is conveniently monitored [[Spectrophotometry|spectrophotometrically]] at 480 nm, the characteristic absorbance maximum of the pink oxidation product. This assay is used to model [[Oxidative Stress]] and to study catecholamine oxidation kinetics under controlled conditions. As water-soluble sodium salt, sodium persulfate offers formulation advantages over the potassium analog in aqueous reaction systems.
