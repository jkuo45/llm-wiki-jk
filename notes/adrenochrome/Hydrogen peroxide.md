# Hydrogen peroxide

**Hydrogen peroxide** (H₂O₂) is a non-radical but highly reactive oxygen species. It is a key signaling molecule in cells but can also contribute to oxidative damage.

## Role in [[Adrenochrome]] Formation
Hydrogen peroxide, along with [[Superoxide anion]], serves as an oxidant that can drive the conversion of [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. This process is especially prominent under conditions of [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Biological Sources
- **Enzymatic:** Produced as a byproduct of [[MAO]] (Monoamine oxidase) activity during the degradation of [[Catecholamines]].
- **Mitochondria:** Formed from the dismutation of [[Superoxide anion]] by superoxide dismutase (SOD).
- **Immune Cells:** Generated during the respiratory burst in [[Neutrophils]].

## Consequences
While less reactive than the [[Hydroxyl radical]], H₂O₂ can diffuse across membranes and react with transition metals (like iron) via the Fenton reaction to produce the highly damaging [[Hydroxyl radical]].

## Connections
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] ↔ Hydrogen peroxide is a major member of the ROS family.
- [[Superoxide anion]] ↔ Precursor to hydrogen peroxide via dismutation.
- [[MAO]] ↔ Enzyme that produces H₂O₂ as a metabolic byproduct.
- [[Adrenochrome]] ↔ Oxidative byproduct whose formation is facilitated by H₂O₂.
