# Interleukin 1β

**Interleukin 1β (IL-1β)** is a potent pyrogenic, pro-inflammatory cytokine of the IL-1 family. Unlike most secreted proteins, mature IL-1β is not produced by conventional signal-peptide cleavage; instead, its inactive precursor pro-IL-1β is transcribed in response to inflammatory cues (TLR/NOD ligands, cytokines) and then proteolytically matured by active [[Caspase-1]] following assembly of the [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]].

The NLRP3 inflammasome is a multiprotein scaffold (NLRP3–ASC–pro-caspase-1) activated by a convergence of signals — potassium efflux, lysosomal destabilization, mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress]]/mtDNA release, and extracellular ATP. Once activated, caspase-1 cleaves pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18, and drives a lytic, inflammatory cell-death mode (pyroptosis). IL-1β then amplifies [[Inflammation]] by inducing COX-2, adhesion molecules, and other cytokines.

> [!info] Aging and redox
> NLRP3 is a core component of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP); mitochondrial ROS are both an activator of the inflammasome and a downstream effector, situating IL-1β within the redox–inflammation axis central to aging.

Therapeutic IL-1 blockade (e.g., anakinra, canakinumab) reduces cardiovascular events, validating IL-1β as a geroscience target.
