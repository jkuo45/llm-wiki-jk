# Rtg3

Rtg3 is a basic helix-loop-helix/leucine-zipper (bHLH/ZIP) transcription factor in [[S. cerevisiae]] and the DNA-binding engine of the retrograde (RTG) response. In healthy cells it is phosphorylated by the conserved kinase cascade (Mks1–Ptc1 axis) and held in the cytoplasm bound to 14-3-3 proteins alongside its partner [[Rtg1]]. When mitochondrial respiration is impaired — loss of mitochondrial DNA, respiratory-chain poisons, or carbon-source shifts — the retention signal is lost, and the Rtg1–Rtg3 heterodimer enters the nucleus to activate a battery of nuclear-encoded genes, prominently the citrate–isocitrate shuttle enzymes (CIT2, ACO1, IDH1/IDH2) that sustain anaplerotic metabolism without functional mitochondria.

The RTG pathway is the yeast counterpart of mammalian mitonuclear stress signaling, conceptually aligned with the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPRmt]] and [[Mitohormesis]]. Rtg3 thus integrates mitochondrial state with nuclear transcription to restore metabolic flexibility, a conserved longevity mechanism studied extensively in yeast as a [[Model Organisms]] system.

Its relevance to adrenochrome and redox biology lies in metabolic reprogramming that influences the cellular reducing environment. By sustaining citrate flux and α-ketoglutarate/glutathione precursor supply, the Rtg3 program supports antioxidant capacity, buffering the [[Oxidative Stress]] that otherwise drives catecholamine autoxidation — the conversion of [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. Rtg3 therefore illustrates how a mitonuclear redox signal can be co-opted for cytoprotection.

> [!info] DNA-binding role
> Rtg3 carries the bHLH/ZIP DNA-binding domain; Rtg1 contributes the heterodimerization needed for high-affinity promoter occupancy.
