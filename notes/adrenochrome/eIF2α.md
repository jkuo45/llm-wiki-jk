# eIF2α

**eIF2α** (eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2 subunit alpha) is a key regulatory hub of the [[Integrated Stress Response]] (ISR). Its phosphorylation at Ser51 by stress-activated kinases attenuates global cap-dependent translation while paradoxically promoting the translation of select stress-responsive mRNAs such as [[ATF4]].

## Mechanism

Under normal conditions, eIF2α is required for delivery of the initiator methionyl-tRNA to the 40S ribosomal subunit. During cellular stress, four kinases phosphorylate eIF2α at Ser51:
- **[[HRI]]** (EIF2AK1) — activated by mitochondrial stress via [[DELE1]] and heme deficiency
- **PERK** (EIF2AK3) — activated by [[ER Stress]]
- **GCN2** (EIF2AK4) — activated by amino acid starvation
- **PKR** (EIF2AK2) — activated by double-stranded RNA

Phosphorylated eIF2α sequesters the guanine nucleotide exchange factor eIF2B, preventing recycling of the eIF2 complex and blocking translation initiation. This reduces overall protein synthesis, conserving resources, while selectively allowing translation of [[ATF4]], which then drives transcription of adaptive genes.

## Role in Mitochondrial Signaling

In the context of the [[_document_ - Mitohormetic Redox-Relay]], mitochondrial matrix ROS activates [[OMA1]], which cleaves [[DELE1]]. Cleaved DELE1 translocates to the cytosol and binds [[HRI]], leading to eIF2α phosphorylation and subsequent [[ATF4]] translation. This OMA1-DELE1-HRI-eIF2α-ATF4 axis provides a direct signaling route from mitochondrial dysfunction to the nucleus.
