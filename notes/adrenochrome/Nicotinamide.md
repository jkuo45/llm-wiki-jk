# Nicotinamide

**Nicotinamide** (NAM), also known as **niacinamide**, is the amide form of **vitamin B3** (niacin). It is an essential precursor in the **NAD⁺ salvage pathway** and a critical regulator of sirtuin enzyme activity, acting as both a substrate for NAD⁺ biosynthesis and an endogenous feedback inhibitor of sirtuins.

## Chemical Properties

Nicotinamide is the water-soluble amide derivative of nicotinic acid (niacin). Unlike niacin, nicotinamide does not cause niacin-associated flushing at high doses, making it the preferred form for therapeutic supplementation. It is biosynthesised from dietary tryptophan or niacin and is converted into [[NMN]] (nicotinamide mononucleotide) by [[NAMPT]], the rate-limiting enzyme in the NAD⁺ salvage pathway.

## Role in NAD⁺ Metabolism

Nicotinamide is at the centre of cellular NAD⁺ homeostasis:

- **Salvage Pathway**: [[NAMPT]] condenses nicotinamide with PRPP to form [[NMN]], which is then converted to [[NAD+]] by [[NMNAT]] enzymes. This salvage pathway accounts for approximately 85% of cellular NAD⁺ production.
- **Sirtuin By-Product**: Sirtuin-mediated deacetylation of protein substrates consumes NAD⁺ and produces nicotinamide as a by-product, establishing a feedback loop.
- **CD38 Substrate**: [[CD38]] and [[CD157]] also consume NAD⁺ to produce nicotinamide and cADPR, competing with sirtuins for the same NAD⁺ pool.

## Sirtuin Feedback Inhibition

Nicotinamide acts as an **endogenous non-competitive feedback inhibitor** of sirtuins:

- **Mechanism**: NAM binds to a conserved pocket within the sirtuin catalytic domain after the release of the ADP-ribose-acetyl-ADP-ribose intermediate, blocking the base-exchange reaction and preventing NAD⁺ re-binding.
- **Potency**: SIRT1–SIRT3 are potently inhibited by physiological concentrations of nicotinamide (IC₅₀ ~30–200 µM). [[SIRT5]] and [[SIRT6]] are relatively resistant to nicotinamide inhibition.
- **Physiological Relevance**: Intracellular NAM concentrations fluctuate with NAD⁺ turnover, providing dynamic feedback regulation of sirtuin activity. During caloric restriction, NAM levels decrease, relieving sirtuin inhibition and contributing to sirtuin activation.

## Therapeutic Applications

Nicotinamide has been investigated for multiple clinical applications:

- **Neuroprotection**: NAM protects against neuronal injury in models of stroke, Parkinson's disease, and Alzheimer's disease by maintaining NAD⁺ levels and supporting mitochondrial function.
- **Metabolic Health**: High-dose nicotinamide improves glucose metabolism and insulin sensitivity in prediabetic individuals through NAD⁺ repletion.
- **Cancer Prevention**: NAM reduces the incidence of non-melanoma skin cancers in high-risk patients (as demonstrated in the ONTRAC trial) through mechanisms involving DNA repair enhancement.
- **Renal Protection**: NAM ameliorates acute kidney injury by preserving NAD⁺ pools and supporting sirtuin-mediated stress resistance.

## Relationship to Other NAD⁺ Precursors

Nicotinamide is distinct from other NAD⁺ precursors:

- **[[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR)**: Converted to NAD⁺ via NRK enzymes, bypassing the rate-limiting NAMPT step.
- **[[Nicotinic acid]] (NA)**: Converted to NAD⁺ via the Preiss-Handler pathway; causes flushing due to GPR109A activation.
- **[[NMN]]**: The immediate product of NAMPT activity; directly converted to NAD⁺ by NMNAT enzymes.
