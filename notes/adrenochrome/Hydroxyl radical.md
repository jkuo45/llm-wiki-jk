# Hydroxyl radical

The **Hydroxyl radical** (•OH) is the most reactive and damaging member of the [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) family. It has an extremely short half-life and reacts instantly with almost any biological molecule it encounters.

## Role in [[Adrenochrome]] Formation
The hydroxyl radical is a potent oxidant that participates in the radical chain process of [[Epinephrine]] [[Oxidation]]. It can drive the formation of [[Adrenochrome]] from adrenaline, especially in environments with high oxidative stress.

## Formation
- **Fenton Reaction:** Produced from [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] in the presence of transition metals (e.g., Fe²⁺).
- **Haber-Weiss Reaction:** Formed from the reaction between [[Superoxide anion]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]].
- **Radiation:** Can be generated by the radiolysis of water (e.g., due to UV or ionizing radiation).

## Biological Impact
Because it cannot be detoxified by enzymes (due to its high reactivity), the hydroxyl radical causes non-specific damage to:
- **DNA:** Leading to mutations and strand breaks.
- **Lipids:** Initiating lipid peroxidation.
- **Proteins:** Causing oxidation and loss of function.
