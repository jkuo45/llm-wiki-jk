# Oxidation

Oxidation is a concept related to [[Adrenochrome]] and catecholamine metabolism.

## Chemical Principles

Oxidation is defined as the loss of electrons, increase in oxidation state, or addition of oxygen to a molecule. In biological systems, oxidation reactions are central to energy metabolism, signaling, and the generation of [[notes/_link/Reactive Oxygen Species]]. The thermodynamic driving force is governed by the redox potential (E₀′) of the half-reaction; for catecholamines, the one-electron oxidation of the catechol ring to an [[o-semiquinone]] radical occurs at approximately +0.3 to +0.5 V at pH 7.0, making them favorable substrates for many oxidizing enzymes.

## Types of Biological Oxidation

**Enzymatic oxidation** is catalyzed by dedicated oxidoreductases. [[MAO]] (monoamine oxidase) deaminates catecholamines via flavin-dependent two-electron oxidation. [[COMT]] methylates the catechol hydroxyls, preventing their oxidation. [[Cyclooxygenases]] and [[Lipoxygenases]] incorporate molecular oxygen into arachidonic acid. [[Myeloperoxidase]] generates [[Hypochlorous acid]] (HOCl), a potent two-electron oxidant that rapidly converts [[notes/_link/Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]].

**Non-enzymatic (autoxidative) oxidation** proceeds spontaneously in the presence of molecular oxygen and is accelerated by transition metals, alkaline pH, and UV radiation. This pathway generates [[Superoxide]] (O₂⁻·) as a byproduct, which dismutates to [[Hydrogen peroxide]] (H₂O₂) and perpetuates oxidative chain reactions.

## Electron Transfer Mechanisms

Catecholamine oxidation proceeds through sequential one-electron transfers. The catechol (QH₂) first loses an electron to form the semiquinone radical (QH·), a transient paramagnetic species detectable by [[EPR spectroscopy]]. A second one-electron oxidation yields the [[o-quinone]], which can undergo intramolecular cyclization (Michael addition) to form the reactive aminochrome intermediate. The semiquinone can also reduce molecular oxygen to [[Superoxide]], establishing a redox-cycling loop that amplifies [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Relevance to Catecholamine Metabolism and Aging

Oxidation is both a normal metabolic fate and a pathological liability for catecholamines. Under physiological conditions, enzymatic oxidation via [[MAO]] and [[COMT]] produces inactive metabolites cleared by the kidney. However, when enzymatic capacity is overwhelmed or antioxidant defenses are compromised, non-enzymatic oxidation divers catecholamine flux toward [[Aminochromes]] and melanin-like polymers. The accumulation of oxidized catecholamine species in [[notes/_link/Substantia Nigra]] dopaminergic neurons — forming [[Neuromelanin]] — is a hallmark of [[Parkinson's Disease]] and normal aging. The intersection of oxidation, [[Mitochondrial dysfunction]], and accumulated macromolecular damage represents a core tenet of the [[Free radical theory of aging]].
