# MITF

[[MITF]] (Microphthalmia-associated transcription factor) is a basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-Zip) transcription factor that serves as the master regulator of melanocyte development, survival, and pigment production. It belongs to the [[MiT]]/TFE family alongside [[TFEB]], TFE3, and TFEC, and shares the conserved helical periodicity motif that couples lysosomal biogenesis to cellular stress adaptation. Germline loss-of-function mutations in *MITF* cause Waardenburg syndrome and Tietz albinism-deafness syndrome, underscoring its non-redundant role in melanocyte specification.

## Role in Melanogenesis and Redox

Within melanocytes and retinal pigment epithelium, MITF drives expression of the melanin synthesis machinery, including [[Tyrosine Hydroxylase|tyrosinase]], TRP1, and DCT, the enzymes that convert [[Tyrosine]] into [[Dopaquinone]] and downstream [[Dopachrome]] and [[5,6-Dihydroxyindole]] intermediates that polymerize into [[Melanins]]. Because melanogenesis is a redox-intensive process that generates ortho-quinones and reactive oxygen species, MITF indirectly governs the cellular capacity to buffer [[Oxidative Stress]]. 

> [!info]
> Neuromelanin in catecholaminergic neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra]] is biochemically related to cutaneous melanin. MITF and the broader MiT/TFE network coordinate the lysosomal clearance and metal-handling systems that influence neuronal pigment accumulation and vulnerability in [[Parkinson's Disease]].

MITF activity is modulated by phosphorylation (MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and AMPK pathways), by SUMOylation, and by nutrient-sensing inputs via [[mTOR]] and [[PGC-1α]]. Under lysosomal stress, MITF translocates to the nucleus to induce lysosomal and autophagy genes, linking pigment-cell identity to the [[Lysosome Biogenesis]] program. Its redox relevance to the adrenochrome/aminochrome axis lies in the shared catechol-quinone chemistry: pigment intermediates such as dopaquinone and aminochromes are electrophilic and can modify proteins, and MITF-controlled antioxidant and lysosomal pathways help detoxify these species.
