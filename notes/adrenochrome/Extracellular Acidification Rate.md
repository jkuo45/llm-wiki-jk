# Extracellular Acidification Rate

**Extracellular Acidification Rate (ECAR)** is a real-time metabolic readout of proton efflux from living cells into the surrounding medium, measured with a pH-sensitive biosensor (classically the [[Seahorse XF Analyzer]]). Because glycolysis converts glucose to lactate with net proton production, ECAR serves as a proxy for **glycolytic flux** and lactate export, complementing the [[Oxygen Consumption Rate]] (OCR) measurement of mitochondrial respiration.

The ECAR/OCR ratio is widely used to classify cellular metabolic phenotype — high-ECAR, low-OCR cells are "glycolytic," whereas the reverse indicates oxidative phosphorylation dependence. Pharmacological probes (glucose, oligomycin, 2-deoxy-D-glucose) resolve glycolytic capacity and reserve. In redox- and mitohormesis-themed studies, ECAR tracks the Warburg-like shift that often accompanies mitochondrial stress: mild respiratory-chain inhibition (e.g., by adrenochrome-linked redox signals or mitochondrial translation blockers) can drive compensatory glycolysis, raising ECAR.

> [!info] Links to adrenochrome redox
> A calibrated redox stress that engages [[Mitohormesis]] frequently rewires substrate use toward glycolysis — the very transition ECAR quantifies — making it a useful endpoint for mitohormetic dosing studies.

ECAR is therefore a practical, non-destructive assay for mapping the metabolic consequences of adrenochromatization and other redox interventions.
