Description

Silver oxide is an oxide of [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver). It is used to prepare other [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) compounds, and in the silver oxide battery. [Silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) is a metallic element with the chemical symbol Ag and atomic number 47. It occurs naturally in its pure, free form, as an alloy with [gold](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Gold) and other metals, and in minerals such as argentite and chlorargyrite. (L808, L809, L825)

## 6 Pharmacology and Biochemistry

### 6.1 Metabolism / Metabolites

[Silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) and its compounds can be absorbed via inhalation, while [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) compounds can also be absorbed orally and dermally. It distributes throughout the body in the blood, particularily to the liver. Insoluble silver salts are transformed into soluble [silver sulfide](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/silver%20sulfide) albuminates, bind to amino or carboxyl groups in RNA, DNA, and proteins, or are reduced to metallic [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) by [ascorbic acid](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/ascorbic%20acid) or catecholamines. Metallic [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) is oxidized and may deposit in the tissues, causing arygria. [Silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) is eliminated primarily in the faeces. (L808)

## 8 Toxicity

### 8.1 Toxicological Information

#### 8.1.1 Toxicity Summary

Metallic [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) is oxidized and may deposit in the tissues, causing arygria. The [silver ion](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/silver%20ion) is known to inhibit [glutathione](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/glutathione) peroxidase and NA+,K+-ATPase activity, disrupting [selenium](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Selenium) -catalyzed sulfhydryl oxidation-reduction reactions and intracellular ion concentrations, respectively. [Silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) nanoparticles are believed to disrupt the mitochondrial respiratory chain, causing oxidative stress, reduced [ATP](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/ATP) synthesis, and DNA damage. (L808, A243, A244, A245, A246)

#### 8.1.2 Carcinogen Classification

Carcinogen Classification

No indication of carcinogenicity to humans (not listed by IARC).

#### 8.1.3 Health Effects

[Silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) itself is not toxic to humans, but most silver salts are. In large doses, [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) and compounds containing it can be absorbed into the circulatory system and become deposited in various body tissues, leading to argyria, which results in a blue-grayish pigmentation of the skin, eyes, and mucous membranes. Argyria is rare, and although, so far as known, this condition does not otherwise harm a person's health, it is disfiguring and usually permanent. Mild forms of argyria are sometimes mistaken for cyanosis. (L809)

#### 8.1.4 Exposure Routes

Oral (L808); inhalation (L808); dermal (L808)

#### 8.1.5 Signs and Symptoms

Exposure to high levels of [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) for a long period of time may result in a condition called arygria, a blue-gray discoloration of the skin and other body tissues. Argyria is a permanent effect but does not appear to be harmful to health. Exposure to high levels of [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) in the air has resulted in breathing problems, lung and throat irritation, and stomach pains. Skin contact with [silver](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/element/Silver) can cause mild allergic reactions such as rash, swelling, and inflammation in some people. (L808)

#### 8.1.8 Treatment

EYES: irrigate opened eyes for several minutes under running [water](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/water). INGESTION: do not induce vomiting. Rinse mouth with [water](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/water) (never give anything by mouth to an unconscious person). Seek immediate medical advice. SKIN: should be treated immediately by rinsing the affected parts in cold running [water](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/water) for at least 15 minutes, followed by thorough washing with soap and [water](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/water). If necessary, the person should shower and change contaminated clothing and shoes, and then must seek medical attention. INHALATION: supply fresh air. If required provide artificial respiration.
