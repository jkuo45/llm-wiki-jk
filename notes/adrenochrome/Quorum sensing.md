# Quorum sensing

Quorum sensing is a cell-to-cell communication system in which bacteria produce, release, and detect diffusible signal molecules (autoinducers) to coordinate gene expression across the population once a critical cell density is reached. Classic examples include the *LuxI/LuxR* acyl-homoserine lactone system in *Vibrio fischeri* and the *Com* peptide systems of Gram-positive species. The functional output is collective behavior — bioluminescence, biofilm formation, virulence factor secretion, and competence — that benefits the community rather than any single cell.

In an aging and redox context, quorum sensing is of interest because the same principle — a secreted molecule whose concentration reports on the abundance or stress state of a compartment — has been proposed as a model for [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|mitochondrial-nuclear communication]]. When mitochondrial protein-folding capacity is exceeded, stressed mitochondria release peptides or metabolites that act as retrograde signals to the nucleus, engaging transcription factors such as [[SKN-1]] (the *C. elegans* [[NRF2]] ortholog) and the [[Rtg1]]/[[Rtg3]] complex to reprogram metabolism and antioxidant defense. This has been likened to quorum sensing in that the signal integrates the status of many organelles rather than a single one.

The relevance to [[Adrenochrome]] and redox biology is indirect but mechanistically connected: mitochondrial-derived reactive oxygen species are a key trigger of the retrograde, hormetic response ([[Mitohormesis]]), and the resulting upregulation of phase-II and antioxidant enzymes helps buffer the [[Oxidative Stress]] that drives catecholamine autoxidation. Quorum-sensing-like peptide relays therefore sit upstream of the resilience pathways that limit conversion of [[Epinephrine]] to adrenochrome and the accumulation of damage with age.

> [!info] Key parallel
> Both bacterial quorum sensing and the mitochondrial UPR rely on a threshold-dependent, population-wide signal rather than an all-or-none single-cell switch.
