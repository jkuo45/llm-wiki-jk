# RAGE

RAGE (Receptor for Advanced Glycation End Products) is a cell surface molecule that recognizes and binds to [[Advanced Glycation End Products]].

## Overview

RAGE is a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily. The interaction between AGEs and RAGE alters cell and organ functions by activating inflammatory signaling pathways, generating [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS), and inducing [[Apoptosis]].

## Pathological Role

RAGE signaling is implicated in:
- [[Inflammation]]
- [[Oxidative Stress]]
- [[Aging]]
- [[Autophagy]]
- Vascular alterations and diabetic vasculopathy.

## Receptor Structure

RAGE is a 35–55 kDa transmembrane protein belonging to the [[Immunoglobulin superfamily]]. The extracellular region comprises three domains: an N-terminal V (variable)-type domain responsible for ligand binding, followed by two C (constant)-type domains (C1 and C2). A single transmembrane helix anchors the receptor, and a short (43-amino acid) cytoplasmic tail mediates intracellular signaling through interactions with [[Diaphanous-1]] (Dia-1) and the [[ERK]] pathway. The V-domain contains a positively charged surface patch that recognizes negatively charged regions of its diverse ligands.

## Ligand Diversity

RAGE is a multi-ligand receptor with remarkable ligand promiscuity. Beyond [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs), RAGE binds [[HMGB1]] (amphoterin), members of the [[S100 protein family]] (S100A8/A9, S100A12, S100B), [[Amyloid β]] oligomers, β-sheet fibrils, [[Mac-1]] integrin, [[Complement]] C3a, [[Phosphatidylserine]], and [[DNA]]. Each ligand class activates overlapping but distinct downstream signaling profiles. This broad ligand repertoire positions RAGE as a sensor for damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) in sterile inflammation.

## Signaling Pathways

Ligand engagement of RAGE activates multiple downstream signaling cascades: (i) the [[NF-κB]] pathway via [[IKKα]]/[[IKKβ]] activation, creating a self-amplifying loop as NF-κB upregulates RAGE expression; (ii) [[MAPK]] pathways ([[ERK1/2]], [[p38 MAPK]], [[c-Jun N-terminal kinase|JNK]]), promoting [[AP-1]] activation and inflammatory gene expression; (iii) [[JAK]]/[[STAT]] signaling, particularly [[STAT3]]; and (iv) [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] pathway, activating [[mTOR]] and inhibiting [[Autophagy]]. Prolonged RAGE activation shifts cells from transient inflammatory responses to sustained pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic states.

## Soluble RAGE as a Decoy Receptor

Alternate splicing and proteolytic cleavage by [[ADAM10]] and [[MMP9]] generate soluble RAGE (sRAGE) and endogenous secretory RAGE (esRAGE), which lack the transmembrane and intracellular domains. sRAGE functions as a decoy receptor, sequestering RAGE ligands and preventing membrane-bound RAGE activation. Low serum sRAGE levels are a biomarker for increased risk of [[Cardiovascular Disease]], [[Diabetes]], [[Metabolic Syndrome]], [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], and [[Alzheimer's Disease]]. Administration of recombinant sRAGE attenuates disease progression in preclinical models of diabetic vasculopathy, atherosclerosis, and neuroinflammation.

## Role in Diabetic and Neurodegenerative Disease

In [[Diabetes]], chronic hyperglycemia drives AGE formation, engaging RAGE to promote [[Insulin Resistance]], endothelial dysfunction, podocyte injury, and [[Atherosclerosis]]. RAGE signaling underlies diabetic complications including nephropathy, retinopathy, neuropathy, and cardiovascular disease. In neurodegeneration, RAGE mediates [[Amyloid β]] transport across the [[Blood-brain barrier]], activates microglial neuroinflammation, and induces neuronal [[Oxidative Stress]]. RAGE expression is elevated in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] brain tissue, and RAGE-amyloid β interaction contributes to synaptic dysfunction and cognitive decline. RAGE also mediates inflammatory signaling in [[Parkinson's Disease]] through α-synuclein-dependent mechanisms.

## Connection to Inflammation and the Adrenochrome Pathway

RAGE signaling intersects with the [[Adrenochrome Pathway]] through multiple mechanisms: (i) adrenochrome-induced [[Oxidative Stress]] promotes AGE formation via glycolysis-derived [[Dicarbonyls]], activating the AGE-RAGE inflammatory axis; (ii) RAGE activation upregulates [[NF-κB]] target genes including [[COX-2]] and [[iNOS]], which amplify oxidative stress and catecholamine oxidation; and (iii) RAGE-mediated inhibition of [[Autophagy]] may impair clearance of aminochrome-damaged mitochondria. Targeting RAGE represents a strategy to break the feed-forward cycle of glycation, oxidative stress, and aminochrome accumulation in [[Aging]] and age-related diseases.
