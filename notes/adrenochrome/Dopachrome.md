# Dopachrome

[[Dopachrome]] is the unstable indole-quinone intermediate produced when [[Dopaquinone]] undergoes intramolecular cyclization and decarboxylative rearrangement. It occupies a pivotal position in melanogenesis: enzymatic tautomerization by dopachrome tautomerase (DCT) converts it to 5,6-dihydroxyindole-2-carboxylic acid (DHICA), whereas non-enzymatic decay yields [[5,6-Dihydroxyindole]] (DHI). Both indoles are then oxidized and polymerized into eumelanin, the predominant form of [[Melanins]].

## Redox Behavior

Like other ortho-quinone-derived indoles, dopachrome can participate in redox cycling, generating [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] such as [[Superoxide anion]] upon one-electron reduction. Its quinone imine moiety is electrophilic and contributes to the protein-bonded pigment aggregates characteristic of mature melanin and [[Neuromelanin]]. The balance between DCT-directed DHICA formation and spontaneous DHI formation determines pigment composition, solubility, and metal-chelating capacity.

> [!info]
> In the brain, dopachrome and related indolequinones arise from oxidative metabolism of [[Dopamine]] and [[L-DOPA]] in catecholaminergic neurons, feeding the age-dependent accumulation of neuromelanin in the [[Substantia Nigra]].

## Relationship to the Aminochrome Pathway

Dopachrome is the dopaminergic analog of the aminochrome intermediates generated from [[Epinephrine]] oxidation. Just as [[Adrenochrome]] and [[Dopaminochrome]] represent aminochrome species on the adrenaline/dopamine side, dopachrome represents the carboxylated indolequinone on the DOPA/melanin side. Shared chemistry—quinone formation, redox cycling, thiol trapping, and polymerization—links pigment biosynthesis to [[Oxidative Stress]] and neuronal vulnerability in [[Parkinson's Disease]].
