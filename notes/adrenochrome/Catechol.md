# Catechol

**Catechol** (pyrocatechol, 1,2-dihydroxybenzene, C₆H₄(OH)₂) is an organic compound consisting of a benzene ring bearing two adjacent hydroxyl groups (ortho-diphenol). It is the parent structural scaffold of the [[Catecholamines|catecholamine]] family, which includes [[Dopamine]], [[Norepinephrine]], and [[Epinephrine]]. Catechol is a white crystalline solid that readily oxidizes to form ortho-quinones, a process mechanistically analogous to the oxidation of [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]].

## Chemical Properties

Catechol exhibits two sequential acid dissociations: pKₐ₁ ≈ 9.5 (first hydroxyl) and pKₐ₂ ≈ 12.8 (second hydroxyl). It is soluble in water, ethanol, and diethyl ether. A classic qualitative test is the formation of an intense green color upon reaction with [[FeCl₃ (Ferric Chloride)|FeCl₃]], characteristic of phenols. Catechol forms stable complexes with many metal ions, including Fe³⁺, Cu²⁺, and Al³⁺, a property relevant to its biological metal-chelation activity.

**Autoxidation & Redox Cycling:** In neutral to alkaline solution, catechol undergoes autoxidation via sequential one-electron transfers: catechol → *o*-semiquinone radical → *o*-benzoquinone. This reaction generates reactive oxygen species ([[ROS]]) such as superoxide (O₂⁻), hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), and hydroxyl radical (•OH), establishing a redox-cycling loop that can deplete cellular antioxidants like [[Glutathione]]. The instability of catechol under alkaline conditions leads to rapid polymerization into brown melanin-like pigments — a process closely related to the browning of [[Epinephrine]] solutions and the formation of [[Neuromelanin]] in catecholaminergic neurons.

## Occurrence & Sources

**Natural occurrence:** Catechol is found widely in the plant kingdom. It is present in onion (*Allium cepa*), tea leaves (*Camellia sinensis*), and the bark of various woody species. In plants, catechol is biosynthesized via the [[Shikimic Acid Pathway|shikimic acid pathway]] and serves as a precursor for lignin and other phenolic polymers. It also functions as an allelochemical, inhibiting the growth of competing plants by disrupting photosynthetic electron transport.

**Industrial production:** Historically, catechol was obtained by distillation of coal tar. Modern industrial synthesis proceeds via hydroxylation of [[Phenol]] with hydrogen peroxide (the [[Raschig process|Raschig hydroxylation]]), yielding a mixture of catechol and hydroquinone.

**Human metabolism:** The catechol moiety is central to mammalian biochemistry. The amino acid **L-3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine ([[L-DOPA]])** is a catechol-containing compound that serves as the biosynthetic precursor to all [[Catecholamines]]. The enzymes [[Tyrosine Hydroxylase]] and [[Aromatic L-Amino Acid Decarboxylase]] introduce and retain the ortho-diphenol structure. [[COMT (Catechol-O-Methyltransferase)]] and [[MAO (Monoamine Oxidase)]] metabolize catecholamines in part by modifying or oxidizing the catechol ring.

## Applications

**Agriculture:** Catechol is a byproduct of certain herbicides and acts as a [[Photosynthesis]] inhibitor by interfering with photosystem II electron transport.

**Photography & Industry:** Historically used as a photographic developer (reducing agent for silver halides). In polymer chemistry, catechol serves as an antioxidant in rubber and plastic production. It is also used as a synthetic precursor in the fragrance and flavor industry.

**Laboratory & Biomedical Research:** Catechol is the standard substrate for *in vitro* [[COMT (Catechol-O-Methyltransferase)|catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT)]] activity assays. It is widely employed as a model compound for studying the oxidation chemistry of [[Catecholamines]] and the formation of [[Melanin|melanins]], including [[Neuromelanin]]. In [[Adrenochrome]] research, catechol serves as a simplified model for the oxidative cascade epinephrine → adrenochrome → melanin, helping elucidate the role of redox-active metal ions and pH in pigment formation.

## Toxicity

Catechol is an irritant to skin, eyes, and the respiratory tract. It is classified by the [[IARC (International Agency for Research on Cancer)|International Agency for Research on Cancer]] as a **Group 2B carcinogen** (possibly carcinogenic to humans), based on animal studies showing induction of forestomach tumors. Chronic exposure can cause skin depigmentation (leukoderma) through toxicity to melanocytes. The toxicity of catechol is mediated predominantly by its oxidation product, *o*-benzoquinone, a highly electrophilic species that rapidly arylates cellular nucleophiles, including cysteine residues on proteins and glutathione, leading to oxidative stress, heme protein damage, and mitochondrial dysfunction. The [[Quinone|quinone]] form depletes intracellular [[Glutathione]] and triggers [[Apoptosis]] in susceptible cell types.
