# Cardiotoxicity

**Cardiotoxicity** refers to the damage to the heart muscle (myocardium) caused by exposure to toxins, drugs, or reactive metabolites. 

## Link to [[Adrenochrome]]
[[Adrenochrome]] is recognized as a cardiotoxic agent. Its formation during periods of high stress or myocardial infarction contributes to heart damage through several mechanisms:
- **[[Redox Cycling]]:** Adrenochrome generates [[Superoxide anion]] and other [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS), causing oxidative damage to cardiomyocytes.
- **Mitochondrial Impairment:** It can uncouple oxidative phosphorylation in heart mitochondria, leading to a loss of ATP production and cell death.
- **Myocardial Stunning:** High local concentrations of oxidized adrenaline are linked to "stunning" of the heart muscle after an ischemic event.

## Clinical Contexts
- **Myocardial Infarction:** Neutrophil infiltration and high adrenaline levels lead to massive adrenochrome formation, worsening tissue damage.
- **Chronic Stress:** Prolonged elevation of catecholamines can lead to cumulative oxidative damage in the heart.
