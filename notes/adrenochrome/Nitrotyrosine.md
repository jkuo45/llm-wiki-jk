# Nitrotyrosine

[[Nitrotyrosine]]—most commonly 3-nitrotyrosine—is the nitrated derivative of the amino acid [[Tyrosine]] and the stable, accumulated footprint of [[Tyrosine Nitration]]. It is formed when peroxynitrite (derived from [[Nitric Oxide]] and [[Superoxide anion]]) or myeloperoxidase ([[Myeloperoxidase]]) using nitrite and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] nitrates protein-bound or free tyrosine. Because nitrotyrosine resists turnover, it serves as a durable biomarker of nitrosative and oxidative injury.

## Biochemistry and Detection

Free 3-nitrotyrosine can be quantified by [[Mass Spectrometry]] and HPLC, while immunodetection localizes nitrated proteins in tissue. Elevated nitrotyrosine tracks with diseases characterized by excessive ROS/RNS flux, including atherosclerosis, neurodegeneration, and sepsis. Nitration can disrupt tyrosine's role in phosphorylation signaling and protein structure.

> [!info]
> In the adrenochrome context, neutrophil [[Myeloperoxidase Pathway]] activity simultaneously generates aminochromes from [[Epinephrine]] and nitrating species from nitrite—making nitrotyrosine a composite marker of the same inflammatory redox milieu.

## Intersection with Adrenochrome, Neuromelanin, and Methemoglobin

Nitrotyrosine accumulation overlaps the [[Methemoglobin]]/nitrite redox system: nitrite, liberated from or reduced by methemoglobin, supplies the nitrogen donor for MPO-dependent nitration. Within catecholaminergic neurons, nitrative damage to proteins complements aminochrome- and [[Neuromelanin]]-driven oxidative injury in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. The shared upstream chemistry binds nitrotyrosine to the broader aminochrome and glyco-oxidative axes of the vault.
