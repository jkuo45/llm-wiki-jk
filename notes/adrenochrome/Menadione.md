# Menadione

**Menadione** (2-methyl-1,4-naphthoquinone, also called vitamin K₃) is a synthetic naphthoquinone that lacks the isoprenoid side chain of the natural vitamin K forms. Its principal biological activity stems from **redox cycling**: menadione is one-electron reduced by enzymes such as NADPH-cytochrome P450 reductase and mitochondrial NADH dehydrogenase to a semiquinone radical, which rapidly transfers an electron to molecular oxygen, regenerating the parent quinone while producing [[Superoxide anion]] and downstream [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]. This generates a sustained burst of [[Oxidative Stress]].

At high doses menadione is cytotoxic — depleting glutathione, oxidizing thiols, and triggering necrotic or apoptotic cell death, which historically limited its use as a vitamin K source in humans. However, at low, subtoxic concentrations menadione acts as a **mitohormetic** agent. In *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, mild menadione exposure activates the mitochondrial retrograde response and stress-resistance pathways that extend replicative [[Lifespan]], an effect squarely within the [[Mitohormesis]] framework.

> [!warning] Human safety
> Menadione is no longer used clinically as a vitamin K supplement because of dose-dependent hemolytic toxicity, particularly in neonates with immature detoxification.

The compound remains a valuable experimental tool for studying redox signaling, [[Redox Cycling]], and the thresholds that separate toxic oxidative damage from adaptive hormesis.
