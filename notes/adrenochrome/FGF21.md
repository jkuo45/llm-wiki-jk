# FGF21

Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is a member of the [[FGF|FGF19 subfamily]] of fibroblast growth factors that functions as an endocrine hormone-like signaling molecule. Unlike canonical FGFs that act locally in a paracrine manner, FGF21 enters the circulation and exerts pleiotropic metabolic effects on liver, adipose tissue, pancreas, and brain. It is robustly induced by mitochondrial stress and is classified as a [[Mitokines]].

## Overview

FGF21 belongs to the [[FGF]] family (FGF19 subfamily, which also includes [[FGF19]] and [[FGF23]]). It is expressed predominantly in the liver (the primary circulating source), with lower expression in [[Adipose tissue]], [[Pancreas]], skeletal muscle, and [[Kidney]]. Hepatic FGF21 transcription is directly regulated by [[PPARα]], which binds the FGF21 promoter in response to fasting, [[Fatty acid oxidation]], and [[Ketogenesis|ketogenic]] stimuli. Circulating FGF21 levels in healthy humans range from approximately 100–500 pg/mL and increase several-fold under metabolic stress, fasting, or exercise.

## Structure

Human FGF21 is synthesized as a 209-amino acid polypeptide ([[Signal peptide|signal peptide]]: residues 1–28; mature protein: 29–209). A defining structural feature is the absence of the canonical [[Heparin sulfate|heparin-binding domain]] present in paracrine FGFs, which allows FGF21 to escape sequestration by [[Extracellular matrix]] [[Proteoglycans]] and enter the bloodstream as an endocrine factor. FGF21 signals through a bipartite receptor complex requiring both a canonical FGF receptor ([[FGFR1c]], [[FGFR2c]], or [[FGFR3c]]) and the obligate co-receptor [[β-klotho]] ([[KLB]]), which confers tissue specificity. [[β-klotho]] is expressed primarily in metabolic tissues — liver, adipose, pancreas, and brain — restricting FGF21 signaling to these targets.

## Mechanism of Action

FGF21 binds to the [[β-klotho]]–[[FGFR]] complex with high affinity, inducing receptor dimerization and autophosphorylation. Downstream signaling proceeds primarily through the [[RAS]]–[[MAPK]]/[[ERK1/2]] pathway and the [[PI3K]]–[[AKT]] pathway, leading to transcriptional reprogramming in target tissues. In [[Adipose tissue]], FGF21 stimulates [[GLUT1]] expression to enhance glucose uptake independently of [[Insulin]] signaling. In the liver, FGF21 activates [[PGC-1α]] to coordinate [[Ketogenesis]], [[Gluconeogenesis]], and [[Fatty acid oxidation]] programs.

## Metabolic Functions

### Liver

In [[Hepatocytes]], FGF21 drives the fasting adaptive response by inducing [[PGC-1α]], which in turn activates genes for [[Fatty acid oxidation]] ([[CPT1A]]), [[Ketogenesis]] ([[HMGCS2]], [[BDH1]]), and [[Gluconeogenesis]] ([[PEPCK]], [[G6PC]]). FGF21 also suppresses [[Lipogenesis]] via inhibition of [[SREBP1c]], protecting against hepatic steatosis.

### Adipose Tissue

In [[White adipose tissue]] (WAT), FGF21 stimulates glucose uptake via upregulation of [[GLUT1]] and promotes the [[Browning of white adipose tissue]] by inducing [[UCP1]], [[PGC-1α]], and [[PRDM16]], thereby increasing thermogenic capacity. In [[Brown adipose tissue]] (BAT), FGF21 enhances [[Oxygen consumption]] and [[Energy expenditure]].

### Pancreas

In the [[Pancreas]], FGF21 acts on [[β-cells]] to promote [[Insulin|insulin secretion]], enhance [[Insulin sensitivity]], and protect against [[Apoptosis]] induced by [[ER stress]] or [[Inflammation]]. [[FGF21]] also supports [[β-cell]] survival and proliferation.

### Brain

FGF21 crosses the [[Blood–brain barrier]] and signals in the [[Hypothalamus]], particularly the [[Suprachiasmatic nucleus]] and [[Dorsomedial hypothalamus]], to increase systemic [[Energy expenditure]] via activation of the [[Sympathetic nervous system]]. Central FGF21 signaling also regulates [[Circadian rhythm|circadian behavior]] and preference for macronutrients, notably suppressing carbohydrate craving and promoting [[Ketogenic diet|ketogenic]] adaptation.

## Induction by Stress

FGF21 is a prototypical [[Mitokines|mitokine]] induced by diverse cellular stresses:

- **[[Mitochondrial stress]]**: [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPR^mt) activates [[ATF4]] downstream of the [[Integrated Stress Response]] (ISR) kinase [[HRI]] ([[EIF2AK1]]), directly driving FGF21 transcription. This pathway is conserved from [[C. elegans]] to mammals.
- **[[ER stress]]**: The [[Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPR) also induces FGF21 via [[ATF4]] and [[CHOP]].
- **[[Fasting]] and [[Caloric restriction]]**: [[PPARα]]–dependent transcriptional activation couples nutrient deprivation to FGF21 production.
- **[[Exercise]]**: Acute exercise elevates circulating FGF21, likely through [[ATF4]] and [[PGC-1α]].
- **[[Cold exposure]]**: Induces FGF21 in [[Brown adipose tissue]] and liver, contributing to thermogenic adaptation.

## Clinical Relevance

FGF21 has attracted substantial interest as a therapeutic target for [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Obesity]], [[NAFLD]]/[[NASH]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome]]. Multiple FGF21 analogs and mimetics have advanced to clinical trials:

- **[[Pegbelfermin]]** ([[BMS-986036]]): A [[PEGylation|PEGylated]] recombinant FGF21 analog developed by [[Bristol-Myers Squibb]]. Phase II trials in NASH demonstrated reductions in [[Liver fat]], improved [[Fibrosis]], and favorable metabolic effects, though development was deprioritized.
- **[[Efruxifermin]]** ([[AMG 876]]/[[BFKB8488A]]): A long-acting [[Fc-fusion]] FGF21 analog ([[Akero Therapeutics]]/[[Amgen]]). Phase IIb trials (HARMONY) showed significant reductions in hepatic fat fraction, resolution of NASH without worsening fibrosis, and improvements in [[LDL cholesterol]], [[Triglycerides]], and [[HbA1c]].
- **Other analogs**: [[LLF-580]], [[MK-3655]], and [[NN-1]] are at various stages of preclinical and clinical development.

FGF21 levels are elevated in [[NAFLD]] and [[NASH]] as a compensatory response, and the degree of elevation correlates with disease severity. Genetic variants near the [[FGF21 locus]] (e.g., [[rs838133]], [[rs838145]]) are associated with altered macronutrient preference — specifically increased carbohydrate intake — in human [[Genome-wide association study|GWAS]].
