# Jeanho Yun

Jeanho Yun is a South Korean biochemist and researcher at the Department of Biochemistry, College of Medicine, Dong-A University in Busan, Korea. He is best known for his work on mitohormesis, mitochondrial stress signaling, and the biology of aging. His research has helped establish the concept that mild mitochondrial dysfunction can activate adaptive stress-response pathways that ultimately extend organismal lifespan.

## Key Research

Yun's most prominent contribution is his co-authorship of the 2014 landmark paper "Mitohormesis" in *Cell Metabolism*, co-authored with [[Toren Finkel]] at the National Institutes of Health. In this study, Yun and Finkel demonstrated that mild mitochondrial stress in *C. elegans* ([[Caenorhabditis elegans]]) can extend lifespan through an adaptive response dependent on the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] ([[UPRmt]]). Their work provided a mechanistic framework for the mitohormesis concept, showing that low-level mitochondrial perturbation triggers compensatory transcriptional programs involving [[ATFS-1]], [[SKN-1]]/[[Nrf2]], and [[UBL-5]].

Beyond the 2014 *Cell Metabolism* paper, Yun has investigated the role of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) in hormetic signaling. His work explores how ROS produced during mild mitochondrial stress act as signaling molecules rather than merely damaging byproducts, activating protective pathways such as the [[UPRmt]] and the [[Antioxidant Response Element]] (ARE)/[[SKN-1]]/Nrf2 axis. This signaling cascade ultimately upregulates chaperones, proteases, and detoxification enzymes that restore proteostasis and mitochondrial function.

Yun has also studied the involvement of specific mitochondrial proteins—including [[SPG-7]] (a mitochondrial metalloprotease), [[HAF-1]] (an ABC transporter), and [[DVE-1]] (a homeobox transcription factor)—in mediating the lifespan-extending effects of mitohormetic interventions in *C. elegans*.

## Contributions

Yun helped establish the mitohormesis paradigm, where low-level mitochondrial stress—rather than being deleterious—triggers an adaptive signaling cascade that extends lifespan. Key contributions include:

- Demonstrating that RNAi knockdown of electron transport chain components (e.g., [[nuo-6]], [[isp-1]], [[cco-1]]) extends *C. elegans* lifespan in a [[UPRmt]]-dependent manner.
- Elucidating the requirement of [[ATFS-1]] and [[UBL-5]] in mediating the protective transcriptional response to mitochondrial dysfunction.
- Providing evidence that mitochondrial stress activates a [[SKN-1]]/Nrf2-dependent transcriptional program that converges with [[UPRmt]] to enhance cellular resilience.
- Contributing to the broader understanding of how [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] at low levels function as second messengers in stress-adaptation signaling rather than purely as toxic byproducts.

His work has implications for understanding aging, age-related diseases, and potential therapeutic strategies that harness hormetic pathways (mitohormesis) to promote healthspan.
