# Keap1

**Keap1 (Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1)** is a redox-sensitive protein that functions as a sensor for oxidative stress and regulates the degradation and nuclear translocation of [[NRF2]].

## Domain Architecture

Keap1 is a 624-amino acid protein with modular structure comprising five distinct domains: an N-terminal BTB (Broad-Complex, Tramtrack, and Bric-à-Brac) domain, an intervening linker region (IVR), a central BTB and C-terminal Kelch (BACK) domain, and six C-terminal Kelch repeats forming a β-propeller structure. The BTB domain mediates Keap1 homodimerization and recruitment of [[Cullin3]] (Cul3) and [[RBX1]] to form a functional [[Cullin-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase]] complex. The Kelch repeats bind directly to the [[NRF2]] ETGE and DLG motifs, tethering Nrf2 for ubiquitination.

## Mechanism of Nrf2 Regulation

Under basal conditions, Keap1 constitutively targets Nrf2 for K48-linked polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, maintaining low Nrf2 protein levels and preventing spurious activation of the [[Antioxidant Response Element]] (ARE) transcriptome. The Keap1-Cul3-E3 ligase complex cycles Nrf2 with a half-life of approximately 20 minutes. Keap1 functions as a substrate adaptor, positioning Nrf2 for ubiquitination at specific lysine residues in its Neh1 domain. The dimeric nature of Keap1 allows simultaneous binding of two Nrf2 molecules via the high-affinity ETGE and lower-affinity DLG motifs, creating a "hinge-and-latch" mechanism that is disrupted by electrophiles.

## Redox-Sensitive Cysteine Sensors

Keap1 is distinguished by its exceptional cysteine content (27 cysteines in human Keap1), with several residues serving as redox-sensitive sensors. Critically, Cys151 in the BTB domain is required for Nrf2 activation by most electrophiles; Cys273 and Cys288 in the IVR are essential for regulation by certain heavy metals and oxidants; and Cys226, Cys434, and Cys613 contribute to overall redox sensitivity. Modification of these cysteines by [[Electrophile|electrophiles]], [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], or reactive nitrogen species induces conformational changes that impair Keap1's ability to ubiquitinate Nrf2, leading to Nrf2 stabilization and nuclear translocation.

## Conformational Changes and Nrf2 Dissociation

Electrophile modification of Keap1's sensor cysteines, particularly Cys151, causes conformational rearrangements within the BTB domain that disrupt the Keap1-Cul3 interaction or alter the spatial orientation of the Kelch domain relative to the E3 ligase. This reduces the ubiquitination efficiency of Nrf2 bound via the DLG motif while retaining ETGE binding. Newly synthesized Nrf2 molecules are no longer efficiently ubiquitinated and instead accumulate, translocate to the nucleus, and activate ARE-driven gene expression.

## Connection to Adrenochrome and Electrophile Sensing

In the context of the [[Adrenochrome Pathway]], adrenochrome and its redox-cycling products act as [[Electrophile|electrophiles]] that can modify Keap1 sensor cysteines, thereby activating the [[NRF2]] stress response. This Keap1-Nrf2-adrenochrome axis represents a feed-forward adaptive mechanism: electrophilic [[Aminochromes]] induce their own detoxification by upregulating [[NQO1]], [[HO-1]], and glutathione-synthesizing enzymes. Therapeutic targeting of Keap1 with small-molecule electrophiles (e.g., [[Sulforaphane]], [[Dimethyl fumarate]]) is an active strategy to boost endogenous antioxidant defenses in neurodegeneration, [[Diabetes]], [[Chronic Kidney Disease]], and [[Aging]].
