# Redox Cycling

**Redox Cycling** is a biochemical process in which a molecule is repeatedly reduced and then re-oxidized, often generating [[oxidative_stress/Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) in the process.

## [[Adrenochrome]] Redox Cycle
Adrenochrome is a potent participant in redox cycling, creating a "vicious cycle" of oxidative damage:
1. **Reduction:** Adrenochrome accepts an electron (e.g., from NADH or enzymes like Mitochondrial Complex I) to form an **Adrenochrome Semiquinone Radical**.
2. **Auto-oxidation:** The semiquinone radical rapidly donates the electron to molecular oxygen (O₂).
3. **Generation of ROS:** This reaction produces a [[Superoxide anion]] (O₂⁻•) and regenerates the original Adrenochrome molecule.
4. **Repetition:** The cycle repeats, catalytically producing ROS without consuming the Adrenochrome.

## Biological Impact
- **Mitochondrial Damage:** Can uncouple oxidative phosphorylation and impair ATP production, particularly in the heart.
- **[[Cardiotoxicity]]:** Linked to heart cell damage during stress or infarction.
- **Amplification:** Allows a small amount of [[Adrenochrome]] to generate a disproportionately large amount of oxidative stress.
