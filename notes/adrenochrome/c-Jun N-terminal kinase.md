# c-Jun N-terminal kinase

**c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK)** is a member of the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) family that phosphorylates the transcription factor c-Jun (and other substrates) on Ser/Thr residues. Three genes encode JNK isoforms (JNK1/2/3); JNK is activated by MAPK kinases MKK4 and MKK7 in response to cytokines, UV, osmotic shock, and — most relevant here — [[Oxidative Stress]] and mitochondrial dysfunction.

JNK occupies a pivotal position in stress signaling. In the mammalian **retrograde response**, mitochondrial stress (ROS, calcium, bioenergetic crisis) activates JNK, which phosphorylates and modulates transcription factors (AP-1, FOXO, p53) that reprogram nuclear gene expression toward antioxidant defense and mitochondrial biogenesis. JNK also balances life/death decisions: transient activation promotes adaptation and [[Autophagy]], whereas sustained activation drives apoptosis via Bim and mitochondrial permeabilization.

> [!info] Longevity duality
> Like many stress kinases, JNK exerts dose-dependent effects — mild activation supports stress resistance and lifespan, while chronic activation (as in obesity/insulin resistance) fuels inflammation and degeneration.

In muscle, JNK intersects with urolithin A–mediated mitophagy pathways, and in cancer it is co-opted to evade apoptosis, illustrating its context-dependent biology.
