# Rasburicase

Rasburicase is a recombinant, PEGylated form of *Aspergillus flavus* urate oxidase used to manage and prevent hyperuricemia and tumor-lysis syndrome, particularly in hematologic malignancies undergoing chemotherapy. It catalyzes the oxidation of poorly soluble uric acid to the far more soluble and readily excreted allantoin, rapidly lowering plasma urate and protecting renal function.

A recognized adverse effect is acquired [[Methemoglobinemia]]. The mechanism is enzymatic generation of hydrogen peroxide as a reaction byproduct; in erythrocytes this oxidizes [[Hemoglobin]]'s ferrous (Fe²⁺) heme to ferric (Fe³⁺) [[Methemoglobin]]. The risk is amplified in patients with [[G6PD deficiency]], who cannot adequately regenerate reduced glutathione to detoxify peroxides, leading to both methemoglobinemia and [[Hemolysis]]. For this reason rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD-deficient individuals, and alternative agents (e.g., febuxostat, allopurinol) are preferred.

The redox theme connects rasburicase to the vault's aminochrome biology: the same peroxide-mediated oxidative stress that converts hemoglobin to methemoglobin also drives catecholamine autoxidation, turning [[Epinephrine]] into [[Adrenochrome]] under oxidative challenge. Rasburicase therefore exemplifies how an exogenous enzyme can impose a transient oxidative load on the erythrocyte, a microcosm of the heme-versus-catechol redox couplings central to adrenochrome research.

> [!warning] G6PD contraindication
> In G6PD deficiency, rasburicase-induced peroxide can cause severe methemoglobinemia and hemolysis.
