# MAO (Monoamine oxidase)

**MAO** (Monoamine oxidase) is a family of enzymes (MAO-A and MAO-B) that catalyze the oxidative deamination of monoamines, including [[Catecholamines]] like [[notes/_link/Dopamine]] and [[notes/_link/Epinephrine]].

## Function
MAO removes the amine group from its substrates, producing an aldehyde and [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] (H₂O₂). It is located on the outer mitochondrial membrane.

## Role in [[Adrenochrome]] Pathway
Inhibition of MAO (by MAOIs) prevents the primary breakdown of [[notes/_link/Epinephrine]]. When both MAO and [[COMT]] are inhibited, catecholamine levels rise significantly. This excess substrate is more likely to undergo non-enzymatic [[Oxidation]] to form [[Adrenochrome]], especially in oxidative environments such as those caused by [[Smoking]] or [[notes/_link/Inflammation]].

## Clinical Relevance
While MAOIs are used for depression and Parkinson's disease, the increased levels of catecholamines they produce can increase the risk of [[Cardiotoxicity]] and neurotoxicity if they are shifted toward the [[Aminochromes]] pathway.

## Connections

- [[notes/_link/Epinephrine]] ↔ Metabolized by MAO.
- [[COMT]] ↔ The other major enzymatic pathway for catecholamine degradation.
- [[notes/_link/Hydrogen Peroxide]] ↔ A byproduct of normal MAO activity.
