# MAO (Monoamine oxidase)

**MAO** (Monoamine oxidase) is a family of enzymes (MAO-A and MAO-B) that catalyze the oxidative deamination of monoamines, including [[Catecholamines]] like [[Dopamine]] and [[Epinephrine]].

## Function
MAO removes the amine group from its substrates, producing an aldehyde and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] (H₂O₂). It is located on the outer mitochondrial membrane.

## Role in [[Adrenochrome]] Pathway
Inhibition of MAO (by MAOIs) prevents the primary breakdown of [[Epinephrine]]. When both MAO and [[COMT]] are inhibited, catecholamine levels rise significantly. This excess substrate is more likely to undergo non-enzymatic [[Oxidation]] to form [[Adrenochrome]], especially in oxidative environments such as those caused by [[Smoking]] or [[Inflammation]].

## Clinical Relevance
While MAOIs are used for depression and Parkinson's disease, the increased levels of catecholamines they produce can increase the risk of [[Cardiotoxicity]] and neurotoxicity if they are shifted toward the [[Aminochromes]] pathway.

## Isoforms and Tissue Distribution

MAO exists as two genetically distinct isoforms encoded by separate genes (MAOA and MAOB) located on the X chromosome. [[MAO-A]] has high affinity for serotonin, norepinephrine, and epinephrine and is predominantly expressed in brain, liver, gastrointestinal tract, and placenta. [[MAO-B]] preferentially metabolizes phenylethylamine, benzylamine, and dopamine and is enriched in brain (especially glial cells) and platelets. Both isoforms are localized to the [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] outer membrane via a C-terminal transmembrane domain.

## Catalytic Mechanism

MAO catalysis proceeds via [[FAD]]-mediated oxidative deamination. The enzyme-bound FAD accepts two electrons from the substrate amine, generating an imine intermediate that is hydrolyzed non-enzymatically to the corresponding aldehyde and ammonia. The reduced FADH₂ is reoxidized by molecular oxygen, producing [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] as a stoichiometric by-product. This obligate H₂O₂ generation links MAO activity directly to mitochondrial oxidative stress and redox signaling.

## Inhibitors

[[MAO inhibitors]] (MAOIs) are classified as reversible or irreversible and as selective or non-selective. [[Selegiline]] (deprenyl) is an irreversible MAO-B inhibitor used in [[Parkinson's Disease]]; [[Moclobemide]] is a reversible MAO-A inhibitor used for depression. [[Phenelzine]] and [[Tranylcypromine]] are non-selective irreversible inhibitors. MAO-A inhibition in the gut is responsible for the "cheese effect" (hypertensive crisis upon tyramine ingestion), whereas MAO-B inhibition is better tolerated due to platelet localization.

## Role in Catecholamine Metabolism vs. Oxidative Cyclization

Under normal physiology, MAO and [[COMT]] cooperatively degrade [[Epinephrine]] to [[Metanephrine]], [[Vanillylmandelic acid]], and other inert metabolites. When MAO is inhibited, catecholamine levels rise and the substrate flux shifts toward non-enzymatic [[Oxidation]] pathways, including the cyclization of epinephrine to [[Adrenochrome]]. This redirecting of catecholamine metabolism toward aminochrome formation has implications for [[Cardiotoxicity]] and neurotoxicity, particularly under conditions of [[Oxidative Stress]] or [[Inflammation]].
