# Coronary artery constriction

**Coronary artery constriction** is the narrowing of the coronary arteries, reducing myocardial blood flow and precipitating oxygen supply-demand mismatch and [[Ischemia]]. Synonyms include **coronary vasospasm** (acute smooth muscle contraction) and **coronary vasoconstriction** (neurohumoral or paracrine-driven). The spectrum ranges from transient, reversible vasospasm to fixed, chronic narrowing from structural disease.

## Overview

The coronary arteries supply the [[Myocardium]] with oxygen and nutrients. Constriction reduces luminal diameter, increases resistance, and when severe causes oxygen demand to exceed supply. Resulting [[Ischemia]] manifests as angina, myocardial dysfunction, arrhythmias, or infarction. Severity is modulated by vascular smooth muscle tone, endothelial integrity, and circulating vasoactive substances.

## Mechanisms

Coronary constriction arises from vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) contraction driven by increased intracellular [[Calcium Ions]] (Ca²⁺):

- **VSMC contraction**: G-protein-coupled receptor activation (endothelin-1, α-adrenergic, thromboxane A₂ receptors) stimulates phospholipase C, generating IP₃ (Ca²⁺ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum) and DAG (PKC activation). L-type voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels further elevate cytosolic Ca²⁺.
- **[[Endothelial cells|Endothelial dysfunction]]**: Impaired [[Nitric Oxide]] (NO) bioavailability — from oxidative degradation, eNOS uncoupling, or reduced expression — shifts the balance toward vasoconstriction and promotes endothelin-1 release.
- **[[Catecholamines|Catecholamine excess]]**: [[Epinephrine]] and [[Norepinephrine]] activate α₁-adrenergic receptors on coronary VSMCs, with β₂-mediated vasodilation overwhelmed during stress.
- **Autonomic activation**: Sympathetic overactivity during stress, cold, or exercise triggers reflex vasoconstriction, especially with endothelial dysfunction.
- **[[Prostaglandins]] and thromboxane A₂**: Vasoconstrictor prostanoids from activated platelets or inflamed endothelium amplify VSMC contraction.

## Causes

Causes include:

- **[[Atherosclerosis]] (chronic, fixed narrowing)**: Plaques of lipid-laden foam cells, smooth muscle, collagen, and calcium progressively narrow the lumen. Plaque erosion or rupture superimposes acute thrombosis.
- **Coronary vasospasm (acute, dynamic narrowing)**: Triggered by:
  - [[Smoking]] — increases oxidative stress, impairs endothelial function, enhances α-adrenergic sensitivity.
  - [[Cocaine]] — inhibits norepinephrine reuptake and directly stimulates VSMC contraction.
  - [[Stress]] and cold exposure — sympathetic α-adrenergic vasoconstriction.
  - [[Hyperventilation]] — respiratory alkalosis triggers spasm in susceptible individuals.
- **[[Endothelial dysfunction]]**: Loss of NO bioavailability, increased endothelin-1, and elevated NADPH oxidase activity create a pro-constrictive phenotype.
- **[[Catecholamines|Catecholamine excess]]**: Pheochromocytoma, exogenous catecholamines, or extreme stress directly constrict coronary arteries.

## Clinical relevance

Coronary artery constriction underlies several cardiac syndromes:

- **[[Variant angina]] (Prinzmetal angina)**: Episodic rest pain from focal coronary spasm, typically without obstructive atherosclerosis; responds to [[Calcium channel blockers]] and nitrates.
- **[[Acute coronary syndrome]] (ACS)**: Dynamic vasoconstriction at a vulnerable plaque exacerbates luminal narrowing, contributing to [[Unstable angina]] and [[Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction]].
- **[[Myocardial infarction]]**: Sustained severe vasoconstriction, especially superimposed on plaque, can precipitate [[ST-elevation myocardial infarction]] (STEMI).
- **[[Arrhythmias]]**: Ischemia alters myocardial electrophysiology, increasing ventricular tachycardia and fibrillation risk.
- **[[Sudden cardiac death]]**: Coronary spasm is implicated in a subset of cases, particularly in young adults without significant atherosclerosis.

## Adrenochrome relevance

[[Adrenochrome]], the oxidation product of [[Epinephrine]], causes coronary artery constriction in animal and ex vivo models through mechanisms distinct from classical α-adrenoceptor activation. In isolated perfused rat hearts and coronary artery ring preparations, adrenochrome produces dose-dependent vasoconstriction not blocked by α-adrenergic antagonists (e.g., phentolamine) but attenuated by [[Cyclooxygenase]] inhibitors (e.g., indomethacin), suggesting [[Prostaglandin]] involvement. The constrictor response is also reduced by [[Calcium channel blockers]] such as [[Verapamil]], implicating calcium flux across VSMC membranes.

This adrenochrome-induced vasoconstriction may contribute to [[Cardiotoxicity]] in high-catecholamine states — including [[Myocardial infarction]], [[Pheochromocytoma]], and [[Stress cardiomyopathy]]. By constricting coronary arteries, adrenochrome could amplify ischemia-reperfusion injury, impair oxygen delivery, and exacerbate [[Oxidative Stress]]. Its pro-oxidant redox cycling further depletes [[Glutathione]], rendering the [[Myocardium]] more vulnerable to ischemic damage.

The clinical significance in humans remains incompletely defined, limited by challenges in measuring local adrenochrome concentrations in the coronary circulation. However, [[Myeloperoxidase]] — which catalyzes adrenochrome formation — is present at sites of coronary inflammation, and adrenochrome has been detected in ischemic cardiac tissue, supporting its role as a locally generated vasoconstrictor and cardiotoxic metabolite.

## Connections

- [[Myocardial infarction]] — sustained coronary constriction can trigger infarction.
- [[Adrenochrome]] — adrenochrome directly constricts coronary arteries via non-adrenergic mechanisms.
- [[Endothelial cells]] — endothelial dysfunction is a key driver of abnormal coronary vasoreactivity.
