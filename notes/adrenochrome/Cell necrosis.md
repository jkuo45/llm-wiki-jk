# Cell necrosis

Cell [[necrosis]] is a form of cell injury that results in the premature death of cells in living tissue by [[autolysis]]. Unlike [[Apoptosis]], which is a tightly regulated, energy-dependent program of cellular suicide, necrosis is traditionally viewed as an unregulated, passive process triggered by overwhelming environmental stress. However, specific forms of regulated necrosis (e.g., [[Necroptosis]]) blur this distinction.

## Overview

### Necrosis versus Apoptosis

| Feature | Necrosis | Apoptosis |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Severe injury, hypoxia, toxins | Physiological signals, mild stress |
| Energy requirement | ATP-depleted | ATP-dependent |
| Cell size | Swelling (oncosis) | Shrinkage |
| Membrane integrity | Early rupture | Preserved (blebbing) |
| DNA fragmentation | Random, smeared | Inter-nucleosomal (ladder) |
| Inflammation | Prominent (pro-inflammatory) | Minimal (anti-inflammatory) |
| Fate | Phagocytosis by scavengers | Apoptotic body clearance |

### Oncosis and Autolysis

**Oncosis** (from Greek *onkos* = swelling) describes the pre-lethal morphological phase preceding necrotic cell death, characterized by cellular swelling, organelle edema, and bleb formation due to failure of ion pumps. **Autolysis** refers to the post-mortem degradation of cells by their own hydrolytic enzymes released from lysosomes, accelerating after membrane rupture. While oncosis is the *process leading to* necrotic cell death, autolysis is the *post-mortem consequence*.

### Morphological Features

The hallmark features of necrosis include:
- **Cell swelling** — Loss of ionic homeostasis causes water influx and cytoplasmic vacuolation.
- **Membrane rupture** — Loss of plasma membrane integrity releases intracellular contents.
- **Organelle breakdown** — Mitochondrial swelling, lysosomal leakage, and nuclear pyknosis → karyorrhexis → karyolysis.
- **Release of [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns]] (DAMPs)** — Cytosolic and nuclear components spill into the extracellular space, triggering [[Inflammation]].

## Types of Necrosis

| Type | Tissue | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Coagulative necrosis** | Heart, kidney, liver (ischemia) | Denatured proteins preserve tissue architecture; eosinophilic cytoplasm; nuclear loss |
| **Liquefactive necrosis** | Brain, abscesses | Enzymatic digestion liquefies tissue; pus formation; cystic cavitation |
| **Caseous necrosis** | [[Mycobacterium tuberculosis]] | Soft, friable, cheese-like; granuloma formation; surrounded by epithelioid macrophages |
| **Fat necrosis** | Pancreas, breast, omentum | Lipases hydrolyze triglycerides → free fatty acids + calcium → saponification (chalky white deposits) |
| **Gangrenous necrosis** | Limbs, GI tract | Coagulative + liquefactive necrosis; secondary bacterial infection; dry (ischemic) or wet (infected) |
| **Fibrinoid necrosis** | Arteries ([[Hypertension]], vasculitis) | Deposition of fibrin-like material in vessel walls; immune complex-mediated damage |

## Mechanisms

### ATP Depletion and Ionic Collapse

[[Ischemia]] or mitochondrial poisons deplete [[Adenosine Triphosphate]] (ATP), disabling the [[Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase]] pump. Intracellular sodium and water accumulate, causing cell swelling (oncosis). [[Calcium]] ions enter via reverse-mode [[Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange]] and voltage-gated channels, precipitating widespread [[Ca²⁺ overload]].

### Ca²⁺ Overload and Calpain Activation

Cytosolic [[Calcium]] overload activates [[Calpains]] — Ca²⁺-dependent cysteine proteases that cleave [[Cytoskeleton]] components (spectrin, talin), plasma membrane proteins, and [[Bid]] (linking to [[Apoptosis]] crosstalk). Prolonged calpain activity leads to membrane blebbing and loss of integrity.

### Reactive Oxygen Species and mPTP Opening

[[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS), generated from leaky mitochondria, [[NAD(P)H Oxidase]], and [[Xanthine Oxidase]], attack membrane phospholipids, proteins, and DNA. Mitochondrial permeability transition pore ([[mPTP]]) opening collapses the mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨₘ), halting ATP synthesis and releasing [[Cytochrome c]] and other pro-death factors.

### Lysosomal Membrane Permeabilization

Lysosomal membrane permeabilization ([[LMP]]) releases hydrolytic [[Cathepsins]] (B, D, L) into the cytosol. [[Cathepsin B]] and [[Cathepsin D]] amplify the death signal by cleaving [[Bid]] to tBid, activating [[Caspase]] cascades, and directly degrading cellular structures. LMP is a critical point of no return in necrotic cell death.

## Pathophysiology: DAMP Release and Sterile Inflammation

Necrotic cell rupture releases [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns]] (DAMPs) that activate innate immunity:
- **[[HMGB1]]** — Nuclear protein; when released, binds [[TLR2]]/[[TLR4]] and [[RAGE]], promoting NF-κB-dependent [[Inflammation]].
- **[[ATP]]** — Extracellular ATP acts on [[P2X7 Receptor]] to trigger [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] assembly and [[IL-1β]] secretion.
- **[[Uric Acid]]** — Released purine metabolite; activates [[NLRP3]] as a danger signal.
- **[[DNA]]** — Self-DNA engages [[TLR9]] (endosomal) and [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] (cytosolic), driving type I [[Interferon]] responses.
- **Other DAMPs** — [[S100 proteins]], [[Heat Shock Proteins]], [[Calreticulin]], [[Histones]].

This cascade defines **sterile inflammation** — inflammation triggered by cell stress and death in the absence of microbial infection — which underlies pathologies such as [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], [[Myocardial infarction]], and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]].

## Adrenochrome Relevance

[[Adrenochrome]], the oxidative metabolite of [[Epinephrine]], can directly induce necrosis in [[Cardiomyocytes]] through several converging mechanisms:
- **[[Oxidative Stress]]** — Adrenochrome undergoes [[Redox Cycling]], generating [[Superoxide anion]] and depleting intracellular [[Glutathione]].
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction** — Adrenochrome inhibits [[Complex I]] of the [[Electron Transport Chain]], collapses ΔΨₘ, and promotes [[mPTP]] opening.
- **[[Ca²⁺ Overload]]** — Adrenochrome impairs [[SERCA]] and [[Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger]] function, elevating diastolic calcium.
- **[[Cardiotoxicity]]** — Chronic adrenochrome exposure in catecholamine-rich environments (e.g., [[Pheochromocytoma]], [[Septic Shock]], [[Myocardial infarction]]) contributes to the transition from reversible injury to frank [[Cardiomyocyte]] necrosis.

The link between adrenochrome-driven necrosis and [[Adrenochrome Hypothesis]] of neuropsychiatric disorders remains speculative but is supported by observations that necrotic cell debris and DAMP release within the brain parenchyma could perpetuate [[Neuroinflammation]].
