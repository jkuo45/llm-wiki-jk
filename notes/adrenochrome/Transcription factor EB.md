# Transcription factor EB

Transcription factor EB (TFEB) is a basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-Zip) transcription factor belonging to the [[MITF]]/[[TFE3]] family. It functions as a master regulator of [[Lysosome|lysosomal]] biogenesis and [[Autophagy]], governing the expression of genes required for lysosomal function, autophagic flux, and cellular clearance. TFEB is encoded by the *TFEB* gene on chromosome 6p21 and drives the [[CLEAR Element|Coordinated Lysosomal Expression and Regulation (CLEAR)]] gene network, a coordinated transcriptional program that controls the expression of lysosomal hydrolases, membrane proteins, and autophagy components.

TFEB was initially identified as a fusion partner in a subset of renal cell carcinomas and was later recognized as the central transcriptional node coupling lysosomal function to nutrient sensing. The protein contains an N-terminal transactivation domain, a bHLH-Zip DNA-binding domain, a nuclear localization signal, and a C-terminal nuclear export signal. This modular architecture enables TFEB to rapidly shuttle between compartments in response to cellular signals.

## Mechanism

TFEB subcellular localization is the primary determinant of its activity. Under nutrient-rich conditions, [[mTORC1]] phosphorylates TFEB at Ser142 and Ser211, creating a docking site for [[14-3-3]] proteins, which retain TFEB in the [[Cytoplasm|cytoplasm]]. This phosphorylation-dependent cytoplasmic tethering is the dominant mechanism suppressing TFEB activity during growth. In parallel, [[ERK2]] can phosphorylate TFEB at Ser142, providing an additional layer of regulation downstream of growth factor signaling.

During starvation, lysosomal stress, or mTORC1 inhibition, the calcium-dependent phosphatase [[Calcineurin]] is activated via lysosomal calcium release through [[MCOLN1]] (mucolipin 1). Calcineurin dephosphorylates TFEB at the critical serine residues, releasing 14-3-3 binding and exposing the nuclear localization signal. Dephosphorylated TFEB rapidly translocates to the [[Nucleus|nucleus]], where it homodimerizes and binds [[CLEAR Element|CLEAR elements]] — GTCACGTGAC consensus motifs present in the promoters of over 50 lysosomal and autophagic genes. A negative feedback loop operates through TFEB-mediated transcriptional upregulation of [[MCOLN1]], which sustains the calcineurin signal, and of [[RRAGC]]/[[RRAGD]], which promotes mTORC1 reactivation once nutrients become available.

## Target Genes

TFEB transcriptionally upregulates a broad network of genes organized into functional modules:

- **Lysosomal enzymes**: [[Cathepsin B]], [[Cathepsin D]], [[Cathepsins|cathepsins]], acid hydrolases, [[LAMP1]], ATP6V1H (vacuolar ATPase subunit), hexosaminidases, [[β-glucuronidase]], [[α-galactosidase]], and [[α-mannosidase]].
- **Autophagy machinery**: [[ATG genes]] (including [[ATG5]], [[ATG7]], [[ATG9B]], [[ATG14]]), [[SQSTM1]]/[[p62]], [[MAP1LC3B]] (LC3B), [[ULK1]], [[Beclin 1]], [[GABARAP]], and [[GABARAPL1]].
- **Lysosomal membrane proteins**: [[LIMP2]] ([[SCARB2]]), [[LAPTM4A]], [[LAPTM4B]], and [[NPC1]].
- **Other targets**: [[PPT1]] (palmitoyl-protein thioesterase 1), [[CLN3]], sulfatases, and lysosomal acid lipase ([[LIPA]]) involved in lipid degradation.

The breadth of this transcriptional program — spanning acidification, substrate degradation, membrane trafficking, and autophagosome formation — establishes TFEB as a single-node master switch for the lysosomal-autophagic axis.

## Physiological Roles

TFEB orchestrates lysosome biogenesis by co-activating over 50 lysosomal genes in a coordinated transcriptional program that includes nearly every known lysosomal hydrolase and membrane protein. It is a potent inducer of autophagic flux, promoting both autophagosome formation and lysosome-dependent degradation. Beyond lysosomal function, TFEB coactivates [[PPARγ]] to drive [[Lipid metabolism|lipid catabolism]] and facilitates clearance of protein aggregates, including [[Huntingtin|mutant huntingtin]] and [[α-synuclein]].

TFEB participates in [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial quality control]] by removing dysfunctional mitochondria via mitophagy and by transcriptionally regulating [[PGC1α]], linking lysosomal biogenesis to mitochondrial homeostasis. In the [[Immune system|immune system]], TFEB supports [[Innate immunity|innate immunity]] by controlling lysosomal function in [[Macrophages|macrophages]] and [[Microglia|microglia]], where it regulates cytokine production, antigen presentation, and pathogen clearance. TFEB also mediates exercise-induced metabolic adaptations through the [[PGC1α]]-[[PPARδ]]-TFEB signaling axis, promoting lipid oxidation and enhancing oxidative metabolism in [[Skeletal muscle|skeletal muscle]].

## Pathology

Dysregulation of TFEB localization or expression is implicated in multiple diseases:

- **Alzheimer's Disease**: TFEB nuclear translocation is impaired in Alzheimer's neurons, reducing lysosomal clearance of [[Aβ|amyloid-β]] and contributing to proteotoxicity and tau pathology. Reduced TFEB activity correlates with progressive lysosomal dysfunction in affected brain regions.
- **Parkinson's Disease**: TFEB-dependent transcription facilitates [[α-synuclein]] clearance via autophagy and lysosomal pathways. TFEB overexpression mitigates α-synuclein pathology in cellular and animal models, and TFEB haploinsufficiency exacerbates α-synuclein accumulation.
- **Gaucher Disease**: TFEB activation enhances clearance of accumulated [[Glucocerebroside|glucocerebroside]] in Gaucher disease models. Mutations in [[GBA1]] impair lysosomal function and TFEB signaling, creating a feed-forward loop of lysosomal dysfunction.
- **Pompe Disease**: In Pompe disease ([[GAA]] deficiency), TFEB overexpression rescues the autophagic block characteristic of the disorder, reducing glycogen accumulation and restoring lysosomal function.
- **Metabolic disorders**: The PGC1α-TFEB axis mediates exercise-induced metabolic adaptations; TFEB activation promotes [[Fatty acid oxidation|fatty acid oxidation]] and [[Mitochondrial biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] in liver and muscle. TFEB also regulates hepatic lipid metabolism, and its dysregulation contributes to [[Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease|NAFLD]].
- **Cancer**: TFEB gene fusions (e.g., [[TFEB]]-[[MALAT1]]) define a subset of [[Renal cell carcinoma|renal cell carcinomas]] with distinct histology. TFEB overexpression can also promote tumor growth through enhanced lysosomal exocytosis and nutrient scavenging.
- **Therapeutic strategies**: Small-molecule TFEB activators — including the [[Curcumin]] analog C1, [[Trehalose]], [[2-Hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin]], and [[Torin 1]] — enhance lysosomal function and autophagic clearance. These compounds are under investigation for lysosomal storage disorders, neurodegenerative diseases, and metabolic syndrome.
