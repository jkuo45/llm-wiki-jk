# Actinonin

**Actinonin** is a natural hydroxamate-containing bacterial secondary metabolite initially isolated from *Streptomyces* spp. and best known as a potent inhibitor of peptide deformylase, the enzyme that removes the N-terminal formyl group from nascent polypeptides in bacteria and mitochondria. Because mitochondrial ribosomes synthesize a small but essential set of inner-membrane proteins (e.g., subunits of complexes I, III, IV, and V), actinonin blocks [[Mitochondria]]l translation, depleting respiratory-chain components and lowering oxidative phosphorylation capacity.

This translational blockade activates the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPRmt): the accumulation of unassembled respiratory subunits is sensed as proteotoxic stress, triggering a retrograde signaling cascade (involving ATFS-1/ATF5, DVE-1, and UBL-5 in nematodes) that induces mitochondrial chaperones and proteases and reprograms nuclear gene expression. In mammalian cells, actinonin similarly engages the integrated stress response via eIF2α phosphorylation and CHOP/ATF4 signaling.

> [!tip] Longevity and cancer relevance
> By mimicking mitochondrial proteotoxic stress, actinonin phenocopies dietary and genetic mitohormetic interventions linked to extended [[Lifespan]]. Its selectivity for mitochondrial over cytosolic translation also makes it a candidate anti-cancer agent, as many tumors are hypersensitive to mitochondrial翻译 stress.

Actinonin is structurally related to the matrix metalloproteinase inhibitor marimastat and has been investigated for both antimicrobial and antineoplastic activity, though clinical development has been limited by off-target effects.
