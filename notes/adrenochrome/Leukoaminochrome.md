# Leukoaminochrome

[[Leukoaminochrome]] is the reduced, colorless (leuco) form of an aminochrome—most notably the leuco derivative of [[Adrenochrome]] (often termed [[Leuco-adrenochrome]]). It is generated when the aminochrome quinone is two-electron reduced, closing the redox cycle that defines the [[Aminochromes]] family. In its cyclized indoline form it is a relatively stable intermediate that can either be re-oxidized back to the aminochrome or divert into polymerization toward [[Melanins]] and [[Neuromelanin]].

## Redox Cycling and Toxicity

The leukoaminochrome ↔ aminochrome couple is central to the [[Redox Cycling]] of adrenochrome. One-electron oxidation of leukoaminochrome produces a semiquinone radical that reduces molecular oxygen to [[Superoxide anion]], perpetuating a self-amplifying cycle of [[Oxidative Stress]]. Two-electron reduction by [[DT-diaphorase]] ([[NQO1]]) or [[Glutathione]]-dependent systems, by contrast, safely channels the leuco form away from semiquinone formation.

> [!warning]
> Leukoaminochrome can undergo metal-catalyzed autoxidation, regenerating the parent aminochrome while spawning reactive oxygen species—a key mechanism by which the adrenochrome pathway inflicts mitochondrial and protein damage.

## Role in Pigment Formation

In catecholaminergic neurons, leukoaminochrome intermediates condense and oxidize into the polymeric pigment [[Neuromelanin]], whose accumulation in the [[Substantia Nigra]] is a hallmark of aging and a modifier of vulnerability in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. The same leuco/aminochrome equilibrium governs formation of adrenochrome-derived pigments elsewhere in the body.
