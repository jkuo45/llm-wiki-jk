# Dapsone

[[Dapsone]] (4,4'-diaminodiphenyl sulfone) is a **sulfone** antibiotic and anti-inflammatory agent used to treat leprosy, *Pneumocystis* pneumonia prophylaxis, dermatitis herpetiformis, and various neutrophil-mediated dermatoses.

## Chemical Properties & Mechanism of Action

- **Antimicrobial:** inhibits bacterial dihydropteroate synthase, blocking folate synthesis (like sulfonamides).
- **Anti-inflammatory:** suppresses [[Neutrophils|neutrophil]] myeloperoxidase-dependent respiratory burst and chemotaxis, underlying its use in inflammatory skin disease.
- **Metabolism:** oxidized by hepatic [[CYP2C9]]/[[CYP3A4]] to **dapsone hydroxylamine**, the metabolite responsible for its hematologic toxicity.

## Toxicology & Redox Relevance

> [!warning] Methemoglobinemia and hemolysis
> Dapsone hydroxylamine is a potent oxidant that converts [[Hemoglobin]] iron to the ferric state, producing [[Methemoglobin]] and dose-dependent [[Methemoglobinemia]] — dapsone is one of the most common causes of drug-induced methemoglobinemia, especially with chronic use. It also drives oxidative hemolysis, markedly worse in [[Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase]] deficiency. Severe methemoglobinemia is treated with [[Methylene blue]], which restores the [[NADH-dependent methemoglobin reductase]] pathway.

This oxidant, [[Reactive Oxygen Species]]-generating chemistry links dapsone to the broader **redox and autoxidation** themes of the [[Adrenochrome]] topic.
