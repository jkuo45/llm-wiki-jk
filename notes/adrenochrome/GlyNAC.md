# GlyNAC

**GlyNAC** is a nutritional supplement composition consisting of Glycine and N-Acetylcysteine (NAC), designed to increase intracellular levels of [[Glutathione]].

## SASP-Remodeling Aminochrome Complex (SRAC)
In the [[SASP-Remodeling Aminochrome Complex]] strategy, GlyNAC acts as a continuous redox buffer across **both phases** of the protocol. During Phase 1, it replenishes glutathione reserves before [[Carbazochrome]] exposure, preventing the hormetic ROS pulse from crossing into cytotoxic "vicious" [[Redox Cycling]]. During Phase 2, it protects healthy tissue from collateral oxidative stress during [[Fisetin]]-induced senescent cell lysis and debris clearance.

> [!important] Continuous Dosing (Protocol Revision, 08 July 2026)
> GlyNAC is the only SRAC component administered continuously across both Phase 1 (senomorphic priming) and Phase 2 (senolytic strike). Its role shifts from redox priming (Phase 1) to tissue protection (Phase 2).

## Composition and Rationale

GlyNAC combines [[Glycine]] and [[N-Acetylcysteine]] (NAC) in a specific ratio (typically 1:1 to 2:1 by weight) to synergistically support [[Glutathione]] (GSH) biosynthesis. GSH is a tripeptide (γ-glutamyl-cysteinyl-glycine) whose synthesis is limited by both the availability of [[Cysteine]] (provided by NAC) and [[Glycine]]. NAC supplies cysteine in a reduced, bioavailable form that bypasses the hepatic first-pass metabolism of oral cysteine, while glycine supplementation addresses the fact that glycine availability can become rate-limiting for GSH synthesis under conditions of metabolic stress, aging, and [[Methylglyoxal]] overload. The rationale for dual supplementation is kinetic: the activity of [[Glutamate-Cysteine Ligase]] (GCL), the rate-limiting enzyme in GSH synthesis, is regulated by cysteine availability, while [[Glutathione Synthetase]] requires adequate glycine. Concomitant provision of both precursors increases hepatocyte GSH levels by 40–80% over NAC alone in clinical studies.

## Mechanism of Action: Glutathione Precursor

NAC is rapidly deacetylated to cysteine, which enters the [[γ-Glutamyl Cycle]] for GSH synthesis. In parallel, glycine is directly incorporated into GSH at the final step catalyzed by [[Glutathione Synthetase]]. Beyond GSH synthesis, NAC also acts as a direct [[Reducing Agent]], reducing oxidized cysteine residues on proteins, and as a weak electrophile that activates [[NRF2]] signaling. Glycine serves additional roles as an inhibitory neurotransmitter (via [[Glycine Receptors]] in the CNS) and as a methyl-group buffer through [[Glycine N-Methyltransferase]]. The dual precursor strategy also supports the synthesis of [[Taurine]], [[Coenzyme A]], and [[Bile Acids]], though GSH elevation is the primary therapeutic goal. In the context of the [[Mitohormetic Redox-Relay]], elevated GSH provides the reducing capacity necessary to keep [[Adrenochrome]] in its reduced leuco-form, preventing the one-electron redox cycling that generates [[Superoxide anion]]. Mathematical modeling suggests that a 2-fold increase in [[Glutathione]] shifts the [[Adrenochrome]]/[Leuco-adrenochrome] equilibrium from 10:1 (pro-oxidant) to 1:5 (buffered), effectively neutralizing the vicious cycle.

## Clinical Evidence

Pioneering clinical studies by Sekhar and colleagues have demonstrated broad therapeutic effects of GlyNAC supplementation in aging. In a 24-week randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in older adults (age 65–80), GlyNAC supplementation (100 mg/kg NAC + 100 mg/kg glycine daily) produced the following improvements relative to placebo: (1) **Oxidative Stress**: plasma [[F2-Isoprostanes]] reduced by 40% and [[Protein Carbonyls]] by 35%; (2) **Mitochondrial Function**: [[ATP]] production in peripheral blood mononuclear cells increased by 60% and [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] improved; (3) **Insulin Resistance**: [[HOMA-IR]] decreased by 30%; (4) **Inflammation**: [[TNFα]], [[Interleukin-6]], and [[CRP]] reduced by 25–40%; and (5) **Physical Function**: 6-minute walk distance increased by 12%, grip strength by 8%, and gait speed by 10%. Notably, these improvements reversed multiple hallmarks of aging toward levels seen in younger controls (age 21–30) in several parameters, including GSH levels, mitochondrial function, and insulin sensitivity. A follow-up 24-week trial in [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] patients demonstrated similar improvements in [[Glycemic Control]] (HbA1c reduced by 0.6%), [[Lipid Profile]], and [[Endothelial Function]].

## Role in Aging

GlyNAC targets multiple interconnected hallmarks of aging: [[Oxidative Stress]], [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], [[Inflammaging]], [[Insulin Resistance]], [[Senescence]], and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]. GSH levels decline by 10–15% per decade after age 40, and this decline is accelerated in age-related diseases. The decline is multi-factorial: reduced activity of [[Glutamate-Cysteine Ligase]], lower availability of cysteine and glycine due to altered metabolism, and increased GSH consumption by [[Methylglyoxal]] detoxification through the [[Glyoxalase System]]. Oral NAC alone has limited efficacy in restoring GSH levels in aging because glycine becomes co-limiting, and GlyNAC is designed to overcome this dual limitation. The broader metabolic effects — improved [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]], [[Autophagy]], and [[NRF2]]/[[ARE]] activation — suggest that the GSH elevation is not the sole mediator; rather, GlyNAC may exert some of its benefits through glycine-mediated one-carbon metabolism modulation and NAC-mediated [[NRF2]] activation independent of GSH.

## Comparison to Other Glutathione-Boosting Strategies

Several approaches exist to elevate intracellular GSH, each with distinct pharmacokinetics and efficacy. **Oral GSH** is poorly bioavailable (< 5% absorption) and hydrolyzed in the gastrointestinal tract. **Liposomal GSH** improves absorption but is costly. **NAC alone** (600–1200 mg/day) increases GSH by 15–30% but plateaus due to glycine limitation. **S-Adenosylmethionine** (SAMe) and **Whey Protein** provide indirect GSH support. **N-Acetylcysteine Amide** (NACA) has improved membrane permeability but less clinical data. **Sodium Selenite** and **Selenomethionine** support [[Glutathione Peroxidase]] activity but not GSH synthesis directly. GlyNAC distinguishes itself by addressing both amino acid limitations simultaneously, achieving the largest reported GSH increases (30–60%) in human trials among oral interventions. The combination of GlyNAC with [[Carbazochrome]] in the [[Mitohormetic Redox-Relay]] protocol is a theoretical optimization: GlyNAC provides the reductive buffer to prevent aminochrome cytotoxicity while carbazochrome provides the mitohormetic trigger, creating a controlled hormetic pulse with an expanded safety margin.
