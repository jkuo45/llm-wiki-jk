# Redox Optimization

**Redox Optimization** is a conceptual operational term within the [[Adrenochrome]] handling protocol, denoting the deliberate maintenance of cellular reducing potential while processing redox-active indolequinones. The goal is to keep the cytosolic and mitochondrial compartments sufficiently reduced to neutralize the electrophilic and ROS-generating species produced during [[Adrenochrome]] autoxidation to [[Aminochrome]].

Central to optimization is the buffering capacity of [[NAD+]]/NADH and [[Glutathione]] (GSH/GSSG). Adrenochrome's semiquinone and quinone forms ([[Adrenochrome Semiquinone Radical]], [[o-quinone]]) redox-cycle, draining reducing equivalents and oxidizing GSH to GSSG. Optimization strategies therefore combine [[NAD+]] precursor supplementation (e.g., [[Nicotinamide]]) with GSH-supporting thiol donors, limiting aminochrome accumulation and protein carbonylation. Effective optimization suppresses the downstream activation of [[NF-κB]] and [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] that would otherwise drive [[Senescence]] and inflammation.

> [!tip] In protocol terms, redox optimization is not a single supplement but a dynamic target — monitored via GSH/GSSG ratio and NAD+/NADH balance — adjusted as catecholamine load and quinone flux change.
