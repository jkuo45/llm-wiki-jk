# MAVS

MAVS (Mitochondrial Antiviral Signaling protein) is an outer mitochondrial membrane protein that plays a central role in the innate immune response to viral infection.

## Function

MAVS acts as a signaling platform that coordinates the cellular response to viral RNA. When [[Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptor|Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptors]] (RLRs) detect unique RNA structures generated during viral infection, they interact with MAVS. This interaction activates MAVS to form large, prion-like aggregates on the mitochondrial surface, which then recruit signaling components including [[TRAF2]], [[TRAF6]], and [[RIPK1]] to activate an innate immune response and interferon production.

## Significance

MAVS links [[Mitochondria]] to the detection of external threats and the orchestration of an antiviral state, highlighting the role of mitochondria as internal sensors of external dangers. A similar role is seen in the activation of the [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] by mitochondrial ROS.
