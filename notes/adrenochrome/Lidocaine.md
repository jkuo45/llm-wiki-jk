# Lidocaine

**Lidocaine** is an amide-type local anesthetic and Class IB antiarrhythmic that blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, producing reversible conduction blockade in nerves and myocardium. Although generally safe at therapeutic doses, lidocaine is recognized as a cause of **acquired [[Methemoglobinemia]]**, particularly with high-dose infusion, topical mucosal application (e.g., teething or burns preparations), or in infants.

The pathogenesis is metabolic: lidocaine and its aromatic amine metabolites oxidize ferroheme (Fe²⁺) of [[Hemoglobin]] to ferriheme (Fe³⁺), yielding [[Methemoglobin]], a form unable to transport oxygen and that shifts the oxygen-dissociation curve leftward. Clinical features include cyanosis refractory to oxygen, chocolate-colored blood, and at high levels neurologic and cardiac toxicity. Definitive diagnosis is by [[Co-oximetry]]; treatment of significant cases uses [[Methylene blue]] to reductively regenerate functional hemoglobin.

> [!warning] Combined anesthetic risk
> Lidocaine shares the methemoglobinemia-prone profile with [[Benzocaine]] and [[Prilocaine]]; concurrent or sequential use of multiple topical anesthetics compounds the oxidation burden.

Recognition matters because the cyanosis can be mistaken for primary cardiopulmonary failure.
