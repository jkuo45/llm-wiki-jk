# Isobutyl nitrite

[[Isobutyl nitrite]] is a volatile **alkyl nitrite** (an ester of nitrous acid) used recreationally as an inhalant marketed as "poppers." Like the related [[Amyl nitrite]], it produces rapid smooth-muscle relaxation and vasodilation.

## Chemical Properties & Mechanism of Action

- A lipophilic, volatile liquid that releases nitric oxide and nitrite species in vivo.
- **Vasodilation:** liberated [[Nitric Oxide]] activates [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]], raising cGMP and relaxing vascular and other smooth muscle — the basis of its recreational and, historically, medicinal use.
- **Oxidant action:** nitrite oxidizes the ferrous (Fe²⁺) iron of [[Hemoglobin]] to the ferric (Fe³⁺) state, forming [[Methemoglobin]], which cannot bind or transport oxygen.

## Toxicology & Adrenochrome Relevance

> [!warning] Methemoglobinemia
> Inhalation of isobutyl nitrite can trigger severe acquired [[Methemoglobinemia]], presenting with [[Cyanosis]], hypoxia, and characteristic "chocolate-brown" blood. Severe cases are treated with [[Methylene blue]], which accelerates the [[NADH-dependent methemoglobin reductase]] ([[Diaphorase]]) system.

Nitrite-driven one-electron oxidation chemistry parallels the **redox/autoxidation** themes of the [[Adrenochrome]] pathway, where catecholamines and iron centers undergo similar oxidative transformations generating [[Reactive Oxygen Species]].
