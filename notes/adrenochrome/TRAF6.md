# TRAF6

TNF receptor-associated factor 6 (TRAF6) is a member of the TRAF family of cytoplasmic adaptor proteins with intrinsic E3 ubiquitin ligase activity. Unlike most TRAFs, TRAF6 assembles Lys63-linked and linear ubiquitin chains via its RING domain in collaboration with the Ubc13/Uev1A E2 complex. It sits at a convergence point for multiple upstream receptors: the interleukin-1/Toll-like receptor adaptor MyD88–IRAK axis, the RIG-I/MDA5–[[MAVS]] antiviral pathway (where it partners with [[TRAF2]]), CD40, RANK, and TNFR superfamily members.

The downstream consequences of TRAF6 activation are broad. K63-linked ubiquitin serves as a scaffold recruiting the TAK1 complex and the IKK and TBK1 kinases, activating [[NF-κB]] and MAP kinases (including [[c-Jun N-terminal kinase]]) and thus driving [[Inflammation]], interferon responses, and osteoclastogenesis. TRAF6-mediated ubiquitin signaling is also integrated with cellular stress and redox circuits: it modulates autophagy and mitophagy through ubiquitin tags that recruit autophagic receptors, linking innate immunity to the quality-control programs relevant to [[Mitochondria]] and [[Oxidative Stress]].

In the adrenochrome/redox frame, chronic TRAF6-driven NF-κB activation is a major source of inflammatory oxidants — activated myeloid cells produce superoxide, [[Nitric Oxide]], and downstream peroxynitrite that accelerate the autoxidation of catecholamines such as [[Epinephrine]] into [[Adrenochrome]] and related aminochromes. TRAF6 therefore represents an upstream immune–inflammatory node that, when overactive, feeds the oxidative burden implicated in aging and aminochrome toxicity.

> [!info] Unique ligase
> TRAF6 is the only TRAF with confirmed E3 ligase activity capable of building Lys63-linked chains.
