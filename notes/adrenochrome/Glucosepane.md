# Glucosepane

Glucosepane is the most abundant and chemically stable protein-protein crosslink belonging to the [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs) family in human structural tissues.

## Tissue Stiffness and Aging

Formed from glucose and lysyl/arginyl residues, glucosepane accumulation drives the systemic stiffening of structural tissues (such as [[Collagen]]) associated with [[Aging]], [[Diabetes]], and vascular complications. 

Humans lack endogenous enzymes capable of directly cleaving the glucosepane ring to restore tissue elasticity.

## Enzymatic Cleavage

Metagenomic screening has identified soil bacterial biocatalysts capable of using glucosepane as a nutrient source, including:
- [[HemF-like]] (coproporphyrinogen III oxidase-like)
- [[BluB-like]] (5,6-dimethylbenzimidazole synthase-like)
- [[QueE-like]] (7-carboxy-7-deazaguanine synthase-like)

The human homolog of [[HemF-like]] and homologous bacterial proteins in the microbiome (such as *Escherichia* and *Pseudomonas*) catalyze the cleavage of glucosepane in vitro, releasing [[Citrulline]] as a byproduct.
