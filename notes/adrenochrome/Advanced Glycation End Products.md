# Advanced Glycation End Products

Advanced Glycation End Products (AGEs) are non-enzymatic modifications of proteins or lipids that form after exposure to sugars.

## Overview

AGEs are formed through the [[Maillard Reaction]], where reducing sugars react with amino groups. This process is accelerated by [[Hyperglycemia]] and [[Oxidative Stress]]. AGEs accumulate in tissues during [[Aging]] and contribute to the pathogenesis of diabetic complications, vascular stiffness, and organ dysfunction.

## Mechanisms of Injury

1. **Extracellular Cross-linking**: AGEs form cross-links (such as the highly stable [[Glucosepane]] and [[Pentosidine]]) with structural proteins like [[Collagen]] and [[Elastic fibers]], reducing tissue elasticity.
2. **Intracellular Modification**: Glycation of intracellular proteins impairs cellular functions and antioxidant enzyme activities (e.g., [[Catalase]], [[Glutathione peroxidase]]).
3. **RAGE Activation**: Interaction with the [[RAGE]] receptor triggers inflammatory signaling and [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] generation, which is opposed by [[AGER1]].

## Enzymatic Management and Clearance

The body and researchers utilize several enzymatic defense systems to manage glycation:
- **Prevention (Dicarbonyl Detoxification)**: The [[Glyoxalase System]] (featuring [[GLO1]] and [[GLO2]]) and [[PARK7]] neutralize reactive dicarbonyl intermediates (like [[Methylglyoxal]], [[Glyoxal]], and [[3-deoxyglucosone]]) before they form stable AGEs.
- **Reversal (Protein Deglycation)**: Enzymes like [[Fructosamine-3-kinase]] (FN3K) phosphorylate early [[Amadori products]] to regenerate undamaged amino acids, while bacterial/fungal [[Fructosyl-Amino Acid Oxidases]] (FAOD) oxidize them.
- **Degradation**: Intracellular AGEs are degraded in the [[notes/_link/Lysosome]] by proteases like [[Cathepsin D]] and [[Cathepsin B]], and extracellularly by [[Matrix Metalloproteinases]]. Refractory crosslinks like [[Glucosepane]] can be cleaved in vitro by bacterial [[HemF-like]], [[BluB-like]], and [[QueE-like]] enzymes.

## Inhibitors and Breakers

- **Inhibitors**: [[Aminoguanidine]], [[Pyridoxamine]], and various [[Polyphenols]].
- **Breakers**: [[Alagebrium]] and [[Epicatechin]].
