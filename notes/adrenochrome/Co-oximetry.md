# Co-oximetry

**Co-oximetry** is a laboratory spectrophotometric method that definitively quantifies the fractions of hemoglobin species in whole blood — including oxyhemoglobin, deoxyhemoglobin, carboxyhemoglobin, and [[Methemoglobin]] — by measuring absorbance at multiple wavelengths. It is the **gold-standard diagnostic test** for [[Methemoglobinemia]].

Unlike standard pulse oximetry (which cannot distinguish methemoglobin from other species and typically reads a spurious ~85% saturation), co-oximetry directly resolves the methemoglobin percentage, enabling accurate diagnosis and dosing of therapy (e.g., [[Methylene blue]]). It also distinguishes concurrent exposures — for example, carbon-monoxide poisoning (carboxyhemoglobin) versus nitrite/anesthetic-induced methemoglobinemia — that produce overlapping clinical cyanosis.

> [!info] Why it matters in redox pathology
> Because adrenochrome and methemoglobin pathways both hinge on heme iron redox state, co-oximetry provides the objective readout of ferric-heme burden underlying the redox disturbances studied in this vault.

Specimen handling matters: samples should be analyzed promptly or kept cold/protected from air to avoid artifactual changes in oxy/methemoglobin ratios.
