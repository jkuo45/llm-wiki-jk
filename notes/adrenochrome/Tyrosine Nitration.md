# Tyrosine Nitration

[[Tyrosine Nitration]] is a post-translational modification in which a tyrosine residue is converted to 3-nitrotyrosine, most prominently by the powerful oxidant peroxynitrite (ONOO⁻) formed from [[Nitric Oxide]] and [[Superoxide anion]]. Myeloperoxidase ([[Myeloperoxidase]]) released from activated [[Neutrophils]] can also nitrate tyrosine using hydrogen peroxide and nitrite, a pathway highly relevant where inflammation and catecholamine oxidation coincide.

## Mechanisms

Peroxynitrite nitrates tyrosine either directly or via intermediate radicals (tyrosyl radical + nitrogen dioxide). MPO-dependent nitration requires nitrite as the nitrogen donor and proceeds through enzymebound oxidizing intermediates. Nitration can alter protein conformation, disrupt phosphorylation motifs, and impair enzyme activity, contributing to [[Protein Carbonylation]]-associated proteotoxicity.

> [!warning]
> Tyrosine nitration marks sites of coincident nitric oxide and superoxide generation—exactly the microenvironment where [[Myeloperoxidase]] from neutrophils oxidizes [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]] and where [[Methemoglobin]]/nitrite redox chemistry operates.

## Intersection with Adrenochrome and Methemoglobin Biochemistry

In inflammatory and ischemic states, neutrophil [[Myeloperoxidase Pathway]] activity produces both aminochromes and nitrating species. [[Methemoglobin]] can be reduced by nitrite and the resulting redox flux feeds nitrosative chemistry. Nitrated proteins accumulate in cardiovascular and neurodegenerative contexts where adrenochrome and neuromelanin oxidative pathways are active, making tyrosine nitration a useful sentinel of combined oxidative and nitrosative injury.
