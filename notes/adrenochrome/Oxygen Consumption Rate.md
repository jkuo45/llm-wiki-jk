# Oxygen Consumption Rate

Oxygen Consumption Rate (OCR) quantifies the rate at which intact cells, isolated [[Mitochondria]], or permeabilized preparations consume dissolved O₂, serving as a direct readout of oxidative phosphorylation and mitochondrial respiratory capacity. It is most often measured in real time using a [[Seahorse XF Analyzer]], which uses transient micro-chamber clamps to track O₂ flux in a 96- or 24-well format, enabling titration with modulators (oligomycin, FCCP, rotenone/antimycin A) that resolve basal respiration, ATP-linked respiration, proton-leak, maximal capacity, and spare respiratory capacity.

OCR is interpreted alongside the [[Extracellular Acidification Rate]] (ECAR, a glycolytic proxy) to place a cell on the oxidative-versus-glycolytic spectrum. In [[Mitohormesis]] and longevity studies, an elevated OCR after mild stress indicates enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis and coupling, reflecting adaptation that raises ATP output while improving resistance to ischemic and oxidative insults. Conversely, a falling OCR signals respiratory-chain failure and a shift toward glycolysis.

The adrenochrome/redox relevance is mechanistic: tight respiratory coupling minimizes electron leak and therefore [[Superoxide anion]] production at Complex I and III. Interventions that raise coupled OCR — NAD⁺ precursors, [[Sirtuins]] activation, [[Urolithins]] — reduce the oxidative drive that converts catecholamines such as [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. OCR is thus a key pharmacodynamic marker for strategies aimed at lowering aminochrome burden via improved redox homeostasis.

> [!info] Coupling matters
> A high OCR with low proton leak denotes efficient respiration and less ROS leak to drive aminochrome formation.
