# N-(carboxymethyl)-lysine

N-(carboxymethyl)-lysine (CML) is a well-characterized and major compound within the class of [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs).

## Overview

CML is formed by the oxidative degradation of [[Amadori products]] or through the reaction of glyoxal with lysine residues. It serves as a significant [[Biomarker]] for glycation stress and [[Aging]].

## Clinical Significance

- **Vessel Stiffness**: Serum concentration of CML correlates with arterial stiffness in elderly individuals.
- **Dietary Source**: CML is a prominent dietary glycotoxin found in cooked foods; high intake accelerates endothelial dysfunction.
- **Accumulation**: CML accumulates in various tissues, including the heart and kidneys, during aging.
