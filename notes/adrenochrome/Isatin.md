# Isatin

**Isatin** (indole-2,3-dione) is an endogenous indole alkaloid found in mammalian tissues and brain, structurally ancestral to the indolequinone chemistry of [[Adrenochrome]] and [[Aminochrome]]. Its five-membered dione ring is the reduced congener of the ortho-quinone species that catecholamines form upon oxidation.

Isatin is a reversible, selective [[Monoamine oxidase]] (MAO) inhibitor — particularly toward MAO-B — and a monoamine-depleting agent. By limiting MAO-driven oxidative deamination of [[Dopamine]] and [[Adrenaline]], isatin modulates catecholamine flux and the upstream substrate available for autoxidation into [[Adrenochrome]]. It also exhibits pro-oxidant and electrophilic behavior akin to indolequinones, capable of engaging thiols such as [[Glutathione]]. Neurologically, isatin has been implicated in anxiety- and depression-like behaviors (the "tribulin" hypothesis) and in seizure modulation, reflecting its monoaminergic actions.

> [!info] Isatin sits at the chemical crossroads of the adrenochrome pathway: a MAO inhibitor that shapes catecholamine availability while itself belonging to the indole-dione redox family.
