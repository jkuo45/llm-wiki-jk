# Epinephrine

[[Epinephrine]], also known as **Adrenaline**, is a hormone and neurotransmitter involved in the body's fight-or-flight response. It is produced primarily by the adrenal glands and certain neurons.

## Biochemical Role
- **Precursor to [[Adrenochrome]]:** Under conditions of [[comt/Oxidative stress]], Epinephrine undergoes [[Oxidation]] to form [[Adrenochrome]].
- **Metabolism:** Primarily metabolized via [[COMT]] (Catechol-O-methyltransferase) and [[MAO]] (Monoamine oxidase) pathways. Inhibition of these pathways can lead to increased Epinephrine levels and potentially shift metabolism toward the [[Adrenochrome]] pathway.

## Physiological Effects
- Increases heart rate, blood pressure, and blood glucose levels.
- Mediates responses to acute stress.

## Connections
- [[Adrenochrome]] ↔ Oxidative byproduct of Epinephrine.
- [[Catecholamines]] ↔ Epinephrine belongs to this class of molecules.
- [[Neutrophils]] ↔ Can oxidize Epinephrine to [[Adrenochrome]] during inflammatory responses.
