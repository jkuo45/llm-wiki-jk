# Onchocerciasis

**Onchocerciasis**, commonly called river blindness, is a neglected tropical disease caused by infection with the filarial nematode *[[Onchocerca volvulus]]*. It is transmitted to humans through the bite of infected blackflies of the genus *[[Simulium]]*, which breed in fast-flowing rivers — hence the name and the endemic clustering around riverine communities in sub-Saharan Africa, Yemen, and parts of Latin America.

## Pathophysiology

Adult worms reside in subcutaneous nodules, where females release thousands of [[Microfilariae]] that migrate through skin and eye tissues. The disease is driven largely by the host immune response to dying microfilariae: Wolbachia endos bacterial debris and parasite antigens provoke [[Inflammation]], leading to intense pruritus, depigmentation ("leopard skin"), lymphadenopathy, and, in the eye, sclerosing keratitis and chorioretinitis that culminate in irreversible blindness. Nodules and skin changes are hallmark clinical signs.

## Epidemiology and Control

Onchocerciasis historically blinded millions. Control rests on annual or semi-annual mass distribution of [[Ivermectin]], which clears microfilariae and temporarily sterilizes females, together with vector control. The [[World Health Organization]] and programs such as the OCP and APOC have slashed transmission; elimination is now a global target. Ivermectin's excellent safety record, built over decades of community use, also underpins its investigation as a repurposed anti-[[Cancer]] agent.

## Link to Cancer Research

The anthelmintic benzimidazole class (e.g., [[Oxfendazole]], [[Mebendazole]]) active against filarial worms shares [[Microtubule]]-disrupting mechanisms with anti-mitotic chemotherapy, and ivermectin's PAK1/[[Akt]] modulation is studied in tumors such as [[Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma]] and [[ovarian cancer]]. Thus onchocerciasis drug development informs oncology repurposing.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

Priorities include macrofilaricidal drugs (doxycycline anti-Wolbachia; [[Oxfendazole]]) to kill adult worms and accelerate elimination, plus diagnostics and surveillance. The disease remains a paradigm of successful global health intervention.
