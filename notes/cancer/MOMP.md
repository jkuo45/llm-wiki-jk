# MOMP

**Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP)** is the decisive, largely irreversible event of the intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway of [[Apoptosis]]. At MOMP, the outer mitochondrial membrane loses its barrier integrity, allowing the efflux of intermembrane-space proteins — most notably [[Cytochrome c]] and [[Smac-DIABLO]] — that license the proteolytic caspase cascade and commit the cell to death.

## Mechanism of MOMP

MOMP is controlled by the [[Bcl-2 family]] of proteins. Pro-survival members (Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, Mcl-1) preserve membrane integrity, whereas "effector" proteins [[Bax]] and [[Bak]] undergo activation and oligomerization to form pores in the outer membrane. Their activation is opposed by "sentinels" [[Bim]] and [[Bid]] (and unleashed from [[PUMA]]/[[NOXA]]), which neutralize the pro-survival block. Once Bax/Bak pores form, MOMP proceeds as a regulated, sometimes partial, permeabilization that can occur at the level of individual mitochondria.

## Physiological and Pathological Roles

Physiologically, MOMP eliminates damaged, infected, or superfluous cells during development and tissue turnover. Pathologically, failure of MOMP underpins the survival of [[Cancer]] cells and their resistance to [[Chemotherapy]] and radiotherapy, since many cytotoxic agents act upstream by triggering mitochondrial apoptosis. Conversely, excessive or mistimed MOMP drives degenerative and ischemic injury.

## Determinants and Regulation

The threshold for MOMP integrates diverse stresses: DNA damage (via [[p53]]), [[Oxidative Stress]], growth-factor withdrawal, and [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] stress. The released [[Cytochrome c]] nucleates the apoptosome (with Apaf-1 and procaspase-9), while [[Smac-DIABLO]] antagonizes [[XIAP]] and related inhibitors, removing the brake on effector caspases. A subset of cells can survive "minor" MOMP through caspase-independent mechanisms, contributing to heterogeneity in treatment response.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

MOMP is a major therapeutic target. BH3 mimetics such as venetoclax (targeting Bcl-2) push cells toward MOMP, and agents that activate Bax/Bak or disable Mcl-1 are in development. Quantifying MOMP (e.g., by cytochrome c release or caspase activation assays) informs prognostic and pharmacodynamic assessment.
