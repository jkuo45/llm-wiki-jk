# Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis

The Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis (GPELF) is a [[World Health Organization]] (WHO) initiative launched in 2000 with the goal of interrupting transmission of [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] (LF) and alleviating the disability and suffering caused by this neglected tropical disease. It is one of the largest public-health drug-distribution efforts in history and a model for global disease elimination.

## Strategy & Mechanisms

GPELF operates through two complementary pillars: (1) **mass drug administration (MDA)** to interrupt transmission by clearing circulating microfilariae in endemic populations, and (2) **morbidity management and disability prevention** for those already affected (hydrocele surgery, hygiene, and skin care to prevent acute dermatolymphangioadenitis). The MDA regimen is principally a co-administered two-drug combination of [[Ivermectin]] (donated by [[Merck & Co. Inc]]) and [[Albendazole]] (donated by [[GlaxoSmithKline]]), with [[Diethylcarbamazine]] used where onchocerciasis is absent. Annual treatment for 4–6 years aims to suppress the reservoir below the transmission threshold.

## Physiological/Public-Health Impact

LF is caused by filarial nematodes (*Wuchereria bancrofti*, *Brugia malayi*, *Brugia timori*) transmitted by mosquitoes; chronic infection leads to lymphedema, elephantiasis, and hydrocele, burdening over 800 million people at risk. By reducing microfilarial density, GPELF blocks vector uptake and halts community spread.

## Relevance to Cancer Research

Although GPELF is a parasitic-disease program, it intersects the cancer knowledge base in two ways. First, the donated anthelmintics — ivermectin and albendazole — are under active investigation for repurposed [[Anticancer]] activity (e.g., ivermectin's inhibition of [[YAP1]], [[PAK1]], and [[Akt]]/[[mTOR]] signaling; albendazole's [[Microtubule]] destabilization akin to other benzimidazoles). Second, the scale-up of MDA provides a real-world platform for studying drug safety, combination dosing, and global access that informs cancer [[Chemotherapy]] delivery in low-resource settings. The GluCl-targeted mechanism of ivermectin ([[Glutamate-gated Cl- channels]]) underscores its selective safety.
