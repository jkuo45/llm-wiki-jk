# Diethylcarbamazine

**Diethylcarbamazine (DEC)** is a piperazine-derived synthetic compound long used to treat filarial nematode infections, including [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] and, less commonly, [[Onchocerciasis]] (limited by the Mazzotti reaction). Beyond antiparasitic use, DEC shows anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and emerging [[Cancer]]-relevant activities, including attenuation of tumor-promoting [[Inflammation]] and lymphangiogenesis.

## Chemistry & Antifilarial Mechanism

DEC is a synthetic diethylcarbamazine citrate salt. Its precise antifilarial mechanism remains incompletely defined but involves suppression of arachidonic-acid metabolism in the parasite (reducing prostaglandin and leukotriene production) and, in host tissues, potentiation of eosinophil- and macrophage-mediated killing of microfilariae. DEC also alters parasite membrane permeability and impairs motility.

## Anti-Inflammatory & Immunomodulatory Actions

DEC inhibits cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase pathways, lowering pro-inflammatory eicosanoids. It modulates innate immunity and has been used to dampen eosinophil-driven inflammation. These properties are mechanistically relevant to oncology, since chronic [[Inflammation]] and eicosanoid signaling (via [[Arachidonic acid]] metabolites) foster tumor initiation, angiogenesis, and [[Metastasis]].

## Emerging Anticancer Relevance

Preclinical studies indicate DEC can suppress tumor-associated lymphangiogenesis and lymphatic spread by interfering with VEGF-C/[[VEGF]] signaling and macrophage recruitment, implicating it in limiting [[Metastasis]] to lymph nodes. DEC's immunomodulatory effects may also enhance antitumor immunity. It has been studied in combination regimens with [[Ivermectin]] and [[Albendazole]] in mass-drug-administration programs, and repurposing interest extends to its potential as a low-cost adjuvant that tempers the inflammatory tumor microenvironment. However, clinical anticancer evidence remains early and hypothesis-generating.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

DEC remains a WHO-essential medicine for filariasis, typically given as annual single-dose mass treatment with [[Ivermectin]]/[[Albendazole]]. Its anticancer potential is investigational; research focuses on (1) blocking lymphatic metastasis via VEGF-C inhibition, (2) resolving protumor [[Inflammation]], and (3) combination with immunotherapy. The Mazzotti reaction (severe pruritus, rash, fever) limits onchocerciasis use and must be managed where DEC is deployed.
