# MKP-3

**MKP-3**, also known as dual-specificity phosphatase 6 ([[DUSP6]]), is a cytosolic phosphatase of the MAP kinase phosphatase (MKP) family. Beyond its canonical role in shutting off ERK1/2 signaling, MKP-3 has an important non-canonical function in the regulation of apoptotic threshold through direct dephosphorylation and stabilization of the anti-apoptotic protein [[Bcl-2]].

## Overview and Structure

MKP-3 is a serine/threonine/tyrosine dual-specificity phosphatase characterized by an N-terminal CH2/kinase-interaction motif (KIM) and a C-terminal catalytic domain. It shows strong substrate preference for [[ERK1/2]] ([[MAPK]]) and localizes prominently in the cytoplasm, positioning it to intersect both growth-factor signaling and mitochondrial apoptosis machinery.

## Mechanism of Action

The classical function of MKP-3 is to inactivate [[ERK]] by hydrolyzing the phosphothreonine and phosphotyrosine residues of the activation loop, providing a negative feedback brake on proliferative RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK signaling. The apoptosis-relevant function is distinct: MKP-3 binds Bcl-2 and removes phosphate groups at Thr56, Thr74, and Ser87. Phosphorylation at these sites (triggered by certain stress kinases) normally primes Bcl-2 for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. By dephosphorylating Bcl-2, MKP-3 prevents its turnover, elevating cellular Bcl-2 levels and reinforcing resistance to [[Apoptosis]]. This couples MAPK pathway status to the mitochondrial death decision.

## Physiological and Pathological Function

Physiologically, MKP-3 tempers mitogenic signaling and protects healthy cells from inappropriate death. In cancer, however, MKP-3 can be co-opted to sustain Bcl-2 abundance, contributing to the hallmark of [[Cancer]] cell survival under stress and to evasion of [[Chemotherapy]]-induced apoptosis. The interplay is context dependent: reduced MKP-3 lowers Bcl-2 and can sensitize tumors, whereas in other settings MKP-3 loss hyperactivates ERK-driven proliferation.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

MKP-3/Bcl-2 crosstalk is a potential therapeutic node. Strategies that disrupt the MKP-3–Bcl-2 interaction, or that combine ERK-pathway modulation with [[Bcl-2]] inhibitors, may restore apoptotic competence. MKP-3 expression is also explored as a biomarker of MAPK pathway activity and treatment response.
