# PAK2

**PAK2 (p21-activated kinase 2)** is a serine/threonine kinase of Group I PAKs activated by the small GTPases Rac1 and Cdc42. It is a bifunctional regulator: full-length PAK2 supports survival, proliferation, and cytoskeletal dynamics, whereas caspase-cleaved PAK2 (PAK-2p34) becomes a pro-apoptotic executor. In cancer, PAK2 can phosphorylate and inhibit [[Caspase-7]], dampening chemotherapeutic drug-induced [[Apoptosis]].

## Structure and Activation

PAK2 contains an N-terminal regulatory domain with a GTPase-binding (PBD) and inhibitory switch, and a C-terminal kinase domain. Binding of active Rac/Cdc42 relieves autoinhibition and enables activation-loop phosphorylation. Distinctively, during apoptosis initiator caspases cleave PAK2 after Asp212, releasing a constitutively active kinase fragment that amplifies the death program.

## Mechanism of Action

As a survival kinase, PAK2 phosphorylates targets in cytoskeletal, migration, and transcriptional networks (e.g., influencing [[Bad]], [[MAPK]] signaling, and [[NFκB]]), promoting the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] such as invasion and survival. Paradoxically, PAK2 also phosphorylates [[Caspase-7]] (and related caspases) in a manner that restricts their full proteolytic activation, thereby opposing chemotherapy-induced apoptosis. This dual nature — prosurvival intact, prodeath when cleaved — makes PAK2 a context-dependent determinant of treatment response.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Physiologically, PAK2 orchestrates actin remodeling during development and immune-cell function. Pathologically, its overexpression or dysregulation in tumors can reinforce apoptotic resistance and [[Metastasis]]. The Caspase-7 phosphorylation axis is a concrete mechanism by which PAK2 blunts cytotoxicity of standard [[Chemotherapy]].

## Clinical and Research Relevance

PAK2 is a candidate biomarker of apoptotic competence and a target for PAK inhibitors aimed at restoring therapy sensitivity. Its caspase-cleavage fragment is also studied as a marker of apoptosis induction in preclinical models.
