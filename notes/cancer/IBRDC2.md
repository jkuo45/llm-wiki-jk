# IBRDC2

[[IBRDC2]] (IAP-binding RING domain-containing protein 2), also known as p53RFP or RNF144B, is an E3 ubiquitin ligase of the RING-family that localizes to the mitochondrial outer membrane. It regulates apoptosis by direct ubiquitination of the pro-apoptotic BCL-2 family protein [[Bax]], positioning it at the intersection of the [[p53]] tumor-suppressor network and mitochondrial cell death.

## Structure & Domains

IBRDC2 contains an N-terminal RING finger (catalytic E3 domain), a central IAP-binding region that interacts with inhibitor-of-apoptosis proteins, and a C-terminal transmembrane segment that anchors it to the mitochondrial outer membrane. Its RING domain recruits E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes to catalyze ubiquitin transfer onto substrate lysine residues.

## Mechanism of Action

IBRDC2 is a transcriptional target of [[p53]]; upon genotoxic stress, p53 induces IBRDC2, which then ubiquitinates [[Bax]] specifically at the mitochondrial outer membrane. This ubiquitination modulates Bax stability and conformational activation, promoting Bax oligomerization, [[MOMP]] (mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization), and [[Cytochrome c]] release — committing the cell to [[Apoptosis]]. Thus IBRDC2 translates p53 signaling into effector-phase mitochondrial death. Dysregulation can divert Bax toward proteasomal degradation instead of activation, dampening apoptosis.

## Physiological Function

IBRDC2 contributes to stress-induced apoptosis in development and tissue homeostasis, and serves as a mitochondrial quality-control node linking DNA damage to intrinsic cell death.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

Because IBRDC2 enforces the p53–Bax death axis, its loss or silencing can blunt apoptosis and cooperate with [[p53]] mutation/inactivation to promote tumor survival and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance. Conversely, agents that elevate IBRDC2 (or mimic its Bax-activating ubiquitination) may restore death in resistant cancers. The enzyme illustrates how mitochondrial E3 ligases fine-tune the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] "resisting cell death" capability and represents a candidate biomarker and therapeutic target in apoptosis-defective tumors.
