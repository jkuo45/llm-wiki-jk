# Oxfendazole

**Oxfendazole** is a [[Benzimidazole]] anthelmintic widely used in veterinary medicine against roundworms, lungworms, and tapeworms. It is the primary active sulfoxide metabolite of [[Fenbendazole]], and the two interconvert via a hepatic redox cycle. Beyond parasitology, oxfendazole is studied as a repurposed anti-[[Cancer]] agent exploiting the same [[Microtubule]]-disrupting mechanism as clinically used anti-mitotic drugs.

## Chemistry and Pharmacokinetics

Oxfendazole (fenbendazole sulfoxide) is oxidized from fenbendazole in the liver and can be reduced back, forming a reversible redox pair; it is further metabolized to fenbendazole sulfone. Like other benzimidazoles ([[Albendazole]], [[Mebendazole]]), it is metabolized by [[Cytochrome P450]] enzymes, including CYP2C19 and cardiac CYP2J2, which influences exposure and potential drug–drug interactions.

## Mechanism of Action

The core target is [[Tubulin]]: oxfendazole binds β-tubulin, inhibiting its polymerization into [[Microtubule]]s. In helminths this paralyzes glucose uptake; in rapidly dividing cancer cells it disrupts the mitotic spindle, arrests cells in metaphase, and triggers mitotic catastrophe and [[Apoptosis]]. This mechanism parallels that of established tubulin-binding chemotherapy (e.g., taxanes, vinca alkaloids), making oxfendazole a candidate low-cost repurposing molecule.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Physiologically, microtubule integrity is essential for cell division and intracellular transport; pathologically, tumors with high proliferative index are especially vulnerable to tubulin inhibition. Oxfendazole's selectivity arises from preferential activity against parasite/rapidly dividing cell tubulin and favorable therapeutic windows observed preclinically.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

Oxfendazole has entered human safety and pharmacokinetic trials to assess repurposing potential, including as an anticancer and anti-filarial (macrofilaricidal) agent (see [[Onchocerciasis]]). Its veterinary track record provides extensive toxicology data accelerating translational evaluation.
