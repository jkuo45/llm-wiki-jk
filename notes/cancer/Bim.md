# Bim

**Bim** (Bcl-2-like protein 11) is a potent pro-apoptotic BH3-only member of the [[Bcl-2 family]]. It is a major initiator of the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]].

## Mechanism
Bim acts as a "direct activator" of the apoptotic effectors [[Bax]] and [[Bak]]. It can also bind and neutralize all anti-apoptotic members of the Bcl-2 family (e.g., [[Bcl-2]], [[Bcl-xL]], [[Mcl-1]]), thereby lowering the threshold for apoptosis.

## Regulation
Bim activity and stability are tightly regulated by several signaling pathways:
- **[[ERK]]**: Phosphorylates Bim at Ser69, promoting its degradation through the [[Ubiquitination|ubiquitin-proteasome pathway]]. This [[Phosphorylation|phosphorylation]] also prevents the interaction between Bim and [[Bax]].
- **[[RSK]]**: Phosphorylates Bim at S93/S94/S98, which is required for [[Beta-TrCP]]-mediated [[Ubiquitination|ubiquitination]].
- **[[JNK]] / [[p38 MAPK]]**: Phosphorylate Bim to promote its pro-apoptotic activity by enhancing binding to anti-apoptotic members.
- **[[PKA]]**: Stabilizes Bim protein.
- **E3 Ligases**: Bim is targeted for degradation by [[SCF Complex]] ([[Beta-TrCP]]), [[c-CBL]], [[TRIM2]], and the [[Elongin B/C-Cullin 2-CIS]] complex.

## Role in Cancer
Reduced expression or loss of Bim is associated with tumor progression and resistance to therapy, particularly in lymphoid malignancies.
