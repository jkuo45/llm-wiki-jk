# Erlotinib

**Erlotinib** (brand name Tarceva) is a small-molecule [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors|tyrosine kinase inhibitor]] that selectively blocks the epidermal growth factor receptor ([[EGFR]]) kinase. It is approved for [[non-small-cell lung cancer]] (NSCLC) and [[Pancreatic Cancer]], where it inhibits proliferation and promotes [[Apoptosis]] in EGFR-dependent tumors, and is being combined with apoptotic sensitizers such as the Bcl-2 inhibitor [[ABT-263]] to improve outcomes.

## Chemistry & Pharmacokinetics

Erlotinib is an anilinoquinazoline that competes with ATP at the EGFR catalytic site. It is orally bioavailable, metabolized mainly by [[CYP3A4]] (with CYP1A2 minor), and excreted hepatically. Plasma exposure is highly variable; inducers (rifampicin) lower and inhibitors (ketoconazole) raise levels. Common toxicities include acneiform rash (a marker of target engagement) and diarrhea; rare but serious interstitial lung disease occurs.

## Mechanism of Action

Erlotinib binds the ATP pocket of EGFR (ErbB1), preventing autophosphorylation and downstream [[MAPK Signaling]] (ERK1/2) and PI3K–AKT signaling. In EGFR-addicted cancers — particularly NSCLC with *EGFR* activating mutations (exon 19 deletions, L858R) — this halts cyclin-D driven proliferation and restores apoptotic competency. Erlotinib also inhibits HER2/HER4 at higher concentrations. By cutting survival signaling, it lowers the threshold for mitochondrial [[Apoptosis]].

## Resistance & Combinations

Acquired resistance typically arises via the *EGFR* T790M gatekeeper mutation (now addressed by osimertinib), MET amplification, or downstream reactivation. To broaden efficacy, erlotinib is paired with agents that disable apoptotic brakes: the Bcl-2 family inhibitor [[ABT-263]] (navitoclax) sensitizes EGFR-blocked cells by freeing BAX/BAK, converting pathway inhibition into death. Such combinations are explored in solid tumors to overcome intrinsic and acquired resistance.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

EGFR is broadly expressed in epithelia; its dysregulation (mutation, amplification, ligand autocrine loops) is a driver across carcinomas, embodying the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] of proliferative and survival signaling. Erlotinib exemplifies genotype-directed targeted therapy: *EGFR*-mutant NSCLC shows dramatic but time-limited responses, underscoring the need for combination and sequential strategies.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Erlotinib is standard first/second-line in *EGFR*-mutant NSCLC and, with gemcitabine, in pancreatic cancer. Current research emphasizes (1) rational combinations with [[BH3 mimetics]]/[[ABT-263]], anti-angiogenics, or immunotherapy; (2) overcoming resistance with next-generation EGFR inhibitors; and (3) biomarker-driven patient selection (EGFR mutation, rash intensity). It remains a reference EGFR-targeted agent informing the broader field of [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors]].
