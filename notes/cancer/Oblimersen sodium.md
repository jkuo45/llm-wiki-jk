# Oblimersen sodium

**Oblimersen sodium** (Genasense, G3139) is an 18-base phosphorothioate antisense oligonucleotide designed to inhibit synthesis of the anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2]] protein. It was the first agent targeting Bcl-2 to enter clinical trials and established the principle of Bcl-2–directed therapy that culminated in BH3 mimetics.

## Structure and Mechanism

Oblimersen is a chemically modified DNA oligomer complementary to the first six codons of human *BCL2* mRNA. After cellular uptake, it forms a DNA–RNA hybrid that recruits [[RNase H]], which cleaves the mRNA, reducing Bcl-2 protein translation. Lower Bcl-2 weakens the mitochondrial apoptotic brake, lowering the threshold for [[MOMP]] and sensitizing cancer cells to [[Apoptosis]] induced by cytotoxic drugs or radiation.

## Clinical Development

Oblimersen was evaluated across hematologic and solid tumors, notably [[Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia]] (CLL), [[Melanoma]], and non-Hodgkin lymphoma, frequently in combination with [[Chemotherapy]] (e.g., dacarbazine, fludarabine). Although it showed modest single-agent activity and variable trial outcomes, it provided critical pharmacodynamic validation that Bcl-2 lowering increases treatment sensitivity and informed later successes with Bcl-2 inhibitors such as venetoclax.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Bcl-2 is overexpressed in many malignancies, where it blocks [[Apoptosis]] and drives resistance. Oblimersen's mechanism addresses a core [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] node — resistance to cell death — by post-transcriptionally suppressing the offending protein rather than competing at its binding pocket.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

Though not broadly approved, oblimersen remains a reference antisense construct and a proof-of-concept for Bcl-2 targeting. Its development advanced oligonucleotide delivery, stabilization (phosphorothioate backbone), and combination trial design, paving the way for modern antisense and siRNA therapeutics.
