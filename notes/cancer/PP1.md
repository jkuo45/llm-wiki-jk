# PP1

Protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) is one of the four major serine/threonine protein phosphatase families (PPP) and one of the most abundant phosphatases in eukaryotic cells. PP1 reverses phosphorylation marks placed by kinases such as [[CaMKII]], thereby shaping signaling networks that govern metabolism, transcription, cell-cycle progression, and apoptosis. In death signaling, PP1 regulates the [[Phosphorylation]] status of [[Caspase-2]] by counteracting CaMKII activity.

## Structure & Mechanism

The catalytic subunit of PP1 (encoded by PPP1CA/B/C) is highly conserved and intrinsically promiscuous; substrate specificity and localization are conferred by a large repertoire of regulatory subunits (e.g., spinophilin, inhibitor-1/PP1 regulatory subunit 1A) that target PP1 to distinct cellular compartments and substrates. PP1 dephosphorylates serines and threonines in motifs often opposite those created by proline-directed and CaMKII kinases. By dephosphorylating sites on [[Caspase-2]] that CaMKII had activated, PP1 modulates the threshold for PIDDosome-dependent apoptosis.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

PP1 integrates signals from cAMP, calcium, and stress pathways to maintain homeostasis. In [[Cancer]], PP1 activity is frequently altered; both loss of tumor-suppressive dephosphorylation and ectopic activity that dampens apoptotic priming have been described. By tuning [[Caspase-2]] activation downstream of DNA damage, PP1 influences the balance between survival and [[Apoptosis]], a node relevant to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]].

## Clinical & Research Relevance

PP1 regulatory subunits are biomarkers and therapeutic handles. Inhibitors such as calyculin A and okadaic acid are research tools; targeting specific PP1 holoenzyme complexes offers selectivity absent from catalytic inhibition. Modulating PP1 to control [[Caspase-2]]-dependent death is a conceptual strategy to sensitize tumors to [[Chemotherapy]] and genotoxic stress.
