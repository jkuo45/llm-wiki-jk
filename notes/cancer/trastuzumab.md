# Trastuzumab

**Trastuzumab** (brand name Herceptin, among others) is a recombinant humanized monoclonal IgG1 [[Antibody]] that binds subdomain IV of the extracellular region of the [[HER2]] (ERBB2) receptor. It is a foundational targeted therapy for HER2-positive [[Breast Cancer]] and [[Gastric Cancer]], and a prototype of precision oncology.

## Mechanism of Action

Trastuzumab inhibits HER2 signaling through several coordinated mechanisms. By binding HER2, it sterically hinders ligand-independent dimerization with other ERBB family members (EGFR, HER3), suppressing downstream PI3K/AKT and MAPK proliferative signaling. It accelerates HER2 receptor internalization and degradation, and it inhibits [[Angiogenesis]] indirectly by reducing VEGF production. Critically, its IgG1 Fc region engages Fcγ receptors on immune effector cells to mediate **antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC)** against HER2-overexpressing tumor cells.

## Resistance and Biomarkers

Response requires HER2 amplification/overexpression, assessed by IHC and FISH. Resistance arises via PIK3CA mutations, PTEN loss, HER2 extracellular-domain cleavage (p95HER2), and activation of compensatory pathways such as [[SRC kinase]]/FAK. Newer agents (pertuzumab, trastuzumab emtansine [T-DM1], trastuzumab deruxtecan) address some resistance mechanisms.

## Role Beyond Direct Therapy

Trastuzumab is also exploited as a **targeting vector** for selective drug delivery. Immunoliposomes bearing trastuzumab can ferry membranolytic peptides such as [[melittin]] specifically to HER2-overexpressing cells, combining receptor targeting with a cytotoxic payload. This synergy leverages trastuzumab's specificity to concentrate payload at the tumor while sparing normal tissue.

## Clinical Use

Given alone or with [[Chemotherapy]] (e.g., docetaxel, paclitaxel) and/or pertuzumab, trastuzumab significantly improves survival in early and metastatic HER2-positive disease. Cardiotoxicity (reduced LVEF) is the principal adverse effect, requiring monitoring. Adjuvant trastuzumab has transformed a historically poor-prognosis subtype into a highly treatable one.
