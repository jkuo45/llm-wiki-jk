# BRCA

**BRCA** (BReast CAncer gene) denotes two tumor suppressor genes, *BRCA1* and *BRCA2*, whose protein products are essential for the repair of DNA double-strand breaks by homologous recombination (HR). Germline pathogenic variants markedly increase lifetime risk of [[Breast Cancer]], [[ovarian cancer]], prostate, pancreatic, and other malignancies, and define tumors exquisitely sensitive to specific therapies.

## Protein Function & DNA Repair

- **BRCA1**: Forms the BRCA1-A complex and, with [[PALB2]], recruits [[BRCA2]]; it also participates in checkpoint control, ubiquitylation (via BARD1), and transcription regulation.
- **BRCA2**: Directly loads [[RAD51]] onto single-stranded DNA to drive strand invasion during HR.
Loss of either gene cripples HR, forcing error-prone repair and genomic instability — a hallmark of [[Cancer]].

## Hallmarks of BRCAness

HR deficiency (HRD) yields characteristic genomic scars (loss of heterozygosity, telomeric allelic imbalance, large-scale transitions). "BRCAness" describes sporadic tumors phenocopying BRCA mutation via other HR defects (e.g., [[ATM]], [[PALB2]] loss), sharing therapeutic vulnerabilities.

## Cancer Risk & Inheritance

- **Penetrance**: *BRCA1* mutations confer ~55–70% lifetime breast and 40–60% ovarian cancer risk; *BRCA2* similarly elevates breast, ovarian, and male breast/prostate risk.
- **Inheritance**: Autosomal dominant; genetic counseling and testing guide surveillance and prophylactic surgery.

## Therapeutic Relevance

- **[[PARP inhibitors]]**: Exploit synthetic lethality — inhibiting base-excision repair in HRD cells causes catastrophic DNA damage and death. Approved in BRCA-mutant ovarian, breast, pancreatic, and prostate cancers.
- **Platinum Chemotherapy**: HRD tumors are sensitive to crosslinking agents.
- **Research Models**: Murine BRCA-mutant models (e.g., the "B.15" model) test agents such as [[melittin]] for selectivity against HRD tumors.
