# Kitasato Institute

The **Kitasato Institute** (Kitasato Kenkyūjo) is a prominent nonprofit research organization in Tokyo, Japan, founded in 1914 by the bacteriologist [[Kitasato Shibasaburō]], who independently discovered the tetanus bacillus and co-discovered the plague bacillus. The Institute is celebrated for foundational contributions to microbiology, infectious disease, and the discovery of numerous bioactive natural products—most notably the [[Avermectin]]s, which led to [[Ivermectin]] and, indirectly, to the [[Mectizan Donation Program]].

## History & Mission

Kitasato Shibasaburō established the institute to advance serum therapy, bacteriology, and public health in Japan. Over the following century it expanded into biochemistry, pharmacology, and natural-product discovery. Its culture of screening soil actinomycetes for bioactivity culminated in the isolation of *Streptomyces avermitilis* and the avermectin family of macrocyclic lactones.

## Scientific Contributions

The Institute's most consequential anticancer-relevant contribution is indirect but enormous: the avermectins discovered there (by [[Satoshi Ōmura]] and Merck scientist William Campbell) became the basis of ivermectin, a broad-spectrum antiparasitic that has since been repurposed and studied for [[Cancer]] biology (e.g., YAP1/EGFR modulation in [[Lung Cancer]], and selective lethality in [[Leukemia]]). The Institute also contributed to vaccine development, antibiotic discovery, and the study of [[Inflammation]] and host defense.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

While the Kitasato Institute is not itself a disease entity, its output reshaped global health: ivermectin underpinned mass-drug-administration campaigns against onchocerciasis and lymphatic filariasis (via the [[Mectizan Donation Program]] with [[Merck & Co. Inc]]), reducing disease burden and, through improved community health, indirect cancer-risk factors. The natural-product discovery paradigm it exemplifies continues to feed [[Drug Repositioning]] efforts in oncology.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

The Institute remains active in infectious disease and natural-product pharmacology, and its legacy illustrates how parasitic-disease therapeutics can cross over into cancer research. Its collections and screening expertise are a resource for identifying novel cytotoxic and immunomodulatory agents relevant to [[Chemotherapy]] and targeted therapy.
