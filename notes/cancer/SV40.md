# SV40

**Simian Virus 40** (SV40) is a small, non-enveloped polyomavirus naturally infecting rhesus macaques. It rose to prominence in cancer biology and public-health controversy because its early proteins — **Large T antigen** and **Small t antigen** — are potent oncoproteins that inactivate key [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]] products.

## Structure and Oncoproteins

The SV40 genome is a circular double-stranded DNA ~5.2 kb in size, packaged in an icosahedral capsid. The alternatively spliced early region encodes Large T antigen (a ~94 kDa multifunctional protein) and Small t antigen. Large T antigen binds and inactivates **p53** and the **retinoblastoma protein (Rb)**, blocking [[Apoptosis]] and cell-cycle arrest, while also recruiting cellular DNA-replication machinery to drive S-phase entry. Small t antigen perturbs protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A), deregulating proliferative signaling. Expression of T antigens can immortalize cultured cells and, in permissive models, drive tumor formation — establishing SV40 as a classic tool for studying viral oncogenesis.

## Historical Controversy in Human Cancer

In the 1960s, some polio and adenovirus vaccines were inadvertently contaminated with SV40. Epidemiologic studies later reported SV40 T-antigen sequences in certain human tumors (notably mesothelioma, [[Brain Cancer|brain tumors]], and osteosarcoma), fueling debate over whether SV40 contributes to human cancer. The consensus of large subsequent analyses is that, while experimental transformation is robust, natural SV40 infection plays at most a minor or unproven role in human malignancy; the association remains contested rather than established.

## Role in Research and Drug Delivery

Beyond oncogenesis, a positively charged sequence (K21KKRKV26) from the SV40 Large T antigen nuclear-localization signal functions as a **cell-penetrating peptide**. In anticancer peptide engineering, this sequence was grafted onto a negatively charged [[melittin]] mutant (DEDE-melittin) to restore membrane interaction and rescue its anticancer lytic activity, illustrating how viral motifs are repurposed for targeted cancer therapeutics.
