# Triple-negative Breast Cancer

**Triple-negative breast cancer** (TNBC) is an aggressive subtype of [[notes/_link/Breast Cancer]] characterized by the lack of expression of estrogen receptors (ER), progesterone receptors (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 ([[HER2]]).

## Characteristics
- **Prognosis**: Associated with poor clinical outcomes and high mortality.
- **Molecular Targets**: Frequently overexpresses [[EGFR]] (approx. 50% of cases), but often shows resistance to standard anti-EGFR therapies.
- **Signaling**: Highly dependent on the [[notes/_link/PI3K]]/[[notes/_link/Akt]]/[[notes/_link/mTOR]] pathway.

## Connections
- [[notes/_link/Breast Cancer]]: The broader disease category.
- [[EGFR]]: Often overexpressed in TNBC.
- [[melittin]]: Shown to selectively induce cell death in TNBC.
- [[Ivermectin]]: Can restore sensitivity to [[tamoxifen]] in TNBC cells and selectively targets [[Cancer Stem Cells]] in this subtype.
- [[docetaxel]]: A chemotherapy agent often used in combination treatments.
