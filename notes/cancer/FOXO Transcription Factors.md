# FOXO Transcription Factors

Forkhead box O (FOXO) transcription factors are a conserved family of DNA-binding proteins (FOXO1, FOXO3, FOXO4, FOXO6 in mammals) that act as master regulators of cellular stress responses, metabolism, cell-cycle arrest, and [[Apoptosis]]. They integrate nutrient, growth-factor, and oxidative signals and are frequent targets of deregulation in cancer.

## Structure & Domains

FOXOs share a conserved "forkhead" winged-helix DNA-binding domain that recognizes the motif (A/T)AAACA. They contain nuclear localization/export sequences and regulatory domains for post-translational modification. FOXOs lack intrinsic kinase activity; their function is controlled by sequestration and modification rather than expression level.

## Mechanism of Action & Regulation

FOXOs are primarily inhibited by the [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] and [[SGK]] pathways: Akt phosphorylates FOXO on three conserved serine/threonine residues, promoting binding to 14-3-3 proteins and cytoplasmic sequestration, thereby shutting off transcription. Conversely, stress kinases ([[JNK]], [[AMPK]]) and deacetylases ([[SIRT1]]) activate FOXO nuclear localization. Once in the nucleus, FOXOs drive expression of:
- Pro-apoptotic BH3-only proteins ([[Bim]], [[Puma]], [[FasL]])
- Cell-cycle inhibitors ([[p27^Kip1]], [[p21]])
- Antioxidant enzymes ([[MnSOD]], [[Catalase]])
- Autophagy and DNA-repair genes

## Physiological Function

FOXOs maintain tissue homeostasis, promote stress resistance and longevity, and govern hematopoietic stem-cell and neuronal survival. They couple metabolic fasting signals to cellular protection.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

In cancer, FOXO activity is frequently suppressed by hyperactive [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] signaling (common in [[Breast Cancer]], [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], and [[Glioblastoma]]), tipping cells toward proliferation and [[Apoptosis]] evasion — core [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. Loss or cytoplasmic mislocalization of FOXO correlates with poor prognosis and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance. Conversely, FOXO activation can be tumor-suppressive but in some contexts (e.g., [[Glucose]] deprivation) promotes survival. Therapeutic strategies aim to reactivate nuclear FOXO (Akt inhibitors, SIRT1 modulators) or exploit FOXO-dependent apoptosis sensitization. FOXOs also interface with [[Autophagy]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] programs that determine treatment response.
