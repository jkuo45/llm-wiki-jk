# Apaf-1

**Apaf-1** (Apoptotic protease-activating factor 1) is a key cytoplasmic protein involved in the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[notes/_link/Apoptosis|apoptosis]].

## Function
Apaf-1 acts as the scaffold for the assembly of the [[Apoptosome]]. Upon binding to [[Cytochrome c]] (released from the mitochondria) and dATP/ATP, Apaf-1 undergoes a conformational change that allows it to oligomerize into a heptameric wheel-like structure.

## Regulation
Apaf-1 activity and oligomerization are regulated by several factors:
- **[[RSK]]**: Phosphorylates Apaf-1 to inhibit its activation in response to [[Cytochrome c]].
- **[[HSP90β]]**: Acts as an [[Apaf-1 inhibitor]], suppressing its oligomerization, particularly in tyrosine kinase-induced leukemias.
- **Transcriptional Regulation**: Apaf-1 is a key transcriptional target for [[p53]] and [[E2F1]].

## Apoptosome Assembly
The Apaf-1 heptamer recruits pro-[[Caspase-9]] molecules through CARD-CARD domain interactions. This clustering of pro-caspase-9 leads to its auto-activation, initiating the caspase cascade.

## Connections
- [[Intrinsic Pathway]]: Essential for mitochondrial-mediated caspase activation.
- [[Cytochrome c]]: Triggers Apaf-1 activation.
- [[Caspase-9]]: Recruited and activated by the Apaf-1 complex.
- [[Apoptosome]]: The final complex formed by Apaf-1.
- [[RSK]] / [[HSP90β]]: Inhibitors of Apaf-1.
- [[p53]] / [[E2F1]]: Transcriptional regulators of Apaf-1.
