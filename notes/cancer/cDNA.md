# cDNA

## Overview

Complementary DNA (cDNA) is [[DNA]] synthesized from an [[RNA]] template by [[reverse transcriptase]]. In molecular biology, cDNA is used for [[gene expression]] analysis, [[cloning]], and [[library]] construction. In the context of [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING signaling]], cytoplasmic DNA (sometimes referred to broadly as cDNA in the literature) includes [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments|chromatin fragments]] and [[mitochondrial DNA]] that activate [[cGAS]].

## Distinction

- **cDNA (molecular biology)**: Artificially synthesized DNA complement of an mRNA transcript; lacks [[introns]].
- **Cytoplasmic DNA**: Naturally occurring DNA species in the cytosol of stressed or [[senescent]] cells; distinct from molecular biology cDNA but occasionally conflated in the literature.

## Role in Senescence

Cytoplasmic DNA accumulation is a hallmark of [[Senescence]]. Sources include:
- [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments]] (CCFs) from [[Lamin B1]] loss and nuclear envelope blebbing.
- [[Mitochondrial DNA]] release from dysfunctional mitochondria.
- [[Micronuclei]] rupture.

These DNA species activate the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] to drive [[SASP]].
