# GLUT1

[[GLUT1]] (glucose transporter 1), encoded by the *SLC2A1* gene, is a facilitative uniporter that transports [[Glucose]] across the plasma membrane of mammalian cells down its concentration gradient. It is the prototypic member of the SLC2A family and a central node in cancer [[Metabolic Reprogramming]].

## Structure & Mechanism

GLUT1 is a ~55 kDa transmembrane protein with 12 α-helical membrane-spanning segments and intracellular N- and C-termini. Transport is bidirectional and passive, driven by the intracellular-to-extracellular glucose gradient maintained by hexokinases. GLUT1 has a high affinity (Km ~1–2 mM) suited to basal uptake, and its trafficking and stability are regulated by [[Hypoxia]] via [[HIF-1α]] and by growth-factor signaling.

## Role in Cancer & the Warburg Effect

GLUT1 is frequently overexpressed at the tumor surface to meet the high demand for [[Glucose]] imposed by the [[Warburg Effect]] — the preference of cancer cells for aerobic [[Glycolysis]] even when oxygen is available. This supports biomass production, redox balance, and survival under hypoxia. High GLUT1 expression correlates with poor prognosis and increased aggressiveness in [[non-small-cell lung cancer]], [[Breast Cancer]], and [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]]. GLUT1 is also exploited for PET imaging using [[FDG]] (^18^F-fluorodeoxyglucose), whose uptake reflects GLUT1 abundance and glycolytic flux.

## Therapeutic Targeting

Starving tumors by inhibiting GLUT1 is an attractive strategy. Small-molecule GLUT1 inhibitors (e.g., STF-31, BAY-876, WZB117) and downstream glycolytic blockade are under study. [[Fenbendazole]], a benzimidazole anthelmintic, down-regulates GLUT1 and reduces [[Glucose uptake]], impairing energy metabolism in tumor cells. Combination with [[Hexokinase 2]] inhibition may synergize. Targeting GLUT1 also radiosensitizes hypoxic tumors by collapsing glycolytic reserve.
