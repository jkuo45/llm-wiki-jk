# PLK1

Polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1) is a conserved serine/threonine kinase and a central regulator of mitotic entry and progression. It is overexpressed in a wide range of human [[Cancer]] types, where its high activity supports the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] feature of chromosomal instability and uncontrolled proliferation. PLK1 also interfaces with cell-death regulation: it phosphorylates the anti-apoptotic protein [[Bcl-xL]] at Ser62 during G2 arrest, contributing to its inactivation.

## Structure & Mechanism

PLK1 contains an N-terminal catalytic kinase domain and a C-terminal polo-box domain (PBD) that binds phosphorylated motifs on substrates and cofactors, directing PLK1 to specific subcellular sites (centrosomes, kinetochores, midbody). Activation requires phosphorylation by [[Aurora A]]/Bora and PLK1 itself (autophosphorylation). PLK1 phosphorylates numerous mitotic substrates including CDC25, cyclin B1, and APC/C coactivators. In the context of apoptosis regulation, phosphorylation of [[Bcl-xL]] at Ser62 alters its anti-apoptotic capacity, linking cell-cycle control to death decisions.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, PLK1 orchestrates centrosome maturation, bipolar spindle formation, chromosome segregation, and cytokinesis. Pathologically, its overexpression drives aneuploidy, resists [[Apoptosis]], and supports tumor growth and [[Metastasis]]. Elevated PLK1 correlates with poor prognosis in breast, ovarian, lung, and colorectal cancers. Its dual role in mitosis and death-pathway modulation makes PLK1 a nodal point connecting proliferation and survival in transformed cells.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

PLK1 is a validated anti-cancer target. Inhibitors such as volasertib (BI 6727), rigosertib, and GSK461364 have entered clinical evaluation, often combined with [[Chemotherapy]] or [[Radiation Therapy]]. Biomarker strategies use PLK1 expression and phospho-histone H3 to identify proliferative tumors likely to respond. Because PLK1 inhibition can trigger mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis, it is a cornerstone of cell-cycle-targeted oncology.
