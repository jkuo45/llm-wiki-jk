# HMGB2

## Overview

HMGB2 (High Mobility Group Box 2) is a member of the [[HMGB]] family of nuclear proteins that bind [[DNA]] and regulate [[chromatin]] structure, [[transcription]], and [[DNA repair]]. Like [[HMGB1]], HMGB2 can be released extracellularly where it functions as a [[DAMP]], activating [[TLR2]]/[[TLR4]] and [[RAGE]] signaling.

## Structure & Function

- Contains two DNA-binding HMG-box domains (A-box and B-box) and an acidic C-terminal tail.
- Binds preferentially to [[cruciform DNA]] and bent DNA structures.
- Involved in [[V(D)J recombination]] and [[RAG complex]] function.
- Regulates [[chromatin]] remodeling at [[enhancer]] and [[promoter]] regions.

## Role in Senescence

HMGB2 is downregulated during [[cellular senescence]], contributing to [[heterochromatin]] loss and [[SASP]] gene derepression. Its loss from chromatin in senescent cells destabilizes heterochromatin domains, permitting expression of [[SASP]] genes. Extracellular HMGB2 from damaged cells may also act as a [[DAMP]] to amplify inflammatory signaling.

## Role in Cancer

- Altered HMGB2 expression is observed in [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], [[Gastric Cancer]], [[Breast Cancer]], and [[leukemia]].
- May promote [[metastasis]] through [[EMT]] induction.
- Prognostic marker in several cancer types.
