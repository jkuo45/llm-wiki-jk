# ABT-263

**ABT-263** (also known as Navitoclax) is a second-generation, orally bioavailable BH3 mimetic. It was developed to overcome the poor solubility and lack of oral bioavailability of its predecessor, [[ABT-737]].

## Mechanism
Like [[ABT-737]], ABT-263 binds with high affinity to anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2 family]] proteins, specifically [[Bcl-2]], [[Bcl-xL]], and [[Bcl-w]]. It functions by disrupting the interaction between these anti-apoptotic proteins and pro-apoptotic BH3-only proteins, thereby promoting [[Apoptosis]].

## Clinical Trials
ABT-263 has entered various stages of clinical trials for the treatment of:
- [[Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia]] (CLL)
- Solid tumors (in combination with [[docetaxel]], [[erlotinib]], or [[irinotecan]])
- Small cell lung cancer

## Side Effects
A notable side effect of ABT-263 is thrombocytopenia (low platelet count), which is caused by the inhibition of [[Bcl-xL]], a protein essential for platelet survival.
