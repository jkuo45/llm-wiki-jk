# ASK1

**Apoptosis signal-regulating kinase 1 (ASK1)**, encoded by the *MAP3K5* gene, is a ~155 kDa serine-threonine [[MAPK|MAP kinase kinase kinase]] (MAP3K) that serves as a central conduit for stress-induced signaling. ASK1 lies upstream of the [[JNK]] and [[p38 MAPK]] cascades and is activated by diverse stressors including reactive oxygen species ([[Oxidative Stress|ROS]]), [[TNF-α]], lipopolysaccharide (LPS), [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]] stress, and [[Calcium Ions|calcium overload]]. Its tight regulation by redox status and interacting proteins makes it a critical node linking environmental stress to inflammatory, apoptotic, and survival outcomes.

## Structure

ASK1 contains several conserved functional domains. The N-terminal region harbors a thioredoxin ([[Trx]]) binding domain that mediates redox-sensitive inhibition. A central serine-rich region serves as a regulatory hub for phosphorylation and protein–protein interactions, while the C-terminal portion contains the kinase domain essential for catalytic activity. An oligomerization domain near the N-terminus facilitates ASK1 dimerization, which is required for trans-autophosphorylation and full enzymatic activation. The full-length protein also contains a coiled-coil region that stabilizes dimer formation.

## Regulation

ASK1 activity is exquisitely controlled by redox status and interacting partners. Under basal, non-stressed conditions, reduced [[Trx]] binds directly to the N-terminal domain of ASK1, maintaining the kinase in an inactive, monomeric state. During oxidative stress, elevated ROS oxidize Trx at its active-site cysteines, causing dissociation from ASK1. Freed from Trx-mediated inhibition, ASK1 undergoes dimerization via its oligomerization domain and trans-autophosphorylation at key residues, including Thr845, leading to full activation.

Additional layers of regulation involve inhibitory [[Phosphorylation|phosphorylation]] by [[AKT]] (at Ser83), which suppresses ASK1 activity and promotes cell survival. The serine-threonine phosphatase [[PP5|PP5]] dephosphorylates ASK1 at sites that modulate its activity, while 14-3-3 proteins bind phosphorylated ASK1 and sequester it in an inactive state. The scaffolding protein [[TNF Receptor Associated Factor|TRAF2]] and [[TRAF6]] promote ASK1 activation in response to TNF-α stimulation. Dimerization itself is essential: monomeric ASK1 shows negligible activity, whereas the dimeric form efficiently phosphorylates downstream targets.

## Signaling

ASK1 occupies a bottleneck position in stress-responsive [[MAPK]] signaling. Upon activation, it phosphorylates and activates the dual-specificity MAP2Ks: [[MKK4]] and [[MKK7]], which in turn activate [[JNK]]; and [[MKK3]] and [[MKK6]], which activate [[p38 MAPK]]. Each cascade elicits distinct but overlapping transcriptional programs that govern inflammation, cell cycle arrest, [[Apoptosis]], and differentiation.

Three major upstream routes converge on ASK1:
- **TNF-α signaling**: Engagement of [[TNFR1]] recruits [[TRADD]], [[TRAF2]], and [[TRAF6]], which interact with and activate ASK1.
- **ER stress**: The ER-resident sensor [[IRE1]] binds TRAF2, which recruits and activates ASK1, linking proteotoxic stress to the JNK/p38 pathways.
- **Direct ROS sensing**: Oxidative stress directly activates ASK1 via Trx oxidation as described above, bypassing receptor-mediated signaling.

## Pathology

ASK1 dysregulation is implicated in a broad spectrum of human diseases.

**Neurodegeneration**: In [[Alzheimer's Disease]], ASK1–JNK signaling drives [[Tau]] hyperphosphorylation and mediates Aβ peptide toxicity. In [[Parkinson's Disease]], the ASK1–p38 axis promotes dopaminergic neuron death, and ASK1 inhibition is neuroprotective in animal models.

**Cancer**: ASK1 exhibits context-dependent roles in malignancy. In [[Lung Cancer]] and [[Colon Cancer]], deletion or silencing of ASK1 accelerates [[KRAS]]-driven tumorigenesis, supporting a tumor-suppressive function. Loss of ASK1 impairs stress-induced apoptosis and allows oncogenic clones to survive genotoxic and oxidative insults. However, in certain settings, persistent ASK1 activation may promote inflammation-driven tumor promotion, illustrating the dual nature of stress signaling in [[Metabolic Reprogramming|tumor biology]].

**Metabolic and liver disease**: In hepatocytes, [[FGF21]] signals through ASK1 to promote hepatic steatosis and fibrosis. ASK1–p38 signaling downstream of metabolic stress contributes to [[Nonalcoholic Steatohepatitis|NASH]] and insulin resistance, making ASK1 a therapeutic target in metabolic dysfunction.

**Ischemia–reperfusion injury**: In cardiac and cerebral ischemia, ROS produced during reperfusion trigger ASK1 activation, leading to cardiomyocyte and neuronal apoptosis. ASK1-deficient mice show reduced infarct size and improved functional recovery.
