# Small-cell lung cancer

**Small-cell lung cancer** (SCLC) is a highly aggressive neuroendocrine carcinoma that accounts for approximately 15% of all lung cancers worldwide. It is one of the deadliest thoracic malignancies, with a strong etiological link to tobacco smoking — over 95% of patients have a significant smoking history. SCLC arises from pulmonary neuroendocrine cells located in the bronchial epithelium. In the United States, an estimated 30,000–40,000 new cases are diagnosed annually. The disease is defined by its rapid doubling time (often 25–80 days), early and widespread metastatic dissemination, and a characteristic clinical course: dramatic initial response to chemotherapy and radiotherapy followed by almost inevitable relapse with acquired chemoresistance.

## Biology

SCLC is a high-grade neuroendocrine carcinoma. The neoplastic cells are small, round to fusiform, with scant cytoplasm, finely granular nuclear chromatin, and absent or inconspicuous nucleoli. The tumor cells express neuroendocrine markers including [[Synaptophysin]], [[Chromogranin A]], and [[CD56]] (NCAM). Genomically, SCLC is defined by near-universal inactivation of [[RB1]] and [[TP53]] — loss of both tumor suppressors occurs in approximately 90% of cases. [[MYC]] family amplification (MYC, MYCN, MYCL) is a frequent secondary event, driving proliferation and contributing to the aggressive phenotype. Delta-like ligand 3 ([[DLL3]]), an inhibitory [[Notch]] ligand, is aberrantly overexpressed on the cell surface of SCLC tumors in roughly 80% of cases and has emerged as a promising therapeutic target. Additional molecular features include frequent [[PTEN]] loss, [[BCL-2]] overexpression, and dysregulation of the [[PI3K]]/[[AKT]]/[[mTOR]] pathway.

## Staging

SCLC has historically been staged using the Veterans Administration Lung Study Group (VALSG) two-tier system:

- **Limited stage (LS-SCLC):** Disease confined to one hemithorax that can be encompassed within a tolerable radiation field. This includes ipsilateral mediastinal and supraclavicular lymph nodes.
- **Extensive stage (ES-SCLC):** Disease beyond the ipsilateral hemithorax, including malignant pleural effusion, contralateral lung or nodal involvement, or distant metastases.

The [[TNM]] staging system (8th edition) is now also recommended for SCLC, though the VALSG classification remains widely used in clinical practice due to its direct impact on treatment decisions.

## Limited-Stage Treatment

For LS-SCLC, treatment is delivered with curative intent. The standard regimen is concurrent platinum-based chemotherapy ([[cisplatin]] plus [[etoposide]]) with thoracic radiotherapy. The preferred radiation schedule is 45 Gy delivered twice daily (BID) over three weeks, though once-daily regimens to higher doses are also used. Following definitive chemoradiation, prophylactic cranial irradiation ([[PCI]]) reduces the incidence of brain metastases by approximately 50% and improves overall survival. With this approach, 5-year overall survival reaches approximately 25% for patients with early-stage disease. Surgical resection — lobectomy with mediastinal lymph node dissection — is reserved for the small minority (~5%) with very early-stage (T1–2 N0) SCLC incidentally found at resection.

## Extensive-Stage Treatment

ES-SCLC is treated with palliative intent. First-line therapy consists of platinum-based chemotherapy ([[carboplatin]] or cisplatin with [[etoposide]]) combined with a [[PD-L1]] inhibitor — either [[atezolizumab]] or [[durvalumab]]. The addition of PD-L1 blockade confers a modest but significant survival benefit, extending median overall survival to approximately 12–13 months; 1-year survival is approximately 50%. The role of PCI in ES-SCLC is controversial and has declined due to negative quality-of-life data from randomized trials. At relapse, options include [[topotecan]] (approved second-line agent), [[lurbinectedin]] (a selective inhibitor of active transcription, approved for relapsed SCLC after platinum-based therapy), or clinical trial enrollment. Response rates to second-line therapy are low (15–25%), particularly in platinum-resistant disease.

## Prognosis

The prognosis for SCLC remains poor. For LS-SCLC, median survival is 20–25 months with a 5-year survival of 20–30%. For ES-SCLC, median survival is 8–12 months with a 5-year survival of approximately 5%. Despite high initial response rates (60–80% in LS-SCLC), nearly all patients eventually relapse with resistant disease. Platinum-resistant relapse (progression within 90 days of completing platinum therapy) carries an especially grim prognosis, with median survival of 3–4 months.
