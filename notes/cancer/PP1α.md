# PP1α

Protein phosphatase 1 alpha (PP1α), encoded by [[PPP1CA]], is the alpha catalytic isoform of [[PP1]] and one of the most abundant serine/threonine phosphatases in cells. PP1α reverses phosphorylation events that control metabolism, transcription, mitosis, and apoptosis. In death signaling, PP1α dephosphorylates and thereby activates [[Caspase-9]] by removing an inhibitory phosphate at Thr125.

## Structure & Mechanism

PP1α shares the conserved PP1 catalytic fold, a β12-αC loop metal-binding site coordinating two Mn²⁺/Fe²⁺ ions essential for phosphoesterase activity, and a hydrophobic groove that binds regulatory subunits and substrate-docking motifs (the RVxF motif). Substrate specificity arises from associated regulatory proteins rather than the catalytic core itself. PP1α recognizes the phosphorylated Thr125 in the caspase-9 prodomain (placed by survival kinases such as [[AKT]]/[[Rsk]]) and removes it, relieving inhibition and permitting apoptosome-driven activation of Caspase-9.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

By activating [[Caspase-9]], PP1α promotes the intrinsic (mitochondrial) [[Apoptosis]] pathway, acting as a pro-death phosphatase in this context. In [[Cancer]], the balance between PP1α activity and upstream survival kinase phosphorylation of Caspase-9 determines apoptotic priming. Reduced PP1α access to Caspase-9 (through scaffold competition or inhibitory phosphorylation of PP1α itself) can support the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] by blocking apoptosis.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

PP1α is a useful experimental tool for dephosphorylation assays and a conceptual therapeutic node: enhancing PP1α-mediated activation of Caspase-9 could overcome resistance to [[Chemotherapy]]. Small-molecule PP1 inhibitors (okadaic acid, calyculin A) are primarily research reagents due to toxicity, whereas targeting regulatory subunits offers more specific manipulation.
