# Endoplasmic Reticulum Pathway

The **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) Pathway** is an initiation route for [[Apoptosis]] that is triggered by ER stress.

## Mechanism
When the endoplasmic reticulum is injured by stresses such as [[Hypoxia]], free radicals, or glucose starvation, it leads to the accumulation of unfolded proteins (the Unfolded Protein Response or UPR).
1.  **Caspase Activation**: In humans, this pathway is believed to involve the activation of [[Caspase-12]] (or Caspase-4).
2.  **Adaptor Recruitment**: The adaptor protein [[TRAF2]] dissociates from pro-caspase-12, allowing for its activation.
3.  **Execution**: Activated caspases from the ER pathway eventually converge on the [[Common Pathway]] to execute cell death.

## Role in Cancer
Cancer cells often experience chronic ER stress due to rapid growth and poor vascularization. They adapt by upregulating survival pathways, but severe ER stress can be harnessed to induce apoptosis.

## Connections
- [[Apoptosis]]: One of the three major initiation pathways.
- [[Hypoxia]]: A common trigger for ER stress.
- [[Common Pathway]]: The final execution phase.
- [[Caspase-12]]: The primary protease associated with this pathway.
