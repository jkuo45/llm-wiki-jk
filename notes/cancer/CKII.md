# CKII

**CKII** (Casein Kinase II), now more commonly denoted [[CK2]], is a ubiquitous and pleiotropic serine/threonine protein kinase that phosphorylates a vast network of substrates involved in cell-cycle progression, signal transduction, transcription, and the regulation of [[Apoptosis]]. CKII is constitutively active and is among the most frequently overexpressed kinases in human cancer, where it functions as a proto-oncogenic survival kinase.

## Structure & Biochemistry

CKII is a heterotetramer of two catalytic subunits (α and/or α') paired with two regulatory β subunits. The catalytic subunits possess an unusual ability to use both ATP and GTP and to phosphorylate substrates at acidic consensus motifs (S/T-x-x-D/E) without requiring a priming phosphate. The β subunit enhances activity, stabilizes the holoenzyme, and directs substrate recognition. Unlike many kinases, CKII is not switched on by classical activation-loop phosphorylation; rather it is tonically active, placing it downstream of and parallel to numerous signaling cascades.

## Mechanism of Action

CKII's substrate catalogue exceeds 300 proteins, spanning transcription factors (e.g., NF-κB, STATs, β-catenin), tumor suppressors (p53, PTEN), cell-cycle regulators (cyclin D1, CDC37), and apoptotic proteins. It phosphorylates and stabilizes the anti-apoptotic protein [[Mcl-1]], inhibits the pro-apoptotic BH3-only protein [[Bid]] and the initiator [[Caspase-2]], and supports PI3K–AKT and WNT signaling. Through these actions CKII simultaneously drives proliferation and disables cell death checkpoints.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, CKII is indispensable for cell viability, DNA repair, and circadian regulation. Pathologically, its constitutive activity is hijacked in malignancy: elevated CKII correlates with aggressive phenotypes across carcinomas, leukemias, and lymphomas. By suppressing [[Apoptosis]] and sustaining survival signaling, CKII contributes directly to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]], particularly resistance to therapy-induced death and proliferative signaling. CKII also promotes [[Inflammation]] and modulates the cellular response to [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Clinical & Research Relevance

CKII/[[CK2]] is a validated oncology target. ATP-competitive inhibitors such as silmitasertib (CX-4945) and CX-5011 have entered clinical evaluation, often combined with [[Chemotherapy]] or [[BH3 mimetics]] to reawaken apoptotic pathways. Because CKII inhibition lowers the threshold for both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis, it is a rational sensitizer for TRAIL receptor agonists and DNA-damaging agents. Monitoring CKII activity and β subunit levels is being explored as a prognostic and pharmacodynamic biomarker.
