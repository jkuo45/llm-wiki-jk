# CYP2C19

**CYP2C19** (Cytochrome P450 Family 2 Subfamily C Member 19) is a hepatic monooxygenase encoded by the *CYP2C19* gene. It metabolizes at least 10% of medicines in clinical use, including [[proton-pump inhibitors]], [[anti-epileptics]], the antiplatelet [[clopidogrel]] (prodrug activation), and the benzimidazole anthelmintics [[Albendazole]] and [[Fenbendazole]]. Its prominent genetic polymorphism makes CYP2C19 a flagship pharmacogenomic enzyme with direct relevance to cancer therapy and repurposed anthelmintic research.

## Structure & Catalytic Activity

Like other [[Cytochrome P450]] enzymes, CYP2C19 is a membrane-bound hemoprotein in the endoplasmic reticulum that uses NADPH and molecular oxygen to insert an oxygen atom into substrates via a high-valent iron-oxo species. It favors lipophilic, weakly basic drugs. The enzyme contributes to both detoxification and, in some cases, activation of prodrugs (e.g., clopidogrel to its active thiol metabolite).

## Mechanism & Pharmacogenomics

*CYP2C19* is highly polymorphic. The *CYP2C19*1 allele is normal (extensive) metabolism; loss-of-function alleles such as *2* and *3* produce poor metabolizers (PM), while gene duplication (*17*) yields ultrarapid metabolizers. PMs exposed to CYP2C19 substrates show elevated drug levels and toxicity (e.g., excessive acid suppression, clopidogrel non-response inversely), whereas rapid metabolizers under-treat. These variants reshape dosing of many agents used alongside oncology regimens, affecting drug–drug interactions with [[Chemotherapy]] and supportive care.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Beyond xenobiotic metabolism, CYP2C19 participates in the clearance of endogenous compounds and contributes to interindividual variability in carcinogen activation/detoxification. Benzimidazoles metabolized by CYP2C19 — [[Albendazole]] and [[Fenbendazole]] — have attracted interest for repurposed anticancer activity (microtubule disruption, [[PAK1]] inhibition), making CYP2C19 status relevant to their intratumoral exposure. Drug–drug interactions with CYP2C19 inhibitors (e.g., some antifungals, omeprazole) can unexpectedly alter the pharmacology of co-administered agents.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Clinical guidelines (CPIC) recommend CYP2C19 genotyping to guide clopidogrel and proton-pump inhibitor dosing. In oncology, understanding a patient's metabolizer status helps predict interactions and toxicity of repurposed anthelmintics and supportive drugs. CYP2C19 is a biomarker for personalized medicine and a target for inhibitor/inducer management to optimize therapeutic index.
