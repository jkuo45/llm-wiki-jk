# Vitronectin

**Vitronectin** is an abundant ~75 kDa glycoprotein found in plasma and the extracellular matrix (ECM) where it promotes cell adhesion, spreading, and migration. It binds a wide range of partners — including [[Integrins]], plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1), and complement — and is a key component of the provisional matrix at sites of wound healing and tumor invasion.

## Structure and Binding Motifs

Vitronectin contains a central **RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) peptide** sequence that is the principal integrin-binding site, engaging αvβ3, αvβ5, αvβ6, and α5β1 [[Integrins]]. It also has a somatomedin B domain (heparin/PAI-1 binding) and a hemopexin-like domain. Conformational changes expose the RGD site upon adsorption to surfaces, a property central to its adhesive function.

## Mechanism of Action

By presenting RGD to αv-family integrins, vitronectin nucleates focal adhesions and activates downstream FAK/Src signaling that drives cytoskeletal reorganization, motility, and survival. It thus supports endothelial sprouting during [[Angiogenesis]] (working alongside [[VEGF]]) and the invasive front of carcinomas undergoing [[EMT]].

## Role in Cancer

Elevated vitronectin in the tumor stroma and plasma has been associated with invasion, [[Metastasis]], and poorer prognosis in several cancers. Its integrin engagement facilitates tumor cell adhesion to matrix and endothelium (a step in hematogenous dissemination), while its competing relationship with other RGD-bearing matrix proteins modulates adhesion dynamics. Vitronectin also influences plasminogen activation via PAI-1, affecting pericellular proteolysis and invasion.

## Therapeutic and Research Relevance

The vitronectin–integrin axis is a target for integrin antagonists (e.g., cilengitide, αvβ3/αvβ5 blockers) evaluated to curb angiogenesis and metastasis. In peptide engineering, vitronectin's RGD motif is the conceptual template for tumor-targeting sequences such as the TGF-β3–derived **RGD1-melittin** used to direct [[melittin]] to integrin-rich tumor vasculature.
