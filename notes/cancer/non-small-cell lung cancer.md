# Non-small-cell Lung Cancer

**Non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC)** encompasses the epithelial lung malignancies other than small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) — principally adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large-cell carcinoma. It accounts for the majority of lung cancer cases and remains a leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide, strongly linked to tobacco smoke as well as to driver mutations in never-smokers.

## Molecular Pathology

NSCLC is characterized by actionable driver alterations: activating [[EGFR]] mutations, [[KRAS]] mutations, [[ALK]] rearrangements, ROS1, BRAF, MET, and others. These converge on proliferative and survival pathways such as [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]]/[[mTOR]] and [[RAS]]–RAF–MEK–ERK, and engage [[NFκB]]–mediated inflammatory and anti-apoptotic programs. Histology and molecular subtyping now guide therapy rather than morphology alone.

## Clinical Presentation and Biomarkers

Patients present with cough, dyspnea, hemoptysis, weight loss, or metastatic symptoms. Diagnosis relies on imaging, biopsy, and molecular profiling. Biomarkers include driver mutations (EGFR, ALK, KRAS), PD-L1 expression for immunotherapy selection, and circulating tumor DNA for monitoring. Staging (TNM) determines surgical candidacy versus systemic therapy.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Management spans surgical resection for early disease, platinum-based [[Chemotherapy]], targeted tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (EGFR/ALK), and immune checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD-1/PD-L1). Resistance via secondary mutations and pathway rewiring remains a central challenge.

## Research with Melittin

[[melittin]], the principal peptide of honeybee venom, shows antitumoral activity in NSCLC models, notably by suppressing [[NFκB]] signaling and by disrupting membrane integrity. It demonstrates synergy with [[docetaxel]] in lung cancer cells, suggesting combinations that overcome resistance and enhance apoptosis. Such natural-product repurposing is an active preclinical avenue.
