# PTPN23

PTPN23 (Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Non-Receptor Type 23), also called HD-PTP (His domain-containing protein tyrosine phosphatase), is a non-receptor tyrosine phosphatase distinguished by an N-terminal Bro1 domain that links it to endosomal sorting and ESCRT machinery. PTPN23 acts as a tumor suppressor by coordinating receptor downregulation, membrane trafficking, and death signaling. It dephosphorylates and regulates the pro-apoptotic protein [[Bak]].

## Structure & Mechanism

PTPN23 contains an N-terminal Bro1 domain (mediating interaction with CHMP proteins of the ESCRT-III complex and with ubiquitinated cargo), a central phosphatase domain with the conserved catalytic motif, and a C-terminal region involved in substrate recruitment. Through its Bro1 domain, PTPN23 participates in multivesicular body biogenesis and lysosomal degradation of receptor tyrosine kinases such as EGFR. Its phosphatase activity modulates signaling from these receptors and acts on [[Bak]], influencing [[Apoptosis]].

## Physiological & Pathological Function

By promoting degradation of oncogenic receptor tyrosine kinases and restraining survival signaling, PTPN23 suppresses proliferation and supports death of damaged cells—consistent with a tumor-suppressive role. Loss or downregulation of PTPN23 in [[Cancer]] permits sustained receptor signaling, defective trafficking, and evasion of [[Apoptosis]], contributing to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. Its effects on [[Bak]] connect trafficking control directly to mitochondrial death commitment.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

PTPN23 expression is investigated as a prognostic marker, with reduced levels associated with aggressive disease. As a tumor suppressor, restoring PTPN23 function or mimicking its downstream effects (enhanced receptor degradation, [[Bak]]-dependent death) is a conceptual therapeutic route, potentially sensitizing tumors to [[Chemotherapy]] and targeted agents.
