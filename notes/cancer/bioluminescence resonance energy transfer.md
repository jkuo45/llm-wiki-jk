# Bioluminescence Resonance Energy Transfer

**Bioluminescence resonance energy transfer (BRET)** is a spectroscopic technique in which energy is transferred non-radiatively from a luminescent donor enzyme (typically luciferase, e.g., *Renilla* or NanoLuc) to a fluorescent acceptor (e.g., YFP, GFP, or a near-infrared fluorophore) when the two are within ~10 nm. Because it requires no external excitation light, BRET avoids autofluorescence and is ideal for live-cell and in vivo assays.

## Principle

The donor is fused to one protein and the acceptor to another. If the proteins interact, the donor and acceptor are brought close; substrate oxidation by luciferase emits light that is resonantly transferred to the acceptor, producing acceptor emission. The **BRET ratio** (acceptor/donor emission) reports interaction, conformational change, or subcellular proximity in real time.

## Role in Cancer Research

- **Receptor Dynamics**: BRET has been used to test whether [[melittin]] or other ligands disrupt binding of EGF to the [[EGFR]], revealing allosteric or competitive interference at the receptor.
- **Protein–Protein Interactions**: Mapping Bcl-2 family complexes (e.g., [[Bax]]/[[Bcl-2]]), β-catenin/TCF, or kinase–substrate pairing in live cells.
- **Kinase Activation**: Real-time readouts of [[SRC kinase]] or [[ERK]] signaling dynamics in tumor models.
- **Drug Screening**: High-throughput BRET biosensors report pathway modulation by candidates such as [[Ivermectin]] or [[BH3 mimetics]].

## Advantages & Limitations

BRET is quantitative, live-cell compatible, and low-background, but is limited by the need for genetic fusion constructs and the ~10 nm interaction range. It complements FRET, co-immunoprecipitation, and [[Annexin V]] apoptosis assays in mechanistic oncology studies.
