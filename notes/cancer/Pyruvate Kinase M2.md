# Pyruvate Kinase M2

[[Pyruvate Kinase M2]] (PKM2) is an isoenzyme of [[pyruvate kinase]] that plays a critical role in the final step of [[Glycolysis]], converting [[phosphoenolpyruvate]] (PEP) to [[pyruvate]].

## Role in Cancer
- **Warburg Effect:** PKM2 is often overexpressed in [[Cancer]] cells. It can exist in a low-activity dimeric form or a high-activity tetrameric form. The dimeric form allows glycolytic intermediates to be diverted into biosynthetic pathways, supporting the [[Warburg Effect]].
- **Non-metabolic Functions:** PKM2 can also translocate to the nucleus and act as a co-activator for transcription factors like [[HIF-1α]] and [[β-catenin]], promoting cell proliferation.
- **DNA Repair:** PKM2 has been shown to regulate [[Homologous Recombination]]-mediated [[DNA double-strand break repair]].
