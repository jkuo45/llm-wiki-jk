# LYN

**LYN** is a member of the Src family of non-receptor tyrosine kinases (SFKs), best known for its roles in B-cell receptor and Fc receptor signaling. In the context of cell death regulation, LYN phosphorylates the initiator caspase [[Caspase-8]] on tyrosine residues (Tyr397 and Tyr465), which suppresses Caspase-8 catalytic activity and thereby restrains the extrinsic [[Apoptosis]] pathway. This inhibitory phosphorylation is counterbalanced by the tyrosine phosphatase [[SHP1]], making LYN/SHP1 a regulatory switch for death-receptor signaling.

## Structure & Activation

Like other SFKs, LYN contains an N-terminal myristoylation/palmitoylation motif for membrane targeting, an SH3 and SH2 domain, and a C-terminal kinase domain with a regulatory tail tyrosine (Tyr507). Kinase activity is held in check by phosphorylation of Tyr507 (by Csk) and unleashed when that site is dephosphorylated. LYN thus integrates membrane-proximal receptor signals into tyrosine phosphorylation cascades affecting survival and death.

## Mechanism of Action

Death receptors (e.g., Fas, TRAIL-R) recruit the DISC containing pro-Caspase-8. LYN binds and phosphorylates Caspase-8 at Tyr397/Tyr465, inhibiting its proteolytic activation and reducing downstream cleavage of [[Bid]] and effector caspases. When the phosphatase SHP1 removes these phosphates, Caspase-8 is de-repressed and apoptosis proceeds. This post-translational "brake" allows survival cues to override death-receptor engagement—a mechanism exploitable in tumors that rely on LYN to resist apoptosis.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

LYN is essential for B-cell development and innate immune cell signaling. Dysregulation contributes to B-cell malignancies and to altered apoptotic thresholds in leukemia/lymphoma. By dampening Caspase-8, LYN can promote resistance to death-ligand therapies and to [[Chemotherapy]], positioning it as a candidate modulator of tumor cell fate alongside other SFKs.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

SFK inhibitors (e.g., dasatinib, which inhibits LYN among others) can relieve Caspase-8 suppression and sensitize tumors to TRAIL or Fas ligand. Combining SHP1-activating or LYN-inhibiting strategies with death-receptor agonists is an area of investigation for overcoming apoptosis resistance. The LYN–Caspase-8 axis also illustrates how non-Ser/Thr phosphorylation finely tunes executioner proteases.
