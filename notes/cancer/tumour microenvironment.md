# Tumour Microenvironment

The [[Tumour Microenvironment]] (TME) is the complex cellular environment surrounding a [[Tumor|tumor]]. It consists not only of the [[Cancer Cells|malignant cancer cells]] themselves but also a diverse array of non-malignant cells, blood vessels, and the [[Extracellular Matrix]] (ECM). The TME is increasingly recognized as a key player in cancer progression, [[Metastasis]], and the failure of conventional therapies.

## Key Components

The TME is a heterogeneous ecosystem. Its major components include:

### Cellular Components
- **Cancer-Associated Fibroblasts (CAFs)**: These cells remodel the ECM, secrete growth factors, and promote [[Tumor Growth]] and [[Angiogenesis]].
- **Immune Cells**:
    - **Pro-inflammatory/Pro-tumorigenic**: [[Tumor-Associated Macrophages]] (TAMs), [[Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells]] (MDSCs), and regulatory [[T cells]] (Tregs) often suppress the anti-tumor immune response.
    - **Anti-tumorigenic**: [[Cytotoxic T cells]] and [[Natural Killer Cells]] (NK cells) can recognize and eliminate cancer cells, but their function is often suppressed within the TME.
- **Endothelial Cells**: These cells line the blood vessels that supply the tumor with nutrients and oxygen, a process called [[Angiogenesis]].

### Non-Cellular Components
- **Extracellular Matrix (ECM)**: A complex network of proteins (like [[Collagen]]) and carbohydrates that provides structural support and acts as a reservoir for signaling molecules.
- **Signaling Molecules**: A "cytokine soup" including growth factors (e.g., [[VEGF]], [[EGF]]), chemokines, and [[Exosomes]] that facilitate communication between cells.
- **Physical Factors**: The TME is often characterized by low oxygen ([[Hypoxia]]), high acidity (low pH), and increased interstitial pressure, all of which contribute to drug resistance and immune evasion.

## Role in Cancer Progression

The TME is not a passive bystander; it actively contributes to the hallmarks of [[Cancer]]:
- **Immune Evasion**: The TME creates an immunosuppressive niche that allows cancer cells to escape detection and destruction by the immune system.
- **Angiogenesis**: Tumors secrete factors like [[VEGF]] to recruit new blood vessels, ensuring a steady supply of nutrients.
- **Invasion & [[Metastasis]]**: CAFs and other cells help degrade the ECM, allowing cancer cells to invade surrounding tissues and eventually spread to distant organs.

## Therapeutic Targeting

Targeting the TME is a major focus of modern [[Oncology]]:
- **Immunotherapy**: Checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., anti-[[PD-1]], anti-[[CTLA-4]]) work by "releasing the brakes" on the immune system, allowing it to attack cancer cells within the TME.
- **Anti-angiogenic Therapy**: Drugs that inhibit [[VEGF]] or its receptor can starve the tumor of its blood supply.
- **Stromal Targeting**: Strategies are being developed to target CAFs or reprogram them to a less supportive phenotype.

## Connections

- [[Cancer]] - The central malignancy that both shapes and is shaped by the TME.
- [[Metastasis]] - A process heavily dependent on TME components for invasion and survival.
- [[Hypoxia]] - A critical physical factor within the TME that drives progression and resistance.
- [[Immunotherapy]] - A class of treatments designed to modulate the immune component of the TME.
- [[Angiogenesis]] - The formation of new blood vessels, a key TME-supported process.
- [[VEGF]] - A primary signaling molecule driving TME-mediated angiogenesis.
- [[Exosomes]] - Extracellular vesicles that mediate communication between cancer and TME cells.
