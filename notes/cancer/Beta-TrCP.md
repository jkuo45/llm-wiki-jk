# Beta-TrCP

**Beta-transducin repeat-containing protein (β-TrCP)** is an F-box protein that serves as the substrate-recognition component of the **SCF^β-TrCP^** E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (named for Skp1–Cullin–F-box). It directs phosphorylated, degron-containing proteins to polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, making it a master regulator of inflammation, proliferation, and survival signaling relevant to [[Cancer]].

## Structure & Mechanism

β-TrCP contains an N-terminal F-box that binds the Skp1–Cullin1 scaffold and seven C-terminal WD40 repeats that recognize the phosphorylated **DSGXXS** degron motif on substrates. Once a substrate is doubly phosphorylated, β-TrCP recruits the SCF ubiquitin ligase, leading to K48-linked polyubiquitination and [[Proteasome|proteasomal]] destruction. Degradation of [[IκBα]] liberates [[NFκB]], linking β-TrCP to inflammatory oncogenesis.

## Key Substrates

- **[[Mcl-1]]**: The pro-survival Bcl-2 family protein is primed for degradation by GSK3β-mediated phosphorylation and β-TrCP recognition; loss of this axis stabilizes Mcl-1 and promotes [[Apoptosis]] resistance.
- **IκBα**: Its destruction activates NFκB survival signaling.
- **β-catenin**: β-TrCP can terminate Wnt signaling by degrading nuclear β-catenin.
- **Emi1, Cdc25A, Wee1**: Cell-cycle regulators whose turnover controls progression.
- **Snail, Claspin**: Contributors to [[EMT]] and DNA-damage checkpoints.

## Physiological & Pathological Roles

β-TrCP integrates mitogenic and stress signals. Deregulation — through mutations in the degron, overexpression, or upstream kinase changes — stabilizes oncogenic substrates and fosters tumor growth, therapy resistance, and [[Metastasis]].

## Therapeutic Relevance

Because β-TrCP controls Mcl-1 turnover, modulating it offers a route to sensitize tumors to [[BH3 mimetics]] and [[Chemotherapy]]. Small-molecule disruptors of the WD40 phosphodegron interface are under exploration, though the broad substrate roster demands selectivity.
