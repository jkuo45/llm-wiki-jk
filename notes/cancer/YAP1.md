# YAP1

**YAP1** (Yes-associated protein 1) is a potent transcriptional co-activator and the chief downstream effector of the [[Hippo Signaling Pathway]], which governs organ size, tissue homeostasis, and regeneration. When Hippo signaling is off, YAP1 escapes phosphorylation, translocates to the nucleus, and with TEAD factors drives expression of genes promoting proliferation, survival, [[EMT]], and [[Metastasis]] — endowing YAP1 with clear oncogenic properties.

## Structure and Regulation

YAP1 contains a WW domain that binds PPxY motifs (e.g., in TEADs) and a transcriptional activation domain. The Hippo kinase cascade (MST1/2 → LATS1/2) phosphorylates YAP1 at Ser127, inducing 14-3-3 binding and cytoplasmic retention; further phosphorylation (Ser381) primes [[Ubiquitination]] and degradation. Mechanical cues (cell density, stiffness, junctional tension) and GPCR/PI3K signals modulate this gate, so YAP1 integrates both biochemical and biomechanical inputs.

## Oncogenic Role in Cancer

YAP1 is overexpressed or hyperactive in [[Gastric Cancer]], [[Lung Cancer]], liver, ovarian, and many other malignancies, where it fuels tumor growth, stemness, and therapy resistance. Nuclear YAP1 correlates with poor prognosis and aggressive [[EMT]]. Because YAP1 lacks a classic druggable pocket, targeting focuses on upstream regulators and downstream partners.

## Therapeutic Targeting and Ivermectin

Repurposed [[Ivermectin]] inhibits YAP1-mediated oncogenic signaling — suppressing YAP1 expression/activity and thereby blocking proliferation in cancers such as gastric and lung carcinoma. Sensitivity to ivermectin in some models depends on baseline YAP1 levels, suggesting YAP1 status as a biomarker for ivermectin selection. Other strategies include TEAD inhibitors, LATS agonists, and vertical inhibition of upstream PI3K/SRC nodes.
