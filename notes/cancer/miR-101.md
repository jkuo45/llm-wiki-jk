# miR-101

**miR-101** is a conserved microRNA that functions as a tumor suppressor across multiple cancers. It is frequently down-regulated in [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] and other malignancies, where its loss correlates with poorer outcome. Mechanistically, miR-101 directly targets the 3′ UTR of the anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 family member [[Mcl-1]], suppressing its translation; restoring miR-101 promotes [[Apoptosis]] and inhibits tumorigenicity, proliferation, and invasiveness.

## Biogenesis & Regulation

Like all microRNAs, miR-101 is transcribed as a primary transcript (pri-miR-101), processed in the nucleus by Drosha/DGCR8 and in the cytoplasm by Dicer into the ~22-nt guide strand loaded onto the RISC/Argonaute complex. Its genomic loci are subject to epigenetic silencing (promoter methylation, histone deacetylation) and copy-number loss in tumors, explaining its frequent underexpression in cancer.

## Mechanism of Action

The mature miR-101 guide base-pairs with target mRNAs, most notably *MCL1*, repressing Mcl-1 protein without necessarily degrading the transcript. Lower Mcl-1 relieves suppression of [[Bax]]/Bak and the mitochondrial apoptosis checkpoint, increasing the death response to oncogenic and therapeutic stress. miR-101 also targets additional oncogenic effectors (e.g., EZH2, COX-2 in some contexts), broadly opposing proliferation and [[EMT]].

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

In hepatocellular carcinoma, miR-101 loss permits Mcl-1 accumulation, supporting survival and chemoresistance. Restoration experiments show reduced colony formation, migration, and tumor growth in vivo. Similar tumor-suppressive roles are reported in prostate, bladder, and ovarian cancers, where miR-101 re-expression sensitizes cells to death and targeted agents.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

miR-101 is a candidate for miRNA replacement therapy (agomirs/nanoparticle delivery) and a predictive biomarker of response to [[Chemotherapy]] or Bcl-2 family inhibitors. Because it converges on the same Mcl-1 node targeted by [[JNK]]-mediated phosphorylation and by BH3 mimetics, miR-101 status could stratify tumors for combination strategies that lower the apoptotic threshold.
