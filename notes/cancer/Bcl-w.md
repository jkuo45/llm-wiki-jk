# Bcl-w

**Bcl-w** (Bcl-2-like protein 2), encoded by the *BCL2L2* gene, is an anti-apoptotic member of the [[Bcl-2 family]]. Like [[Bcl-2]], [[Bcl-xL]], and [[Mcl-1]], it preserves mitochondrial integrity by binding and sequestering the multidomain effectors [[Bax]] and [[Bak]], thereby blocking mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP) and the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]].

## Structure & Domains

Bcl-w contains the four canonical BH domains (BH1–BH4) and a C-terminal transmembrane anchor that tethers it to the outer mitochondrial membrane and endoplasmic reticulum. The BH3-binding hydrophobic groove accommodates the BH3 helices of BH3-only proteins such as [[Bim]] and [[Puma]]. Structurally, Bcl-w is among the most "closed" pro-survival Bcl-2 proteins, with a deeply buried groove that confers particular selectivity.

## Mechanism of Action

Under survival signals, Bcl-w occupies and neutralizes activators and effectors, preventing MOMP. Its expression is regulated transcriptionally by the [[NFκB]] and [[STAT3]] pathways and post-translationally by phosphorylation and ubiquitination. Loss or inhibition of Bcl-w frees Bax/Bak to oligomerize and permeabilize mitochondria, committing the cell to death.

## Physiological Function

Bcl-w is expressed in the nervous system, intestine, and testis, where it protects post-mitotic and rapidly renewing cells. In mice, Bcl-w deletion causes male sterility (testis apoptosis) and mild neurological phenotypes, confirming its tissue-protective role.

## Role in Cancer

- **Overexpression**: Bcl-w is upregulated in colorectal, gastric, bladder, and some hematological cancers, contributing to survival and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance.
- **Therapeutic Target**: Broad-spectrum [[BH3 mimetics]] such as [[ABT-737]] and [[ABT-263]] (navitoclax) inhibit Bcl-w. Because Bcl-w shares redundancy with Mcl-1, combinational inhibition is often required for efficacy.
- **Biomarker**: Elevated BCL2L2 correlates with poor response to apoptosis-targeted agents, informing patient stratification.
