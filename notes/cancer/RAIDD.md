# RAIDD

RAIDD (RIP-associated protein with a death domain), also known as CRADD, is a death-domain-containing adaptor protein that nucleates the PIDDosome—a multiprotein complex that activates [[Caspase-2]] in response to genotoxic and mitotic stress. RAIDD bridges the sensor [[PIDD]] (p53-induced protein with a death domain) to the apical caspase, thereby initiating a defined branch of [[Apoptosis]].

## Structure & Mechanism

RAIDD contains an N-terminal caspase recruitment domain (CARD) and a C-terminal death domain (DD). The DD mediates homotypic interaction with the DD of [[PIDD]], while the CARD recruits the CARD of pro-Caspase-2. Upon DNA damage or prolonged mitotic arrest, PIDD oligomerization creates a scaffold on which RAIDD assembles multiple Caspase-2 zymogens into the active [[PIDDosome]], enabling Caspase-2 proximity-driven activation. Activated Caspase-2 can amplify death through [[Bid]] truncation and mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

The PIDD–RAIDD–Caspase-2 axis functions as a genomic-stress sensor that eliminates cells with irreparable DNA damage, guarding against transformation. In [[Cancer]], this checkpoint is frequently attenuated (through PIDD or Caspase-2 silencing, or [[PP1]]/CaMKII tuning of Caspase-2 phosphorylation), allowing damaged cells to survive and acquire mutations—feeding the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. RAIDD thus acts as a tumor-suppressive conduit for stress-induced death.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

RAIDD/PIDDosome integrity is a biomarker of apoptotic competence and a determinant of response to DNA-damaging [[Chemotherapy]] and radiation. Strategies that restore PIDDosome assembly or bypass Caspase-2 dependence are explored to overcome resistance. RAIDD remains a key experimental tool for dissecting apical caspase activation.
