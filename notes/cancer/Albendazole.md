# Albendazole

**Albendazole** is a broad-spectrum [[Benzimidazole]] anthelmintic that disrupts parasite microtubule assembly. It is a mainstay of mass drug administration programs against soil-transmitted helminths and is used with [[Ivermectin]] or [[Diethylcarbamazine]] for [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] elimination. Repositioning studies have explored albendazole and related benzimidazoles as anticancer agents because they also interfere with mammalian [[Tubulin]] dynamics and tumor metabolism.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

Albendazole is a carbamate-substituted benzimidazole. Its active metabolite, **albendazole sulfoxide** (ricobendazole), is formed by hepatic microsomal oxidation and is the principal circulating species. Like other benzimidazoles, it binds the colchicine site of β-tubulin, blocking polymerization into [[Microtubule]]s.

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

- **Mechanism**: Binding to parasite β-[[Tubulin]] inhibits microtubule-dependent glucose uptake and motility, causing energy depletion and death. In mammalian tumor cells the same interaction arrests the cell cycle at G2/M and interferes with mitotic spindle formation, inducing [[Apoptosis]].
- **ADME**: Poor aqueous solubility; take with fatty food to boost absorption. Extensive first-pass metabolism; the sulfoxide/sulfone metabolites distribute widely, including into cysts (neurocysticercosis) and some tumors.

## Anticancer Repositioning

Benzimidazoles show pleiotropic antitumor activities:
- Induce [[Apoptosis]] and mitotic catastrophe via tubulin inhibition.
- Suppress the [[Warburg Effect]] by inhibiting [[Glycolysis]] and [[Hexokinase II]].
- Activate [[p53]] by suppressing the E3 ligases [[MDM2]] and [[MdmX]].
- Overcome [[Multidrug Resistance]] by evading classical efflux pumps (e.g., P-gp).

Preclinical work positions albendazole, [[Mebendazole]], and [[Fenbendazole]] as inexpensive adjuncts against glioblastoma, colorectal, and breast models, though clinical evidence remains limited.

## Clinical Relevance

In oncology, albendazole is primarily relevant as a parasitic Infection management tool in immunocompromised or cancer patients (e.g., prevention of cysticercosis in [[Brain Tumor]] or transplant settings) and as a candidate repurposed agent under investigation. Safety is generally good; teratogenicity precludes use in pregnancy.
