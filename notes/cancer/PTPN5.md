# PTPN5

PTPN5 (Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Non-Receptor Type 5), also known as STEP (STriatal-Enriched protein tyrosine Phosphatase), is a non-receptor tyrosine phosphatase predominantly expressed in the central nervous system. STEP regulates neuronal signaling by dephosphorylating [[MAPK]] (ERK1/2), Fyn, and Pyk2, thereby controlling synaptic plasticity and excitability. Although best characterized in neurons, STEP's signaling nodes overlap with proliferation and survival pathways relevant to [[Cancer]].

## Structure & Mechanism

STEP contains an N-terminal kinase-interaction motif (KIM) that binds MAPK and proline-rich sequences, a phosphatase domain with the conserved catalytic motif, and a C-terminal tail. STEP inactivates [[MAPK]]/ERK by dephosphorylating Thr/Tyr in the activation loop, terminating growth-factor and stress signaling. It similarly dephosphorylates the Src-family kinase Fyn and the focal adhesion kinase Pyk2, modulating cytoskeletal and adhesion dynamics.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

In neurons, STEP maintains signaling homeostasis and is implicated in neurodegeneration and neuropsychiatric disease when dysregulated. In neoplasia, the MAPK/ERK pathway that STEP opposes is a classic [[Oncogene]]-driven route; aberrant STEP expression or activity can therefore temper or, when lost, unleash proliferative signaling. Its adhesion-related substrates also intersect with [[EMT]] and [[Metastasis]], linking STEP biology to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] beyond its neural home turf.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

STEP is primarily a biomarker and therapeutic target in neuroscience, but its shared MAPK regulation makes it a point of cross-talk with cancer signaling networks. Inhibitors and activators of STEP developed for CNS disorders may inform combinatorial strategies where MAPK/ERK output determines response to [[Chemotherapy]] or targeted therapy.
