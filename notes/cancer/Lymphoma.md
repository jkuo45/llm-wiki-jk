# Lymphoma

Lymphoma encompasses a diverse group of neoplasms originating from lymphocytes (B cells, T cells, or natural killer cells) that arise within lymphoid tissue. It is the most common hematologic malignancy in the developed world, with approximately 550,000 new cases of Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) diagnosed globally each year. Lymphomas are broadly divided into two main categories: Hodgkin lymphoma (HL), accounting for roughly 10% of cases, and Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), comprising the remaining 90%.

## Hodgkin Lymphoma

Hodgkin lymphoma is defined histologically by pathognomonic Reed-Sternberg cells — large, multinucleated B cells with abundant cytoplasm and prominent nucleoli — embedded within a reactive inflammatory microenvironment. HL is consistently of B cell origin, representing clonal germinal center B cells that have undergone crippling immunoglobulin gene mutations but escaped [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]].

The most common subtype is nodular sclerosis HL (~60–70%), followed by mixed cellularity, lymphocyte-rich, and lymphocyte-depleted subtypes. HL has a bimodal age distribution, peaking in young adulthood (20–34) and again in older adults (>55). Prognosis is favorable, with approximately 85% 5-year overall survival across all stages.

Standard first-line treatment for early-stage HL is ABVD chemotherapy (Adriamycin/doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, dacarbazine) followed by involved-field radiation. For advanced-stage disease, escalated BEACOPP is an alternative with higher initial response but greater toxicity. In relapsed disease, the antibody-drug conjugate brentuximab vedotin (anti-CD30) has shown significant efficacy, and PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) are active in post-transplant salvage settings.

## Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma

Non-Hodgkin lymphoma represents a heterogeneous collection of lymphoid malignancies with diverse clinical behaviors, molecular pathogenesis, and treatment approaches. Approximately 85% of NHLs are of B cell origin, with the remaining 15% arising from T cells or NK cells.

### B Cell NHL

The most common B cell NHL subtypes include:

- **Diffuse Large B Cell Lymphoma (DLBCL)** — the most frequent NHL overall, accounting for ~30–40% of all cases. DLBCL is an aggressive lymphoma defined by diffuse effacement of architecture by sheets of large atypical B cells. Two major molecular subtypes exist by gene expression profiling: germinal center B cell (GCB) and activated B cell (ABC), the latter carrying poorer prognosis. Standard treatment is R-CHOP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) every 21 days for 6 cycles, achieving ~60–70% cure rates. Relapsed disease is managed with salvage chemotherapy (R-ICE or R-DHAP) followed by autologous stem cell transplantation. CAR T-cell therapies (axicabtagene ciloleucel, tisagenlecleucel) are effective in third-line and beyond.

- **Follicular Lymphoma** — the most common indolent NHL. It is characterized by the hallmark t(14;18)(q32;q21) translocation that places the BCL2 gene under the control of the immunoglobulin heavy chain enhancer, leading to constitutive overexpression of the anti-apoptotic protein BCL-2. Follicular lymphoma typically follows a relapsing-remitting course. Management ranges from watchful waiting for asymptomatic low-tumor-burden disease to immunochemotherapy with R-bendamustine or R-CVP (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, vincristine, prednisone). Maintenance rituximab following initial response has been shown to prolong progression-free survival. Histologic transformation to DLBCL occurs in approximately 2–3% of patients per year and carries a guarded prognosis.

- **Mantle Cell Lymphoma** — characterized by the t(11;14)(q13;q32) translocation resulting in CCND1 (cyclin D1) overexpression. It exhibits a spectrum of behavior from indolent to aggressive and is generally considered incurable with conventional chemotherapy. Modern approaches incorporate Bruton tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., ibrutinib, acalabrutinib) and CAR T-cell therapy.

- **Marginal Zone Lymphoma** — includes nodal, splenic, and extranodal subtypes. Extranodal marginal zone lymphoma of mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT lymphoma) is the most common form and is frequently linked to chronic antigenic stimulation. Gastric MALT lymphoma is strongly associated with *Helicobacter pylori* infection; first-line treatment is H. pylori eradication therapy with antibiotics and proton pump inhibitors, which leads to regression in approximately 70–80% of localized cases.

- **Burkitt Lymphoma** — a highly aggressive NHL driven by MYC translocation (t(8;14)). Endemic (African), sporadic, and immunodeficiency-associated forms exist. Intensive multi-agent chemotherapy (e.g., CODOX-M/IVAC) yields high cure rates.

- **Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia/Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (CLL/SLL)** — considered the same disease entity with different presentation (leukemic vs. lymphomatous). It is an indolent B cell malignancy with a characteristic CD5+/CD23+ immunophenotype.

- **Lymphoblastic Lymphoma** — typically of precursor T cell (less commonly B cell) origin, closely related to acute lymphoblastic leukemia, treated with intensive ALL-type regimens.

### T Cell NHL

T cell NHLs are less common and generally carry a worse prognosis than their B cell counterparts. Major subtypes include anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL, characterized by ALK rearrangements), peripheral T cell lymphoma not otherwise specified (PTCL-NOS), angioimmunoblastic T cell lymphoma (AITL), and cutaneous T cell lymphomas (mycosis fungoides, Sézary syndrome). Treatment typically follows CHOP-like regimens, though outcomes remain suboptimal, and novel agents such as brentuximab vedotin (for ALCL), histone deacetylase inhibitors, and hypomethylating agents are increasingly used.

## Connections

- [[Bim]] — Pro-apoptotic BH3-only protein; dysregulated in lymphoma, particularly in DLBCL and follicular lymphoma where BCL-2 overexpression sequesters Bim, disabling the apoptotic threshold
- [[Bcl-xL]] — Anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family member; overexpression in certain NHL subtypes confers chemotherapy resistance
- [[Bcl-2]] — Hallmark anti-apoptotic protein overexpressed in follicular lymphoma via t(14;18) translocation; also upregulated in DLBCL and CLL/SLL
- [[Mcl-1]] — Anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family member; critical survival factor in multiple myeloma and some NHLs; emerging therapeutic target
- [[Leukemia]] — Related hematologic malignancy; CLL/SLL represents the historical bridge between lymphoma and leukemia classification
- [[Rituximab]] — Monoclonal anti-CD20 antibody; backbone of modern B cell NHL therapy across DLBCL (R-CHOP), follicular (R-bendamustine/R-CVP), and maintenance settings
- [[CHOP]] — Chemotherapy regimen (cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, prednisone) that forms the cytotoxic foundation of R-CHOP
- [[Apoptosis]] — Programmed cell death pathway that is universally dysregulated in lymphoma; BCL-2 family members, p53, and MYC converge on apoptotic control
