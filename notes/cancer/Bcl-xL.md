# Bcl-xL

**Bcl-xL** (B-cell lymphoma-extra large) is a potent anti-apoptotic member of the [[Bcl-2 family]]. It is a major regulator of the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]].

## Role in Cancer
- **Overexpression**: Bcl-xL is frequently overexpressed in various cancers, including [[leukemia]], [[Breast Cancer|breast cancer]], and [[non-small-cell lung cancer]].
- **Resistance**: High levels of Bcl-xL contribute to resistance against chemotherapy and radiotherapy by sequestering pro-apoptotic proteins like [[Bax]] and [[Bak]].
- **Drug Target**: It is a primary target of BH3 mimetics such as [[ABT-737]] and [[ABT-263]].

## Regulation
Bcl-xL activity is modulated by [[Phosphorylation|phosphorylation]]:
- **[[Polo kinase 3]]**: Phosphorylates Bcl-xL at Ser49 during cell cycle progression and checkpoints.
- **[[CDK1]]**: Phosphorylates Bcl-xL during mitotic arrest, providing a link between mitosis and [[Apoptosis]].
- **Retrotranslocation**: Bcl-xL can block the mitochondrial translocation of [[Bax]] by re-directing it back to the cytoplasm.

## Side Effects of Targeting
Inhibition of Bcl-xL (e.g., by [[ABT-263]]) causes thrombocytopenia (low platelet count) because Bcl-xL is essential for the survival of mature platelets.
