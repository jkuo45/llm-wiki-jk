# PTPN2

PTPN2 (Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Non-Receptor Type 2), also known as T-cell protein tyrosine phosphatase (TC-PTP), is a ubiquitously expressed non-receptor protein tyrosine phosphatase. It acts as a brake on multiple oncogenic and inflammatory signaling cascades and is increasingly appreciated as a tumor suppressor and regulator of anti-tumor immunity. PTPN2 dephosphorylates [[JAK2]] and [[STAT3]], dampening cytokine and growth-factor signaling, and also acts on the pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 family protein [[Bak]].

## Structure & Mechanism

PTPN2 contains an N-terminal catalytic phosphatase domain with the conserved HCxxR motif essential for phosphocysteine catalysis, and a C-terminal tail directing nuclear/cytoplasmic localization and substrate engagement. Through its phosphatase activity it removes activating phosphotyrosines from [[JAK2]], [[STAT3]], [[ERK]], and receptor tyrosine kinases, terminating proliferative and survival signals. PTPN2 also dephosphorylates [[Bak]], modulating its pro-apoptotic activation and thereby influencing [[Apoptosis]].

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, PTPN2 maintains Signaling homeostasis in hematopoietic and epithelial cells and restrains excessive inflammation. In [[Cancer]], PTPN2 is frequently deleted or downregulated (notably in colorectal, breast, and leukemia contexts), permitting sustained JAK/STAT and MAPK signaling that supports the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. Loss of PTPN2 also impairs cytotoxic T-cell priming within the tumor microenvironment, enabling [[Immune Evasion]] and reducing response to immunotherapy.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

PTPN2 is a candidate biomarker of immune-cold tumors and a therapeutic target. Pharmacologic PTPN2 inhibition (e.g., with MSI-1436/lictrestivint) is being explored to enhance checkpoint-blockade efficacy by unleashing T-cell and tumor-intrinsic death signaling. Its regulation of [[Bak]] and [[Apoptosis]] further positions PTPN2 as a node connecting death signaling to immune surveillance.
