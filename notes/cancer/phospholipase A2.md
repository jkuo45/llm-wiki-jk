# Phospholipase A2

**Phospholipase A2 (PLA2)** denotes a superfamily of enzymes that hydrolyze the *sn*-2 ester bond of membrane glycerophospholipids, releasing a free fatty acid (commonly arachidonic acid) and a lysophospholipid. These lipid mediators feed eicosanoid synthesis, membrane remodeling, and signaling implicated in [[Inflammation]] and [[Cancer]].

## Structure and Isoforms

PLA2 comprises multiple families: secretory (sPLA2, small Ca²⁺-dependent extracellular enzymes), cytosolic (cPLA2, arachidonate-selective), and calcium-independent (iPLA2) forms, plus platelet-activating factor acetylhydrolases. Their differing localization and regulation tailor lipid signaling to context. sPLA2s are notably abundant in animal venoms.

## Mechanism of Action

By liberating arachidonic acid, PLA2 initiates prostaglandin/leukotriene production via COX and LOX pathways, linking membrane turnover to [[Inflammation]] and proliferative signaling. In venom, sPLA2 cooperates with cytolytic peptides to disrupt membranes. Interestingly, certain secretory PLA2s can themselves trigger apoptosis — e.g., bumblebee venom sPLA2 induces death in leukemia cells by inhibiting phosphorylation of the survival kinase [[Akt]], lowering the threshold for [[Apoptosis]].

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Physiologically, PLA2 maintains membrane homeostasis and generates bioactive lipids for immunity and repair. Pathologically, upregulated PLA2/sPLA2 activity fuels tumor-promoting [[Inflammation]], angiogenesis, and membrane dynamics that support the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. The interplay with [[Akt]] positions PLA2 as both a pro-survival modulator and, in venom context, a death inducer.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

PLA2 isoforms are therapeutic targets (e.g., sPLA2 inhibitors in inflammatory disease) and biomarkers. The anticancer properties of bee-venom components — where [[melittin]] provides the dominant cytotoxicity but sPLA2 contributes apoptotic and immunomodulatory effects — are actively studied for repurposing against carcinomas and leukemias.
