# A1/Bfl-1

**A1/Bfl-1** (Bcl-2-related protein A1), encoded by the *BCL2A1* gene, is an anti-apoptotic member of the [[Bcl-2 family]]. Like its relatives [[Bcl-2]], [[Bcl-xL]], and [[Mcl-1]], it preserves mitochondrial integrity and promotes cell survival under stress. A1/Bfl-1 is distinguished by its strong transcriptional induction downstream of inflammatory and survival signals, particularly the [[NFκB]] and [[STAT3]] pathways, making it a key effector of cytokine-mediated survival in hematopoietic cells.

## Structure & Domains

A1/Bfl-1 is a 175-amino-acid protein that retains the canonical four Bcl-2 homology (BH) domains — BH1, BH2, BH3, and BH4 — but, unlike the multi-domain pro-survival proteins, it is unusual in being largely unstructured in solution and in having a short half-life (minutes to a few hours). The BH3 domain is required for its interaction with pro-apoptotic BH3-only proteins, while the hydrophobic groove formed by BH1–BH3 accommodates the BH3 helices of activators such as [[Bim]] and [[Puma]].

## Mechanism of Action & Anti-Apoptotic Function

A1/Bfl-1 inhibits the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]] by binding and sequestering both the BH3-only sensors (e.g., [[Bik]], [[Bmf]]) and the multidomain effectors [[Bax]] and [[Bak]], preventing mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP). Its rapid turnover is tightly regulated by the ubiquitin-proteasome system; the E3 ligase [[c-Myc]] induction and phosphorylation events modulate its stability. Because A1/Bfl-1 is induced rather than constitutively expressed, it acts as an inducible "stress buffer" that protects cells from otherwise lethal insults.

## Physiological Function

A1/Bfl-1 is expressed prominently in neutrophils, monocytes, and other leukocytes, where it sustains the lifespan of immune cells during infection and inflammation. Germline deletion in mice causes neutrophilia followed by premature neutrophil apoptosis, confirming its non-redundant role in myeloid survival. It is also induced in endothelial cells and certain epithelial compartments under inflammatory cytokine exposure.

## Role in Cancer & Therapeutic Relevance

- **Therapy Resistance**: High A1/Bfl-1 expression is associated with resistance to [[Chemotherapy]], radiation, and [[BH3 mimetics]], especially in B-cell malignancies (e.g., chronic lymphocytic leukemia, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma) and in [[Melanoma]]. Because A1/Bfl-1 is only weakly inhibited by navitoclax ([[ABT-263]]) and not by venetoclax ([[ABT-199]]), it is a recognized mediator of "primed-but-resistant" tumors.
- **Targeted Strategies**: Developing A1/Bfl-1-selective BH3 mimetics (e.g., A-1331852, S63845) is an active area of drug discovery. Combination of MCL-1/A1 inhibitors with BCL-2 or BCL-XL inhibitors can overcome redundancy in the pro-survival network.
- **Biomarker Potential**: A1/Bfl-1 mRNA levels correlate with NFκB activity and may help stratify patients likely to respond to apoptosis-targeted agents.
