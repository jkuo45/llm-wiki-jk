# BH3 mimetics

**BH3 mimetics** are small-molecule inhibitors designed to occupy the hydrophobic BH3-binding groove of anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2 family]] proteins, displacing the pro-apoptotic effectors [[Bax]] and [[Bak]] and thereby unleashing the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]]. They translate the structural logic of BH3-only proteins (e.g., [[Bim]], [[Puma]]) into druggable compounds.

## Major Agents & Selectivity

- **[[ABT-737]]**: First-generation pan-inhibitor of Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, and Bcl-w (weak vs Mcl-1).
- **[[ABT-263]] (navitoclax)**: Oral analog; thrombocytopenia limits dosing due to Bcl-xL inhibition on platelets.
- **[[ABT-199]] (venetoclax)**: Bcl-2-selective; approved for chronic lymphocytic leukemia and other hematological malignancies.
- **Mcl-1–selective**: S63845, AZD5991 — critical where Mcl-1 drives resistance.
- **A1/Bfl-1–selective**: A-1331852 — addresses resistance to other mimetics.

## Mechanism of Action

By binding the groove, BH3 mimetics neutralize the "apoptosis brake." When the pro-survival capacity is exceeded, Bak/Bax oligomerize, mitochondria release [[Cytochrome c]], and the [[Apoptosome]] activates [[Caspase-9]] → [[Caspase-3]]. Efficacy depends on "primed" tumor cells with high BH3-only pressure and low Mcl-1.

## Resistance & Combination Strategy

- **Mcl-1/A1 up-regulation** permits escape; combining Bcl-2 with Mcl-1 inhibitors overcomes redundancy.
- **BCL-2 mutations** and altered dependence confer intrinsic resistance.
- Synergy with [[Chemotherapy]], [[BTK inhibitors]], and [[Venetoclax]]-based regimens is well established.

## Clinical Relevance

Venetoclax is a landmark targeted therapy demonstrating that restoring apoptosis is therapeutically actionable. The class exemplifies the broader strategy of targeting the apoptotic apparatus in [[Cancer]].
