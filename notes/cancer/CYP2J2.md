# CYP2J2

**CYP2J2** (Cytochrome P450 Family 2 Subfamily J Member 2) is an arachidonic-acid-metabolizing P450 prominently expressed in the heart and, notably, in many tumor cells. It is a major enzyme converting [[Arachidonic acid]] to [[epoxyeicosatrienoic acids]] (EETs), lipid mediators with vasodilatory, anti-inflammatory, and potentially anti-tumor properties. CYP2J2 also metabolizes several drugs, including the benzimidazole anthelmintics [[Albendazole]] and [[Fenbendazole]].

## Structure & Expression

CYP2J2 is an endoplasmic-reticulum hemoprotein of the [[Cytochrome P450]] superfamily. Although first characterized in cardiomyocytes, it is ectopically expressed in a wide range of malignancies (breast, lung, colon, leukemia), where it can exceed normal-tissue levels. Its transcriptional regulation involves forkhead and nuclear-factor pathways responsive to growth and stress signals.

## Mechanism of Action

CYP2J2 epoxidates arachidonic acid at carbon 8, 9, 11, and 14 to form the corresponding EETs (8,9-, 11,12-, 14,15-EET). EETs activate soluble epoxide hydrolase–regulated signaling that promotes vasodilation, inhibits [[Inflammation]], and modulates ion channels and endothelial function. In cancer models, CYP2J2-derived EETs have been reported to suppress proliferation and induce differentiation, and to limit [[Oxidative Stress]]. CYP2J2 additionally hydroxylates endogenous eicosanoids and xenobiotics, contributing to drug clearance of benzimidazoles relevant to repurposed anticancer strategies.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, CYP2J2/EET signaling protects the cardiovascular system and dampens inflammatory injury. Pathologically, its tumor-selective expression is a double-edged sword: EETs may restrain tumor growth and angiogenesis, yet CYP2J2 can also detoxify chemotherapeutics or generate proliferative lipids depending on context. Elevated CYP2J2 in malignancies makes it both a potential tumor suppressor axis and a metabolic route affecting drug exposure.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

CYP2J2 is investigated as a biomarker of tumor metabolic phenotype and as a target for modulation. Soluble epoxide hydrolase inhibitors that stabilize EETs are explored for cardioprotection and, potentially, anticancer adjuvant effects. Understanding CYP2J2 activity matters for predicting the intratumoral metabolism of [[Albendazole]]/[[Fenbendazole]] and interactions with [[Chemotherapy]]. Its differential expression offers a selectivity handle for tumor-targeted approaches.
