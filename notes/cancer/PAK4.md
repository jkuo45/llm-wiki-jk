# PAK4

**PAK4 (p21-activated kinase 4)** is a Group II PAK and a Rho-family GTPase effector (Cdc42/Rac) that functions as a core oncogenic kinase. Unlike Group I PAKs, PAK4 is constitutively partly active and is markedly overexpressed or amplified in many human cancers, where it promotes the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]].

## Structure and Activation

PAK4 shares the conserved kinase domain and a Cdc42/Rac-binding (PBD) region but lacks the autoinhibitory Switch motif of Group I PAKs, contributing to its high basal activity. Binding to Cdc42/Rac1 localizes and further stimulates PAK4 at membranes and the cytoskeleton.

## Mechanism of Action

PAK4 phosphorylates a network of oncogenic substrates: it inactivates the pro-apoptotic protein [[Bad]] at Ser112 (relieving Bcl-2-family–mediated death and opposing [[MOMP]]); it activates the [[Raf]]/[[MEK]]/[[ERK]] cascade to drive proliferation; it stabilizes [[β-catenin]] to sustain Wnt/β-catenin transcriptional programs; and it modulates [[Akt]] and cytoskeletal effectors to support survival and [[Metastasis]]. Through these nodes PAK4 integrates upstream Rho-GTPase signaling with downstream growth and survival pathways.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Physiologically, PAK4 contributes to embryogenesis, neurite outgrowth, and actin organization. Pathologically, PAK4 is amplified in breast, ovarian, and gastric cancers and overexpressed in lung and pancreatic tumors; its activity correlates with poor prognosis, chemoresistance, and invasive phenotype. PAK4 thereby exemplifies how cytoskeleton-linked kinases become cancer drivers.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

PAK4 is a therapeutic target, with allosteric and ATP-competitive PAK inhibitors in preclinical development, often combined with [[Chemotherapy]] or RAF/MEK blockers. It is also a biomarker candidate for pathway activation and a node linking [[Ivermectin]]'s anti-tumor actions (PAK1/PAK4 inhibition) to apoptotic restoration in models such as [[Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma]] and [[ovarian cancer]].
