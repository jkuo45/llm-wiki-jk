# DAMP

## Overview

Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns (DAMPs) are endogenous molecules released from damaged, stressed, or dying cells that activate the [[Innate Immune System|innate immune system]]. They serve as "danger signals" and are critical in [[Inflammation]], [[Senescence]], and [[Cancer]].

## Key DAMPs

- **Cytosolic [[DNA]]**: Chromatin fragments and [[mitochondrial DNA]] in the cytosol activate the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]].
- **[[HMGB1]]**: A nuclear protein released during necrosis and by senescent cells; binds **[[TLR4]]** and **[[RAGE]]**.
- **[[ATP]]**: Released from damaged cells; activates **[[P2X7 receptor|P2X7]]** purinergic receptors.
- **[[Uric Acid]]**: Released from damaged cells; activates the **[[NLRP3 inflammasome]]**.
- **[[S100 proteins]]**: Calcium-binding proteins that signal through RAGE.
- **[[Heat Shock Proteins]]**: Chaperones released during cell stress that activate TLRs.
- **[[IL-1α]]**: A nuclear cytokine that acts as an alarmin upon release.

## Role in Senescence

Senescent cells secrete and release DAMPs as part of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]. Cytosolic [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments|chromatin fragments]] and [[mitochondrial DNA]] act as DAMPs that activate [[cGAS]], driving STING-dependent SASP expression. [[HMGB1]] release from senescent cells further amplifies inflammatory signaling.

## Role in Cancer

- **Tumor-promoting**: Chronic DAMP release in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]] fuels pro-tumorigenic inflammation.
- **Tumor-suppressive**: Acute DAMP release from dying cancer cells after therapy can trigger immunogenic cell death (ICD), activating antitumor immunity.
- **Dual role**: Context-dependent; chronic versus acute DAMP exposure determines outcome.

- [[cGAS-STING Pathway]]: DAMP-sensing pathway activated by cytosolic DNA
- [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]: Senescence-associated secretory phenotype driven by DAMP signaling
- [[Inflammasome]]: Activated by DAMPs such as uric acid and ATP
- [[Immunogenic Cell Death]]: DAMP release from dying cancer cells triggers antitumor immunity
