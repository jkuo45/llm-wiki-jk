# c-ABL

**c-ABL** (gene *ABL1*) is a ubiquitously expressed non-receptor tyrosine kinase that transmits stress, DNA-damage, and adhesion signals. In the apoptotic response to genotoxic injury, c-ABL regulates [[Caspase-9]] autocleavage and activation, linking DNA damage to the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]]. Its oncogenic fusion **BCR-ABL** (the [[Philadelphia chromosome]]) drives chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) and is the target of [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor|tyrosine kinase inhibitors]] such as imatinib.

## Structure & Activation

c-ABL contains an N-terminal tyrosine kinase domain, an SH2/SH3 module, and a C-terminal actin-binding region. It is normally restrained in the nucleus/cytoplasm; genotoxic stress (activated [[p53]], [[ATM]]/ATR) phosphorylates and activates c-ABL, enabling it to phosphorylate substrates including p73, p53, and Caspase-9.

## Mechanism in Apoptosis

c-ABL phosphorylates and enhances **[[Caspase-9]] autocleavage/activation** downstream of mitochondrial commitment, amplifying the [[Apoptosome]]-driven cascade. It also cooperates with the p73 tumor suppressor to induce apoptosis independent of p53, positioning c-ABL as a tumor-suppressive stress responder.

## Physiological Function

Beyond apoptosis, c-ABL governs cell adhesion, cytoskeletal remodeling, and oxidative stress responses. It integrates cues from the extracellular matrix and growth factors.

## Cancer Relevance

- **Oncogenesis**: BCR-ABL is constitutively active, proliferative, and anti-apoptotic; its inhibition by imatinib/nilotinib/dasatinib is a paradigm of targeted [[Cancer]] therapy.
- **Therapeutic Resistance**: ABL kinase-domain mutations confer TKI resistance, necessitating next-generation inhibitors.
- **Apoptotic Priming**: c-ABL activation by DNA-damaging [[Chemotherapy]] (e.g., genistein, doxorubicin) can sensitize tumors; combining TKIs with [[BH3 mimetics]] is explored to overcome survival signaling.
