# Apitherapy

**Apitherapy** is a branch of complementary and alternative medicine that uses products derived from [[Honeybee]]s — honey, pollen, propolis, royal jelly, and [[Bee Venom]] (apitoxin) — for therapeutic purposes. In the cancer context, apitherapy is studied for the antitumor properties of bee venom, particularly its major peptide [[melittin]].

## Products & Active Constituents

- **Bee Venom / melittin**: Membrane-active peptide inducing [[Apoptosis]] and [[Inflammation]] modulation; inhibits [[Wnt/β-catenin]] and [[NFκB]] signaling, and reduces [[Metastasis]] and [[Angiogenesis]].
- **Propolis**: Resinous mixture rich in flavonoids and phenolics with antioxidant and pro-apoptotic activities.
- **Royal jelly & honey**: Contain 10-hydroxy-2-decenoic acid and other compounds with modest antiproliferative effects.

## Potential in Oncology

- **Accessibility**: Apitherapy offers low-cost, readily available products, attractive for resource-limited settings, resonating with community-health models seen in [[African Programme for Onchocerciasis Control]].
- **Mechanistic Research**: Elucidating how melittin and propolis engage [[Apoptosis]], [[Autophagy]], and immune pathways is key to developing safe derivatives.
- **Adjuvant Concept**: Bee products are investigated as sensitizers that enhance conventional [[Chemotherapy]] while potentially reducing dose-limiting toxicity.

## Safety & Evidence

Clinical evidence remains preliminary; systemic bee venom carries risks of anaphylaxis and cardiotoxicity (melittin lyses membranes broadly). Modern approaches use purified peptides or nanoparticle-encapsulated melittin to improve tumor selectivity. Apitherapy should not replace evidence-based cancer treatment.
