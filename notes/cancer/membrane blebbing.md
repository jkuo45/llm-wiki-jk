# membrane blebbing

**Membrane blebbing** is a characteristic morphological feature of the execution phase of [[Apoptosis]] in which the plasma membrane detaches from the cortical actin cytoskeleton and balloons outward into dynamic, bulbous protrusions (blebs). Blebbing reflects the cytoskeletal collapse orchestrated by executioner caspases and is one of the canonical "apoptotic body" precursors by which dying cells package their contents for engulfment.

## Mechanism of Bleb Formation

Healthy cells maintain membrane–cortex attachment via linker proteins (e.g., ezrin/radixin/moesin, spectrin). During apoptosis, [[Caspase-3]] and other executioner caspases cleave these linkers and, critically, activate the Rho-associated kinase [[ROCK1]] by removing an inhibitory C-terminal fragment. Active ROCK1 phosphorylates myosin light chain (MLC) and MLC phosphatase, increasing actomyosin contractility. The contracting cortex pushes the unsupported plasma membrane outward, forming a bleb that lacks underlying F-actin until一道 actin reassembles at its neck.

## Progression & Outcome

Blebs are transient and rounded; they may retract or, as apoptosis completes, pinch off as apoptotic bodies containing organelles and chromatin. These bodies expose "eat-me" signals (phosphatidylserine) and are recognized by phagocytes, enabling clean removal without inflammation. Thus blebbing is both a biomechanical signature of death and a packaging step for silent clearance.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Blebbing is not exclusive to apoptosis—it also occurs in mitosis, cell migration, and [[Metastasis]] (where bleb-based migration aids invasion through confined spaces)—but in the death context it is a readout of executioner caspase activity. In cancer, the extent of blebbing reports on the efficacy of [[Chemotherapy]], death-ligand therapy, and natural products such as [[melittin]] that trigger caspase-dependent apoptosis.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Because blebbing is a rapid, microscopy-visible marker of apoptosis, it is widely used in high-content screening to assess cytotoxic drug candidates and to distinguish apoptosis from [[Necrosis]] (which lacks blebbing and instead swells). Molecular dissection of the ROCK1/Caspase-3 axis also suggests that ROCK inhibitors can modulate the morphology and immunogenicity of dying tumor cells.
