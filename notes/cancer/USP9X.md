# USP9X

Ubiquitin-specific peptidase 9 X-linked (USP9X) is a ~290 kDa deubiquitinating enzyme encoded on the X-chromosome (escapes X-inactivation). It belongs to the deubiquitinase (DUB) family, USP subclass, and functions primarily by removing polyubiquitin chains from substrate proteins, thereby preventing their proteasomal degradation. USP9X is best known for deubiquitinating and stabilizing [[Mcl-1]], a key anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2 family]] member, but it also regulates a broad network of substrates involved in cell survival, signaling, and cancer progression.

## Structure & Domains

USP9X contains a large N-terminal unstructured region implicated in protein–protein interactions and subcellular localization. The catalytic core is a ubiquitin C-terminal hydrolase (UCH) domain that harbors a canonical Cys-His-Asp catalytic triad essential for its isopeptidase activity. The C-terminal region includes a ubiquitin-binding domain that facilitates substrate recognition and processing. This modular architecture allows USP9X to engage diverse substrates and to be regulated by post-translational modifications and binding partners.

## Substrates

The most extensively characterized USP9X substrate is [[Mcl-1]]. By removing Lys48-linked polyubiquitin chains from Mcl-1, USP9X prevents its [[HUWE1]]-mediated proteasomal degradation, leading to Mcl-1 accumulation and enhanced apoptotic blockade. This mechanism is a major driver of apoptosis resistance in [[Mcl-1]]-dependent cancers.

Beyond [[Mcl-1]], USP9X deubiquitinates and stabilizes or modulates a wide range of substrates:
- [[ITCH]] – an E3 ubiquitin ligase involved in immune regulation
- [[SMAD4]] – a central mediator of TGF-β signaling
- [[Notch Signaling]] – through deubiquitination of Notch receptors
- [[β-Catenin]] – promoting Wnt signaling stability
- [[MARK4]] – a kinase involved in cell polarity and microtubule dynamics
- [[eIF4G]] – a translation initiation factor
- [[TSC2]] – a tumor suppressor in the PI3K-Akt-mTOR pathway
- [[Survivin]] – an inhibitor of apoptosis protein
- [[HER2]] (ErbB2) – a receptor tyrosine kinase oncogene

This diverse substrate repertoire positions USP9X as a central hub in oncogenic signaling networks.

## Regulation

USP9X activity and expression are controlled at multiple levels. [[Akt]]-mediated phosphorylation enhances USP9X deubiquitinase activity, linking growth factor signaling to Mcl-1 stabilization. USP9X is also modified by glycosylation, which may influence its stability and substrate access. Binding partners and subcellular compartmentalization further tune its substrate specificity. Transcriptional dysregulation of USP9X is frequently observed in cancer, with overexpression driven by oncogenic signaling and epigenetic mechanisms.

## Relevance in Cancer

USP9X is overexpressed in a wide spectrum of malignancies, including acute myeloid leukemia ([[AML]]), chronic lymphocytic leukemia ([[CLL]]), [[lymphoma]], [[Breast Cancer]], [[cholangiocarcinoma]], [[Pancreatic Cancer]], and [[Lung Cancer]]. Its upregulation correlates with poor prognosis and chemoresistance, largely through sustained [[Mcl-1]] stabilization.

The dependence of many cancers on USP9X-mediated [[Mcl-1]] stabilization creates a therapeutic vulnerability. Pharmacological inhibition of USP9X – using agents such as [[WP1130]] (degrasyn) or EOAI3402143 – synergizes with [[BH3 mimetics]] (e.g., [[Mcl-1]]-selective inhibitors) to restore apoptosis in otherwise resistant cells. USP9X inhibition also sensitizes [[Cancer Stem Cells]] to therapy by reducing stemness-associated survival signaling.

Given its role in stabilizing multiple oncoproteins and its broad overexpression across cancer types, USP9X is an attractive therapeutic target. Ongoing efforts focus on developing selective USP9X inhibitors for use in combination with [[BH3 mimetics]] and other targeted agents.
