# Hallmarks of Cancer

The [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] are the core biological capabilities that all cancers acquire during their multistep development, originally articulated by Douglas Hanahan and Robert Weinberg (2000) and expanded (2011) into a unifying framework for tumor biology. The concept distills the complexity of malignancy into a set of shared enabling characteristics.

## The Ten Hallmarks

1. **Sustaining Proliferative Signaling** — autonomous growth-factor production and receptor activation (e.g., [[EGFR]], [[RAS]]).
2. **Evading Growth Suppressors** — inactivation of [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]]s such as [[RB1]] and [[p53]].
3. **Resisting Cell Death** — disabling [[Apoptosis]] via [[BCL-2]] family or [[Caspase-8]] inhibition.
4. **Enabling Replicative Immortality** — telomerase reactivation.
5. **Inducing Angiogenesis** — neovessel formation through [[VEGF]] and [[Angiogenesis]].
6. **Activating Invasion and Metastasis** — [[EMT]] and dissemination.
7. **Deregulating Cellular Energetics** — [[Metabolic Reprogramming]] and the [[Warburg Effect]].
8. **Evading Immune Destruction** — immune checkpoint and suppression of surveillance.
9. **Genome Instability and Mutation** — mutator phenotypes enabling diversification.
10. **Tumor-Promoting Inflammation** — [[Inflammation]] via the [[Tumor Microenvironment]].

## Enabling Characteristics & Emerging Hallmarks

Two "enabling" traits — genome instability and tumor-promoting inflammation — fuel the acquisition of the core hallmarks. Emerging additions include unlocking phenotypic plasticity, non-mutational epigenetic reprogramming, [[Polymicrobial]] ecosystem exploitation, and senescence-like cellular states. The [[Tumor Microenvironment]] (stroma, immune cells, vasculature, ECM such as [[Fibronectin]]) supplies the permissive context in which hallmarks are expressed.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Each hallmark suggests therapeutic vulnerabilities: MEK/EGFR inhibitors (proliferative signaling), immune checkpoint blockade (immune evasion), anti-VEGF agents (angiogenesis), glycolysis/[[GLUT1]] blockade (energetics), and apoptosis restoration (BH3 mimetics). Recognizing the hallmarks frames why multi-target approaches — including repurposed drugs such as [[Ivermectin]] that hit several hallmarks at once ([[YAP1]], [[Akt]]/[[mTOR]], [[PAK1]]) — may overcome the redundancy that drives [[Chemotherapy]] resistance.
