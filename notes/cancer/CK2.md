# CK2

**CK2** (Casein kinase 2, historically CKII) is a ubiquitous, pleiotropic serine/threonine protein kinase that is constitutively active and frequently overexpressed in cancer. It phosphorylates [[Caspase-2]] at Ser157 and the BH3-only protein [[Bid]], among many substrates, inhibiting their pro-apoptotic functions and thereby reinforcing tumor cell survival.

## Structure & Regulation

CK2 is a heterotetramer composed of two catalytic subunits (α or α') and two regulatory β subunits. Unlike CK1, CK2 does not require priming phosphorylation and is constitutively active; its β subunit modulates substrate specificity and tetramer stability. CK2 is unusually promiscuous, phosphorylating >300 substrates at acidic determinants (S/T-x-x-D/E). Its activity is sustained by intracellular signaling rather than by classical activation-loop control, making it a persistent survival kinase.

## Mechanism of Action

Two apoptotic substrates are particularly relevant. First, CK2 phosphorylates [[Caspase-2]] at Ser157 within its catalytic domain, a modification that suppresses caspase-2 activation and apoptotic commitment. Second, CK2 phosphorylates [[Bid]] at sites that block its cleavage by [[Caspase-8]] and its pro-apoptotic activity at mitochondria. By disabling both the caspase-2 initiator node and the Bid amplification loop, CK2 broadly protects cells from apoptosis. CK2 additionally stabilizes anti-apoptotic proteins such as Mcl-1 and phosphorylates PTEN, AKT substrates, and β-catenin, knitting together proliferative and anti-death signaling.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

CK2 is essential for viability and participates in transcription, translation, and cell-cycle control. Its constitutive activity is co-opted in [[Cancer]]: CK2 is overexpressed or hyperactive in cancers of the breast, prostate, colon, lung, and blood, where it drives proliferation and [[Apoptosis]] resistance. CK2's suppression of caspase-2 and Bid helps tumor cells survive oncogenic and therapeutic stress, contributing to the evasion of cell death — a core [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. CK2 also promotes [[Inflammation]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] pathways that fuel malignancy.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

CK2 is an attractive oncology target. Small-molecule ATP-competitive inhibitors (silmitasertib/ CX-4945, CX-5011) and substrate-competitive peptides are in preclinical and early clinical development, often combined with [[Chemotherapy]] or death-receptor agonists to overcome resistance. Silmitasertib has shown activity in selected solid tumors and in cholangiocarcinoma. Because CK2 inhibition reactivates the caspase-2 and Bid apoptotic nodes, it is being paired with TRAIL/[[BH3 mimetics]] to selectively kill tumor cells while sparing normal tissue.
