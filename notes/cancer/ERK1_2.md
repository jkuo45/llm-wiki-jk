# ERK1_2

**ERK1/2** (Extracellular Signal-Regulated Kinase 1 and 2), also known as p44 (ERK1) and p42 (ERK2) MAP kinases, are the terminal effector kinases of the [[MAPK Signaling]] cascade (RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK). They integrate mitogenic and stress inputs to regulate proliferation, survival, differentiation, metabolism, and [[Apoptosis]], and are among the most frequently dysregulated nodes in [[Cancer]].

## Structure & Activation

Encoded by *MAPK3* (ERK1) and *MAPK1* (ERK2), these serine/threonine kinases share a typical MAPK fold. They are activated by [[MEK]]1/2 via dual phosphorylation on Thr/Glu/Tyr motifs (Thr202/Tyr204 in ERK1; Thr185/Tyr187 in ERK2). MEK is activated by RAF downstream of membrane [[RAS]] GTPases, which relay signals from receptor tyrosine kinases such as [[EGFR]], FGF receptor, and MET. Activated ERK1/2 translocate to the nucleus to phosphorylate transcription factors (ELK1, c-FOS, c-JUN, MYC) and Myc-regulated programs.

## Mechanism of Action

ERK1/2 phosphorylate a broad substrate set controlling the cell cycle (Cyclin D, [[CDK1]]/[[CYCLIN B1]] regulators), translation (p90^RSK^, S6K), and apoptosis. Anti-apoptotically, ERK phosphorylates [[Bim]] (leading to [[c-CBL]]-mediated degradation) and stabilizes [[Mcl-1]], while restraining pro-death factors. Proliferatively, ERK drives *MYC* and *CCND1* expression and ribosomal biogenesis. ERK2 is the dominant isoform for viability; ERK1 can modulate ERK2 intensity and contributes to specific transcriptional outputs.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, ERK1/2 mediate responses to growth factors, stress, and cytokines in development and tissue homeostasis. In [[Cancer]], upstream mutations (*RAS*, *BRAF* V600E, *NF1*, receptor amplification) cause constitutive ERK1/2 activation, driving the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] — sustained proliferation and evasion of [[Apoptosis]]. ERK hyperactivation also promotes [[EMT]], [[Angiogenesis]], and metabolic reprogramming, and its inhibition can paradoxically engage feedback that reactivates RTKs.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

ERK1/2 signaling is a cornerstone oncology target. Approved agents include BRAF inhibitors and [[MEK]] inhibitors; direct ERK1/2 inhibitors (e.g., ulixertinib) are in trials to overcome MEK/BRAF resistance. Because ERK maintains [[Bim]] degradation, combining MAPK blockade with [[BH3 mimetics]] or [[Chemotherapy]] restores apoptotic killing. p-ERK immunohistochemistry and *RAS*/*BRAF* genotyping guide therapy; adaptive feedback (e.g., via [[EGFR]] or CRAF) informs rational combination design.
