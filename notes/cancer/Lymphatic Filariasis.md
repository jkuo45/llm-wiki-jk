# Lymphatic Filariasis

**Lymphatic filariasis**, commonly known as elephantiasis, is a neglected tropical disease caused by thread-like filarial nematodes transmitted by mosquitoes. It is characterized by profound, debilitating swelling of the limbs and genitals due to progressive damage to the lymphatic system. The three causative species are [[Wuchereria bancrofti]] (≈90% of cases), [[Brugia malayi]], and [[Brugia timori]]. Control rests on mass drug administration with [[Ivermectin]], [[Diethylcarbamazine]], and [[Albendazole]].

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Infected mosquitoes deposit third-stage larvae that migrate to lymphatic vessels and mature into adult worms, producing [[Microfilariae]] that circulate in blood or skin. Chronic infection elicits [[Inflammation]] and fibrosis of lymphatic endothelium, leading to lymphedema, [[Hydrocele]] (in men), and recurrent acute dermatolymphangioadenopathy. Secondary bacterial skin infections compound tissue damage. The disease chiefly causes disability and stigma rather than direct mortality.

## Diagnosis & Biomarkers

Diagnosis uses nighttime blood smears or antigen tests (e.g., filarial antigen cassette for W. bancrofti) to detect microfilariae or adult-worm antigens; ultrasound ("filarial dance sign") can visualize adult worms. Morbidity is staged by lymphedema grade and hydrocele presence rather than by a single circulating biomarker.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Global elimination programs (led by the [[World Health Organization]] and the [[Mectizan Donation Program]] for onchocerciasis/co-endemic filariasis) distribute annual combination antiparasitic therapy: ivermectin plus albendazole, or diethylcarbamazine plus albendazole. Ivermectin paralyzes microfilariae and enhances host clearance; albendazole targets the intestinal microtubules of adult worms. Research focuses on triple-drug regimens (IDA: ivermectin, diethylcarbamazine, albendazole) that accelerate suppression of transmission. While not a cancer, the disease intersects the broader ivermectin oncology narrative through shared pharmacology.
