# CYCLIN B1

**CYCLIN B1** is a regulatory protein that forms the master mitotic complex with [[CDK1]] — the maturation-promoting factor (MPF) — to govern the G2/M cell-cycle transition. Beyond proliferation, the CDK1–CYCLIN B1 complex phosphorylates [[Caspase-2]] at Ser308, suppressing caspase-2 activation and thereby inhibiting [[Apoptosis]] during mitosis. Cyclin B1 abundance is a recognized marker of proliferation and a prognostic factor in several cancers.

## Structure & Cell-Cycle Control

Cyclin B1 belongs to the B-type cyclins characterized by a conserved cyclin box that binds and activates CDK1. Its expression rises through S and G2 phases and peaks at mitosis; the protein is degraded by the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C) following metaphase, allowing mitotic exit. Nuclear localization of cyclin B1 is controlled by phosphorylation of its cytoplasmic retention motif, ensuring timely CDK1 activation at the nucleus.

## Mechanism of Action

As the activating partner of [[CDK1]], CYCLIN B1 enables CDK1 to phosphorylate the diverse substrates that execute mitosis (nuclear envelope breakdown, spindle assembly, chromosome condensation). Critically for apoptosis control, the CDK1–CYCLIN B1 complex phosphorylates [[Caspase-2]] at Ser308, a site that blocks caspase-2 maturation. This couples mitotic entry to apoptotic restraint: actively dividing cells keep caspase-2 off, while loss of cyclin B1 at mitotic exit (or under genotoxic arrest) relieves this inhibition and permits caspase-2–driven death.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Precise cyclin B1 oscillation is essential for genomic stability. In [[Cancer]], cyclin B1 is frequently overexpressed or dysregulated, sustaining CDK1 activity, chromosomal instability, and resistance to death — features tied to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. High CYCLIN B1 correlates with advanced stage, high grade, and poor prognosis in breast, lung, and cervical carcinomas. Its anti-apoptotic phosphorylation of caspase-2 can be co-opted by tumors to survive therapeutic stress.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Cyclin B1/CDK1 is targeted by CDK inhibitors and by mitotic poisons such as [[docetaxel]] and [[paclitaxel]], which disrupt spindle function and trap cells in a lethal mitotic state once cyclin B1–CDK1 checkpoints fail. Combining CDK4/6 or CDK1 inhibition with agents that activate caspase-2 is aStrategy to convert arrest into death. CYCLIN B1 levels serve as a proliferation and prognostic biomarker, and APC/C activators that force cyclin B1 degradation are being investigated to induce mitotic catastrophe in resistant tumors.
