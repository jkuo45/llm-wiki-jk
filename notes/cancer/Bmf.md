# Bmf

**Bmf** (Bcl-2-modifying factor) is a [[Bcl-2 family|BH3-only protein]] that initiates the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]] by activating [[Bax]] and [[Bak]] and antagonizing anti-apoptotic proteins such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL. It is unusual among BH3-only proteins in being constitutively expressed and held inactive at the cytoskeleton until stress releases it.

## Structure & Cytoskeletal Sequestration

Bmf contains a BH3 domain and a C-terminal [[Actin]]-binding motif (Dynactin1/p62 interaction). In healthy cells, Bmf is tethered to the actin/myosin V motor complex, keeping it away from mitochondria. Detachment — triggered by loss of cell anchorage (anoikis), cytoskeleton disruption, or death signals — liberates Bmf to engage the apoptotic machinery.

## Regulation by ERK2 Phosphorylation

A central tumor-relevant control is **[[ERK2]] [[Phosphorylation|phosphorylation]] at Ser77**, which inhibits Bmf's pro-apoptotic function. ERK-mediated phosphorylation promotes Bmf retention at the cytoskeleton and impairs its ability to activate Bax/Bak, thereby suppressing apoptosis. Oncogenic [[RAS]]/ERK signaling (common in carcinomas) thus neutralizes Bmf, fostering survival and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance.

## Physiological Function

Bmf enforces apoptosis upon loss of attachment (anoikis) and during hematopoietic homeostasis, preventing survival of mislocalized cells — a natural barrier to [[Metastasis]]. It collaborates with [[Bim]] and [[Bik]] to set the death threshold.

## Cancer Relevance

- **Metastatic Suppression**: By killing detached cells, Bmf restrains dissemination; its inactivation by ERK2 aids [[EMT]] and metastasis.
- **Therapeutic Vulnerability**: MEK/ERK inhibitors stabilize Bmf, restoring anoikis; [[BH3 mimetics]] can bypass the need for Bmf entirely.
- **Biomarker**: ERK activation status predicts Bmf silencing and apoptotic priming.
