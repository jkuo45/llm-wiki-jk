# CDK1

**CDK1** (Cyclin-dependent kinase 1, also called CDC2) is the central kinase governing eukaryotic cell division. In complex with [[CYCLIN B1]], it forms the maturation-promoting factor (MPF) that drives the G2/M transition and orchestrates mitosis. Beyond proliferation, CDK1 tempers cell death by phosphorylating [[Caspase-2]] at Ser308, inhibiting caspase-2 activation during mitosis — a safeguard that prevents accidental apoptosis in dividing cells.

## Structure & Regulation

CDK1 is a 34-kDa serine/threonine kinase whose activity is controlled by cyclin binding and inhibitory phosphorylation. In interphase, Wee1/Myt1 kinases phosphorylate CDK1 on Tyr15 and Thr14, holding it inactive. Entry into mitosis requires dephosphorylation by the CDC25 phosphatase, allowing the CDK1–[[CYCLIN B1]] complex to phosphorylate hundreds of substrates controlling nuclear envelope breakdown, spindle assembly, and chromosome condensation. CDK1 is itself kept in check by CDK inhibitors (e.g., p21^CIP1^, p27^KIP1^) and by the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C), which later degrades cyclin B1 to exit mitosis.

## Mechanism of Action

A notable non-mitotic substrate is [[Caspase-2]]. CDK1–[[CYCLIN B1]] phosphorylates caspase-2 on Ser308 within its catalytic domain, a modification that blocks caspase-2 maturation and apoptotic commitment. This couples cell-cycle position to death licensing: as long as cells are proliferating under CDK1 control, caspase-2 stays inert; only when CDK1 activity falls (e.g., during genotoxic arrest) can caspase-2 activate and trigger apoptosis. Thus CDK1 acts as a mitotic anti-apoptotic gatekeeper.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

CDK1 is essential for viability and is tightly linked to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]], particularly sustained proliferative signaling and evasion of [[Apoptosis]]. Many tumors overexpress cyclin B1 or disable the checkpoint kinases that restrain CDK1, producing chromosomal instability and mitotic catastrophe when stressed. Conversely, the failure to suppress caspase-2 appropriately can sensitize dividing cancer cells to death. CDK1 hyperactivity also fuels the amplification of centrosomes and multipolar spindles seen in aggressive carcinomas.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

CDK1 is a long-standing oncology target. Although pan-CDK inhibitors (e.g., roscovitine, dinaciclib) have modest selectivity, newer approaches combine CDK4/6 inhibitors with agents that unleash caspase-2. The CDK1–caspase-2 axis explains why mitotic poisons such as [[docetaxel]] and [[paclitaxel]] can be antagonized by checkpoint activation, and why disabling CDK1 under genotoxic stress converts arrest into death. Biomarkers of CDK1/CYCLIN B1 abundance inform prognostic stratification in several carcinomas.
