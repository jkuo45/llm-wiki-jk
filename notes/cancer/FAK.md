# FAK

Focal Adhesion Kinase (FAK), also known as PTK2, is a non-receptor cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase that functions as a central integrator of signals from the extracellular matrix (ECM) to the cytoskeleton and nucleus. Encoded by the *PTK2* gene, FAK is recruited to integrin-containing focal adhesions where it transduces mechanical and chemical cues into downstream pathways controlling cell survival, proliferation, migration, and invasion.

## Structure & Domains

FAK is organized into an N-terminal FERM (band 4.1, ezrin, radixin, moesin) domain that binds integrin β-subunits and growth-factor receptors, a central catalytic kinase domain containing the autophosphorylation site Tyr397, and a C-terminal FAT (focal adhesion targeting) domain that mediates binding to paxillin, talin, and p130Cas. Autophosphorylation of Tyr397 creates a high-affinity binding site for the Src family kinases, and the resulting FAK–Src complex phosphorylates additional substrates (Tyr576/577 in the activation loop, Tyr861, Tyr925) that amplify pro-migratory and pro-survival signaling.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

FAK is activated downstream of [[Integrins]], receptor tyrosine kinases ([[EGFR]], [[HER2]]), and [[GPCR]]s. Once phosphorylated, FAK engages several axes: the [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] pathway (promoting survival and growth), the [[MAPK]]/ERK cascade (proliferation), and the Rho GTPase network (migration and cytoskeletal remodeling). FAK also cross-talks with [[c-Met]] and [[VEGF]] receptors to coordinate [[Angiogenesis]] and [[Metastasis]]. Its scaffolding role at focal adhesions is critical for the turnover of adhesion sites during cell motility.

## Physiological Function

In normal tissues FAK is essential for embryonic development, tissue repair, and immune cell trafficking. Its kinase activity is tightly regulated by adhesion-dependent localization, preventing inappropriate proliferation.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

FAK is overexpressed or hyperactivated in numerous malignancies, including [[Breast Cancer]], [[Glioblastoma]], [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], and [[Ovarian Cancer]], where it drives invasion, anoikis resistance, and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance. Elevated FAK correlates with poor prognosis and advanced stage. FAK is a validated therapeutic target: small-molecule inhibitors (defactinib, PF-562271, VS-6063) and antisense approaches are in clinical evaluation, often combined with [[ immunotherapy ] or cytotoxic agents to overcome resistance. FAK inhibition can also restrain [[Tumor Microenvironment]] stiffness and dissemination.

## Connections

- [[Integrins]]: Upstream mechanotransducers that recruit and activate FAK at focal adhesions.
- [[PI3K]]: Major survival/growth axis activated downstream of FAK.
- [[MAPK]]: Proliferative ERK cascade engaged by FAK–Src complexes.
- [[Metastasis]]: FAK-driven migration and invasion underpin dissemination.
- [[Angiogenesis]]: FAK cooperates with VEGF signaling in neovessel formation.
- [[Glioblastoma]]: Aggressive tumor with frequent FAK hyperactivation.
- [[HER2]]: Receptor tyrosine kinase that converges on FAK signaling.
