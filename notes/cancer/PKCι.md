# PKCι

Protein kinase C iota (PKCι) is an atypical isoform of the [[Protein Kinase C]] family, closely related to [[PKCζ]], that is activated independently of calcium and diacylglycerol. Accumulating evidence identifies PKCι as a candidate [[Oncogene]] in several human cancers, where it drives survival, proliferation, and invasion. PKCι phosphorylates and inactivates the pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 family protein [[Bad]], promoting cell survival.

## Structure & Mechanism

PKCι shares the atypical PKC architecture: an N-terminal PB1 domain (interacting with Par6 and p62/[[SQSTM1]]), a C1 domain without functional DAG-binding cysteines, and a C-terminal catalytic domain. PKCι is activated downstream of Rac1/Cdc42 and phospholipase D/phosphatidic acid and is a core component of the Par6 polarity complex. It phosphorylates [[Bad]] at Ser112, Ser136, and Ser155, blocking Bad's interaction with anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 proteins and thereby inhibiting apoptosis. PKCι also activates NF-κB and supports anchorage-independent growth.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

In normal cells, PKCι contributes to cell polarity and mitotic spindle function. In [[Cancer]], PKCι is amplified or overexpressed in lung (especially non-small cell), ovarian, breast, and pancreatic carcinomas and is linked to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]—sustained proliferation, evasion of apoptosis, and [[Metastasis]]. By phosphorylating [[Bad]], PKCι enforces a survival signal that protects tumor cells from [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Chemotherapy]]-induced death. PKCι also reinforces [[EMT]] and invasive phenotypes.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

PKCι is investigated as a biomarker of aggressive disease and as a therapeutic target. Inhibitors such as aurothiomalate (ATM), CRT0066101, and isoform-selective agents reduce tumor growth and overcome apoptosis resistance in preclinical models. Combining PKCι inhibition with standard [[Chemotherapy]] is a strategy to disable survival signaling in PKCι-driven tumors.
