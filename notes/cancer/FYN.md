# FYN

FYN is a Src family non-receptor tyrosine kinase (SFK) encoded by the *FYN* gene. Together with Src, Yes, and Lyn, FYN transduces signals from receptors and adhesion complexes to control proliferation, survival, migration, and immune regulation. FYN is notable in cancer for its ability to phosphorylate and inhibit the apical [[Apoptosis]] initiator [[Caspase-8]].

## Structure & Domains

Like other SFKs, FYN contains an N-terminal myristoylation/palmitoylation motif for membrane targeting, an SH3 domain, an SH2 domain that binds phosphotyrosines, a catalytic SH1 kinase domain, and a C-terminal regulatory tail with Tyr531 (mouse numbering; Tyr528 human) whose phosphorylation by Csk keeps the kinase closed/inactive. Dephosphorylation or disruption of this tail activates FYN.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

FYN relays signals from receptor tyrosine kinases ([[EGFR]], [[HER2]]), [[Integrins]], and immune receptors into the [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], [[MAPK]]/ERK, and STAT pathways. Critically, in [[Colon Cancer]] and other tumors FYN phosphorylates [[Caspase-8]] on Tyr397 (Tyr380 in certain contexts), which blocks caspase-8 proteolytic activation and dampens death-receptor-mediated apoptosis. This places FYN as a brake on extrinsic cell death, complementing its pro-survival signaling.

## Physiological Function

FYN regulates T-cell receptor signaling, neuronal development, and cytoskeletal dynamics. Its redundancy with Src means single knockouts are viable, but combinatorial loss is embryonic lethal.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

FYN is amplified or activated in several cancers, including [[Colon Cancer]], [[Breast Cancer]], and [[Glioblastoma]], where it supports invasion, survival, and therapy resistance. By inhibiting Caspase-8, FYN contributes to resistance against death-receptor agonists and [[Chemotherapy]]. FYN also cooperates with [[EMT]] programs to enable [[Metastasis]]. SFK inhibitors (dasatinib, saracatinib, bosutinib) inhibit FYN and can restore apoptosis sensitivity, and are being tested in combination regimens. Targeting FYN–Caspase-8 crosstalk is an active area for sensitizing resistant tumors.
