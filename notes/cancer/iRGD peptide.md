# iRGD peptide

The **iRGD peptide** (cyclic CRGDKGPDC) is a tumor-penetrating peptide engineered from the vascular homing sequence of the fibronectin type III repeat 10 (the RGD motif) fused to a CendR (C-end Rule) motif. It enhances the delivery of co-administered drugs and nanoparticles by a two-step mechanism: first binding αv-class [[Integrins]] on tumor vasculature and disrupted tumor vessels, then undergoing proteolytic processing that unmasks the CendR sequence, which engages [[Neuropilin-1]] to mediate extravasation and deep tissue penetration.

## Structure & Design

iRGD is a 9-amino-acid cyclic disulfide-bridged peptide (Cys-Arg-Gly-Asp-Lys-Gly-Pro-Asp-Cys). The internal RGD tripeptide confers αvβ3/αvβ5 [[Integrins]] binding, while the C-terminal R/KXXR-style sequence becomes the active CendR motif after cleavage by tumor-associated proteases (e.g., furin, elastase, MMPs) between the RGD and CendR portions. The exposed CendR motif (e.g., RGDK/R) binds Neuropilin-1 with high affinity, triggering an endocytic/transcytotic transport that carries attached or co-injected cargo into the tumor parenchyma.

## Mechanism of Action

Systemically administered iRGD accumulates at tumor vessels through integrin binding. Tumor-selective proteolysis then generates the CendR ligand, which binds Neuropilin-1 on endothelial and tumor cells and activates an endothelium-crossing pathway. This "homing-and-penetration" cascade increases vascular permeability specifically within tumors, improving the distribution of concurrently delivered chemotherapeutics (e.g., [[Doxorubicin]], [[Paclitaxel]]), antibodies, and nanoparticle formulations well beyond the perivascular rim.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because the CendR activation depends on tumor-associated proteolysis and Neuropilin-1 overexpression, iRGD acts preferentially in malignant tissue, sparing normal vasculature. It has been shown to increase intratumoral drug concentrations and antitumor efficacy while reducing systemic toxicity in models of breast, pancreatic, and [[Lung Cancer]], and to improve penetration of poorly permeable agents such as [[Trastuzumab]] and Doxil.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

iRGD is a leading example of tumor-penetrating peptide technology and is being advanced in preclinical and early clinical development, often combined with established [[Chemotherapy]] or antibody-drug conjugates. Variants and chimeras (iRGD linked directly to payloads) are under study to exploit the same integrin→Neuropilin-1 handoff for imaging and targeted delivery.
