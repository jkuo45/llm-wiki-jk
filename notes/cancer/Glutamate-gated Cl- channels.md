# Glutamate-gated Cl- channels

Glutamate-gated chloride channels (GluCls) are ligand-gated ion channels belonging to the Cys-loop receptor superfamily. Present in invertebrates (nematodes, insects, arthropods) but absent from vertebrates, GluCls are the principal molecular target of the anthelmintic [[Ivermectin]] and related [[Avermectin]]s. Their absence in humans is a key reason for the selective safety of these drugs.

## Structure & Mechanism

GluCls are pentameric channels assembled from subunits such as GluClα and GluClβ. Binding of glutamate (or, at much higher potency, ivermectin) opens the pore, permitting chloride influx that hyperpolarizes the neuron or muscle cell membrane and silences electrical activity. Ivermectin acts as a potent allosteric agonist, locking the channel open at concentrations far below those needed for vertebrate receptors. This causes flaccid paralysis and starvation of the parasite.

## Why Vertebrates Are Spared

Human cells lack GluCls; instead, inhibitory neurotransmission uses [[GABA]]_A receptors and glycine receptors. Although ivermectin can potentiate some vertebrate GABA_A and glycine receptors at very high concentrations, therapeutic plasma levels are far below the threshold for clinically significant central effects — explaining the wide therapeutic index. This selectivity underpins the massive safe use of ivermectin in the [[Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis]] and onchocerciasis control.

## Relevance to Cancer Research

The GluCl mechanism is central to understanding ivermectin's safety margin when repurposed as an [[Anticancer]] agent. In mammalian tumors, ivermectin does not act via GluCls; rather, it engages host targets such as [[PAK1]], [[YAP1]], and the [[Akt]]/[[mTOR]] axis, inducing [[Apoptosis]] and [[Autophagy]] and inhibiting [[Angiogenesis]]. Recognizing that GluCls are invertebrate-specific clarifies why ivermectin's anticancer effects in humans arise from mammalian, not parasitic, pathways.
