# OCP

The **Onchocerciasis Control Programme in West Africa (OCP)** was a landmark international public-health campaign (1974–2002) that controlled onchocerciasis (river blindness) across 11 West African countries. Commanded by the [[World Health Organization]] with support from the World Bank, UNDP, and donor agencies, it became a model for large-scale neglected-tropical-disease control.

## Strategy and Operations

The OCP initially relied on **vector control** — aerial larviciding of rivers to kill blackfly ([[Simulium]]) larvae — to interrupt transmission. From the late 1980s it added **mass drug administration** of [[Ivermectin]] (donated as [[Mectizan]] by Merck), which suppressed microfilariasis in endemic populations. The integrated vector-plus-chemotherapy approach drove transmission below sustainable levels across the original participating countries.

## Significance for Cancer/Therapy Research

Although OCP targeted a parasitic disease, its infrastructure and the donated-drug model directly enabled the oncology-relevant repurposing research surrounding ivermectin. The vast safety and pharmacokinetic experience generated by OCP — and by successor programs (APOC, ONCHO) — underpins contemporary investigations of [[Ivermectin]] as an anti-cancer agent (e.g., in [[Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma]] and [[ovarian cancer]]), where its PAK1-inhibitory and [[Akt]]/[[mTOR]]-modulating activities are studied.

## Outcomes

The OCP protected tens of millions from blindness and severe morbidity, freed arable land for cultivation, and demonstrated that community-directed treatment with ivermectin is logistically and economically viable. It laid the foundation for the global elimination agenda now coordinated by the WHO.
