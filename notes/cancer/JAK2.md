# JAK2

**Janus kinase 2 (JAK2)** is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase of the JAK family that couples type I/II cytokine receptors to the [[JAK-STAT signaling pathway|JAK/STAT]] cascade. Upon ligand binding, JAK2 trans-phosphorylates its receptor and downstream STAT transcription factors (notably [[STAT3]] and STAT5), orchestrating gene programs for proliferation, survival, differentiation, and [[Inflammation]]. Dysregulated JAK2 activity is a driver in hematologic malignancies and supports numerous solid tumors.

## Structure & Activation

JAK2 contains seven JAK-homology (JH) domains: the C-terminal JH1 is the kinase domain, JH2 is a pseudokinase domain that autoregulates JH1, and JH3–JH7 mediate receptor association. In the resting state the pseudokinase domain restrains the kinase. Ligand engagement (e.g., by erythropoietin, growth hormone, or IL-6 family cytokines) induces receptor dimerization, juxtaposing JAK2 molecules to enable trans-activation phosphorylation of activation-loop tyrosines in JH1.

## Mechanism of Action

Activated JAK2 phosphorylates STAT proteins on conserved tyrosine residues; phosphorylated STATs dimerize via SH2 domains and translocate to the nucleus to induce transcription of anti-apoptotic ([[BCL-XL]], [[Mcl-1]]) and proliferative (cyclin D) genes. JAK2 also cross-talks with [[PI3K]]/AKT and [[MAPK]]/ERK cascades, reinforcing survival signals and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance. Aberrant activation—through mutation, translocation, or autocrine cytokine loops—locks cells into a proliferative state.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

The somatic **JAK2 V617F** mutation (a constitutively active JH2-disrupting substitution) is present in the majority of myeloproliferative neoplasms, including polycythemia vera, essential thrombocythemia, and primary myelofibrosis, and is a classic driver of [[Leukemia]]-related clones. In solid tumors, JAK2/STAT3 overactivation fuels [[EMT]], [[Metastasis]], and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment. The peptide [[melittin]] has been shown to inhibit JAK2/[[STAT3]] signaling in [[ovarian cancer]], illustrating a natural-product route to pathway suppression.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

JAK2 is a validated therapeutic target. Ruxolitinib and fedratinib (JAK1/2 inhibitors) are approved for myelofibrosis and polycythemia vera, alleviating splenomegaly and inflammatory symptoms. In solid tumors, JAK/STAT blockade is investigated to overcome resistance and restore immune surveillance, often combined with checkpoint inhibitors. Monitoring JAK2 V617F allele burden guides myeloproliferative neoplasm management.
