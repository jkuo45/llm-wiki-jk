# irinotecan

**Irinotecan** (brand name Camptosar; generic CPT-11) is a semisynthetic camptothecin analog and a flagship cytotoxic agent in the treatment of [[Colorectal Cancer]] and small cell lung cancer. It is a water-soluble prodrug that is converted in vivo to the active metabolite **SN-38**, a potent inhibitor of [[DNA Topoisomerase I]]. By trapping the topoisomerase I–DNA cleavage complex, SN-38 converts normally tolerable DNA single-strand breaks into catastrophic lesions during replication, triggering [[Apoptosis]].

## Chemistry & Pharmacokinetics

Irinotecan is a camptothecin derivative bearing a bispiperidine side chain that improves aqueous solubility. After intravenous administration it is hydrolyzed by carboxylesterases—primarily in liver and intestine—to SN-38, which is 100–1000-fold more potent. SN-38 is inactivated by hepatic UDP-glucuronosyltransferase 1A1 ([[UGT1A1]]) to the poorly active SN-38G. Genetic variants in [[UGT1A1]] (notably the *28 promoter polymorphism) reduce clearance, increasing severe neutropenia and diarrhea risk and necessitating dose adjustment.

## Mechanism of Action

Topoisomerase I relieves torsional stress during transcription and replication by creating transient single-strand breaks via a covalent DNA–enzyme intermediate. Irinotecan/SN-38 stabilizes this "cleavable complex," preventing religation. When a replication fork collides with the trapped complex, the single-strand break is converted into a double-strand break, activating ATM/ATR and p53-dependent [[Apoptosis]] and cell-cycle arrest. This makes proliferating tumor cells preferentially vulnerable.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Irinotecan is a cornerstone of metastatic colorectal cancer, used alone or in combinations such as FOLFIRI (with [[5-Fluorouracil]]/leucovorin) and often paired with anti-angiogenic or anti-EGFR antibodies. It is also standard in extensive-stage small cell lung cancer and used in gastric, pancreatic, and cervical cancers. Its efficacy is limited by acquired resistance (topoisomerase I downregulation, enhanced DNA repair, drug efflux via ABC transporters) and by gastrointestinal toxicity.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Beyond conventional use, irinotecan is being explored in rational combinations, including with BH3 mimetics such as [[ABT-263]] (navitoclax) in early-phase trials for solid tumors, where topoisomerase poisoning and Bcl-2 family inhibition are intended to synergize in apoptosis induction. Liposomal and antibody-conjugated formulations aim to improve the therapeutic index.
