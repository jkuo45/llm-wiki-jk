# CK1

**CK1** (Casein kinase 1) is a family of highly conserved serine/threonine protein kinases (isoforms α, β, γ1–3, δ, ε) that phosphorylate a remarkably broad set of substrates involved in vesicle trafficking, circadian rhythms, Wnt/β-catenin signaling, and apoptosis. In the apoptotic context, CK1 phosphorylates the BH3-only protein [[Bid]] to inhibit its cleavage by [[Caspase-8]], thereby blocking the mitochondrial amplification of the extrinsic death pathway.

## Structure & Isoforms

Individual CK1 isoforms share an N-terminal catalytic domain and a C-terminal regulatory tail containing autophosphorylation sites that modulate substrate access. Unlike many kinases, CK1 often requires a "priming" phosphate on a neighboring residue (generated by another kinase) to dock and phosphorylate its target — a hallmark of its substrate specificity. This priming dependence lets CK1 act downstream of diverse signaling inputs.

## Mechanism of Action

Upon death-receptor activation, [[Caspase-8]] cleaves [[Bid]] to generate tBid, which translocates to mitochondria, oligomerizes [[BAX]]/[[BAK]], and triggers cytochrome c release — coupling extrinsic and intrinsic apoptosis. CK1 phosphorylates Bid at sites that render it a poor substrate for caspase-8 cleavage, blunting tBid formation. By suppressing this [[Bid]]→mitochondria feed-forward loop, CK1 attenuates the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] triggered by extrinsic signals, functioning as an apoptotic brake. CK1 can also phosphorylate other apoptotic regulators (e.g., pivotal components of the β-catenin destruction complex), linking proliferation and death control.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

CK1 isoforms are ubiquitously expressed and essential for development. Their anti-apoptotic phosphorylation of Bid promotes cell survival under sub-lethal stress but, when overactive in tumors, can confer resistance to death-receptor–mediated immune killing (e.g., TRAIL/FasL). Conversely, loss or inhibition of CK1 can sensitize cancer cells to extrinsic apoptosis. CK1 also regulates [[β-catenin]], implicating it in WNT-driven oncogenesis (e.g., colon cancer), illustrating its dual role in proliferation and death.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

CK1 inhibitors (e.g., D4476, PF-4800567) are explored as sensitizers that restore Bid cleavage and mitochondrial apoptosis in resistant cancers, and as modulators of WNT signaling. Because CK1 phosphorylates many substrates, selectivity across isoforms is a key drug-design challenge. Biomarker studies link CK1δ/ε overexpression to poor prognosis in several carcinomas, supporting its evaluation as a therapeutic node where inhibiting survival phosphorylation could enhance [[Chemotherapy]] or immunotherapy efficacy.
