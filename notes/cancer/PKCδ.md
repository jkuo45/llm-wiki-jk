# PKCδ

Protein kinase C delta (PKCδ) is a member of the novel (calcium-independent, diacylglycerol-responsive) subfamily of [[Protein Kinase C]] serine/threonine kinases. Unlike classic isoforms, PKCδ is activated by diacylglycerol (DAG) and phospholipids without requiring calcium, and it is uniquely implicated as a pro-apoptotic kinase. PKCδ links [[Oxidative Stress]], growth factor withdrawal, and death-receptor signaling to the execution of [[Apoptosis]].

## Structure & Mechanism

PKCδ comprises an N-terminal regulatory region (C1 domain with DAG/phorbol ester-binding cysteine-rich motifs, and a C2 domain) and a C-terminal catalytic kinase domain. Upon activation, PKCδ translocates to membranes, mitochondria, and nuclei. A distinctive feature is its own proteolytic cleavage by [[Caspase-3]] during apoptosis, which generates a constitutively active catalytic fragment that amplifies the death signal—a feed-forward loop. PKCδ also directly phosphorylates [[Caspase-3]] to regulate its activity and can phosphorylate substrates such as [[FOXO]] transcription factors and [[p38 MAPK]] pathway components.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

PKCδ is a context-dependent regulator: in many epithelial, hematopoietic, and neuronal cells it promotes [[Apoptosis]] in response to genotoxic stress, UV, and [[Oxidative Stress]]; in other settings (e.g., certain fibroblasts and vascular smooth muscle) it can drive proliferation. In [[Cancer]], PKCδ can act as a tumor suppressor by enforcing apoptosis, but its apoptotic fragment may also contribute to chemotherapy responses. Loss or silencing of PKCδ is associated with survival advantage in some tumors, supporting the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] evasion of cell death.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

PKCδ is a candidate biomarker for apoptotic competence and a target for modulating therapy response. Inhibitors (e.g., rottlerin, though non-specific) and activators are studied to tune apoptotic sensitivity. Its cleavage by [[Caspase-3]] makes PKCδ a useful experimental readout of caspase activation, and its dual role is relevant to designing [[Chemotherapy]] regimens that exploit apoptotic priming.
