# Lung Cancer

**Lung cancer** is the most frequently diagnosed cancer and the leading cause of cancer-related death globally. It is broadly divided into non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC; ~85%, including adenocarcinoma, squamous, and large-cell) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC; ~15%). Driven predominantly by tobacco carcinogens but increasingly by targetable mutations (EGFR, ALK, KRAS, ROS1) and immune-checkpoint biology, lung cancer remains a major focus of both targeted and repurposed therapeutics—including [[Ivermectin]], which inhibits proliferation and [[Metastasis]] through several nodes.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Carcinogenesis follows accumulated mutations in oncogenes and [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]]s (e.g., TP53, RB1, LKB1) compounded by [[Inflammation]] and [[Oxidative Stress]]. Histologic and molecular subtyping guides therapy: EGFR-mutant NSCLC is treated with EGFR inhibitors such as [[erlotinib]], while immunotherapy benefits a subset of patients. Metastatic dissemination is facilitated by [[EMT]], in which epithelial cells acquire mesenchymal, invasive traits.

## Role of Ivermectin

Preclinical studies show ivermectin inhibits lung cancer cell proliferation and spread through multiple mechanisms:
- **YAP1 Inhibition:** Ivermectin suppresses [[YAP1]] activity (a Hippo-pathway effector driving proliferation), reducing growth in cells such as H1299.
- **EGFR Targeting:** Combined with erlotinib, ivermectin achieves synergistic killing by regulating [[EGFR]] signaling.
- **EMT Inhibition:** Ivermectin attenuates EMT, lowering metastatic capacity.
- **HSP27 Inhibition:** It inhibits phosphorylation of [[HSP27]], a small heat-shock protein that supports survival and anti-EGFR drug resistance, thereby sensitizing cells to EGFR-targeted agents.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

These findings position ivermectin as a candidate adjuvant that could potentiate existing targeted drugs and curb metastasis, an area of active [[Drug Repositioning]]. Combination with erlotinib and other TKIs is of particular interest in EGFR-driven NSCLC. Validation in clinical cohorts and formulation for lung-tissue delivery remain research priorities.
