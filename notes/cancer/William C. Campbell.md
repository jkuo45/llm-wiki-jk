# William C. Campbell

**William C. Campbell** (born 1930, Ramelton, Ireland) is an Irish-American biologist and parasitologist renowned for the discovery of the **avermectins**, a class of antiparasitic compounds from *Streptomyces* bacteria that led to [[Ivermectin]]. For this work he shared the **2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** with Satoshi Ōmura (who isolated the producing strain) and Youyou Tu (artemisinin).

## Scientific Contribution

Working at [[Merck & Co. Inc]], Campbell built on Ōmura's collection of *Streptomyces avermitilis* strains. Culture broths yielded avermectin, whose semisynthetic derivative **ivermectin** proved extraordinarily effective against a broad range of nematodes and arthropods. Ivermectin binds glutamate-gated chloride channels in invertebrates, causing paralyzing hyperpolarization and death — selective because vertebrates lack these channels. It became the cornerstone therapy for onchocerciasis, strongyloidiasis, lymphatic filariasis, scabies, and ectoparasitoses.

## Link to Cancer and Global Health

Although an antiparasitic, ivermectin has since been found to inhibit multiple oncology-relevant pathways — including the [[Wnt]]/β-catenin, [[YAP1]], and PAK1 axes — and is under investigation as a repurposed anticancer agent. Campbell's discovery therefore bridges tropical disease control and cancer pharmacology. Mass drug administration of ivermectin, coordinated with the [[World Health Organization]] and [[TDR]], has prevented blindness and disability in millions, indirectly reducing chronic infection-driven [[Inflammation]] that can predispose to malignancy.

## Recognition

Campbell received numerous honors besides the Nobel, including membership in the National Academy of Sciences. His career exemplifies the translational arc from soil microbe to globally deployed medicine.
