# RAF1

RAF1, commonly called C-Raf, is a serine/threonine kinase and a central node of the [[MAPK]]/ERK signaling cascade. Together with A-Raf and B-Raf, RAF1 relays signals from activated Ras through MEK to ERK, controlling proliferation, differentiation, and survival. RAF1 is implicated in many [[Cancer]] types, particularly where upstream [[RAS]] or receptor tyrosine kinase activation drives the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]].

## Structure & Mechanism

RAF1 comprises an N-terminal Ras-binding domain, a regulatory region with 14-3-3 binding sites, and a C-terminal kinase domain. Upon growth-factor stimulation, active Ras-GTP recruits RAF1 to the membrane, where phosphorylation (by Src, PAK, and others) and 14-3-3 displacement activate it. RAF1 phosphorylates MEK1/2, propagating ERK activation. In the death-regulation arm, RAF1 phosphorylates the pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 family protein [[Bad]] at Ser112, neutralizing Bad's ability to liberate [[Bax]]/Bak and thereby inhibiting [[Apoptosis]] and promoting survival.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, RAF1 couples mitogenic cues to cell-cycle entry and stress survival. Pathologically, upstream [[RAS]] or RTK mutations lock RAF1 signaling on, sustaining proliferation and blocking apoptosis. While B-Raf V600E is the more famous oncogenic event, RAF1 amplification and dimerization-driven activation also occur in malignancies. The anti-apoptotic phosphorylation of [[Bad]] is one mechanism by which RAF1 enforces the survival phenotype of transformed cells.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

RAF1/MEK/ERK is a major targeted-therapy axis; MEK inhibitors (trametinib) and ERK inhibitors are used or in trials, especially in RAS/BRAF-driven tumors. Understanding RAF1's role in Bad phosphorylation informs why survival signaling must be co-targeted with [[Chemotherapy]] to induce durable apoptosis.
