# Mectizan

**Mectizan** is the trade name under which [[Merck & Co. Inc]] markets [[Ivermectin]] for human use, most prominently in the treatment and control of [[Onchocerciasis]] (river blindness) and, in combination regimens, [[Lymphatic Filariasis]]. Chemically identical to veterinary/antiparasitic ivermectin, Mectizan is an orally administered macrocyclic lactone that targets invertebrate glutamate-gated chloride channels. Its human-health legacy is inseparable from the [[Mectizan Donation Program]], which supplies it free of charge to endemic populations.

## Chemistry & Pharmacological Action

Mectizan/ivermectin is a semisynthetic avermectin derivative (a 22,23-dihydro analog of avermectin B1). It binds glutamate-gated chloride channels and, at higher concentrations, GABA-gated channels in nematodes and arthropods, increasing membrane permeability to chloride, causing flaccid paralysis and death of parasites. In mammalian cells, off-target effects include modulation of chloride flux, [[ROS]] generation, and interference with oncogenic signaling ([[YAP1]], [[EGFR]]), the basis for repurposing interest.

## Mechanism in Parasitosis

A single annual dose clears skin [[Microfilariae]] (the larval stage of *Onchocerca volvulus*), arresting transmission and preventing the inflammatory blindness and skin disease of onchocerciasis. It is microfilaricidal rather than reliably adulticidal, so repeated dosing is required to suppress the reservoir.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Through mass administration, Mectizan prevents the morbidity that characterizes filarial disease. Although a parasiticidal product, the molecule's broad mechanistic reach has made it a subject of [[Drug Repositioning]] in [[Cancer]]—studied for inhibiting proliferation and [[Metastasis]] in [[Lung Cancer]] and for selective cytotoxicity in [[Leukemia]]—linking a tropical-disease medicine to oncology.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Mectizan remains essential to WHO elimination goals and is among the most widely distributed donated medicines in history. Its extensive safety database supports exploratory oncology trials and combination studies. Formulation and dosing for cancer indications (where higher, sustained exposure may be needed) are active research questions distinct from the single-dose antiparasitic paradigm.
