# Wnt

**Wnt** signaling comprises a family of evolutionary conserved pathways initiated by secreted Wnt glycoproteins binding Frizzled/LRP receptors. The canonical **Wnt/β-catenin** arm controls gene transcription via β-catenin/TCF complexes and is pivotal in embryonic patterning, stem-cell maintenance, and tissue regeneration. Its dysregulation is among the most frequent events in human [[Cancer]].

## Pathway Mechanisms

In the OFF state, a destruction complex (APC, Axin, GSK-3β, CK1) phosphorylates β-catenin, marking it for [[Ubiquitination]] and proteasomal degradation. Wnt binding to Frizzled and LRP5/6 inhibits this complex; stabilized β-catenin accumulates, translocates to the nucleus, and with TCF/LEF factors drives targets such as *MYC*, *CCND1*, *AXIN2*, and *LGR5*. Non-canonical Wnt pathways (planar cell polarity, Wnt/Ca²⁺) regulate polarity and calcium flux independently of β-catenin.

## Role in Cancer

Canonical Wnt activation occurs via inactivating mutations in **APC** or **β-catenin (CTNNB1)** — the hallmark of [[Colorectal Cancer]] — and via upstream receptor/ligand overexpression in [[Breast Cancer]], hepatocellular, and other tumors. Constitutive Wnt signaling sustains cancer stem cells, proliferation, and [[EMT]], and confers therapy resistance. The pathway thus satisfies multiple [[Hallmarks of Cancer]].

## Therapeutic Targeting

Direct β-catenin/TCF inhibition has proven difficult; approaches include Porcupine (Wnt acylation) inhibitors, Frizzled antagonists, and anti-LRP5/6 antibodies. Notably, [[Ivermectin]] inhibits Wnt/β-catenin in breast and colorectal cancer models by blocking β-catenin–TCF interaction and downregulating AXIN2 and LGR5. Kinases such as [[PAK1]] can facilitate Wnt/β-catenin signaling, offering combinatorial nodes. Repurposing ivermectin illustrates cross-linkage between antiparasitic and oncology research.
