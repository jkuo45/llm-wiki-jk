# Microfilariae

**Microfilariae** are the early, motile larval stage in the life cycle of parasitic nematodes of the family Onchocercidae (the filarial worms). Released live by adult worms residing in lymphatic vessels or subcutaneous tissues, microfilariae circulate in the host's blood or migrate through the skin, where they are taken up by biting mosquitoes (or other vectors) to continue development—completing transmission. In disease, their density and persistence drive pathology and define therapeutic endpoints.

## Biology & Life Cycle

Adult female worms produce sheathed or unsheathed microfilariae that enter circulation, often with pronounced circadian periodicity matching vector feeding times (e.g., nocturnal for *Wuchereria bancrofti*). Inside the insect vector they molt to infective third-stage larvae, which are deposited on the skin and migrate to lymphatic or subcutaneous sites to mature. Thus microfilariae are both the disseminating stage within the host and the transmission stage to the environment.

## Physiology & Pathology

In [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] (caused by [[Wuchereria bancrofti]], [[Brugia malayi]], [[Brugia timori]]) and [[Onchocerciasis]] (caused by [[Onchocerca volvulus]]), living microfilariae provoke relatively little acute inflammation, but their death—whether spontaneous or drug-induced—releases antigens that incite [[Inflammation]], lymphatic endothelial injury, and fibrosis. Chronic infection leads to lymphedema, hydrocele, and skin changes; in onchocerciasis, dead microfilariae in the eye cause blindness.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Antifilarial therapy targets microfilariae: [[Ivermectin]] (marketed as [[Mectizan]]) rapidly clears skin microfilariae of onchocerciasis by paralyzing and enhancing host clearance, and [[Diethylcarbamazine]] kills circulating microfilariae in lymphatic filariasis. Measuring microfilarial burden (blood smear, skin snip, antigen assays) before and after treatment gauges success of mass-drug-administration programs such as the [[Mectizan Donation Program]]. While not a cancer entity, microfilariae connect to the broader ivermectin pharmacology relevant to [[Drug Repositioning]] in [[Cancer]].
