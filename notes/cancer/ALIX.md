# ALIX

**ALIX** (ALG-2-interacting protein X; gene *PDCD6IP*, also called programmed cell death 6-interacting protein) is a conserved cytosolic adaptor best known for its role in endosomal sorting and exosome biogenesis. Although originally named for an interaction with the apoptosis-linked protein ALG-2, ALIX itself is not a death executor; rather it is a hub that channels cargo into multivesicular bodies (MVBs) and extracellular vesicles.

## Structure & Domains

ALIX contains an N-terminal Bro1 domain (binds the ESCRT-III subunit CHMP4), a central V domain that binds syntenin and syndecan, and a C-terminal proline-rich region that interacts with ALG-2 and TSG101. This architecture allows ALIX to bridge the endosomal sorting complex required for transport (ESCRT) machinery with cargo and the cytoskeleton.

## Mechanism & Exosome Biogenesis

ALIX collaborates with TSG101 and the ESCRT-III complex to invaginate the MVB limiting membrane, packaging cargo — including [[PD-L1]], [[EGFR]], and tetraspanins — into intraluminal vesicles that are released as exosomes. By controlling which proteins are shed versus retained on the plasma membrane, ALIX shapes the tumor cell surface proteome.

## Physiological Function

ALIX is essential for embryonic development, viral budding (e.g., HIV-1), and the homeostatic turnover of membrane receptors. It maintains epithelial polarity and cytokinesis.

## Role in Cancer

- **EGFR Regulation**: In basal-like [[Breast Cancer]], loss of ALIX correlates with heightened [[EGFR]] signaling and proliferative drive, suggesting ALIX normally dampens receptor abundance via exosomal clearance.
- **Immune Evasion**: [[PD-L1]] is secreted through ALIX-dependent exosomes. Downregulation of ALIX retains PD-L1 on the cell surface, intensifying immunosuppression and aiding [[Tumor Immune Evasion]]. Thus ALIX acts as a contextual modulator of anti-tumor immunity.
- **Metastasis & EMT**: ALIX-supported exosome release can deliver pro-[[Metastasis]] and [[EMT]]-promoting cargo to the tumor microenvironment.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Modulating ALIX could either block immune-suppressive exosomes (enhancing checkpoint blockade) or, conversely, promote shedding of oncogenic receptors. Its dual role makes it a nuanced target in [[Cancer Immunotherapy]].
