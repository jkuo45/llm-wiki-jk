# c-CBL

**c-CBL** (Casitas B-lineage lymphoma proto-oncogene) is a RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase that functions as a critical negative regulator of receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) signaling. Originally identified as the cellular homolog of the retroviral *v-cbl* oncogene, c-CBL couples the activated state of upstream signaling receptors to their ubiquitination and eventual lysosomal or proteasomal degradation. Beyond its canonical role in downregulating growth factor receptors, c-CBL participates in apoptotic regulation, including [[ERK]]-dependent [[Bim]] degradation, linking survival signaling to the disposal of pro-apoptotic BH3-only proteins.

## Structure & Domains

c-CBL contains an N-terminal tyrosine kinase binding (TKB) domain composed of a four-helix bundle, a calcium-binding EF-hand, and a variant SH2 domain that together recognize phosphorylated tyrosine motifs (pTyr-X-Asn-X-X-pTyr) on activated receptors. The C-terminal half houses the RING finger domain, which recruits the E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme, and a proline-rich region that binds downstream adaptors such as CIN85 and endophilins. The RING domain is essential for E3 ligase activity; loss of this region converts c-CBL into a dominant-negative scaffold that sustains receptor signaling.

## Mechanism of Action

Following ligand binding, RTKs such as the epidermal growth factor receptor ([[EGFR]]), platelet-derived growth factor receptor, and stem cell factor receptor (c-KIT) are autophosphorylated on activation-loop tyrosines. c-CBL is recruited via its TKB domain, becomes tyrosine-phosphorylated, and catalyzes the covalent attachment of ubiquitin chains to the receptor. Polyubiquitinated receptors are sorted into multivesicular bodies and degraded, terminating proliferative and survival signals. In parallel, growth-factor-activated [[ERK]] signaling drives c-CBL to ubiquitinate the pro-apoptotic protein [[Bim]], marking it for proteasomal destruction and thereby reinforcing cell survival. This explains how sustained MAPK signaling protects tumor cells from apoptosis.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

As a tumor suppressor in its wild-type form, c-CBL restrains mitogenic signaling. However, somatic mutations, focal deletions, and promoter methylation of *CBL* occur in a range of malignancies — notably myelodysplastic syndromes, acute myeloid leukemia, and juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia — producing RING-domain-deficient or loss-of-function variants that fail to degrade RTKs. Such mutant c-CBL acts in a dominant-negative manner, causing accumulation of activated receptors, constitutive [[RAS]]-[[MAPK Signaling]], and heightened proliferation. Conversely, the viral *v-cbl* oncogene and certain C-terminal truncations generate constitutively active, scaffolding-dominant proteins that transform fibroblasts. CBL family redundancy with CBL-B and CBL-c modulates immune checkpoint signaling and T-cell tolerance as well.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

*CBL* mutation status is now incorporated into molecular profiling of myeloid neoplasms and informs risk stratification. Because CBL-deficient tumors depend on sustained RTK output, they may be sensitive to downstream pathway inhibition via [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors]] or [[MEK]]/ERK blockade. Research also exploits c-CBL's ligase activity in targeted protein degradation strategies. The balance between c-CBL-mediated receptor downregulation and its anti-apoptotic effects on [[Bim]] makes it a nuanced node where restoring Bim levels could resensitize resistant cancers to [[Chemotherapy]].
