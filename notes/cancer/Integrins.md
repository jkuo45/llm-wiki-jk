# Integrins

**Integrins** are a family of evolutionarily conserved, non-covalently associated α/β heterodimeric transmembrane receptors that physically and biochemically couple the extracellular matrix ([[ECM]]) to the intracellular actin cytoskeleton. They are central to cell adhesion, migration, proliferation, and survival, and they are pivotal in [[Cancer]] progression, where they govern [[Angiogenesis]], invasive migration, and [[Metastasis]].

## Structure & Diversity

The human integrin family comprises 18 α and 8 β subunits that pair to form 24 distinct receptors, each with defined ligand specificity. The extracellular headpiece (composed of the α I-domain or β propeller and the β I-like domain) contains the ligand-binding site; ligand affinity is regulated allosterically through "bent" (low-affinity) versus "extended" (high-affinity) conformations. The short cytoplasmic tails lack enzymatic activity but recruit large adaptor complexes (talin, kindlin, α-actinin).

## Mechanism of Action

Integrins transduce signals in both directions. **Outside-in signaling** occurs when ECM ligands (e.g., fibronectin, collagen, laminin) engage the receptor, triggering clustering and assembly of focal adhesions that activate [[FAK]], [[SRC]], [[PI3K]]/AKT, and [[MAPK]] pathways to drive survival and proliferation. **Inside-out signaling** originates from intracellular cues (e.g., growth factor receptors, [[Ras]]) that alter talin/kindlin binding to switch integrins to high affinity. On endothelial and tumor cells, integrins such as αvβ3 and α5β1 direct [[Angiogenesis]] by stabilizing nascent vessels and presenting growth factors.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

In normal physiology integrins mediate wound healing, immune cell trafficking, and tissue morphogenesis. In cancer, integrin upregulation and altered glycosylation enhance tumor cell adhesion to and remodeling of the [[ECM]], promoting invasion through basement membranes, survival under anchorage-independent conditions, and resistance to [[Apoptosis]] and [[Chemotherapy]]. αvβ3 and αvβ5 are strongly associated with neovascularization, while β1 and β4 integrins support epithelial tumor invasion and [[EMT]].

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Integrins are exploited for tumor-targeted delivery. The [[RGD peptide]] motif (Arg-Gly-Asp) binds αv-family integrins; the tumor-penetrating [[iRGD peptide]] uses an RGD sequence to home to tumor vasculature and then engages [[Neuropilin-1]] for deep penetration. Integrin antagonists (e.g., cilengitide, an αvβ3/αvβ5 inhibitor) and antibody-drug conjugates have been evaluated clinically, often in combination with anti-angiogenic or cytotoxic therapy.

## Connections

- [[ECM]] - Extracellular matrix ligands that engage integrins and initiate signaling.
- [[Angiogenesis]] - Neovessel formation directed by αv integrins on endothelium.
- [[FAK]] - Core focal adhesion kinase downstream of integrin clustering.
- [[Metastasis]] - Dissemination enabled by integrin-mediated invasion and survival.
- [[RGD peptide]] - Minimal integrin-binding motif used in targeting.
- [[iRGD peptide]] - Tumor-penetrating RGD-derived peptide.
- [[Neuropilin-1]] - CendR receptor engaged by iRGD after integrin binding.
- [[Chemotherapy]] - Often resisted by integrin survival signaling.
