# Wuchereria bancrofti

**Wuchereria bancrofti** is a thread-like lymphatic filarial nematode and the principal cause of human **lymphatic filariasis (LF)**, a neglected tropical disease responsible for massive disabling lymphedema (elephantiasis) and hydrocele. It is transmitted by mosquito vectors (chiefly *Culex*, *Anopheles*, *Aedes*, and *Mansonia* species) and perpetuates chronic infection through circulating [[Microfilariae]] in the blood.

## Life Cycle and Biology

Infected mosquitoes deposit L3 larvae during a blood meal; larvae migrate to lymphatic vessels and mature into long-lived adult worms (females up to 10 cm) that reside in nodal sinuses. Adults release sheathed [[Microfilariae]] that enter circulation, exhibiting nocturnal periodicity aligned with vector biting. A mosquito ingesting microfilariae supports development to L3, completing the cycle. Humans are the only natural reservoir.

## Pathophysiology and Links to Cancer

Adult worms incite lymphangitis and lymphatic obstruction; the resulting stasis and recurrent bacterial cellulitis produce elephantiasis. Chronic LF sustains **lymphatic [[Inflammation]]** and local immunosuppression, and the associated chronic antigenic and inflammatory burden is biologically relevant to malignancy risk in endemic settings. Moreover, the immunomodulatory milieu of filarial infection can alter host responses to co-infections and, theoretically, to tumor immunosurveillance — a research area linking parasitic disease ecology to [[Cancer]] outcomes.

## Diagnosis and Treatment

Diagnosis uses night blood films, antigen tests (ICT), and ultrasound (filarial dance sign). Vector control and mass drug administration with [[Ivermectin]] (often combined with [[Diethylcarbamazine]] and albendazole) aim to interrupt transmission; doxycycline targeting *Wolbachia* endosymbionts sterilizes and kills adult worms. The [[World Health Organization]] leads the Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis.
