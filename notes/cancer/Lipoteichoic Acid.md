# Lipoteichoic Acid

## Overview

Lipoteichoic acid (LTA) is a major constituent of the [[cell wall]] of [[Gram-positive bacteria]]. It is a [[PAMP]] recognized by the [[innate immune system]], primarily through [[TLR2]] in complex with [[TLR6]] and [[CD36]].

## Structure

- A polymer of [[glycerolphosphate]] or [[ribitolphosphate]] subunits linked by phosphodiester bonds.
- Anchored to the bacterial membrane via a [[glycolipid]] moiety.
- [[D-alanine]] and [[N-acetylglucosamine]] substitutions along the backbone modulate immunogenicity.

## Immune Recognition

- **[[TLR2]]/[[TLR6]]**: Primary receptor complex for LTA recognition.
- **[[CD36]]**: Co-receptor facilitating LTA binding to TLR2/6.
- **Signaling**: Activates [[MyD88]]-dependent [[NF-κB]] and [[MAPK]] pathways, inducing [[TNF-α]], [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], and [[IL-1β]].

## Role in Cancer

LTA from gut microbiota can contribute to chronic [[Inflammation]], potentially promoting [[Colorectal Cancer]] and [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] through TLR2-mediated signaling. Gut microbiota composition influences systemic [[LPS]] and LTA levels, which modulate [[immune checkpoint]] responses and cancer risk.
