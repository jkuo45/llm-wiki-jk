# HIV-1

**HIV-1** (Human immunodeficiency virus type 1) is a lentivirus within the Retroviridae family and the causative agent of the global [[AIDS]] pandemic. Approximately 38 million people worldwide live with HIV-1. HIV-1 is the pandemic form of HIV, with Group M (Main) responsible for >95% of infections and divided into subtypes A–K and circulating recombinant forms (CRFs). [[HIV|HIV-2]], by contrast, is largely confined to West Africa, exhibits lower transmissibility, and progresses to AIDS more slowly.

## Structure

HIV-1 is an enveloped retrovirus approximately 100–120 nm in diameter. Its genome consists of two copies of positive-sense single-stranded RNA (~9.7 kb) encased within a conical capsid. Key structural proteins include:

- **Gag**: cleaved into capsid (p24), matrix (p17), and nucleocapsid (p7).
- **Pol**: encodes enzymatic proteins — [[Reverse transcriptase]] (RT), [[Integrase]] (IN), and [[Protease]] (PR).
- **Env**: encodes the envelope glycoproteins gp120 (surface subunit, receptor binding) and gp41 (transmembrane subunit, membrane fusion).

Accessory proteins — [[TAT]], Rev, Nef, [[Vpr]], Vpu, and Vif — regulate transcription, nuclear export, immune evasion, and infectivity.

## Life Cycle

1. **Attachment**: gp120 binds the [[CD4]] receptor and a coreceptor ([[CCR5]] or [[CXCR4]]).
2. **Fusion**: gp41 mediates membrane fusion, releasing the viral core into the cytoplasm.
3. **Uncoating**: The capsid disassembles, releasing the viral RNA–protein complex.
4. **Reverse Transcription**: [[Reverse transcriptase]] converts ssRNA into double-stranded DNA.
5. **Nuclear Import**: The pre-integration complex is imported into the nucleus.
6. **Integration**: [[Integrase]] catalyzes proviral DNA insertion into the host genome.
7. **Latency or Transcription**: The provirus can remain transcriptionally silent (latent reservoir) or be actively transcribed via host RNA polymerase II, driven by the viral LTR promoter (enhanced by [[TAT]] and [[NFκB]]).
8. **Translation & Assembly**: Viral proteins are synthesized, and new virions assemble at the plasma membrane.
9. **Budding**: Immature virions bud from the host cell.
10. **Maturation**: [[Protease]] cleaves Gag and Gag-Pol polyproteins into functional subunits, producing infectious mature virions.

## Pathogenesis

HIV-1 infection leads to progressive [[CD4]]+ T cell depletion through multiple mechanisms: direct viral killing, bystander [[Apoptosis]] of uninfected cells, pyroptosis of abortively infected CD4+ T cells, and chronic immune activation. As CD4 counts fall below 200 cells/µL, the host becomes susceptible to [[Opportunistic infection]]s characteristic of [[AIDS]]:

- *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PCP)
- Tuberculosis (TB)
- Cerebral toxoplasmosis
- Cytomegalovirus (CMV) retinitis
- [[Kaposi sarcoma]] (caused by HHV-8)
- Esophageal candidiasis, cryptococcal meningitis, and disseminated *Mycobacterium avium* complex

Chronic [[HIV]] infection also drives immune senescence, systemic [[Inflammation]], and elevated risk for cardiovascular disease, neurocognitive impairment, and certain [[Cancer|cancers]] (e.g., [[Kaposi sarcoma]], non-Hodgkin lymphoma, cervical cancer).

## Treatment

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) is the standard of care. ART suppresses viral replication to <50 copies/mL, enables [[CD4]]+ T cell recovery, and prevents transmission (Undetectable = Untransmissible, U=U). Major drug classes:

- **NRTIs** (nucleoside/nucleotide [[Reverse transcriptase]] inhibitors): [[Tenofovir]] disoproxil fumarate (TDF), [[Tenofovir]] alafenamide (TAF), emtricitabine (FTC), lamivudine (3TC).
- **NNRTIs** (non-nucleoside [[Reverse transcriptase]] inhibitors): efavirenz, rilpivirine, doravirine.
- **PIs** ([[Protease]] inhibitors): darunavir, atazanavir (boosted with ritonavir or cobicistat).
- **INSTIs** ([[Integrase]] strand transfer inhibitors): dolutegravir (DTG), bictegravir (BIC), raltegravir (RAL), cabotegravir (CAB).
- **Entry/Fusion Inhibitors**: maraviroc (CCR5 antagonist), enfuvirtide (gp41 fusion inhibitor), fostemsavir (gp120 attachment inhibitor), ibalizumab (post-attachment [[CD4]]-directed mAb).

Fixed-dose single-tablet regimens (e.g., Biktarvy [BIC/FTC/TAF], Triumeq [DTG/ABC/3TC]) simplify adherence. Long-acting injectable ART (Cabenuva [CAB/RPV] monthly or every 2 months) is available for virologically suppressed patients.

## Prevention

- **PrEP** (pre-exposure prophylaxis): daily oral [[Tenofovir]]/emtricitabine (Truvada or Descovy) or long-acting injectable cabotegravir.
- **PEP** (post-exposure prophylaxis): 28-day course of three-drug ART initiated within 72 hours of potential exposure.
- Barrier protection (condoms), needle exchange programs, voluntary medical male circumcision, and prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT) through universal ART during pregnancy and breastfeeding.

## Connections

- [[AIDS]]: HIV-1 infection progresses to AIDS when CD4+ T cell count falls below 200 cells/µL or AIDS-defining opportunistic infections occur.
- [[CD4]]: Primary receptor for HIV-1 entry; CD4+ T cell depletion drives immunosuppression.
- [[Reverse transcriptase]]: Viral enzyme that converts viral RNA into proviral DNA; target of NRTIs and NNRTIs.
- [[Integrase]]: Viral enzyme that integrates proviral DNA into the host genome; target of INSTIs.
- [[Protease]]: Viral enzyme that cleaves Gag/Gag-Pol polyproteins for virion maturation; target of PIs.
- [[Opportunistic infection]]: Hallmark of AIDS-defining illness in the setting of HIV-1-induced immune deficiency.
- [[Antiretroviral]]: Class of drugs targeting various stages of the HIV-1 life cycle; cornerstone of ART.
- [[NFκB]]: Host transcription factor that activates HIV-1 LTR-driven proviral transcription; links immune activation to viral replication.
- [[Apoptosis]]: HIV-1 depletes CD4+ T cells partly through induction of apoptosis in bystander cells.
- [[Cancer]]: HIV-1/AIDS increases risk of certain cancers, including Kaposi sarcoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and cervical cancer.
