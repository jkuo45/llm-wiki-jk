# TDR

The **Special Programme for Research and Training in Tropical Diseases (TDR)** is a global programme of scientific collaboration established in 1975 and co-sponsored by the [[World Health Organization]], UNICEF, UNDP, the World Bank, and (formerly) The Rockefeller Foundation. TDR's mandate is to foster an evidence base for the control, elimination, and eradication of neglected tropical diseases (NTDs), and to build research capacity in endemic low- and middle-income countries.

## Mission and Structure

TDR funds and coordinates operational and translational research on a defined portfolio of NTDs — including [[Onchocerciasis]] (river blindness), [[Lymphatic Filariasis]], malaria, tuberculosis, dengue, leishmaniasis, schistosomiasis, and soil-transmitted helminthiases. It emphasizes implementation science: how to deliver existing tools (e.g., [[Ivermectin]] mass drug administration) effectively, monitor drug resistance, and measure progress toward elimination targets. A distinctive feature is its training component, which strengthens the careers of researchers from disease-endemic nations.

## Relevance to Cancer and Global Health

Although TDR does not focus on malignancy per se, NTD-endemic regions overlap heavily with communities bearing the highest [[Cancer]] mortality from preventable causes (infection-associated cancers, late presentation, limited pathology access). Control of chronic parasitic infections reduces long-term [[Inflammation]] and immunosuppression that can predispose to malignancy, and TDR's health-system strengthening indirectly supports oncology capacity. ## Notable Initiatives

TDR has supported mapping of vector distributions (e.g., [[Simulium]] for onchocerciasis, mosquito vectors for lymphatic filariasis), pharmacovigilance for antiparasitic drugs, and strategies for comorbidity management in co-infected populations. It collaborates with the WHO NTD Roadmap and the Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis.
