# RGD peptide

The RGD peptide is the tripeptide sequence arginine–glycine–aspartic acid, the minimal and most widespread cell-adhesion motif displayed by extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins such as [[Fibronectin]], [[Vitronectin]], and [[Fibrinogen]]. RGD is recognized by a subset of [[Integrins]], the major receptors that mediate cell–ECM adhesion, cytoskeletal organization, survival, and migration. Because integrin engagement is essential for [[Angiogenesis]], invasion, and [[Metastasis]], RGD sequences and their mimetics are important both as biological tools and as anti-cancer agents.

## Structure & Mechanism

The RGD motif adopts a specific loop conformation in native ECM proteins that fits the ligand-binding pocket of integrins (e.g., αvβ3, αvβ5, α5β1). Cyclization of RGD into constrained peptides (e.g., cilengitide) and incorporation into larger tumor-homing peptides (e.g., iRGD, which also engages [[Neuropilin-1]]) improve receptor selectivity and tissue penetration. Competitive binding of RGD mimetics blocks integrin-mediated adhesion, disrupts focal adhesions, and induces anoikis (detachment-induced [[Apoptosis]]) in endothelial and tumor cells.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, RGD–integrin interactions govern tissue morphogenesis, wound healing, and hemostasis. In [[Cancer]], integrin signaling through RGD sites supports the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] by enabling sustained survival, [[Angiogenesis]], and invasion. αvβ3/αvβ5 are markedly upregulated on tumor vasculature and some tumor cells, making them attractive targets. RGD peptides therefore inhibit neovascularization and reduce [[Metastasis]] in preclinical models.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Cilengitide, a cyclic RGD pentapeptide, reached clinical trials (notably glioblastoma) as an integrin antagonist, illustrating the therapeutic concept. iRGD is used to enhance tumor penetration and drug delivery of conjugated payloads. RGD motifs are also exploited in imaging probes for integrin-positive lesions and as components of biomaterials. The peptide remains a foundational element of adhesion and anti-angiogenic research.
