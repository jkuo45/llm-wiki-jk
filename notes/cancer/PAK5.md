# PAK5

**PAK5 (also PAK7)** is a Group II p21-activated kinase originally identified as a brain-enriched, Cdc42-activated kinase important for neuronal survival and morphogenesis. It has since been implicated in oncogenesis, where its overexpression in several carcinomas contributes to proliferation, migration, and resistance to [[Apoptosis]].

## Structure and Activation

Like other Group II PAKs, PAK5 harbors a C-terminal kinase domain and an N-terminal Cdc42/Rac-binding region without the strong autoinhibitory switch of Group I PAKs, conferring appreciable basal activity. Interaction with Cdc42/Rac1 recruits PAK5 to membranes and the actin cytoskeleton to execute its signaling roles.

## Mechanism of Action

PAK5 phosphorylates the pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 family protein [[Bad]] at Ser112, neutralizing Bad's capacity to permeabilize mitochondria and trigger [[MOMP]], thereby protecting cells from death. PAK5 also engages survival and cytoskeletal pathways — modulating [[ERK]], [[Akt]], and actin dynamics — supporting tumor cell growth, [[Metastasis]], and stress resistance. Its anti-apoptotic function is mechanistically parallel to that of [[PAK4]], reflecting convergent Group II PAK biology.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

Physiologically, PAK5 promotes neuronal differentiation and protects neurons from apoptotic insults. Pathologically, PAK5 is upregulated in glioma, lung, breast, and gastric cancers, where it fosters the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]], particularly evasion of cell death and increased invasive capacity. Its brain-enriched origin may explain heightened relevance in neuro-oncology.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

PAK5 is a candidate therapeutic and prognostic node. Group II PAK inhibitors that target PAK4/PAK6/PAK5 are under study to restore apoptotic susceptibility and curb invasion, and PAK5 expression is explored as a biomarker of PAK-pathway activation in tumors.
