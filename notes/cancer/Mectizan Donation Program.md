# Mectizan Donation Program

The **Mectizan Donation Program (MDP)** is a pioneering public–private partnership established in 1987 to manage the donation of [[Mectizan]] (brand-name [[Ivermectin]]) by [[Merck & Co. Inc]] for the control and elimination of [[Onchocerciasis]] (river blindness) and, subsequently, [[Lymphatic Filariasis]]. Conceived after the drug's dramatic efficacy against onchocercal microfilariae was demonstrated, the MDP became the template for large-scale philanthropic drug donation and a cornerstone of global neglected-tropical-disease elimination.

## Structure & Partners

The MDP coordinates donated drug supply with endemic-country ministries of health, the [[World Health Organization]], and non-governmental implementation partners. Merck committed to donate Mectizan for as long as needed, free of charge, with the program handling logistics, distribution, and pharmacovigilance. This model later inspired analogous donations (e.g., azithromycin for trachoma, praziquantel for schistosomiasis).

## Mechanism of Public-Health Impact

By annually treating at-risk populations with Mectizan, the program suppresses [[Microfilariae]] in skin and lymphatics, interrupting transmission and preventing the morbidity that defines these diseases—blindness in onchocerciasis and debilitating lymphedema/hydrocele in lymphatic filariasis. Mass drug administration also reduces secondary bacterial infection and disability-adjusted life-years lost.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Although not an oncology entity, the MDP is thematically linked to cancer research through the shared pharmacology of ivermectin. The same compound donated for parasitosis is under active [[Drug Repositioning]] study for [[Cancer]] (e.g., YAP1/EGFR modulation in [[Lung Cancer]], selective lethality in [[Leukemia]]), illustrating how donor-funded discovery can seed cross-domain therapeutic hypotheses.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

The MDP is widely cited as a benchmark for sustainable access to essential medicines and for the feasibility of eliminating transmission through community-directed treatment. Its decades-long operation generated enormous safety and distribution data that inform ivermectin use broadly, including investigational oncologic applications.
