# RSK

Ribosomal S6 kinase (RSK, also p90RSK) is a family of serine/threonine kinases (RSK1–4) lying immediately downstream of the [[MAPK]]/ERK pathway. RSK transduces mitogenic and survival signals into phosphorylation of a broad substrate set controlling translation, metabolism, cell-cycle progression, and apoptosis. RSK is a key effector by which ERK signaling enforces the survival phenotype of many [[Cancer]] cells.

## Structure & Mechanism

RSK has a bipartite architecture with two functional kinase domains: an N-terminal domain that phosphorylates most substrates and a C-terminal domain that autophosphorylates and is activated by [[ERK]] (via phospho-Thr in the C-terminal domain and PDK1-mediated phosphorylation of the N-terminal activation loop). Once active, RSK phosphorylates [[Bim]] (inhibiting its pro-apoptotic function), [[Apaf-1]] (dampening apoptosome assembly), and [[Bad]] (reducing mitochondrial death), and it reinforces growth via ribosomal protein S6 and translation factors. It also phosphorylates the pro-apoptotic transcription factor [[FOXO]].

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Physiologically, RSK couples growth-factor signaling to protein synthesis and survival. In [[Cancer]], RSK is hyperactivated downstream of [[RAS]]/RAF/MEK/ERK and supports the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]—sustained proliferation, resistance to cell death, and metabolic rewiring. By phosphorylating [[Bim]], [[Apaf-1]], and [[Bad]], RSK blunts both intrinsic and extrinsic [[Apoptosis]], protecting tumor cells from [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Chemotherapy]].

## Clinical & Research Relevance

RSK is a candidate therapeutic target, particularly in RAS/RAF-driven tumors where inhibiting RSK may restore apoptotic priming. Combination of MEK/ERK inhibitors with RSK blockade is studied to prevent compensatory survival signaling. RSK phosphorylation status serves as a pharmacodynamic biomarker of MAPK-pathway output in clinical trials.
