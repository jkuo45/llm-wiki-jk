# Satoshi Ōmura

Satoshi Ōmura is a Japanese microbiologist, professor emeritus at Kitasato University, and co-recipient of the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (shared with William C. Campbell and Youyou Tu) for the discovery of avermectins—a class of compounds derived from the soil bacterium [[Streptomyces]] that led to [[Avermectin]] and its semisynthetic derivative [[Ivermectin]]. His work established a paradigm for mining microbial natural products as therapeutics, including agents now investigated in [[Cancer]].

## Scientific Contributions

Ōmura pioneered systematic cultivation and screening of actinomycetes, building vast culture collections and developing methods to coax secondary-metabolite production. His collaboration with Merck identified the avermectin-producing strain, which Campbell developed into ivermectin—one of the most widely used anthelmintics in human and veterinary medicine. Beyond avermectins, Ōmura's group discovered many bioactive microbial metabolites, including compounds with anticancer, antifungal, and immunosuppressive activity.

## Relevance to Cancer Research

Although ivermectin is best known as an antiparasitic, it has attracted intense repurposing interest in oncology. Preclinical studies show ivermectin can inhibit [[Cancer]] cell proliferation and [[Metastasis]], potentiate radiation, and overcome resistance to agents such as [[docetaxel]] and [[Enzalutamide]] (the latter in [[Prostate Cancer]], where it also inhibits [[HSP27]] phosphorylation). These activities—spanning PAK1 inhibition, [[Wnt]]/β-catenin modulation, and autophagy effects—trace back to the natural-product discovery tradition Ōmura established.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Ōmura's legacy underscores the value of microbial biodiversity for drug discovery and supports ongoing screening of Streptomyces metabolites for novel anti-cancer agents. His career is a model for translational natural-product pharmacology bridging parasitology and oncology.
