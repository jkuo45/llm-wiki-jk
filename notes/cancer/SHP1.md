# SHP1

SHP1 (Src homology 2 domain-containing protein tyrosine phosphatase 1), encoded by [[PTPN6]], is a cytosolic protein tyrosine phosphatase best known as a negative regulator of cytokine and immune-receptor signaling. In death signaling, SHP1 plays a positive role in extrinsic [[Apoptosis]] by dephosphorylating and activating [[Caspase-8]]—specifically removing inhibitory phosphates at Tyr397 and Tyr465, an event that requires SHP1 to bind Caspase-8 at Tyr310.

## Structure & Mechanism

SHP1 contains two N-terminal SH2 domains (N-SH2, C-SH2), a central catalytic phosphatase domain, and a C-terminal tail with regulatory tyrosines. In the basal state, the N-SH2 domain autoinhibits the catalytic site; phosphotyrosine motifs in receptors or adaptors relieve this inhibition by binding the SH2 domains, localizing and activating SHP1. In the Caspase-8 interaction, SHP1 binds a phosphorylated Tyr310 site on Caspase-8 and then dephosphorylates Tyr397/Tyr465—residues that, when phosphorylated, restrain caspase activation—thereby licensing the death-inducing signaling complex (DISC) output.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

SHP1 restrains hematopoietic and epithelial signaling, maintaining tolerance and preventing hyperproliferation. In [[Cancer]], SHP1 is frequently epigenetically silenced (promoter methylation) or lost, particularly in leukemias and lymphomas, permitting sustained survival signaling and contributing to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. Conversely, its ability to activate [[Caspase-8]] positions SHP1 as a tumor-suppressive enabler of death-receptor apoptosis, and its loss can render tumors resistant to [[Chemotherapy]] and TRAIL-based agents.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

SHP1 promoter methylation is a prognostic/diagnostic biomarker, and demethylating agents can restore SHP1 expression. Targeting the SHP1–Caspase-8 axis is a conceptual route to sensitize tumors to death-receptor ligands and cytotoxic therapy. SHP1 remains a key node linking immune signaling to apoptotic execution.
