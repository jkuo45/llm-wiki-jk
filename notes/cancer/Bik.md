# Bik

**Bik** (Bcl-2-interacting killer; also called NBK) is a [[Bcl-2 family|BH3-only protein]] that acts as an initiator of the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]]. It directly activates the effectors [[Bax]] and [[Bak]] (and neutralizes anti-apoptotic relatives such as Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL), triggering mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization.

## Structure & Activation

Bik contains only the BH3 domain, the minimal death-promoting motif. Unlike the sensitizer BH3-only proteins, Bik is a direct activator that binds and oligomerizes Bax/Bak. Its pro-apoptotic potency is normally held in check by rapid proteasomal turnover.

## Regulation by SRC/ERK Signaling

A key tumor-relevant mechanism is that **[[SRC kinase]]** promotes Bik degradation via an **[[ERK]]-dependent** phosphorylation cascade. ERK-mediated phosphorylation creates a phosphodegron that targets Bik for ubiquitination and destruction, suppressing apoptosis and enhancing survival. Oncogenic SRC/ERK activity (common in many carcinomas) thus disables a natural death trigger, contributing to tumor progression and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance.

## Physiological Function

Bik is expressed in epithelial tissues and the immune system, where it enforces deletion of damaged or superfluous cells. It cooperates with other BH3-only proteins ([[Bim]], [[Puma]], [[Bmf]]) to set the apoptotic threshold.

## Cancer Relevance

- **Tumor Suppressor Role**: By initiating apoptosis, Bik constrains oncogenesis; its loss or degradation removes a barrier to [[Cancer]].
- **Therapeutic Vulnerability**: Restoring Bik (e.g., via MEK/ERK inhibitors that stabilize it, or [[BH3 mimetics]] that bypass it) can re-engage death.
- **Biomarker**: SRC/ERK activation status may predict Bik abundance and apoptotic priming.
