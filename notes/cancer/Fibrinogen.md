# Fibrinogen

Fibrinogen (Factor I) is a ~340 kDa soluble plasma glycoprotein synthesized in the liver and circulating at 2–4 mg/mL. It is the precursor of fibrin, the structural backbone of blood clots, and a multifunctional player in hemostasis, inflammation, and [[Tumor Microenvironment]] biology.

## Structure & Domains

Human fibrinogen is a dimer of three pairs of polypeptide chains (Aα, Bβ, γ)2 linked by disulfide bonds. Each monomer contains an N-terminal coiled-coil "central domain" and globular C-terminal nodules. Critically, the Aα chain carries an **[[RGD peptide]]** motif and the γ chain exposes a C-terminal [[KQAGDV]] sequence; both serve as integrin-binding sites. Thrombin cleaves fibrinopeptides A and B, exposing polymerization sites that allow fibrin monomers to assemble into a branching network.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Upon vascular injury, thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin, which polymerizes and is cross-linked by [[Factor XIIIa]] into a stable clot. The exposed RGD motif engages [[Integrins]] such as αIIbβ3 (platelets), αvβ3, and α5β1, transducing outside-in signals that promote platelet aggregation, cell adhesion, and migration. Fibrin(ogen) also binds [[Fibronectin]], [[vitronectin]], and growth factors, organizing the provisional ECM.

## Physiological Function

Fibrinogen is indispensable for hemostasis and wound healing. It also modulates endothelial cell function and serves as an acute-phase reactant, rising during inflammation.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

Fibrinogen bridges coagulation and malignancy. Tumors exploit the fibrin-rich ECM for [[Metastasis]] — circulating tumor cells can hitchhike on platelet-fibrin clots (the "seed and soil" niche) to evade immunity and colonize distant sites. Elevated plasma fibrinogen is a negative prognostic marker in [[Breast Cancer]], [[Colorectal Cancer]], and [[Lung Cancer]]. Fibrinogen fragments (e.g., [[D-dimer]]) are used clinically as biomarkers of thrombotic burden. Anticoagulants and integrin antagonists (e.g., αIIbβ3 blockers) are being investigated to curb tumor dissemination and the pro-metastatic [[Tumor Microenvironment]]. Fibrinogen also fuels [[Inflammation]] via macrophage integrin signaling.
