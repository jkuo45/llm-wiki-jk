# Cancer Stem Cells

Cancer Stem Cells (CSCs) are a subpopulation of cells within a tumor that possess characteristics similar to normal stem cells, specifically the ability to self-renew and differentiate into multiple cell types.

## Role in Cancer
CSCs are widely recognized as a main cause of tumor recurrence, metastasis, and resistance to conventional chemotherapy and radiotherapy. They can maintain activity during treatment and rapidly proliferate when the environment is suitable.

## Markers and Regulation
Key proteins associated with the self-renewal and differentiation of CSCs include [[Nanog]], [[Oct4]], and [[Sox2]]. The [[PAK1]]-[[notes/_link/STAT3]] axis is also a significant regulatory pathway for CSC formation.

## Therapeutic Targeting
Targeting CSCs is crucial for effective cancer therapy.
- [[Ivermectin]]: Preferentially targets and inhibits CSCs in breast cancer cells. It inhibits the expression of [[Nanog]], [[Oct4]], and [[Sox2]] and acts through the [[PAK1]]-[[notes/_link/STAT3]] axis.

## Connections
