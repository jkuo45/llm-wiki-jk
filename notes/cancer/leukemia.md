# Leukemia

**Leukemia** is a heterogeneous group of blood cancers that originate in the bone marrow and hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells, resulting in the accumulation of large numbers of abnormal white blood cells that impair normal hematopoiesis. Major categories include acute lymphoblastic (ALL), acute myeloid (AML), chronic myeloid (CML), and chronic lymphocytic (CLL) leukemias. Beyond conventional [[Chemotherapy]] and targeted agents, leukemia cells have been shown vulnerable to natural products such as [[Honeybee venom|honeybee venom]], its principal peptide [[melittin]], and the antiparasitic [[Ivermectin]].

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Leukemogenesis arises from acquired mutations in genes controlling proliferation, differentiation, and [[Apoptosis]]—e.g., the BCR-ABL fusion in CML, FLT3 mutations in AML, and TP53 loss. These lesions produce clonal expansion of immature blasts, bone marrow failure (anemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia), and tissue infiltration. The malignant clone often exhibits heightened dependence on survival kinases such as [[Akt]] and on anti-apoptotic proteins, creating therapeutic vulnerabilities.

## Role of Bee Venom & Ivermectin

Both honeybee venom and melittin display cytotoxic potency against leukemia cells. Bumblebee venom induces apoptosis in human chronic myelogenous leukemia cells by inhibiting phosphorylation of [[Akt]], a key survival kinase. [[Ivermectin]] preferentially kills leukemia cells at low concentrations through chloride-dependent membrane hyperpolarization and [[ROS]] production, engaging mitochondrial dysfunction. It shows synergistic lethality with conventional antileukemic agents [[cytarabine]] and [[Daunorubicin]], suggesting repositioning potential in combinatorial regimens.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Standard therapy combines cytotoxic nucleosides (cytarabine, daunorubicin) with targeted approaches (tyrosine kinase inhibitors for BCR-ABL, venetoclax for BCL-2). The demonstrations that melittin and ivermectin selectively stress leukemia cells via Akt and chloride/ROS axes provide mechanistic footholds for adjunct strategies and for overcoming resistance. Research continues into delivery (e.g., nanoparticle-encapsulated melittin) to spare normal marrow.
