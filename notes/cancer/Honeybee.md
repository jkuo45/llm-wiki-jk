# Honeybee

The European **honeybee** (*Apis mellifera*) is a eusocial insect of the genus *Apis*, valued agriculturally for pollination and historically in medicine for its hive products. Within the cancer knowledge base, the honeybee is relevant because [[Honeybee venom|bee venom]] and its principal peptide [[melittin]] exhibit broad [[Anticancer]] activity across multiple tumor models.

## Biology & Products

A honeybee colony produces honey (sugar-rich, antimicrobial), propolis (resin-rich with flavonoids and phenolics), royal jelly, and venom. [[Honeybee venom]] is delivered by the sting apparatus and is a complex mixture of peptides (melittin ~50%, apamin, mast-cell degranulating peptide), enzymes, and biogenic amines. Apitherapy is the traditional medicinal use of these products.

## Anticancer Mechanisms of Venom & Melittin

[[melittin]], a 26-amino-acid amphipathic cationic peptide, is the major antitumor constituent. It disrupts cancer cell membranes (selective at certain doses), and intracellularly inhibits key oncogenic nodes: it suppresses [[HER2]] phosphorylation in [[HER2-enriched breast cancer]], impairs [[EGFR]] signaling, and perturbs [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] and [[Wnt]] pathways. Melittin also induces [[Apoptosis]] and [[Autophagy]] and restricts [[Metastasis]] and [[Angiogenesis]]. Synergy with conventional [[Chemotherapy]] and with [[Ivermectin]] is under investigation. Propolis polyphenols add antioxidant and pro-apoptotic effects.

## Physiological & Ecological Note

Honeybees are also important as [[Model Organism]]s for studying immunity, social behavior, and toxin ecology, and as pollinators whose decline has broad ecological implications. Their venom apparatus is a natural source of structurally defined peptides informing drug design.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

Honeybee-derived products are being translated as adjunctive [[Medical Treatment]] and as templates for peptide therapeutics. Melittin-loaded nanoparticles and immunotoxins are in preclinical oncology. Safety (anaphylaxis risk) and delivery remain challenges; nonetheless, honeybee venom exemplifies how natural-product venoms yield cancer-relevant modalities.
