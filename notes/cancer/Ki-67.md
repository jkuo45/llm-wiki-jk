# Ki-67

**Ki-67** is a large nuclear protein encoded by the *MKI67* gene and is one of the most widely used immunohistochemical markers of cell proliferation in pathology. It is strictly associated with cell-cycle activity, being expressed during all active phases (G1, S, G2, and mitosis) but absent in quiescent (G0) cells. The fraction of Ki-67-positive tumor nuclei—the **Ki-67 labeling index**—serves as a quantitative measure of proliferative fraction and a surrogate for tumor aggressiveness and growth rate.

## Structure & Biology

Ki-67 is a ~320–360 kDa protein localized to the nucleolus and perichromosomal regions. It associates with chromatin and the ribosomal DNA loci and is implicated in ribosome biogenesis and the organization of mitotic chromosomes, though its precise molecular function remains incomprecisely defined. Its tight cell-cycle regulation makes it an ideal endogenous proliferation reporter: antigen expression rises from G1, peaks in S/G2/M, and is rapidly lost as cells exit to G0.

## Mechanism of Action as a Marker

Because Ki-67 protein is stable through mitosis but degraded upon entry into quiescence, immunohistochemical staining of a tumor section yields a snapshot of the proportion of cells currently cycling. Pathologists count positively stained nuclei over a defined field to compute the labeling index. High indices correlate with rapid turnover, higher histologic grade, and—depending on tumor type—worse prognosis or greater chemotherapy sensitivity.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Ki-67 is physiologic in renewing tissues (bone marrow, gut epithelium, skin). In oncology it is integral to grading and treatment decisions across carcinomas and lymphomas; for example, in breast cancer it helps distinguish luminal A from luminal B biology and informs decisions about chemotherapy intensity, and in neuroendocrine tumors it stratifies grading. In [[triple-negative breast cancer]] and other aggressive subtypes, elevated Ki-67 tracks with proliferative drive and metastatic potential.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Ki-67 is both a diagnostic/prognostic biomarker and a pharmacodynamic readout. Combination strategies that suppress proliferation lower the index: in triple-negative breast cancer models, combined treatment with the peptide [[melittin]] and the taxane [[docetaxel]] significantly reduces Ki-67 expression, indicating dampened tumor cell proliferation. Serial Ki-67 assessment (e.g., on baseline vs. post-neoadjuvant-core-biopsy) is used to gauge treatment response, and efforts continue to standardize scoring and digital-image analysis.
