# CendR motif

The **C-end Rule (CendR)** motif is a short peptide sequence — typically R/K-X-X-R/K at the C-terminus — that binds the vascular receptor [[Neuropilin-1]] (NRP1) and triggers an active transcytosis/endocytosis program enabling deep penetration of tissues. The prototypical example is the tumor-homing peptide **iRGD** (CRGDK/RGPD/EC), whose exposed C-terminal RGD/K motif engages NRP1 after proteolytic priming. In oncology, the CendR principle is exploited to ferry conjugated drugs, nanoparticles, and imaging agents across the compromised tumor endothelium into the extravascular tumor mass.

## Discovery & Sequence Logic

The CendR rule was formulated from phage-display screens identifying peptides that home to tumor vasculature and require a basic C-terminal motif for tissue penetration. The minimal consensus is an arginine or lysine at the C-terminus, an intervening two residues (any), and a second basic residue four positions upstream (R/K-X-X-R/K). A free C-terminus is essential; blocking the C-terminal carboxylate abolishes activity. Primary RGD-containing peptides (e.g., [[Cilengitide]]) bind αv integrins for vascular targeting, whereas CendR peptides additionally engage NRP1 to switch from mere adhesion to transmural penetration.

## Mechanism of Action

iRGD first binds αv integrins on tumor endothelium via its internal RGD sequence, localizing to tumor vessels. Tumor-associated proteases then expose the C-terminal CendR motif, which binds [[Neuropilin-1]] on endothelial and perivascular cells. NRP1 engagement activates an endocytic/transcytotic cascade (involving NRP1–plexin signaling and actin remodeling) that internalizes the peptide and transports it through the endothelium into the tumor interstitium. Covalently linked payloads — chemotherapeutics, antibodies, liposomes — are co-delivered, dramatically increasing intratumoral accumulation and homogeneity.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

NRP1 is broadly expressed on tumor vasculature and some cancer cells, and its engagement by CendR peptides preferentially exploits the leaky, protease-rich tumor microenvironment. This provides a tumor-selective delivery advantage over normal vessels. CendR-mediated penetration counters the poor diffusion that limits conventional nanomedicines, which often stall at the tumor periphery.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

CendR peptides are being advanced as delivery vehicles for [[Chemotherapy]] (e.g., co-administered doxorubicin), antibody–drug conjugates, and diagnostics. iRGD has shown enhanced antitumor efficacy and reduced systemic toxicity in preclinical models and early clinical exploration. Limitations include NRP1 expression heterogeneity and protease dependence. Engineered CendR variants with improved protease resistance and affinity are under development, and the motif is increasingly paired with checkpoint or [[Angiogenesis]] inhibitors to normalize vessels for better penetration.
