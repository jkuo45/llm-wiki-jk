# Trichuris

**Trichuris** (commonly known as whipworms) is a genus of parasitic nematodes belonging to the phylum Nematoda and family Trichuridae. Major species include *T. trichiura* (human pathogen), *T. suis* (pigs), and *T. vulpis* (dogs). Together with [[Ascaris]] and hookworms, *Trichuris* is classified as a soil-transmitted helminth ([[STH]]), collectively infecting over 1 billion people worldwide.

## Biology

Adult whipworms measure 30–50 mm in length. The anterior end is thin and filamentous — this esophageal portion burrows into the intestinal mucosa. The posterior end is thicker and houses the reproductive organs, giving the worm a characteristic whip-like morphology. Eggs are barrel-shaped with distinctive bipolar mucus plugs, making them highly resistant to environmental conditions and capable of surviving in soil for years under favorable conditions.

## Life Cycle

The life cycle is direct. Unembryonated eggs are shed in feces into soil, where they embryonate in 2–4 weeks (dependent on warmth and moisture). Upon ingestion of embryonated eggs, larvae hatch in the [[small intestine]] and penetrate the intestinal villi for 1–2 weeks. They then migrate to the [[cecum]] and [[colon]], where adults embed their thin anterior ends into the mucosa. Females produce 2,000–20,000 eggs per day, sustaining transmission in endemic areas.

## Disease

*Trichuris trichiura* causes [[trichuriasis]]. Heavy worm burdens — particularly in children — can lead to dysentery, [[rectal prolapse]], growth retardation, and anemia. The host immune response is characterized by a [[Th2]]-dominated profile with elevated [[IgE]] and [[eosinophilia]], typical of helminth infections. Chronic infections in endemic regions contribute to malnutrition and impaired cognitive development in school-aged children.

## Treatment and Control

Standard treatment involves [[Albendazole]] (400 mg single dose) or [[Mebendazole]] (500 mg single dose). [[Ivermectin]] is also effective, particularly in combination therapy for mixed STH infections. Mass drug administration (MDA) programs, improved sanitation, and health education remain the cornerstone of control efforts.
