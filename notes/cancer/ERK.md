# ERK

**ERK** (extracellular signal-regulated kinase) refers to the effector kinases of the RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK limb of the [[MAPK Signaling]] cascade — principally ERK1/2 (p44/p42) plus ERK5. ERK generally promotes cell survival and proliferation by phosphorylating and inactivating pro-apoptotic proteins such as [[Bim]] and stabilizing anti-apoptotic proteins such as [[Mcl-1]], making it a central node in oncogenic growth and apoptotic resistance.

## Structure & Activation

ERK1/2 are serine/threonine kinases activated by dual phosphorylation on conserved Thr and Tyr residues by the upstream kinase [[MEK]]1/2. MEK itself is activated by RAF kinases downstream of [[RAS]] GTPases, which integrate growth-factor receptor signals (e.g., [[EGFR]]). Once activated, ERK translocates to the nucleus and cytoplasm to phosphorylate >200 substrates — transcription factors (ELK1, c-Fos, MYC), ribosomal S6 kinase, and apoptotic regulators.

## Mechanism of Action

A key anti-apoptotic mechanism is ERK-mediated phosphorylation of the BH3-only protein [[Bim]]. Phosphorylated Bim is recognized by the E3 ligase [[c-CBL]] and targeted for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, removing a critical trigger of mitochondrial apoptosis. ERK also phosphorylates and stabilizes [[Mcl-1]], an anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family member, and can restrain [[Caspase-2]] and [[BAD]] activity. Through these actions, sustained ERK signaling keeps the intrinsic death pathway disengaged while driving cyclin D expression and proliferation.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

ERK signaling governs differentiation, growth, and survival in normal tissue. In [[Cancer]], upstream mutations ([[RAS]], *BRAF* V600E, *EGFR* amplification) cause constitutive ERK activation — a hallmark of proliferative signaling and death evasion. Overactive ERK both fuels tumor growth and protects cells from therapy-induced apoptosis, underpinning resistance to [[Chemotherapy]] and targeted agents. Paradoxically, abrupt ERK inhibition can trigger a cytotoxic ERK "addiction" collapse in some tumors.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

The RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK axis is a premier oncology target. BRAF inhibitors (vemurafenib, dabrafenib) and [[MEK]] inhibitors (trametinib, cobimetinib) are approved in melanoma, lung, and thyroid cancers; ERK1/2 inhibitors are in development for resistance bypass. Because ERK sustains [[Bim]] degradation, combining MAPK blockade with [[BH3 mimetics]] or Bim-stabilizing agents is a leading strategy to convert survival signaling into death. ERK phosphorylation (p-ERK) is a routine biomarker of pathway activity.
