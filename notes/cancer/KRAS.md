# KRAS

## Overview

KRAS (Kirsten rat sarcoma viral oncogene homolog) encodes a small [[GTPase]] that cycles between active ([[GTP]]-bound) and inactive ([[GDP]]-bound) states, relaying signals from [[receptor tyrosine kinases]] (RTKs) to downstream pathways including [[MAPK]], [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], and [[RalGDS]].

## Mutations in Cancer

KRAS is the most frequently mutated [[oncogene]] in human cancer. Common hotspot mutations occur at codons **G12**, **G13**, and **Q61**, impairing [[GTPase]] activity and locking KRAS in the active GTP-bound state.

- **[[Pancreatic Cancer]]**: ~90% KRAS mutations (G12D most common)
- **[[Colorectal Cancer]]**: ~40% (G12V, G12D, G13D)
- **[[Lung Cancer]]** ([[non-small-cell lung cancer|NSCLC]]): ~30% (G12C most common in adenocarcinomas)
- **Co-occurring mutations**: KRAS mutations often co-occur with [[LKB1]]/[[STK11]] loss, [[KEAP1]]/[[NRF2]] alterations, or [[TP53]] mutations.

## Role in Senescence & STING

In [[Lung Cancer]], KRAS mutations co-occurring with [[LKB1]] loss (KL genotype) lead to silencing of [[STING]] through [[DNMT1]]-mediated [[DNA methylation]]. This results in reduced [[type I interferon]] signaling, lower [[PD-L1]] expression, and immune evasion. KL tumors show resistance to [[Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors]].

## Therapeutic Targeting

- **Direct inhibitors**: [[Sotorasib]] ([[AMG 510]]) and [[Adagrasib]] ([[MRTX849]]) target KRAS G12C in NSCLC.
- **Pan-KRAS inhibitors**: Emerging agents targeting the active state (e.g., [[RMC-6236]]).
- **Indirect targeting**: [[MEK inhibitors]], [[ERK inhibitors]], combination strategies.
