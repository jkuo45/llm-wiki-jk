# LeucoMB

**LeucoMB** is the reduced (leuco) form of [[Methylene blue|methylene blue (MB)]], a tricyclic phenothiazine. In its catalytic redox cycle, MB accepts electrons from [[Mitochondria|complex I]] to become LeucoMB, which then donates those electrons to complex IV, regenerating MB — a cycle that enhances electron transport and protects against oxidative damage.

## Mechanism

- **Redox cycling**: MB + e⁻ (from complex I) → LeucoMB; LeucoMB → MB + e⁻ (to complex IV, cytochrome c oxidase). This shuttling bypasses complex III inefficiencies and outcompetes oxygen for electrons, limiting ROS.

## Role in Senescence (Senotherapeutic)

- MB/LeucoMB redox cycling enhances complex IV activity, elevates the [[NAD+]]/[[NADH]] ratio, improves ΔΨm, reduces lipid peroxidation, and increases ATP in disease models.
- In aging models, MB extends the replicative lifespan of normal human fibroblasts and reduces SA-β-gal activity and [[p16 INK4a|p16]] in aged primary human fibroblasts, suggesting improved mitochondrial function delays senescence onset. LeucoMB is the active electron-donating intermediate enabling these effects.
