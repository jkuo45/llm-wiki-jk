# Glyceraldehyde 3-Phosphate

**Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (G3P)** is a three-carbon triose phosphate central to [[Glycolysis|glycolysis]]. It is oxidized and phosphorylated to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate by glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase ([[GAPDH]]), a reaction that consumes [[NAD+]] to produce NADH and is the primary glycolytic step generating reducing equivalents.

## Mechanism

- **GAPDH step**: Glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate + Pi + NAD+ → 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate + NADH + H+. This couples carbon oxidation to cytosolic NAD+ consumption.
- **Upstream/downstream**: G3P is produced from fructose 1,6-bisphosphate by aldolase and feeds the lower glycolytic pathway toward pyruvate and ATP.

## Relevance to Senescence

- [[NAD+]] is required for the GAPDH reaction. In senescent cells the NAD+/NADH ratio falls (due to reduced [[Citric Acid Cycle|cycle]]/[[Oxidative Phosphorylation|ETC]] flux and NAD+ consumption by repair pathways), which **inhibits glycolysis and ATP production**.
- The resulting rise in the AMP+ADP/[[ATP]] ratio activates [[AMPK]], reinforcing senescent phenotypes (cell-cycle arrest via p53/p21, [[SASP]] modulation) and driving mitochondrial biogenesis as a compensatory response.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: States GAPDH converts glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate using NAD+, and that the lowered NAD+/NADH ratio in senescence inhibits glycolysis/ATP and raises AMP/ATP, activating AMPK.
