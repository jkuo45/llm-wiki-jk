# Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns

**Damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)** are endogenous molecules released by stressed, damaged, or dying cells that alert the innate immune system. In [[Senescence|cellular senescence]], mitochondrial DAMPs are central to sustaining the pro-inflammatory [[SASP]] and linking mitochondrial dysfunction to inflammation and aging.

## Major Mitochondrial DAMPs in Senescence

- **mtDNA**: Released into cytosol/extracellular space via minority MOMP ([[miMOMP]]). Contains unmethylated [[CpG Islands]] recognized by [[TLR9]], and is sensed in the cytosol by [[cGAS]]→[[STING]] to induce [[Type I Interferon|type I interferons]] and SASP factors. Circulating mtDNA rises with age and tracks inflammatory cytokines.
- **Cardiolipin**: Accumulates in senescent cells; can itself induce senescence and activates the [[NLRP3]] inflammasome.
- **TFAM (mitochondrial transcription factor A)**: Extracellular TFAM is pro-inflammatory; when bound to mtDNA it engages [[RAGE]] on [[Plasmacytoid Dendritic Cells|plasmacytoid dendritic cells]], enabling internalization and TLR9 recognition.
- **Oxidized mtDNA** also activates NLRP3, promoting [[IL-1β]] secretion and NF-κB activation; mtDNA synthesis is required for NLRP3 engagement.

## Role in Senescence

- Mitochondrial DAMP release converts mitochondrial dysfunction into innate immune activation, making DAMPs the mechanistic bridge between metabolic defects and the SASP.
- Blocking mtDNA release (e.g., BAX inhibition) reduces SASP expression in bone and improves healthspan measures, validating DAMPs as SASP drivers.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: Surveys release of DAMPs (mtDNA, cardiolipin, TFAM) in senescence and their activation of cGAS-STING, TLR9, and NLRP3 to drive the SASP; notes more work is needed to fully elucidate DAMP contributions.
