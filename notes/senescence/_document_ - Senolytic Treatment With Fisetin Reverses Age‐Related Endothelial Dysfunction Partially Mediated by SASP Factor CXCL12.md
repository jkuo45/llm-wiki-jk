## ABSTRACT

Advancing age is the strongest risk factor for [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular diseases]] ([[Cardiovascular Disease|CVDs]]), primarily due to progressive vascular [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]]. [[Senescence]] and the [[SASP|senescence‐associated secretory phenotype (SASP)]] contribute to age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] by promoting [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] and [[Inflammation]], which reduce [[Nitric Oxide|nitric oxide (NO)]] [[Bioavailability]]. However, the molecular changes in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells (ECs)]] and their role in [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] with aging remain unclear. As such, we sought to identify the EC-related signaling pathways, endothelial-associated [[SASP]] factors, and their impact on endothelial function with aging. [[Single-cell RNA-seq|Single-cell transcriptomics]] was performed on aortas from young (6mos) and old (27mos) female and male mice with and without in vivo senolytic treatment with [[Fisetin]] (100 mg/kg/day administered intermittently) to characterize EC senescence and transcript expression changes. Circulating levels of [[SASP]] factors were measured to assess systemic changes associated with aging and fisetin treatment. Plasma exposure experiments were conducted in isolated mouse arteries and cultured human aortic [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] to determine the causal role of the circulating [[SASP]] milieu and specific [[SASP]] factors in mediating [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and underlying mechanisms of action. Senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] exhibited elevated expression of [[SASP]] factors, particularly *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]*, which was reversed by fisetin supplementation, with responses also reflected in circulating [[CXCL12]] concentrations. Plasma from old mice impaired endothelial function by inducing vascular cell senescence, reducing [[Nitric Oxide|NO]], increasing mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], shifting receptor and promoting [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT)]]—effects partially driven by [[CXCL12]] and prevented by fisetin. These results identify the [[SASP]] and [[CXCL12]] as drivers of age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and establish mechanisms of senolytic intervention with fisetin supplementation.

**Keywords:** cellular senescence, CXCL12, endothelial dysfunction, senolytics, vascular aging

---

> [!important] Key Finding
> Single-cell transcriptomics identifies that oral, intermittent [[Fisetin]] supplementation improves endothelial function by targeting senescent [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cell]]-derived [[SASP]] factors and [[CXCL12]] in old animals. The [[SASP]] and specifically [[CXCL12]] were found to drive age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] by promoting vascular cell senescence, stimulating mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], reducing [[Nitric Oxide|nitric oxide]] [[Bioavailability]], and supporting endothelial cell transdifferentiation ([[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]]).

## 1. Introduction

Advancing age is the primary risk factor for [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular diseases]] ([[Cardiovascular Disease|CVDs]]), which remain the leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide (Olshansky et al. [^32]; Martin et al. [^28]). Impaired vascular [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] is a key antecedent to the development of clinical [[Cardiovascular Disease|CVDs]] with aging (Lakatta and Levy [^20]). The hallmark of aging, [[Senescence]], directly contributes to age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]], in part by promoting chronic [[Inflammation]] and mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] (López-Otín et al. [^22]; Campisi [^3]; Clayton et al. [^5]; Campisi and d'Adda di Fagagna [^4]). Despite the adverse effects of [[Senescence]] on endothelial function, the molecular changes that occur in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells (ECs)]] and the direct mechanisms by which senescent cells induce [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] with advanced age remain incompletely defined.

A putative mechanism by which senescent cells drive physiological dysfunction is through the production and secretion of the circulating [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP)]] milieu, a collection of [[Cytokines|cytokines]], [[Chemokine|chemokines]], [[Protease|proteases]], and [[Growth Factor|growth factors]] (Coppé et al. [^6]). The circulating [[SASP]] milieu is highly heterogeneous and dependent on cell type and [[Senescence]] inducer. Thus, the specific [[SASP]] factors that are upregulated with aging in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] in vivo remain to be determined. The circulating [[SASP]] milieu may induce cellular and tissue-level dysfunction by driving mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], chronic [[Inflammation]], induction of [[Senescence]] in neighboring cells/tissues, and cell transdifferentiation (i.e., [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndoMT)]]) (Coppé et al. [^6]). Importantly, the circulating [[SASP]] milieu is in constant and direct contact with the endothelium, and changes that occur in the circulation contribute directly to age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] (Bloom et al. [^2]; Mahoney et al. [^26]). As such, [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] may be more susceptible to entering a senescent state given the constant interaction with the circulating [[SASP]] milieu (Bloom et al. [^2]; Yousefzadeh et al. [^45]). However, the circulating [[SASP]] factors originating from senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and their potential impact on endothelial function remain incompletely understood. As such, gaining an EC-specific understanding of [[Senescence]]- and circulating [[SASP]] milieu-related molecular mechanisms within the endothelium may be exploited as translational diagnostic biomarkers, and may serve as putative therapeutic targets for novel interventions.

[[Senolytic|Senolytics]]—compounds that selectively target and eliminate excess [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] (Kirkland and Tchkonia [^18]; Zhu et al. [^49])—have demonstrated efficacy for suppressing excess vascular cell senescence and improving vascular function in old mice (Clayton et al. [^5]; Mahoney, Venkatasubramanian, et al. [^27]; Roos et al. [^35]). Although several [[Senolytic|senolytic therapies]] are currently being advanced in preclinical and clinical studies, the natural senolytic [[Fisetin]] shows efficacy in targeting senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] in culture (Zhu et al. [^48]) and evoking favorable effects on [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] with aging (Mahoney, Venkatasubramanian, et al. [^27]), and has an encouraging safety profile for clinical translation (Yousefzadeh et al. [^46]). However, the effects of fisetin on senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and the [[SASP]] in vivo, and how these effects contribute to the beneficial effects of fisetin on age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] are incompletely understood.

In the present study, we sought to characterize the vasculature from young (6 months) and old (27 months) mice, leveraging in vivo fisetin supplementation to explore the molecular, cellular, and physiological mechanisms modulated with aging and [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]]. The central goals of this study were to: (1) assess how aging affects EC senescence in vivo at a single-cell level and identify the key [[Senescence]]-associated signaling pathways; (2) identify [[SASP]] factors upregulated in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] with aging that may modulate endothelial function; and (3) evaluate the contributions of the circulating [[SASP]] milieu and specific [[SASP]] factors in mediating age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]].

We first uncovered that in relation to other vascular cell types, [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] were highly susceptible to becoming senescent with aging and were effectively eliminated by [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]]. Within senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], we identified *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] to be the most highly upregulated with aging and ameliorated with fisetin [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]], and we observed similar patterns in circulating concentrations of [[CXCL12]] protein. Finally, we revealed a causal role of the circulating [[SASP]] milieu in inducing age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] mediated, in part, by the elevated concentration of [[CXCL12]]. Overall, these findings distinguish senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] as senolytic targets, establish the circulating [[SASP]] milieu as a driver of age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]], and identify [[CXCL12]] as a specific circulating [[SASP]] factor that mediates EC senescence and dysfunction with aging that can be modulated with fisetin [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]]. Further, we uncover mechanisms underlying the circulating [[SASP]] milieu- and [[CXCL12]]-mediated [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] including [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] production, mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], and EC transdifferentiation ([[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]]).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1. Animals

Mouse experiments, including the import, housing, experimental procedures, and euthanasia, were performed strictly under an Animal Study Proposal (ASP #496-LCS-2026) and associated amendments, reviewed and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee (ACUC) of the [[National Institute on Aging]] (NIA). All mice were group-housed at 22°C set point ±3 under a standard 12 h light/dark cycle and fed ad libitum. Relative humidity was maintained at 30%–70%. Procedures associated with plasma-mediated vascular [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] experiments were performed and approved by the University of Colorado Boulder Institutional ACUC (procotol 2618).

For the intervention period, young (6 months) and old (27 months) male and female [[C57BL-6|C57BL/6N mice]] were randomly assigned to receive vehicle (10% ethanol, 30% PEG400 and 60% Phosal 50 PG) or [[Fisetin]] (100 mg/kg/day in vehicle). Treatment was administered via oral gavage using an intermittent dosing paradigm—1 week on; 2 weeks off; 1 week on, as previously established senolytic dosing paradigm (Figure [1A](#acel70500-fig-0001)) (Mahoney, Venkatasubramanian, et al. [^27]; Yousefzadeh et al. [^46]).

### 2.2. Aortic Single-Cell RNA Sequencing

Aortic [[Single-cell RNA-seq|single-cell RNA sequencing]] (RNAseq) analysis was performed, as previously described (Mazan-Mamczarz et al. [^29]). Briefly, whole aortas from 2-3 mice (per sex and treatment group) were collected, cleaned and digested. Viable cells were sorted using [[Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting]] for [[10x Genomics]] library preparation. The single-cell libraries were prepared with Chromium Next GEM Single Cell 3' Kit v3.1 ([[10x Genomics]]) with Chromium Next GEM Chip G Single Cell Kit ([[10x Genomics]]) according to the manufacturer's protocol with Chromium Controller ([[10x Genomics]]). ~10,000 single cells were used for GEM generation, and the cDNAs were then checked on the Agilent Bioanalyzer with High Sensitivity DNA kit (Agilent). Prepared cDNAs were then used for library preparation, that were checked on the Agilent Bioanalyzer with the DNA 1000 kit (Agilent Technologies). The libraries were sequenced with an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencer at a depth of 70,000–120,000 reads per cell. Sequencing data are deposited in the NCBI's Gene Expression Omnibus repository GSE296698.

Single-cell RNAseq data were processed using Cell Ranger (version 8.0.1) with the mouse reference GRCm39-2024-A ([[10x Genomics]]). The obtained read count matrices were subsequently analyzed in R using the Seurat package, version 5.2.0, with default parameters in all functions, unless otherwise specified (Hao et al. [^12]). Differentially abundant RNA (DAR) testing was performed with the following cutoffs: adjusted *p*-value < 0.05, average log2 fold change > 0.25, and minimum percentage of cells in either group > 0.1 (Mazan-Mamczarz et al. [^29]). Pathway analysis was performed using [[KEGG]] (version 111.0) (Kanehisa et al. [^17]) and [[Reactome]] (version 90) (Milacic et al. [^30]) and was based on DAR testing. Inference and analysis of cell-cell communication was performed using [[CellChat]] (version 2.1.2) (Jin et al. [^16]).

### 2.3. Plasma Protein Quantification

For multiplex analysis, custom murine Luminex Assay kits were designed by R&D Biosystems and executed according to the manufacturer's instructions.

### 2.4. Plasma-Mediated Vascular Endothelial Function

To assess the role of the circulating [[SASP]] milieu (plasma) and [[CXCL12]] on [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]], an ex vivo isolated artery bioassay was leveraged, as previously described (Mahoney et al. [^26]). In brief, carotid arteries were excised from young (3-6 months), intervention-naïve wildtype mice and cannulated onto pressure myographs. Plasma collected from young vehicle, old vehicle, and old fisetin-treated mice was diluted to 5% and perfused luminally through the pressurized arteries for 24 h prior to assessing [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]]. Following plasma perfusion, [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] was measured by endothelium-dependent dilation ([[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation|EDD]]) and endothelium-independent dilation (EID) in response to increasing doses of [[Acetylcholine]] (ACh) and [[Sodium Nitroprusside]] (SNP), respectively, as described previously (Mahoney et al. [^26]; Mahoney, Venkatasubramanian, et al. [^27]). All dose-response data are presented as percent dilation relative to maximum diameter to account for differences in baseline vessel diameter.

### 2.5. Cultured ECs

Human aortic [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] ([[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]]) were grown in basal media supplemented with 5% plasma collected from young, old vehicle, and old fisetin-treated mice. EC [[Nitric Oxide|nitric oxide (NO)]] and mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity were assessed after 2 h plasma incubation by staining with the fluorescent probe Hoechst (nuclei stain; Thermo Fisher) and either 10 μM diaminorhodamine-4M AM (DAR-4M AM; Sigma-Aldrich; to quantify NO production) for 45 min or 5 μM [[MitoSOX]] (Thermo Fisher; to quantify [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] superoxide bioactivity) for 30 min. [[Senescence-Associated Beta-Galactosidase]] (SA-β-Gal) staining and RNA extractions for gene expression were performed after 24 h of plasma exposure.

### 2.6. CXCL12 Protein Addition and Inhibition

Recombinant mouse [[CXCL12]] protein (R&D Systems, Cat. #460-SD-050/CF) in sterile [[Phosphate-Buffered Saline|PBS]] was added back to plasma from young and old fisetin-treated mice, such that the concentration of [[CXCL12]] matched the average levels in old vehicle-treated mice (3210 pg/mL) for CXCL12 add-back experiments. 1 μg/mL of [[LIT-927]] (Selleckchem, Cat. #S8813) (Xiong et al. [^44]) in DMSO was added to the old vehicle plasma for CXCL12 inhibition experiments.

## 3. Results

### 3.1. Whole Aortic Single-Cell Transcriptomics Identifies Senescent Cell Population With Aging That Are Ameliorated With Senolytic Treatment

To gain insights into the vascular transcriptional changes that occur with aging, we leveraged [[Single-cell RNA-seq|single-cell RNAseq]] to compare aortas from young (6 months) and old (27 months) [[C57BL-6|C57BL/6N mice]]. To study the effects of in vivo [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] on the vasculature, we supplemented young and old mice with vehicle or [[Fisetin]], as previously described (Mahoney, Venkatasubramanian, et al. [^27]) (Figure [1A](#acel70500-fig-0001)). [[Single-cell RNA-seq]] workflows allow for the characterization of transcriptomic profiles from distinct cell types of the aorta to identify cell type-specific changes with aging (Mahoney, Dey, et al. [^25]). Unsupervised clustering of the merged samples revealed 11 distinct cell types in the aorta (Figure [1A](#acel70500-fig-0001), Figure [S1](#acel70500-supitem-0001)).

We next performed whole-aorta pseudo-bulk RNAseq to determine the general transcriptomic changes with aging and [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] using differentially abundant RNA (DAR) analysis. Age-related differences (young vs. old vehicle) revealed 4589 DARs (2859 higher, 1730 lower) (Figure [1B](#acel70500-fig-0001)). [[Fisetin]] supplementation in old mice unveiled 3143 DARs with more reduced transcripts than elevated ones (1401 higher, 1742 lower) compared to old vehicle (Figure [1B](#acel70500-fig-0001)). Old fisetin mice had a more similar transcriptomic profile to young vehicle mice (974 higher, 931 lower) compared to old vehicle mice (Figure [1B](#acel70500-fig-0001)). The young groups (young vehicle vs. young fisetin) were the least different with 628 DARs (240 higher, 388 lower) (Figure [1B](#acel70500-fig-0001)). [[Reactome]] pathway analysis revealed [[Senescence]] as the most highly upregulated pathway with aging, followed by pathways related to immune dysregulation and cell cycle arrest (Figure [1C](#acel70500-fig-0001)).

Given the changes in [[Senescence]] pathways observed in the vasculature with advanced age, we next sought to assess how [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] were affected relative to other vascular cell types. First, we assessed canonical [[Senescence]] transcripts *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]* and *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]* and key [[SASP]] factor transcripts to identify upregulations in endothelial, [[Macrophages|macrophage]], neutrophil-like, and neural cells (Figure [1D,E](#acel70500-fig-0001)). To gain insight into the cell senescence-related transcripts in the vasculature that were modulated with aging and [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]], we assessed genes in the holistic senescent cell signature [[SenMayo]] (Saul et al. [^36]) that were upregulated with aging (old vehicle vs. young vehicle) and downregulated with [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] (old fisetin vs. old vehicle). We found differences in transcripts involved in the [[SASP]], cell cycle arrest maintenance, and pro-inflammatory signaling (Figure [1F](#acel70500-fig-0001)). Next, we leveraged module scoring of [[SenMayo]]; we identified [[Senescent Cells|senescent cell]] populations in several vascular cell types, including endothelial, [[Macrophages|macrophage]], and neutrophil-like cell populations in aortas from old vehicle mice (Figure [1G](#acel70500-fig-0001)). In the aortas from old mice, [[Fisetin]] had the greatest effects on the [[SenMayo]] signature in the EC population, reinforcing the senolytic effects of fisetin on senescent EC burden with aging (Figure [1G](#acel70500-fig-0001)). Together, these data demonstrate a transcriptional shift in the vasculature with aging and suggest that [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with fisetin in old mice returns the transcriptome towards a young aortic cell composition by mitigating vascular cell senescence burden, particularly in the endothelium.

### 3.2. Senescent EC Burden Increases in the Aorta With Aging and Is Ameliorated by Senolytic Treatment

We next performed a targeted interrogation of the [[Senescence]]-related changes in the vascular EC population with aging and [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]]. To isolate EC-specific changes, we performed unsupervised clustering of the EC cluster, which produced 12 unique subclusters based on shared transcript expression patterns (Figure [2A](#acel70500-fig-0002)). EC composition revealed minimal differences between treatment groups in the majority of subclusters, with the exception of subcluster 10, which increased with age (10.7-fold, *p* < 0.0001) and reduced (0.02-fold, *p* < 0.0001) back to young levels in old mice supplemented with [[Fisetin]] (Figure [2B](#acel70500-fig-0002)).

We next leveraged the EC-specific senescent signature [[EndoSen]] (Guduric-Fuchs et al. [^11]) to determine which subclusters were enriched by [[Senescence]]-associated signaling pathways. [[EndoSen]] module scoring revealed [[Senescence]] enrichment in endothelial subclusters 4, 7, 9, and 10, with the highest expression being in subcluster 10 (Figure [2C](#acel70500-fig-0002)). Moreover, endothelial subcluster 10 had the highest [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression of the canonical [[Senescence]] and [[SASP]] markers compared to the other subclusters (Figure [2D](#acel70500-fig-0002)).

To further characterize the senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], we leveraged DAR testing on subcluster 10 to identify gene targets that are modulated with aging and [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]]. Age-related differences (old vs. young vehicle) revealed 435 DARs (278 increased, 135 reduced), suggesting a large impact of aging on senescent endothelial subcluster 10 (Figure [2E](#acel70500-fig-0002)). [[Fisetin]] supplementation in old mice unveiled 211 DARs with more reduced than elevated transcripts (34 increased, 177 decreased) (Figure [2E](#acel70500-fig-0002)). Notably, no transcripts were significantly modulated between old fisetin and young vehicle or between the young mouse groups, suggesting that senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] from old mice that received [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] have a similar transcriptomic profile to that of [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] from young mice and that fisetin has minimal effects on young, healthy [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] (Figure [2E](#acel70500-fig-0002)).

The top increased DARs that distinguished the putative senescent subcluster 10 from other EC subclusters represented transcripts involved in [[Extracellular Matrix]] remodeling, stress signaling, cell cycle maintenance, and the [[SASP]] (Figure [2F](#acel70500-fig-0002)). Pathway analysis of the DARs from senescent endothelial subcluster 10 revealed pathways involved in cardiovascular pathology (i.e., [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Cardiomyopathy]]), [[Senescence]], [[Cancer]], and a general dysregulation of signaling pathways (Figure [2G](#acel70500-fig-0002)). Finally, we identified the top 50 DARs that distinguished the old vehicle group from the other treatment conditions, representing transcripts that were most increased with aging and lower with [[Fisetin]] supplementation in endothelial subcluster 10 (Figure [2H](#acel70500-fig-0002)). We found that these transcripts largely encompass canonical senescence markers (e.g., *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]*) and [[SASP]] factors. Together, these findings demonstrate increased expression of [[Senescence]]- and [[SASP]]-related signaling pathways in [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] with aging that were ameliorated by [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]].

### 3.3. Senolytic Treatment Mitigates the Endothelial-Derived and Circulating SASP in Old Mice

The [[SASP]] represents a collection of pro-inflammatory molecules including [[Cytokines|cytokines]], [[Chemokine|chemokines]], [[Protease|proteases]], and [[Growth Factor|growth factors]] that have varied roles in modulating physiological function (Coppé et al. [^6]; Wang et al. [^40]). Our next aim was to identify candidate circulating [[SASP]] factors based on those differentially expressed in the endothelium, particularly those driving the patterns observed in senescent EC subcluster 10. In EC subcluster 10, we observed several [[SASP]] factors with increased transcripts with aging (young vehicle v. old vehicle) that were reduced with [[Fisetin]] (old vehicle v. old fisetin), including *[[CCL4|Ccl4]]*, *[[CCL7|Ccl7]]*, *[[TIMP4|Timp4]]*, *[[IGFBP3|Igfbp3]]*, *[[S100A9|S100a9]]*, *[[CCL8|Ccl8]]*, *[[FGF1|Fgf1]]*, *[[GDF15|Gdf15]]*, *[[PAI-1|Serpine1]]*, and *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* [[MRNA|mRNAs]] (Figure [3A](#acel70500-fig-0003)).

Provided the changes observed in [[MRNA|mRNAs]] encoding [[SASP]] factors from senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], we sought to determine if these factors were translated and secreted into the circulating plasma. We selected [[SASP]] proteins to measure based on: (a) the magnitude of age-related differences in senescent endothelial transcript expression and whether these differences with aging were influenced by [[Fisetin]] supplementation; and (b) for biological plausibility of altering [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]]. We measured the protein concentration of candidate [[SASP]] factors in the plasma from treated animals. Among these factors, higher concentrations with aging were observed in [[CCL8]], [[FGF Basic]], [[S100A9]], [[PAI-1]], and [[CXCL12]] when comparing young vehicle and old vehicle mice (Figure [3B](#acel70500-fig-0003)). Notably, the elevated [[PAI-1]] and [[CXCL12]] protein expression with aging was ameliorated by [[Fisetin]] supplementation in old animals (Figure [3B](#acel70500-fig-0003)).

[[PAI-1]], a platelet protein that inhibits fibrin degradation (Morrow et al. [^31]), is a well-established marker of [[Senescence]] (Vaughan et al. [^39]). [[CXCL12]] is a [[Chemokines|chemokine]] that regulates [[Angiogenesis]] (Ara et al. [^1]) and is implicated in atherosclerotic progression (Döring et al. [^7]). Importantly, [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] are a key vascular source of *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* (Figure [S2A,B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)), and endothelial-associated [[CXCL12]] is a key driver of [[Cardiovascular Disease|CVD]] <sup>30</sup>. Although *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* is expressed in non-senescent cells, it is upregulated in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and highly correlated to canonical [[Senescence]] and [[SASP]] transcripts (Figure [S2C-E](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The enrichment of *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* in this specific subpopulation suggests that it may serve as a localized source of [[SASP]] signaling, potentially influencing neighboring [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] through paracrine or autocrine mechanisms. Given the novelty and putative biological plausibility of [[CXCL12]] in regulating [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] (whereas [[PAI-1]] primarily affects platelets), we next sought to investigate the targets of senescent EC-associated [[CXCL12]].

### 3.4. Senolytic Treatment Mitigates Senescent EC-Derived CXCL12 Signaling

To understand how senescent EC-derived [[CXCL12]] influences the aged vasculature, we performed an unbiased cell-cell communication analysis using the bioinformatic tool, [[CellChat]] (Jin et al. [^16]), on the [[Single-cell RNA-seq|single-cell RNAseq]] dataset. Mapping of senescent EC-derived [[CXCL12]] with its 2 receptors, *[[ACKR3|Ackr3]]* and *[[CXCR4|Cxcr4]]*, revealed contrasting interaction patterns (Figure [4A](#acel70500-fig-0004)). *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]-[[ACKR3|Ackr3]]* interactions were higher in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] from old vehicle communicating with other senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], non-senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], [[B Cell|B cells]], [[Macrophages|macrophages]], and [[T Cell|T cells]], suggesting increased autocrine signaling and immune cell signaling with aging (Figure [4A](#acel70500-fig-0004)). In contrast, *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]-[[CXCR4|Cxcr4]]* interactions were lower in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] from old vehicle communicating with other senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], non-senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], [[Fibroblast|fibroblast]] and mesothelial cells, suggesting deficient cell-cell communication among vascular cells (Figure [4A](#acel70500-fig-0004)). Importantly, both these trends were reversed in old animals treated with [[Fisetin]], suggesting a return to physiological interactions.

Differential interaction strength analysis showed that the senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] exhibited a robust increase in "outgoing" interaction strength compared to all other identified cell types (Figure [4B,C](#acel70500-fig-0004)). These cells established extensive communication pathways targeting non-senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]], and various immune populations, including [[Macrophages|macrophages]] and [[Neutrophils|neutrophils]] (Figure [4B,C](#acel70500-fig-0004)). Notably, we observed a strong self-signaling signal within the senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], suggesting an autocrine signaling that may serve to reinforce the senescent phenotype (Figure [4B,C](#acel70500-fig-0004)).

To determine if [[CXCL12]] was working through this autocrine manner, we first looked at the expression of receptors *[[ACKR3|Ackr3]]* and *[[CXCR4|Cxcr4]]* in the senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]]. Consistently, we found that while *[[ACKR3|Ackr3]]* [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] levels were lower in the old vehicle mice compared to the other groups, *[[CXCR4|Cxcr4]]* [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] were higher in the old vehicle mice compared to the other groups (Figure [4D](#acel70500-fig-0004)). Together, these findings suggest that senescent EC-derived [[CXCL12]] may act in an autocrine manner to fuel EC senescence and dysfunction. As such, we next sought to determine if [[CXCL12]] was directly implicated in age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] transduced by the circulating [[SASP]] milieu and if mitigation of elevated [[CXCL12]] was a mechanism by which [[Fisetin]] supplementation elicited its benefits on [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] with aging.

### 3.5. Senolytic Treatment Attenuates Circulating SASP Milieu-Induced Endothelial Dysfunction With Aging

> [!info] Key Mechanistic Finding
> *Circulating SASP milieu- and CXCL12-induced endothelial dysfunction.* [[CXCL12]] was identified as a critical circulating [[SASP]] factor mediating age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]]. [[Fisetin]] supplementation reduced circulating [[CXCL12]] levels, and [[CXCL12]] inhibition with [[LIT-927]] rescued endothelial function.

Given the observation that select circulating [[SASP]] factors changed with aging and [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]], we next aimed to evaluate the functional role of the circulating [[SASP]] environment on age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and the specific effects of [[CXCL12]] as a candidate circulating [[SASP]] factor that might be responsible for transducing the effect of the circulating environment on [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]]. We first tested the effects of the plasma from the young vehicle, old vehicle, and old fisetin groups on [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] using carotid artery endothelium-dependent dilation ([[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation|EDD]]) following intraluminal plasma perfusion. Average peak [[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation|EDD]] was lower following exposure to plasma from old vehicle mice compared to young mice (−17%; *p* < 0.0001), suggesting that exposure to the aged systemic environment is sufficient to impair [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] (Figure [5A](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S3B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Peak [[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation|EDD]] was 14% higher following exposure to plasma from old fisetin supplemented mice compared to plasma from old vehicle mice (*p* = 0.001) (Figure [5A](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S3A,B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)).

To evaluate the functional contributions of circulating [[CXCL12]] to the impairment observed with the aged [[SASP]] milieu, we added back recombinant mouse [[CXCL12]] to old fisetin plasma such that the average concentrations of plasma [[CXCL12]] were similar to that of old vehicle plasma. The addition of [[CXCL12]] to old fisetin plasma reduced peak [[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation|EDD]] compared to old fisetin plasma alone (−22%, *p* < 0.0001), ameliorating differences with the old vehicle group (*p* = 0.465) (Figure [5A](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S3A,B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). No differences in endothelium-independent dilation were observed between any of the groups or conditions (*p* = 0.342), indicating that differences observed in response to plasma exposure occurred in an endothelium-dependent manner (Figure [5B](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S3C,D](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Together, these data suggest that the circulating [[SASP]] milieu underlies age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and its detrimental effects are associated with elevated [[CXCL12]] and largely reversed by [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]].

> [!info] CXCL12 Drives Vascular Cell Senescence
> *Circulating SASP- and CXCL12-induced vascular cell senescence.* One mechanism by which the [[SASP]] elicits its adverse effects is through a humoral-based induction of [[Senescence]] (Clayton et al. [^5]; Wang et al. [^40]). As such, to investigate the mechanisms by which the circulating [[SASP]] modulates [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]], we sought to assess SASP-induced [[Senescence]] by measuring the canonical marker of senescent cell burden [[Senescence-Associated Beta-Galactosidase]] (SA-β-Gal) intensity (Kurz et al. [^19]; González-Gualda et al. [^10]) in arteries (aortic rings) exposed to plasma from young vehicle, old vehicle, and old fisetin mice. We found that when normalized to total cell count there was an increase in SA-β-Gal positive cells in arteries exposed to old versus young plasma (6.1-fold, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5C](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). SA-β-Gal positive cells were lower in arteries exposed to plasma from old fisetin-treated mice compared to old vehicle mice (−86%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5C](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The addition of exogenous [[CXCL12]] to old fisetin plasma increased arterial SA-β-Gal positive cells (4.8-fold, *p* < 0.0001) compared to old fisetin plasma alone (Figure [5D](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)).

To isolate the effects of plasma exposure on [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]], we exposed plasma from the treated animals to cultured [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells]] ([[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]]). Similar to arteries, [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old vehicle plasma had greater SA-β-Gal intensity than [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to young vehicle plasma (5.6-fold, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5D](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). SA-β-Gal positive cells were lower in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to plasma from old fisetin-treated mice compared to old vehicle mice (−56%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5D](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Inhibition of [[CXCL12]] with [[LIT-927]] (a neutraligand of [[CXCL12]] (Regenass et al. [^34])) in old vehicle plasma reduced SA-β-Gal positive cells (−44%, *p* < 0.0001) compared to old vehicle plasma alone (Figure [5D](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The addition of exogenous [[CXCL12]] to plasma increased SA-β-Gal positive cells in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old fisetin plasma (82%, *p* < 0.0001) compared to old fisetin plasma alone (Figure [5D](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)).

In addition to SA-β-Gal activity, we confirmed the effects of the [[SASP]] and [[CXCL12]] on [[Senescence]] through the endothelial [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression of the canonical [[Senescence]] biomarkers *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]* and *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]*. We found that [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to plasma from old vehicle mice had higher *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]* (45-fold, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]* (6.2-fold, *p* < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to young plasma (Figure [5E,F](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Relative to old vehicle plasma exposure, old fisetin plasma exposure resulted in lower *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]* (−85%, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]* (−57%, p < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression (Figure [5E,F](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4C,D](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The addition of [[LIT-927]] to old vehicle plasma reduced *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]* (−59%, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]* (−62%, p < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to old vehicle plasma alone (Figure [5E,F](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4C,D](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The addition of [[CXCL12]] to old fisetin plasma increased *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]* (100%, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]* (128%, p < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to old fisetin plasma alone (Figure [5E,F](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S4C,D](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). In combination, these data indicate that the circulating [[SASP]] milieu directly induces vascular EC senescence, in part through [[CXCL12]], and that circulating [[SASP]] milieu-induced senescence with aging may be prevented by [[Fisetin]] supplementation.

> [!info] CXCL12 Impairs NO and Increases Mitochondrial ROS
> *Circulating SASP- and CXCL12-induced NO production and mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity.* Age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] is primarily mediated by reduced [[Nitric Oxide|nitric oxide (NO)]] [[Bioavailability]], which is a key feature of senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] (Hayashi et al. [^13]). Therefore, we assessed NO production in cultured [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] exposed to plasma, examining the effects of both the addition and inhibition of [[CXCL12]]. This allowed us to determine the direct role of [[CXCL12]] in mediating differences in EC NO production induced by the circulating [[SASP]] milieu in the context of aging and [[Fisetin]] supplementation. [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old vehicle plasma exhibited lower NO production compared to young plasma (−24%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5G](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). This age-related impairment in NO production was prevented in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old fisetin plasma (20% vs. old vehicle plasma, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5G](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Compared to plasma alone, the addition of exogenous [[CXCL12]] impaired NO production in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to young vehicle (−20%, *p* < 0.0001) and old fisetin (−13%, *p* < 0.0001) plasma (Figure [5G](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Further, [[CXCL12]] inhibition by [[LIT-927]] increased NO production in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old vehicle plasma (10%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5G](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5A](#acel70500-supitem-0001)).

Reduced NO production may occur as a result of [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], as excessive superoxide scavenges NO to reduce its [[Bioavailability]] (Erusalimsky [^8]). Thus, we sought to determine whether the circulating [[SASP]] milieu increased mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity and if this was mediated by [[CXCL12]]. To do so, we next measured [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]]-derived superoxide bioactivity in isolated arteries (Figure [5H](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)) and cultured [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] (Figure [5I](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5C](#acel70500-supitem-0001)) following plasma exposure with the addition and inhibition of [[CXCL12]]. Arterial mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity was higher in aortas exposed to old vehicle plasma compared to young plasma (3.5-fold, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5H](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity was lower in arteries exposed to plasma from old fisetin-treated mice compared to old vehicle-treated mice (−58%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5H](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Compared to old fisetin plasma alone, the addition of exogenous [[CXCL12]] increased mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity (2.2-fold, *p* = 0.001) (Figure [5H](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)).

Similar to arteries, [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old vehicle plasma had greater basal mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity than young plasma (14%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5I](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5C](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity was lower in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to plasma from old fisetin-treated mice compared to old vehicle mice (−6%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5I](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5C](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). [[CXCL12]] inhibition with [[LIT-927]] reduced mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old vehicle plasma (−10%, *p* < 0.0001) (Figure [5I](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5C](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Exogenous [[CXCL12]] induced mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to old fisetin plasma (5%, *p* = 0.029) (Figure [5I](#acel70500-fig-0005), Figure [S5C](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). Together, these data suggest that the circulating [[SASP]] milieu impairs NO production and promotes excess mitochondrial superoxide bioactivity in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and these SASP-mediated differences are driven in part by [[CXCL12]] and may be prevented by [[Fisetin]] supplementation.

### 3.6. Senolytic Treatment Mitigates Endothelial-To-Mesenchymal Transition (EndoMT) With Aging

[[CXCL12]] plays a crucial role in promoting the [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] ([[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]]), a transdifferentiation process by which [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] lose their characteristic traits while acquiring the contractile features of [[Mesenchymal Stem Cells|mesenchymal cells]] (Fleenor et al. [^9]; Ramadhiani et al. [^33]; Mahoney et al. [^24]). Importantly, the [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]] is highly implicated in vascular aging and previous studies report shifts in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] towards more contractile properties and reduced NO production (Fleenor et al. [^9]; Ramadhiani et al. [^33]). As such, we next sought to determine if the [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]] was an underlying mechanism mediating SASP- and [[CXCL12]]-induced dysfunction in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]]. To do so, we first investigated whether senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] exhibit characteristics of [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]] and then assessed whether [[Fisetin]] supplementation could prevent [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]] induced by the circulating [[SASP]] and [[CXCL12]]. We initially measured transcripts specific to [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and [[Mesenchymal Stem Cells|mesenchymal cells]] in the senescent endothelial subcluster 10 and found that the old vehicle group demonstrated reduced endothelial characteristics and higher mesenchymal markers compared to the other groups (Figure [6A](#acel70500-fig-0006)).

To determine whether the age-related circulating [[SASP]] milieu promotes [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]] and whether these effects are mediated by [[CXCL12]], we exposed [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] to plasma from treated mice, either with [[CXCL12]] added or inhibited. We then assessed the expression of the most affected endothelial markers (*[[CDH5|Cdh5]]* and *[[PECAM1|Pecam1]]* [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]]) and mesenchymal markers (*[[TGFB1|Tgfb1]]* and *[[ACTA2|Acta2]]* [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]]). [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to plasma from aged vehicle-treated mice showed significantly reduced [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression of *[[CDH5|Cdh5]]* (−72%, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[PECAM1|Pecam1]]* (−54%, *p* < 0.0001) compared to cells exposed to plasma from young mice. This age-related increase was mitigated by exposure to old fisetin plasma in *[[CDH5|Cdh5]]* (3.1-fold, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[PECAM1|Pecam1]]* (2-fold, *p* < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to old vehicle plasma (Figure [6B,C](#acel70500-fig-0006), Figure [S6A,B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The addition of [[LIT-927]] to old vehicle plasma increased *[[CDH5|Cdh5]]* (4-fold, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[PECAM1|Pecam1]]* (2.4-fold, p < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to old vehicle plasma alone (Figure [6B,C](#acel70500-fig-0006), Figure [S6A,B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The addition of [[CXCL12]] to old fisetin plasma reduced *[[CDH5|Cdh5]]* (−45%, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[PECAM1|Pecam1]]* (−55%, *p* < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to old fisetin plasma alone (Figure [6B,C](#acel70500-fig-0006), Figure [S6A,B](#acel70500-supitem-0001)).

Contrary to the observed changes in endothelial markers, [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]] exposed to plasma from old vehicle mice had higher *[[TGFB1|Tgfb1]]* (21-fold, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[ACTA2|Acta2]]* (7.7-fold, *p* < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to young plasma. Compared to old vehicle plasma, exposure to old fisetin plasma reduced *[[TGFB1|Tgfb1]]* (−88%, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[ACTA2|Acta2]]* (−89%, *p* < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression (Figure [6D,E](#acel70500-fig-0006), Figure [S6C,D](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). The addition of [[LIT-927]] to old vehicle plasma reduced *[[TGFB1|Tgfb1]]* (−83%, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[ACTA2|Acta2]]* (−76%, p < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to old vehicle plasma alone (Figure [6D,E](#acel70500-fig-0006)). The addition of [[CXCL12]] to old fisetin plasma increased *[[TGFB1|Tgfb1]]* (5.4-fold, *p* < 0.0001) and *[[ACTA2|Acta2]]* (5.4-fold, p < 0.0001) [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] expression compared to old fisetin plasma alone (Figure [6D,E](#acel70500-fig-0006), Figure [S6C,D](#acel70500-supitem-0001)). In combination, these data indicate that the circulating [[SASP]] milieu and [[CXCL12]] promote [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]] in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and that these age-related changes in cell phenotypes are prevented by [[Fisetin]] supplementation.

## 4. Discussion

In the present study, we assessed vascular [[Senescence]] at single-cell resolution in the context of aging and [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]]. Our findings demonstrate that [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] are particularly susceptible to undergoing [[Senescence]] with aging and that fisetin, a naturally occurring [[Senolytic|senolytic]] compound, effectively eliminates senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] in old animals. Across senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and in circulation, several [[SASP]]-associated factors increased with aging and were reduced by fisetin supplementation. Among these, *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] showed one of the most pronounced changes in association with aging and fisetin treatment; however, given the small size of the endothelial subpopulation and the systemic effects of fisetin, we cannot attribute circulating [[CXCL12]] levels entirely to this cluster, though [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] appear capable of amplifying this response. Regardless of the tissue of origin, our findings demonstrate that the circulating [[SASP]] milieu contributed to age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and the effects of the circulating [[SASP]] milieu were largely mediated by [[CXCL12]]. Fisetin-induced reductions in circulating [[CXCL12]] contribute to the beneficial effects of fisetin on [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] by mitigating mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], improving [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] production, and favorably modulating endothelial transdifferentiation ([[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]]). These results provide novel insights into the role of [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] and the [[SASP]] in vascular aging and the potential of [[Senolytic|senolytic therapy]] to mitigate age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]].

Vascular [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] is a key risk factor, indicator, and predictor for [[Cardiovascular Disease|CVD]]. [[Senescence]] has emerged as a potent underlying mechanism of age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and a likely contributor to [[Cardiovascular Disease|CVDs]], including [[Atherosclerosis]] (Honda et al. [^14]), [[Hypertension]] (Westhoff et al. [^41]), and occlusive stroke (Torres-Querol et al. [^38]). Previous studies have characterized age-related increases in [[Senescence]] burden in aortic samples from mice (Xie et al. [^43]), primates (Zhang et al. [^47]), and humans (Li et al. [^21]). Excess [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] accumulate in the vasculature among various mammals to promote chronic, low-grade [[Inflammation]] and excess [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] through the [[SASP]] (Hu et al. [^15]; Suda et al. [^37]). But despite this initial evidence of vascular health improvements with [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]], there is a lack of information on EC senescence in vivo and the effects of [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] on [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]]. In the present study, aortic senescent cell characterization identified [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] to develop a high [[Senescence]] burden with aging, which was reduced by [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]]. Previous studies have demonstrated that fisetin reduces whole-aorta cell senescence burden in vivo (Mahoney, Venkatasubramanian, et al. [^27]) as well as in cultured senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] in vitro (Mahoney, Venkatasubramanian, et al. [^27]; Zhu et al. [^48]). At a single-cell level, our study explored the effects of [[Senescence]] burden with aging and following in vivo fisetin supplementation across multiple cell types and identified that [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] undergo [[Senescence]] with aging and can be eliminated by in vivo [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with fisetin. Importantly, the senescent EC population that we identified was involved in pathways related to CV pathologies and revealed a high expression of [[SASP]]-related transcripts. As such, our findings align with previous studies reporting the efficacy of [[Senolytic|senolytic agents]] in reducing senescent cell burden in aging.

The circulating [[SASP]] milieu is composed of a heterogeneous collection of pro-oxidant and pro-inflammatory factors that vary depending on cell origin and [[Senescence]] induction. As such, the circulating [[SASP]] milieu is considered a major mediator of the adverse effects of [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]], but direct evidence for the impact of the [[SASP]] on [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] remains minimal. Emerging evidence indicates the circulating [[SASP]] milieu as a driver of age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] (Mahoney et al. [^26]); however, the specific component(s) of the circulation responsible for transducing impairments in [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] remain to be elucidated. One of the key findings in the present study is the elucidation of the role of the circulating [[SASP]] milieu as an underlying mechanism of age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]]. Our plasma exposure experiments in isolated arteries establish a causal role for the aged circulating [[SASP]] milieu in impairing [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]], with these age-related impairments being largely prevented in old animals that received [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]].

We next sought to determine candidate factors in the circulating [[SASP]] milieu that may be responsible for mediating [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] with aging, as identification of these molecules would provide insight into mechanisms by which [[Senescence]] drives [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and establish novel therapeutic targets. The [[Chemokines|chemokine]] encoded by *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* [[Messenger RNA|mRNA]] emerged as a factor most highly upregulated in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] with aging, with its expression reduced following fisetin [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] as determined by [[Single-cell RNA-seq|single-cell transcriptomics]]. Additionally, the concentration of [[CXCL12]] was elevated in the plasma of old animals and restored to young levels by fisetin supplementation. The addition and inhibition of [[CXCL12]] in our plasma exposure experiments support a causal role for [[CXCL12]] in age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]], as inhibition of [[CXCL12]] in old vehicle plasma improved endothelial responses and adding exogenous [[CXCL12]] to young plasma induced [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]]. Moreover, the addition of [[CXCL12]] to old fisetin plasma recapitulated age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]], indicating that [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]] mitigated circulating [[SASP]] milieu-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]], in part, by targeting [[CXCL12]]. Collectively, these findings establish an altered circulating [[SASP]] milieu as an underlying mechanism by which [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]] improves [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] with aging.

The circulating [[SASP]] milieu stimulates [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] through underlying mechanisms that promote chronic [[Inflammation]] and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] (Bloom et al. [^2]; Mahoney et al. [^24]). Notably, the pro-inflammatory circulating [[SASP]] milieu may promote [[Senescence]] systemically and in nearby cells, further promoting tissue-level dysfunction (Coppé et al. [^6]). Senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] also contain dysfunctional [[Mitochondria]] which are key regulators of the [[SASP]] (Wiley et al. [^42]). Dysfunctional [[Mitochondria]] in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] produce excess [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], which scavenges [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] to reduce its [[Bioavailability]] (Hayashi et al. [^13]). Here, we showed that the exposure of young, isolated mouse arteries and cultured arterial [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] to the aged circulating [[SASP]] milieu induced humoral [[Senescence]], reduced [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] production, and stimulated mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]]. Importantly, these age-related manifestations were prevented in arteries and [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] exposed to plasma from old animals supplemented with [[Fisetin]]. Importantly, this approach allowed us to model the integration of both systemic and locally secreted factors and suggests that fisetin favorably modulates the circulating [[SASP]] milieu to prevent vascular aging mechanisms that drive [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]].

Given that [[CXCL12]] has been implicated in inflammatory (Döring et al. [^7]; Lu et al. [^23]) and [[Senescence]]-related pathways (Saul et al. [^36]; Guduric-Fuchs et al. [^11]), its effects as a circulating [[SASP]] factor provide mechanistic insight into how [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] develops with aging. [[CXCL12]] regulates the transduction of downstream signaling pathways involved in the [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]], a process whereby [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] lose their inherent endothelial phenotypes and gain enhanced contractile properties (Ara et al. [^1]; Döring et al. [^7]; Lu et al. [^23]). The circulating [[SASP]] milieu-mediated [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] was largely stimulated by independent effects of [[CXCL12]] via humoral [[Senescence]] induction, reduced [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] production, and stimulated mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]]. While our data demonstrate that [[CXCL12]] is a potent driver of [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]], the exact directionality—whether primarily driven by systemic levels or local autocrine/paracrine signaling within the vessel wall—remains to be fully elucidated. Previous studies demonstrate that EC-derived [[CXCL12]] promotes [[Atherosclerosis]] progression (Döring et al. [^7]) and our findings support that [[CXCL12]] induces [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] likely through the [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]], a manifestation that is prevented by [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]]. As such, it is likely that the observed dysfunction results from an integrated signaling environment where [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] function as both a source and a target of [[CXCL12]]. The ability of fisetin to ameliorate [[CXCL12]]-driven [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] further underscores its therapeutic potential in mitigating vascular aging.

## 5. Limitations

One limitation of the current study is that while we demonstrated that [[Fisetin]] supplementation reduces circulating [[CXCL12]], the systemic tissue distribution of fisetin and [[CXCL12]] must be considered. Fisetin is known to distribute widely into tissues including the liver, kidneys, and brain, after oral administration (Yousefzadeh et al. [^46]). As such, it is likely that fisetin works pleiotropically across multiple tissues to reduce the systemic production of [[CXCL12]]. Furthermore, while we identified *[[CXCL12|Cxcl12]]* as a key [[SASP]] factor within a specific EC cluster, we cannot definitively conclude that senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] are the sole source of systemic [[CXCL12]]. Moreover, the current study design does not distinguish between the effects of circulating [[CXCL12]] and potential autocrine/paracrine signaling within the endothelium. While senescent endothelial cluster 10 represents a transcriptomic source, the relative contribution of local versus systemic [[CXCL12]] to the observed phenotype requires further investigation using cell-specific knockout models. Finally, while the [[CXCL12]]/[[CXCR4]]/[[ACKR3]] axis is a well-known regulator of vascular homeostasis (Wiley et al. [^42]), our study did not employ receptor inhibition to confirm if the observed level of [[Senescence]] is a direct result of [[CXCL12]] signaling or a secondary effect of the broader circulating [[SASP]] milieu. Future studies utilizing [[CXCL12]] administration and receptor inhibition are necessary to determine whether CXCL12-induced senescence is a direct receptor-mediated event or a consequence of the broader systemic environment.

## 6. Conclusions

In conclusion, this study identifies that senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] accumulate with advancing age and promote the secretion of [[SASP]] factors into circulation. [[Senolytic|Senolytic intervention]] with [[Fisetin]] effectively eliminated senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and highlighted [[CXCL12]] as a critical [[SASP]] factor that is increased with advanced age and reduced by [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]]. Mechanistically, we uncovered that the circulating [[SASP]] milieu and [[CXCL12]] induce [[Senescence]], reduce [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] production, promote mitochondrial [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], and stimulate [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]] to confer [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]]. By demonstrating that [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]] effectively reduces [[CXCL12]] levels and improves [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]], our findings provide mechanistic insight into the therapeutic potential of [[Senolytic|senolytic compounds]] in vascular aging. Importantly, these findings may have important implications for ongoing clinical trials investigating the efficacy of fisetin supplementation for improving [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]] in healthy mid-life and older adults ([NCT06133634](https://clinicaltrials.gov/ct2/show/NCT06133634)). These results provide physiological and mechanistic insight that supports the development, establishment, and translation of [[Senolytic|senolytic therapies]] to improve cardiovascular health with aging.
