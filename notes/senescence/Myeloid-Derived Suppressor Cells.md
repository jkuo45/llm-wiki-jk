# Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells

Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) are a heterogeneous population of immature myeloid cells with potent immunosuppressive activity. They arise from pathological, emergency myelopoiesis and fail to fully differentiate into mature granulocytes, monocytes, or dendritic cells. Two major subsets are recognized: **polymorphonuclear (PMN-MDSC)** and **monocytic (M-MDSC)**. MDSCs are a key link between the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]], chronic inflammation, and immune evasion in cancer and ageing.

## Origin & Differentiation

- MDSCs represent a state of **arrested myeloid differentiation** driven by sustained inflammatory and growth-factor signalling.
- SASP factors such as [[GM-CSF]] stimulate myeloid cell production while blocking terminal differentiation, expanding the immature suppressive pool — an example of how the SASP reprograms the differentiation status of neighbouring cells (see [[Paracrine Reprogramming]]).

## Recruitment & Expansion by the SASP

- **[[GM-CSF]]**: stimulates myeloid expansion and biases differentiation toward immunosuppressive MDSCs.
- **[[CCL2]]** (and other chemokines): recruit CCR2+ myeloid precursors into senescent/tumour tissue.
- **[[IL-6]]** and other pro-inflammatory SASP cytokines: promote MDSC accumulation and activation via [[JAK]]/[[STAT3]] signalling.

## Mechanisms of Immunosuppression

- Depletion of L-arginine via arginase-1 (ARG1) and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), starving T cells.
- Production of [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] and peroxynitrite that impair T-cell receptor signalling.
- Induction of [[Regulatory T cells|regulatory T cells (Tregs)]] and suppression of natural killer and effector T-cell function.

## Role in Senescence & Cancer

- In the [[Tumor Microenvironment]], SASP-recruited MDSCs suppress anti-tumour immunity, protect the cancer stem-cell niche, and promote angiogenesis and metastasis.
- MDSCs can impair immune-mediated clearance of senescent cells, allowing senescent-cell accumulation and sustained SASP output.
- By dampening [[Senescence Surveillance|immune surveillance]], MDSCs shift the balance of the SASP from beneficial (tumour-suppressive) to detrimental (tumour-promoting, pro-ageing).

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- Elevated MDSC frequencies correlate with tumour burden, metastasis, and poor prognosis across many cancers, and rise with age as part of [[Immunosenescence]] and [[Inflammaging]].
- Therapeutic strategies target MDSC recruitment (CCR2/CXCR2 antagonists), expansion, and suppressive function to restore anti-tumour immunity and improve immunotherapy responses.
