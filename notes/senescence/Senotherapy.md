# Senotherapy

Senotherapy is the class of interventions that aim to mitigate the deleterious effects of [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]] on aging and age-related disease. It is generally divided into two complementary approaches: [[Senolytic|senolytics]], which selectively eliminate senescent cells, and [[Senomorphic|senomorphics]], which suppress the pro-inflammatory [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] without killing the cell.

## Overview

The accumulation of [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] with age contributes to [[Age-Related Diseases]] and [[Inflammaging]]. Senotherapy seeks to interrupt this by either removing the source (senolytics) or damping its inflammatory output (senomorphics). The 2024 npj Aging review on the epigenetic landscape of senescence notes that epigenetic modulators — inhibitors of [[KDM4]], [[DOT1L]], and [[BRD4]], as well as nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors targeting retroelement inflammation — could be repurposed as senomorphics (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Senolytics vs Senomorphics

- **Senolytics** clear senescent cells via apoptosis, often through inhibition of [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways]] (e.g., BCL-2 family, PI3K/AKT). They are typically dosed intermittently.
- **Senomorphics** blunt SASP expression (e.g., via [[NFKB]], [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], or histone-modifying enzymes) while preserving the growth-arrest and potentially beneficial acute functions of senescence. They generally require continuous administration.

## Therapeutic Trade-offs

Because the SASP is context-dependent — beneficial in acute senescence (wound healing, tumor suppression via immune clearance) but detrimental in chronic senescence — broad SASP inhibition carries risks, including impaired immune surveillance of pre-neoplastic cells. Identifying safe, model-independent epigenetic senomorphics remains an active challenge.
