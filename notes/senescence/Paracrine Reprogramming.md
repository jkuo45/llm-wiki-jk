# Paracrine Reprogramming

**Paracrine reprogramming** is the process by which factors of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] secreted by [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] alter the **differentiation status** of neighbouring cells. Beyond inducing growth arrest ([[Paracrine Senescence]]), SASP factors act as potent paracrine **[[morphogens]]** that can push recipient cells toward stemness, dedifferentiation, transdifferentiation, or altered lineage commitment. The direction of the effect is largely **dose- and time-dependent**: transient/acute SASP exposure is pro-regenerative and plasticity-promoting, whereas chronic SASP is pro-fibrotic, pro-tumorigenic, and disruptive to normal differentiation.

> [!important] Core principle — biphasic effect
> A short pulse of SASP promotes plasticity, stemness and reprogramming that supports regeneration; sustained SASP exposure drives paracrine arrest, [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|EMT]], fibrosis and cancer stemness. Duration and intensity determine whether the outcome is beneficial or pathological.

## Mechanisms

### Induction of Stemness and Cellular Plasticity

- Short-term SASP exposure induces **stemness and plasticity in keratinocytes**, upregulating stem-cell markers and enhancing regenerative capacity (Ritschka et al., 2017, *Genes Dev*; PMID 28143833).
- **[[IL-6]]** is a central driver: senescence and its SASP promote **in vivo reprogramming toward pluripotency** (OSKM/Yamanaka-factor–mediated), with IL-6 enhancing tissue plasticity (Mosteiro et al., 2016, *Science*; PMID 27884981). Senescence thereby acts as a tissue-context signal that facilitates reprogramming of neighbours.
- Chronic exposure reverses this benefit, engaging a paracrine senescence/arrest program instead — illustrating the biphasic nature.

### TGFβ-Driven Fate Switching and EMT

- **[[TGFβ]]** is an early-phase SASP factor (Notch-dependent switch) and a master regulator of differentiation. In neighbours it drives:
  - **[[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|EMT]]** — epithelial cel### TGFβ-Driven Fate Switching and EMTls lose polarity/identity and acquire migratory, mesenchymal, stem-like traits (relevant to fibrosis and cancer invasion).
  - **Myofibroblast differentiation** — together with **[[PDGFAA]]**, TGFβ converts fibroblasts/progenitors into contractile myofibroblasts during wound healing; chronic signalling drives pathological fibrosis.

### Growth-Factor–Directed Lineage Shifts

- **[[VEGF]]** promotes endothelial differentiation and angiogenesis in the microenvironment.
- **[[HGF]]**, **[[Amphiregulin]]** ([[EGFR]] ligand), and other SASP mitogens can shift epithelial/progenitor cells toward proliferative, less-differentiated states.
- In bone, SASP (**[[TGFβ]]**, **[[GDF15]]**) **reduces mesenchymal progenitor pools** and skews their differentiation balance (osteogenic vs. adipogenic), impairing fracture healing.

### Immune-Cell Fate Skewing

- **[[IL-6]]** + **[[IL-1β]]** + **[[TGFβ]]** together drive naive T-cell differentiation toward the **[[TH17]]** lineage.
- **[[GM-CSF]]** and chemokines (**[[CCL2]]**) skew myeloid differentiation toward immunosuppressive myeloid-derived suppressor cells and specific [[Macrophages|macrophage]] phenotypes, reshaping the tissue immune landscape.

### Cancer Stemness

> [!warning] Detrimental context
> Chronic SASP (**[[IL-6]]**, **[[IL-8]]** via [[JAK]]/[[STAT3]]) promotes cancer stem-cell (CSC) traits and stemness in neighbouring epithelial/tumour cells, enhancing self-renewal, [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|EMT]], and therapy resistance.

Cancer stem cells (CSCs) are a subpopulation of tumour cells with self-renewal capacity, multi-lineage differentiation potential, and disproportionate responsibility for tumour initiation, metastasis, and relapse. Paracrine SASP signalling from senescent stromal fibroblasts, senescent tumour cells, and therapy-induced senescent cells is a major non-cell-autonomous route by which non-stem tumour cells **acquire or reinforce a CSC state**.

#### Core Signalling Axes

- **[[IL-6]]–[[JAK]]–[[STAT3]]**: The dominant axis. IL-6 binds IL-6R/[[gp130]], activating STAT3, which transcriptionally sustains stemness factors (e.g., OCT4, NANOG, SOX2) and upregulates the CSC marker **CD44**. STAT3 also creates a feed-forward loop by inducing further IL-6 secretion, locking recipient cells into a self-renewing state.
- **[[IL-8]]–[[CXCR2]]**: IL-8 (CXCL8) signalling through CXCR1/[[CXCR2]] expands the CSC pool, promotes mammosphere/tumoursphere formation, and drives an invasive, EMT-associated phenotype in breast and colorectal models.
- **Developmental pathways**: SASP-driven reactivation of **[[Wnt signaling|Wnt/β-catenin]]** and **[[Notch]]** signalling in neighbouring cells reinforces stem-cell transcriptional programs. SASP-derived Wnt ligands and Notch ligand crosstalk help convert differentiated tumour cells back toward a progenitor-like state.

#### EMT–Stemness Coupling

[[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|EMT]] and stemness are mechanistically intertwined: SASP-driven **[[TGFβ]]**, IL-6, and IL-8 induce EMT transcription factors (SNAIL, SLUG, ZEB1, TWIST) that simultaneously confer mesenchymal, migratory traits **and** CSC properties. Cells passing through EMT acquire the CD44^high^/CD24^low^ signature characteristic of breast CSCs, linking paracrine reprogramming of differentiation status directly to metastatic seeding.

#### Therapy Resistance and Therapy-Induced Senescence (TIS)

- **[[Therapy-Induced Senescence|TIS]] cells** are a clinically important SASP source: chemotherapy/radiotherapy spares a fraction of tumour cells that enter senescence and secrete a pro-stemness SASP, paradoxically enriching CSCs in surrounding non-senescent cells and fuelling relapse.
- Some senescent tumour cells can themselves **escape senescence** and re-enter the cycle with amplified stem-cell traits ("senescence-associated stemness"), a state associated with elevated Wnt signalling and CDK1 activity (Milanovic et al., 2018, *Nature*; PMID 29258295).
- SASP-induced CSCs upregulate drug-efflux transporters (ABC family) and anti-apoptotic programs, contributing to chemoresistance.

#### Microenvironmental Reinforcement

Within the [[Tumor Microenvironment]], SASP simultaneously (1) reprograms tumour cells toward stemness, (2) recruits immunosuppressive myeloid cells ([[GM-CSF]], [[CCL2]]) that protect the CSC niche, and (3) remodels the ECM (MMPs) to create a permissive perivascular/hypoxic niche that maintains CSC quiescence and stemness.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - The Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype The Dark Side of Tumor Suppression|SASP: The Dark Side of Tumor Suppression]]
> Senescent fibroblast SASP (IL-6, IL-8) stimulates proliferation, EMT, and stem-like traits in neighbouring premalignant and malignant epithelial cells; p53 loss in senescent cells amplifies these pro-tumorigenic paracrine effects.

#### Therapeutic Implication

Because TIS-derived SASP can enrich CSCs, a **"one-two punch"** strategy is proposed: induce senescence with chemotherapy, then clear the senescent cells with [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytics]] before their SASP can reprogram neighbours toward stemness. [[Senomorphic Therapy|Senomorphics]] targeting IL-6/JAK/STAT3 ([[Ruxolitinib]]) similarly aim to interrupt paracrine CSC induction.

## Routes of Delivery

- **Soluble factors** — cytokines and growth factors signalling through recipient-cell receptors.
- **Extracellular vesicles / exosomes** — transfer of proteins ([[IFITM3]]), miRNAs, and mitochondrial cargo that reprogram recipient transcriptional and differentiation programs.
- **miRNAs** — mediate fibroblast→keratinocyte communication in skin, tuning differentiation programs.

## Acute vs. Chronic SASP

| Feature | Acute / transient SASP | Chronic SASP |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant factors | Early TGFβ, PDGF, pulsed IL-6 | Sustained IL-6/IL-8, TGFβ, MMPs |
| Effect on neighbours | Plasticity, stemness, reprogramming, regeneration | Paracrine arrest, EMT, fibrosis, cancer stemness |
| Physiological role | Wound healing, embryonic patterning, organ/limb regeneration | Aging, [[Inflammaging]], fibrosis, tumour promotion |

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

- **Regeneration**: Transient senescence and its SASP support wound healing and organ regeneration (kidney, heart, liver, salamander limb) by promoting recipient-cell plasticity.
- **Development**: Programmed senescence contributes to embryonic patterning, with [[TGFβ]] as a key mediator (see [[Paracrine Senescence]]).
- **Fibrosis**: Chronic TGFβ/PDGF-driven myofibroblast conversion underlies pulmonary, hepatic, and renal fibrosis.
- **Cancer**: Sustained SASP promotes stemness, EMT, and therapy resistance in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]].

## Therapeutic Implications

The plasticity-altering effects of chronic SASP provide additional rationale for [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytics]] (removing the source cells) and [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphics]] (suppressing SASP production). Harnessing the acute, pro-regenerative window is an emerging strategy in regenerative medicine.
