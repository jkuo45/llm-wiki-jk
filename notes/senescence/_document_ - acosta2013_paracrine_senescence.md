## A complex secretory program orchestrated by the inflammasome controls paracrine senescence

**Juan Carlos Acosta, Ana Banito, Torsten Wuestefeld, Athena Georgilis, Peggy Janich, Jennifer P. Morton, Dimitris Athineos, Tae-Won Kang, Felix Lasitschka, Mindaugas Andrulis, Gloria Pascual, Kelly J. Morris, Sadaf Khan, Hong Jin, Gopuraja Dharmalingam, Ambrosius P. Snijders, Thomas Carroll, David Capper, Catrin Pritchard, Gareth J. Inman, Thomas Longerich, Owen J. Sansom, Salvador Aznar Benitah, Lars Zender and Jesus Gil**

> [!important] Key Findings
> [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]] cells can transmit senescence to neighbouring normal cells via secreted SASP factors — a phenomenon termed **paracrine senescence**. This occurs both in culture and in human and mouse models of OIS *in vivo*. The [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] component [[TGFβ]] family ligands play a major role by regulating [[CDKN2B|p15^INK4b]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]] expression. The entire SASP is controlled by inflammasome-mediated [[IL-1α]] signalling.

**Oncogene-induced senescence (OIS)** is crucial for tumour suppression. Senescent cells implement a complex pro-inflammatory response termed the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype (SASP)]]. The SASP reinforces senescence, activates immune surveillance and paradoxically also has pro-tumorigenic properties. Here, we present evidence that the SASP can also induce [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]] in normal cells both in culture and in human and mouse models of OIS *in vivo*. Coupling quantitative proteomics with small-molecule screens, we identified multiple SASP components mediating paracrine senescence, including [[TGFβ]] family ligands, [[VEGF]], [[CCL2]] and [[CCL20]]. Amongst them, TGF-β ligands play a major role by regulating [[CDKN2B|p15^INK4b]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]]. Expression of the SASP is controlled by [[Inflammasome|inflammasome]]-mediated [[IL-1α]] signalling. The inflammasome and IL-1 signalling are activated in senescent cells and IL-1α expression can reproduce SASP activation, resulting in senescence. Our results demonstrate that the SASP can cause paracrine senescence and impact on tumour suppression and senescence *in vivo*.

Senescence protects damaged cells from neoplastic transformation by inducing a stable growth arrest. Concomitant with this arrest, cells secrete a complex mixture of factors referred to as the SASP or senescence-messaging secretome. The SASP can exert opposing and contradictory effects. Initial studies focused on the pro-tumorigenic properties of the SASP but the SASP also mediates important tumour suppressive effects. Specific components of the SASP such as IGFBP-7, PAI-1, [[IL-6]] and [[CXCR2]]-binding chemokines (such as IL-8 or GROα) can reinforce senescence. The SASP also contributes to the surveillance and elimination of senescent cells by the immune system — a process known as [[Senescence Surveillance|senescence surveillance]].

It is unclear whether pro-senescence effects can be exerted in a non-cell-autonomous fashion (paracrine) in addition to a cell-autonomous fashion (autocrine) and whether senescence can be transmitted to normal cells. Early experiments where young and old fibroblasts were mixed suggested that senescence was exclusively cell intrinsic, although more recently a bystander senescence response has been suggested. However, although some factors secreted by senescent cells, such as IL-6, reinforce senescence in an intracrine fashion, others such as IGFBP-7 can exhibit paracrine effects. In this investigation, we present unequivocal evidence supporting that senescence can be transmitted in a paracrine fashion, and provide insights into the pathways regulating and mediating paracrine senescence.

## RESULTS

### Paracrine transmission of senescence by cells undergoing OIS

To understand whether cells undergoing [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]] can transmit senescence in a non-cell-autonomous manner, we established co-cultures of senescent and normal human IMR90 fibroblasts. Cells were distinguished by expressing an mCherry fluorescent marker (Cherry) in the normal IMR90 cells. The Cherry-positive and -negative populations were monitored using high content analysis microscopy.

> [!info] Experimental Design
> IMR90 ER:RAS cells expressing a chimaeric fusion protein that activates on 4-hydroxytamoxifen (4OHT) treatment were co-cultured with normal IMR90 Cherry cells. RAS activation triggers growth arrest, induces senescence effectors and the SASP. When co-cultured, normal IMR90 Cherry cells also stopped proliferating — demonstrating non-cell-autonomous (paracrine) transmission of senescence.

Normal IMR90 Cherry cells also underwent arrest when co-cultured with IMR90 MEK:ER cells, an alternative model of OIS. Expression of the ER-binding domain alone, or treatment with 4OHT, did not affect the growth of control cells. These experiments were performed using early passage IMR90 cells to exclude confounding effects of [[Replicative Senescence]]. Normal human mammary epithelial cells (HMECs) also underwent arrest on co-culture with HMECs undergoing OIS (HMEC ER:RAS). Moreover, HMEC ER:RAS cells induced the arrest of normal IMR90 fibroblasts, suggesting that **paracrine senescence can be transmitted between different cell types**.

### Paracrine senescence is a stable arrest mediated by secreted factors

> [!info] Stable Arrest
> Normal cells arrested with a slight delay compared with cells undergoing OIS in co-cultures, consistent with a paracrine response. SASP components ([[CXCL1]], [[IL-8]], [[CCL20]], [[activin A]] or [[VEGFc]]) were induced as early as 2–3 days after [[RAS]] activation.

Using Transwell inserts that ensure physical separation of the cells, IMR90 cells at the bottom exhibited a senescent morphology and became arrested when co-cultured in the presence of senescent cells in the top chamber. After 7 days of Transwell co-culture, normal IMR90 cells were split and cultured alone for 14 additional days — cells that had undergone paracrine senescence continued exhibiting features of senescence, demonstrating that the **transmitted phenotype is stable**.

> [!tip] Conditioned Medium Is Sufficient
> Conditioned medium (CM) from IMR90 cells expressing active forms of RAS, RAF or MEK was sufficient to induce paracrine senescence. Cells exposed to CM showed reduced BrdU incorporation, higher SA-β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal) positivity, and the arrest persisted after CM withdrawal.

> [!warning] Spatial Restriction
> Paracrine senescence is spatially restricted: normal fibroblasts in close proximity (within ~1 mm) to OIS cell clusters showed reduced [[BrdU]] incorporation, while those located further away (>1 mm) were unaffected. Serial CM transfer showed limited transmissibility — primary CM induced secondary senescence, but tertiary cells only slowed proliferation without SA-β-Gal positivity.

### Paracrine senescence resembles a full senescence response

Co-cultured IMR90 Cherry cells showed increased levels of oxidative and DNA damage and activation of [[p16 INK4a|p16^INK4a]], [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]] and [[IL-8]] expression — suggesting a full transmission of senescence.

> [!info] Transcriptomic Equivalence
> Global gene expression profiling revealed a high correlation (Pearson r = 0.67) between OIS and paracrine senescence. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering grouped them together, and a senescence-associated transcriptional signature was significantly upregulated during paracrine senescence. A paracrine senescence signature was enriched across multiple senescence models: replicative senescence, oncogene-induced senescence, stress-induced senescence, mouse PanIN lesions, and human sessile serrated adenomas (SSAs).

> [!important] Core Arrest Machinery
> Paracrine senescence depends on the same tumour suppressor networks as OIS: knockdown of [[p16 INK4a|p16^INK4a]], [[p53]], or [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]] partially rescued paracrine senescence, confirming that the [[DNA Damage Response|DDR]]-independent arrest converges on the canonical p16/Rb and p53/p21 pathways.

### Multiple components of the SASP mediate paracrine senescence

The secretome of cells undergoing OIS was catalogued using stable isotope labelling of amino acids in culture (SILAC) quantitative proteomics. Amongst the top hits were chemokines, [[TGFβ]] family ligands or [[VEGF]].

> [!info] Drug Screen Identification
> A library of 78 chemical compounds targeting SASP receptor pathways was screened for ability to impair paracrine senescence. Key hits targeted:
> - **[[VEGFR2]]/[[FLT3]]** receptors
> - **[[TGFBR1]]** ([[ALK5]]/[[TGF-β receptor type I]])
> - **[[CCR2]]** ([[CCL2 receptor]])
>
> These compounds inhibited paracrine senescence in a dose-dependent manner. RNAi knockdown of CCR2 or TGF-β receptors ALK4, ALK5 and ALK7 confirmed their role.

### The TGF-β pathway mediates paracrine senescence

> [!info] TGF-β Is the Major Paracrine Senescence Mediator
> Although TGF-β1 was induced during OIS, other TGF-β family ligands — including BMP6, BMP2, inhibin A and GDF15 — were more acutely upregulated. Both SMAD2/3 and SMAD1/5 phosphorylation were upregulated in cells undergoing paracrine senescence, corroborating involvement of both TGF-β and BMP branches. Combination of blocking antibodies targeting TGF-β1, activin A or BMP2 partially rescued paracrine senescence. TGFBR1 inhibitors prevented SMAD2/3 phosphorylation and blunted paracrine senescence arrest, with effects correlating with impaired [[CDKN2B|p15^INK4b]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]] induction.

> [!important] In Vivo Validation — TGFBR1 Knockout
> In a mouse model of pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) driven by [[KRas]] G12D, conditional deletion of TGF-β receptor type I (TGF-βR1) in the pancreas attenuated OIS: lesions showed decreased SA-β-Gal positivity and increased Ki67+ proliferation. *Pdx1–cre Kras[G12D/+]* mice with TGF-βR1 deletion succumbed to pancreatic and skin cancer within 3 months, whereas wild-type Kras[G12D] animals had a latency of over a year — demonstrating that TGF-β signalling is essential for OIS-mediated tumour suppression *in vivo*.

### Activation of the inflammasome controls SASP production

> [!info] IL-1α Is the Master SASP Regulator
> IL-1α was identified as one of the most robust inducers of multiple SASP components. Ectopic IL-1α expression phenocopied the SASP of OIS cells. Inhibiting TGFBR1 did not affect the secretome induced by IL-1α, and whereas IL-1α inhibition partially prevented SASP induction by TGF-β, the converse was not true — suggesting IL-1 has a more prominent role than TGF-β in controlling the SASP.

> [!important] Inflammasome Activation During OIS
> Cells undergoing OIS secreted the processed, mature forms of both IL-1α and IL-1β, suggesting inflammasome activation. Indeed, IMR90 cells undergoing OIS exhibited [[Caspase-1]] activity. The inflammasome was also activated *in vivo*: in Braf[V600E]-driven murine SSAs and in Kras[G12D]-driven PanIN lesions. Protein levels of inflammasome components (caspase-1, ASC/PYCARD, NLRP3) were upregulated during OIS without changes to mRNA levels — suggesting protein stabilization. Treatment with caspase-1 or [[IL-1R]] inhibitors, but not TGFBR1 inhibitors, blunted SASP component expression during OIS — demonstrating that inflammasome activation is a cause rather than an effect of the SASP.

### The inflammasome and IL-1 signalling reinforce senescence

> [!info] IL-1α Induces Senescence
> Ectopic IL-1α expression arrested IMR90 cells, causing senescence accompanied by increased oxidative and DNA damage, and induction of [[p53]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]]. Conversely, knockdown of the [[IL-1R]] or inflammasome components partially prevented OIS. Treatment with caspase-1 inhibitors partially prevented p21^CIP1 induction and cell cycle arrest during OIS.

> [!important] In Vivo IL-1 Blockade Reduces Senescence
> In a mouse model where OIS is induced in hepatocytes through Nras[G12V] transposon delivery, treatment with an IL-1R inhibitor or a combination of drugs (targeting IL-1R, VEGFR2, CCR2 and TGFBR1) for 12 days reduced the percentage of senescent hepatocytes and increased the percentage of Nras+ cells remaining — reflecting reduced immune clearance of senescent cells and/or senescence inhibition. IL-1R inhibitor also allowed NRas+ cells to proliferate, confirming that IL-1 signalling is required for senescence maintenance *in vivo*.

### Paracrine senescence is observed in mouse and human models of OIS in vivo

> [!info] In Vivo Evidence Across Models
> Paracrine senescence was demonstrated in three *in vivo* models:
> 1. **Mouse liver (Nras[G12V])**: Senescent hepatocytes were surrounded by clusters of immune cells positive for p21^Cip1 and p16^Ink4a.
> 2. **Mouse skin (K5-Sos Egfr[wa2/+])**: Stromal cells (K5−) in the vicinity of senescent papillomas (K5+) showed elevated p16^Ink4a and p21^Cip1, while normal skin showed no senescent stromal cells.
> 3. **Human sessile serrated adenomas (SSAs)**: Epithelial cells in SSAs were positive for activated BRAF and p21^CIP1 but negative for Ki67. Stromal p21^CIP1+ cells were significantly increased near SSAs compared with normal colonic crypts (P = 0.03). These cells had immune or fibroblast morphology.

## DISCUSSION

> [!info] Paracrine Senescence Expands the SASP Repertoire
> The SASP can potentiate tumorigenic properties, recruit the immune system, or reinforce senescence. Paracrine senescence adds another function: cells undergoing OIS can propagate senescence to surrounding tissue. This could expand the senescent footprint of preneoplastic lesions and promote immune clearance, or alternatively propagate senescence to surrounding normal tissue.

> [!tip] Open Questions
> Not all cells surrounding preneoplastic lesions undergo paracrine senescence. Levels of soluble factors, concentration gradients, cell-type susceptibility, and whether cells are dividing or arrested all influence which cells undergo paracrine senescence *in vivo*. Recent work suggests tumours can induce p16^INK4a expression in surrounding stromal and infiltrated immune cells — similar to what was observed here.

The authors identified multiple SASP factors with previously unrecognized roles in controlling senescence, including TGF-β family members (activin A and GDF15), VEGF, and the chemokines CCL2 and CCL20. Although multiple SASP components amplify the response — at least in part by activating [[NFKB|NF-κB]] — **IL-1α is a key regulator not linked previously with senescence induction**. The ability of the inflammasome to regulate senescence adds to the pro- and anti-tumour effects of the inflammasome.

In conclusion, cells undergoing OIS can transmit paracrine senescence to their neighbours. Paracrine senescence is a complex response regulated by the inflammasome and IL-1 signalling. Understanding the significance and regulation of paracrine senescence may be the first step towards manipulating it for therapeutic benefit.
