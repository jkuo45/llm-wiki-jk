# Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction

**Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction (SAMD)** refers to the impairment of mitochondrial structure and function that is a hallmark of [[Senescent Cells]]. It is both a downstream consequence of arrest and an active *driver* of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]], notably in mitochondrial dysfunction-associated senescence (MiDAS; Wiley et al., 2016, PMID 27716483).

## Key Features

- **Reduced Oxidative Phosphorylation (OXPHOS)**: Decreased electron-transport-chain efficiency and lowered ATP generation, particularly complexes I and IV.
- **Glycolytic shift**: Senescent cells rely more on aerobic glycolysis to meet biosynthetic and secretory demands of the SASP ("Warburg-like" phenotype).
- **Increased Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) Production**: Dysfunctional complexes (especially complex I/III) elevate [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], causing further [[DNA Damage]] and amplifying DDR-independent arrest.
- **Impaired Mitophagy**: Failure to clear damaged mitochondria (defective [[Mitophagy|mitophagic]] flux) permits accumulation of ROS-emitting organelles.
- **[[Minority MOMP]] (miMOMP)**: Sublethal mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization by [[BAX]]/[[BAK]] that releases [[mtDNA]] into the cytosol without triggering [[Apoptosis]].

## Metabolic Adaptation (SCAPs)

Senescent cells deploy senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs) that include mitochondrial adaptations — e.g., upregulation of anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2]] family proteins and rewired fuel use — that keep the cell alive despite mitochondrial stress while sustaining high SASP output.

## Role in Senescence

SAMD is not merely a by-product but a propagator of senescence:
- Released [[mtDNA]] activates the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] innate-immune sensor, driving type I interferon and NF-κB–dependent SASP.
- Mitochondrial ROS activate [[NF-κB]] and stress kinases ([[p38 MAPK]]).
- The glycolytic/secretory metabolic state supports the energetic cost of SASP export.

## Cross-talk with Other Hallmarks

SAMD links mitochondrial stress to [[Inflammaging]] (via cGAS-STING), to [[Lamin B1]] loss (nuclear envelope defects secondary to mitochondrial stress), and to [[Paracrine Senescence]] (SASP-mediated spread). It is a central node in the consensus Hallmarks of Senescence (Gorgoulis et al., 2019).

## Therapeutic Targeting

Strategies under study include:
- **Mitophagy enhancers** (e.g., urolithin A) to clear damaged mitochondria.
- **Antioxidants / [[Resveratrol]]** to quench ROS.
- **miMOMP inhibitors** (BCL-2/xL antagonists in the opposite sense, or [[BAX]]/[[BAK]] modulation) to block mtDNA release.
- **[[Metformin]]**, which improves mitochondrial function and has senomorphic effects.
Targeting SAMD aims to suppress SASP and improve healthspan without killing the senescent cell.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

SAMD underlies the metabolic dysfunction of aged tissues and contributes to SASP-driven pathologies such as [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]], atherosclerosis, and [[Osteoarthritis]]. Because mtDNA release is upstream of cGAS-STING, SAMD is a tractable upstream target for [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphics]].
