# Senescence Surveillance

**Senescence surveillance** is the process by which the [[Immune System|immune system]] recognizes and eliminates [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]]. It serves as a critical fail-safe mechanism that reinforces the tumor-suppressive function of [[Senescence|cellular senescence]] by clearing pre-malignant and damaged cells before they can undergo transformation. The concept was first experimentally demonstrated by Kang et al. (2011, *Nature* 479:547-551; PMID 22080947), who showed that immune-mediated clearance of senescent hepatocytes prevents liver cancer development.

## Molecular Mechanism

### SASP-Mediated Immune Recruitment
Senescent cells secrete [[SASP|SASP factors]] that act as "find-me" and "activate-me" signals for immune cells:
- **Chemokines**: [[CCL2]] (MCP-1), [[CCL5]], [[CXCL1]], [[CXCL10]] — recruit monocytes, macrophages, and [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]]
- **Cytokines**: [[IL-6]], [[IL-1β]], [[TNFα]] — activate immune effector functions
- **Adhesion molecules**: ICAM-1 — facilitates immune cell adhesion to senescent cells
- **Type I interferons**: [[IFN-β]] — activates NK cell and CD8+ T cell cytotoxicity

### Immune Effector Cells
Multiple immune cell types participate in senescence surveillance:
- **[[Natural Killer Cells]]**: Directly kill senescent cells through NKG2D receptor recognition of stress ligands (MICA, MICB, ULBP2) upregulated on senescent cells
- **[[Macrophages]]**: Phagocytose senescent cells; SASP factors polarize them toward a pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype
- **[[T Lymphocyte|CD4+ and CD8+ T cells]]**: Recognize senescent cell antigens; CD4+ T cells orchestrate the immune response
- **[[Neutrophils]]**: Recruited by SASP chemokines to the sites of senescent cell accumulation

### cGAS-STING Contribution
The [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING pathway]] in senescent cells promotes type-I interferon production, which enhances NK cell and T cell-mediated killing. In the liver, inhibiting cGAS-STING signaling impairs senescence surveillance of pre-malignant hepatocytes, permitting tumor development (Dou et al., 2017, *Nature* 550:402-406; PMID 28976970).

## Role in Cancer

### Tumor Suppression
- Clearance of [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|oncogene-induced senescent cells]] in benign lesions prevents progression to malignancy
- Senescence surveillance maintains the pre-malignant state: impairing surveillance (e.g., by immunosuppression or aging) accelerates tumorigenesis
- SASP-driven immune recruitment is essential for the full tumor-suppressive effect of senescence

### Immune Evasion by Senescent Cells
Aging and chronic inflammation impair immune function, leading to senescence surveillance failure:
- Declining NK cell and T cell function with age permits senescent cell accumulation
- Senescent cells can upregulate immune checkpoint molecules ([[PD-L1]]) to evade killing
- [[Prostaglandin E2|PGE2]] secreted by senescent [[Hepatic Stellate Cells|HSC]] suppresses anti-tumor immunity in obesity-associated [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma|HCC]] (Loo et al., 2017, *Cancer Discov* 7:522-538; PMID 28202625)

## Therapeutic Implications

- **Enhancing surveillance**: STING agonists and immune checkpoint inhibitors may restore senescence surveillance in aging and cancer
- **Senolytic combination**: Clearing senescent cells with senolytics may help when endogenous surveillance fails
- **Vaccination**: Senescent cell vaccines are being explored to prime the immune system for senescent cell clearance
