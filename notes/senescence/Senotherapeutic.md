# Senotherapeutic

A senotherapeutic is any intervention that selectively targets [[Senescence|senescent cells]] to mitigate aging and age-related disease. The term spans two complementary strategies: **senolytics**, which kill senescent cells by exploiting their [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways|anti-apoptotic dependencies]], and **senomorphics**, which suppress the deleterious [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] (senescence-associated secretory phenotype) without eliminating the cells.

## Mechanism of Action

Senolytics such as [[Fisetin]], [[Quercetin]] (often combined with the kinase inhibitor [[Dasatinib]]), and navitoclax disrupt pro-survival networks, triggering apoptosis of damaged cells. Senomorphics instead dampen [[Inflammation]] and tissue remodeling driven by SASP factors. Both reduce the chronic inflammatory burden of [[Inflammaging]].

## Physiological & Longevity Effects

Clearing senescent cells in murine models improves [[Healthspan]], restores [[Tissue Plasminogen Activator|tissue function]], and alleviates multiple [[Age-Related Diseases]]. Because senescent cells accumulate with [[Aging]] and [[Telomere]] attrition, senotherapeutics address a recognized [[Hallmarks of Aging|hallmark of aging]].

## Therapeutic Landscape

Senolytics are in early human trials for frailty, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, and diabetic complications; combination and intermittent dosing aim to spare transient, beneficial senescence (e.g., wound healing).

> [!warning] Transient Senescence is Protective
> Acute senescence aids tissue repair and tumor suppression; senotherapeutics must avoid chronically disabling these physiological roles.
