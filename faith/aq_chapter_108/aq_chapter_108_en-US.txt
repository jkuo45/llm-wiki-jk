### Chapter 108: Jesus Rebukes Selfishness and Hypocrisy 🕊️

The multitudes were consumed by wild, selfish thoughts, ignoring the rights and needs of their neighbors. The strong trampled the weak in a frantic race to be the first to receive a blessing. Observing this, Jesus likened the crowd to a cage of untamed beasts and a den of stinging vipers, driven by fiendish greed for selfish gain. 🐍 (1-3)

Jesus warned that benefits gained by those who see only themselves are as fleeting as baubles in the morning light. ☀️ Such gains are unreal and pass away; though the selfish soul is fed today, the food does not assimilate, and the soul fails to grow, remaining in a constant state of hunger. 🏜️ (4-5)

He shared a parable of a selfish man obsessed by a spirit of the air. Though the spirit is cast out by the Omnific Word, it wanders through dry places seeking rest. Finding none, it returns to the man's "house," which has been left unlocked. 🏚️ Finding it swept and clean, the spirit returns with seven other spirits even more unclean, making the man's final state sevenfold more wretched than the first. 🌑 This, He explained, is the fate of those who snatch blessings belonging to others. (6-11)

When a woman in the crowd praised the mother of such a man of God, Jesus replied:
> "Yes, blest is she; but doubly blest are they who hear, receive and live the word of God." 📖 (12-13)

At a feast prepared by a wealthy Pharisee, Jesus and His disciples were guests. When Jesus did not perform the ritual washing required by Pharisaic rules, the host was marveled. Jesus addressed the hypocrisy, noting that while Pharisees cleanse the outside of the body, their hearts remain full of wickedness, extortion, and deceit. 🏺 He reminded them:
> "Did not the God who made the outside of the body make the inside, too?" 🤔 (14-19)

Jesus then pronounced woes upon the Pharisees for titheing herbs while neglecting judgment and the love of God, and for seeking high status in synagogues and marketplaces. 🏛️ He called them "tinselled gentry" whose actions did not reflect service to the Lord. 🎭 (20-22)

When a lawyer took offense, Jesus extended the rebuke to the masters of the law. He accused them of heaping heavy burdens on others without lifting a finger to help. ⛓️ He condemned them for building tombs for prophets their fathers killed, while they themselves continued to persecute the holy men, apostles, and seers sent by God. 🩸 (23-27)

He declared that the blood of all holy men, from Abel to Zacharias, stained the hands of that ungodly generation. ✋ He concluded by accusing the lawyers of snatching the keys of knowledge—neither entering themselves nor allowing others to pass through the doors. 🔑 (28-32)

These truths struck like a thunderbolt from heaven, provoking intense resentment from the Pharisees, lawyers, and scribes. ⚡ They responded with torrents of abuse and began to counsel how they might legally ensnare Him to shed His blood. ⚖️ (33-34)
