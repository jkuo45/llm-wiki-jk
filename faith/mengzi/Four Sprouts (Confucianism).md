# Four Sprouts (Confucianism)

The **Four Sprouts** (*duān*) are innate ethical dispositions in [[Mencius|Mencian]] philosophy that serve as the foundation for the four cardinal virtues.

## The Four Sprouts and Virtues
1. **The feeling of compassion**: The sprout of [[Benevolence (Ren)|Benevolence]] (*rén*).
2. **The feeling of disdain**: The sprout of [[Righteousness (Yi)|Righteousness]] (*yì*).
3. **The feeling of respect**: The sprout of [[Propriety (Li)|Propriety]] (*lĭ*).
4. **The feeling of approval and disapproval**: The sprout of [[Wisdom (Zhi)|Wisdom]] (*zhì*).

## Cultivation and Extension
[[Mencius]] argues that while these sprouts are innate, they are incipient and must be "extended" or "filled out" through reflection (*sī*). This cultivation requires a stable environment where basic physical needs are met, as well as ethical education.

## Connections
- **[[Human Nature (Confucianism)]]**: The sprouts are the primary evidence for Mencius's claim that human nature is good.
- **[[Xunzi]]**: Rejected the idea of innate sprouts of virtue, arguing that morality is the result of deliberate effort and [[Ritual (Li)|Ritual]].
