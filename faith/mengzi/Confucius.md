# Confucius

**Confucius** (551–479 BCE) was the founder of the [[Confucianism|Confucian]] tradition. He sought to revive the ideals of the ancient sage kings to address the chaos of the [[Zhou Dynasty]].

## Key Themes
- **[[Ritual (Li)]]**: The importance of formal ceremonies, etiquette, and social mores in shaping character.
- **[[Benevolence (Ren)]]**: The central virtue of humanity and Goodness.
- **[[Filial Piety]]**: Respect for parents and elders as the "root" of virtue.
- **Education**: A focus on both learning the classics and reflecting on their meaning.

## Relationship with Mencius
[[Mencius]] lived about a century after Confucius and saw himself as a defender and explicator of Confucius's Way. While Confucius focused on the practice of ritual and study, Mencius further developed the philosophical psychology of innate goodness.

## Connections
- **[[Mencius]]**: The "Second Sage" who systematized and defended Confucius's teachings.
- **[[Analects]]**: The primary collection of Confucius's sayings.
- **[[Zhou Dynasty]]**: The historical context of his life and teachings.
