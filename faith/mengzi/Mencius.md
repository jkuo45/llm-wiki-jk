# Mencius

Mencius (fourth century BCE), or [[Mengzi]], was a [[Confucianism|Confucian]] philosopher often called the "Second Sage." He is most famous for his doctrine that [[Human Nature (Confucianism)|human nature is good]].

## Life and Background
Mencius lived during the second half of the [[Zhou Dynasty]], a time of social and intellectual ferment. He self-identified as a follower of [[Confucius]] and sought to advise rulers to govern through ethical inspiration and [[Benevolence (Ren)]] rather than coercion. He believed that rulers must possess [[Virtue (De)]] to truly lead the people.

## Key Doctrines
- **[[Four Sprouts (Confucianism)|The Four Sprouts]]**: Innate tendencies toward virtue ([[Benevolence (Ren)|Benevolence]], [[Righteousness (Yi)|Righteousness]], [[Propriety (Li)|Propriety]], and [[Wisdom (Zhi)|Wisdom]]).
- **[[Extension (Tui)|Extension]]**: The process of "filling out" these innate sprouts through **[[Reflection (Si)]]** and practice, starting from **[[Filial Piety (Xiao)]]**.
- **Politics**: Advocated for the [[Well-field System]] and held that rulers have a responsibility to provide for the people's physical needs to allow for moral cultivation. He believed that government should align with the will of **[[Heaven (Tian)]]**.
- **[[The Way (Dao)]]**: Mencius sought to restore the Way of the ancient sage-kings.

## Philosophical Opponents
Mencius defended the Confucian tradition against:
- [[Mozi]] and the doctrine of **[[Impartial Caring]]**.
- [[Yang Zhu]] and his focus on self-preservation.
- Later critiques came from [[Xunzi]], who argued that human nature is bad.

## Connections
- **[[Confucius]]**: Mencius's primary inspiration and the founder of the tradition.
- **[[Zisi]]**: Confucius's grandson and traditionally Mencius's teacher, forming the lineage from the founder to the Second Sage.
- **[[Neo-Confucianism]]**: This later movement, led by figures like **[[Zhu Xi]]**, elevated Mencius's status and grouped the ***[[Mengzi (Text)]]*** into the **[[Four Books]]**.
- **[[Wang Yangming]]**: Another Neo-Confucian who drew heavily on Mencius's ideas of innate knowledge.
