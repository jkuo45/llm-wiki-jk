# Human Nature (Confucianism)

The debate over **Human Nature** (*xìng*) is a central theme in [[Confucianism]].

## Mencius's View
[[Mencius]] famously claimed that "human nature is good." This means that humans have innate tendencies toward virtue, described as the [[Four Sprouts (Confucianism)]]. If provided with a healthy environment and proper education, these sprouts will naturally develop into full virtues.

## Xunzi's Critique
[[Xunzi]] argued against Mencius, stating that "human nature is bad." He believed that human impulses are naturally self-interested and that goodness is only achieved through "deliberate effort" and the transformative power of [[Ritual (Li)|Ritual]].

## Neo-Confucian Interpretation
[[Zhu Xi]] later interpreted Mencius's claim by distinguishing between the "original nature" (which is fundamentally good) and the "physical endowment" (which can be clouded).

## Connections
- **[[Four Sprouts (Confucianism)]]**: The internal source of goodness in Mencius's system.
- **[[Yang Zhu]]**: An earlier thinker who focused on self-preservation as the primary aspect of human nature.
- **[[Xunzi]]**: The primary internal critic of Mencius's optimism regarding nature.
