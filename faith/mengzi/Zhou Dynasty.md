# Zhou Dynasty

The **Zhou Dynasty** (c. 1046–221 BCE) was a pivotal period in Chinese history during which the foundational concepts of Chinese philosophy, including [[Confucianism]], [[Mohism]], and [[Daoism]], were developed.

## Historical Context
The Zhou Dynasty is traditionally divided into two periods:
- **Western Zhou** (c. 1046–771 BCE): A period of relative stability and the establishment of the [[Mandate of Heaven]].
- **Eastern Zhou** (770–221 BCE): Divided into the Spring and Autumn period and the [[Warring States Period]]. [[Mencius]] lived during the latter half of this era.

## Philosophical Significance
During the Eastern Zhou, the "Hundred Schools of Thought" emerged as "masters" articulated various accounts of [[The Way (Dao)]] to address the social and political chaos of the time. Key concepts like [[Heaven (Tian)]] and [[Virtue (De)]] were central to the political and ethical discourse of this period.
