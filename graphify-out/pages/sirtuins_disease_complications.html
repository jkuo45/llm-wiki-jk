SIRTs in Health & Disease — a Complication-by-Complication Atlas

The seven mammalian sirtuins (SIRT1–SIRT7) are expressed aberrantly in almost every disease state, acting as tumor suppressors and/or oncogenes and as protective or harmful factors depending on genetic context, tissue, tumor type and stage. This page turns the task-output complication compilation (cancer → aging) into interactive charts of who protects, who harms, and who flips sign.

Why the same enzyme can protect one organ and harm another

Context-dependence has three interacting sources at the disease level.

One enzyme, many organs

SIRT4 suppresses GI cancers and protects metabolism yet aggravates cardiac hypertrophy/fibrosis.
SIRT2 harms the brain (AD, PD, HD, stroke, asthma) yet protects joints and kidney fibrosis.
SIRT3 is near-universally protective in heart/lung/kidney injury — but its low-ROS "guardian" phenotype creates cancer radio/chemoresistance. "Protective" or "harmful" is always conditional on the organ.

Dose and stage rule

Cardiac SIRT1: 2.5× and 7.5× overexpression attenuate hypertrophy; 12.5× converts it into pathology.
CRC SIRT1: heterozygous KO drives c-Myc/glutamine proliferation, homozygous KO triggers apoptosis.
SIRT7 rises with positive leukemia treatment response and falls on relapse — a monitoring marker, not a fixed tumorigenic role. Direction is meaningless without dose and stage.

SIRT dossiers — follow one enzyme across all systems

Every isoform carries a protective face and a harmful face. The balance depends on tissue, dose, and stage.

SIRT1

the most-studied double agent — protective in most systems, harmful in most cancers Nucleus
+ cytosol

Protective across systems

  • CVD — low/moderate dose cardiovascular protection (hypertrophy 2.5×/7.5×, HF antioxidant, atherosclerosis eNOS, MI/R, hypertension)
  • Respiratory — COPD anti-inflammatory (NF-κB), lung-fibrosis AT2 self-renewal, asthma, ALI
  • Digestive — NAFLD lipid homeostasis, liver I/R via MFN2, pancreatitis, intestinal I/R
  • Nervous — near-universal neuroprotection (AD, PD, HD, stroke, epilepsy, cognition, SCI)
  • Renal / endocrine / motor — DKD, diabetic neuropathy/retinopathy, obesity, OA, osteoporosis, IDD, muscle

Harmful / adverse

  • Cancers — HCC, GC, PC, OC, EC, CC, leukemia (EMT, chemoresistance, stemness), tamoxifen/cisplatin resistance
  • Cardiac hypertrophy at high dose — 12.5× exacerbates (Akt/PDK1)
  • NAFLD / alcoholic hepatitis — intestinal SIRT1 intensifies ferroptosis; myeloid SIRT1 harmful
  • HBV — agonists promote viral transcription
Context variables: dose (cardiac 2.5×/7.5× protective vs 12.5× pathogenic; CRC heterozygous KO → proliferation, homozygous KO → apoptosis), tumor type (suppressor in some BC/LC/HCC models, promoter in others), IBD (deficiency pro-inflammatory, yet deletion protective via Foxp3+ Treg).

SIRT2

the axis-opposer — harms the brain, protects joints & kidney fibrosis Cytosol

Protective / suppressive

  • Musculoskeletal — OA (p65/ECM), IDD, skeletal-muscle protein metabolism
  • Kidney — TGF-β1 fibroblast activation suppression in CKD fibrosis
  • Cancers — BRCA1–BARD1 (breast), IDH1 (CRC), AKR1C1/STAT3 (NSCLC), ovarian cisplatin sensitization
  • Other — IBD (Wnt/β-catenin), atherosclerosis (macrophage polarization), obesity antagonism (in some readings)
  • AML — NAMPT/SIRT2 activation reduces proliferation

Harmful / pro-disease

  • Neurodegeneration — AD (β-secretase, Fzd H4K16), PD (α-syn toxicity), HD, ischemic stroke (serum biomarker ↑)
  • Asthma — enhances Th2 allergic inflammation
  • Liver — I/R injury (MKP-1 deacetylation → MAPK), fibrosis (SIRT2/ERK/c-Myc)
  • AKI — KO ameliorates cisplatin injury
  • Basal-like breast invasion (Slug), endometrial stemness, T-ALL (LMO2), lung tumor growth (PGM K100 → NADPH)
Context: cytosolic tubulin/cell-cycle sirtuin that often opposes the protective SIRT1/3/6 axis. Splits cleanly within one organ: in leukemia, activation helps AML while inhibition suppresses T-ALL; in diabetes it promotes glucose uptake yet counterbalances Akt in neurons.

SIRT3

the mitochondrial guardian — near-universal protection with a cancer caveat Mitochondria

Protective (dominant)

  • Kidney — AKI (sepsis, FAO, ischemia), CKD fibrosis (PDH E1α), kidney stones (Nrf2/HO-1)
  • CVD — hypertrophy (FoxO1/3, PARP-1), fibrosis, MI/R, hypertension, DCM (p53/PFKFB3)
  • Respiratory — COPD, lung fibrosis, asthma, ALI (MnSOD, NLRP3)
  • Metabolic — obesity (macroautophagy/lipid droplets), muscle glucose uptake, FLD, memory/cognition, epilepsy
  • Motoric — IDD, muscle atrophy; anti-cancer EMT suppression (Twist, ERα–p53)

Harmful / adverse

  • Cancer therapy resistance — radioresistance (ATM–Chk2), chemo-resistance in CRC (SOD2/PGC-1α)
  • OA — overexpression promotes chondrocyte apoptosis / OA progression
  • FLD — liver-specific knockdown alleviated alcoholic feeding injury
  • Alcoholic hepato-injury, cervical cancer lipogenic invasion (ACC1)
Context: the primary mitochondrial sirtuin; genuinely dual only in OA and fatty liver. The same low-ROS "guardian" phenotype that prevents injury keeps established tumors alive under therapy. Elevated in node-positive breast cancer and OSCC; serum SIRT3 is a lung-cancer diagnostic (AUC 0.918).

SIRT4

glutamine & lipid brake — a tumor suppressor with cardiac and hepatic costs Mitochondria

Protective / suppressive (dominant)

  • GI cancers — HCC (glutamine metabolism, p16/p21 apoptosis), CRC (E-cadherin), GC (anti-EMT), BC (negatively regulates SIRT1)
  • Metabolic — protects against dyslipidemia/lipogenesis in some models, diabetic kidney
  • CVD — MI/R (decreased after injury; overexpression shrinks infarct)
  • Liver fibrosis — SIRT4/Smad4 axis restrains lipid accumulation/fibrogenesis

Harmful / adverse

  • Cardiac — the only sirtuin with adverse cardiac effects: aggravates Ang II hypertrophy/fibrosis via MnSOD inhibition
  • NAFLD — deacetylates/destabilizes mitochondrial trifunctional protein-α → lipid accumulation
  • Ovarian cancer — high SIRT4 ↔ poor survival
  • Reduced amino-acid-stimulated insulin secretion; suppresses hepatic FAO — metabolically "thrifty"
Context: loss is uniformly prognostic for poor survival in HCC, gastric, CRC, bladder, breast cancer. GDH mono-ADP-ribosylation directly throttles glutamine–α-KG anaplerosis — a natural brake on glutamine-addicted tumors that is costly to systemic metabolism and cardiac stress responses.

SIRT5

the nicotinamide-resistant deacylase — truly mixed across systems Mitochondria

Protective / contextual

  • Hepatic — KO → impaired FAO steatosis; fibrosis increases in KO; protects in liver metabolic disease
  • Kidney — AKI mitochondrial protection (FAO balance)
  • CVD — prevents age-related hypertrophy; loss increases cardiac infarct (succinylation)
  • Epilepsy, pancreatic cancer (favorable prognosis), antioxidant defense (SOD1/IDH2)

Harmful / pro-disease

  • Metabolic — DM progression (PDX1 repression, inhibits insulin secretion)
  • Cancers — CRC (deglutarylation-glutaminolysis, LDHB→autophagy), HCC (OXPHOS→glycolysis), ovarian cisplatin resistance (Nrf2/HO-1)
  • Stroke — BBB permeability via occludin degradation → IR brain damage
Note: almost no classical deacetylase activity (desuccinylase/demalonylase/deglutarylase) and resists nicotinamide inhibition — stays active in tissues where SIRT1–3 are suppressed. In the task's cross-cutting tally: protective in hepatic steatosis/epilepsy/AKI; pro-disease in DM, CRC, HCC.

SIRT6

the most consistently protective — dual only in lung, HCC and obesity Nucleus (heterochromatin)

Protective (dominant)

  • CVD — hypertrophy, fibrosis (AMPK–ACE2), HF (telomerase), atherosclerosis, CAD, MI/R, hypertension
  • Respiratory — COPD, lung fibrosis, asthma, ALI/ARDS (macrophage polarization)
  • Digestive — NAFLD (ChREBP/SREBP-1c), HBV cccDNA suppression, IBD (Rspo1), intestinal I/R
  • Kidney & aging — AKI, CKD fibrosis/VC (MDL-800), telomere preservation, 53BP1
  • Most cancers — anti-glycolysis (hnRNP A1), JAK2/STAT3 inhibition, DDR (CHD4), survivin/c-MYC repression

Harmful / dual

  • Lung cancer — snail-dependent KLF4 transrepression → EMT/metastasis
  • HCC — high SIRT6 correlates with chemoresistance
  • Obesity — exacerbates diet-induced obesity (STAT3 acetylation ↓, POMC ↓)
  • Chemoresistance in breast/prostate — the DDR/austerity program that prevents initiation sustains established tumors in therapy
Bottom line: the most unambiguously protective sirtuin across CVD, respiratory, digestive, kidney and most cancers. Its genome-stability/austerity program is the double edge during cancer therapy, where its pro-repair activity rescues stressed tumor cells.

SIRT7

the least-studied, most-inconsistent sirtuin Nucleus → nucleolus

Protective / contextual

  • Leukemia — rises with positive treatment response, falls on relapse → monitoring biomarker
  • HBV — restricts transcription/replication via cccDNA H3 desuccinylation
  • Lung fibrosis — SIRT7 decline in fibroblasts is profibrotic (Smad3)
  • Cardiac fibrosis — KO also reported to result in fibrosis
  • Anti-metastatic — H3K18 deacetylation silences EMT genes

Harmful / dual

  • Liver — high expression in alcoholic hepatitis / NAFLD is harmful
  • Kidney — SIRT7-deficient mice protected against AKI; promotes tubular damage/inflammation
  • Asthma — promotes airway remodeling (TGF-β1 ASMC)
  • Cardiac fibrosis — enhanced expression/phosphorylation promotes fibrosis (Smad2/ERK)
  • Cancer — ribosome-biogenesis/growth driver (RNA Pol I), p21 suppression in most cancers
Context: RNA Pol I / ribosome-biogenesis driver whose roles are frequently inconsistent (dual in HBV, cardiac fibrosis, CRC). Its H3K18 mark is the canonical sign-flip: lifting p21 (pro-proliferative) in one promoter, silencing EMT genes (anti-metastatic) at another. Undrugged target.

Recent research (2017–2026) reframes the "substrate" list.
  • Beyond deacetylation. Sirtuins are broad deacylases: SIRT1–3 additionally carry robust delactylase (Kla) activity and act on lysine malonylation (Kma), β-hydroxybutyrylation (Kbhb) and succinylation (Ksucc) — so the modification column is a floor, not a closed list (Zhang et al., Glycoconj J 2026, PMID 42329469).
  • Per-isoform deacylase selectivity. Each mammalian sirtuin possesses multiple individual deacylase activities spanning formyl → acetyl → succinyl → myristoyl substrates; dissecting per-activity physiology now requires deacylase-selective mutants/inhibitors (Zheng, Chem Biol Drug Des 2024, PMID 39556442).
  • SIRT2 as a drug target. The α-tubulin/H4K16 deacetylase axis is an active discovery target (selective inhibitors and degraders) — a therapeutic hot spot (Advances in SIRT2-Targeted Therapeutics, Med Res Rev 2026, PMID 42087377).
  • Residue-level precision. SIRT1 deacetylates p53 K320/K373/K382, SUV39H1 K266, p300 K1020/K1024, LKB1 K48, XPA K63/K67, Ku70 (multi-Lys) and NF-κB/RelA K310; SIRT3 deacetylates MnSOD K122 and IDH2 K413; SIRT6 deacetylates CtIP K432/K526/K604 and mono-ADP-ribosylates PARP1 K521; SIRT7 deacetylates H3K18 (Barber et al. 2012); SIRT5 desuccinylates CPS1.

Cross-cutting observations & the bottom line

SIRT1 is the most extensively studied and shows opposing roles across and within diseases. Tumor suppressor vs promoter in breast, lung, HCC, CRC; protective vs harmful in hypertrophy, fatty liver, IBD, AKI, kidney fibrosis, diabetes and lung injury. Its name is a caveat, not an answer.
SIRT2 often opposes the SIRT1/SIRT3/SIRT6 protective axis. Harmful in AD, PD, HD, asthma and liver I/R; mostly protective in OA, IDD, kidney fibrosis and neuropathic pain — and it splits within single organs (AML vs T-ALL).
SIRT3 is the mitochondrial workhorse with near-universal protective roles in AKI, MI/R injury, diabetic cardiomyopathy, lung fibrosis, DKD and metabolic disease (dual only in OA and fatty liver), with a cancer caveat: its low-ROS phenotype confers therapy resistance.
SIRT4 is predominantly tumor-suppressive and metabolically protective (BC, HCC, CRC, GC, diabetes, DKD) — but the only sirtuin with adverse cardiac effects (aggravates Ang II hypertrophy/fibrosis), a living example of tissue-specific sign.
SIRT6 is consistently protective across CVD, respiratory, digestive, kidney and most cancers (context-dependent only in lung and HCC). The most reliable "anti-disease" sirtuin at the system level.
SIRT7 is the least studied and most inconsistent (dual in HBV, cardiac fibrosis, CRC) — a monitoring biomarker in leukemia whose RNA-Pol-I axis drives proliferation.
SIRT5 is genuinely mixed — protective in hepatic steatosis, epilepsy, AKI; pro-disease in DM, CRC, HCC; and nicotinamide-resistant, so it stays active where SIRT1–3 are pharmacologically suppressed.
Caveat. Every sirtuin shows dual, context-dependent behavior — an organ or tumor's phenotype cannot be read from a single sirtuin in isolation. The same enzyme is simultaneously a tumor suppressor in one tissue, a pro-disease driver in another, and a dose-dependent switch within a single organ. Where the task output records conflicting studies (SIRT1 in lung, SIRT7 in fibrosis), the conflicting verdicts themselves are the data. That is why sirtuin modulation — activator or inhibitor — must be tissue-, stage- and dose-aware, and why SIRT1's dose window and SIRT4's glutamine brake are the most instructive therapeutic leads this compilation yields.
