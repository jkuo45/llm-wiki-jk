# Adrenochrome, aminoguanidine and methylene blue protocol review

Scope: synthesis of vault sources in `src/notes/adrenochrome/` (260 files), `src/notes/_link/` entity and document notes, `src/tasks/adrenochrome_mb_ag/` (26 files), recent tasks (`task_output_adrenochrome_eryptosis_crossover_04_Nov_2026`, `task_output_hardcoded_individual_biomarker_gates_26_Sep_2026`, Aug 2026 node-network traces) and the published page `web/public/pages/en-US/adrenochrome-protocol.html`, compared with external literature through 12 November 2026. **Extended 18_Nov_2026:** the review now also carries five novel longevity-directed hypotheses (synthesized from the 18_Nov_2026 research session, with external component research through 18 November 2026) and a plausibility assessment of those hypotheses against the MRR/GOPS/SRAC protocol constructs; see the two sections before the Summary. Evidence tiers: Tier 1 = human randomized trial; Tier 2 = human cohort/association or historical clinical use; Tier 3 = rodent/in-vivo animal; Tier 4 = in-vitro or ex-vivo human cells; Tier 5 = hypothesis/untested.

The published page is labelled `[Speculative]` and states `untested in humans` (`adrenochrome-protocol.html:6-7,42,44-48`). That label is consistent with the vault evidence: the MRR, GOPS and SRAC combinations have no direct combination study; GOPS self-scores `7.2/10` and concedes it admits no direct combo studies (`_document_ - GOPS - glyco-oxidative proteostasis.md:29-39,93`).

## Recent external research relevant to vault claims

### Aminochrome and autophagosome-lysosome fusion, 2026

Briceño et al., *Antioxidants* 15(6):739 (PMC13295424; PMID 42352045), differentiated SH-SY5Y dopaminergic neurons exposed to 50 uM aminochrome for 16 h: LC3b-LAMP1 Pearson colocalization decreased 27% (`p<0.001`), LC3b-II intensity increased 81% (`p<0.01`), perinuclear vimentin aggregates increased 6-fold (`p<0.001`), ubiquitin mRNA increased 9-fold at 4 h (`p<0.001`), and no added LC3b-II increase with bafilomycin A1, consistent with impaired autophagic flux. Vinblastine potentiated the fusion block; rapamycin (10 uM) and trehalose (100 mM) preincubation partially attenuated death despite the fusion block. Mechanism reported: aminochrome-tubulin adducts, microtubule destabilization, blocked autophagosome-lysosome fusion, perinuclear and nuclear ubiquitin aggregates. Tier 4. The finding is in SH-SY5Y cells; it remains untested as a causal mechanism in human Parkinson disease.

A 2026 preprint on aminochrome as single-neuron neurotoxin is consistent with this direction. This search did not identify a human aminochrome exposure trial.

### Carbazochrome hemostasis, 2025

Carbazochrome sodium sulfonate plus tranexamic acid was reported more effective than tranexamic acid alone for bleeding control in a 2025 meta-analysis (PMID 40676674, cited by 6 at search time). A 2025 randomized triple-blind study of intravenous carbazochrome sodium sulfonate (PMID 39914600) addresses perioperative bleeding. No NRF2/SIRT1, Complex III superoxide, or hormesis endpoint was reported in these sources. Tier 1 for the bleeding indication; Tier 5 for MRR triggering.

### Methylene blue, 2024-2026

This search did not identify a new randomized human trial of methylene blue for cognition, tau pathology, methemoglobinemia-independent mitochondrial benefit, or lifespan. Vault claims rest on earlier work: Atamna, FASEB J. 2008 (fibroblast senescence); Atamna, Redox Biol. 2015; Tucker, Mol Neurobiol. 2018; Xiong et al., Sci Rep. 2017, all cited in `senescence/_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence...:946-966`. Tier 4 for senescence delay in culture; Tier 3 for tau-transgenic mouse prevention data where treatment started before pathology.

### Aminoguanidine, 2024-2026

A 2024 glycation review (Uceda et al., Biophys Rev) retains pimagedine as prototype dicarbonyl trap for methylglyoxal and glyoxal. No revival of the diabetic nephropathy program was identified. The negative human result (Tier 1-negative) therefore stands as the highest-tier human evidence, and the vault's long-term use carries the reported toxicity pattern (pro-oxidant activity, autoimmune reactions, vitamin B6 depletion; `Aminoguanidine.md:38-45`).

## Protocol structure as documented in vault

- **MRR (mitohormetic redox relay)** (`_document_ - MRR - mitohormesis.md`, created 2026-06-15, updated 2026-07-09): carbazochrome trigger plus methylene blue shunt plus NR/NMN fuel plus urolithin A cleanup, proposed to activate NRF2/SIRT1/PGC-1a. Proposed assays only (`:66-74`); Tier 4-5.
- **GOPS (glyco-oxidative proteostasis)** (`_document_ - GOPS - glyco-oxidative proteostasis.md`, 2026-06-19): [[Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine]] plus spermidine 1-5 mg plus carnosine 500 mg-1 g. No direct combination study (`:39,93`); Tier 5.
- **SRAC (SASP-remodeling aminochrome complex)** (`_document_ - SRAC - SASP-Remodeling Aminochrome Complex.md`, 2026-07-04, updated 2026-07-09): Phase 1 AMM plus methylene blue plus rapamycin 5 mg/week plus GlyNAC, then Phase 2 fisetin; nanoparticle delivery removed and rapamycin/fisetin sequenced in the 08-09 Jul revision (`:18-24`); Tier 4-5.
- **Outline dosing** (`outline_adrenochrome_mb_ag_protocol_27_JUN_2026-00.md:53-55`): methylene blue 0.5-2 mg/kg/day oral; aminoguanidine 150-300 mg twice daily; histamine-intolerance risk flagged. The web protocol prints methylene blue 37-75 mg/day, rapamycin 5 mg/week, fisetin 500-1500 mg, aminoguanidine 150-300 mg twice daily (`:439-526`), with a disclaimer that all dosing is estimates, not validated regimens (`:540`).

## Unresolved mechanistic contradictions

### Carbazochrome redox activity

`Carbazochrome.md:43-45` states the semicarbazone blocks one-electron reduction, rendering carbazochrome redox-silent, while `Carbazochrome.md:49` claims MRR-relevant superoxide generation from Complex III. The Aug 2026 Round-3 trace (`node_network_adrenochrome_24_AUG_2026/...:35-38`) adjudicated carbazochrome as redox-attenuated and removed the Complex-I edge as an evidence-based null. The MRR trigger claim remains undemonstrated with semicarbazone chemistry as currently described.

### Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine and dicarbonyl trapping

[[Aminoguanidine]] traps 1,2-dicarbonyls through its free, nucleophilic hydrazine group (`-NH-NH2`). In [[Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine]], that group is consumed to form the hydrazone bond to the indoline-dione core (`_document_ - glyco-oxidative modulation.md:38-45`). AMM AGE inhibition therefore lacks a stated mechanism; the vault lists prodrug hydrolysis, guanidine-only activity, or metal chelation as untested alternatives, yet GOPS/SRAC documents continue to assert dicarbonyl trapping by AMM in senescent cells. Systematic evaluation of AMM in modern aging or collagen crosslinking models is lacking in peer-reviewed literature (`glyco-oxidative modulation.md:55`).

### Aminochrome and autophagy direction

The 2026 SH-SY5Y results report aminochrome blockade of autophagosome-lysosome fusion with accumulation of perinuclear and nuclear aggregates. The protocol direction (deliberate aminochrome-family redox cycling intended to activate autophagy) is opposite to the reported effect of aminochrome itself; rapamycin-containing phases attenuate death in vitro without restoring fusion. Briceño et al. methods used 50 uM aminochrome (480 nm, molar extinction coefficient 3058 M-1 cm-1); these values are specific to that model and are not generalized to other aminochromes.

## Safety gates identified within vault evidence

- **[[NQO1]] rs1800566:** C609T T/T is functionally null (2-4% wild-type activity, Siegel et al. 1999). The Sep 2026 gate analysis (`task_output_hardcoded_individual_biomarker_gates_26_Sep_2026.md:123-129`) reports ~20% carrier rate in East Asians and no stratification in MRR dosing; NQO1-null subjects clear quinones via one-electron CYP450/b5-reductase cycling instead of two-electron detoxification. `NQO1.md:62` records the same gap.
- **[[G6PD]] deficiency:** methylene blue contraindication for hemolysis. Framed correctly in the vault as an admissibility gate, but no screening protocol is defined (gap analysis 09 Jul 2026 `:35-40`).
- **Serotonin syndrome:** methylene blue is a potent MAO-A inhibitor; co-administration with SSRI/SNRI agents is contraindicated (`_link/_document_ - methylene blue and aminguanidine.md:85`).
- **Histamine intolerance:** aminoguanidine inhibits gut diamine oxidase; long-term use is reported to produce dietary histamine intolerance and autoimmune reactions (`_link/_document_ - methylene blue and aminguanidine.md:87,89-90`).
- **Methemoglobinemia:** methylene blue is both the proposed shuttle and the antidote for methemoglobinemia; co-oximetry, not pulse oximetry, is required to detect methemoglobin (`task_output_adrenochrome_gap_analysis_09_JUL_2026.md:35-40`).
- **[[MnSOD]] rs4880 and [[COMT]]:** Sep 2026 gates report a 30-40% shift in MnSOD activity altering the SIRT3/SIRT4 hormetic window by the same fraction (`:102-104`), and [[COMT]] status as a gate for adrenochrome/dopaminochrome formation (`:73`). Neither gate is encoded in protocol dosing.
- **Hoffer hypothesis:** [[Adrenochrome Hypothesis]] in schizophrenia (Hoffer/Osmond, 1950s) was not confirmed in Canadian MRC and APA trials and was abandoned by mainstream psychiatry by the 1970s (`Abram Hoffer.md:39`). Mixed/unreplicated erythrocyte lysis reports remain unreliable (`Adrenochrome Hypothesis.md:31`).

## Cross-reference to Nesci and Algieri 2026 (inflammaging review)

Source: Nesci S, Algieri C. Mitochondrial dysfunction and inflammaging: a mechanistic bridge between bioenergetic failure and senescence. Geroscience 2026-10-08, doi:10.1007/s11357-026-02588-y (`src/notes/senescence/_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction and inflammaging a mechanistic bridge between bioenergetic failure and senescence.md`). The review does not discuss [[Adrenochrome]], [[Aminoguanidine]], [[Carbazochrome]] or [[Methylene blue]] directly except for [[Urolithin A]] as a MRR component; cross-reference below is therefore mechanistic, not direct.

### Shared mechanism: OXPHOS decline and Complex I/III electron leak

Nesci and Algieri frame ageing as a vicious cycle in which impaired OXPHOS lowers ATP output and membrane potential while increasing electron leak at [[Complex I]] and [[Complex III]], generating [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] that damage mtDNA, lipids and proteins further. The vault MRR protocol proposes [[Methylene blue]] as a Complex I-to-Complex IV electron shunt and carbazochrome/MB pulsing as a hormetic ROS signal. The review's framing is consistent with the vault's own dose-flip observation (MB wellness window ~0.5-4 mg; pro-oxidant flip above 2 mg/kg, `_link/Methylene blue.md:32-33`): in aged mitochondria with compromised antioxidant buffering and altered [[Iron|iron]] homeostasis, added redox cycling is reported to sensitize cells to lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis rather than to remain adaptive. The review defines [[Ferroptosis]] by iron-dependent phospholipid peroxidation with stimulus-dependent mitochondrial involvement (dispensable after direct [[GPX4]] inhibition in the systems examined), while the vault's adrenochrome-as-ferroptosis-inducer claim (GPX4 selenocysteine arylation, analogous to RSL3) remains Tier 5.

### NAD+/NADH ratio and the MiDAS secretome

In MiDAS, reduced [[NAD+]]/[[NADH]] ratio engages [[AMPK]]-dependent [[p53]] activation: the axis maintains [[p21]]-dependent arrest while restraining the [[IL-1beta|IL-1]]-dependent arm of the [[SASP]], producing a rewired rather than amplified secretome. Restoration of redox balance (e.g., pyruvate supplementation in experimental systems) partially rescues the IL-1 arm and shifts MiDAS toward a more canonical SASP. The vault protocol includes NR/NMN to raise NAD+ status and expects reduced senescence burden. Under the MiDAS model, NAD+ repletion predicts a qualitative shift in SASP composition, not measured clearance of senescent cells. The review reports no human data establishing that NAD+ restoration removes senescent cells or slows ageing.

### Autophagy, mitophagy and lysosomal flux

Nesci and Algieri state that increased autophagy-marker abundance should not be equated with increased degradative flux; impaired acidification or proteolytic activity limits mitochondrial turnover despite upstream mitophagy signals, and mitophagic clearance restrains [[NLRP3 inflammasome|NLRP3]] activation in macrophage systems. The 2026 aminochrome data (50 uM, LC3b-LAMP1 colocalization -27%, no added LC3b-II with bafilomycin A1) are consistent with this flux-block pattern. MRR/SRAC assign rapamycin, [[Urolithin A]] and trehalose-like induction the cleanup role. The review reports that in the cited RCT of urolithin A (n=66 older adults, 2 months), muscle endurance improved but the primary outcomes, 6-min walk distance and maximal ATP production, were not statistically significant between groups, and the authors state these data do not establish slowed ageing or mitophagy mediation. The protocol's cleanup step therefore has the same Tier 1-negative status in humans as aminoguanidine.

### Minority MOMP, mtDNA DAMPs and readouts absent from protocol

The review describes [[Minority MOMP|minority MOMP]] enabling BAX/BAK-dependent mtDNA release and [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]]-mediated SASP regulation without cell death, with mtDNA, [[Cardiolipin]], [[N-Formyl Peptides|N-formyl peptides]] and mitochondrial ROS engaging [[TLR9]], [[NLRP3]] and cGAS-STING to produce sterile inflammation. The authors state prevalence and quantitative contribution of minority MOMP to physiological human ageing are not established, and that inflammatory output should be interpreted jointly by metabolic state, membrane integrity, DAMP accessibility and neighbour sensing capacity. The vault protocol's proposed readouts (SIRT3/SIRT4 ratio, HRV-gated dosing, methemoglobin by co-oximetry) do not include cytosolic mtDNA, cGAS-STING activity or NLRP3 readouts. A quinone-pulsing protocol evaluated without DAMP sensing would miss the review's central amplification loop.

### Heteroplasmy threshold and genotype gates

The review's mtDNA threshold model (functional recessiveness until a mutation- and tissue-specific heteroplasmy threshold, modulated by copy number) is structurally parallel to the vault's NQO1 rs1800566 gate (T/T functionally null, one-electron cycling instead of two-electron detoxification). Both describe competence thresholds that convert subclinical load into overt dysfunction, yet neither threshold is encoded in protocol dosing. Nesci and Algieri caution that respiratory deficiency in a cell does not imply ROS, senescence and inflammation occur in the same cells; the protocol's tissue-calibrated hormetic window makes the same unstated assumption in reverse.

### Clinical translation limits shared with SRAC

The review reports a randomized phase II trial of dasatinib plus quercetin (n=60 postmenopausal women) with no significant between-group difference in bone resorption marker, an open-label phase I dasatinib plus quercetin study in Alzheimer's disease (n=5, feasibility only, dasatinib but not quercetin detected in CSF), and an uncontrolled dapansutrile study in acute gout. SRAC uses fisetin sequentially rather than dasatinib plus quercetin, so the cited trials are analogous rather than direct, but the translation gap is shared: no validated tissue biomarker of senescence burden, mitophagy or mtDAMP activity in humans, no defined responder population, and a reported risk that chronic inflammasome or nucleic-acid-sensing suppression compromises host defence. The review concludes that changes in circulating biomarkers or selected performance measures do not establish restoration of mitochondrial quality control or slowing of ageing; the same conclusion applies to the vault protocol's biomarker claims.

## Five novel longevity-directed hypotheses (added 18_Nov_2026)

The following hypotheses extend adrenochrome biology beyond the constructs reviewed above and beyond the vault's earlier sirtuin, senotherapeutic, ferroptosis and eryptosis syntheses. Each is anchored in biology the vault documents but has never developed in a longevity direction, and each was checked against external literature on its surrounding components — phosphatase redox chemistry, HSC aging intervention, neuromelanin–iron stoichiometry, adrenergic receptor redox regulation and aging, neutrophil–senescence immunology, oxidant-activated prodrug design. Direct adrenochrome literature was deliberately excluded from that enrichment, per the framing of the originating research question. Ranked by information yield per unit effort, not plausibility. All five inherit the safety gates listed above (NQO1 rs1800566, G6PD, MnSOD rs4880, COMT, co-oximetric methemoglobin).

### H1 — Hematopoietic stem cell redox preconditioning against immunosenescence

**Falsifiable statement:** chronic low-dose [[Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine]] (AMM) or carbazochrome, at exposures that do not raise [[Methemoglobin]] or deplete marrow glutathione, preserves [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell]] self-renewal and lymphoid output in aged mice by pre-inducing redox defenses, measurably delaying the myeloid skew quantified by the vault's CCL5–[[CCR5]] axis. **Alternative it must beat:** the historical radioprotection was a membrane- or aminoguanidine-moiety effect specific to acute radiation insult, irrelevant to the slow oxidative attrition that drives baseline HSC aging — in which case AMM changes no aging endpoint, or (worse) accelerates [[Clonal Hematopoiesis]].

Established basis (vault): AMM used clinically in Japan as a hemostatic capillary stabilizer and radioprotective agent, attributed to shielding cell membranes and hematopoietic progenitors from radiation oxidative damage (`_document_ - glyco-oxidative modulation.md`; Tier 2 historical). Aging HSCs accumulate oxidative DNA damage, activating p38 MAPK and suppressing self-renewal with myeloid skew (Tier 3–4). [[SIRT3]] falls in aged HSCs; its restoration partially reverses the decline (Tier 3). Aged marrow cytotoxic CD4+ T cells drive myelopoiesis through CCL5–CCR5, normalized by [[Maraviroc]] (Tier 3).

External enrichment (18_Nov_2026): the mechanism class is validated — DDO1002, a KEAP1–NRF2 protein–protein-interaction inhibitor, activates the ARE program, lowers ROS, delays senescence and improves HSC aging and stress response in mice (Li et al., Life Medicine 2024, PMID 39872153; Tier 3); Nrf2-null HSCs are ROS-hypersensitive and exhaust. Nrf2-null mice also develop age-dependent anemia through shortened erythrocyte lifespan and ineffective erythropoiesis (PMC11047311; Tier 3), tying the mechanism to the erythroid line and the vault's eryptosis corpus. A validated in-vivo endpoint exists: depleting myeloid-biased HSCs rejuvenates aged immunity (Ross et al., Nature 2024, 10.1038/s41586-024-07238-x; Tier 3). Human HSCs durably "remember" inflammatory stress (Nature 2026, 10.1038/s41586-026-10522-7; Tier 3–4) — supporting the preconditioning-imprinting premise and, equally, its deleterious alternative.

Extension (Tier 5): a redox-active indoline-dione scaffold delivered chronically at mildly hormetic exposure could keep the primitive-HSC Nrf2 arm primed, lowering the cumulative oxidative lesion burden that both suppresses self-renewal directly and sustains the inflammatory marrow environment feeding myelopoiesis. No vault document connects AMM radioprotection to the immunosenescence chain.

Design: 20–22-month C57BL/6 mice, four arms (n=12): vehicle; low-dose AMM; low-dose carbazochrome sulfonate; maraviroc positive control. 12 weeks. Endpoints: LSK/GMP/CLP fractions, neutrophil:lymphocyte ratio, competitive repopulation, 8-oxo-dG in sorted HSCs (LC-MS/MS), p-p38 in Lin−Sca1+cKit+ cells; Tet2-mutant VAF dynamics in a sensitized arm. In vitro: old vs. young Lin−Sca1+cKit+ cells, AMM/carbazochrome pretreatment then H₂O₂ challenge; requirement test in Nrf2-null HSCs. Success: ≥30% restoration of lymphoid:myeloid ratio at equal methemoglobin burden, NRF2-dependent. Falsifier: any increase in 8-oxo-dG or mutant-clone VAF versus vehicle. Note that AMM's dicarbonyl-trapping claim is already adjudicated broken above and forms no part of this hypothesis.

### H2 — Transient PTP1B redox switching for insulin sensitization

**Falsifiable statement:** in insulin-resistant cells, a nanomolar, minute-scale [[Adrenochrome]] pulse produces predominantly H₂O₂-mediated (indirect), thioredoxin-reversible oxidation of the [[PTP1B]] catalytic cysteine — transiently disinhibiting insulin receptor phosphorylation and glucose uptake — whereas direct quinone arylation of PTP1B or [[PTEN]] remains below detection at that dose; the same pulse at micromolar concentrations crosses into irreversible arylation and must be excluded. **Alternative it must beat:** adrenochrome's electrophilic quinone dominates over its redox-cycling output at all useful concentrations, so any PTP inhibition is irreversible, non-signaling, and co-inactivates PTEN — the "insulin sensitizer" is actually a pan-phosphatase alkylator with pro-tumorigenic Akt signaling.

Established basis (vault): PTP1B Cys215 is reversibly oxidized by H₂O₂ to a sulfenyl-amide, reduced back by the [[Thioredoxin]] system (Tier 4, established biochemistry); PTEN Cys124 oxidation sustains [[PI3K]]–[[Akt]] signaling and chronic oxidation converts a regulatory signal into pathology (Tier 4); [[SIRT1]] improves [[Insulin Sensitivity]] partly by repressing PTP1B (Tier 3–4); adrenochrome [[Redox Cycling]] generates superoxide → dismutation → H₂O₂ (Tier 4); adrenochrome arylates protein thiols via [[Michael addition]] (Tier 4). No adrenochrome–phosphatase edge exists anywhere in the vault graph.

External enrichment (18_Nov_2026): the target is clinically validated in humans — IONIS-PTP1BRx antisense produced HbA1c reduction, improved insulin sensitivity and weight loss over 26 weeks (Digenio et al., PMID 29439147; Tier 1), and the MD-18 peptide inhibitor was safe in Phase 1a with insulin-sensitizing signals (ADA Diabetes 2026 abstract; Tier 2). The question reduces to whether a transient redox pulse is a feasible inhibition mode. Constraint: PTP1B is inhibited by H₂O₂ but not superoxide (PMC8113030) — new prediction: adrenochrome-pulse-dependent PTP1B oxidation should be SOD-dependent. Warning: quinone-methide activity probes covalently modify all six PTP1B cysteines (2004 activity-probe study; journal locator unverified), and celastrol, a quinone-methide triterpene, inhibits PTP1B/TCPTP in the hypothalamus in vivo while covalently modifying other cysteine targets (J Med Chem 2018, 10.1021/acs.jmedchem.8b01224; Tier 3) — the competing chemistry is real, though selective covalent inactivation via allosteric C121 is achievable with designed conjugates (Punthasee et al., Biochemistry 2017, PMID 28345882; Tier 4). In the millimolar-GSH cytosol, S-glutathionylation is the more probable reversible species than sulfenamide (PMC5318241), and 14-3-3ζ binds the oxidized form (PMC6982540) — both measurable covariates.

Extension (Tier 5): the two chemistries have different concentration dependencies — indirect H₂O₂ signaling requires only catalytic redox turnover (effective at low nanomolar parent compound), direct arylation follows second-order kinetics on free quinone. A dosing window may exist where PTP1B experiences mostly reversible chemistry, mirroring the distinction the vault draws for NF-κB/Keap1 cysteines at low concentrations.

Design (decisive first experiment): recombinant PTP1B, adrenochrome 1 nM–50 µM, 0–60 min; parallel readouts of activity recovery after Trx/DTT washout (reversible fraction) and intact-mass LC-MS for adducts (irreversible fraction). The hypothesis lives only if a ≥10:1 reversible window exists below the cytotoxic EC50. Cellular follow-up: insulin-resistant HepG2/3T3-L1, window-dose pulse then insulin; IR β phosphotyrosine, Akt T308/S473, 2-NBDG uptake; PEG-catalase (should abolish), NAC (should abolish), basal-pAkt (must not rise — rising basal Akt indicates PTEN co-inactivation, a kill signal); SOD modulation arms; activity-based cysteine profiling for off-target breadth; celastrol and the C121 conjugate as named comparators.

### H3 — Directed low-occupancy neuromelanin-type pigment as a labile-iron buffer

**Falsifiable statement:** in iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons, slow intracellular delivery of [[Leuco-adrenochrome]] at doses generating low-occupancy, early-stage [[Melanins]]-type pigment increases labile-iron sequestration and reduces iron-catalyzed ROS and [[Ferroptosis]] vulnerability to an RSL3-type challenge; beyond an occupancy threshold the effect reverses. **Alternative it must beat:** neuromelanin accumulation in the [[Substantia Nigra]] is a correlate — or cause — of neuronal vulnerability, in which case added pigment is monotonically harmful.

Established basis (vault): adrenochrome polymerizes into neuromelanin-like pigment; NM is built from [[5,6-Dihydroxyindole]]/DHICA and catechol-derived quinones; NM sequesters reactive quinones/aminochromes into its stable polymer and accumulates with age (triples layer; Tier 4). Transition metals catalyze the Fenton chemistry that both forms and injures catecholaminergic tissue; [[Ferritin]]/ceruloplasmin normally sequester labile iron (Tier 4).

External enrichment (18_Nov_2026): direct experimental support — isolated human NM blocks Fenton hydroxyl-radical production dose-dependently, spares ascorbate, and prevents iron-driven dopamine oxidation to toxic quinones, protective even under iron overload into the pigment (Zecca et al., PMID 18624918; Tier 4). Normal-SN NM is ~50% Fe(III)-saturated with residual capacity (PMC1187132), and NM's iron-binding capacity is 10-fold greater than synthetic dopamine melanin (PMID 12907248). Counter-evidence: single-cell XRF/MRI across the chimpanzee lifespan found a stable iron:NM ratio, low cytosolic labile iron throughout adulthood, and no evidence of NM saturation or age-increased iron-mediated toxicity (Büttner et al., bioRxiv 2026, 10.1101/2026.05.08.721830; Tier 3–4, preprint) — weakening the inverted-U's saturation arm while strengthening the low-occupancy protective arm. Revised prediction: a protective plateau across physiological iron loads, with the failure mode being pigment volume/load rather than saturation chemistry; the inverted-U remains the falsifier for the overload regime.

Design: iPSC midbrain dopaminergic neurons, leuco-adrenochrome at 3 doses for 2 weeks; pigment quantification, calcein-AM labile iron, Mössbauer/ICP-MS on isolated granules, hydroxyl-radical output under Fe²⁺/H₂O₂ challenge, [[Ferrostatin-1]]-rescuable ferroptosis survival; secondary cardiac sympathetic-neuron site. Monotonic harm falsifies. Risk: the delivery adds exactly the precursor chemistry implicated in Parkinsonian proteotoxicity — the 2026 aminochrome autophagy-block data above predict that free aminochrome escaping polymerization is harmful; nearly all flux must route into polymer, which is unproven.

### H4 — Self-limiting beta-2 adrenergic inactivation as a transient sympatholysis

**Falsifiable statement:** a single ultra-low systemic exposure to a short-lived adrenochrome derivative transiently reduces vascular beta-2 adrenergic receptor signaling competence (recovering over days as receptors turn over), and sparse repeated pulses produce a net reduction in chronic adrenergic drive with resensitization. **Alternative it must beat:** covalent thiol modification by a small electrophile is insufficiently receptor-selective, and any hemodynamic effect is nonspecific toxicity.

Established basis (vault): adrenochrome directly inactivates the beta-2 adrenergic receptor through covalent thiol modification, preventable by reducing agents (Tier 4); adrenochrome formation sequesters epinephrine away from receptor signaling (Tier 4–5); plasma half-life is minutes (Tier 4). Aging context (external, Tier 2–3): plasma norepinephrine ~66% higher in older men, chronic catecholamine drive downregulates β-receptors.

External enrichment (18_Nov_2026), which weakens the hypothesis: β2AR redox regulation is physiological and reversible (agonist- and H₂O₂-driven S-sulfenation increases ligand binding; irreversible over-oxidation disables signaling; Burns & Moniri, PMID 21917560; PMC6917825; PMC7031529; Tier 4) — adrenochrome's covalent inactivation is the pathological, irreversible arm of that axis. In older humans, two weeks of clonidine reduced plasma norepinephrine (−47%) but β-adrenergic resensitization was significantly blunted versus young (PMID 17086093; Tier 2) — the falsifier is partially realized before any experiment. Propranolol upregulated cardiac β-receptor density ~50% in aged rats (PMID 1660554; Tier 3) and the documented restoration route is GRK2 lowering (PMC6383565), not receptor inactivation; aged atria lose βAR responsiveness while aged ventricles lose innervation entirely (PMID 29938794). Verdict: deprioritized; the only defensible reframing (reversible S-sulfenation-level modulation) is chemistry adrenochrome does not deliver.

### H5 — MPO-activated leuco-adrenochrome prodrug for neutrophil-directed senolysis

**Falsifiable statement:** [[Leuco-adrenochrome]] is substantially inert toward senescent fibroblasts in isolation, but in co-culture with activated [[Neutrophils]] it is oxidized by [[Myeloperoxidase]]-derived oxidants to the active aminochrome, producing selective death of neighboring NQO1/GSTM2-deficient senescent cells while proliferating cells and neutrophil-free tissue are spared — neutrophil infiltration is the targeting mechanism, not an off-target. **Alternative it must beat:** leuco-adrenochrome oxidizes readily enough in transit (trace oxygen, ambient metals) that it never reaches the inflamed site intact, reproducing the untargeted systemic toxicity that disqualifies free adrenochrome.

Established basis (vault): MPO released by activated neutrophils accelerates epinephrine conversion to adrenochrome (Tier 4) — the only in-vivo documented enzymatic generation route in the corpus; senescent cells with downregulated [[NQO1]]/[[GSTM2]] fail to detoxify aminochromes (the vault's senolytic hypothesis); senescent/SASP-rich tissue is where neutrophils infiltrate (Tier 4); the leuco form requires inert-atmosphere storage because it oxidizes spontaneously (Tier 4) — the transit problem. The vault's senolytic hypothesis has no delivery solution; this hypothesis proposes the body's own neutrophil oxidant chemistry as the activation layer.

External enrichment (18_Nov_2026), the strongest of the five: the targeting premise is now measured — senescent endothelial cells promote pathogenic neutrophil adhesion and extravasation in vivo (PMC11387759; Tier 3–4); SASP chemokines recruit neutrophils to senescent cells (Majewska & Krizhanovsky, Nature Aging 2025; Van Avondt, Immunol Rev 2023). The therapeutic logic exists in physiology: in ischemic retinopathy, senescent vascular units activate neutrophils to release NETs that clear senescent endothelial cells and enable vascular regeneration (Binet et al. 2020, cited therein; Tier 3) — H5 amplifies an existing clearance mechanism. The prodrug class has in-vivo proof of concept: N-alkylaminoferrocene prodrugs (pro-NAAFs) are activated within neutrophils by MPO-derived HOCl/HO•, amplify local ROS, restored inflammation-resolving NET formation and resolved chronic arthritis in mice with tolerability (Euler et al., Redox Biology 2026, PMID 42472513; Tier 3); a 2018 review laid out the neutrophil-elastase-activated design grammar (Reshetnikov et al., PMID 30150984). New context: aged organs accumulate senescent CXCR4+ neutrophils themselves through failed EP2-dependent macrophage efferocytosis, and restoring clearance reverses organ-aging phenotypes in mice (Science 2026, 10.1126/science.aea3075; Tier 3) — the aged-tissue activator population is larger than assumed, and senescent neutrophils are both a potential co-target and an off-target risk.

Design: chemistry gate first — stopped-flow kinetics of leuco-adrenochrome oxidation by MPO/H₂O₂/Cl⁻, HOCl alone, and air-saturated buffer ± Fe/Cu; requirement: MPO rate ≥10× transit oxidation. Co-culture senolysis (irradiation-senescent IMR-90 + fMLP-activated human neutrophils ± leuco-adrenochrome; annexin V/PI by cell type, SA-β-gal; MPO-inhibition and neutrophil-removal controls; NQO1-null stratification). In vivo gate: p16-3MR/INK-ATTAC mice, local vs. systemic dosing, no methemoglobin or troponin signal at efficacious systemic doses. Risks: on-target/off-tissue senolysis in infected or healing wounds (wound-healing impairment is a hard stop); formulation may need a further-stabilized derivative, requiring re-proof of MPO selectivity.

## Hypotheses versus protocol constructs — plausibility assessment (added 18_Nov_2026)

The central asymmetry: all three protocol constructs share one structure — a generic geroprotector stack (methylene blue + NR/NMN + urolithin A; spermidine + carnosine; rapamycin + fisetin + GlyNAC) plus an adrenochrome-family component assigned a trigger role. In every case the vault's own adjudication (above) breaks that trigger's chemistry: MRR's carbazochrome cannot generate the superoxide pulse that names the protocol; GOPS's AMM cannot trap dicarbonyls and its UPR-hormesis premise is unevidenced while aminochrome blocks autophagic flux; SRAC's distinctive targeting layer was removed in the July revision, leaving a rapamycin-anchored stack with an AMM ornament. The protocols are therefore "more likely to work" only in the sense that their non-adrenochrome components might work — and that outcome validates nothing about adrenochrome. The differentiating claim of each protocol is exactly its weakest link. Conversely, H2 and H5 use the family's documented chemistry (electrophilic/redox duality; MPO substrate) as the active principle pointed at externally validated targets (PTP1B, Tier 1; neutrophil-localized ROS amplification, Tier 3), concentrating all uncertainty in one stated, cheaply killable chemistry question each.

## Summary

Recent evidence does not position adrenochrome-family compounds as validated protocol agents in humans. The highest-tier human data are negative for aminoguanidine (failed Phase III), positive only for narrow non-longevity indications for methylene blue (methemoglobinemia) and carbazochrome (hemostasis). The most recent mechanistic work (2026, Tier 4) characterizes aminochrome as an autophagosome-lysosome fusion blocker and proteotoxic agent in dopaminergic cells. Vault MRR/GOPS/SRAC constructs remain Tier 5 hypotheses with three load-bearing internal contradictions (carbazochrome redox silence vs trigger; hydrazine consumption vs AGE trapping; NQO1/G6PD genotype absent from dosing).

The 18_Nov_2026 extension sharpens that verdict rather than softening it: as adrenochrome-centered claims, the five novel hypotheses outrank the protocol constructs — H5 and H2 above all, because each maps a documented chemical property of the family onto an externally validated target (MPO-localized activation; clinically validated PTP1B inhibition) while the protocols require the family to perform functions its own chemistry contradicts. The protocols retain stronger individual co-actives (rapamycin above all), but their efficacy would be unattributable to adrenochrome. Any future protocol revision requires, at minimum: a free-hydrazine assay for AMM, in-vivo hydrolysis kinetics for the conjugate, direct Complex III superoxide measurement at stated nanomolar carbazochrome doses, genotype-stratified (NQO1, G6PD, rs4880) dose ranges before human exposure is proposed — and, per the new hypotheses, the two cheapest decisive experiments available to the program: the recombinant PTP1B reversible:irreversible dose-fork (H2) and the MPO vs. spontaneous oxidation kinetics of leuco-adrenochrome (H5).
