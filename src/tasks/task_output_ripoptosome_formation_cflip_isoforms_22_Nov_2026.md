# Feoktistova et al., Molecular Cell 2011 — Results and Discussion Review

## Conceptual map of the death-switch mechanism

**Decision flow.** Assembly licenses; execution decides.

```
cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs) present
  → modified RIPK1 held by the proteasome → no assembly (survival/apoptosis)

cIAPs lost (SMAC mimetic, TLR3/TRIF, MG132, genotoxic stress)
  → modified RIPK1 accumulates → 2 MDa Ripoptosome licenses (necessary but not sufficient)
      ├─ Caspase-8 homodimers → cleave RIPK1 → disassemble → apoptosis
      ├─ cFLIPL-dominant      → cleave RIPK1 → disassemble → survival
      └─ cFLIPS-dominant      → no cleavage → RIPK1 → RIPK3 → MLKL → necrosome → necroptosis
           (requires intact RIPK3 and MLKL; otherwise fully protected)
```

## Summary

In the absence of cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs), a 2 MDa (approximately 2 megadaltons) intracellular platform termed the Ripoptosome forms spontaneously — it contains receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1, also written RIP1), Fas-associated death domain (FADD), Caspase-8, Caspase-10 and cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein (cFLIP; FLICE = FADD-like ICE, i.e. caspase-8) isoforms — and activates apoptosis or receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3, also written RIP3)-dependent necroptosis. Caspase-8 activity within the complex disassembles it by cleaving scaffold proteins; the long isoform (cFLIPL) blocks formation and restrains the activity, while the short isoform (cFLIPS) promotes assembly and suppresses the activity, so when cIAPs are absent, cFLIPS cells show necroptosis.

The six Results items are each followed by their physiological consequences (cell physiology and tissue-level outcome) and their pathological consequences (injury, inflammatory disease, cancer and developmental pathology). The Discussion is retained below as a non-itemized synthesis, and its consequences are integrated into the corresponding Results items. Evidence tiers are retained: cell-biological and biochemical findings are distinguished from later animal models, reviews and hypotheses. Acronyms are expanded at first use in each item, so every numbered section reads on its own. Basis for the consequence statements: the primary paper (Feoktistova et al., *Molecular Cell* 2011, DOI [digital object identifier] 10.1016/j.molcel.2011.06.011; Tier 3 — biochemical and cell-biological), the vault notes [[Ripoptosome]], [[Necroptosis]], [[MLKL]], [[necrosome]], [[Caspase-8-c-FLIP Rheostat]], [[Smac mimetics]], [[c-FLIP]], and later review/systematic evidence cited inline. Death-mode terminology follows the 2011 usage; the Nomenclature Committee on Cell Death 2012 (NCD-2012) restyling is noted where it changes the reading. Three abbreviations used throughout are defined once here: TIR = Toll/interleukin-1 receptor domain (as in TRIF, the TIR-domain-containing adaptor-inducing interferon-β), MDa = megadaltons (as in the 2 MDa platform), and FLICE = FADD-like ICE, i.e. caspase-8 (as in the FLICE-like inhibitory protein, cFLIP).

## Results

### 1. TLR3-induced cell death is blocked by cIAPs and proceeds in a Caspase- and RIP1-kinase-dependent manner independent from TNF signaling

Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) ligand (poly(I:C), i.e. polyinosinic-polycytidylic acid, a synthetic double-stranded RNA) induced cell death in HaCaT (spontaneously immortalized human keratinocyte line), MET-1 (mouse mammary epithelial line) and A5RT3 (HaCaT-derived metastatic clone) cells; pretreatment with an inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP) antagonist (compound A, a small-molecule Smac mimetic — second mitochondria-derived activator of caspases [SMAC] mimetic) or TWEAK (TNF-like weak inducer of apoptosis) sensitized HaCaT and MET-1 but not A5RT3. Caspase-8 and receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1) knockdown respectively reduced and eliminated TLR3-induced death in the absence of cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs); the combination of Z-VAD (Z-VAD-FMK, a benzyloxycarbonyl-Val-Ala-Asp-fluoromethylketone pan-caspase inhibitor) and Nec-1 (Necrostatin-1, a RIPK1 kinase inhibitor) fully rescued, with cell death morphology consistent with simultaneous apoptosis and necroptosis. High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) release, a Caspase-8-independent marker, was unchanged by Z-VAD, reduced by Nec-1, and fully suppressed by the Z-VAD + Nec-1 combination. Death was independent of autocrine tumor necrosis factor receptor 1 (TNFR1) signaling: TNFR1 knockdown and blocking Fas-associated death domain (FADD)-containing death receptor ligands (TNFR2-Fc, soluble tumor necrosis factor receptor 2 [TNFR2]; TRAILR2-Fc, soluble TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand receptor 2 [TRAILR2]; CD95-Fc, soluble Fas receptor [CD95]) did not alter TLR3 death. Caspase-8 immunoprecipitation showed that cIAPs depletion increased Caspase-8–RIPK1 association upon TLR3 stimulation; the complex contained FADD, the cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein (cFLIP) cleavage fragment p43, Caspase-10 p47/p43 and TIR-domain-containing (TIR = Toll/interleukin-1 receptor domain) adaptor-inducing interferon-β (TRIF), but not in TNFR1 complex II. Primary human keratinocytes (PKs) were sensitized to TLR3 death by cIAP/X-linked inhibitor of apoptosis (XIAP) depletion, independent of TNFR1.

**Physiological consequences.**

- poly(I:C) is the viral double-stranded RNA model and TLR3 is expressed in the tested epithelial lines; death proceeds on the Caspase-8 + RIPK1-kinase arm while cIAPs are present, and neither TNFR1 knockdown nor blocking of TNFR2/TRAILR2/CD95 ligands alters it — the TLR3 death complex is a platform distinct from TNFR1 complex II. Consequence: an innate double-stranded-RNA-sensing route to death that requires no death-receptor ligand, available in any TLR3-bearing cell once the cIAP brake is removed.
- HMGB1 release (a Caspase-8-independent marker) was unchanged by Z-VAD and reduced by Nec-1. Consequence: the lytic arm leaks a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) that the apoptotic arm does not, so the physiological difference between the two outcomes is a sterile-danger signal and not only a morphology.
- Primary keratinocytes are sensitized by cIAP/XIAP depletion independent of TNFR1, so the route is generic to TLR3-bearing epithelium rather than restricted to the transformed lines.

**Pathological consequences.**

- Viral double-stranded RNA sensing can injure epithelium when cIAP/XIAP restraint fails, and HMGB1 release converts the resulting epithelial death into a DAMP signal that can sustain sterile inflammation. TLR3 agonists are investigated as anticancer agents in TLR3-expressing epithelial tumors (PMCID PMC13256595; PMCID PMC7545934).
- Because this route is TNFR1-independent, TNFR1 blockade is not expected to prevent death in this configuration (mechanistic inference from the paper).
- In cholangiocarcinoma, high TLR3 or low RIPK1 status was associated with more invasiveness, and Smac mimetic attenuated poly(I:C)-induced invasion — linking the platform to both tumor progression and treatment response (PMCID PMC7545934). In malignant mesothelioma, cisplatin downregulates cFLIP, restoring caspase-8-mediated TLR3 apoptosis; patient-derived cells were sensitive to poly(I:C) alone (2020).

### 2. cIAPs block spontaneous Ripoptosome formation; RIPK1 is required

Cell death required Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) expression and TIR-domain-containing adaptor-inducing interferon-β (TRIF). Knockdown of Caspase-8 reduced TLR3 death in the absence of cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs) (rescued by Nec-1 but not Z-VAD); knockdown of receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3) produced no protection, while Z-VAD protected independent of Nec-1 — the two effectors being Caspase-8 (apoptosis) and RIPK3 (necroptosis); combined RIPK3 and Caspase-8 knockdown fully protected. Knockdown of receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1) fully protected; Ripoptosome formation (Caspase-8 immunoprecipitation) required RIPK1. IAP antagonist alone produced Caspase-8–RIPK1–Fas-associated death domain (FADD) interaction insufficient for death; additional poly(I:C) stimulation recruited TRIF into the complex.

**Physiological consequences.**

- At cIAP loss a stress platform becomes a death switch without any ligand engagement; because assembly is necessary but not sufficient (item 5), cIAP loss licenses death rather than executing it, and effector competence (RIPK3 present) decides the outcome.
- Spontaneous assembly is cell-type specific — HaCaT and MET-1 (transformed) yes, primary keratinocytes no — so in this system the cIAP-loss route operates mainly in transformed cells.
- cIAPs control a compartmentalized, "modified" RIPK1 pool targeted to proteasomal degradation (the proteasome inhibitor MG132 alone triggers assembly; MG132 plus IAP antagonist suppresses it). Consequence: the cIAP gate sits on a post-translational degradation cycle rather than on steady-state protein levels, and proteasome blockade alone is sufficient to license the platform; a lysosomal second control mechanism is suggested but speculative.

**Pathological consequences.**

- cIAP depletion or proteasome blockade can license spontaneous death without receptor ligation, and RIPK1 is the required node — the basis for investigating RIPK1 inhibition in preclinical ischemia–reperfusion injury and neurodegeneration (Nec-1 reduced infarct volume after middle cerebral artery occlusion in mice).
- Cell-type specificity means cIAP-antagonist toxicity is not uniform: transformed lines assemble the platform, whereas primary keratinocytes do not do so spontaneously; combined cIAP/X-linked inhibitor of apoptosis protein (XIAP) loss can nevertheless sensitize primary keratinocytes (item 1), so normal-tissue injury remains possible.
- Whether natural proteostasis failure triggers this route in human protein-aggregation disease is untested; MG132 provides a pharmacological model, not evidence of such a disease mechanism (Tier 5).
- The platform is a convergence node for tumor necrosis factor receptor 1 (TNFR1)-, TLR3/dsRNA-, genotoxic and proteotoxic stress, so its deregulation can produce inflammatory death across organs. Assembly alone does not establish tissue injury; execution markers such as phosphorylated RIPK3 and mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL) are more directly tied to pathological death (mechanistic inference; no validated clinical biomarker is established by the paper).
- Systemic Smac-mimetic cIAP loss also affects immune cells: stabilization of NF-κB-inducing kinase (NIK) and non-canonical nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) activation promote B-cell survival and co-stimulatory signaling in dendritic cells and T cells, while tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα) production can sensitize cells to Complex II death. High-dose Smac-mimetic treatment carries systemic-toxicity and cytokine-release-syndrome risk (PMCID PMC7226512).

### 3. cFLIP is a critical regulator of the Ripoptosome

A5RT3 cells (HaCaT-derived metastatic clone; no endogenous cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein [cFLIP]): knockdown of cFLIP sensitized to Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) death. In A5RT3, cFLIP knockdown produced spontaneous Ripoptosome formation in the absence of cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs), amplified by TLR3 stimulation, with Caspase-10 accumulating in the complex.

**Physiological consequences.**

- Without cFLIP restraint the platform assembles on its own at cIAP loss and TLR3 stimulation amplifies it, with Caspase-10 accumulating in the complex — a second, Caspase-8-parallel execution arm that kills when the Caspase-8 arm is compromised.
- cFLIP is the only constituent of the complex whose absence makes the platform assemble spontaneously, so it is the molecule that holds the platform in a survival configuration; this is the basis for treating it as a target rather than merely as a Caspase-8 inhibitor.

**Pathological consequences.**

- Loss of cFLIP restraint can permit spontaneous platform assembly; in epithelial tissues, combined loss of Fas-associated death domain (FADD)/Caspase-8/cFLIP control is associated with receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3)-dependent colitis-like pathology (vault [[Necroptosis]]). Caspase-10 accumulation suggests execution redundancy that may defeat selective Caspase-8 blockade.
- In cancer, cFLIP overexpression is a resistance mechanism to death ligands and chemotherapy; cFLIP downregulation is a sensitization strategy but can also compromise epithelial protection ([[Caspase-8-c-FLIP Rheostat]]).

### 4. cFLIP isoforms differentially regulate poly(I:C)-induced death by regulation of the Ripoptosome

In HaCaT (spontaneously immortalized human keratinocyte line; low endogenous cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein [cFLIP]): the long isoform (cFLIPL) protected from Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) death under all conditions, while cells expressing the short isoform (cFLIPS) died spontaneously upon depletion of cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs) — a receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1)-kinase-dependent, Z-VAD-insensitive death. Ripoptosome immunoprecipitation: cFLIPL suppressed RIPK1/Fas-associated death domain (FADD) association after IAP antagonist/TLR3; cFLIPS increased RIPK1 association (total RIPK1 levels slightly elevated). HeLa cells (human cervical cancer line) showed the same pattern: cFLIPS was protective from IAP antagonist, cFLIPL inhibitory. In primary keratinocytes (PKs), spontaneous Ripoptosome formation was not detectable without cIAPs function; high endogenous cFLIPL levels in PKs versus HaCaT argue that spontaneous formation is cell-type specific, occurring mainly in transformed cells in a cFLIP-isoform-dependent manner.

**Physiological consequences.**

- The isoform present, not total cFLIP, sets the quality of death. With Caspase-8 intact, the cFLIPL heterodimer retains enough activity to cleave RIPK1 and disassemble the platform — survival with limited substrate cleavage and no full Caspase-8 activation; with Caspase-8 inactive or blocked and cFLIPS present, RIPK1 persists uncleaved and RIPK1 → RIPK3 (receptor-interacting protein kinase 3) → necroptosis executes, which is why the death is Z-VAD-insensitive.
- HaCaT and HeLa show the same isoform opposition with opposite outcome polarity (cFLIPS death versus cFLIPS protection): the outcome depends on the isoform present relative to the Caspase-8 pool and on the stimulus, not on cFLIP abundance alone ([[c-FLIP]]: the cFLIPL:cFLIPS ratio, not total cFLIP, is the fate-determining parameter).
- High cFLIPL in primary keratinocytes versus low in HaCaT is the stated basis for cell-type-restricted spontaneous assembly — a brake that distinguishes normal from transformed epithelium in this system.

**Pathological consequences.**

- cFLIPS shifts cells toward lytic, damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP)-releasing necroptosis rather than quiet apoptosis; sustained cIAP loss in a cFLIPS-high tissue would therefore favor necroinflammation (mechanistic inference; direct tissue evidence is limited).
- cFLIPL protects epithelium but can confer tumor resistance. The cFLIPL:cFLIPS ratio is a candidate predictive biomarker for Smac-mimetic/TLR3 therapy; the opposite HaCaT and HeLa outcome polarity shows that total cFLIP abundance alone cannot predict pathology or treatment response.
- High cFLIPL in primary keratinocytes explains a potential therapeutic window, not complete safety, because combined cIAP/X-linked inhibitor of apoptosis protein (XIAP) depletion can still sensitize primary cells.
- RIPK3/mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL) loss makes cells necroptosis-resistant; tumors that lose these components can evade necroptotic killing and are reported to be poorly immunogenic (PMCID PMC6983729; *Annual Review of Pathology* 2017). The exact cFLIPS:cFLIPL ratio predictive of either outcome remains unresolved.

### 5. Promotion of necroptosis by cFLIPS in the absence of cIAPs is independent of TNFR1 signaling and occurs in a 2 MDa complex

HaCaT-cFLIPS cells (HaCaT line expressing the short cFLIP isoform): inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP) antagonist alone induced spontaneous Ripoptosome formation (no TIR-domain-containing adaptor-inducing interferon-β [TRIF] association). Tumor necrosis factor receptor 1 (TNFR1) knockdown, autocrine TNFR1 blockade, and Caspase-8 immunoprecipitation in TNFR1 knockdown cells all showed unchanged spontaneous Ripoptosome formation and receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1) association; TNFR1 complex I/II analysis excluded Caspase-8 recruitment through the receptor — the complex cannot be called TNFR1 complex II. Nec-1 (Necrostatin-1, a RIPK1 kinase inhibitor) or receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3) knockdown fully blocked IAP antagonist-induced death; Ripoptosome formation preceded death detection. Gel filtration (Superose 6 column): RIPK1, Fas-associated death domain (FADD) and Caspase-8 were mainly monomeric; a 2 MDa complex was detected in the column range of fractions 4–6; after cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs) degradation, Caspase-8/RIPK1/cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein (cFLIP) association appeared only in the 2 MDa fractions — cIAPs block assembly of the 2 MDa platform.

**Physiological consequences.**

- Nec-1 or RIPK3 knockdown fully blocks IAP-antagonist-induced death and RIPK3 is required: the necrosome — the RIPK1/RIPK3/mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL) execution complex — not the ripoptosome, is the death executor, and combined RIPK3 + Caspase-8 knockdown protects fully, so the platform carries two independent kill vectors ([[necrosome]]).
- Execution chain downstream of the persisting platform: uncleaved RIPK1 recruits RIPK3 through the RIP homotypic interaction motif (RHIM); RIPK3 phosphorylates MLKL; phosphorylated MLKL oligomerizes via its N-terminal four-helix bundle and translocates to phosphatidylinositol-phosphate-rich plasma-membrane domains, aided by binding of inositol hexakisphosphate (IP6), where it forms cation channels → osmotic swelling, membrane rupture, damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) release (mitochondrial DNA, ATP, high mobility group box 1 [HMGB1], nuclear contents) ([[MLKL]]; Dhuriya & Sharma 2018, *Journal of Neuroinflammation*, PMCID [PubMed Central identifier] PMC3004764). A later addition to the execution arm is lysosomal membrane permeabilization with cathepsin B release (*Cell Death & Differentiation* 2023, DOI 10.1038/s41418-023-01237-7).
- DAMP amplification closes the loop: released DAMPs are recognized by pattern-recognition receptors — toll-like receptors (TLRs), the NLRP3 inflammasome (NOD = nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain, LRR = leucine-rich repeat, pyrin domain; NLRP3 = NOD-, LRR- and pyrin-domain-containing protein 3), cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS) – stimulator of interferon genes (STING), and retinoic acid-inducible gene I (RIG-I) / mitochondrial antiviral-signaling protein (MAVS) — and the cytosolic RNA sensors RIG-I and MAVS feed interferon and tumor necrosis factor alpha (TNFα) production back into further necroptotic death. Consequence: the lytic death does not end with the dying cell; it converts a local stimulus into a self-amplifying inflammatory loop (Dhuriya & Sharma 2018; *Experimental and Molecular Medicine* 2020; *Nature Immunology* 2025).
- Platform assembly precedes death detection and the Caspase-8/RIPK1/cFLIP association appears only in the 2 MDa fractions after cIAP degradation, so formation is a licensing step and execution competence decides — which is why a 2 MDa complex can exist without killing the cell.

**Pathological consequences.**

- Necrosome execution → MLKL membrane disruption → DAMP release is implicated in cardiac, renal and pulmonary ischemia–reperfusion injury, viral lung injury, chronic inflammation and cancer (*Nature Immunology* 2025; PMCID PMC3004764).
- TNFR1 independence means anti-TNF or TNFR1-directed strategies will not fully block this route.
- RIPK3 is required, so RIPK3/MLKL loss or inhibition prevents death; preclinical Nec-1, RIPK3 and MLKL interventions are tissue-protective in several models, but species differences and context dependence limit direct human translation.
- Platform assembly preceding death creates a potential early biomarker and intervention window; execution markers such as phosphorylated MLKL are more directly tied to tissue injury than assembly alone (mechanistic inference).
- **Developmental pathology:** Casp8⁻/⁻Fadd⁻/⁻ embryos die, and Ripk3⁻/⁻, Ripk1⁻/⁻ or Mlkl⁻/⁻ rescue identifies unchecked necroptosis as the lethal event; Cflip⁻/⁻ requires combined Fadd⁻/⁻Ripk3⁻/⁻ rescue, showing that cFLIP restrains both apoptosis and necroptosis.
- **Ischemia–reperfusion:** cardiac models show improved ejection fraction with Ripk3 loss and RIPK3-dependent calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMKII) activation in cardiomyocytes; renal Mlkl⁻/⁻ kidneys show reduced inflammation at 12 h, lower creatinine and tubular injury at 24 h, with increased apoptosis, and the first 3 h after reperfusion is a candidate treatment window (*Frontiers in Immunology* 2023); lung ischemia–reperfusion shows graft-cell necroptosis driving chemokine (C-X-C motif) ligand 1 (CXCL1)-mediated neutrophil recruitment to subpleural vessels, followed by endothelial Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)/NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4) signaling and neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) that disrupt capillaries and impair graft function (*Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 2022).
- **Neurodegeneration and stroke:** RIPK1-dependent neuronal loss and Nec-1 neuroprotection are preclinical observations; necroptosis is discussed in Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s and multiple-sclerosis models, but a causal human contribution is not established here (vault [[Necroptosis]]).
- **Infection:** necroptosis can be protective through infected-cell elimination and inflammasome-dependent interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) release. Influenza A nonstructural protein 1 (NS1) promotes MLKL oligomerization → NLRP3 activation → IL-1β release, supporting virus clearance; severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2)-induced necroptosis in lung epithelial cells can instead contribute to cytokine storm and multi-organ failure (*Nature Immunology* 2025). RIPK3 can also activate the NLRP3 inflammasome directly, and the MLKL–NLRP3 axis links necroptosis to pyroptotic cytokine release.
- **Cancer:** RIPK3-mediated necroptosis can suppress acute myeloid leukemia (AML) through killing of leukemia-initiating cells, while chronic necrotic inflammation can promote hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and colorectal carcinoma (CRC); necroptosis-deficient tumors may escape immunosurveillance (*Journal of Hematology & Oncology* 2025). cFLIP overexpression confers TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL)/Fas-ligand and chemotherapy resistance across cancers, so lowering cFLIP can re-sensitize tumors to death ligands ([[Caspase-8-c-FLIP Rheostat]]).
- **Enteric and skin inflammation:** loss of epithelial FADD/Caspase-8 restraint is associated with RIPK3-dependent colitis-like pathology, and loss of SHANK-associated RH domain-interacting protein (SHARPIN) produces TNFR1-dependent proliferative dermatitis (vault [[Necroptosis]]).
- **Therapeutic window and safety:** Smac mimetics (including birinapant) entered early clinical trials and can trigger tumor-cell death directly or prime cells to death-ligand and chemotherapy agents; high-RIPK3 cells may route to necroptosis, while RIPK3 inhibition can redirect the platform toward apoptosis. Response biomarkers would need Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3), cIAP1/2, cFLIP isoform, RIPK3/MLKL and Caspase-8 status; renal necroptosis is concentrated early after reperfusion and stroke protection with Nec-1 is preclinical. No validated clinical biomarker set or established clinical efficacy for the exact Smac-mimetic/TLR3 combination follows from these sources.
- **Evidence boundary:** the disease links above are predominantly murine or cell-model findings; they support mechanism and target rationale, not established clinical efficacy in humans.

### 6. cFLIPS but not cFLIPL blocks Caspase-8 activity within the Ripoptosome

HaCaT-cFLIPS Ripoptosome (HaCaT line expressing the short isoform of cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein [cFLIP]) contained uncleaved receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1) (even with the pan-caspase inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK present); control cells contained cleaved RIPK1 (the p39 fragment), indicative of Caspase-8 activity within the complex, and Caspase-8 activity disassembles the complex by cleaving scaffold proteins. Flag-immunoprecipitation of the complex: in cFLIPS cells, RIPK1, Fas-associated death domain (FADD) and (after Toll-like receptor 3 [TLR3]) TIR-domain-containing adaptor-inducing interferon-β (TRIF) co-precipitated; in cells expressing the long cFLIP isoform (cFLIPL), less complex formation and mostly cleaved Caspase-8 — Caspase-8/cFLIPL heterodimers retaining catalytic activity at a level sufficient for selected substrate cleavage (including RIPK1) but insufficient for full Caspase-8 activation. Proteasome inhibitor MG132 (N-acetyl-leucyl-leucyl-norleucinal) alone induced spontaneous Ripoptosome formation despite cellular inhibitor of apoptosis protein (cIAP) presence; MG132 plus IAP antagonist suppressed it. Model: cIAPs prevent Ripoptosome formation by ubiquitinating a "modified" RIPK1 pool targeted to proteasomal degradation; proteasome blockade alone triggers spontaneous formation.

**Physiological consequences.**

- With cFLIPL the platform is a survival complex: Caspase-8/cFLIPL heterodimers cleave RIPK1 and disassemble it, releasing no apoptotic signal. With cFLIPS the platform persists — uncleaved RIPK1, with RIPK1/FADD/TRIF co-precipitated — and the RIPK1–RIPK3 (receptor-interacting protein kinase 3)–MLKL (mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein) axis executes lytic necroptosis.
- The physiological difference between the two isoform states is therefore the difference between a silent, immunologically tolerated death and a lytic, damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP)-releasing death with downstream inflammatory amplification.
- MG132 alone triggering assembly despite cIAP presence, and MG132 plus IAP antagonist suppressing it, place the cIAP gate on the turnover of a modified RIPK1 pool and imply a lysosomal second control mechanism (speculative).

**Pathological consequences.**

- Caspase-8/cFLIPL residual activity cleaves RIPK1 and prevents lytic death; loss of that activity raises the risk of inflammatory tissue damage even when apoptosis is already blocked.
- RIPK3 inhibition can redirect the same platform toward apoptosis: in cancer this may preserve tumor killing with less necroinflammation, whereas in ischemia–reperfusion injury it changes death modality without necessarily preventing cell loss. The therapeutic direction is therefore disease-dependent.

## Discussion

**The 2 MDa Ripoptosome as the regulatory platform.** The 2 MDa complex integrates multiple upstream stress signals converging at receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1); on depletion of cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs) it forms spontaneously and is necessary but not sufficient for cell death. The complex contains Fas-associated death domain (FADD), Caspase-8, Caspase-10 and cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein (cFLIP) isoforms; the 2011 nomenclature has since been absorbed into the tumor necrosis factor receptor 1 (TNFR1) complex II framework — current usage often describes the same components as a TNF-independent complex-II variant or as a TIR-domain-containing adaptor-inducing interferon-β (TRIF)-recruited necrosome — but the paper's model is retained: RIPK1 is the core scaffold, and the platform links Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) (via TRIF) to the death effectors. Its physiological and pathological implications are integrated into Results items 2, 5 and 6.

**Regulation by cIAPs.** cIAPs block spontaneous assembly of the 2 MDa platform; the MG132 data argue they prevent assembly by ubiquitinating and degrading "modified" RIPK1; IAP antagonists themselves trigger cIAP autoubiquitination and degradation. The MG132-suppression of the MG132-plus-IAP-antagonist complex formation points to a lysosomal pathway as a second cIAP control mechanism (speculative, based on MG132's incomplete effect). The degradation gate, systemic immune effects of Smac mimetics and the speculative proteostasis link are integrated into Results items 2 and 6.

**Caspase-8 activity as the death-quality rheostat.** Caspase-8 activity within the Ripoptosome determines the death quality: Caspase-8/cFLIPL heterodimers cleave scaffold proteins (receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 [RIPK1]) to disassemble the complex; cFLIPL–Caspase-8 heterodimers retain activity sufficient for RIPK1 cleavage but insufficient for full activation. In the absence of cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs), the short isoform of cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein (cFLIP; cFLIPS) prevents this activity, so the accumulated RIPK1 activates receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3)-dependent necroptosis — the stoichiometry of Caspase-8, cFLIPL and cFLIPS sets whether apoptosis or necroptosis occurs. Where Caspase-8 and RIPK3 are both absent, cells are fully protected. The paper explicitly notes the unresolved question of how the stoichiometric ratio of cFLIPL and cFLIPS impacts the outcome. The death-quality and redirect consequences are integrated into Results items 4, 5 and 6.

**Cell-type specificity and in vivo relevance.** Spontaneous assembly was observed mainly in transformed cells (HaCaT-cFLIPS); primary keratinocytes (PKs) express high cFLIPL and show no spontaneous assembly upon inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP) antagonist treatment. In vivo, double knockout of Caspase-8 and Fas-associated death domain (FADD) is embryonic lethal and is rescued by receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3)/receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1) loss — consistent with necroptosis routing when the complex cannot be disassembled. The developmental, ischemia–reperfusion, neurodegenerative, infectious and cancer consequences are integrated into Results items 2 and 5.

**Translational proposals.** The paper proposes combining Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) agonists with inhibitor of apoptosis protein (IAP) antagonists (SMAC mimetics, i.e. second mitochondria-derived activator of caspases mimetics). It also flags cellular FLICE-like inhibitory protein (cFLIP) as a critical regulator and a potential target. Therapeutic rationale, biomarkers and safety limits are integrated into Results items 1, 2, 4 and 5.

**Points left open by the paper.** Stoichiometry of Caspase-8/cFLIPL/cFLIPS within the complex; the specific ubiquitin chains that cellular inhibitor of apoptosis proteins (cIAPs) attach to receptor-interacting protein kinase 1 (RIPK1) and whether the ubiquitination occurs on the same "modified" pool that assembles the complex; whether spontaneous, Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3)-independent assembly occurs in primary cells (the data argue it is cell-type specific); the receptor-interacting protein kinase 3 (RIPK3)–mixed lineage kinase domain-like protein (MLKL) necrosome downstream of this platform as a drug target distinct from RIPK1 kinase inhibition; and whether the TIR-domain-containing adaptor-inducing interferon-β (TRIF)-recruited necrosome reported in current reviews corresponds to this same 2 MDa platform.

The unresolved points carry direct consequences: stoichiometry sets death quality and inflammatory burden; ubiquitin-chain identity determines the assembly-competent RIPK1 pool and therefore tissue specificity; cell-type variability determines the safety margin of cIAP antagonism in normal tissue; the RIPK3–MLKL necrosome is an execution arm targetable separately from RIPK1 (GSK872 and HS-1371 for RIPK3, necrosulfonamide for MLKL); and equivalence between the TRIF-recruited necrosome and this platform determines how directly the findings transfer to pattern-recognition-receptor-driven infectious and sterile inflammatory disease. Targeting RIPK3–MLKL separately from RIPK1 could block necroptotic inflammation while preserving Caspase-8-dependent apoptosis, but this remains a preclinical hypothesis.
