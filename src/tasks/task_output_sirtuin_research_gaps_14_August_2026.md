## Mechanism & Validation of Sirtuin Modulators (STACs / inhibitors)

- **STAC activation mechanism remains controversial.** SIRT1 activation by resveratrol and the SRT compounds (SRT1720/1460/2183) could often only be demonstrated with fluorophore-tagged substrates; resveratrol's *in vivo* effects may be indirect (via the cAMP–Epac1–AMPK pathway) rather than direct SIRT1 binding. The genuine on-target contribution of SIRT1 activation vs. off-target effects is unresolved (Rejuvenating Sirtuins; SRT1460, Sirtuin Fluorimetric Activity notes).
- **Suramin's anticancer activity is of unknown provenance.** It is unclear whether suramin's anticancer effects stem from sirtuin inhibition or from other modes of action (adenosine receptor antagonism, ceramide accumulation). Its anionic, high-MW nature limits therapeutic application (Rejuvenating Sirtuins).
- **Lack of disease-model standardization.** Intense efforts to develop isoform-specific modulators suffer from contradictory reports and limited cross-validation due to non-standardized models; hurdles of side effects, stability, and selectivity remain before any molecule reaches the clinic (Sirtuins and Biological Relevance — future perspectives).
- **Need for selective, potent modulators + delivery systems.** Many novel SIRT1/other modulators remain preclinical due to specificity, bioavailability, safety, and translation challenges. Priorities: isoform-selective modulators, improved delivery, combination therapies, and genomics/biomarker-guided personalization (SIRT1 nexus; SRT2104).

## Isoform-Specific Functional Gaps

- **SIRT3 longevity untested in vivo.** No SIRT3-overexpressing transgenic mouse exists to directly test lifespan extension — a critical gap. Human SNP associations with longevity are inconsistent across cohorts (SIRT3.md).
- **SIRT4 mechanistic void.** The mechanism by which SIRT4 negatively modulates insulin secretion, fatty-acid oxidation, and mitochondrial gene expression "remains elusive and in vivo data are actually absent." Few studies on SIRT4 in apoptosis/autophagy; its role in cardiac fibrosis and metabolic inflexibility needs exploration (Sirtuins and Biological Relevance; sirtuins in health and disease).
- **SIRT5 understudied in disease.** The biological significance of SIRT5's positive correlation with insulin sensitivity "still remains to be confirmed"; SIRT5-specific mechanisms in cardiac fibrosis, CAD, hypertension, and atherogenesis need further study (Sirtuins and Biological Relevance; sirtuins in health and disease).
- **SIRT6/SIRT7 in autophagy & cardio.** SIRT6's mechanism of promoting autophagy "needs further study"; SIRT7's role in autophagy and in cardiac hypertrophy/fibrosis is inconsistent and under-investigated (sirtuins in health and disease).
- **SIRT2 context-dependence.** Opposing roles of SIRT2 in cardiac contractility, apoptosis, proliferation, and fibrosis across cell types need resolution; precise role in cardiac contractile function under physiological/pathological conditions is undefined (Sirtuins and Biological Relevance).
- **Whole-family imbalance.** SIRT2 and SIRT7 are far less studied in metabolism than SIRT1/3/6; research should focus on these under-explored isoforms across metabolism subtypes (sirtuins in health and disease).

## Longevity & Human Translation

- **Human longevity benefit of SIRT1 remains controversial.** Overexpression extends lifespan in lower organisms and improves mouse health, but the longevity benefit in humans is unresolved; SIRT1 *deficiency* is consistently linked to age-related disease. The interplay between SIRT1 expression and rate of aging "remains outstanding" (SIRT1 nexus; Sirtuins and Biological Relevance).
- **Tissue-/cell-/gene-specific context.** We are "just starting to appreciate" the tissue-, cell-type-, and gene-specific processes regulated by different sirtuins; identifying these is necessary to fill current knowledge gaps in aging (Sirtuins and Biological Relevance).
- **SIRT3 human-aging link unconfirmed in some populations.** Some (not all) studies link SIRT3 locus polymorphisms to elderly survival; newer studies failed to confirm in other populations (Sirtuins and Biological Relevance).

## Network, NAD+ Compartmentalization & Translational Physiology

- **NAD+ spatial/temporal regulation unclear.** The compartmentalized impact of NAD+ metabolism on sirtuin activity, and its spatial/temporal regulation, "are not yet fully understood." Most mechanistic insights derive from preclinical systems with limited direct human translation (Sirtuin Network).
- **Network-based therapeutic targeting is complicated** by context-dependent and sometimes opposing isoform roles. Future work should define sirtuin-network dynamics in human metabolic disease, identify isoform-specific tissue functions, and develop strategies to selectively modulate activity or restore NAD+ homeostasis (Sirtuin Network).
- **No clinical assay for SIRT3/SIRT4 ratio.** No validated assay exists to measure the SIRT3/SIRT4 ratio in patients; surrogate markers (MnSOD acetylation, MitoSOX, 8-OHdG) lack standardization, and non-invasive proxies (cf-mtDNA, acetyl-carnitine) await validation (SIRT3-SIRT4 Ratio).

## Disease-Specific Open Questions (from sirtuins in health and disease, 2026-07-09)

- **Inflammation.** Whether SIRT depletion is a common pathological change in inflammation-related diseases, and resolution of conflicting SIRT7/p65 translocation data, need further study. Role of SIRTs in inflammatory responses remains incompletely defined.
- **Apoptosis.** Whether novel histone modifications (sumoylation, ubiquitination) participate in SIRT-regulated apoptosis "remains largely unknown." SIRT3's pro- vs anti-apoptotic role per disease type, and SIRT4/SIRT5/SIRT6/SIRT7 involvement, are under-studied.
- **Autophagy/mitophagy.** Mechanisms of mtSIRTs (SIRT3–5) on autophagy "still unclear"; SIRT6 autophagy promotion and SIRT7 autophagy need further investigation. Exact SIRT–autophagy co-regulation across subcellular localizations is a new field to explore.
- **Cancer duality.** Figuring out the mechanisms/conditions enabling opposing tumor-suppressor vs tumor-promoter roles of SIRTs is "one of the main challenges" of therapeutic significance; exact BC (and other cancer) molecular mechanisms "still uncertain."
- **Cardiovascular.** Limited studies on SIRT2/4/5/7 in heart failure, MI/reperfusion, hypertension, CAD, and atherogenesis; relevant molecular mechanisms "need to be further explored," especially for under-investigated isoforms.
- **Metabolism & oxidative stress.** Many more SIRT-regulated redox/oxidative-stress pathways "need to be verified"; roles of other SIRTs in regulating metabolism subtypes should be increased.

## Neurovascular & Delivery

- **Neurovascular SIRT1 incompletely understood.** Role in neurodegenerative/neurovascular disease warrants further investigation; deeper mechanistic understanding within the neurovascular unit is critical for defining therapeutic windows and context-dependent modulation (SIRT1 nexus).
- **SIRT7-specific pharmacology nascent.** The SIRT7–GATA4 axis is a candidate therapeutic target, but SIRT7-specific pharmacology remains in its infancy; precise delivery of sirtuin modulators (e.g., to limit maladaptive cardiac remodeling) "remains challenging" (GATA4; Histone H2A.Z).

## Key Excerpts — Sirtuin Modulator Chemistry & Pharmacology

Direct excerpts from sirtuins-topic documents describing specific small-molecule modulator compounds, their potency/selectivity, and mechanism. These support and illustrate the "Mechanism & Validation" and "Isoform-Selective Pharmacophore" themes above.

### Sirtuins and their Biological Relevance in Aging and Age-Related Diseases (2026-08-12)

> Compound 6 was found to be a highly potent inhibitor of the deacetylation reaction catalysed by sirt1. Compound 6 may have much less inhibitory effect on reactions catalysed by sirt2 and sirt3 compared with sirt1. SirReal 2 and Compound 7 were screened from an internal compound library, and were demonstrated to be potent sirt2 inhibitors. Compound 8 is another potent and selective sirt2 inhibitor. X-ray analysis showed that when this compound binds to the active domain of sirt2, it may form a rigid cyclic structure by intramolecular hydrogen bonding. Therefore, it may tightly bind with sirt2, highly potently and selectively inhibiting the activity of sirt2. In addition, AK-7 is a brain-permeable selective sirt2 inhibitor, neuroprotective in vitro by reducing polyglutamine inclusions and cholesterol levels in neurons. Tenovin-6, salermide and benzodeazaoxaflavin are small-molecule inhibitors of sirt1 and sirt2. Suramin inhibits sirt5 by binding into the B- and C-pockets of the NAD+ binding site as well as the substrate-binding site.

### sirtuins in health and disease (s41392-022-01257-8, 2026-07-09)

> SRT compounds, such as SRT1460 (EC1.5 = 2.9 μM), SRT1720 (EC1.5 = 0.16 μM), SRT2104 (EC1.5 = 0.43 μM), SRT2183 (EC1.5 = 0.36 μM), and SRT3025 (EC1.5 < 1 μM), were identified in 2007 as selective SIRT1 activators, which were more potent than resveratrol.

> UBCS039 is a pyrrolo[1,2-a]quinoxaline reported as the first synthetic activator of SIRT6 deacetylase activity (EC50 = 38 μM), inducing time-dependent activation of autophagy and deacetylation of SIRT6-targeted histones in several human tumor cell lines. The bis-benzenesulfonamide-based prodrug MDL-800 is a potent and selective SIRT6 activator (EC50 = 10.3 μM), decreasing both H3K9ac and H3K56ac and suppressing TGF-β1-induced myofibroblast activation and ECM production via SIRT6-dependent β-catenin acetylation and the TGF-β1/Smad pathway.

> Nicotinamide is the endogenous inhibitor of SIRTs, formed from NAD+ during catalysis. It inhibits SIRT1 and SIRT2 with IC50 values of approximately 120 μM and 100 μM, respectively. AK-7, a benzamide (nicotinamide mimic)-containing compound, shows selective SIRT2 inhibition, improving behavioral and neuropathological phenotypes and prolonging survival in R6/2 HD mice, and limiting disease in autoimmune encephalomyelitis and lupus-prone MRL/lpr mice.

> SirReal2 is a potent SIRT2 inhibitor (IC50 = 0.14 μM) with minimal effects on SIRT1 and SIRT3. According to X-ray crystallography, SirReal2 exerts its potency and selectivity based on a ligand-induced structural rearrangement of the SIRT2 active site and interacts with residues in an unknown binding pocket located near the zinc-binding domain, known as the "selective pocket".

> Tenovin-1 and its more water-soluble analog tenovin-6 were discovered via phenotypic screening of 30,000 drug-like small molecules able to activate p53. Tenovin-6 IC50 values: SIRT1 = 21 μM; SIRT2 = 10 μM; SIRT3 = 67 μM. Both compounds decrease tumor growth in vitro at one-digit micromolar concentrations and delay tumor growth in vivo without significant general toxicity.

> EX-527 (Selisistat) is the first known selective (over SIRT2/3) and cell-permeable SIRT1 inhibitor (IC50 = 0.098 μM), decreasing tumor growth in endometrial and lung cancer xenografted mice and reducing cisplatin resistance in HEC1B cells with mutated p53. AC-93253 preferentially inhibits SIRT2 (IC50 = 6 μM). Inauhzin inhibits SIRT1 (IC50 = 0.72 μM), inducing ribosomal stress and activating p53 by dually targeting SIRT1 and inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase 2.

> Suramin, a polyanionic urea derivative originally used as an adenosine receptor antagonist, is a potent SIRT inhibitor with IC50 of 297 nM, 1150 nM, and 22 μM for SIRT1, SIRT2, and SIRT5, respectively. Aristoforin inhibits SIRT1 (IC50 = 7 μM) and SIRT2 (IC50 = 21 μM). AGK2 is a selective SIRT2 inhibitor (IC50 = 3.5 μM) identified from a concentrated compound library.

> Currently, inhibitors of SIRT1/SIRT2 are relatively adequate, whereas there are no good inhibitors of SIRT3–7 to date, especially SIRT4 and SIRT7. With regard to SIRT activators, a great deal of work has been conducted on molecules targeting SIRT1. Further studies are needed to investigate activators and inhibitors of other SIRTs.

### Rejuvenating Sirtuins — The Rise of a New Family of Cancer Drug Targets (2026-08-13)

> Suramin inhibits SIRT1/SIRT2 at nanomolar concentrations and appears to be selective for SIRT1, but it is still unclear to which extent its anticancer activity is due to sirtuin inhibition or, instead, to other modes of action, such as adenosine receptor activation or ceramide accumulation. The optimization of the suramin scaffold led to NF675, the most potent and selective molecule in this series (20-fold selectivity for SIRT1 over SIRT2), yet its modest drug-likeness — high molecular weight and anionic nature — limits therapeutic application.

> EX-527, an indole compound discovered by large high-throughput screening, inhibits SIRT1 at nanomolar concentrations with remarkable selectivity over SIRT2 and SIRT3, as well as good pharmacokinetic properties, but its anticancer activity as a single agent appears weak due to its selectivity for SIRT1 and poor activity on other sirtuins.

> Nicotinamide is a micromolar non-competitive inhibitor of SIRT1 and SIRT2 that interacts with pocket C of the binding site; SIRT1 inhibition with nicotinamide has anticancer activity in B-cell chronic lymphocytic leukemia and prostate cancer. However, nicotinamide and NAD+ analogs have limited therapeutic potential as demonstrated by unsuccessful searches for more potent inhibitors of this class.

### sirtuins — Shedding light on structure, function and regulation of human sirtuins (comprehensive review)

> Yeast Sir2 and human SIRT2 are inhibited by sirtinol. Suramin is a potent inhibitor of many sirtuins, like SIRT1, SIRT2 and SIRT5. Cambinol has the most effective efficacy against Burkitt lymphoma cell lines. Salermide causes apoptosis in cancer cells without relying on p53, and both Cambinol and Salermide inhibit SIRT1 and SIRT2. Unlike activators (of which only SIRT1 activators have been produced), high-throughput and in silico screenings have identified sirtuin inhibitors for SIRT1, SIRT2, SIRT3 and SIRT5.

## Prioritized Themes for New Entity Notes / Investigation

- Direct vs. indirect STAC mechanism (fluorophore-artifact resolution, cAMP–Epac1–AMPK axis).
- Human translation gap: SIRT1/SIRT3 longevity and NAD+ compartmentalization.
- Under-studied isoforms: SIRT2, SIRT4, SIRT5, SIRT7 across autophagy, apoptosis, CVD, metabolism.
- Validated clinical biomarkers/assays (SIRT3/SIRT4 ratio; isoform-selective activity readouts).
- Isoform-selective modulator chemistry overcoming selectivity/bioavailability/off-target limits.
- Contradictory SIRT roles in inflammation (esp. SIRT7) and cancer duality resolution.
