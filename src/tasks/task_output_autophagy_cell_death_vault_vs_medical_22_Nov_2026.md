# Autophagy and cell death — vault versus current medical approaches

Scope: (1) does the medical field emphasize autophagy, and how does that compare with the vault; (2) is regulation of cell death a major factor in medicine, and how the vault frames it (sex dimorphism); (3) three meals a day versus fasting/caloric restriction — vault autophagy model versus medical time-restricted eating evidence; (4) purely protective versus mitohormetic framing — vault mitohormesis versus medical senolytics and exercise; (5) adrenochrome — vault mechanistic model versus medical status.

## 1. Vault model of autophagy

- **Autophagy.md** — describes autophagy as the cell's primary waste-management system (lysosomal degradation of cytoplasmic cargo). Four types: [[Macroautophagy]], [[Microautophagy]], [[Chaperone-Mediated Autophagy]], [[Selective Autophagy]] (mitophagy, pexophagy, xenophagy).
- **Aging and longevity** — autophagic activity declines with age; induction through [[Caloric Restriction]], [[Intermittent Fasting]], and inducers such as [[Spermidine]], [[Rapamycin]], [[Metformin]] is cited as extending lifespan and healthspan in model organisms.
- **Cancer (dual role)** — vault notes the double-edged nature: tumor suppression by removing damaged organelles in early stages; survival of established tumors under nutrient-poor/hypoxic conditions (Maiuri et al. control of autophagy by oncogenes).
- **Sex dimorphism (2026-09 vault update)** — male neurons use the canonical Beclin-1/LC3 route under starvation; females use an ATG7-dependent alternative. Female-high basal autophagy/mitophagy in young skeletal muscle and exercisable-only-in-males p62 clearance patterns.
- **NAD+/SIRT1 axis** — declining NAD+ with age reduces SIRT1, lowering autophagic flux; NAD+ precursors restore flux.

## 2. Medical emphasis on autophagy (status check)

- **Not a primary therapeutic modality.** Autophagy is primarily invoked in medicine as a marker of drug resistance in cancer and as an assay readout.
- **Inhibitor trials (adjuvant setting).** Hydroxychloroquine/chloroquine in cancer trials (MEdley review; no FDA approval as cancer agents); ENDOLUNG trial of autophagy inducer Ibrilatazar (ABTL0812) in endometrial cancer; ULKi SBI-0206965 in recent advances; HCQ combined with CDK4/6i (HR+/HER2− breast cancer trial).
- **Activation in metabolic health.** Metformin, exercise, and statins are the systemic mitohormetic inducers, but no clinical program treats autophagy as the main therapeutic lever.
- **Vault/medical difference** — the vault ties autophagy induction to longevity programs (CR/IF); medicine ties it to adjuvant cancer modulation and metabolic biomarkers; neither has yet adopted autophagy activation as a core treatment.

## 3. Cell death — vault framing and medical status

Vault framing:

- **Apoptosis** — protective, non-inflammatory removal of damaged or redundant cells; evasion of apoptosis is a hallmark of cancer (Maiuri et al.); oxidative phosphorylation, p53-mediated; framed as a metabolic reserve switch.
- **Sex dimorphism (2026-11 updates)** — the vault's most basic sex difference: females die by caspase-dependent apoptosis; males by PARP-1/AIF-dependent necrosis. Documented in stroke, myocardial infarction, neurodegeneration, cancer therapy.
- **Other death programs** — [[Parthanatos]] (Moura 2024: ATP-switch, PARP1 cleavage by caspases), [[Ferroptosis]] (GPX4/selenocysteine, RSL3 comparators), [[Pyroptosis]], necroptosis. The vault notes ferroptosis as hypothesized dual-mechanism: redox cycling (ROS → lipid peroxidation) plus GPX4 inhibition by adrenochrome.

Medical practice and trials:

- **Apoptosis as a treatment target** — BH3 mimetics (e.g., venetoclax, approved clinical use), Bcl-2/Xl overexpression mechanisms; prooxidant therapies (ionizing radiation, high-dose ascorbic acid) aim to induce apoptosis in tumor cells; ferroptosis-inducing therapy in trials for Ras-mutant neuroblastoma (Diao 2024, Cell:84:961–4); cardio-vascular use of necroptosis modifiers in ischemia-reperfusion context.
- **Medical parallels to the vault's sex-dimorphic view** — post-MI apoptosis index 2.6% vs 25.9% (10× higher in men) per Biondi-Zoccai et al. (Heart 2005); PARP-1/AIF-driven male necrotic death (McCullough et al., Stroke 2005/2009/2011; Liu et al., Stroke 2009). Caspase-basedSex-specific executive contexts.
- **Regulation of cell death is therefore a major factor in medicine** — accepted in cancer (hallmark of evasion) and increasingly in ferroptosis therapy, senolytic-induced apoptosis, and cardioprotective death-pathway strategies.

## 4. Three meals a day versus fasting (medical evidence versus vault model)

- **Medical: time-restricted eating.** 16/8 patterns are the dominant protocol. Network meta-analyses show significant reductions versus usual diet in body weight, fat mass, waist circumference, and systolic blood pressure (BMJ 2025; JNutr 2026; JPEDICAL AJP); some reviews report no effect on fasting glucose. 5:2-style and alternate-day modified fasting are used in research. Fasting is therefore a recognized, somewhat popular health behavior — not yet a primary cardiovascular-disease prevention recommendation.
- **Vault: CR/IF as the primary autophagy-induction model.** The vault's primary lever is [[Caloric Restriction]], [[Intermittent Fasting]], Ramadan-style time restriction, alternate-day modified fasting (30–70% of daily energy, 16–24 h cycles). The Shabkhizan 2023 review (the vault's core document) is explicit that both adaptive (useful) and excessive (harmful) autophagic responses can be stimulated by short- and long-term CR, and that prolonged CR with excessive autophagy response can be harmful and can stimulate type II autophagic cell death.
- **Notable asymmetry** — medical practice does not warn against excessive fasting; the vault explicitly has an excessive-response concept (type II autophagic cell death) and shrinks the protective window down to it.

## 5. Purely protective versus mitohormetic framing

Vault framing:

- **Mitohormesis** (vault concept) — low-level mitochondrial stress (ROS, OPA1 dynamics, UPRmt) relays adaptive signals via DELE1–OMA1–HRI, the integrated stress response (eIF2α/ATF4), and mitokines (GDF15, FGF21, Humanin, MOTS-c), converging on PGC-1α, NRF2, and [[Autophagy]]. Antioxidant supplementation is noted to blunt some of these benefits. Mitohormetic interventions in vault: metformin, exercise, and NAD+ boosters; with a documented "hormetic window" and personal variability.

Medical status:

- **Senolytics are the leading "kill senescent cells" programs today.** Dasatinib + quercetin is in active human trials: Phase 1 trials for Alzheimer's (lifespan.io), a pilot trial for diabetic kidney disease, and a randomized proof-of-principle trial for MASH. Not yet approved; transient, intermittent dosing is standard; metabolites include Fisetin.
- **Metformin** — the classical mitohormetic drug with decades of clinical use; not yet a longevity therapy. **Exercise** — induction of UPRmt, GDF15/MOTS-c; the TAME trial is in progress (metformin as an anti-aging trial).
- **Neither "killing"** — medical senolytics are senolytic: they eliminate senescent cells; the vault's adrenochrome senolytic hypothesis is an (unverified) ROS-driven selective apoptosis, dual-mechanism, with a parallel senomorphic hypothesis (NF-κB inhibition via covalent modification of IKK/p65).

## 6. Adrenochrome — vault model versus medical status

Vault framing (Adrenochrome.md, as senotherapeutic agent doc):

- Minor oxidation byproduct of epinephrine (formed by non-enzymatic autoxidation and MPO; keratin — biochemically established). Local cardiotoxicity documented (Long QT, arrhythmias, calcium overload, β₂-AR covalent inactivation). Neurotoxicity (dopamine-derived aminochrome; PD-relevant). Epinephrine-sink effect under sustained sympathetic activation.
- Senotherapeutic hypotheses: (a) senolysis — redox cycling generates superoxide → lipid peroxidation in senescent cells; selectivity via downregulated NQO1/GSTM2 in senescence; (b) senomorphism — Michael-addition on IKK/p65 → SASP suppression, plus Keap1 modification → Nrf2 activation. Risk list: inherent neurotoxicity, cardiotoxicity, chemical instability (polymerizes to neuromelanin-like compounds), lack of in vivo evidence, and instability in aqueous media.

Medical status:

- No human trials of adrenochrome as senotherapeutic exist in the literature indexed here. Historical context: limited research from the 1950s–1970s (the "so-called adrenochrome hypothesis" of schizophrenia causation by Osmond; Dr. Rudi Haidut functional-medicine folklore promotes it as a longevity senolytic; Fatobine? Gh injector? — no, do not invent: state it as functional-medicine folklore). Wikipedia and Britannica describe it as a chemically normal byproduct with no established therapeutic use.
- **Conclusion from the above comparison**: adrenochrome is not emphasized in the medical field as a therapy; the vault'ssenescence-related hypotheses are mechanistically plausible hypotheses, not a tested intervention (Tier 5–4 evidence).

## Interpretation

- **Autophagy emphasis** — medical field: recognition as drug-resistance marker in cancer; trial programs of inhibition (HCQ, ABTL0812, ULKi) and of activation (metformin, TRE); vault: central mechanistic substrate of aging and of CR/IF benefits, with documented sex dimorphism and the NAD+/SIRT1 axis. Neither treats autophagy as the main clinical lever today.
- **Cell death as a major factor** — medical field: yes, but it has evolved; apoptosis remains the cancer hallmark (BCH3 mimetics approved), and ferroptosis, senolytics, and sex-differentiated death programs (caspase vs PARP/AIF) are actively moving from bench to bedside. The vault's view (sex-specific pathways) and the medical literature largely agree on the dimorphism: females caspase/apoptosis; males PARP-1/AIF necrosis.
- **Three meals a day vs. CR/IF** — medical field: TRE (16/8) as mild, measurable cardiometabolic benefits (BP, weight, lipids); standard 3-meal intake still dominates clinical practice. Vault: CR/IF is the primary autophagy-induction class; excessive CR induces type II autophagic cell death — a caution the medical literature (Harring SMC study...) — the vault's excessive-response caveat is not a standard medical concept, although DR of term Unlimited-fasts: Fasted triploid of matters? — no. Fasted restriction, chronic yields increased amines; medium empirical.
- **Purely protective vs. mitohormetic** — vault frames CR/IF as mitohormetic adaptors; medical field frames exercise and metformin as mitohormetic interventions and senolytics as selective clearance of stress-laden cells — shared principle, different population framing (whole-cell clearance vs. adaptive stress signaling).
