# Polyol Pathway

## Overview

The polyol pathway is a two-enzyme metabolic route in which glucose is converted to sorbitol by [[Aldose reductase]] and then to [[Fructose]] by sorbitol dehydrogenase. Under normoglycemia it handles a small fraction of glucose, but under hyperglycemia up to a third of cellular glucose may be routed through it in susceptible tissues.

## Mechanism & Consequences

The pathway consumes NADPH (in the aldose reductase step) and produces fructose, which is more reactive than glucose and feeds additional glycation reactions. Because sorbitol is poorly membrane-permeable, its accumulation causes osmotic stress in the lens, nerve, and kidney. The combined effects of NADPH depletion (compromising [[Glutathione]] regeneration) and enhanced [[Fructose]] production promote oxidative stress and [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] accumulation.

> [!warning]
> The polyol pathway represents a non-Maillard route through which elevated glucose contributes to protein glycation and the tissue damage seen in diabetic complications — it becomes especially active under hyperglycemia.

## Clinical Relevance

This pathway links poorly controlled diabetes with accelerated AGE accumulation and diabetic complications including neuropathy, retinopathy, nephropathy, and cataract. Aldose reductase inhibitors and [[Aminoguanidine]]'s polypharmacology target this route.
