# Febuxostat

## Pharmacodynamics

Febuxostat is a non-purine, selective inhibitor of [[Xanthine Oxidase]]. Unlike [[Allopurinol]], which only inhibits the reduced form of the enzyme, febuxostat blocks both the oxidized and reduced forms, achieving more complete suppression of urate production. It is a reversible, mixed-type inhibitor that binds in the molybdenum-pterin channel of the enzyme.

> [!important]
> Febuxostat carries cardiovascular black-box warnings in some populations, and like allopurinol it does not address pre-existing urate crystal deposits immediately.

## Pharmacokinetics

Febuxostat is primarily metabolized by the liver (glucuronidation and oxidation), which permits its use in some renally impaired patients. This hepatic route distinguishes it from allopurinol and its renally cleared metabolite oxypurinol.

## Applications

Febuxostat is used for the management of hyperuricemia and [[Gout]]. By inhibiting [[Xanthine Oxidase]], it lowers [[Uric Acid]] production and concurrently reduces [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] generated by the enzyme, potentially conferring antioxidant benefits beyond urate lowering. However, cardiovascular safety concerns have limited broad adoption relative to allopurinol.
