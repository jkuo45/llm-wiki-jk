# Eosinophil Peroxidase

## Overview

Eosinophil peroxidase (EPO) is a cationic heme-containing peroxidase stored in the specific granules of eosinophils and released during degranulation. It is the eosinophil counterpart of [[Myeloperoxidase]] in neutrophils, using [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] as co-substrate to oxidize halides and pseudohalides into reactive oxidants.

## Mechanism

Using bromide (Br−) and H2O2, EPO generates the brominating oxidant [[Hypobromous Acid]] (HOBr). This reaction is the eosinophil analog of neutrophil-derived [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl) production. EPO therefore mediates the brominating branch of the phagocyte oxidant arsenal during eosinophilic inflammation, such as allergic asthma and helminth infections.

> [!info]
> HOBr is less studied than HOCl, and bromination biomarkers are less established, but HOBr is similarly reactive toward proteins. Bromotyrosine adducts are elevated in allergic airway disease and serve as specific markers of eosinophil-mediated oxidant damage.

## Physiological & Pathological Role

EPO contributes to host defense against parasites and modulates inflammation. However, its oxidant products can also damage host tissue: HOBr chlorinates/brominates protein tyrosine residues, generating stable adducts used as biomarkers of eosinophil activation. Uncontrolled eosinophil peroxidase activity is implicated in allergic airway disease, asthma exacerbations, and tissue remodeling.
