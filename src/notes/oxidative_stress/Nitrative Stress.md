# Nitrative Stress
Nitrative stress is a condition in which the production of [[Reactive Nitrogen Species]] exceeds the body's ability to neutralize them, leading to damage to proteins, lipids, and DNA. A key mediator is [[Peroxynitrite]], which is formed from the reaction of [[Nitric Oxide]] and superoxide.

## Peroxynitrite Chemistry: Formation and Decomposition

Peroxynitrite (ONOO−) is formed by the near-diffusion-limited reaction between [[Nitric Oxide]] (•NO) and [[Superoxide Radicals]] (O2•−), with a rate constant of 6.7 × 10^9 M^−1·s^−1. This rate is approximately three times faster than the dismutation of superoxide by [[Superoxide Dismutase|SOD]] (~2 × 10^9 M^−1·s^−1), meaning that •NO effectively outcompetes SOD for superoxide wherever both species coexist. Peroxynitrite is a strong oxidant (E°′ ~1.4 V at pH 7.4) relative to other biological species. At physiological pH, peroxynitrite rapidly protonates to peroxynitrous acid (ONOOH, pKa ~6.8), which undergoes homolytic cleavage to form [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] (•OH) and nitrogen dioxide (•NO2) — two highly reactive secondary radicals that propagate oxidative damage independently. Alternatively, peroxynitrite reacts with CO2 (present at ~1.3 mM in biological fluids) to form the nitrosoperoxycarbonate anion (ONOOC(O)O−), which decomposes to •NO2 and carbonate radical (CO3•−), the latter being a potent one-electron oxidant (E°′ ~1.6 V). This CO2-dependent pathway is the dominant route of peroxynitrite decomposition in vivo (~90% of flux), redirecting nitrative chemistry toward carbon-centered radicals.

## Protein Tyrosine Nitration

The most characteristic and biomedically relevant modification of nitrative stress is 3-nitrotyrosine (3-NT) formation. This occurs when peroxynitrite-derived radicals (primarily •NO2 and CO3•−) abstract an electron from the tyrosine phenolate group, producing a tyrosyl radical that couples with •NO2 to form 3-nitrotyrosine. The reaction requires transition metal catalysis (iron or copper) or the presence of CO3•−, explaining why 3-NT formation is spatially restricted to sites of inflammation and metal deposition. 3-NT is a stable post-translational modification that cannot be repaired by cellular enzymes; functional consequences depend on the specific tyrosine residue modified. In [[Superoxide Dismutase|MnSOD]], nitration of Tyr-34 completely inactivates the enzyme, exacerbating mitochondrial oxidative stress. In [[Cytochrome c]], nitration of Tyr-74 inhibits its electron transfer function and promotes [[Apoptosis|apoptosome]] formation. In [[actin]], nitration of Tyr-91 and Tyr-198 disrupts filament polymerization, contributing to cytoskeletal collapse in inflamed cells. [[Protein Tyrosine Phosphatases]] (PTPs) contain essential catalytic cysteine residues that are also susceptible to peroxynitrite-mediated oxidation and nitration, leading to sustained tyrosine kinase signaling. Elevated 3-NT levels are documented in over 50 human pathologies and serve as both a biomarker and mediator of tissue injury in [[Atherosclerosis]], [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]], [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury|ischemia-reperfusion injury]], and [[Sepsis]].

## Lipid and DNA Nitration

Nitrative stress modifies biological [[Lipids|lipids]] through nitration of unsaturated fatty acids, yielding nitrated lipids such as nitrolinoleic acid and nitro-oleic acid. Unlike the destructive nature of protein nitration, nitrated lipids can act as endogenous [[Signaling Molecules]] that activate [[PPARγ|peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma]] (PPARγ) and inhibit [[NF-κB]] signaling, representing a potential negative feedback loop in inflammation. Nitrated fatty acids also undergo [[Michael addition]] reactions with cysteine thiols in proteins, including the Keap1 sensor, leading to [[NRF2|Nrf2]] activation and induction of [[HO-1|heme oxygenase-1]], [[NQO1]], and [[Glutathione]] synthesis.

DNA nitration primarily involves the formation of 8-nitroguanine (8-NO2-G) from the reaction of [[Reactive Nitrogen Species|RNS]] with guanine residues. Unlike 8-oxo-2′-deoxyguanosine (8-oxo-dG, the signature DNA lesion of [[Oxidative Stress]]), 8-NO2-G is chemically unstable: it spontaneously depurinates to form an [[Abasic Site|apurinic/apyrimidinic (AP) site]], causing mutations and strand breaks. 8-NO2-G also adducts to lysine residues in histones, generating DNA-protein crosslinks that disrupt [[Chromatin|chromatin]] structure. Helicobacter pylori-infected gastric epithelium shows massive 8-NO2-G accumulation, linking nitrative stress to [[Gastric Cancer|gastric carcinogenesis]]. Similarly, chronic [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] conditions such as [[Hepatitis C Virus|hepatitis C]], [[Ulcerative Colitis]], and [[Barrett's Esophagus]] are associated with elevated 8-NO2-G and increased cancer risk through this genotoxic pathway.

## Mitochondrial Nitrative Stress

[[Mitochondria]] are both sources and targets of nitrative stress. [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|Mitochondrial complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase)]] and complex III (cytochrome bc1 complex) are major sites of [[Superoxide Radicals|superoxide]] production, particularly under conditions of high membrane potential or electron transport chain impairment. [[Nitric Oxide]] produced by [[Mitochondrial NOS|mitochondrial nitric oxide synthase (mtNOS)]] reacts with this superoxide to form [[Peroxynitrite]] within the mitochondrial matrix. Peroxynitrite inactivates [[Superoxide Dismutase|MnSOD]] (by Tyr-34 nitration) and [[Aconitase]] (by disrupting its [4Fe-4S] cluster), impairing the TCA cycle and antioxidant defenses simultaneously. Complex I is inactivated by peroxynitrite-mediated nitration of specific tyrosine residues in the ND1, ND3, and ND5 subunits, and this damage is a hallmark of [[Parkinson's Disease]] in the [[Substantia Nigra]]. The [[Permeability Transition Pore|mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP)]] opens in response to peroxynitrite-induced protein nitration and lipid peroxidation, triggering [[Cytochrome c|cytochrome c release]] and [[Apoptosis|apoptotic cell death]]. This mitochondrial nitrative stress cascade is now recognized as a central mechanism in [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], where the abrupt reintroduction of oxygen generates a burst of both [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] and [[Reactive Nitrogen Species|RNS]] that overwhelms matrix defenses.

## Therapeutic Strategies

Interventions against nitrative stress target peroxynitrite formation, scavenging, or downstream repair. Small-molecule peroxynitrite decomposition catalysts, such as [[Fe(III)-porphyrin compounds|metalloporphyrins]] (e.g., FeTMPyP, MnTE-2-PyP), accelerate ONOO− isomerization to nitrate, acting as catalytic scavengers. [[Uric Acid|Uric acid]] is a physiological peroxynitrite scavenger (responsible for >50% of plasma ONOO−-scavenging capacity in humans), which may partially explain the evolutionary maintenance of [[Uric Acid|uricase]] inactivation in hominids. [[EGCG]] ([[EGCG]]), [[Melatonin]], and [[Glutathione]] also directly scavenge peroxynitrite. [[N-Acetylcysteine]] (NAC) replenishes [[Glutathione]] levels, indirectly limiting nitrative damage. At the level of [[Nitric Oxide]] production, selective iNOS inhibitors ([[1400W]], [[L-NIL]]) have shown benefit in animal models of sepsis and neuroinflammation, though clinical translation has been limited.
