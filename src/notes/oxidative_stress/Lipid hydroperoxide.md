# Lipid hydroperoxide

A **lipid hydroperoxide** (LOOH) is the primary stable product of the [[Lipid Peroxidation]] chain reaction, formed when a [[Lipid peroxyl radical]] (LOO•) abstracts a bis-allylic hydrogen from an adjacent [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA)]]. LOOH accumulation is the hallmark of unchecked oxidative membrane damage and is a defining biochemical feature of [[Ferroptosis]].

## Formation and Fate

LOOH is produced during the propagation phase of lipid peroxidation:

> LOO• + PUFA–H → LOOH + L• (new carbon-centered radical)

Each LOOH carries an oxygen–oxygen bond that can be cleaved by reduced transition metals, branching the chain reaction:

- **Iron-dependent decomposition:** LOOH + Fe²⁺ → LO• (alkoxyl radical) + OH⁻ + Fe³⁺. The alkoxyl radical (LO•) is even more reactive than LOO• and initiates new chains.
- **Peroxide reduction:** LOOH can be detoxified by [[Glutathione Peroxidase 4]] (GPX4), which reduces phospholipid hydroperoxides to lipid alcohols (LOH) using two equivalents of [[Glutathione]] (GSH). Inactivation of GPX4 — by genetic deletion, selenium deficiency, or electrophilic inhibitors (e.g., RSL3) — causes lethal LOOH accumulation, the proximate trigger of [[Ferroptosis]].

## Biological Consequences

LOOH accumulation in membranes disrupts bilayer integrity: the polar hydroperoxide group inserts into the hydrophobic core, increasing membrane permeability, promoting fusion, and altering lipid–protein interactions. In [[Mitochondria]], cardiolipin hydroperoxidation facilitates cytochrome c release and [[Apoptosis]]. In the plasma membrane, LOOH oxidation products ([[Malondialdehyde]], [[4-Hydroxynonenal]]) form covalent adducts with proteins and DNA, propagating damage beyond the lipid phase.

## Detection and Quantification

LOOH is the most direct measure of ongoing lipid peroxidation. Common assays include:

- **C11-BODIPY** — a fluorescent fatty-acid analog that shifts fluorescence upon oxidation; compatible with live-cell imaging and flow cytometry.
- **FOX assay** (Ferrous oxidation–xylenol orange) — quantifies LOOH by Fe²⁺ oxidation at acidic pH.
- **LC-MS/MS** — identifies specific LOOH species (e.g., PE-oxPUFAs) and is the gold standard for [[Ferroptosis]] research.
- **Iodometric titration** — classical method measuring LOOH by iodine liberation.

## Connections

- [[Lipid Peroxidation]]: The chain reaction that produces LOOH as its primary product.
- [[Lipid peroxyl radical]]: Direct precursor that abstracts H to form LOOH.
- [[Ferroptosis]]: Cell death driven by unchecked GPX4-dependent LOOH accumulation.
- [[Glutathione Peroxidase 4]]: The sole enzyme that detoxifies phospholipid hydroperoxides.
- [[Glutathione]]: Co-substrate for GPX4; depletion sensitizes to LOOH accumulation.
- [[4-Hydroxynonenal]]: Cytotoxic aldehyde fragmentation product derived from LOOH.
- [[Malondialdehyde]]: Aldehyde biomarker formed from LOOH decomposition.
- [[Iron]]: Catalyzes LOOH → LO• decomposition, branching the peroxidation chain.
- [[Vitamin E]]: Chain-breaking antioxidant that prevents LOOH formation by intercepting LOO•.
- [[Apoptosis]]: Triggered by cardiolipin hydroperoxidation in mitochondria.
- [[Atherosclerosis]]: LOOH-oxidized LDL is a core pathogenic event.
