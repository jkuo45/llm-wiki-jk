# Formamide

## Chemical Properties

Formamide (HCONH2) is the simplest amide of formic acid, containing a single carbon center. It is a colorless liquid miscible with water and a widely used laboratory solvent and reagent. In chemistry databases, aminoguanidine is formally classified as a derivative of formamide, reflecting the one-carbon backbone linking its nitrogenous substituents.

> [!note]
> Functionally, aminoguanidine behaves as a nucleophilic hydrazine derivative rather than an amide, but the formamide classification highlights the single-carbon core that makes it one of the smallest enzyme-inhibitory drugs (MW 74.09 g/mol, zero rotatable bonds).

## Relevance to Redox & Glycation Research

The formamide scaffold is relevant to this vault primarily through its connection to [[Aminoguanidine]], an anti-glycation and enzyme-inhibitory compound studied for diabetic complications and AGE inhibition. Understanding the scaffold clarifies the structure-function reasoning that underpins the pharmacology of aminoguanidine and related one-carbon drug cores.
