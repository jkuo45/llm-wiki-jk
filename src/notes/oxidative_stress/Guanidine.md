# Guanidine

## Chemical Properties

Guanidine (CH5N3) is a strongly basic compound whose guanidinium group is protonated at physiological pH, enabling strong hydrogen bonding and protein binding. It is a planar, resonance-stabilized cation that forms bidentate hydrogen bonds with biological anions such as carboxylates and phosphates.

## Pharmacological Role

Guanidine derivatives are common pharmacophores in enzyme inhibitors. [[Aminoguanidine]] is described as a derivative of guanidine: the guanidinium moiety combined with a hydrazine-like amino terminus generates a bifunctional molecule that can interact with enzyme active sites and dicarbonyl species.

> [!info]
> This dual functionality underpins aminoguanidine's role as a monoamine oxidase and nitric oxide synthase inhibitor used to manage diabetic complications, as well as its ability to scavenge dicarbonyls such as methylglyoxal.

## Relevance to Redox & Glycation Research

Guanidine-based chemistry connects directly to anti-glycation pharmacology and the dicarbonyl stress that drives [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] formation, placing guanidine at the intersection of enzyme inhibition and AGE research.
