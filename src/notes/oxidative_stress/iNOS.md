# iNOS

**iNOS (Inducible Nitric Oxide Synthase, NOS2)** is a calcium-independent isoform of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] that produces high, sustained fluxes of [[Nitric Oxide]] (micromolar range) in response to inflammatory and immunological stimuli.

## Expression & Activation

iNOS is not constitutively expressed; its transcription is induced by pro-inflammatory cytokines ([[TNF-alpha]], [[IL-1β]], [[IFN-gamma]]) and microbial products ([[LPS]]) through [[NF-κB]], [[STAT1]], and [[IRF1]]-dependent pathways. Unlike [[eNOS]] and [[nNOS]], iNOS activity is independent of intracellular Ca²⁺ transients because it binds calmodulin tightly even at resting Ca²⁺ levels, enabling sustained NO production lasting hours to days. iNOS is expressed in [[Macrophage|macrophages]], [[Neutrophils|neutrophils]], hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, vascular smooth muscle cells, and glial cells.

## Physiological Functions

- **Innate Immunity**: Micromolar NO is a potent antimicrobial agent, inhibiting microbial respiration, DNA replication, and Fe-S cluster proteins in pathogens phagocytosed by macrophages.
- **Wound Healing**: iNOS-derived NO regulates angiogenesis, fibroblast proliferation, and collagen deposition.
- **Tumor Immunity**: iNOS activity in tumor-associated macrophages can have both tumoricidal and tumor-promoting effects depending on concentration and context.

## Pathological Roles

- **[[Nitrative Stress]]**: iNOS-derived NO rapidly reacts with [[Superoxide Radicals]] to form [[Peroxynitrite]], driving 3-nitrotyrosine formation, protein nitration, and oxidative damage.
- **[[Septic Shock]]**: Excessive iNOS-derived NO causes systemic vasodilation, hypotension, and vascular hyporeactivity to vasoconstrictors.
- **[[Cardiovascular Disease]]**: Vascular iNOS contributes to [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and atherosclerotic plaque instability.
- **[[Neurodegeneration|Neurodegenerative Diseases]]**: Glial iNOS produces sustained NO bursts that contribute to neuronal injury in [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]], [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]], and [[Multiple Sclerosis]].
- **[[Diabetes Mellitus]]**: iNOS upregulation in pancreatic islets and [[Peroxynitrite]] formation damage β-cells.
- **[[Chronic Inflammatory Diseases]]**: iNOS is elevated in [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]] synovium, inflammatory bowel disease, and [[Asthma]].
