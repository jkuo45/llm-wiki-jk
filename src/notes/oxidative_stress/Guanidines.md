# Guanidines

## Chemical Properties

Guanidines are a chemical family built around the guanidinium group, a highly basic, resonance-stabilized cation that forms strong bidentate hydrogen bonds with biological anions. Their basicity (pKa ≈ 12.5–13.5) ensures the group remains protonated at physiological pH.

## Pharmacological Significance

Membership in this class is pharmacologically meaningful because guanidines frequently appear in drugs that interact with amine-handling enzymes and ion channels. Examples include aminoguanidine, metformin, and guanethidine. In [[Aminoguanidine]], the guanidine scaffold supplies the positively charged center that contributes to binding and enzyme inhibition relevant to its application as a diamine oxidase, monoamine oxidase, and nitric oxide synthase inhibitor in diabetic complications research.

> [!info]
| Target | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Diamine oxidase | Reduced histamine metabolism |
| Monoamine oxidase | Altered amine turnover |
| Nitric oxide synthase | Reduced NO production |
| Dicarbonyls | Anti-glycation activity |

## Relevance

The guanidine pharmacophore bridges enzyme pharmacology, redox biology (via iNOS and NO signaling), and glycation research (via dicarbonyl scavenging), making it a recurring motif in anti-glycation drug development.
