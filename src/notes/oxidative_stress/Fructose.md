# Fructose

## Overview

Fructose is a monosaccharide, the sweetest of the common hexoses. It is more reactive than glucose as a reducing sugar because its open-chain keto form exists at higher equilibrium concentrations, making it a more efficient donor for protein glycation.

## Polyol Pathway Connection

The [[Polyol Pathway]] provides a non-Maillard route through which elevated glucose generates fructose: glucose is converted to sorbitol by [[Aldose reductase]] and then to fructose by sorbitol dehydrogenase. Because fructose is a more reactive reducing sugar than glucose, its production feeds additional glycation reactions and contributes to [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] accumulation. The pathway becomes especially active under hyperglycemia, linking poorly controlled [[Diabetes Mellitus]] with accelerated AGE formation and the tissue damage seen in diabetic complications.

> [!warning]
> Fructose is considerably more glycating than glucose: even modest polyol-pathway activity can meaningfully increase intracellular AGE precursor flux.

## Clinical Relevance

Fructose's high glycation potential is relevant to diabetic complications and to the dietary AGE literature, where high-fructose corn syrup intake is debated as a contributor to AGE burden and metabolic disease.
