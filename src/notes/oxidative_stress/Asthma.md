# Asthma
Asthma is a common long-term inflammatory disease of the airways of the lungs. It is characterized by variable and recurring symptoms including reversible airflow obstruction and bronchospasm. [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] play significant roles in the [[Inflammation]] of the airways. Antioxidants like [[Vitamin E]] have been studied for their potential protective effects.

## Oxidative Mechanisms in Asthma Pathophysiology

### Environmental Oxidant Exposure and Airway Epithelium
The airway epithelium is directly exposed to environmental oxidants, including [[Ozone]] (O₃), particulate matter (PM2.5), and [[cigarette smoke]], which deplete airway surface liquid antioxidants and trigger an inflammatory cascade. These exogenous ROS activate the [[NADPH Oxidase]] system in epithelial cells, leading to production of [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]. The transcription factor [[NRF2]] (nuclear factor erythroid 2–related factor 2) — a master regulator of antioxidant response element (ARE)-driven gene expression — is dysregulated in asthmatic airways, resulting in insufficient upregulation of antioxidant enzymes such as [[Catalase]], [[Superoxide Dismutase]], and [[Glutathione Peroxidase]].

### Eosinophil and Neutrophil Contributions
In eosinophilic asthma, activated eosinophils release [[Eosinophil Peroxidase]] (EPO), which utilizes Br⁻ and SCN⁻ to produce hypobromous acid (HOBr) and hypothiocyanite (OSCN⁻). In neutrophilic asthma, [[Neutrophils]] recruited to the airways generate copious ROS via [[NADPH Oxidase]] and release [[Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO), producing [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl). These potent oxidants damage airway epithelial cells, increase mucus secretion, and amplify airway hyperresponsiveness.

### Glutathione Depletion and Lipid Peroxidation in Bronchoalveolar Lavage Fluid
The epithelial lining fluid (ELF) normally contains high concentrations of [[Glutathione]] (approximately 100-fold higher than plasma). In asthma, the ratio of reduced (GSH) to oxidized (GSSG) glutathione in [[bronchoalveolar lavage fluid]] is significantly decreased, reflecting severe depletion of antioxidant capacity. Concurrent [[Lipid Peroxidation]] generates elevated levels of [[Malondialdehyde]] (MDA) and [[8-Isoprostane]] in exhaled breath condensate (EBC), both of which serve as non-invasive biomarkers of airway oxidative injury.

### Clinical and Therapeutic Implications
Oxidative stress in asthma correlates with disease severity, frequency of exacerbations, and corticosteroid resistance. [[N-Acetylcysteine]] (NAC) replenishes intracellular [[Glutathione]] and has been studied as an adjunctive mucolytic and antioxidant. Dietary antioxidant supplementation with [[Vitamin C]] ([[Ascorbic Acid]]), [[Vitamin D]], and [[Polyphenols]] (e.g., [[Flavonoids]] from [[Quercetin]]) has shown modest benefit in epidemiological studies. The novel therapeutic [[Sulforaphane]] — an Nrf2 activator found in cruciferous vegetables — is under investigation for restoring antioxidant gene expression in asthmatic airway epithelium.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

Asthma affects ~300 million people worldwide. Sirtuins show mixed pro- and anti-inflammatory roles:

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Decreased in severe asthma; anti-inflammatory via IL-6 regulation through Akt; inhibits IL-9-producing CD4⁺ T-cell differentiation via mTOR–HIF-1α/glycolysis; the miR-138-5p/SIRT1 axis blocks [[NF-κB]] in asthmatic mice.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Enhances allergic asthmatic inflammation — pharmacologic ablation attenuates, genetic overexpression exaggerates the phenotype; aggravates via Th2 responses and macrophage polarization.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Upregulation reduces bronchial epithelial apoptosis and airway inflammation.
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Upregulated in asthmatic bronchial epithelial cells; ameliorates airway remodeling via [[EMT]] regulation (suppressing TGF-β1/Smad3 and c-Jun N-terminal kinase).
- **[[SIRT7]]**: Upregulated; promotes airway remodeling via TGF-β1-induced airway smooth muscle proliferation/migration.
