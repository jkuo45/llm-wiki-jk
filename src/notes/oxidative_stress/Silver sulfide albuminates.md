# Silver sulfide albuminates

## Overview

Silver sulfide albuminates are soluble complexes in which silver sulfide is bound to albumin and other plasma proteins. They are formed in the body when insoluble [[Silver salts]] are transformed into soluble, protein-bound species.

## Systemic Distribution

Insoluble silver salts are transformed in the body into soluble silver sulfide albuminates, which circulate bound to protein. This transformation is central to silver's systemic distribution because the albumin-bound species can travel through the blood to tissues.

> [!info]
> Formation of silver sulfide is chemically significant, paralleling the mineral argentite. The protein-bound silver then interacts with cellular RNA, DNA, and protein amino or carboxyl groups, or is reduced to metallic silver by [[Ascorbic Acid]] or [[Catecholamines]].

## Relevance

Albuminate transport is the key link between silver exposure and tissue deposition. It enables the silver to reach distant tissues where it interacts with biomolecules or precipitates, ultimately contributing to the permanent pigmentation of [[Argyria]].
