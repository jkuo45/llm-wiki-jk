# Gold

## Overview

Gold (Au, atomic number 79) is a noble metal that occurs naturally in free form and in native alloys with [[Silver]] and other metals. Native silver-gold alloys (electrum) are geologically important because they represent the metallic state of both precious metals outside conventional ores.

## Relevance to Silver Toxicology

Gold's significance in this vault is primarily comparative. Understanding silver's alloying behavior with gold matters toxicologically: metallic silver that is oxidized in the body may be reduced back to the elemental state by [[Ascorbic Acid]] or [[Catecholamines]] and deposited in tissues, driving the pigmentation seen in [[Argyria]]. The same redox chemistry of noble metals — oxidation, transport, and re-reduction to the elemental state — underlies their deposition and toxicology.

> [!note]
> Gold compounds themselves have biomedical uses (e.g., chrysotherapy in rheumatoid arthritis), but the note's connection to oxidative stress runs through the shared redox chemistry of noble metals.

## Applications

Gold is widely used in electronics, jewelry, and increasingly in nanomedicine (gold nanoparticles). Its inertness contrasts with the bioactivity of [[Silver ion|silver ions]], highlighting the diversity within noble-metal biology.
