# Glycotoxins

## Overview

Glycotoxins are exogenous compounds capable of readily increasing in vivo levels of [[Advanced Glycation End Products]]. They originate from food processing (e.g., thermal treatment producing dietary AGEs) and from cigarette smoke, where curing of tobacco leaves generates reactive glycation compounds.

## Route of Exposure

Cerami and colleagues found that glycotoxins from cigarettes are inhaled into the alveoli, then transported to the bloodstream or taken up by lung cells, where they interact with other glycation products and contribute to AGE formation. Smoking therefore adds to the body's AGE burden beyond diet and endogenous production.

> [!warning]
> This exogenous route of AGE generation helps explain the accelerated tissue aging and cardiovascular risk observed in smokers — smoking is effectively an inhaled source of pro-aging glycation load.

## Clinical Relevance

Glycotoxins link environmental exposure to the aging phenotype. Their contribution to AGE burden is relevant to cardiovascular disease, diabetic complications, and age-related tissue damage. Understanding exogenous glycotoxin exposure complements the study of endogenous [[Glycation]] and informs lifestyle-based interventions against AGE accumulation.
