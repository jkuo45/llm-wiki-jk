# Methionine sulfoxide reductases

## Overview

Methionine sulfoxide reductases (Msrs) are repair enzymes that reduce methionine sulfoxide back to [[Methionine]] using the [[Thioredoxin]] system. Two stereospecific isoforms exist: MsrA (repairs the S-form) and MsrB (repairs the R-form), which together can restore both configurations of oxidized methionine.

## Mechanism

Protein methionine residues are major targets of oxidants including [[Hypochlorous Acid]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]. Methionine oxidation to methionine sulfoxide can be a benign, reversible sink for oxidants — because MsrA/MsrB catalyze its reduction back to methionine, the residue acts as an endogenous antioxidant that detoxifies oxidants without permanent damage.

> [!info]
| Oxidant | Target | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| HOCl | Methionine → Met-SO | MsrA/MsrB via thioredoxin |
| H2O2 | Methionine → Met-SO | MsrA/MsrB |

## Caveats

Repeated oxidation and repair of the same residue has a kinetic cost and is not unlimited, so chronic stress still accrues damage. Under sustained oxidative stress, repair capacity is overwhelmed and protein function declines, contributing to aging and disease.
