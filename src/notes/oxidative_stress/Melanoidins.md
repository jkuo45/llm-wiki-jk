# Melanoidins

## Chemical Properties

Melanoidins are the high-molecular-weight, brown-pigmented final products of the [[Maillard Reaction]], formed during thermal food processing. They are found in bread crust, bakery products, and coffee, and are responsible for much of the color and flavor of cooked foods.

## Biological Activity

Ames and colleagues found that melanoidins increased the number of anaerobes, clostridia, and bifidobacteria in cultures of human fecal bacteria. Borrelli and colleagues found that bread crust melanoidins can promote growth of some bifidobacterial strains, indicating a possible prebiotic effect.

> [!info]
> These findings show that not all Maillard products are detrimental: some dietary glycation products may support gut bacteria considered health-beneficial, in contrast to the pro-inflammatory effects of other [[Advanced Glycation End Products]].

## Relevance

Melanoidins are only partially absorbed and largely reach the colon, where they modulate the gut microbiota. Their antioxidant, antimicrobial, and prebiotic activities complicate the simple narrative that all Maillard products are harmful, informing balanced interpretation of dietary AGE research.
