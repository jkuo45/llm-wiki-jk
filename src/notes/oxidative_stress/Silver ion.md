# Silver ion

## Overview

The silver ion (Ag+) is the monovalent cation of metallic [[Silver]] and the principal bioactive species released from [[Silver compounds]]. Its interaction with enzymes underpins both silver's antimicrobial activity and its toxicity.

## Enzyme Inhibition

The silver ion is known to inhibit [[Glutathione Peroxidase]] and Na+,K+-ATPase activity, disrupting selenium-catalyzed sulfhydryl oxidation-reduction reactions and intracellular ion concentrations, respectively.

> [!warning]
| Enzyme | Consequence of inhibition |
|---|---|
| Glutathione Peroxidase | Impaired hydroperoxide detoxification |
| Na+,K+-ATPase | Disrupted ion homeostasis |

Glutathione peroxidase is the selenoenzyme that detoxifies hydroperoxides, so its inhibition by silver impairs cellular antioxidant defense and promotes [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Significance

This enzyme-inhibitory activity is a key mechanism linking silver exposure to toxicity and mirrors the redox disruption that makes silver compounds effective antimicrobials. Chronic silver ion exposure also contributes to systemic deposition and [[Argyria]].
