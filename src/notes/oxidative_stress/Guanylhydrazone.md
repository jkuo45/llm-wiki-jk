# Guanylhydrazone

## Chemical Properties

A guanylhydrazone is the condensation product of a carbonyl compound (aldehyde or ketone) with a guanidine-bearing hydrazine such as [[Aminoguanidine]]. The reaction forms a stable C=N-N guanylhydrazone linkage at the carbonyl group.

## Example: Adrenochrome Monoaminoguanidine

Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine (AMM), historically known as S-Adchnon or adrenochrome guanylhydrazone, is formed by reacting [[Adrenochrome]] — an oxidation product of [[Epinephrine]] containing an indoline-5,6-dione core — with aminoguanidine. The reaction forms a stable guanylhydrazone linkage at one of the carbonyl groups of the dione structure.

> [!info]
> AMM therefore combines a redox-active indoline core with an established anti-glycation agent, providing a dual-action glyco-oxidative modulator designed to simultaneously target oxidative stress and protein crosslinking by advanced glycation end products.

## Relevance

Guanylhydrazone chemistry is central to designing compounds that conjugate redox-active scaffolds to anti-glycation pharmacophores. Whether the bound aminoguanidine retains dicarbonyl-scavenging activity is a key open question, with [[Prodrug]] hydrolysis as one proposed mechanism.
