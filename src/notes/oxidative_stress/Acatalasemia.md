# Acatalasemia

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Acatalasemia (Takahara's disease) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by mutations in the [[Catalase]] gene that produce near-absent catalase activity. Because catalase is the primary enzyme degrading [[Hydrogen Peroxide]], deficient tissues accumulate H2O2. The most characteristic pathology arises in the oral cavity, where hydrogen peroxide produced by bacterial metabolism cannot be degraded, fostering gangrene-prone necrotizing gingivitis and severe periodontal destruction. The direction of pathology is enzyme loss → local oxidant accumulation → tissue injury.

> [!info]
> Many acatalasemic individuals are surprisingly asymptomatic, indicating that redundant peroxide-removing systems (glutathione peroxidases, peroxiredoxins, thioredoxin-dependent pathways) partially compensate for catalase loss.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

The classic presentation includes recurrent oral mucosal ulcers, progressive gingival necrosis, and tooth loss. In Japan, acatalasemia was historically identified during school-based oral examinations. Diagnosis relies on enzyme assays of erythrocyte or leukocyte catalase activity. Most variants are hypomorphic, retaining low residual activity, which explains the variable penetrance.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Management is largely supportive: meticulous oral hygiene, treatment of anaerobic oral infections, and avoidance of hydrogen peroxide-releasing mouthwashes. There is no enzyme-replacement therapy, but the condition highlights the physiological role of catalase in redox defense and its redundancy with other antioxidant systems.
