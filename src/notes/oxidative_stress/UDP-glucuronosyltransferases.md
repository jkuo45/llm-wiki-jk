# UDP-glucuronosyltransferases

## Overview

UDP-glucuronosyltransferases (UGTs) are a superfamily of phase II conjugating enzymes localized to the endoplasmic reticulum that transfer glucuronic acid from UDP-glucuronic acid to a wide range of lipophilic substrates, including polyphenols, drugs, and endogenous metabolites.

## Role in Polyphenol Metabolism

Once absorbed, [[Fisetin]] is extensively conjugated by UGTs and sulfotransferases in the intestinal epithelium and liver, producing fisetin glucuronide and fisetin sulfate metabolites that circulate in the plasma.

> [!warning]
| Effect | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Extensive phase II conjugation | Reduced biological activity of conjugates |
| Rapid biliary/urinary excretion | Short plasma half-life |
| Low aglycone tissue levels | Poor oral bioavailability |

Because these conjugates have reduced biological activity and are rapidly excreted via bile and urine, the parent aglycone fisetin reaches only low concentrations in tissues.

## Relevance

The extent of this phase II metabolism is a primary cause of fisetin's poor oral bioavailability and short plasma half-life, and it is one reason most preclinical efficacy studies have used intraperitoneal injection rather than oral dosing. UGT-mediated conjugation is a general bottleneck for dietary polyphenol bioactivity.
