# Glutathione S-transferase π1

## Overview

Glutathione S-transferase π1 (GSTP1) is a member of the cytosolic [[Glutathione S-Transferase]] family, a phase II detoxification enzyme that conjugates [[Glutathione]] to electrophilic substrates, including many chemotherapeutic agents. It is the most abundant GST isoform in many human tissues and is frequently overexpressed in tumors, where it contributes to [[Drug Resistance]] by detoxifying anticancer drugs.

## SIRT3-Mediated Regulation

[[SIRT3]], a mitochondrial sirtuin, downregulates both the mRNA and protein levels of GSTP1. This downregulation reduces the capacity of cancer cells to detoxify chemotherapeutics. In [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] models, SIRT3 overexpression promotes sorafenib-induced apoptosis and thereby enhances drug sensitivity.

> [!important]
| Feature | Consequence |
|---|---|
| SIRT3 ↑ → GSTP1 ↓ | Reduced sorafenib detoxification |
| Reduced GSTP1 | Enhanced drug sensitivity |
| Sorafenib-induced apoptosis | Tumor suppression |

The directionality is SIRT3 upstream of reduced GSTP1 and enhanced drug responsiveness.

## Clinical Relevance

GSTP1 expression status could predict sorafenib response in liver cancer, and boosting SIRT3 may help overcome drug resistance in this malignancy. Beyond oncology, GSTP1 protects cells against oxidative stress and electrophile-mediated damage, connecting this enzyme to cellular redox defense.
