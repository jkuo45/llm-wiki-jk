# Preparation for Oxidative Stress

[[Preparation for Oxidative Stress]] (POS) is transient upregulation of SOD, catalase, GST, GPX, and GCL during hypoxia/estivation that prepares animals for the reoxygenation redox burst. Documented in 83 species across 8 phyla; 60-70% of analyzed species POS-positive.

> [!info]
> Paradoxical mechanism: hypoxia raises mitochondrial superoxide (reduced ETS, electron availability as limiting factor), which signals via NF-kB, FoxO, [[Nrf2]] plus kinase/phosphatase shifts to boost antioxidants before reoxygenation.

## Evidence

- Otala lactea estivation: SOD/catalase/GST +80%, reverting on arousal; Rana pipiens 30 h anoxia: catalase/GST activation.
- Mytilus galloprovincialis 8-24 h air exposure and Pelteobagrus vachelli hypoxia: enzymes rise then return to baseline on re-submersion/reoxygenation.
- Inverted-U examples: scallop SOD +15-50% at 12 h then -40-60% at 7-21 days; shrimp SOD/GPX U-curves; Neohelice granulata GCL +53%, GPX +100% only under severe hypoxia.
- Meets hormesis criteria: 10%+ inverted-U stimulation or 3% J-curve reduction with return; species/tissue oxygen thresholds; early-life priming stronger.
