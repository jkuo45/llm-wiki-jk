# narcolepsy

## Overview

Narcolepsy is a chronic neurological disorder characterized by excessive daytime sleepiness, disrupted nocturnal sleep, and, in type 1, cataplexy. It is typically driven by loss of orexin-producing neurons in the hypothalamus, leading to instability of sleep–wake boundaries.

## Clinical Presentation & Pathophysiology

Core symptoms include persistent sleepiness, sleep attacks, sleep paralysis, and hypnagogic hallucinations. The disorder is strongly associated with destruction of [[Orexin]] neurons, a deficiency that destabilizes arousal control. Diagnosis rests on clinical history, the multiple sleep latency test, and in type 1, low cerebrospinal fluid orexin levels.

> [!info] Wake-promoting treatment
> [[Modafinil]] is a wakefulness-promoting agent approved for narcolepsy, acting via [[Dopamine Transporter]] inhibition and orexin-system modulation, improving daytime alertness with lower abuse liability than classical psychostimulants.

## Therapeutics

Pharmacologic management centers on [[Modafinil]] and its derivatives, with other stimulants and sodium oxybate used as adjuncts for cataplexy. Modafinil's efficacy anchors its off-label cognitive and [[nootropic]] uses, and its effects intersect with dopaminergic signaling relevant to the [[COMT]] system.
