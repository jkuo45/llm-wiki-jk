# Muscle Mass

## Overview

Muscle mass is the quantity of contractile tissue in the body, a major determinant of strength, metabolic health, and resilience across the lifespan. Its preservation opposes sarcopenia and frailty and is a central goal of exercise and nutritional interventions.

## Creatine and Hypertrophy

[[Creatine]] supplementation increases muscle creatine and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)]] stores and, particularly when combined with resistance training, promotes gains in lean tissue mass and strength. Randomized trials in older adults show greater gains in muscle mass and isometric strength with creatine plus heavy resistance training compared to training alone, and meta-analyses confirm larger gains in muscle mass and upper-body strength in older individuals supplementing creatine during resistance exercise. Creatine also decreases protein breakdown and can preserve muscle mass during energy-restricted weight loss.

> [!tip] Clinical utility
> Creatine's protein-sparing and anabolic-supporting effects make it useful for maintaining muscle across the lifespan and in rehabilitation populations, complementing [[Exercise]] and retarding [[Muscle Atrophy]].

## Mechanisms

The ergogenic effects of creatine on muscle stem cells and hypertrophy intersect with the phosphagen energy system mediated by [[Creatine Kinase]], which buffers [[ATP]] during high-intensity effort and supports training adaptations.
