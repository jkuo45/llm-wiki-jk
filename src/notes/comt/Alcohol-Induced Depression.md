# Alcohol-Induced Depression

## Overview

Alcohol-induced depression is a depressive syndrome emerging from chronic alcohol exposure, characterized by anhedonia, reduced sociability, and altered neuro-immune signaling. The gut microbiota has emerged as a key mediator of these neuropsychiatric effects, providing a new axis for intervention.

## NR Protection via the Microbiome

Nicotinamide riboside ([[Nicotinamide Riboside]]) protects against alcohol-induced depression-like behaviors in mice by altering gut microbiota composition. Dietary supplementation with NR for 10 weeks at 400 mg/kg/day reduced both pro- and anti-inflammatory cytokine levels, inhibited [[Microglia]] activation, and provided protection against alcohol-induced depression-like behavior. The effect was attributed to NR upregulating brain-derived neurotrophic factor ([[BDNF]]) through microbiota changes: brain BDNF correlated positively with bacteria enriched in the NR group, including *Barnesiella*, *Alloprevotella*, *Prevotella*, *Alistipes*, and *Mucispirillum*.

> [!important] Causal evidence
> Fecal microbiota transplantation from NR-treated donors reproduced the protective features in germ-free recipients, demonstrating a genuine gut-microbiota–gut-brain axis mechanism rather than a correlational one.

## Implications

These findings link the [[Gut-Brain Axis]] and the [[Gut Microbiome]] to affective resilience, and position NAD+ precursor supplementation as a candidate adjunct in alcohol-related mood disorders and [[Depression]] more broadly, alongside its roles in [[NAD+]] metabolism and neuroinflammation.
