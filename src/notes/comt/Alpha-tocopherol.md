# Alpha-tocopherol

**[[Alpha-tocopherol|α-Tocopherol]]** is the predominant form of [[Vitamin E]] in human plasma and tissue, and the form preferentially used by the body. It is a chromanol with a 5,7,8-trimethylated ring and a saturated 16-carbon phytyl tail — the most potent of the four [[Tocopherols]] as a lipid-phase chain-breaking antioxidant.

## Chemical Properties and Classification

α-Tocopherol (C₂₉H₅₀O₂) is a fat-soluble, yellow viscous oil. Its 6-hydroxychroman ring carries the phenolic hydrogen that terminates [[Lipid Peroxidation]], while the phytyl tail anchors the molecule in the bilayer or lipoprotein core. The trimethylated ring donates that hydrogen faster than the di- or mono-methylated β/γ/δ forms, which is why α is the reference vitamer for biological activity.

Two stereochemical forms exist commercially:

- **RRR-α-tocopherol** (*d*-α-tocopherol, "natural"): the single stereoisomer produced by plants, with the 2*R*,4'*R*,8'*R* configuration. This is the form α-TTP binds.
- **All-*rac*-α-tocopherol** (*dl*-α-tocopherol, "synthetic"): a racemate of eight stereoisomers from chemical synthesis. Only the 2*R* isomers are preferentially retained; the 2*S* forms are cleared faster, so all-*rac* has roughly half the biological activity per milligram of RRR.

Label units expose the difference: 1 mg RRR-α-tocopherol = 1.49 IU, whereas 1 mg all-*rac* = 1.0 IU.

## Mechanism of Action

α-Tocopherol is the principal **chain-breaking antioxidant** of biological membranes. In the propagation phase of [[Lipid Peroxidation]], a lipid peroxyl radical (LOO•) abstracts the phenolic hydrogen:

> LOO• + TocOH → LOOH + TocO•

The resulting α-tocopheroxyl radical is resonance-stabilized and too unreactive to abstract hydrogen from a neighboring [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|PUFA]], so the chain terminates. Rate constants approach 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹.

α-Tocopherol does not act alone. [[Vitamin C]] regenerates TocO• back to TocOH at the membrane–water interface, [[Glutathione]] regenerates ascorbate, and [[Glutathione Reductase]] plus NADPH regenerate glutathione — so one tocopherol molecule can trap many radicals. [[Coenzyme Q10]] and [[GPX4]] provide parallel lipid-phase defenses.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> α-Tocopherol was used as a positive control in DPPH free-radical scavenging assays. At identical concentrations (10 and 25 µM), resveratrol exhibited equivalent DPPH scavenging activity to vitamin E. Vitamin E (10 µM) also restored HDL3-mediated cholesterol efflux in macrophages under oxidative stress.

α-Tocopherol also inhibits protein kinase C, smooth-muscle proliferation, and platelet aggregation by non-antioxidant mechanisms, and modulates transcription of scavenger receptors and adhesion molecules — activities distinct from radical trapping.

## Absorption, Transport, and Pharmacokinetics

α-Tocopherol is absorbed in the small intestine with dietary fat into mixed micelles, incorporated into chylomicrons, and delivered to peripheral tissues and the liver via lipoprotein lipase. In hepatocytes, **α-tocopherol transfer protein (α-TTP)** selectively binds the α form and packages it into VLDL for resecretion.

This selectivity is the reason plasma is dominated by α-tocopherol even though γ-tocopherol is the major dietary form in Western diets. Competing vitamers are not retained; they are ω-hydroxylated by CYP4F2 and excreted as CEHC metabolites. Consequently, high-dose α-tocopherol supplementation can displace γ-tocopherol and other antioxidants from the same transport and membrane slots.

Plasma α-tocopherol is a poor marker of tissue status because lipoprotein concentration strongly influences it; the erythrocyte hemolysis test and adipose-tissue content are more functional readouts. Typical plasma concentrations are 12–46 µmol/L, largely carried by LDL.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Membrane and lipoprotein defense**: α-tocopherol is the dominant radical trap in LDL; LDL oxidizability is inversely related to its tocopherol content. This is central to [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Oxidized LDL]] formation.
- **Ferroptosis suppression**: by scavenging membrane lipid peroxyl radicals, α-tocopherol acts parallel to the [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione]] axis and to the FSP1–[[Coenzyme Q10]] system. [[Ferrostatin-1]] is a synthetic analog of this chemistry.
- **Neuroprotection**: the brain's high [[Docosahexaenoic acid|DHA]] content makes tocopherol defense critical. Defective α-TTP causes ataxia with vitamin E deficiency (AVED), and supplementation partially corrects the phenotype.
- **Supplementation outcomes**: trial results are mixed and genotype-dependent. The ATBC trial (50 mg/day in smokers) reported increased prostate cancer, SELECT (400 IU/day) raised prostate risk 17%, and HOPE found no cardiovascular benefit. Observational dietary intake is neutral-to-favorable. See [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox]] and [[Mitohormesis]] for why a standing antioxidant load can blunt adaptive redox signaling.
- **Dietary sources**: wheat germ, sunflower and safflower oils, almonds, and olive oil are α-rich; corn and soybean supply mostly γ. Unrefined oils retain far more than refined ones — see the comparative table under [[Vitamin E]].

## Interaction with COMT

The response to [[Alpha-tocopherol]] supplementation is significantly influenced by the [[COMT]] [[Val158Met]] polymorphism. This interaction is particularly notable in studies regarding [[Cancer]] prevention and [[Cardiovascular Disease]].

- **Slow [[COMT]] (rs4680 A/A):** Associated with a reduction in cancer rates when taking vitamin E.
- **Fast [[COMT]] (rs4680 G/G):** May experience a slight increase in cancer risk with vitamin E supplementation.

The proposed mechanism is that catechol-estrogen quinone handling differs by COMT activity, so the same antioxidant load shifts redox and estrogen-metabolite balance differently across genotypes. A 10-year follow-up to a vitamin E (α-tocopherol) supplementation trial found that COMT rs4680 variants modulated cancer risk.
