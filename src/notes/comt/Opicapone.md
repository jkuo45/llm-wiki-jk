# Opicapone

## Overview

Opicapone is a third-generation, peripherally restricted [[COMT Inhibitors|COMT inhibitor]] used as an adjunct to levodopa in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Its long duration of action permits once-daily dosing, and its minimal brain penetration lowers the hepatotoxicity risk seen with [[Tolcapone]].

## Pharmacodynamics

By blocking COMT-mediated O-methylation of [[Levodopa]], opicapone prolongs levodopa availability in the bloodstream and thus its delivery to the brain. This extends the duration of dopaminergic stimulation, reducing motor fluctuations and off time and improving motor symptom control. The drug adds to the action of peripherally acting inhibitors and complements the catechol-O-methyltransferase biology described in [[COMT]].

> [!info] Therapeutic niche
> Peripheral restriction avoids central COMT inhibition — preserving frontal dopamine catabolism — while prolonging levodopa half-life, a strategy first refined with [[Entacapone]].

## Clinical Profile

Opicapone is generally well tolerated, with a favorable safety profile relative to earlier agents. It represents the evolution of COMT inhibition as a therapeutic strategy in dopaminergic therapy and exemplifies the clinical relevance of the [[COMT]] enzyme targeted in this cluster.
