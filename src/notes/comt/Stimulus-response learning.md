# Stimulus-response learning

**[[Stimulus-response learning]]** (S-R learning) is a form of procedural/habit learning in which a specific stimulus comes to automatically trigger a stereotyped behavioral response, independent of outcome valuation. It contrasts with goal-directed (deliberative) action and is the basis of habits, skills, and conditioned motor routines.

## Neural Basis

S-R learning depends critically on [[Dopamine]] signaling within the [[Striatum]], particularly the dorsolateral and dorsomedial striatum, where [[Medium spiny neurons]] ([[MSNs]]) integrate cortical and dopaminergic input. Dopamine reinforces the stimulus–response association by enabling corticostriatal long-term potentiation. The D1/D2 MSN balance and downstream basal ganglia output gate the likelihood that a cue will evoke the habitual response.

## Impact of COMT

Because striatal dopamine tone is shaped locally by [[COMT]] ([[Catechol-O-methyltransferase]]) — given sparse [[Dopamine Transporter]] in this region — COMT activity strongly influences S-R acquisition. Studies in COMT-overexpressing (COMT-OE) mice show that elevated [[COMT]] activity accelerates dopamine clearance and leads to **slower acquisition** of stimulus-response tasks. Proposed mechanisms include reduced dopamine availability at MSN synapses (impairing reinforcement) and poorer top-down control over response selection, manifesting as increased impulsivity or perseveration. Conversely, slower COMT (Met allele) preserves dopamine and can facilitate habit formation, though at the cost of flexibility.

## Relationship to Cognition and Pathology

S-R learning underlies both adaptive expertise and maladaptive habits (addiction, compulsive behaviors). Its COMT dependence links genetic dopamine regulation to individual differences in habit formation, relevant to [[Parkinson's Disease]], addiction, and [[Schizophrenia]], where striatal dopamine signaling is disrupted. The balance between habit (S-R) and goal-directed systems is a core axis of computational psychiatry.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

COMT genotype is a candidate moderator of rehabilitation and habit-formation training. Understanding S-R mechanisms informs therapeutic approaches to compulsive and addictive disorders and to motor learning after injury.
