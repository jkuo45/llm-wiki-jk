# Adiposity

## Overview

Adiposity refers to the accumulation of body fat, particularly in white adipose tissue, and is a central driver of metabolic disease and age-related morbidity. Protein restriction (PR) is a well-characterized dietary intervention that reduces adiposity while simultaneously increasing energy expenditure — an effect that occurs despite increased food intake, distinguishing it from conventional caloric restriction.

## Mechanisms

Dietary protein restriction lowers body fat and improves [[Glucose]] homeostasis in young and aged rodents, with benefits most pronounced in males but also present in females, and remains effective when initiated late in life (18–20 months). The metabolic effects are largely mediated by [[FGF21]], which is induced during low-protein feeding and promotes energy expenditure through [[UCP1]]-dependent thermogenic beiging of white adipose tissue and increased thermogenesis in [[Brown Adipose Tissue]]. PR also shifts central brain signaling toward protein appetite, reinforcing protein-seeking behavior over energy intake.

> [!info] Protein appetite trade-off
> PR increases food intake to compensate for low protein, yet fat mass still falls — energy expenditure rises faster than intake because [[FGF21]]-driven thermogenesis outweighs the caloric surplus.

## Clinical Evidence

Randomized controlled trials in humans confirm that low-protein diets reduce body weight and fat mass despite increased caloric intake, mirroring the rodent findings. The effects scale with the magnitude of protein restriction and interact with [[Energy Expenditure]], [[Obesity]], and insulin sensitivity, positioning PR as a candidate strategy for metabolic healthspan extension alongside [[Protein Restriction]] and [[Aging]] research.

## Connections

  - [[FGF21]]: Primary endocrine mediator transducing low-protein intake into thermogenic energy expenditure.
  - [[UCP1]]: Uncoupling protein whose induction in beige/brown fat underlies the thermogenic dissipation of energy.
  - [[Brown Adipose Tissue]]: Site of UCP1-dependent thermogenesis activated during protein restriction.
  - [[Protein Restriction]]: Dietary intervention that reduces adiposity despite increased food intake.
  - [[Energy Expenditure]]: Raised by PR, explaining fat loss independent of caloric intake.
  - [[Obesity]]: The pathological extreme of adiposity that PR interventions counter.
  - [[Glucose]]: PR improves glucose homeostasis in parallel with fat loss.
