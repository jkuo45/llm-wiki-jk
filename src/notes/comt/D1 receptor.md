# D1 receptor

## Overview

The D1 receptor is a Gs-coupled dopamine receptor enriched on the direct pathway of the basal ganglia. It is one of the two principal dopamine receptor classes partitioning striatal output into opposing direct and indirect pathways, with D1 on the direct pathway promoting movement facilitation.

## Mechanism & Pathways

In [[Medium spiny neurons]], D1 activation on the direct pathway disinhibits thalamocortical output, facilitating movement. The D1/D2 dichotomy provides the framework for understanding [[Dopamine]] action in the [[Basal Ganglia]]: D1-expressing neurons of the direct pathway promote movement, while [[D2 receptor]]-expressing neurons of the indirect pathway inhibit it. D1 signaling is also implicated in reward and incentive learning, linking dopamine tone to motivation.

> [!info] COMT relevance
> Dopamine action at D1 receptors is a key node modulated by the dopamine tone that [[COMT]] helps set in regions of lower [[Dopamine Transporter]] density, such as the [[Prefrontal Cortex]].

## Clinical Relevance

D1 receptor signaling is central to motor control and is thus relevant to [[Parkinson's Disease]] and to dopaminergic modulation of [[Working Memory]] and cognition. Because D1 expression is sparse in the prefrontal cortex, cortical dopamine dynamics rely heavily on extrasynaptic diffusion, where [[COMT]]-dependent catabolism — shaped by [[Val158Met]] genotype — governs synaptic availability.
