# Creatine Kinase

## Overview

Creatine kinase (CK) is the enzyme family that catalyzes the reversible phosphorylation of creatine by ATP, producing [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)]] and ADP. The creatine kinase reaction couples the phosphocreatine pool to cellular ATP demand, making it central to energy metabolism in tissues with high and fluctuating energy requirements.

## Structure & Isoforms

Tissue-specific creatine kinase isoforms exist as cytosolic dimers — muscle (MM), brain (BB), and mixed (MB) — and as mitochondrial isoforms. These are expressed in skeletal muscle, brain, heart, and other tissues, with the enzyme colocalized to sites of ATP consumption and production. Creatine itself is transported into cells by [[Creatine Transporters (CRTR)]] and synthesized via the [[Guanidinoacetate (GAA)]] pathway.

> [!info] Energy buffering
> The CK reaction buffers ATP levels during bursts of activity and transports high-energy phosphate between sites of production (mitochondria) and consumption (myofibrils), a system known as the creatine phosphate shuttle.

## Physiology & Pathology

Because creatine kinase is expressed in several tumors, the enzyme is a candidate interface through which [[Creatine]] supplementation could influence cancer cell energy metabolism. Serum CK levels are a routine clinical biomarker of muscle damage. Its role in buffering ATP links CK to the benefits of creatine supplementation on [[Muscle Mass]], strength, and high-intensity performance.
