# Folinic acid

**[[Folinic acid]]** (leucovorin; 5-formyltetrahydrofolate) is a chemically reduced, stable folate derivative that differs fundamentally from folic acid: it does not require the [[MTHFR]] enzyme for its initial activation. Instead, it is converted to 5,10-methenyltetrahydrofolate by folinic acid isomerase and then to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate ([[Methylfolate]]) downstream, effectively entering the folate pathway "midstream."

## Chemical Properties & Metabolism

As a 5-formyl substituted tetrahydrofolate, folinic acid is resistant to the dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR) step needed by folic acid and does not depend on MTHFR—the rate-limiting enzyme impaired in [[MTHFR]] variants (e.g., C677T, A1298C). Once converted, it contributes one-carbon units to the [[Methylation Cycle]], supporting the remethylation of homocysteine to methionine and the production of [[SAM|S-Adenosylmethionine]]. Because it is not delivered as the fully methylated end product, it provides folate *capacity* without an immediate "methyl hit."

## Comparison with Methylfolate

[[Methylfolate]] (5-MTHF) is the bioactive, methyl-donating folate. For slow [[COMT]] individuals, high-dose methylfolate can sharply increase methyl group availability, precipitating anxiety, irritability, or insomnia—the so-called overmethylation response. [[Folinic acid]] is therefore often preferred as a gentler alternative: it supports folate status and methylation capacity while letting the body titrate actual methyl donation according to need, avoiding sudden shifts in [[Methyl groups]] supply to [[COMT]] and other SAM-dependent enzymes.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Folinic acid is clinically established as an antidote to folic-acid antagonist toxicity (methotrexate, pyrimethamine) and as adjunctive therapy in cerebral folate deficiency and autism-spectrum protocols. In oncology it "rescues" normal cells from antifolate chemotherapy. For those with slow [[COMT]] or [[MTHFR]] variants pursuing methylation support, folinic acid—complemented by non-methylated B12 forms such as [[Hydroxocobalamin]] or [[Adenosylcobalamin]]—offers a measured approach to the [[Methylation Cycle]] without overstimulation.
