# Phosphocreatine (PCr)

## Overview

Phosphocreatine (PCr) is the phosphorylated, high-energy storage form of creatine, holding roughly two-thirds of the body's creatine pool while the remainder exists as free creatine. It functions as the rapid phosphagen buffer that maintains [[ATP]] availability during intense exercise, ischemia, hypoxia, and high metabolic demand.

## Mechanism

The [[Creatine Kinase]] (CK) reaction reversibly interconverts creatine and PCr. The energy liberated by breaking PCr down into phosphate and creatine (~10.3 kcal/mol) is used to resynthesize ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate, providing an immediate, oxygen-independent energy reserve that precedes oxidative metabolism.

> [!info] Immediate energy
> The PCr/CK system is the fastest source of ATP regeneration, powering the first seconds of maximal effort and sustaining cellular energetics during ischemia and hypoxia.

## Relevance to Supplementation

[[Creatine]] supplementation raises muscle creatine and PCr stores, expanding this reserve and thereby enhancing high-intensity exercise capacity and training adaptations. The PCr pool thus links creatine intake to [[Muscle Mass]], strength, and performance outcomes.
