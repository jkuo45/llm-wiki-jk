# nootropic

## Overview

A nootropic is a substance purported to enhance cognitive function — alertness, attention, memory, or executive function — in healthy individuals. The category spans prescription drugs used off-label, supplements, and investigational compounds, and sits at the intersection of pharmacology, genetics, and cognitive enhancement.

## Modafinil as the Prototype

[[Modafinil]] is widely used off-label as a nootropic to enhance alertness, attention, and executive function in healthy individuals, capitalizing on its dopaminergic and wake-promoting actions. Its cognitive-enhancing effects are modest and, as with therapeutic use, modulated by [[COMT]] genotype, with fast genotypes showing greater benefit.

> [!important] Genotype dependence
> Nootropic efficacy is not uniform: [[Val158Met]] variation in [[COMT]] alters cortical dopamine catabolism, so the same dose of modafinil produces genotype-dependent cognitive gains.

## Context & Limitations

Off-label nootropic use is common despite indications limited to [[narcolepsy]] and related sleep disorders. Modest effect sizes, inter-individual variability, and the dopamine-bridge to the [[COMT]] system illustrate why personalized, genotype-informed approaches matter for cognitive enhancement. Effects engage the [[Prefrontal Cortex]] and [[Dopamine]] networks central to [[Executive Function]] and [[Working Memory]].
