# Aerobic Capacity

## Overview

Aerobic capacity is the integrative measure of the body's ability to take up, transport, and utilize oxygen during sustained exercise, classically quantified as maximal oxygen consumption (VO₂max). It is a strong predictor of cardiovascular health, metabolic function, and longevity. NAD+ precursors have emerged as nutritional interventions capable of enhancing aerobic capacity.

## NMN and Mitochondrial Support

Nicotinamide mononucleotide ([[NMN]]) enhanced aerobic capacity in a randomized, double-blind trial in amateur runners. Long-term administration of NMN in drinking water at 100 or 300 mg/kg/day for 12 months increased mitochondrial respiratory ability in skeletal muscle and reversed age-associated gene-expression changes in a tissue-specific manner in aged mice. These benefits are attributed to NMN raising [[NAD+]] levels, which activates [[Sirtuins]] and supports oxidative metabolism, [[Energy Expenditure]], and [[Oxygen Consumption Rate]].

> [!info] Mechanism
> By replenishing [[NAD+]], NMN restores sirtuin-driven [[Mitochondrial Function]] and oxidative phosphorylation, coupling mitochondrial biogenesis to improved oxygen utilization during exercise.

## Relevance

Aerobic capacity enhancement via NAD+ precursors positions NMN as a performance- and healthspan-enhancing agent, complementing endurance training, [[Zone 2 Cardio]], and other [[Exercise]] interventions that improve mitochondrial health.
