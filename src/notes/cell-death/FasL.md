# FasL

## Overview

[[FasL]] (Fas ligand, CD95L/CD178) is a type II transmembrane protein and member of the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) superfamily that induces [[Apoptosis]] by binding the Fas receptor ([[Fas]], CD95/APO-1). FasL is critical for immune homeostasis, including activation-induced cell death (AICD) of T cells, elimination of virus-infected and malignant cells, and immune privilege in certain tissues.

## Structure & Activation

FasL is expressed as a membrane-bound protein on cytotoxic T cells and NK cells. It can be cleaved by metalloproteinases (e.g., [[MMP-7]], [[ADAM10]]) to release a soluble form (sFasL), which has reduced apoptotic activity. FasL trimerization upon Fas receptor binding induces receptor clustering and recruitment of the adaptor FADD and procaspase-8 to form the [[DISC]].

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Extrinsic Apoptosis**: FasL binding to Fas recruits FADD and [[Caspase-8]] to the DISC, triggering Caspase-8 auto-activation. Active Caspase-8 then cleaves executioner caspases ([[Caspase-3]], [[Caspase-7]]) or Bid for mitochondrial amplification.
- **Activation-Induced Cell Death (AICD)**: Repeated T cell receptor stimulation upregulates Fas and FasL, causing T cells to kill each other — a mechanism for limiting immune responses.
- **Non-Apoptotic Signaling**: FasL–Fas interaction can also activate NF-κB, MAPK, and PI3K pathways in certain contexts, promoting proliferation or inflammation rather than death.

> [!info] SHP1 Restores FasL Sensitivity
> [[SHP1]]-mediated dephosphorylation of [[Caspase-8]] at Tyr380 is required for FasL to efficiently induce apoptosis. Tumors that silence SHP1 maintain Caspase-8 in its phosphorylated, non-functional state, rendering them resistant to FasL-mediated killing by cytotoxic T cells.

## Role in Cancer

- **Immune Evasion**: Some cancer cells downregulate FasL to avoid immune detection, while others upregulate FasL to induce apoptosis in infiltrating immune cells, a phenomenon known as "tumor counterattack."
- **Resistance**: Defects in FasL signaling contribute to the evasion of programmed cell death in various tumors, including [[cervical cancer]]. Tumor cells can also evade FasL-mediated immune killing by downregulating Fas, upregulating decoy receptors, or silencing SHP1 to maintain Caspase-8 phosphorylation.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Autoimmunity**: Defective Fas/FasL signaling causes autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome (ALPS) in humans, characterized by lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, and autoantibody production.
- **Immune Privilege**: FasL expression in the eye, testes, and placenta kills incoming immune cells, maintaining immune-privileged sites.
