# Erythronecroptosis

**Erythronecroptosis** is the regulated, lytic death of mature [[Erythrocytes]] executed by the [[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] necrosome axis — the erythrocyte counterpart of [[Necroptosis]], named after its discovery of RIPK1, RIPK3 and MLKL expression in mature RBCs (LaRocca et al. 2014).

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Mature erythrocytes lack TNFR1/TNFR2/TRAIL-R1/DR4/DR5, so their necroptosis has cell-specific features — hence a separate name rather than reuse of "necroptosis."

## Mechanism

- Induction: Fas/FasL signaling, CD59 ligation combined with pore formation, ROS, ceramide, and advanced glycation end products (AGEs).
- Execution mirrors nucleated cells: RIPK1/RIPK3 necrosome (RIPK1/FADD/caspase-8 complex) → RIPK3 phosphorylates MLKL → oligomeric MLKL permeabilizes the membrane; plasma membrane integrity is lost and cell contents escape, making the death morphologically close to [[Hemolysis]].
- Diagnostic signature (the review's assay panel): p-RIPK1/p-RIPK3/p-MLKL by WB/ELISA; death blocked by Nec-1 (RIPK1), GSK'872 or HS-1731 (RIPK3), NSA (MLKL), BAY 61-3606 (Syk), PPI (Src) — and **not** by the pan-caspase inhibitor [[Z-VAD-FMK]].

## Relationship to eryptosis

- [[Caspase-8]] sits inside the necrosome as a brake: its activation prevents necroptosis, so erythrocyte necroptosis mirrors the apoptosis↔necroptosis switch of nucleated cells with caspase-8 again acting as the fate selector.
- Eryptosis and erythronecroptosis appear mutually exclusive, implying that erythrocytes make context-dependent cell-fate decisions rather than disintegrating passively.
- No evidence yet links erythrocyte necroptosis to DAMP release, but its lytic nature makes liberation of intracellular content likely — an underappreciated immunogenic question.
