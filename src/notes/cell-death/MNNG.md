# MNNG

**MNNG** (N-methyl-N'-nitro-N-nitrosoguanidine) is a methylating alkylating agent used experimentally to inflict a high load of DNA damage. It is the most widely used tool to trigger [[PARP1]] hyperactivation and [[Parthanatos]], and a classic mutagen/carcinogen in chemical-carcinogenesis research.

## Mechanism and use

- Methylates DNA to form O6-methylguanine and N7-methylguanine together with strand breaks, eliciting a robust [[DNA Damage]] response.
- Hyperactivates [[PARP1]], driving [[PAR]] synthesis and the downstream parthanatos cascade; acute high-dose exposure (commonly 50–500 µM for 5–25 min in HeLa, CHO, and MEFs) is the standard model.
- Killing is blocked by [[PARP inhibitors|PARP inhibition]] (e.g. DPQ, PJ-34) but not by the pan-caspase inhibitor [[Z-VAD-FMK]], the phenotypic criterion that separates parthanatos from [[Apoptosis]].
- Used alongside MMS in [[Parthanatos]] studies of [[NAD+]] depletion, free-[[PAR]] generation, and [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]]-dependent versus -independent death.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Parthanatos Moura 2024 molecular mechanisms more questions than answers|Moura et al. 2024]]
> MNNG and [[MMS]] are the prototypical alkylator triggers of parthanatos; the review notes that the downstream route can differ by insult (e.g. Ca2+ chelation protects H2O2- but not MNNG-induced death), so MNNG and oxidative-stress triggers are not interchangeable.

## Connections

- [[PARP1]] — hyperactivated by MNNG-induced DNA breaks.
- [[Parthanatos]] — MNNG is its canonical experimental trigger.
- [[PAR]] — the polymer produced downstream of MNNG-induced PARP1 activity.
- [[PARG]] — erases MNNG-generated PAR; its manipulation modulates MNNG death.
- [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] — released in MNNG-triggered parthanatos in AIF-dependent models.
- [[Z-VAD-FMK]] — fails to rescue MNNG death, ruling out caspase-dependent apoptosis.
- [[DNA Damage]] — methyl-DNA adducts and strand breaks are the initiating lesion.
- [[MMS]] — companion alkylator trigger with a different adduct spectrum.
