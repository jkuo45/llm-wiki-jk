### Overview

Caspases (cysteine aspartate-specific proteases) are a conserved family of proteolytic enzymes that function as central regulators of cell death and inflammation. They are synthesized as inactive zymogens and activated by proteolytic cleavage, then cleave cellular substrates after aspartate residues. The family is conventionally divided into initiator caspases, which sense and amplify death or inflammatory signals, and executioner caspases, which dismantle the cell. Caspases therefore sit at the heart of both [[Apoptosis]] and inflammatory cell-death programs relevant across [[cancer]] biology.

### Mechanism / Structure

Caspases share a catalytic cysteine and a conserved substrate specificity for aspartate, but they diverge functionally:

- **Initiator caspases** (e.g., [[Caspase-2]], [[Caspase-10]]) contain large prodomains that recruit them into activation platforms such as the PIDDosome or death-receptor complexes, where induced proximity drives autocleavage and activation.
- **Executioner caspases** (e.g., [[Caspase-6]]) have short prodomains and are activated by initiator caspases; they then cleave structural and regulatory substrates — for example, caspase-6 cleaves nuclear lamins, driving the structural collapse of the nucleus during apoptotic execution.
- **Inflammatory caspases** process cytokine precursors (e.g., pro-IL-1β) and, via gasdermin cleavage, drive pyroptosis.

> [!info] Taxonomy vs. mechanism
> "Caspase" is the overarching family; individual members such as Caspase-2, Caspase-6, Caspase-10, and [[Caspase-12]] are specific types with distinct activation platforms and substrates. The family framework clarifies how a single enzymatic architecture is repurposed for death, inflammation, and developmental remodeling.

### Role in Cell Death and Cancer

Within the architecture of [[cancer]] biology, caspases are the effector machinery of apoptotic elimination and a frequent target of dysregulation. Initiator caspases transduce DNA damage (Caspase-2 via the PIDDosome, responding to genotoxic stress and mitotic catastrophe) and death-receptor signals (Caspase-10 amplifying caspase-8 in the extrinsic pathway; its silencing is common in tumors). Executioner caspases such as Caspase-6 carry out the ordered proteolysis that produces the apoptotic morphology. Loss or silencing of these enzymes contributes to evasion of apoptosis, a hallmark of malignancy.

### Clinical & Research Significance

Because caspases execute the cell-death programs that chemotherapy and radiotherapy aim to engage, they are both biomarkers of treatment response and targets for modulation. Restoring caspase-dependent apoptosis in resistant tumors and understanding context-specific caspase roles (initiator versus executioner versus inflammatory) remain active research fronts. The family's diversity — from ER-localized [[Caspase-12]] to lamina-cleaving Caspase-6 — underscores that "caspase" denotes a functional superfamily rather than a single pathway.
