# MMS

**MMS** (methyl methanesulfonate) is a direct-acting, SN2 methylating alkylating agent used experimentally to induce DNA damage. It is a companion trigger to [[MNNG]] in studies of [[PARP1]] hyperactivation and [[Parthanatos]].

## Mechanism and use

- Predominantly methylates ring nitrogens, generating N3-methyladenine and N7-methylguanine, which are repaired by [[Base Excision Repair]] (BER) rather than by O6-methylguanine DNA methyltransferase (the adduct that dominates [[MNNG]] damage).
- Replication forks stall at unrepaired lesions, producing strand breaks that activate [[PARP1]] and can drive PARP1-dependent death.
- Unlike MNNG, MMS is often used as a lower, BER-type genotoxin; depending on dose/cell type it can kill by [[Apoptosis]] or by regulated necrosis/parthanatos.
- In parthanatos studies, MMS is grouped with MNNG as an alkylator that generates a high load of PARP1-activating DNA breaks.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Parthanatos Moura 2024 molecular mechanisms more questions than answers|Moura et al. 2024]]
> The review lists MMS alongside MNNG as a classic exogenous source of the high DNA-break load that triggers PARP1 hyperactivation and parthanatos.
