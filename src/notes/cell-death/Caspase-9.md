# Caspase-9

## Overview

[[Caspase-9]] is the initiator protease of the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]. It is activated within the [[Apoptosome]] complex, formed when [[Cytochrome c]] released from mitochondria binds [[Apaf-1]] and recruits pro-caspase-9 in the presence of dATP/ATP. Active Caspase-9 then cleaves and activates executioner caspases ([[Caspase-3]], [[Caspase-7]]), committing the cell to apoptosis.

## Structure & Activation

Procaspase-9 contains a prodomain (CARD domain) and a catalytic domain. Upon [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]], [[Cytochrome c]] binds Apaf-1 in the cytosol, inducing Apaf-1 oligomerization into the heptameric [[Apoptosome]]. Procaspase-9 is recruited via CARD–CARD interactions, where proximity-induced dimerization activates it without proteolytic cleavage (though cleavage by [[Caspase-8]] or [[Caspase-3]] enhances activity).

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Active Caspase-9 cleaves [[Caspase-3]] and [[Caspase-7]] at specific Asp residues, initiating the execution phase of apoptosis. Caspase-9 activity is regulated by:

- **Inhibition**: [[XIAP]] directly binds and inhibits Caspase-9, preventing its activation. Phosphorylation of Caspase-9 at Ser196 by [[Akt]] also suppresses its activity.
- **Activation**: Release of XIAP (via [[Smac DIABLO]] or TBK1-mediated degradation) liberates Caspase-9.

> [!info] Akt-Mediated Phosphorylation of Caspase-9
> [[Akt]] phosphorylates Caspase-9 at Ser196 (human), suppressing its activity. This represents a key node linking growth factor signaling to apoptotic threshold control in cancer cells.

## Regulation

Caspase-9 activation is tightly controlled by various inhibitors and post-translational modifications:

- **[[Inhibitor of Apoptosis Proteins|IAPs]]**: [[XIAP]] is a potent endogenous inhibitor of Caspase-9. [[Smac DIABLO]] relieves this inhibition.
- **[[Phosphorylation]]**:
    - **[[Akt]]**: Phosphorylates Caspase-9 at Ser196 (human specific) to inhibit its activity, particularly in [[Prostate Cancer]] and [[Colon Cancer]] cells.
    - **[[PKA]]**: Regulates Caspase-9 at Ser99, Ser183, and Ser195, though some studies show no functional effects.
    - **[[CDK1]], [[DYRK1A]], [[ERK]], [[p38 MAPK]]**: Phosphorylate Caspase-9 at Thr125, suppressing its activity and mitotic cell death. This can be reversed by the phosphatase [[PP1α]].
    - **[[PKCζ]]**: Phosphorylates Caspase-9 at Ser144 to inhibit its pro-apoptotic function.
    - **[[c-ABL]]**: Tyrosine kinase that phosphorylates Caspase-9 at Tyr153 to promote its autocleavage and activation in response to DNA damage.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer**: Tumors frequently upregulate [[XIAP]] or activate [[Akt]] to suppress Caspase-9 activity, raising the apoptotic threshold. Downregulation of Caspase-9 has been linked to poor clinical outcomes in stage II colorectal cancer.
- **Neurodegeneration**: Excessive Caspase-9 activation contributes to neuronal death in ischemic injury and neurodegenerative diseases.
