# TRAIL

## Overview

[[TRAIL]] (TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand), also known as TNFSF10 or CD253, is a type II transmembrane protein and member of the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) superfamily that induces [[Apoptosis]] preferentially in transformed and tumor cells while sparing most normal cells. TRAIL initiates the [[Extrinsic Pathway]] by binding death receptors [[DR4]] (TRAIL-R1) and [[DR5]] (TRAIL-R2).

## Structure & Activation

TRAIL is expressed as a type II membrane protein that can be cleaved by proteases (e.g., metalloproteinases, caspases) to release a soluble extracellular domain. The soluble TRAIL trimer binds [[DR4]] and [[DR5]], inducing receptor trimerization and recruitment of the adaptor protein FADD and procaspase-8 to form the Death-Inducing Signaling Complex (DISC), leading to activation of [[Caspase-8]].

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Extrinsic Apoptosis**: TRAIL binding to DR4/DR5 recruits FADD and [[Caspase-8]] to the DISC. Caspase-8 undergoes proximity-induced auto-activation, then directly cleaves executioner caspases ([[Caspase-3]], [[Caspase-7]]) or cleaves [[Bid]] to engage the mitochondrial amplification loop.
- **Decoy Receptors**: TRAIL also binds decoy receptors DcR1 and DcR2, which lack functional death domains and compete with DR4/DR5 for TRAIL binding, modulating apoptotic sensitivity.

> [!info] SHP1 Controls TRAIL Sensitivity
> The efficacy of TRAIL depends on the phosphorylation state of [[Caspase-8]]. [[SHP1]]-mediated dephosphorylation of Caspase-8 at Tyr380 restores apoptotic sensitivity to TRAIL. Tumors that silence SHP1 maintain Caspase-8 in its phosphorylated, non-functional state, rendering them resistant to TRAIL.

## Selective Toxicity

TRAIL is of significant interest in oncology because it has the unique ability to selectively induce apoptosis in cancer cells while sparing normal cells. This selectivity is partly due to the presence of decoy receptors (DcR1, DcR2) on normal cells that bind TRAIL without initiating an apoptotic signal.

## Role in Cancer Therapy

Many therapeutic strategies aim to harness the TRAIL pathway, including:

- **Recombinant TRAIL** (dulanermin): Direct administration of the ligand.
- **Agonistic Antibodies**: Mapatumumab and conatumumab targeting DR4 or DR5.
- **Synergy**: Combining TRAIL with IAP antagonists (e.g., [[Smac DIABLO]] mimetics) to overcome resistance.

Resistance mechanisms include decoy receptor overexpression, FLIP upregulation, and Caspase-8 silencing/phosphorylation. High levels of [[XIAP]] can also confer resistance to TRAIL-induced apoptosis.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer Therapeutics**: Recombinant TRAIL and TRAIL receptor agonist antibodies have been tested in clinical trials with mixed results; resistance mechanisms are clinically important.
- **Immune Surveillance**: TRAIL is expressed on NK cells, T cells, and macrophages, contributing to immune surveillance against tumors and virus-infected cells.
