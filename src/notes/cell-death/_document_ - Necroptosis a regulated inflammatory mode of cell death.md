# Necroptosis: a regulated inflammatory mode of cell death (Dhuriya & Sharma 2018)

Timeline review of [[Necroptosis]] — the [[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] alternative to [[Apoptosis]] when [[Caspase-8]] is blocked — covering initiation, classical vs non-classical necrosomes, phospho-acceptor control, [[Inflammation]] coupling, infection, and neurodegenerative disease.

> [!note]
> Core model — death/TLR/IFN receptors → [[RIPK1]] deubiquitination → [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]] RHIM necrosome → [[RIPK3]]-phosphorylated [[MLKL]] pores in the plasma membrane → lytic, [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMP]]-releasing death. Two preconditions: [[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] expression and [[Caspase-8]] inhibition (with in vivo exceptions).

## Molecular mechanism

- Classification: extrinsic ([[TNFα]]), intrinsic ([[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]), ischemia-mediated intrinsic. Best-characterized arm is [[TNFR1]]: Complex I (TRADD/FADD/[[RIPK1]]/TRAF2-5/cIAP1-2; [[RIPK1]] ubiquitination → [[NF-κB]]/MAPK survival) → on [[Caspase-8]] loss, [[CYLD]] deubiquitinates [[RIPK1]] → Complex II (FADD/TRADD/[[RIPK3]]/[[Caspase-8]]) → [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]] RHD/RHIM necrosome → [[RIPK3]] phosphorylates [[MLKL]] → oligomeric [[MLKL]] either recruits Na+/Ca++ channels or binds phosphatidylinositol phosphates to form pores.
- Normally [[Caspase-8]]/cFLIP-L heterodimers suppress the pathway by cleaving [[RIPK1]]/[[CYLD]] (and TRIF); [[Caspase-8]] inhibition flips extrinsic [[Apoptosis]] to [[Necroptosis]]. [[RIPK3]] overexpression alone suffices; inducible [[RIPK3]] dimerization drives [[MLKL]]-dependent death without [[RIPK1]].
- [[PGAM5]] mitochondrial role (Wang et al.) is flagged controversial — complete mitochondrial depletion leaves [[Necroptosis]] intact in some models.

## Non-classical necrosome

- RHIM-domain proteins [[TLR3]]/[[TLR4]]-TRIF and [[ZBP1]]/DAI form [[RIPK1]]-independent necrosomes with [[RIPK3]]. TRIF lacks kinase activity, so its [[RIPK3]] activation differs mechanistically; [[RIPK1]] inhibition does not impair [[TLR3]]-TRIF [[Necroptosis]].

## Phospho-acceptor sites

- [[RIPK1]]: Ser89 (inhibitory; S89D reduces, S89A increases kinase activity), Ser161 (pro-necrotic; S161A reduces), Ser321 and autophosphorylated Ser166 required.
- [[RIPK3]]: Ser204 (mouse; Ser199 human; S204A blocks [[Necroptosis]]), Ser232 (MLKL-recruitment surface, not kinase control), Ser227 (permissive MLKL-binding conformation; phosphomimetic blocks).
- Truncated kinase-dead [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]] form amyloid fibrils; kinase phosphorylation gates RHIM amyloid-like necrosome assembly. [[MLKL]] (not [[RIPK3]]) level decides apoptosis-vs-[[Necroptosis]] in T231A/S232A-mutant experiments (Moriwaki & Chan).

## [[Necroptosis]] and [[Inflammation]]

- Unlike [[Apoptosis]], cytokine secretion is primal: [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMP]] release plus direct [[RIPK3]] inflammasome activation (caspase-8-mediated and [[NLRP3]]-mediated arms → caspase-1/11 → mature IL-1β). Whether [[MLKL]] is obligatory for [[NLRP3]] activation is contested (some knockouts preserve IL-1β maturation).
- [[RIPK1]] also drives cytokines independently as a scaffold ([[TNFα]]-[[NF-κB]]/JNK, TLR4-IL-1α, autocrine [[TNFα]], SHIP-defective spontaneous disease). [[Caspase-8]] is dual: promotes IL-1β maturation in some scaffolds, restrains [[RIPK3]]-[[MLKL]]-[[NLRP3]] assembly in others.

## Infectious and non-infectious disease

- Bacteria: E. coli NleB1 GlcNAcylates FADD/[[RIPK1]] death domains to block death (colonization factor); [[RIPK3]]/[[Caspase-8]]/FADD loss sensitizes to Yersinia; [[RIPK3]]/[[TNFα]] in M. tuberculosis granulomas with [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]]-driven [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]; RIPK3/MLKL up in alcoholic/drug liver injury (Nec-1 or [[RIPK3]] depletion protects); heme→TLR4→[[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]/[[TNFα]]→[[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]] synergy in malaria/leishmaniasis hemolysis.
- Viruses: MCMV M45/vIRA (RHIM) blocks [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]] and DAI-[[RIPK3]] binding; HCMV IE1 acts downstream of [[MLKL]]; [[RIPK3]]-KO mice fail viral [[Necroptosis]] and are hypersusceptible; HIV T-cell [[Necroptosis]] rises as [[Caspase-8]] falls.
- Sterile: [[RIPK3]]-KO reduces necrotic area, cytokines, and atherosclerosis; I/R injury across tissues.

## Neurodegeneration and therapeutics

- [[Necroptosis]] implicated in [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's]] (6-OHDA/PC12, high [[RIPK1]]), ALS (SOD1-G93A rescued by [[RIPK3]] silencing/Nec-1; OPTN mutation → [[RIPK1]] axonopathy; sporadic-ALS astrocyte→motor-neuron killing blocked by NSA/[[RIPK1]] knockdown), multiple sclerosis (TNFα oligodendrocyte death), SCI (p-MLKL in astrocytes/microglia; M1-microglia→astrocyte killing; Nec-1 protective), retinal degeneration, Gaucher-like microglial [[RIPK3]].
- Agents: [[Necrostatin-1]] (blocks [[RIPK1]] phosphorylation/translocation; with zVAD complementary; also Akt/mTOR anti-apoptotic/autophagy effects post-TBI; AD/PD/HD models in Table 1), 24(S)-hydroxycholesterol ([[24(S)-Hydroxycholesterol]]; low = anti-amyloidogenic, high = non-apoptotic death in low-[[Caspase-8]] neurons), [[SAHA]]/HDAC inhibition (up cFLIP-L/[[NF-κB]]/p38, inactivates JNK).

## Conclusion

[[RIPK3]]/[[RIPK1]] as therapeutic targets for inflammatory and neurodegenerative disease, with the caveat that non-necroptotic scaffolding functions must be dissected from kinase-dependent death.
