# Ferrostatin-1

**Ferrostatin-1** (Fer-1) is a synthetic small-molecule ferroptosis inhibitor that suppresses [[Lipid Peroxidation]] by acting as a chain-breaking [[Antioxidants|antioxidant]] in lipid bilayers. It is the most widely used tool compound for validating ferroptosis in cell culture and animal models.

## Mechanism of Action

Fer-1 contains a cyclohexylamine moiety that is oxidized by lipid peroxyl radicals (LOO•) to an aromatic amine, terminating the lipid peroxidation chain reaction. Unlike [[Vitamin E]], which intercepts LOO• in a stoichiometric manner, Fer-1 is regenerable at the membrane interface, providing sustained protection. It does not chelate iron, chelate ROS directly, or inhibit enzymatic sources of ROS — its activity is confined to chain-breaking within the membrane.

## Research Applications

- Distinguishing ferroptosis from apoptosis, necroptosis, and other cell death modalities (rescue by Fer-1 is a defining criterion)
- Validating the role of lipid peroxidation in disease models of [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]], and [[Cardiotoxicity]]
- As a positive control in C11-BODIPY and TBARS assays
