# Caspase-6

**Caspase-6** is an effector (executioner) caspase activated during the terminal execution phase of [[Apoptosis]]. Although less studied than [[Caspase-3]], it occupies a distinctive role in dismantling the nuclear envelope through cleavage of lamin A/C, making it a principal driver of the morphological collapse that defines apoptotic cell death.

## Structure & Activation

Caspase-6 is produced as an inactive zymogen (~33 kDa) with a short prodomain. Unlike initiator caspases, it lacks DED or CARD recruitment domains and is instead activated by upstream initiators — [[Caspase-8]] (extrinsic) and [[Caspase-9]] (intrinsic) — as well as by feedback amplification from [[Caspase-3]]. Mature caspase-6 is a heterotetramer of large (p18) and small (p11) subunits sharing the conserved catalytic dyad (Cys-His) and substrate specificity for aspartate residues, particularly the (V/E)T/D motif.

## Mechanism of Action

A signature substrate is nuclear **lamin A**, an intermediate-filament protein underpinning the nuclear lamina. Caspase-6 cleaves lamin A at a conserved site, severing the lamina meshwork and precipitating nuclear envelope breakdown, [[chromatin condensation]], and DNA fragmentation. Caspase-6 also processes other structural and signaling proteins, including keratin 18, huntingtin, and the amyloid precursor protein, implicating it in both apoptosis and non-apoptotic proteolytic events. Its activation can therefore propagate cellular demolition even when caspase-3 is partially inhibited, providing a backup execution route.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

Caspase-6 is pivotal for the ordered demolition of cells during development and homeostasis. In disease, its nuclear-targeting activity is co-opted in chemotherapy-induced apoptosis of tumors. Conversely, caspase-6-mediated cleavage of neuronal structural proteins has been implicated in [[Neurodegeneration]] (e.g., axonal degeneration in Alzheimer's and Huntington's disease models), illustrating that the same protease serves both tumor suppression and tissue injury depending on context. In [[Cancer]], robust caspase-6 activation generally favors therapy response, whereas its suppression contributes to apoptotic resistance.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Because caspase-6 can execute apoptosis independently of caspase-3, it is a candidate target for overcoming caspase-3-resistant malignancies. Small-molecule caspase-6 activators and peptides that disrupt its inhibitory interactions are under preclinical investigation. Conversely, caspase-6 inhibitors are explored neuroprotectively. Biomarkers of caspase-6 activity (e.g., circulating cleaved cytokeratin-18 fragments) reflect epithelial tumor cell death and are used as pharmacodynamic readouts in early-phase trials.

**Necroptosis regulation.** Caspase-6 promotes the inflammatory response triggered by [[RIPK3]] binding to [[ZBP1]] through interaction with [[RIPK3]] in viral infectious disease.
