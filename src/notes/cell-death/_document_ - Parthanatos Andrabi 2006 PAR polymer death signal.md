# Poly(ADP-ribose) (PAR) polymer is a death signal (Andrabi et al. 2006)

Discovery paper for [[Parthanatos]]: the product of [[PARP1]] hyperactivation, [[PAR]] polymer itself, is a previously uncharacterized cell-death signal — not just [[NAD+]] depletion.

> [!important]
> Key claim — purified [[PAR]] delivered into neurons kills; degrading it with [[PARG]] or phosphodiesterase 1 blocks death; neutralizing [[PAR]] antiserum or cytosolic [[PARG]] overexpression blocks [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] excitotoxicity; [[PARG]] transgenic mice resist stroke while [[PARG]] heterozygotes are sensitized.

## Results

### [[PAR]] polymer is directly toxic

- [[PAR]] synthesized by in vitro [[PARP1]] automodification (mean ~40-mer, range 6–100-mer) delivered by BioPorter lipid reagent kills ~50% of cortical neurons at 80 nM; pretreatment with [[PARG]] or phosphodiesterase 1 abolishes toxicity.
- Charge control poly(A) is not toxic; [[PAR]] kills [[PARP1]]-KO neurons, proving the polymer acts downstream of the enzyme.
- Toxicity extends to [[HeLa Cells|HeLa]] cells (~70% death at 80 nM complex polymer).

### Length- and dose-dependent, caspase-independent

- Fractionated [[PAR]]: 16-mer weak, 30-mer modest, ~60-mer (40–100 range) causes ~80% neuronal death at 80 nM.
- Dose response from 20 nM upward; these sizes/concentrations match intact-cell levels during [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] excitotoxicity (~80 nM at 60 min) and MNNG toxicity.
- Broad-spectrum caspase inhibitor z-VAD-fmk fails to protect neurons or [[HeLa Cells|HeLa]] cells; [[PAR]] does not directly activate caspases.

### Interfering with [[PAR]] signaling protects

- Anti-[[PAR]] antiserum (immunodepletes purified [[PAR]] and nuclear supernatant [[PAR]]) delivered intracellularly reduces [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] (500 uM, 5 min) neuronal death by ~50%; preimmune/normal serum has no effect.
- Cytosolic (exon-1-deleted) wild-type [[PARG]] adenovirus lowers cytosolic [[PAR]] and halves [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] death; catalytically dead mutant (E756A/E757A) and GFP controls do not.
- Same protection holds for MNNG (50 uM, 15 min) killing of [[HeLa Cells|HeLa]] cells — [[PARP1]]-dependent, caspase-independent, blocked by DPQ but not z-VAD.

### [[PARG]] dosage sets vulnerability in vitro and in vivo

- [[PARG]] heterozygous cortical neurons accumulate ~2x [[PAR]] after [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] (higher basal too) and show ~20% excess death — matching the purified-[[PAR]] dose response.
- [[PARG]] siRNA in [[HeLa Cells|HeLa]] cells reduces activity/protein, prolongs MNNG-induced [[PAR]] accumulation, and increases death ~30%.
- PrP-promoter [[PARG]] transgenic mice: −62% hemisphere, −52% striatal, −59% cortical infarct after 120-min MCAO.
- [[PARG]] heterozygous mice: +56% hemisphere, +56% striatal, +62% cortical infarct after 90-min MCAO. Perfusion, temperature, and deficit scores matched.

## Discussion

- [[PAR]] is a signaling molecule initiating the death cascade after [[PARP1]] activation; [[NAD+]] decrements alone are insufficient (energy-replete cells die from [[PAR]]; neutralizing [[PAR]] protects despite [[PARP1]] activation).
- Residual toxicity after [[PAR]] depletion reflects incomplete depletion (20 nM still toxic) or parallel pathways.
- Therapeutic implication: interfering with [[PAR]] actions (neutralization, [[PARG]] potentiation) is a strategy for stroke, trauma, [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury|ischemia-reperfusion]], [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's]], and related [[PARP1]]-injury indications.

## Methods (abridged)

Primary cortical cultures (E14–15, 12–14 DIV); [[PAR]] synthesis/purification (Alvarez-Gonzalez/Jacobson; Kiehlbauch DEAENPR fractions); BioPorter delivery (3–4 h); Hoechst 33342/PI computer-assisted counting; anti-[[PAR]] polyclonal and anti-[[PARG]] blots; exon-1-deleted WT/mutant [[PARG]] adenovirus (CMV); intracellular [[PAR]] ELISA; intralumenal-filament MCAO with laser-Doppler verification and TTC infarct volumetry; ANOVA + Tukey, p < 0.05.
