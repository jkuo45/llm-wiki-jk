# Noncanonical NETosis

**Noncanonical NETosis** is the lytic [[NETosis]] pathway deployed when [[Neutrophils]] detect [[Gram-Negative Bacteria]] in their cytosol. It relies on bacterial sensing by the [[Noncanonical Inflammasome]] and proceeds through [[Caspase-4]]/[[Caspase-11]]- and [[Gasdermin D|GSDMD]]-driven NET extrusion. The signaling resembles cell lysis by [[Pyroptosis]], but the morphological outcome is neutrophil death by NETosis with nuclear delobulation, DNA extrusion, DNA–MPO colocalization, and histone citrullination (Chen et al. 2018).

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> Neutrophils are atypical inflammasome responders: they do not undergo [[Caspase-1]]-dependent pyroptosis during canonical inflammasome signaling, yet they die on noncanonical inflammasome activation. NET extrusion in this pathway requires caspase-4/11 and GSDMD but proceeds independently of [[Neutrophil Elastase]], [[Myeloperoxidase]], and [[PAD4]] (Chen et al. 2014; Karmakar et al. 2015; Monteleone et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2018).

## Sensing by the noncanonical inflammasome

[[Inflammasome|Inflammasomes]] divide into caspase-1-activating (canonical) and caspase-11 (mouse) / caspase-4/5 (human)-activating (noncanonical) classes. The noncanonical inflammasomes sense cytosolic [[Lipopolysaccharide|LPS]] of Gram-negative bacteria; LPS detection activates caspase-4/5 in humans and caspase-11 in mice. Both classes cleave GSDMD to trigger GSDMD-p30 plasma-membrane pores, release proinflammatory cytokines ([[IL-1β]], [[IL-18]]), and cause lytic death (Kayagaki et al. 2015; Shi et al. 2015; Liu et al. 2016).

## NET extrusion mechanism

Human and murine neutrophils infected with cytosol-accessing Gram-negative bacteria (Δ*sifA Salmonella enterica*, *Citrobacter rodentium*) died and extruded NETs with the hallmark NETosis morphology (Chen et al. 2018). Mechanistically:

1. GSDMD-p30 pores permeabilize granule and nuclear membranes and eventually rupture the plasma membrane.
2. Nuclear GSDMD-p30 pores allow [[Caspase-11]] access to chromatin, where it degrades histones in a function analogous to NE in [[Suicidal NETosis]].
3. Chromatin is expelled during plasma-membrane rupture, producing the [[Neutrophil Extracellular Traps|NET]] and neutrophil death.
4. GSDMD-driven [[NLRP3]] inflammasome activation triggers IL-1β release, recruiting and activating neutrophils to kill NET-entrapped bacteria.

PAD4-dependent histone citrullination accompanies noncanonical NETosis but is not required for death or NET extrusion (Chen et al. 2018).

> [!warning]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> Do not assume NE/MPO/PAD4 dependence from suicidal NETosis. Noncanonical NETosis is the NE/MPO/PAD4-independent arm; [[Caspase-11]] substitutes for NE as the histone-degrading activity.

## Host defense function

The pathway suppresses bacterial residence in the neutrophil cytosol and prevents in vivo microbial dissemination:

- In vitro, noncanonical NETosis suppressed neutrophil cytosolic infection with Δ*sifA Salmonella* (Chen et al. 2018).
- Murine *Casp11* or *Gsdmd* deficiency promoted microbial dissemination to a secondary infection site during Δ*sifA Salmonella* challenge (Chen et al. 2018; Aachoui et al. 2013; Storek and Monack 2015; Thurston et al. 2016).
- DNase I dismantling of NETs at a primary infection site promoted bacterial dissemination in wild-type mice but not in *Casp11*- or *Gsdmd*-deficient mice, indicating that NET structures themselves mediate host defense, likely by immobilizing bacteria for destruction by newly recruited neutrophils (Chen et al. 2018).

## Therapeutic relevance

Suicidal and noncanonical NETosis converge on a single executioner, [[Gasdermin D]]. This convergence suggests GSDMD inhibitors may have therapeutic potential in NET-associated diseases including thrombosis, [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]], and autoimmune vasculitis (Burgener & Schroder 2020). Whether distinct NETosis pathways produce NETs with different immunomodulatory or antimicrobial properties remains untested.

## Connections

- [[Noncanonical Inflammasome]] — specifies the pathway by direct cytosolic LPS sensing via caspase-4/5 (human) or caspase-11 (mouse).
- [[Caspase-11]] — gains chromatin access through GSDMD-p30 nuclear pores and cleaves histones in place of NE.
- [[Caspase-4]] — human orthologous sensor for cytosolic LPS.
- [[Gasdermin D]] — GSDMD-p30 pores permeabilize granule, nuclear, and plasma membranes; shared executioner with [[Suicidal NETosis]].
- [[Lipopolysaccharide]] — cytosolic ligand that initiates noncanonical inflammasome assembly.
- [[Gram-Negative Bacteria]] — pathogens sensed when they escape the phagosome into the cytosol.
- [[Pyroptosis]] — related lytic cell death whose machinery is reused, with NETosis morphology as the outcome.
- [[Neutrophil Elastase]] / [[Myeloperoxidase]] — dispensable for this pathway, unlike suicidal NETosis.
- [[PAD4]] — citrullination accompanies but is not required for death or NET extrusion.
- [[NLRP3]] — GSDMD-driven inflammasome activation licenses IL-1β release and neutrophil recruitment.
- [[IL-1β]] — recruits and activates neutrophils that kill NET-entrapped bacteria.
- [[Suicidal NETosis]] — the other GSDMD-dependent lytic pathway; differs in trigger and protease requirements.
- [[Vital NETosis]] — contrast arm: PAD4-dependent, non-lytic, membrane-preserving.
