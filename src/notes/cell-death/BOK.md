# BOK

BOK (BCL-2-related ovarian killer protein) is a pro-apoptotic member of the [[Bcl-2 family]] that has emerged as a key regulator of [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] flux, particularly in the context of [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]].

## Mechanism in Mitophagy

BOK facilitates [[Parkin]] translocation to damaged [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] by competing with the [[Mcl-1]]/Parkin complex. By displacing Parkin from its inhibitory interaction with MCL-1, BOK promotes the initiation of mitophagic clearance. This mechanism is distinct from the canonical [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] activation pathway.

## Role in Alzheimer's Disease

In APPswe/PS1dE9 models of [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]], the loss of BOK is closely linked to mitochondrial accumulation. [[Hippocampus|Hippocampal]] overexpression of BOK can restore mitophagy flux and improve cognitive function by alleviating [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]]. BOK therefore represents a potential therapeutic target for restoring mitophagy in AD.
