# Vital NETosis

**Vital NETosis** (also "leukotoxic hypercitrullination") is the rapid [[NETosis]] pathway in which [[Neutrophils]] release [[Neutrophil Extracellular Traps|NETs]] without immediate cell death. Unlike the slower lytic [[Suicidal NETosis]] and [[Noncanonical NETosis]], neutrophils undergoing vital NETosis retain migration, [[Phagocytosis]], and bacterial killing while extruding chromatin. Defining features are rapid histone citrullination, nuclear blebbing, and vesicular transport of nuclear blebs to the plasma membrane, where chromatin is externalized without plasma-membrane permeabilization (Clark et al. 2007; Pilsczek et al. 2010; Yipp et al. 2012; Konig and Andrade 2016).

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> Vital NETosis is triggered by specific bacteria, bacterial products, [[TLR4]]-activated [[Platelets|platelets]], and [[Complement System|complement]] proteins. It is NADPH-oxidase-independent and proceeds without apparent NE-dependent histone clipping; [[PAD4]]-mediated histone citrullination is the obligatory chromatin-decondensing step (Pilsczek et al. 2010; Douda et al. 2015; Pieterse et al. 2018).

## Receptor-mediated triggering

Several surface receptors mediate the rapid response:

- [[TLR2]] is required for vital NETosis during murine in vivo skin infection with *Staphylococcus aureus*; TLR2 or [[TLR4]] blockade suppresses NET formation induced by in vitro *S. aureus* exposure (Yipp et al. 2012; Wan et al. 2017).
- Neutrophil exposure to bacterial [[Lipopolysaccharide|LPS]] in whole blood triggers vital NETosis that requires TLR4 expression on platelets and platelet–neutrophil interaction via P-selectin (Pieterse et al. 2016).
- [[Complement System|Complement receptor 3]] recognizes β-glucan of *C. albicans* immobilized by [[Fibronectin]], inducing robust NET formation (Byrd et al. 2013).

## Calcium signaling and PAD4

[[Calcium Signaling|Calcium influx]], [[PAD4]] activation, and histone citrullination are common requirements. Ionophores, bacterial products, or increased extracellular pH trigger mitochondrial [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] generation, calcium influx, and calcium-activated PAD4 enzymatic function; PAD4 then citrullinates histones to mediate DNA decondensation (Douda et al. 2015; Naffah de Souza et al. 2017).

- NADPH oxidase function is dispensable for vital NETosis, unlike suicidal NETosis (Pilsczek et al. 2010; Douda et al. 2015).
- PAD4 deficiency in *Shigella flexneri*-infected neutrophils abolished histone citrullination, vital NETosis, and bacterial killing (Li et al. 2010).
- A selective PAD4 inhibitor abrogated vital NETosis induced by ionomycin or *S. aureus* (Lewis et al. 2015).

> [!important]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> PAD4 is essential for vital NETosis but dispensable for PMA-driven suicidal NETosis and for [[Noncanonical NETosis]]. Histone citrullination is therefore a marker of the vital arm, not of NETosis in general.

## DNA source

The origin of extruded DNA is contested:

- Nuclear blebbing releases histone-rich nuclear [[DNA]] (Pilsczek et al. 2010; Yipp et al. 2012; Gupta et al. 2018).
- Other studies report [[Mitochondrial DNA]] in NETs (Yousefi et al. 2009; Keshari et al. 2012; Itagaki et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2015; Lood et al. 2016), and mitochondrial-DNA-bearing NETs are reported to elicit proinflammatory cytokine production from immune cells.

## Disease contexts

Vital NETosis is often observed in thrombosis and autoimmune diseases including [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]], and autoimmune vasculitis (Clark et al. 2007; Kessenbrock et al. 2009; Fuchs et al. 2010; Martinod et al. 2016). Whether the resulting NETs differ in immunomodulatory or antimicrobial properties from those of lytic pathways remains untested.
