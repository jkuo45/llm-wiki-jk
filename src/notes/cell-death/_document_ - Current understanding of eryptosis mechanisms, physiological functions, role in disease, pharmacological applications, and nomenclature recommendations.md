# Current understanding of eryptosis (Tkachenko et al. 2025)

Consortium review (F. Lang's group lineage) that names and standardizes [[Eryptosis]] as a distinct regulated cell death (RCD) modality of mature [[Erythrocytes]], separates it from [[Apoptosis]], erythrocyte senescence, [[Hemolysis]] and [[Erythronecroptosis]], and argues eryptosis is druggable.

> [!note]
> Thesis — [[Eryptosis]] is the Ca2+-driven, phosphatidylserine (PS)-exposing death of organelle-free erythrocytes; confirmation requires PS externalization **plus** elevated intracellular Ca2+. All phenotypic hallmarks are Ca2+-mediated: Gardos-channel K+ efflux (shrinkage), [[Calpain]] cytoskeleton digestion (blebbing), [[Scramblase]] activation (PS exposure).

## Facts

- Mature [[Erythrocytes]] adjust cell fate on incoming life/death signals and execute two defined RCD modalities: eryptosis (apoptosis-like) and erythronecroptosis (necroptosis-like).
- Purpose: shorten the lifespan of damaged cells and remove them from circulation **before** [[Hemolysis]] releases proinflammatory [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMPs]].
- >600 PubMed papers by end-2024; term coined 2005 at Tübingen.

## Erythrocyte structure as the constraint on cell death

- Mature RBCs are 7–8 µm biconcave, hemoglobin-filled, nucleus- and organelle-free products of [[Erythropoiesis]]; enucleation (rate-limiting; FOXO3, E2F2, miR-30a/miR-191/miR-181a, F-actin, dynein, tropomodulin, p38 MAPK) and mitochondrial clearance by mitophagy free space for Hb.
- Organelle-containing erythroid precursors execute full [[Apoptosis]] (intrinsic/extrinsic), RIPK1-dependent [[Necroptosis]], and iron-driven [[Ferroptosis]]; mature RBCs retain only [[Fas]]/[[FasL]], [[Caspase-3]], [[Caspase-8]], and the RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL axis as "heritage" components.
- Protein synthesis is negligible in mature RBCs, so cell-death regulation is purely post-translational (activity adjustment, phosphorylation, channel gating) — no transcriptional or epigenetic layer.

### Canonical and non-canonical erythrocyte functions

- Gas transport (Hb as O2 carrier and ~23% of CO2 via [[Carbonic Anhydrase]] and anion exchanger 1), NO bioavailability tuning via eNOS/oxyHb scavenging and hypoxic NO release from S-nitrosohemoglobin, hemostasis (ATP/ADP export, PS-driven thrombin, RBC-derived microvesicles activating factor XII, vWF–ABO glycosylation, polyhedrocytes reducing clot embologenicity), GSH-based redox scavenging, and immunity ([[TLR9]] binding cell-free DNA, [[Duffy Antigen Receptor for Chemokines|DARC]] chemokine scavenging, extracellular Hb as bactericidal ROS source).

## ACD vs RCD in erythrocytes

- [[Hemolysis]] is the accidental cell death (ACD) of RBCs — mechanical membrane rupture, no signal transduction, therefore not druggable; osmotic fragility is a key parameter, and band 3–aquaporin-1 interaction supports osmotic resistance.
- Lytic death liberates Hb, heme, methemoglobin, ATP, HSP70 and IL-33 as DAMPs, driving TLR4/NF-κB endothelial and macrophage activation, TLR4-independent ROS/heme-iron NET formation, MyD88/TRIF microglial activation, [[NLRP3]]/[[Pyroptosis]] and heme-driven [[Necroptosis]]/[[Ferroptosis]], complement recruitment, and renal hemoprotein filtering injury.
- Only two molecularly defined RCD modalities exist in mature RBCs: eryptosis and erythronecroptosis (term coined for RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL-dependent lytic death; mature RBCs lack TNFR1/TNFR2/TRAIL-R1/DR4/DR5, hence the erythrocyte-specific variant).
- Ferroptosis is unconfirmed in mature RBCs (iron loading, GPX4 present, but the ferroptosis execution/transcriptional machinery is absent); iron overload in hemochromatosis gives PS exposure plus calpain activation — better called "iron overload-driven cell death."

### Eryptosis vs senescence

- Senescent erythrocytes (≈120-day lifespan) show surface-area/volume loss, density increase, reduced deformability, cation loss, desialylation, enzymatic dysregulation, [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], hemichrome, glycated Hb, reduced CD47, and altered protein 4.1a/4.1b ratio; AE1 conformational change creates a senescence-specific antigen.
- Clearance rate is the discriminator: senescent cells take **days**, eryptotic cells **minutes**. Aged cells are nonetheless more eryptosis-prone (oxidation-induced PS exposure), so PS-positive senescent cells should be scored as eryptotic.

## Morphology

- Shrinkage (K+ efflux), blebbing (calpain-mediated cytoskeleton degradation), PS externalization, granularity/SSC change, spiculation, loss of biconcave shape; occasional swelling instead of shrinkage. Morphometric software (Jacob et al.) scores size, irregularity, granularity, central halo.
- [[Anion Exchanger 1|AE1/Band 3]] holds membrane–cytoskeleton connectivity and biconcave shape; its cytoplasmic N-terminal domain is cleaved by caspase-3 and the protein by calpain.

## Signaling

### Ca2+ — the master regulator

- Cytosolic Ca2+ is held at low nM against low-mM extracellular Ca2+ by low resting permeability plus plasma-membrane Ca2+-ATPase and Na+/Ca2+ exchanger; RBCs cannot store Ca2+, so influx through cation channels is obligatory ("cation channel-driven eryptosis").
- Candidate entry routes: TRPC family ([[TRPC6]] in human, TRPC4/5 in mouse; TRPC3 in progenitors), ionotropic glutamate receptors ([[NMDA receptor|NMDA]], AMPA — AMPA antagonism blunts Cl--removal-induced Ca2+ entry), mechanosensitive [[Piezo1]], and voltage-gated Cav2.1. Channels are mostly closed at rest and opened by ROS, hyperosmolarity, or Cl-- removal with gluconate replacement.
- Ca2+ then opens the Gardos channel [[Gardos Channel|KCa3.1/KCNN4]] → K+ efflux, hyperpolarization, Cl- exit, water loss (shrinkage; blocked by charybdotoxin/clotrimazole); inhibits flippase and activates [[Scramblase]] (PS exposure); activates calpain (blebbing, but calpain-1-null mice have unaltered RBC lifespan).

### ROS

- RBC ROS sources differ from nucleated cells: Hb autoxidation (iron-mediated Fenton chemistry), NADPH oxidase, and xanthine oxidoreductase — not mitochondria.
- Defense is GSH/PPP-based: G6PD + 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase supply NADPH for glutathione reductase; the authors propose a "5R" antioxidant pharmacology principle (right species, place, time, level, target).
- ROS-mediated eryptosis ("ROS-mediated eryptosis") is triggered by H2O2, tert-butyl hydroperoxide, Bay 11-7082, parthenolide, dimethyl fumarate (also by direct G6PD inhibition → GSH depletion), carrageenan E407a, desipramine; N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC) blocks it in vitro and prolongs mouse RBC half-life in vivo. UV-activated nanoparticles raise ROS in leukocytes without eryptosis.

### Ceramide

- No ER, so erythrocyte ceramide comes only from plasma-membrane sphingomyelin hydrolysis (de novo synthesis is impossible); sphingosine induces eryptosis, sphingosine-1-phosphate does not (sphingosine kinase detoxifies it).
- Triggers: hyperosmotic shock, secreted SMases during inflammation/sepsis/COVID-19, *C. perfringens* epsilon toxin, platelet-activating factor, cigarette smoke extract (neutral SMase), Cu2+ (leukocyte acidic SMase secretion).
- Downstream in human RBCs: Fas receptor oligomerization in lipid rafts, DISC assembly, cleaved caspase-8/-3, PS exposure and shrinkage — "lipid-driven eryptosis."

### Caspases and the caspase-8 fate switch

- Caspases are largely non-essential for eryptosis: the key Ca2+ rise occurs without caspase-3, and in vitro eryptosis rarely recruits caspase-3 — but ROS-activated caspase-3 does promote PS externalization. Caspases were probably retained for non-apoptotic roles in erythropoiesis/maturation rather than death.
- [[Fas]]/[[FasL]]/FADD/DISC are retained; in aged RBCs Fas colocalizes with lipid-raft markers (Gαs, CD59), oxidative stress drives Fas translocation into rafts and caspase-8/-3 activation (NAC blocks it; calpain-independent). Arsenic- and lead-exposed rat RBCs show Fas aggregation/DISC assembly; organosulfur therapy suppresses ceramide-driven Fas aggregation. Cigarette smoke extract acts through p38 MAPK → DISC.
- Erythrocyte necroptosis forms a RIPK1/FADD/caspase-8 necrosome in which caspase-8 prevents death, mirroring nucleated cells; eryptosis and erythronecroptosis appear mutually exclusive → caspase-8 as the cell-fate switch, arguing RBCs are decision-capable entities between life and death.

### Kinases

| Kinase | Effect on eryptosis |
| --- | --- |
| [[AMPK]] | Anti-eryptotic (energy sensor; AMPKα-null mice: splenomegaly, severe anemia) |
| PAK2 | Required for AMPK-mediated protection |
| cGKI | Anti-eryptotic (NO–sGC–cGMP arm; cGKI-null mice: anemia, splenomegaly) |
| JAK3 | Pro-eryptotic (JAK3-null/inhibited cells show lower PS exposure — anti-eryptotic) |
| MSK1/2 | Anti-eryptotic (MSK-null spleens accumulate eryptotic RBCs) |
| PDK1 | Pro-eryptotic (PDK1-null cells have lower Ca2+) |
| PKC (incl. PKCα) | Pro-eryptotic; ATP depletion drives PKCα membrane translocation |
| CK1α, p38 MAPK, CDK4 | Pro-eryptotic (inhibitors reduce stress-induced eryptosis) |

Note: CK1α, PKC and JAK3 are largely antiapoptotic in nucleated cells but pro-eryptotic in RBCs — an artifact of the truncated effector set.

### Nitric oxide

- NO is a Ca2+-independent suppressor: nitroprusside blocks even ionomycin-induced eryptosis; eNOS-null mice clear RBCs faster. Mechanism: Hb-derived S-nitrosohemoglobin → soluble guanylate cyclase → cGMP → cGKI; dibutyryl-cGMP rescues.

### GTPases

- Rac1/2 maintain RBC cytoskeleton and sickle-cell morphology; NADPH oxidase (Rac-regulated) drives the ROS of sickle RBCs; the Rac1 inhibitor NSC23766 ameliorates oxysterol-induced eryptosis; Rac1 also facilitates *Plasmodium* invasion.

### Energy metabolism

- Metabolism is one reaction chamber: glycolysis, PPP, redox, purine/nucleoside, methionine salvage, glyoxalase, carnitine, membrane transport (~83 enzymes/systems, 129 metabolites). Glucose enters via GLUT1 (also dehydroascorbate); [[Hexokinase-1|HK]] commits it.
- Glucose removal or 2-deoxyglucose → fast ATP loss → PKCα activation → serine phosphorylation of cation channels → Ca2+ rise → PS exposure + shrinkage. AMPK senses this balance. Other ATP-depleting eryptosis triggers: chromium, arsenic, riboflavin/UV during storage, lysophosphatidic acid, vanadate, d-ribose glycation of Hb.

### Eryptosis vs apoptosis

- Shared morphology and triggers, different wiring: no APAF-1, cytochrome c, caspase-2/-6/-7/-9; death-receptor set reduced to Fas/FasL; Ca2+ drives death directly instead of via MOMP; ROS has fewer downstream routes; no lipid-order collapse (no intracellular membranes to exchange cholesterol/phospholipid); no DNA fragmentation, no epigenetic/transcriptional control.

## Physiology and clearance

- Eryptosis is a defense arm: energy depletion, oxidative stress and hyperosmolarity shorten RBC lifespan and clear damaged cells; uncompensated eryptosis causes [[Anemia]], so RBC count reflects the balance of pro- and antieryptotic inputs plus [[Erythropoiesis]].
- Compared with [[Hemolysis]] (unwanted, releases Hb that blocks renal filtration, scavenges NO, drives vasoconstriction/adhesion/endothelial activation), eryptosis is non-lytic and pre-emptive.
- Clearance routes: PS "eat-me" signal read by macrophages (efferocytosis, minutes); desialylation via bacterial neuraminidase also provokes PS exposure; immune clearance of senescent cells by germline-encoded naturally occurring IgG autoantibodies (NOAbs), frequently against [[Anion Exchanger 1|AE1]]; extracellular histone-driven aggregation, splenic retention and ghost removal.
- Hepatic (not splenic) sequestration dominates in pathology: hepatic sinusoidal PS-dependent sequestration via stabilin-1/2 on sinusoidal endothelial cells, [[Kupffer Cell|Kupffer-cell]] engulfment into erythrophagolysosomes, removal of ~80% of RBC-derived vesicles within 5 min via scavenger receptors; [[CD47]]–SIRPα is the "don't-eat-me" brake.
- Immunogenicity: no evidence for DAMP release in eryptosis (membrane stays intact), but eryptosis drives extracellular-vesicle formation (oxidative stress/Ca2+/PS asymmetry) whose EV-associated DAMPs are found in stored red-cell concentrates; PS-dependent efferocytosis reprograms macrophages toward an antiinflammatory/antioxidant, heme-oxygenase-1-upregulated phenotype — protective in experimental intracerebral hemorrhage.

## Disease roles

- **Renal:** enhanced eryptosis is a fundamental contributor to renal anemia in [[Chronic Kidney Disease]] (uremic toxins, inflammation, oxidative stress, energy depletion, Ca2+ imbalance) and ESRD on hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis. Uremic triggers: indoxyl sulfate (Ca2+ rise, scrambling, ceramide; OAT2/NADPH oxidase, GSH-independent), acrolein (ceramide → Ca2+), indole-3-acetic acid, urea, p-cresol, vanadate (ATP/glycolysis inhibition), IL-1β/IL-6. Higher rates in stages G4–G5; PTH independently predicts eryptosis in HD; in PD, rates rise with loss of residual diuresis and rGFR and correlate with dialysate volume (glucose-based dialysate), and peritonitis triples eryptosis with correlations to CRP, IL-6, IL-1β and effluent NGAL.
- **Hepatic:** liver is the main RBC bank/iron-recycling organ; enhanced eryptosis in hepatic failure, hyperbilirubinemia and hepatitis B acute-on-chronic liver failure; bilirubin/bile acids act directly and a vicious cycle forms (RBC loss → more bilirubin → Ca2+ influx, SMase, ceramide → more eryptosis); albumin buffers bilirubin toxicity; PS exposure shortens coagulation time (cirrhosis).
- **Neurological:** altered calpain/ceramide production triggers eryptosis in [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's]]; amyloid-β causes oxidative RBC damage, membrane phospholipid disruption and volume loss via ceramide in [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's]].
- **Cardiovascular/metabolic:** untreated [[Hypertension|hypertension]] shows erythrocyte lipid peroxidation, depleted GSH and reduced CAT/SOD/GPx; T1DM/T2DM hyperglycemia raises PS-positive RBCs, methylglyoxal inhibits glycolysis (ATP/GSH loss), β2-microglobulin adds PS exposure; PS-positive erythrocytes are an independent risk factor for anemia in dialysis patients.
- **Autoimmune:** SLE patients show higher PS exposure, cytosolic Ca2+, ROS and shrinkage; SLE serum induces eryptosis in healthy RBCs. In autoimmune hemolytic anemia, eryptosis is driven mainly by cold IgM/IgA (complement C5-dependent, C8 supporting, C9 dispensable) rather than warm IgG; [[Antiphospholipid Syndrome|APS]] autoantibodies induce eryptosis and may explain the prothrombotic state. [[Erythropoietin]] both stimulates erythropoiesis and inhibits eryptosis — though sustained high EPO makes cells more eryptosis-prone.
- **COVID-19:** endotheliopathy, microclots, NETs, complement deposition on RBCs, ↑RDW and sPLA2-IIA; [[Long COVID]] plasma carries fibrin amyloid microclots coating erythrocytes, with stiffened, non-discocytic, less deformable cells — an under-measured microcirculatory/oxidative-stress marker.
- **Sepsis:** septic plasma induces eryptosis within 15 min, correlating with Endotoxin Activity Assay, oxidative stress and inflammation; neutrophil/macrophage ROS/RNS, neuraminidase activity, and plasma EVs reduce deformability — eryptosis tracks severity and prognosis.
- **Cancer:** lung cancer anemia is driven by RBC turnover rather than reduced erythropoiesis, with paradoxically elevated EPO and enhanced eryptosis; cytostatics (topotecan, cisplatin) and tamoxifen are pro-eryptotic; PS-positive RBCs add a procoagulant/endothelial burden. Caution: inhibiting eryptosis might interfere with tumor-cell apoptosis.
- **Malaria:** *Plasmodium*-infected RBCs generate ~2× more radicals, open nonselective cation channels (Na+/Ca2+ entry also favors parasite growth) and undergo eryptosis, so infected cells — and the parasite — are cleared. Eryptosis-promoting traits (G6PD deficiency, sickle cell anemia, thalassemia trait, GLUT1/AE1 deficiency) confer partial protection; pro-eryptotic xenobiotics (lead, paclitaxel, cyclosporine, PGE2, curcumin, amphotericin B, dimethyl fumarate, chlorpromazine) also slow parasitemia.
- **Inherited blood disorders:** sickle cell anemia (PS exposure, ROS, Ca2+, RBC microparticles; cumene hydroperoxide pro-oxidant vs NAC), thalassemia, G6PD deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis (PS exposure highest in young cells).
- **Aging:** eryptosis and senescence are distinct (minutes vs days) but interact — old cells are more oxidation-primed; RBC indices (MCHC, MCV, metabolism) drift with human age.

## Pollutants, toxicology and nanotoxicology

- **Lead (occupational):** 88.3% lower δ-ALAD, 2.4× TBARS, 32.8% lower GSH/GSSG, elevated Ca2+, μ-calpain activation, 2.82% vs 0.1% PS exposure; PLA2 activity mediates better than SMase (PLA2/PGE2 axis); in vitro lead acts ROS-independently via Ca2+ and caspase-3.
- **Metals:** aluminum (PS exposure, Ca2+ rise, AE1 degradation; EPO protective), nickel (0.5–10 mM NiCl2: Ca2+ overload, ROS, p38 MAPK-dependent lytic death), chromium VI (≥10 µM: Ca2+ rise, ATP depletion, PS exposure without ceramide), rotenone (Ca2+, ceramide, shrinkage, annexin V).
- **Substitutes:** BPA/BPF/BPS/BPAF all pro-eryptotic at occupational/subacute concentrations — BPS is not a safe BPA replacement; brominated flame retardants pro-eryptotic at environmental concentrations (0.001–0.01 µg/ml) via ROS without calpain/Ca2+, TBBPA > TBBPS (so TBBPS substitution is validated); organophosphate flame retardants far less erythrotoxic (TCPP > TCEP).
- **Metabolites:** phthalate parent compounds (DBP/BBP) are more hemolytic/eryptotic than metabolites (MBP/MBzP).
- **Pesticides:** DEET (1–5 mM, Ca2+-dependent shrinkage and PS exposure) and bromfenvinphos (≥100 µM, ROS + Ca2+).
- **Cigarette smoke:** PS externalization higher in smokers (21 vs 21 study; 2023 cohort: 418 smokers vs 1000 never vs 605 ex-smokers), dose-dependent with daily cigarettes, correlating with CRP and erythrocyte GSH; CSE mechanism = p38 MAPK → DISC → caspase-8/-3 via neutral SMase/ceramide.
- **Nanomaterials:** eryptosis is proposed as a more sensitive, reproducible, mechanistically informative hemocompatibility endpoint than hemolysis (>110 compounds trigger eryptosis below the hemolytic threshold); triggers include citrate- and PVP-coated Ag NPs, Si NPs, GdVO4:Eu3+, CTAB-coated Au nanorods, antimicrobial nanofibers, Fe3O4, TiO2, SOD-CAT-PLGA, dextran-polyacrylamide Ag NPs; internalized and non-internalized (pristine SiO2, LaVO4:Eu3+ — Piezo1-linked Ca2+) particles both trigger it; smaller CeO2 NPs are more potent; coatings reduce eryptosis.

## How to study eryptosis

- **Samples:** ethics approval + Declaration of Helsinki; EDTA/citrate/heparin anticoagulation; exclude known modulators of RBC survival; ≥3 independent experiments × 2–3 technical replicates (6–9 readings); PS exposure is independent of BMI, age, gender and ABO/Rh.
- **Isolation:** density-gradient centrifugation (2500 RPM, 15 min, RT); verify purity by CD235a+/CD45−; hematocrit 0.4–30% published (low density primes hemolysis/eryptosis); Ringer-type buffers preferred over RPMI (nutrient-poor vs rich) — effect of media under-studied.
- **Markers (eryptosis):** annexin V–FITC by flow cytometry (gold standard); aminophospholipid translocase activity via NBD-PS; FSC/MCV shrinkage; SSC granularity; microscopy; Fluo-4/AM or X-Rhod-1 Ca2+; ROS panel (DCF, Amplex Red H2O2, specific superoxide/hydroxyl/singlet-oxygen probes, BODIPY lipid peroxides, protein carbonylation/sulfenylation, 3-nitrotyrosine, CAT/SOD, GSH/GSSG/GPx/GST/GR via mercury orange, 5-CMFDA total thiols, TAC); μ-calpain (CMAC); NO (DAF-FM); energetics (glucose uptake, HK/PFK-1/PK, G6PD/transaldolase/transketolase, 2,3-DPG, ATP/ADP, NAD(H)/NADP(H), lactate, pH, Na+/K+-ATPase); B12/folate; membrane potential (DiOC2(3)); membrane protein electrophoresis/WB; β-galactosidase (senescence); Phen Green SK (iron); ESR aggregation.
- **Markers (hemolysis):** supernatant Hb at 405 nm, Drabkin's reagent (all Hb except sulfhemoglobin), K+/LDH/AST release, and creatine kinase as a surrogate for membrane rupture (cytosolic metabolites inflate CK readouts).
- **Markers (necroptosis):** p-RIPK1/p-RIPK3/p-MLKL necrosome assembly by WB/ELISA; p-Syk/p-Src; death blocked by Nec-1 (RIPK1), GSK'872/HS-1731 (RIPK3), NSA (MLKL), BAY 61-3606 (Syk), PPI (Src), and **not** by Z-VAD-FMK.
- **Caveat:** no genetic manipulation is possible in RBCs, so pharmacology is the only tool; negative inhibitor results do not exclude a pathway, and inhibitors have off-target effects — confirm target protein levels by WB/ELISA alongside.

## Therapeutic exploitation

- **Targets:** cytosolic Ca2+, ceramide, ROS, NO/cGMP, energy metabolism, PS externalization, ion channels, Fas signaling, kinases, caspases. Since the harmful downstream effects are mainly driven by PS exposure, eryptosis-targeting drugs should prevent PS externalization.
- **Induction:** >110 inducers documented (metals, drugs, kinase inhibitors, toxins, alkaloids, xenobiotics); teriflunomide both induces tumor apoptosis and inhibits eryptosis (anemia mitigation); β-cryptoxanthin is pro-eryptotic and hemolytic — a candidate anti-malarial.
- **Inhibition:** antioxidants/antiinflammatory/ion-modulating agents (NAC, organosulfur compounds), EPO, dietary functional foods; targets for hemolytic anemias, CKD and metabolic syndromes. Human in vivo confirmation is still missing.
- **Nomenclature recommendations:** cation channel-driven eryptosis (Ca2+ influx), ROS-mediated eryptosis, lipid-driven eryptosis, extrinsic eryptosis (Fas), caspase-dependent eryptosis, RBC enzyme deficiency-dependent erythrocyte death, "iron overload-driven cell death" for the unconfirmed ferroptosis-like mode.
- **Open problems:** how ROS selects cell fate; whether Ca2+ elevation is the irreversible commitment point; downstream effectors of eryptotic kinases; platelet-vs-erythrocyte comparison (platelet intrinsic apoptosis uses mitochondrial apoptosome caspase-9/-3, Bcl-XL/Bak/Bax, and lacks Fas); diagnostic/prognostic value of eryptosis as a cardiovascular risk biomarker.

## Conclusion

[[Eryptosis]] is a genuine, Ca2+-centered RCD of anucleate cells, distinguishable from [[Apoptosis]], senescence, [[Hemolysis]] and [[Erythronecroptosis]]; its druggability hinges on preventing PS exposure, and the field's biggest gaps are in vivo/animal validation, immunogenic consequences, and standardized marker panels.
