## Overview

Executioner (effector) caspases are the terminal proteases of the apoptotic cascade. In mammals the core executioners are **[[Caspase-3]]** and **[[Caspase-7]]** (with caspase-6 acting on select substrates). Once activated by initiator caspases (caspase-8, -9, -10 via the death-receptor or mitochondrial/intrinsic pathways), they cleave hundreds of cellular substrates, producing the morphological and biochemical hallmarks of **[[Apoptosis]]**.

## Activation and Mechanism

- **Initiator → executioner relay**: Caspase-9 (apoptosome) or caspase-8/-10 (DISC) process and activate executioner procaspases by cleavage at intersubunit linkers.
- **Substrate cleavage**: Executioners cleave structural (e.g., lamin, actin), repair (PARP), and regulatory proteins, fragmenting DNA (via CAD activation), collapsing the cytoskeleton, and exposing phosphatidylserine.
- **Amplification**: A modest initiator signal is amplified into rapid, irreversible cellular dismantling.

> [!important] Executioner caspase activation is the point of no return in apoptosis; upstream BCL-2 family and IAP regulation determines whether the threshold is reached.

## Distinction from Inflammatory Caspases

Unlike the inflammatory caspases (e.g., caspase-1, -4, -5, -11 that activate [[Gasdermin D]] and drive pyroptosis), executioner caspases mediate silent, immunologically contained cell death rather than inflammatory rupture. The broader **[[Caspases|Caspase]]** family spans both branches.

## Physiological and Pathological Roles

Executioner caspases enforce developmental pruning, tissue homeostasis, and elimination of damaged/oncogenic cells. Defective execution (e.g., caspase-3 loss) impairs apoptosis and can promote genomic instability, while excessive execution contributes to neurodegeneration and tissue atrophy. In aging, senescent-cell resistance to executioner caspase killing (via SCAPs) is a barrier to clearance.
