# PAANIB-1

**PAANIB-1** is a brain-penetrant, selective small-molecule inhibitor of the nuclease function of [[MIF]] (PAAN, parthanatos-associated AIF nuclease). It is a pharmacological probe for the terminal DNA-fragmentation step of [[Parthanatos]] and a neuroprotection candidate.

## Mechanism and use

- Selectively inhibits the PD-D/E(X)K nuclease activity of [[MIF]] that, after [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]]-dependent nuclear co-translocation, executes large-scale DNA fragmentation.
- Spares MIF's tautomerase and cytokine activities and does not broadly inhibit other nucleases (e.g. APE1), separating the parthanatotic nuclease arm from MIF's inflammatory functions.
- Brain-penetrant and protective in models of [[Parkinson's Disease]] (α-synuclein preformed fibrils, MPTP), supporting MIF/PAAN as a druggable terminal effector rather than only an upstream node.
- In the 2022 *Cell* study (Park et al.), it prevented neurodegeneration and spared upstream PAR formation and AIF–MIF binding.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Parthanatos Moura 2024 molecular mechanisms more questions than answers|Moura et al. 2024]]
> The review cites a selective MIF-nuclease inhibitor that protects cells from parthanatos in a mouse model of parkinsonism as key evidence that MIF/PAAN is the DNA-fragmenting executioner downstream of AIF.
