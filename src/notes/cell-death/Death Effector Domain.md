# Death Effector Domain

The **death effector domain (DED)** is the ~100-residue C-terminal α-helical domain of [[FADD]], procaspase-8/10, the [[c-FLIP]] isoforms and the viral [[vFLIP]] proteins. DED–DED dimerization is the physical step that converts a trimerized death receptor (or a deubiquitinated [[RIPK1]]) into an activated caspase-8 platform, and it is the interaction on which the two competing models of the [[DISC]] — a long "death-effector filament" versus a short chain — are built.

## Structure

Two tandem DEDs form a compact helical bundle that dimerizes through a hydrophobic interface, with each DED contributing to the interface so that the dimer is roughly twofold symmetric. The domain is structurally distinct from the [[FADD]] death domain, which binds the death-domain tails of [[Fas]], [[DR4]], [[DR5]] and [[TNFR1]].

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Adaptor-mediated recruitment.** [[FADD]] presents its DED to the DED of procaspase-8/10 and of [[c-FLIP]], so the initiator caspase reaches the receptor-bound adaptor by dimerization rather than by enzymatic reaction.
- **Filament versus chain.** When overexpressed in cells, the DEDs of procaspase-8, FADD and c-FLIP form filament-like "death-effector filaments", supporting a model in which procaspase-8 chains through repeated DED–DED contacts. Quantitative work at native DISCs contradicts the long-filament picture: mass spectrometry finds several-fold more procaspase-8 than FADD with little c-FLIP (Dickens; Schleich), and quantitative Western blotting caps (procaspase-8 + c-FLIP):FADD near 2:1 (Majkut).
- **Two-step activation.** DED-level assembly permits procaspase-8 prodomain cleavage within a dimer; DED-chain clustering then drives interdimer cleavage of the enzymatic domain and cytoplasmic release of fully processed caspase-8 (Kallenberger; Majkut).
- **Isoform capping.** [[c-FLIP|FLIP_S]] and [[c-FLIP|FLIP_R]] incorporate into the chain, bind procaspase-8 without activating it, and cap chain elongation; the N-terminal prodomain of procaspase-8 left behind after complete processing is structurally related to FLIP_S/R and forms a negative-feedback brake on further procaspase-8 activation (Schleich).

> DEDs are the shared docking module of the whole death-receptor complex family: the viral FLIPs captured a host DED module (two DEDs, no caspase domain), which is the evolutionary origin of the cellular [[c-FLIP]] isoforms. The same DED chain is assembled by the receptor-independent [[Ripoptosome]], where FLIP_L heterodimers cleave and inactivate [[RIPK1]] (survival) whereas FLIP_S heterodimers cannot, leaving RIPK1 to hand off to [[RIPK3]]–[[MLKL]] ([[Necroptosis]]).

## Physiological Function

DED–DED assembly is the physical common denominator of death-receptor apoptosis, ripoptosome formation and caspase-8 activation on pattern-recognition receptor platforms (for example the atypical [[TLR3]]–[[TRIF]]–caspase-8 complex, which requires [[RIPK1]] but not FADD).

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Therapeutic anchoring.** The DED interface is the docking site for the [[vFLIP]] viral mimics and for stapled peptides designed to displace [[c-FLIP|FLIP_L]] from death receptors.
- **Viral persistence.** KSHV K13 and other vFLIPs occupy the DED interface to block death-receptor apoptosis while activating NF-κB.
- **Druggability.** Because the DED interface is shared between FADD, procaspase-8 and c-FLIP, it is the point at which isoform composition translates into a pharmacological outcome.

## Connections

- [[FADD]] — the adaptor whose DED nucleates the DED chain
- [[Caspase-8]] — initiator caspase recruited and activated through its DEDs
- [[Caspase-10]] — second initiator caspase with tandem DEDs; absent in rodents
- [[c-FLIP]] — catalytically dead DED-bearing paralog whose isoforms cap or extend the chain
- [[vFLIP]] — viral DED mimics of c-FLIP
- [[DISC]] — the receptor-bound platform assembled through DED–DED dimerization
- [[Ripoptosome]] — the receptor-independent counterpart built on the same DED chain
- [[TLR3]] / [[TRIF]] — RIPK1-dependent, FADD-independent caspase-8 activation platform
- [[Homodimerization]] — the dimerization chemistry that the DED interface exemplifies
