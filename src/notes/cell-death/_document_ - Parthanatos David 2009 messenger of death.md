# Parthanatos, a messenger of death (David et al. 2009)

Foundational review arguing [[PAR]] polymer — not [[NAD+]] consumption — is the cytotoxic effector downstream of [[PARP1]] overactivation, acting via mitochondrial [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] release.

> [!note]
> Thesis — [[PARP1]] supports life (repair/transcription/mitosis) at low activity and delivers death at overactivation; the death arm is [[Parthanatos]]: complex [[PAR]] → [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] translocation → chromatinolysis.

## Background: PARPs and [[PAR]] synthesis

- 17-member PARP family (catalytic NAD+-fold homology to bacterial ADP-ribosylating toxins); [[PARP1]] is the stroke/diabetes/inflammation/neurodegeneration-implicated member.
- [[PARP1]] domains: DNA-binding zinc zippers, bipartite NLS with caspase-cleavage site, automodification/BRCT domain, C-terminal catalytic NAD+-fold. Uses [[NAD+]] → ADP-ribose + nicotinamide; ester linkage to Glu/Asp; 2'-1' ribose-ribose polymerization with ~1 branch per 20–50 units; length/branching vary by stimulus.
- [[PAR]] half-life set by [[PARG]] and ARH3 lyase cleavage of glycosidic bonds; structural heterogeneity (free/bound, linear/branched, short/long; up to 200–400 units) may encode life-vs-death specificity across PARPs.

## [[PARP1]] in life and death

- Mild damage → 500x catalytic activation, PARylation of histones/helicases/HMG/topoisomerases/repair factors/transcription factors → repair. Excess genotoxic stress → excess [[PAR]] → death.
- [[PARP1]] KOs: hypersensitive to alkylators/radiation (repair defect) but protected from LPS shock, ischemia, MPTP excitotoxicity. [[PARP2]] KO protects focal but not global ischemia; double KO embryonic lethal — distinct polymer roles hypothesized.

## Regulator of death: excitotoxicity → [[NAD+]] debate → [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]]

- Excitotoxic chain: [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] overactivation → Ca2+ → nNOS → NO + superoxide → peroxynitrite → DNA damage → [[PARP1]]; PARP inhibitors and [[PARP1]] KO resist stroke/[[NMDA receptor|NMDA]]/MPTP.
- Suicide (NAD+) hypothesis audit: [[NAD+]]/[[ATP]] fall and glycolysis block (GAPDH) are real, and pyruvate/alpha-ketoglutarate or [[NAD+]]/Namnt repletion can partially rescue — but mitochondrial [[NAD+]] stays physiological during genotoxic stress, [[BAX]]/calpain KOs survive MNNG despite massive [[NAD+]] drops, and infarct protection in [[PARP1]] KO occurs without energy-status difference (Goto 2002; Paschen). Conclusion: depletion insufficient; [[SIRT1]]-related [[NAD+]] effects are a parallel axis.
- [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] biology: 67 kDa precursor → 62 kDa mature (MLS cleavage) → 57 kDa post-stimulus form; FAD/NADH-binding glutathione-reductase-like fold, NADH-oxidase/superoxide chemistry; Complex-I maintenance and redox scavenging (Harlequin: −18% Complex I, −30% catalase, late cerebellar/retinal degeneration, oxidative stress).
- [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] kills from the nucleus (chromatin condensation/fragmentation via [[Cyclophilin A]], caspase-inhibitor-resistant; blocked by neutralizing antibodies, cysteine-protease inhibition, HSP70 overexpression). Nuclear translocation documented across excitotoxicity, [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's]], perinatal hypoxia-ischemia, trauma, stroke; [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] knockdown/Harlequin reduces infarct.

## [[Parthanatos]]: [[PAR]] toxicity, properties, [[PARG]] genetics, [[Mitochondria]]

- [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] neurons and MNNG [[HeLa Cells|HeLa]] cells surge [[PAR]]; immunodepletion or [[PARG]] overexpression reduces death; [[PARG]] transgenics smaller MCAO infarcts, heterozygotes larger. MNNG-nuclei-derived [[PAR]] releases [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] from isolated [[Mitochondria]]; delivered [[PAR]] induces [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] translocation; [[PARG]]/phosphodiesterase I catabolism abolishes release; [[PARG]] overexpression prevents [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]]-induced translocation.
- Properties: shares apoptotic/necrotic cytology via distinct mechanism; caspase-independent; nucleus→cytosol [[PAR]] migration → [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] translocation. Toxicity threshold ~20 nM, near-complete killing ~80 nM for >60-mer; poly(A) charge control nontoxic; lengths achieved in [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]]/MNNG models.
- [[PARG]] genetics: 110 kDa nuclear, 102/99 kDa cytosolic (human), 59–60 kDa isoform; exon 2–3 deletion (59 kDa intact) viable with elevated [[PAR]]/infarcts plus compensatory 65 kDa mitochondrial form; exon 4 null lethal E3.5 (trophoblasts survive only with benzamide; hypersensitive to MNNG/menadione); Drosophila loss-of-function larval-lethal/neurodegenerative. [[PARP1]]/[[PARP2]] redundancy and PARP-2-specific viable phenotypes discussed.
- [[PAR]]→[[Mitochondria]]: outer-membrane permeabilization vs permeability-transition pore (ANT/[[VDAC]]/[[Cyclophilin D]]; cyclosporin A/bongkrekic acid data; [[Cyclophilin D]]-KO ischemia protection) debated; [[BAX]] KO compromises [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] release but may only delay (12-h window; [[Bcl-2]] overexpression precedent); calpain cleavage of [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] required for full release in some models (calpain-site mutant, calpastatin protection; MNNG ordering calpain→[[BAX]]) but [[PAR]]→calpain→[[BAX]] linkage unmapped. Non-covalent [[PAR]] binding (acid/chaotrope/detergent/salt-resistant; branched > long linear > short; H1 > H2A > H2B = H3 > H4) as candidate transducer; systematic [[PAR]]-target identification proposed as next step.

## Conclusion

[[PAR]]-induced [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] translocation defines parthanatos; open questions are the [[PAR]]→permeabilization/calpain/[[BAX]] wiring and the druggable [[PAR]]-binding targets.
