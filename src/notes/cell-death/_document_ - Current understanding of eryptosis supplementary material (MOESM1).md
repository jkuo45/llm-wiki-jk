# Supplementary Material — Current understanding of eryptosis (Tkachenko et al. 2025)

Supplementary material (MOESM1) for the consensus review [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025]], companion document to the main text. Contains five tables: [[Eryptosis]] assay buffer formulations (Table S1), [[Eryptosis]] modulators by target (Table S2), approaches for studying eryptosis inhibition (Table S3), compounds that significantly induce eryptosis with concentration/time/mechanism annotations (Table S4), and major inhibitors with their effect readouts (Table S5).

> [!note]
> Confirmation of eryptosis in these protocols still requires elevated intracellular Ca2+ **plus** phosphatidylserine externalization; Table S2 separates inhibitors/activators by target class (surface proteins, eryptosis mediators, necroptosis mediators, ROS/RNS neutralization), while Tables S4/S5 annotate each compound with dose, incubation time, Ca2+ influx and ceramide formation (+/blank) and other readouts.

> [!note]
> Note — All values are in g/L. Dextran and PEG are in w/v. Osmolarity is in mOsm/L.
> Combinations of the above ingredients are possible (e.g., Ca2+-free and K+-rich) provided that the resulting buffer remains isosmotic. This requires modifying the concentrations of each ingredient based on its nature (a penetrating or a non-penetrating solute) and on the intended purpose.
> Unique interactions with the studied toxicant must be borne in mind when formulating these buffers as lower or higher concentrations of the added ingredient(s) may be required.
> It is important to consider that some ingredients may be antihemolytic but proeryptotic (e.g., PEG) and vice versa.
> Urea, sucrose, glucose, and mannitol may be increased to study the behavior of the compound of interest under conditions of hyperosmotic stress.
> Lipid-rich bovine serum albumin “Albumax” may be added at 5 g/L as it contains pro-survival factors and has a drug-binding capacity1.
> ATP may be added at 0.12 or 0.25 g/L2.
> Caffeine may be added at 0.1 g/L3.
> Uric acid (0.168 g/L) may also be added as it demonstrates cytoprotective effects4.
