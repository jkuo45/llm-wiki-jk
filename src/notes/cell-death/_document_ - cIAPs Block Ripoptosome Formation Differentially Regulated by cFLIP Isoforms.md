# cIAPs Block Ripoptosome Formation, a [[RIPK1|RIP1]]/[[Caspase-8]] Containing Intracellular Cell Death Complex Differentially Regulated by [[c-FLIP|cFLIP]] Isoforms

**Summary**

## Abstract

The intracellular regulation of cell death pathways by [[cIAPs]] has been enigmatic.

> [!important] Spontaneous Ripoptosome assembly
> Loss of cIAPs promotes the spontaneous formation of an intracellular platform activating either [[Apoptosis]] or [[Necroptosis]]. This 2 MDa complex designated [[Ripoptosome]] is necessary but not sufficient for cell death.

It contains [[RIPK1]], [[FADD]], [[Caspase-8]], [[Caspase-10]], and [[c-FLIP]] isoforms.
[[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] prevents Ripoptosome formation, whereas, intriguingly, [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] promotes Ripoptosome assembly.

> [!tip] Caspase activity as the “rheostat”
> When [[cIAPs]] are absent, caspase activity is the “rheostat” that is controlled by [[c-FLIP]] isoforms in the Ripoptosome and decides if cell death occurs by [[RIPK3|RIP3]]-dependent [[Necroptosis]] or caspase-dependent [[Apoptosis]].

[[RIPK1]] is the core component of the complex. As exemplified by our studies for TLR3 activation, our data argue that the Ripoptosome critically influences the outcome of membrane-bound receptor triggering. The differential quality of cell death mediated by the Ripoptosome may cause important pathophysiological consequences during inflammatory responses.

## Updates since 2011

> [!info] Scope of this update
> Compiled by web search on 19 November 2026 from indexed abstracts, reviews, and clinical reports. Claims are attributed to their sources; quantitative values are stated only where the source reports them. Primary data behind the 2011 paper were not re-analysed.

### The ripoptosome concept was absorbed into the TNF receptor 1 complex II framework

The 2011 nomenclature has not been retained in its original sense. ==Current usage of “ripoptosome” refers most often to the TNF receptor 1 complex II assembly (RIPK1–[[FADD]]–[[Caspase-8]]–[[c-FLIP]]), which assembles when cIAP1/2-mediated ubiquitination of [[RIPK1]] is blocked;== the 2 MDa, TNF-independent platform characterised here is described in reviews as a complex IIb-type variant or as a TRIF-recruited necrosome (Kashkar and Pasparakis, *Molecular Cell* 2025; Newton et al., *Cell* 2024; *Frontiers in Immunology* 2026, necroptosis network review). The paper's central mechanistic claims — loss of [[cIAPs]] licenses a [[RIPK1]] scaffold complex, and [[Caspase-8]] activity within that complex is the switch between [[Apoptosis]] and [[Necroptosis]] — are retained in current models of TNF signalling.

### cFLIP isoform mechanism is now defined structurally

Cryo-EM of the FADD–[[Caspase-8]] complex (Fox et al., *Nature Communications* 2021) and of death effector domain assembly in FADD–procaspase-8–[[c-FLIP]] complexes (Yang et al., *Nature Communications* 2024) provide a structural basis for the differential behaviour reported here: [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] perturbs [[Caspase-8]] DED filament formation, while [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]]–[[Caspase-8]] heterodimers retain catalytic activity at a level sufficient for selected substrate cleavage (including [[RIPK1]]) but not for full caspase-8 activation. This supports the “rheostat” framing of the 2011 paper at the structural level.

### In vivo genetics refine the isoform claims

Shaw et al. (*PNAS* 2025;122:e2415992122) reported that FLIP(L) permits apoptotic and inflammatory signalling and inhibits necroptosis in mice without [[Caspase-8]] oligomerization, consistent with the 2011 conclusion that [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] restrains [[Necroptosis]] while allowing limited caspase activity. Newton et al. (*Cell Death & Differentiation* 2025) reported that cFLIP suppresses caspase-1- and [[MLKL]]-independent perinatal lethality driven by auto-processing-impaired [[Caspase-8]] D387A. The 2011 question of how the stoichiometric ratio of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] and [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] sets the outcome remains unresolved and is now usually framed as the ratio of [[Caspase-8]] to [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]].

### TLR3-induced death is not RIPK1-dependent in most cell types

TLR3- and TLR4-triggered [[Necroptosis]] generally proceeds through a [[TRIF]]–[[RIPK3]] necrosome, with [[RIPK3]] recruited through the TRIF RHIM independently of [[RIPK1]]; a [[RIPK1]] requirement has been reported in macrophages but not in fibroblasts or endothelial cells (Kaiser et al. 2013; *Experimental & Molecular Medicine* 2022 review). This qualifies the 2011 observation that [[RIPK1]] knockdown fully protected HaCaT cells from poly(I:C)-induced death: [[RIPK1]] dependence is cell-type specific, and TRIF–[[RIPK3]] can bypass it. [[TRIF]] is now itself recognised as a necroptosis scaffold, and its proteasomal turnover terminates TLR3/4 necroptosis (Zfand5-dependent [[TRIF]] degradation, *Cell Death & Disease* 2026).

### RIPK1 is not only a scaffold, and RIPK3 detection remains difficult

[[RIPK1]] stabilises the human [[ZBP1]]–[[RIPK3]] complex and promotes its polymerisation into detergent-insoluble amyloid structures, and is required for ZBP1-driven necroptosis in human cells (Amusan et al., *PLoS Biology* 2025;23:e3002845). The same polymerised, detergent-insoluble assembly is a reported feature of [[RIPK3]]-containing [[necrosome]]s, which is consistent with the 2011 report that [[RIPK3]] could not be detected in the ripoptosome by standard immunoprecipitation.

### IAP antagonism in cancer: clinical outcomes

The translational proposal made in 2011 — combining [[TLR3]] agonism with [[Inhibitor of Apoptosis Proteins|IAP]] antagonists — remains preclinical; no [[TLR3]] agonist is in clinical use as an anticancer agent. The IAP antagonists themselves have produced limited clinical benefit. Birinapant phase I/II studies reported no complete or partial responses with stable disease as the best response in a minority of patients. Xevinapant (Debio 1143) improved overall survival in a phase 2 study in unresectable locally advanced head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (reported 2022), while an interim analysis of the phase 3 TrilynX study indicated the event-free survival goal was unlikely to be met. The strategy of combining a Smac mimetic with a pan-caspase inhibitor to route cells into [[Necroptosis]] was explored preclinically (Brumatti et al.) but has not become standard care.

### RIPK1 inhibitors: clinical translation has lagged the mechanism

Necrostatin-1 and [[Necrostatin-1s]] remain standard preclinical tools. Clinically, GSK2982772 showed no benefit in rheumatoid arthritis or ulcerative colitis and its development was discontinued; DNL747 was paused in favour of DNL788; SAR443820 produced no clinical benefit in the phase 2 HIMALAYA trial in ALS, with elevated hepatic enzymes as the most common cause, and further development in ALS was not pursued (*Lancet Neurology* 2026;25:900–910). Phase 2 studies of SAR443820 in multiple sclerosis and Alzheimer’s disease continue. The finding of this paper that [[RIPK1]] kinase activity is required for ripoptosome-driven [[Necroptosis]] has therefore not yet translated into an approved therapy.

### cFLIP has become a therapeutic target in its own right

In B16F10 melanoma, [[c-FLIP]] knockout removed resistance to [[TNFR1]]-induced [[Apoptosis]] and prevented tumour growth in immunocompetent mice, whereas ablation of [[cIAPs]] or of [[RIPK1]] did not alter melanoma growth (Stachelscheid et al., *Cell Death & Disease* 2026;17:671). This inverts the therapeutic emphasis of the 2011 paper: rather than removing [[cIAPs]] to unleash [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]]-controlled [[Caspase-8]] activity, current work targets [[c-FLIP]] directly to permit it. [[c-FLIP]] is likewise a dependency in activated B-cell-like diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, where B-cell-specific CFLAR deletion prevents tumour formation. Small molecules that disrupt the [[Caspase-8]]/[[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] heterodimer (“FLIPins”) enhance death-receptor-induced [[Necroptosis]] and [[Apoptosis]] in pancreatic and colorectal cancer models (*Frontiers in Cell and Developmental Biology* 2024; *Communications Biology* 2024).

### Points from 2011 that remain unresolved

- The stoichiometry of [[Caspase-8]], [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]], and [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] within the assembled complex, which the paper proposed as the determinant of death quality.
- The identity of the [[RIPK1]] ubiquitin chains installed by [[cIAPs]] on the “modified” [[RIPK1]] pool and the mechanism that sequesters [[RIPK1]] into a 2 MDa compartment; related work now implicates USP13-mediated [[cIAPs]] stabilisation and [[SMURF1]]/USP5 control of [[RIPK3]] ubiquitination.
- Whether spontaneous, [[TNFR1]]-independent complex assembly occurs in human primary cells; the 2011 data were obtained in transformed epithelial lines and primary keratinocytes.
- Whether the [[RIPK3]]–[[MLKL]]–[[Necroptosis]] axis downstream of this platform is a drug target distinct from [[RIPK1]] kinase inhibition, given the failure of [[RIPK1]] inhibitors in ALS and ulcerative colitis.

**Highlights**

► [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] promotes [[Necroptosis]] in the absence of [[cIAPs]]

## Introduction

==Cell death pathways are regulated at several different levels. Initiation points of these signals are organelles such as the mitochondria. Alternatively there are numerous examples of signaling platforms in cells named death-inducing signaling complex ([[DISC]]), [[TNFR1 complex II]], [[Apoptosome]], PIDDosome, toll-like receptor (TLR) complexes, or the [[Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptor|RIG-I]] complex that can initiate cell death.== The longstanding observations that identical death stimuli such as [[TNFR1]], [[TLR3]] ligands, or death ligands may also activate programmed necrosis ([[Necroptosis]]) depending on the cellular context complicate the understanding of cell death platforms. ==A contribution of another intracellular death platform containing [[RIPK1]] was proposed to be induced by excessive DNA damage requiring autocrine TNF secretion. Other studies showed that necroptosis induced by [[TNFR1]] requires receptor-interacting protein 3 ([[RIPK3]]), which is phosphorylated and activated by [[RIPK1]]. However, the regulation of [[Apoptosis]] or [[Necroptosis]] induced by a receptor-mediated or a genotoxic signal is far from being fully understood and is potentially of high clinical importance.==

The largely endosomal [[TLR3]] recognizes virus-derived double-stranded RNA or its synthetic homolog poly(I:C) and signals for inflammatory responses through [[NF-κB]] and type I interferon induction. As other membrane-bound receptors, [[TLR3]] has the capacity to induce [[Apoptosis]] in a TIR domain-containing adaptor-inducing interferon-β ([[TRIF]])-dependent manner. The recruitment and binding of [[TRIF]] via the RIP homotypic interaction motif (RHIM) is required for the transduction of apoptotic signals. However, the biochemical and molecular basis of [[TLR3]]-induced [[Apoptosis]] or the propensity of this receptor to induce [[Necroptosis]] has not been resolved to date. [[RIPK1]] also contains a C-terminal death domain (DD) and thereby interacts with the adaptor molecule [[Fas]]-associated death domain protein ([[FADD]]). In turn, FADD promotes recruitment of the initiator [[Caspase-8]] (for review, see) by homotypic interaction of the death effector domains ([[Death Effector Domain|DEDs]]) of both proteins. This signaling platform initiates cell death induction, tightly regulated by antiapoptotic proteins such as cellular FLIP-inhibitory protein ([[c-FLIP|cFLIP]]). From the 11 known cFLIP isoforms, the long ([[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]]) and the short ([[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]]) isoforms are prominently expressed in cultured tumor cells.

A number of compounds that were originally designed to mimic binding of the mitochondrial protein Smac/DIABLO to the N-terminal IAP-binding motif (IBM) of [[XIAP]] (smac mimetics) also target [[cIAPs]]. These compounds induce rapid autoubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of [[cIAPs]] and are therefore rather IAP antagonists. More recently it was reported that [[cIAPs]] promote K11-ubiquitination of [[RIPK1]] and subsequent degradation, thereby protecting against [[TNFR1]]-induced cell death. ==The molecular interplay of apoptotic and necroptotic signals has gained great attention by the very recent genetic evidence of [[RIPK3]], [[Caspase-8]] and [[RIPK1]]–[[FADD]] double knockout mice. In these reports, loss of [[RIPK3]] or [[RIPK1]] protects from embryonic lethality caused by [[Caspase-8]] or [[FADD]] deficiency, respectively. These reports demonstrate the high relevance of these signaling modules for development or, more recently, for T cell death in mice. Nonetheless, the intracellular signaling platform that regulates apoptotic and necroptotic signals remains to be identified.==

In this report we have characterized the intracellular signaling platform containing [[Caspase-8]], [[Caspase-10]], [[RIPK1]], [[FADD]], and [[c-FLIP]] isoforms. ==We demonstrate that loss of [[cIAPs]] promotes [[TLR3]]-induced [[RIPK1]]-[[RIPK3]]-mediated [[Necroptosis]].== This signal is initiated at a 2 MDa complex distinct from [[TNFR1 complex II]]. ==To be consistent with other high Mw complexes, we termed this signaling platform [[Ripoptosome]], based on the core scaffold function of [[RIPK1]]. We then show that cells with low [[RIPK1]] or high [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] levels are resistant to Ripoptosome formation and therefore cell death==. Intriguingly, in the absence of [[cIAPs]], [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] was able to protect from Ripoptosome-induced [[Apoptosis]] but promoted [[Necroptosis]] that is caused by the lack of caspase activity within the complex. Cancer cells have the susceptibility to spontaneously form the Ripoptosome in response to IAP antagonists that may promote killing in a [[RIPK1]] kinase-dependent manner by [[Necroptosis]]. Our study thus points to interesting therapeutic options for combining [[TLR3]] agonists with IAP antagonists, with [[c-FLIP]] isoforms representing critical regulators of the qualitative and quantitative responses from this complex.

## Results

### TLR3-Induced Cell Death Is Blocked by cIAPs and Proceeds in a Caspase- and RIP1-Kinase-Dependent Manner Independent from TNF Signaling

We first tested whether [[cIAPs]] control [[TLR3]]-mediated cell death. IAP antagonist (compound A) or TNF-like weak inducer of apoptosis (TWEAK) induces rapid degradation of cIAP1 or cIAP2. Sensitivity to the artificial [[TLR3]] ligand poly(I:C) upon treatment with IAP antagonist or TWEAK was studied in HaCaT keratinocytes, A5RT3, and MET-1 squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) cells. IAP antagonist promoted poly(I:C)-induced cell death of HaCaT and MET-1, but not of A5RT3 (Figure 1A; see Figures S1A and S1B available online). TWEAK treatment comparably sensitized HaCaT to [[TLR3]] stimulation, whereas A5RT3 or MET-1 cells were not sensitized (Figures S1B and S1C). Knockdown of [[cIAPs]] 1, but not cIAP2, sensitized HaCaT cells to [[TLR3]]-induced cell death, revealing the decisive role of cIAP1. IAP antagonist further sensitized cIAP1 knockdown cells, indicating the contribution of cIAP2 to cell death resistance (Figure S1D). Next we tested the caspase inhibitor zVAD-fmk (zVAD) and the inhibitor of the kinase [[RIPK1]], [[Necrostatin-1]]. Neither zVAD nor Nec-1 blocked poly(I:C)-induced cell death in the presence (Figure 1B, panels 2–4; Figure S1E, panels 2–4) or absence (Figure 1B, panels 7–9; Figure S1E, panels 7–9) of [[cIAPs]]. The combination of zVAD and Nec-1 largely rescued the cells (Figure 1B, panels 5 and 10; Figure S1E, panels 5 and 10). This suggests that simultaneous rescue from [[Apoptosis]] and [[Necroptosis]] is achieved only by the combined use of zVAD and Nec-1. Of note, although zVAD alone induces [[Necroptosis]] in some cells such as L929 (for review, see), in our cellular models of human epithelial cells this effect is not detected (compare Figure 4A; data not shown).

The release of high mobility group box 1 protein ([[HMGB1]]) in the cellular supernatant is a characteristic of caspase-independent cell death. In line with this finding, [[TLR3]]-induced release of HMGB-1 in the absence of [[cIAPs]] was unaffected by zVAD and marginally decreased by Nec-1 but completely suppressed by the combination of zVAD and Nec-1 (Figure 1C). We next analyzed cell death morphology by fluorescence microscopy studies. Typical apoptotic cells (membrane blebbing, DNA condensation) were detectable after poly(I:C) stimulation in the presence and absence of IAP antagonist (Figure 1D). Nec-1 did not alter the cell death phenotype. In the absence of [[cIAPs]], zVAD modified cell death morphology (Figure 1D, right panels): we observed a more rounded shape and a lack of DNA condensation. Combined addition of zVAD and Nec-1, however, fully rescued the cells (Figure 1D, lowest panel). We conclude that in the absence of [[cIAPs]] and under conditions of caspase blockade, [[TLR3]] stimulation results in [[RIPK1]]-dependent necroptosis. IAP antagonist may induce autocrine production of [[TNFR1]]. Knockdown of [[TNFR1]] (Figure 1E) or treatment with a number of soluble receptors (TNFR2-Fc, [[DR5|TRAILR2-Fc]], and [[Fas|CD95]]-Fc) or the combination thereof did not significantly alter [[TLR3]]-mediated cell death (Figure 1F; Figure S1F). Taken together, our data demonstrate that [[cIAPs]] control [[TLR3]]-mediated [[Apoptosis]] and [[Necroptosis]] independent from death ligand-induced autocrine loops. Importantly, our data using several different models of transformed epithelial cells demonstrate that such stress responses are highly dependent on the cellular context (e.g., regulated in a cell type-specific manner).

To tackle the molecular mechanisms responsible for the death signal, we performed immunoprecipitation (IP) of the [[Caspase-8]]-interacting complex under various conditions (Figure 1G). [[cIAPs]] depletion increased the interaction of [[Caspase-8]] with [[RIPK1]] upon [[TLR3]] stimulation (Figure 1G, panel 4). [[TLR3]]-induced [[Caspase-8]] complex not only contained [[RIPK1]], [[FADD]], the [[c-FLIP]] cleavage fragment p43, and [[Caspase-10]] p47/p43 but also the TLR3 adaptor [[TRIF]]. In contrast, [[TRIF]] was absent in TNF-induced complex II ([[TNFR1 complex II]]), indicative of the specific recruitment of the complex to [[TLR3]]. To investigate the physiological relevance of our data in cell lines, we investigated the [[TLR3]]-induced cell death in primary human keratinocytes (PKs) (Figure 1H). These experiments demonstrated that loss of [[cIAPs]] combined with inactivation of [[XIAP]] promotes [[TLR3]]-induced cell death (Figure 1H, panels 4 and 6) independent from [[TNFR1]] signaling (Figure 1H, panels 7–12).

### cIAPs Block Spontaneous Ripoptosome Formation, a Complex that Modulates the Qualitative and Quantitative Cell Death Responses

We had observed two different phenotypes of [[TLR3]]-induced cell death and found molecules involved in either [[Apoptosis]] or [[Necroptosis]] in the same intracellular complex. We thus reasoned that both cell death responses are elicited at the same signaling platform. The next goal was to define the contribution of key proteins in IAP-controlled cell death pathway. Cell death was fully dependent on [[TLR3]] expression and largely on [[TRIF]] (Figures S2A and S2B). Knockdown of [[Caspase-8]] (Figure 2A) resulted in partial suppression of [[TLR3]]-induced cell death in the absence of [[cIAPs]] that was largely rescued by Nec-1, but not by zVAD (Figure 2C, panels 3–5). This finding indicated that [[Caspase-8]] is contributing to the execution of the apoptotic cell death in the absence of [[cIAPs]], as previously suggested. Knockdown of [[RIPK3]] (Figure 2B) did not result in protection from [[TLR3]]-induced cell death. However, cell death was largely blocked by zVAD independent from Nec-1 treatment (Figure 2C, panels 9–12), indicating that [[RIPK3]] determines [[Necroptosis]] in our cellular systems. Thus, loss of [[RIPK3]] disables [[TLR3]]-mediated [[Necroptosis]] without affecting the [[Apoptosis]] response. Combined knockdown of [[RIPK3]] and [[Caspase-8]] largely protected cells from cell death altogether (Figure 2C, panels 15–18), indicating that [[Caspase-8]] and [[RIPK3]] are the two critical effectors of [[TLR3]]-induced cell death in the absence of [[cIAPs]]. We next investigated the function of [[RIPK1]]. Knockdown of [[RIPK1]] (Figure 2D, inset) resulted in full protection from [[TLR3]]-[[cIAPs]] (Figure 2D and Figure S2C) or [[TLR3]]-TWEAK-mediated cell death (Figure S2D). Having defined the decisive role of [[RIPK1]] for [[TLR3]]-mediated cell death in the absence of [[cIAPs]], we next characterized the [[Caspase-8]]-interacting signaling platform by IP. These experiments revealed that treatment with IAP antagonist alone resulted in [[Caspase-8]]-[[RIPK1]]-[[FADD]] interaction (Figure 2E, panel 2) which is insufficient for cell death induction (compare Figure 2D, panel 2). Additional stimulation with poly(I:C) resulted in formation of a complex that also included [[TRIF]] (Figure 2E, panels 3–4). Knockdown of [[RIPK1]] interfered with complex formation under all conditions (Figure 2E, panels 5–8). These data demonstrate that [[RIPK1]] is decisive for the activation and formation of the [[Caspase-8]]-[[RIPK1]] signaling complex which we thus designate “[[Ripoptosome]]”.

### cFLIP Is a Critical Regulator of the Ripoptosome

We had noted that A5RT3 cells were not sensitized to [[TLR3]]-induced cell death by IAP antagonist or TWEAK (Figure 1A). Since [[c-FLIP]] regulates [[Caspase-8]]-dependent cell death, we next studied the impact of this molecule for [[TLR3]]-mediated cell death. Retroviral infection with a control construct resulted in a minor sensitization to [[TLR3]] ligation (Figure 3A, white bars). However, knockdown of [[c-FLIP]] resulted in a major sensitization to [[TLR3]]-induced death (Figure 3A, panel 2), further increased by depletion of [[cIAPs]] (Figure 3A, panel 7). Inhibitor studies in these cells revealed that cell death was largely [[Apoptosis]] (Figure 3A, panel 3 or 8). When we studied Ripoptosome formation in these cells, A5RT3 lacking [[c-FLIP]] showed spontaneous Ripoptosome formation in the absence of [[cIAPs]] (Figure 3B, panel 6) with further increase upon [[TLR3]] stimulation (Figure 3B, panel 7–8). Interestingly, downregulation of [[c-FLIP]] led to accumulation of large amounts of [[Caspase-10]] in the Ripoptosome. These data argue that [[c-FLIP]] controls Ripoptosome formation by blocking the association of [[FADD]], [[RIPK1]], and [[Caspase-10]] with [[Caspase-8]].

### cFLIP Isoforms Differentially Regulate Poly(I:C)-Induced Cell Death by Regulation of the Ripoptosome

Next we investigated functional differences of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] or [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] in HaCaT which showed low endogenous [[c-FLIP]] expression. We then studied quantitative and qualitative cell death responses to [[TLR3]] stimulation by Annexin V-PI staining (Figure 4A) and crystal violet assays (Figures S3A and S3B). Loss of [[cIAPs]] promoted [[TLR3]]-induced cell death in vector controls (Figure 4A) independent from [[RIPK1]]-kinase activity (Figure 4A, Figures S3A and S3B, panel 5). zVAD protected cells from [[Apoptosis]] (concluded from the absence of Annexin V+/PI− cells) but only marginally protected from cell death (Figure 4A, control cells). These data closely correlated with our crystal violet results (Figures S3A and S3B, white bars). Combination of Nec-1 and zVAD entirely blocked cell death. [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] completely protected from poly(I:C)-induced cell death under all conditions (Figure 4A, Figures S3A and S3B, black bars). In contrast, loss of [[cIAPs]] resulted in spontaneous cell death in HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]], and the outcome of additional [[TLR3]] ligation was minor (Figure 4A, Figures S3A and S3B, gray bars). Remarkably, this cell death was solely [[RIPK1]] kinase dependent, whereas zVAD had no protective effect (Figure 4A, Figures S3A and S3B, compare panels 4 and 5, gray bars). These data show that [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] profoundly changes the qualitative response to [[TLR3]] ligation whenever [[cIAPs]] are absent. We next studied Ripoptosome formation in these cells. Loss of [[cIAPs]] in HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] increased [[RIPK1]] association with the complex (Figure 4B, panels 4 and 8). Interestingly, treatment with IAP antagonist alone was sufficient to induce spontaneous complex formation in HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]], although [[TRIF]] was not associated (Figure 4B, panel 6). [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] suppressed the increased association of [[RIPK1]] or [[FADD]] with the complex upon IAP antagonist/[[TLR3]] stimulation (Figure 4B, panels 10 and 12). Of interest, total levels of [[RIPK1]] slightly increased in HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]]. To investigate if Ripoptosome formation is a more general phenomenon, we next studied HeLa cells. HeLa cells were similarly sensitized to [[TLR3]] cell death by loss of [[cIAPs]] function (Figure S3C, panel 3). Inhibitor studies revealed that cell death proceeds in a purely [[Apoptosis]] manner (Figure S3C, panel 4), most likely due to the lack of [[RIPK3]] expression (Figure S3D). In line with this assumption, ectopic expression of wild-type [[RIPK3]] (RIP3 WT) but not a kinase deficient mutant of [[RIPK3]] (RIP3 KD) in HeLa cells led to increased caspase-independent [[TLR3]]-induced cell death in the absence of [[cIAPs]] (Figure S3F). In contrast to the data in HaCaT, HeLa cells were largely protected from [[TLR3]]-induced cell death by both [[c-FLIP]] isoforms. [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]]-expressing cells were not sensitive to IAP antagonist alone (Figure S3C, black and gray bars). When we studied Ripoptosome formation in these cells, [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] but not [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] was inhibitory, similar to HaCaT (Figure S3E, compare panels 2, 6, and 10). This effect was particularly evident because [[RIPK1]] was strongly induced by overexpression of both [[c-FLIP]] isoforms in HeLa (Figures S3E, TL). To investigate the formation of the Ripoptosome under more physiological conditions, we studied its formation in PKs (Figure 4C). Whereas HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] spontaneously formed the Ripoptosome it was not detectable in PK in the absence of [[cIAPs]] function (Figure 4C). This finding might be explained by high levels of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] expression in PK (Figure 4C, panels 5 and 6 versus 7–8) and argues that spontaneous Ripoptosome formation is highly cell type specific and mainly occurring in transformed cells in a [[c-FLIP]] isoform-dependent manner. Taken together, our data suggest that [[c-FLIP]] isoforms regulate Ripoptosome formation in opposite directions and that the caspase-like domain of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] inhibits Ripoptosome formation or its stability. It remains to be determined how the stoichiometric ratio between [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] and [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] impacts the physiological or pathophysiological signaling. Our studies using large differences in stoichiometry of the different [[c-FLIP]] isoforms in this context serve as “proof-of-principle” studies.

### Promotion of [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green|Necroptosis]] by [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] in the Absence of [[cIAPs]] Is Independent of [[TNFR1]] Signaling and Occurs in a 2 MDa Complex

To dissect the molecular mechanism of the differential effect of [[c-FLIP]] isoforms, we next investigated spontaneous cell death occurring in HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] upon [[cIAPs]] depletion. We first studied if autocrine TNF signaling might explain this phenomenon. Therefore we knocked down [[TNFR1]], which did not alter cell death induced by IAP antagonist alone (Figure 5A, panel 2). Of note, increased cell death by exogenous addition of [[TNFR1]] was fully blocked by [[TNFR1]] knockdown, whereas sensitization by addition of IAP antagonist was unaltered (Figure 5A, panel 4). Annexin V/PI staining revealed that cells died within 8 hr without appearance of a predominant Annexin V+/PI− cell population (Figure S4A). Ripoptosome formation preceded the first detection of cell death and persisted until 12–18 hr after inactivation of [[cIAPs]] (Figure S4B). [[TNFR1]] knockdown (Figure 5B, right panel) and/or blockade of autocrine [[TNFR1]] (Figure S4A, right panel) also did not protect from cell death. In contrast, Nec-1 (Figure 5C, panel 4) or [[RIPK3]] knockdown (Figure 5D) fully blocked IAP antagonist-induced cell death in HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]]. To exclude the possibility that Ripoptosome formation is mediated by [[TNFR1]], we performed [[Caspase-8]] IP in [[TNFR1]] knockdown cells. Despite efficient knockdown of [[TNFR1]] (Figure 5E, total lysate), [[Caspase-8]]-[[RIPK1]] interaction was unchanged (Figure 5E, panels 2 and 4). Moreover, [[TNFR1]] complex I and II analysis in HaCaT in the absence of [[cIAPs]] excluded the possibility that [[TNFR1]] recruits [[Caspase-8]] via [[RIPK1]] to the receptor (Figure S4C, panels 3–4). These data argue that recruitment of [[RIPK1]] to the [[TNFR1]] complex I is independent from Ripoptosome formation and most likely represents a different pool of [[RIPK1]] than present in the Ripoptosome. Consequently it cannot be called [[TNFR1 complex II]].

To investigate the composition of the native [[RIPK1]]-[[Caspase-8]] interaction platform in more detail, we perfomed gel filtration analysis. [[RIPK1]] and [[Caspase-8]] were mainly detected in their monomeric form (Figure 5F, left panels; [[RIPK1]], fractions 18–21; [[Caspase-8]], fractions 20–23), indicating that only a small amount of total cellular [[RIPK1]] and [[Caspase-8]] interacts. This is clearly evident since both proteins were detected in fractions corresponding to a complex of 2 MDa (Figure 5F, left panels, fractions 4–6). We next performed [[Caspase-8]] IP, using pooled samples from the column fractions. Interestingly, these experiments showed that upon [[cIAPs]] degradation [[Caspase-8]]/[[RIPK1]]/[[c-FLIP]] interaction occurred in the 2 MDa complex but not in the lower MW fractions (Figure 5F, right panel; compare panels 6–10 with panels 1–5). Our data argue that [[cIAPs]] block spontaneous Ripoptosome formation in the 2 MDa complex and that other thus far unknown molecules are present in this 2 MDa complex. Taken together, our data argue that the Ripoptosome is a large intracellular complex that is negatively regulated by [[cIAPs]] and that its formation is necessary for cell death execution. [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] deviates cell death to [[RIPK3]]-dependent [[Necroptosis]] in a [[TNFR1]]-independent manner in the Ripoptosome.

### [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] but Not [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] Blocks [[Caspase-8]] Activity within the Ripoptosome

To get further mechanistic insight into how the Ripoptosome activates apoptotic and necroptotic death, we decided to study enzymatic activity within the native complex utilizing complex-associated proteins as substrates. As shown in Figure 6A, HaCaT-[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] cells formed a strong Ripoptosome containing uncleaved [[RIPK1]] (Figure 6A, panels 6 and 8). In contrast, control cells contained comparable amounts of uncleaved and cleaved [[RIPK1]] (p39), indicative of [[Caspase-8]] activity within the complex (Figure 6A, panels 2 and 4). In line with the suggestion of Cho et al., our data indicate that [[Caspase-8]] activity within the Ripoptosome blocks [[Necroptosis]] by caspase-mediated [[RIPK1]] cleavage. Due to the fact that large amounts of [[Caspase-8]] are uncomplexed (Figure 5F), we wanted to extend our studies with a different bait for the IP of the complex. We thus established cell lines expressing Flag-tagged [[c-FLIP]] isoforms. The response to IAP antagonist and [[TLR3]] ligation was similar to cells expressing untagged [[c-FLIP]] isoforms (data not shown). We precipitated the Ripoptosome using IP against the Flag-tag (Figure 6B). In the absence of [[cIAPs]], [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] was complexed with [[RIPK1]], [[FADD]], and, after [[TLR3]] ligation, [[TRIF]], whereas [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] exhibited less complex formation. Intriguingly, the Ripoptosome of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]]-expressing cells mostly contained cleaved [[Caspase-8]], indicative of catalytic activity of [[Caspase-8]]/[[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] heterodimers. In contrast, the Ripoptosome of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]]-expressing cells mainly contained uncleaved [[Caspase-8]], suggesting that the catalytic activity in the complex is repressed.

The question arose as to how [[cIAPs]] prevent spontaneous Ripoptosome formation. One likely explanation is degradative ubiquitination of [[RIPK1]] by [[cIAPs]], as shown for K11-linked polyubiquitination or the constant K48-linked degradation in tumor cells. We thus studied complex formation in the presence of the proteasomal inhibitor MG-132. IAP antagonist-induced [[cIAPs]] degradation was blocked by MG-132 (compare Figure 6C, lane 10 versus 12; lane 14 versus 16). Interestingly, MG-132 treatment alone facilitated formation of the Ripoptosome (Figure 6C, panel 5 versus 7). Moreover, degradation of [[cIAPs]] correlated with increased [[RIPK1]] protein levels (Figure S3E, panels 14–15) together with spontaneous Ripoptosome formation in HeLa cells (Figure S3E, panel 2). Lastly, the simultaneous treatment with IAP antagonist and MG-132 suppressed Ripoptosome formation (Figure 6C, compare panel 2 with 4 and panel 6 with 8). Of note, formation of the Ripoptosome in the presence of IAP antagonist and MG-132 occurs despite the fact that MG-132 decreases total [[RIPK1]] levels (Figure 6C, compare panels 9–10 with 11–12, and panels 13–14 with 15–16). Taken together, these data suggest that [[cIAPs]] constantly prevent Ripoptosome formation and stabilization by ubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of a fraction of [[RIPK1]], at least in tumor cell lines. Future studies are required to corroborate this hypothesis in more detail.

## Discussion

A number of different molecular platforms have been shown to contain the kinase [[RIPK1]]. Some of these platforms (such as [[TNFR1 complex I]]; [[NOD-like Receptors|NOD]] complex) activate NF-κB, whereas others ([[TNFR1 complex II]]; [[TRAIL]] or [[Fas|CD95]] DISC) signal for [[Apoptosis]]. In this report we have described an intracellular complex that integrates different upstream stress signals converging at the kinase [[RIPK1]]. Loss of [[cIAPs]] allows for caspase-dependent and [[RIPK1]]-dependent cell death initiated at this complex. Consequently, it has the ability to induce both [[Apoptosis]] and [[Necroptosis]] based on the expression and activity of the different molecular components. These include [[RIPK1]], [[FADD]], [[Caspase-8]], [[Caspase-10]], and [[c-FLIP]] isoforms. Although we show that [[c-FLIP]] negatively regulates the recruitment of [[Caspase-10]] or [[FADD]] into the complex, the biological significance of [[Caspase-10]] or its activity to the assembly or activity of the complex is to date unclear and is under current investigation. We have studied the complex in three different epithelial cell lines, arguing that formation of the complex is a common event. Since [[RIPK1]] proved to be indispensible for the formation of this complex, we suggest calling it “[[Ripoptosome]]”. Our data clearly show that the qualitative response to Ripoptosome activation is highly cell type specific and depends on the stoichiometry of the molecular components. This notion is supported by our [[RIPK3]] overexpression studies in HeLa cells. HeLa undergo [[Necroptosis]] upon [[cIAPs]] depletion whenever [[RIPK3]] is present in these cells. The data support the previous findings of the crucial relevance of [[RIPK3]] kinase function for the execution of [[Necroptosis]]. Taken together, our data demonstrate the important contribution of the Ripoptosome to the qualitative and quantitative responses upon membrane-bound receptor triggering.

Spontaneous formation of the Ripoptosome is suppressed by the E3 [[Ubiquitin]] ligases [[cIAPs]] 1 or -2 that induce [[RIPK1]] ubiquitination and degradation. This conclusion is based on our studies using the [[Proteasome]]. Our data furthermore suggest that the Ripoptosome is necessary but insufficient for induction of [[Apoptosis]] and is disassembled by complex-intrinsic caspase activity, most likely by [[RIPK1]] cleavage (Figure 7). This is supported by previous data showing that the [[Z-VAD-FMK]] increases [[Caspase-8]]-[[RIPK1]] complex stability or, potentially, formation. When [[Caspase-8]] activity in the complex is low, [[RIPK1]] kinase/[[RIPK3]]-dependent [[Necroptosis]] is the consequence. Importantly, we have excluded the possibility that the Ripoptosome is a mere secondary [[TNFR1 complex II]] induced by autocrine [[TNFR1]] or other death ligands. This notion is supported by our data showing that spontaneous Ripoptosome formation was not altered upon [[TNFR1]] knockdown.

[[Caspase-8]]-interacting platforms have been largely investigated in respect to death receptor signaling. Our studies of [[TLR3]]-induced cell death give biochemical evidence that a death signaling platform interacts in a stimulus-dependent manner with [[TRIF]], the sole adaptor of [[TLR3]]. Recently, [[Caspase-8]]-mediated cleavage of [[RIPK1]] was demonstrated to negatively regulate the activation of IRF3 in the [[Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptor|RIG-I]] complex, arguing for a central role of [[RIPK1]] and [[Caspase-8]] not only for cell death but also for inflammatory or antiviral responses. In the absence of [[cIAPs]] the Ripoptosome spontaneously forms and presumably facilitates subsequent Ripoptosome recruitment to [[TRIF]]. These data together with our previous study indicate that the Ripoptosome can be recruited to different receptors or their adaptors. We propose that this interaction may be achieved either via RHIM or the DD of [[RIPK1]]. Of note, [[RIPK1]] is found in [[TNFR1 complex I]], although [[Caspase-8]] is activated downstream of [[TNFR1 complex I]]. Adding to this point, the absence of [[cIAPs]] does dramatically alter [[RIPK1]] modification in complex I, but this modification of [[RIPK1]] is not associated with recruitment of [[Caspase-8]] to [[TNFR1 complex I]] (compare Figure S4C). We speculate that [[RIPK1]] recruited to [[TNFR1]] does not necessarily represent the same pool of [[RIPK1]] found in the Ripoptosome and does not transit to complex II. Rather, [[TNFR1 complex II]] might associate with the Ripoptosome whenever [[cIAPs]] are absent. Future studies are required to delineate this point in more detail.

To further understand the role of [[Caspase-8]] activation in Ripoptosome signaling that is limited when solely using pharmacological inhibitors, we studied the impact of [[c-FLIP]]. Our data show that [[c-FLIP]] isoforms differentially regulate formation and activity of the Ripoptosome. It is noteworthy that both [[c-FLIP]] isoforms suppressed [[TLR3]]-triggered [[Apoptosis]], but not [[Necroptosis]] cell death. [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] forms heterodimers with [[Caspase-8]] that have enzymatic activity but are unable to form fully active heterotetramers of [[Caspase-8]]. The [[Caspase-8]]/[[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] heterodimers cleave only selected substrates. We suggest that one of the key substrates within the Ripoptosome is [[RIPK1]]. Cleavage of [[RIPK1]] (the main scaffold of the Ripoptosome) may then promote disassembly of the complex, as previously suggested for [[TNFR1 complex II]]. This needs to be formally proven in the future, although the very recent genetic evidence underlines the physiological relevance of our studies in vivo. In contrast, [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]]-[[Caspase-8]] heterodimer is predicted to not have enzymatic activity. This notion is supported by our data (Figure 6A), in which we show that [[RIPK1]] cleavage within the Ripoptosome is achieved even in the presence of [[Z-VAD-FMK]], whereas it is fully blocked by [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]]. In line with this finding, expression of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] is associated with increased levels of [[RIPK1]] in the Ripoptosome, sufficient for [[RIPK3]] activation and thereby [[Necroptosis]]. Of note, we were unable to detect [[RIPK3]] within the Ripoptosome, possibly due to the lack of specific reagents for biochemical analysis. Therefore, we currently cannot conclusively prove the point that [[RIPK3]] is recruited to Ripoptosome, although it was described in the [[TNFR1 complex II]] of Jurkat T cells. Summing up these data, our report suggests that [[c-FLIP]] isoforms are the critical “rheostat” of [[Caspase-8]] activity in the Ripoptosome, regulating the quality of [[Apoptosis]] or [[Necroptosis]]. It remains to be answered how different stoichiometric quantities of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]] and [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] may influence the qualitative outcome of Ripoptosome-induced [[Apoptosis]] or [[Necroptosis]]. This intriguing question merits future experimental studies, likely by using in ex vivo systems developed for the study of death receptor-associated complexes. Interestingly, inhibition of [[RIPK1]] kinase function with Nec-1 also promotes complex disassembly or blocks its assembly. We speculate that [[RIPK1]] autophosphorylation might be required for Ripoptosome stabilization. Clearly, future studies about the role of different [[RIPK1]] phosphorylation sites will undoubtedly be of high interest.

One important issue is the mechanism whereby [[cIAPs]] impact on Ripoptosome formation. Our chromatographic gel filtration assay gave important insights. First, the amount of [[RIPK1]], [[Caspase-8]], and [[FADD]] associated with the Ripoptosome in HaCaT cells is rather small. Second, the uncomplexed components of the Ripoptosome studied thus far were found in the 2 MDa range before inactivation of [[cIAPs]]. We speculate that the part of [[RIPK1]] associated with the 2 MDa fractions defines the amount of [[RIPK1]] capable to form the Ripoptosome. The question of whether the translocation of [[RIPK1]] is another mechanism of assembly of the complex, as indicated by the [[RIPK1]] overexpression studies by Tenev et al. (, in this issue of Molecular Cell), needs to be clarified in future studies. In our cellular model, specific interaction of endogenous [[RIPK1]] and [[Caspase-8]] is seen only upon degradation of [[cIAPs]] in these fractions. It is currently unclear what differentiates [[RIPK1]] associated with the 2 MDa fractions from monomeric [[RIPK1]]. We speculate that stress-associated posttranslational modification (e.g., phosphorylation), binding of additional (known or unknown) proteins, the transfer of [[RIPK1]] to a cellular compartment to be identified, or all of the above named reasons are necessary. We suggest that the fraction of [[RIPK1]] eluting with the 2 MDa fractions could represent this compartmentalized/modified [[RIPK1]]. We further hypothesize that [[cIAPs]] only target the modified [[RIPK1]] for ubiquitination (likely at K377) and proteasomal degradation. This explains why certain cell types (in which large fractions of [[RIPK1]] are accessible to [[cIAPs]] [e.g., HeLa, compare Figure S3E, panel 15]) show higher [[RIPK1]] protein levels upon treatment with IAP antagonist, whereas others (HaCaT) do not exhibit this effect. IAP antagonists cause rapid autoubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of [[cIAPs]]. When we studied Ripoptosome formation in the presence of a proteasomal inhibitor, we found that the Ripoptosome spontaneously forms in the presence of [[cIAPs]] whenever the 26S [[Proteasome]] is blocked. This suggests that it is not the degradation of [[cIAPs]] that is a prerequisite for Ripoptosome formation. Rather, we suggest that it is [[cIAPs]]-mediated degradation of compartmentalized [[RIPK1]] that precludes Ripoptosome signaling (Figure 7). Based upon the MG132 studies, another intriguing aspect was noted. [[cIAPs]] are degraded upon blockade of the [[Proteasome]]. These results suggest that another degradative pathway is involved in the control of [[cIAPs]] levels in the cell. We hypothesize that this degradative pathway involves the lysosome, as it was already shown that [[cIAPs]] can be targeted to lysosomes by TWEAK. An elegant recent study has investigated the nature of ubiquitin chains attached to [[RIPK1]] in vivo using mass spectroscopy of complex-associated proteins. It was found that in contrast to the rather monomorphous linear ubiquitin modification of NEMO, the [[RIPK1]] ubiquitination is highly complex and contains numerous different types of ubiquitin chains in vivo (e.g., K48, K11, linear [M1], and K63), at least in [[TNFR1 complex I]]. Undoubtedly it will be intriguing to study the nature of ubiquitin modifications of [[RIPK1]] and the functional impact of [[cIAPs]] more directly in the Ripoptosome.

The effector function of proapoptotic molecules such as [[Caspase-8]] or [[FADD]] is very well established. The mechanistic involvement of [[FADD]], [[RIPK1]], [[Caspase-8]], or [[c-FLIP]] in nonapoptotic signals has been a matter of intense debate over the past decade. In vivo evidence for the relevance of these nonapoptotic signals was limited, hampered by the embryonic lethality of [[Caspase-8]], [[c-FLIP]], or [[FADD]] knockout mice. Several very recent reports have provided compelling evidence that the absence of core Ripoptosome components (namely [[FADD]] and [[Caspase-8]]) is lethal due to augmented [[RIPK1]]-[[RIPK3]]-dependent [[Necroptosis]]. The molecular mechanism of the Ripoptosome that we propose in this report explains the phenotype observed in the genetic models. [[RIPK1]] knockout mice are viable due to the central role of this molecule in Ripoptosome formation. When [[FADD]] or [[Caspase-8]] are absent, the disassembly of the Ripoptosome is impossible, because [[FADD]] cannot recruit [[Caspase-8]] necessary for [[RIPK1]] degradation. The absence of [[RIPK3]] blocks the effector phase of [[Necroptosis]] downstream (or within) the Ripoptosome. Certainly additional studies are needed that involve experiments about the differential role of [[c-FLIP]] in mice, although this is challenging due to the fact that genes encoding [[c-FLIP]] and [[Caspase-8]] are closely clustered.

Viruses, such as the Kaposi's sarcoma-associated herpes virus (KSHV), can confer [[Apoptosis]] resistance and tumor progression by expression of [[vFLIP]] isoforms that resemble [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] and are upregulated during late stages of KHSV-induced sarcoma. Our current findings that show that cells expressing high levels of [[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] initiate [[Necroptosis]] upon [[cIAPs]] depletion may prove to be of high pathophysiological relevance. [[cIAPs]] may be critically required to control virus-induced infections, tumor development, or alternatively the host response influenced by either [[Apoptosis]] or [[Necroptosis]]. [[TLR3]] is widely expressed on tumor cells, and its signals affect a wide range of physiological and pathophysiological processes. The negative regulation of the Ripoptosome by [[cIAPs]] as suggested in this report could also influence the immune response against tumors. Existing evidence suggests that [[TLR3]] ligands can be used as anticancer agents and that a combination of CD40 and TLR agonists may synergistically act as immunostimulants by suppression of tumor growth in mice. Expression of [[cIAPs]] by tumor cells may thus be required to evade the tumor immune response. Antagonists of [[Inhibitor of Apoptosis Proteins|IAP]] function such as those used in this study may therefore represent a potential antitumor strategy to overcome necrosis resistance activated by [[TLR3]] agonists for instance in Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)-induced epithelial cancers (nasopharyngeal carcinoma). In turn, this may lead to increased immune responses favoring tumor elimination. The intriguing pathophysiological role of [[cIAPs]]/[[RIPK1]] regulation during [[TLR3]] signaling leading to Ripoptosome activation, as demonstrated in this report, requires further intense investigation in the future. Since transformation of PK favors a [[Necroptosis]] response, while “contracting” the [[Apoptosis]] response to chemotherapeutics, tight control of the Ripoptosome by [[cIAPs]] could prevent successful treatment of epithelial malignancies such as cervical cancer. Understanding the assembly, regulation, and degradation of the Ripoptosome in more detail will likely open up avenues for tumor therapy.

## Experimental Procedures

### Cytotoxicity Assays

The following stimulation conditions were used throughout the studies: cells were either prestimulated with [[Z-VAD-FMK]] (10 μM), [[Necrostatin-1]] (50 μM), TNFR2-Fc (30 μg/ml), TWEAK (0.5 μg/ml), or IAP antagonist (100 nM, unless otherwise specified) alone or in the respective combinations for 1 hr followed by stimulation with poly(I:C) (2 μg/ml for crystal violet assays, Annexin V externalization, [[HMGB1]] release, and immunofluorescence microscopy; or 20 μg/ml for IP).

Crystal violet staining of attached, living cells was performed 18–24 hr after stimulation exactly as described. For statistical analysis, the standard error of mean (SEM) was determined for three to five independent experiments of each cell line and stimulatory condition (represented by error bars in the figures). For the detection of phosphatidylserine externalization, cells were stimulated and analyzed exactly as previously described. For analysis of [[HMGB1]] release, cells were stimulated for 18 hr. Cell-free supernatants and cellular lysates were analyzed for HMGB1 protein expression by western blotting.

### Coimmunoprecipitation of Caspase-8- and cFLIP-Bound Complexes

For the precipitation of [[Caspase-8]]-bound proteins and [[c-FLIP]]-bound proteins, 1 × 107 cells were used. Cells were prestimulated with either [[Z-VAD-FMK]] (10 μM) or IAP antagonist (100 nM) alone or the respective combinations thereof for 1 hr followed by stimulation with 20 μg/ml of poly(I:C) for 3 hr or HF-TNF for 2 hr. Details of the procedure are exactly as described. For coimmunoprecipitation of Flag-tagged complexes, 40 μl of anti-Flag M2 agarose beads (Sigma, Germany) were added to lysates. Samples were incubated for 14–18 hr on an end-over-end shaker at 4°C. Precipitates were washed five times with lysis buffer before protein complexes were eluted from dried beads by addition of standard reducing sample buffer and boiling at 95°C. Equal amounts of precipitates were analyzed by western blotting (; see also the Supplemental Experimental Procedures).

### Chromatographic Gel Filtration Assay

Cellular lysates were separated on a Superose 6 HR 10/30 size-exclusion column and an AKTA Purifier protein purification system (GE Healthcare) essentially as described previously. Lysates (3–4 mg of total protein) were separated at a flow rate of 0.4 ml/min, and 0.5 ml fractions were collected at 4°C in 5% (w/v) sucrose, 20 mM HEPES, 120 mM NaCl, 2 mM EDTA, 2 mM KCl, and 1% Triton X-100 (pH 7.5). The column was calibrated with the indicated protein standards (GE Healthcare, Figure 5F). For each sample, three separate column separations were performed and repeat fractions collected in the same tubes. Aliquots (100 μl) from each fraction were retained for western blotting. For IP, fractions were then pooled (1–5, 6–10, 11–16, 17–22, and 23–28), and IPs were performed as described above followed by western blot analysis.

### Figure 7. Working Model of Activation and Signaling by the Ripoptosome and Its Regulation by [[c-FLIP]] Isoforms

![Figure 7](https://cdn.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/blobs/1c4e/3163271/c553e7ae971c/gr7.jpg)

[Figure 7 on PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3163271/figure/Fig7/)

> [!abstract] Figure/Table
> A fraction of [[RIPK1]] is constantly “modified” (likely by cellular stress signals) to an active conformation. [[cIAPs]] ubiquitinate “modified” [[RIPK1]] targeting it to degradation by the 26S [[Proteasome]]. The unknown cellular compartment contains not only “modified” [[RIPK1]] but also [[Caspase-8]], [[FADD]], and [[c-FLIP]]. Addition of IAP antagonist leads to autoubiquitination of [[cIAPs]] and degradation. This is the signal for the formation of the Ripoptosome, a complex of “modified” [[RIPK1]], [[FADD]], [[Caspase-8]]/[[Caspase-10]], and [[c-FLIP]] isoforms. The Ripoptosome can be recruited to RHIM-binding modules such as [[TRIF]], thereby linking it to [[TLR3]]. Depending on the composition of the Ripoptosome, it activates [[Apoptosis]] and/or [[Necroptosis]]. [[Caspase-8]] homodimers within the Ripoptosome form active [[Caspase-8]] and subsequent [[Apoptosis]]. [[c-FLIP|cFLIPL]]-[[Caspase-8]] heterodimers within the Ripoptosome have enzymatic activity in the complex insufficient to generate active [[Caspase-8]] heterotetramers necessary for [[Apoptosis]] induction, although sufficient for [[RIPK1]] cleavage. [[Caspase-8]]/[[c-FLIP|cFLIPS]] heterodimer lacks proteolytic activity that is required not only for full [[Caspase-8]] activation but also for [[RIPK1]] degradation. Thus [[RIPK1]] accumulates in the Ripoptosome and initiates [[Necroptosis]] via [[RIPK3]] in a [[RIPK1]] kinase-dependent manner.
