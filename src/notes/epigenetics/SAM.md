## Overview

S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) is the universal methyl donor for cellular methylation reactions, including [[DNA Methylation]]. It is synthesized from methionine and ATP, and its availability is governed by [[Methyl Donor|methyl-donor]] supply and one-carbon metabolism.

## Role in DNA Methylation

[[DNMT1]], the maintenance DNA methyltransferase, transfers the methyl group from SAM to the C5 position of cytosine residues on the nascent DNA strand, generating 5-methylcytosine and S-adenosylhomocysteine ([[SAH]]) as products.

> [!info]
> Because SAM availability reflects one-carbon metabolism and methyl-donor supply, fluctuations in its levels can influence methylation maintenance and, consequently, [[Gene Silencing|gene silencing]], transposable element repression, and overall epigenetic stability.

## One-Carbon Metabolism

SAM is produced by methionine adenosyltransferase and is recycled: SAH is hydrolyzed to homocysteine, which can be remethylated to methionine via [[Folate]]- and B12-dependent reactions. Nutritional status — dietary methionine, folate, choline, and betaine — therefore directly tunes SAM levels and methylation capacity.

## Clinical Relevance

SAM deficiency is associated with DNA hypomethylation, genomic instability, and altered gene expression. Conversely, the supplement SAMe has been studied for methylation support, though effects on global methylation are context-dependent and not always predictable. The SAM/SAH ratio (methylation index) is a common readout of methylation capacity.
