## Overview

Pericentromeric satellite repeats are arrays of tandemly repeated DNA flanking the [[Centromere]]. In humans, these include classical satellites 1, 2, and 3 (e.g., alpha satellites at the centromere, juxtacentromeric satellites 2 and 3 on chromosomes 1, 9, and 16). They are among the most densely methylated sequences in the genome.

## Function

These repeats are the main targets for methylation by [[DNMT3b]], the de novo [[DNA Methylation|DNA methyltransferase]]. Methylation of pericentromeric satellites is essential for constitutive [[Heterochromatin]] formation and centromere function.

> [!important]
> By methylating these repetitive sequences, DNMT3b helps maintain centromere stability and prevent aberrant recombination and chromosome missegregation.

## Pathological Significance

Loss of this methylation — as in [[ICF Syndrome]], where DNMT3b is mutated — leads to centromeric instability, decondensation of satellite regions, and the characteristic clinical features of immunodeficiency and facial anomalies. Reduced satellite methylation is also observed with aging and in cancer, where it contributes to genome instability.

## Research Relevance

Pericentromeric satellite repeats serve as sensitive reporters of global methylation status and heterochromatin integrity. Their derepression is linked to retrotransposon activation and innate immune signaling during [[Senescence]].
