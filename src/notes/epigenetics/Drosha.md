# Drosha

## Overview

**Drosha** (gene *RNASEN*) is a nuclear **[[Ribonuclease III|RNase III]]-family endoribonuclease** that catalyzes the first step of canonical [[MicroRNA]] biogenesis. Together with its cofactor **[[DGCR8]]**, Drosha forms the **[[Microprocessor complex|Microprocessor]]**, which recognizes and cleaves primary miRNA transcripts (pri-miRNAs) in the nucleus.

## Structure & Domains

- ~159 kDa protein containing **two tandem RNase III domains (RIIIDa and RIIIDb)** that dimerize intramolecularly to form a single processing center, plus a **double-stranded RNA-binding domain (dsRBD)**.
- The two RNase III domains each cleave one strand of the pri-miRNA stem, producing a staggered cut with a characteristic **2-nucleotide 3' overhang**.
- An N-terminal region mediates nuclear localization and interaction with DGCR8.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

> [!info] Microprocessor cleavage
> The Microprocessor measures ~11 bp from the single-stranded/double-stranded (ssRNA–dsRNA) junction at the base of the pri-miRNA hairpin and cleaves the stem, releasing a ~60–70 nt **pre-miRNA** hairpin.

1. RNA Pol II transcribes a **pri-miRNA** with a stem-loop.
2. **[[DGCR8]]** binds the apical junction and heme cofactor, orienting the substrate.
3. **Drosha** cleaves the stem, generating **pre-miRNA**.
4. Pre-miRNA is exported to the cytoplasm by **[[Exportin-5]]** and processed by **[[Dicer]]** into mature miRNA duplexes loaded onto **[[Argonaute]]** in the [[RISC]].

## Physiological Function & Regulation

- Essential for the maturation of most canonical miRNAs; global miRNA loss upon *Drosha* deletion is embryonic lethal and disrupts stem-cell differentiation.
- Non-canonical roles include cleaving certain mRNAs and processing ribosomal RNA.
- Activity is regulated by post-translational modifications (phosphorylation, acetylation), auto-regulatory feedback with DGCR8, and subcellular localization.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- Dysregulated Drosha/Microprocessor activity globally reshapes the miRNA-based [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] regulatory network, affecting [[Senescence]], the [[SASP]], and [[Inflammaging]].
- Altered expression is implicated in numerous cancers and in age-related decline of miRNA homeostasis.
- Electrophilic quinones (e.g., [[Adrenochrome]]) may covalently modify Drosha/DGCR8, potentially altering pri-miRNA processing rates.
