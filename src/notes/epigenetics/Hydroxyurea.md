# Hydroxyurea

**Hydroxyurea** (L-hydroxyurea) is a small molecule inhibitor of [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]] (RNR) and is widely utilized as both an antineoplastic and immunosuppressive agent. By interfering with the production of deoxyribonucleotides, it acts as a potent modulator of [[DNA Replication]] and can induce [[DNA Replication Stress]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[Senescence]].

## Mechanism of Action

The primary mechanism of hydroxyurea is the inhibition of [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]], the rate-limiting enzyme responsible for the *de novo* synthesis of deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs).

1. **RNR Inhibition**: Hydroxyurea acts as a free radical scavenger. It targets the tyrosyl radical located in the R2 subunit of the ribonucleotide reductase enzyme. By quenching this radical, hydroxyurea prevents the enzyme from converting ribonucleoside diphosphates (NDPs) into their corresponding deoxyribonucleoside diphosphates (dNDPs).

2. **dNTP Depletion**: The inhibition of RNR leads to a rapid and significant depletion of the intracellular dNTP pools (dATP, dGTP, dCTP, and dTTP). This depletion is particularly impactful during the [[S Phase]] of the [[Cell cycle]], as the availability of these building blocks is critical for high-fidelity and high-speed DNA synthesis.

3. **Replication Stress and DNA Damage**: The scarcity of dNTPs causes the [[DNA Polymerase]] to stall during [[DNA Replication]], leading to widespread [[DNA Replication Stress]]. This stalling triggers the [[DNA Damage Response]] (DDR), involving the activation of the [[ATR]] and [[ATM]] signaling pathways. Depending on the severity and duration of the stress, the cell may undergo [[Apoptosis]] or enter a state of permanent cell cycle arrest known as [[Senescence]].

## Clinical Relevance

Hydroxyurea has several important clinical applications:

- **Polycythemia Vera**: It is a first-line therapy for managing erythrocytosis in patients with polycythemia vera. By inhibiting RNR in erythroid progenitor cells, it effectively reduces the total red blood cell mass and helps prevent thrombotic complications.
- **Sickle Cell Disease**: Hydroxyurea is used to improve the clinical course of sickle cell disease. It works by stimulating the production of [[Fetal Hemoglobin]] (HbF) in red blood cells. Increased levels of HbF interfere with the polymerization of hemoglobin S (HbS), thereby reducing the incidence of vaso-occlusive crises and other complications.
- **Chronic Myeloid Leukemia (CML)**: While not the primary therapy in the era of tyrosine kinase inhibitors, hydroxyurea is sometimes used to manage high white blood cell counts in CML patients.
- **Oncology**: It is used in various combination chemotherapy regimens to slow the proliferation of malignant cells by inducing replication stress.

## Side Effects and Limitations

Common side effects of hydroxyurea include:
- **Myelosuppression**: The most frequent toxicity, including leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and anemia, due to its effect on all rapidly dividing hematopoietic cells.
- **Gastrointestinal effects**: Nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea.
- **Dermatological effects**: Skin irritation or rashes.
- **Long-term use**: Potential for secondary malignancies, though this is less common than with other alkylating agents.
