## Overview

Genome stability is the property of a cell or organism to preserve the integrity of its genetic information across replication, repair, and cell division. It depends on accurate [[DNA Repair]], telomere maintenance, and the silencing of mobile genetic elements. Loss of genome stability is one of the [[Hallmarks of Aging]] and is a major driver of cancer and age-related degeneration.

## Sirtuin-Dependent Maintenance

A key contributor is [[SIRT6]], which deacetylates [[Histone H3]] at lysine 9 (H3K9) on telomeric chromatin, promoting the recruitment of factors that maintain [[Telomere]] structure and function. SIRT6 also suppresses [[LINE-1]] retrotransposon activity, limiting the insertional mutagenesis and DNA damage caused by retrotransposition.

> [!important]
> SIRT6-deficient cells exhibit marked genomic instability, telomere dysfunction, and premature cellular [[Senescence]] (Michishita et al., 2008; Van Meter et al., 2014). This demonstrates that a single histone deacetylase coordinates multiple layers of genome protection.

## Mechanistic Layers

- **DNA repair** — [[Homologous recombination repair]] and NHEJ (via [[DNA-PKcs]]) correct [[Double-Strand Break|double-strand breaks]].
- **Telomere maintenance** — H3K9 deacetylation and shelterin function prevent erosion and fusion of chromosome ends.
- **Repeat silencing** — [[Retrotransposon]] repression (LINE-1, [[HERV]]) prevents insertional damage; its failure during aging triggers innate immune signaling via the cGAS-STING axis.
- **Epigenetic fidelity** — faithful [[DNA Methylation]] maintenance by [[DNMT1]] and [[UHRF1]] preserves repressive marks that stabilize the genome.

## Pathology

Genomic instability underlies cancer initiation, replicative senescence, and progeroid syndromes. Interventions that enhance [[SIRT6]] activity or suppress retrotransposition (e.g., [[NRTI|NRTIs]]) are being explored to restore genome stability in aging.
