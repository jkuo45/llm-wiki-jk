## Overview

DNA-PKcs is the catalytic subunit of the DNA-dependent protein kinase ([[DNA-PK]]), a serine/threonine kinase that forms the core of the non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) pathway for repairing [[Double-Strand Break|DNA double-strand breaks]] (DSBs). Together with the [[Ku70]]/Ku80 heterodimer, it binds broken DNA ends and coordinates their ligation, making it essential for genomic integrity in the [[Nucleus]].

## Structure & Function

DNA-PKcs is a large (~470 kDa) PI3K-related kinase. Its C-terminal kinase domain is essential for autophosphorylation and for signaling that recruits downstream NHEJ factors (including Artemis and XRCC4-ligase IV). DNA-PKcs is recruited to DSBs primarily by the Ku heterodimer, which loads onto free DNA ends and translocates inward to allow kinase activation.

## SIRT6 Regulation

> [!important]
> [[SIRT6]] promotes DNA-PKcs mobilization and stability at DSBs, enhancing DNA repair capacity. In SIRT6-deficient cells, DNA-PKcs recruitment to damage sites is impaired, contributing to defective DSB repair and genomic instability (McCord et al., 2009; PMID 20157594).

This regulatory link ties sirtuin activity directly to [[Genome stability|genome stability]] and positions DNA-PKcs as a downstream effector of sirtuin-dependent DNA repair. By counteracting DSB accumulation and the associated cellular senescence, the SIRT6–DNA-PKcs axis supports telomere maintenance and healthy aging.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Loss or inhibition of DNA-PKcs sensitizes cells to ionizing radiation and radiomimetic drugs, making it a target in cancer radiosensitization. Conversely, overactivation can contribute to aberrant repair and genomic instability. Because DNA-PKcs functions in NHEJ rather than [[Homologous recombination repair]], cells deficient in both pathways show severe repair defects — the basis for synthetic lethal approaches in some cancers.
