## Overview

Sas2p is a MYST-family [[Histone Acetyltransferase|histone acetyltransferase]] in budding yeast (*[[Saccharomyces cerevisiae]]*). It acetylates [[Histone H4]] at lysine 16 (H4K16), a modification that opens chromatin and marks euchromatic regions. Sas2p operates in opposition to the sirtuin deacetylase Sir2p ([[Sir2 (yeast)]]), which removes H4K16 acetylation.

## Role at the Telomere-Euchromatin Boundary

At telomeres, Sir2p and Sas2p regulate H4K16 acetylation at the telomere-[[Euchromatin]] boundary, establishing a sharp transition between silenced subtelomeric [[Heterochromatin]] and active euchromatin. Sas2p's acetylation activity counteracts Sir2p spreading, delimiting the silenced domain.

> [!important]
> Sir2p levels decline with age as H4K16 acetylation rises, and constitutive H4K16 acetylation shortens lifespan. This links subtelomeric silencing to yeast longevity and extends the sirtuin-longevity connection beyond ERC suppression.

## Mechanism & Longevity

Directionally, the Sir2p/Sas2p balance at telomeres determines lifespan. By maintaining H4K16 deacetylation at subtelomeric regions, Sir2p preserves the silenced state that supports longevity. Sas2p-dependent H4K16ac, in excess, promotes loss of silencing and accelerates aging.

## Research Significance

The yeast Sir2/Sas2 counterbalance is a paradigm for how antagonistic histone-modifying enzymes shape chromatin boundaries and influence aging, with implications for understanding [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]]-mediated longevity mechanisms in higher organisms.
