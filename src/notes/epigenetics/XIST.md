## Overview

XIST (X-inactive specific transcript) is a long [[Non-coding RNA|non-coding RNA]] (lncRNA) that is the primary driver of [[X-Chromosome Inactivation]], the process by which one X chromosome is transcriptionally silenced in female cells to ensure appropriate gene dosage.

## Mechanism

XIST is expressed from the future inactive X and coats that chromosome in cis. Once coating begins, XIST recruits chromatin-modifying enzymes that establish repressive marks — including [[Polycomb Group Proteins]] and [[EZH2]] depositing H3K27me3, alongside [[macroH2A]] deposition — compacting the chromosome into a transcriptionally silent Barr body.

> [!important]
> This silencing is heritable through cell divisions, making XIST a paradigm for long-range, RNA-mediated [[Gene Silencing|epigenetic gene silencing]].

## Regulation

XIST expression is controlled by the antisense transcript TSIX and by pluripotency factors such as [[Oct4]], which repress XIST in pluripotent cells and allow reactivation of the silent X during reprogramming. XIST is silenced on the active X, maintaining monoallelic expression.

## Research & Clinical Relevance

XIST is a model system for studying lncRNA function, nuclear architecture, and chromatin regulation. XIST-based tools are explored for targeted gene silencing and for correcting dosage imbalances in chromosomal aneuploidies. XIST expression state also serves as a marker of epigenetic identity in stem cell and aging research.

## Sex Differences

[[KDM6A]] and [[KDM5C]] (both XCI escapees) facilitate Xist upregulation by removing H3K27me3 at its promoter. Escape genes ([[KDM6A]], [[DDX3X]], [[KDM5C]], context-dependent [[ATRX]]) remain biallelic despite Xist coating (see [[EXITS]]). Xist ribonucleoprotein complexes contribute to female-biased autoimmunity alongside TLR7 escape and estrogen dose (Chang et al., *Cell* 2024).
