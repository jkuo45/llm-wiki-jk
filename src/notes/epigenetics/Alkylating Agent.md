# Alkylating Agent

Alkylating agents are electrophilic compounds that covalently attach an alkyl group to nucleophilic atoms in biomolecules. Their anticancer effect derives almost entirely from alkylating DNA bases — most prominently the N7 position of guanine, the most nucleophilic site in the duplex — and from the bulky adducts and crosslinks this creates.

> [!info] The founding anticancer drug class
> Nitrogen mustard was the result of wartime poison-gas work. Its cytotoxic effect was observed when a shipyard commander with lymphoma was given nitrogen mustard as a nitrogen-bridge test, and his malignancy regressed. That observation became the first chemotherapy, and the nitrogen mustard family remains in use today.

## Chemical Classes and Canonical Drugs

The class divides by the leaving group and the reactive intermediate generated in vivo.

| Class | Canonical agent | Activating chemistry |
|---|---|---|
| Nitrogen mustard | [[Cyclophosphamide]], [[Ifosfamide]], [[Mitomycin C]] | Intramolecular aziridinium formation displaces chloride |
| Alkyl sulfonate | [[Busulfan]] | Direct bimolecular substitution of the sulfonate |
| Nitrosourea | Carmustine, lomustine, semustine | Carbamoylation and chloroethylation producing O6-chloroethylguanine |
| Platinum complex | [[Cisplatin]] | Aquation yields a diaqua species that binds N7 guanine |

[[Cyclophosphamide]] is a [[Prodrug]]: cytochrome P450 activation (principally CYP2B6 and CYP3A4) opens the ring to 4-hydroxycyclophosphamide and aldophosphamide, which delivers the alkylating phosphoramide mustard. Aldophosphamide spontaneously breaks down to phosphoramide mustard — the active alkylating species — and to [[Acrolein]], an electrophile responsible for much of the haemorrhagic cystitis seen with the drug. Mesna is co-administered to neutralise the acrolein in urine, although it does not prevent the dose-limiting myelosuppression.

## Mechanism of Action

> [!info] Alkylation is not cell-cycle specific, but misrepair is
> Because adducts are not removed by the replication machinery's proofreading, they block both DNA synthesis and transcription directly. The cytotoxicity, however, comes mainly from the cells that *fail* to repair them.

Lesions produced depend on the drug:

- **Monoalkylation** at N7-guanine. This is the most common lesion and by itself is relatively well tolerated, because it is repaired by base excision repair through [[Alkyladenine glycosylase|alkyladenine DNA glycosylases]] — the [[M1dA|3-methyladenine]] lesion, for example, is excised by AAG/MPG.
- **Intrastrand crosslinks** between adjacent guanines, as with [[Mitomycin C]].
- **Interstrand crosslinks** between opposite strands, as with the phosphoramide mustard of [[Cyclophosphamide]] and with [[Cisplatin]].
- **DNA–protein crosslinks** formed by phosphoramide mustard, which are particularly disruptive to replication-fork progression.

Crosslinks and bulky mono-adducts stall the replicative fork and activate a [[DNA Damage Response]] involving [[p53]]. Outcomes are heterogeneous: [[Apoptosis]], mitotic catastrophe, [[Cell Cycle Arrest]] and — a signature long-term consequence of this class — [[Senescence]]. Long-lived cells that survive alkylation and then re-enter the cell cycle years later are the accepted explanation for the therapy-related myeloid neoplasms and solid tumours seen after [[Cyclophosphamide]].

> [!warning] Cumulative, dose-dependent toxicity
> The same nondiscrimination against proliferating normal tissue that makes alkylating agents effective causes myelosuppression, infertility (both sexes, often permanent from cumulative dose), and a documented teratogenic risk. Cyclophosphamide carries a boxed warning for embryo-fetal toxicity. Second malignancies are the clearest evidence that these agents leave a durable mutational footprint.

## Clinical Role

Alkylating agents are now rarely used as single agents in solid tumour treatment. Their roles are distinctive rather than obsolete:

- [[Cyclophosphamide]] as a backbone of multiagent lymphoma regimens and, at lower dose, as an immunosuppressant in transplant conditioning and in autoimmune disease, where its cytotoxicity is deliberately kept below the lethal threshold.
- Cyclophosphamide or platinum agents alongside methotrexate in the primary treatment of paediatric bone sarcomas, where cytotoxic dose intensity is the point.
- High-dose cyclophosphamide with bone marrow rescue.
- Dacarbazine and temozolomide in melanoma and glioma respectively; nitrosoureas and temozolomide in glioma for their blood–brain barrier penetration.
- Nitrogen mustard was the original bladder instillagent and remains in use.

> [!tip] A durable footprint in ageing research
> Because alkylating agents impose fixed, non-repaired lesions, they are used as a controlled source of [[DNA Damage]] in models of induced senescence and of the ageing phenotypes that follow it. This makes them a mechanistically clean tool rather than merely a therapeutic class.

## Connections

- [[Cyclophosphamide]] — the prototypical alkylating agent, and the vault's most fully worked example; it contributes the prodrug-activation and acrolein-toxicity branches.
- [[Mitomycin C]] — a bifunctional alkylating agent used both as a cytotoxin and as a topical bladder instillation, with an unusual reductively activated aziridine chemistry.
- [[Cisplatin]] — not an alkylator in the strict sense but produces the identical N7-guanine adduct cascade, and is the structural analogy most often drawn.
- [[DNA Damage]] — alkylation is the mechanism by which this class converts a chemical into genotoxic stress; the entire downstream biology of the vault's DNA-damage notes runs through this reaction.
- [[p53]] — the principal arbiter of the fate of an alkylated cell, deciding between repair, arrest, senescence and apoptosis.
- [[Apoptosis]] — the dominant death route for alkylation-damaged cells in proliferating tissues.
- [[Senescence]] — the durable consequence of sublethal alkylation in non-dividing cells, and the mechanistic link to therapy-induced second malignancy.
- [[Acrolein]] — the unwanted electrophilic by-product of cyclophosphamide activation that drives its urotoxicity.
- [[Alkyladenine glycosylase]] — the base-excision repair machinery that makes simple monoalkylation tolerable and defines the tolerance ceiling of the class.
- [[Bladder Cancer]] — the tumour whose local therapy with alkylating agents (mitomycin C, nitrogen mustard) is still standard practice.
- [[Chemotherapy]] — the parent modality; alkylating agents are its oldest and mechanistically most mutagenic subcategory.
