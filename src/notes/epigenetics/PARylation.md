## Overview

PARylation is the post-translational modification in which poly(ADP-ribose) polymers are synthesized from [[NAD+]] onto protein acceptors, catalyzed by poly(ADP-ribose) polymerases ([[PARP]] family, chiefly [[PARP1]]). It functions in [[DNA Repair]], chromatin regulation, and stress responses, but excessive PARylation consumes NAD+ and can deplete cellular energy reserves.

## Mechanism

Upon DNA damage, PARP1 binds strand breaks and synthesizes PAR chains on itself and histones, recruiting DNA repair factors. Each ADP-ribose unit transferred consumes one NAD+ molecule. Under sustained DNA damage — or when repair is defective — PARP activity becomes chronic, and NAD+ depletion impairs energy metabolism and sirtuin activity.

> [!warning]
> Increased PARylation is a hallmark of [[Alzheimer's Disease]], and it could be decreased in AD mice with [[Nicotinamide Riboside|nicotinamide riboside]] (NR) supplementation.

## PARylation in Neurodegeneration

Elevated PARylation has also been detected in several other neurodegenerative disorders that involve DNA repair defects, including Cockayne syndrome, xeroderma pigmentosum, and ataxia-telangiectasia, where PARP-mediated NAD+ depletion plays a major role in pathogenesis.

> [!info]
> Despite the underlying DNA repair deficiencies, NR dramatically improved the phenotype of each of these conditions in mouse models, prolonging survival by more than three times in ataxic mice.

## Therapeutic Implications

Restoring NAD+ via precursors like NR or [[NMN]] counters PARP-driven NAD+ depletion and supports both sirtuin activity and DNA repair capacity. Conversely, [[PARP inhibitors]] are used in oncology — where PARylation inhibition is synthetically lethal with homologous recombination repair deficiency — and are also being explored as neuroprotective agents.

## Connections

- [[PARP]]: Enzyme family that catalyzes PARylation using NAD+.
- [[PARP1]]: Principal nuclear PARP executing DNA-damage-induced PARylation.
- [[NAD+]]: The substrate consumed during PARylation.
- [[Nicotinamide Riboside]]: Precursor that restores NAD+ and lowers PARylation-driven depletion.
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]: Disorder in which increased PARylation is a hallmark.
- [[DNA Repair]]: Process whose failure drives chronic PARylation.
- [[PARP inhibitors]]: Drugs blocking PARylation, used in cancer therapy.
- [[Sirtuins]]: NAD+-dependent enzymes indirectly inhibited by PARP-driven NAD+ depletion.
