# FRAT2

FRAT2 (Frequently Rearranged in Advanced T-cell lymphomas 2) is a cytosolic protein that acts as a positive modulator of canonical [[Wnt Signaling]] by antagonizing [[GSK3β]] (glycogen synthase kinase 3 beta). It belongs to the evolutionarily conserved FRAT/GBP (GSK-3-binding protein) family, originally identified through its frequent genomic rearrangement in aggressive T-cell malignancies. FRAT2 binds directly to the N-terminal domains of GSK3, competing with Axin for GSK3 access and thereby protecting β-catenin from phosphorylation-dependent destruction.

## Structure & Domains

FRAT2 is a relatively small (~270 amino acid) intrinsically disordered protein lacking enzymatic activity of its own. Its functional core is the FRAT box (also called the GSK3-binding domain or GBD), a conserved ~40-residue motif that mediates physical interaction with GSK3β. The FRAT box adopts an amphipathic alpha-helical conformation upon binding. FRAT2 shares this domain with its paralog FRAT1 and with the related protein GBP. Unlike many Wnt pathway components, FRAT2 has no catalytic domain; its role is purely regulatory/scaffolding through competitive protein–protein interactions.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

In the absence of Wnt ligand, the destruction complex — composed of [[Axin]], [[APC]], [[CK1]], and [[GSK3β]] — phosphorylates β-catenin, marking it for [[Ubiquitin Ligase]]-mediated proteasomal degradation. FRAT2 binds GSK3β and blocks its interaction with Axin, preventing efficient β-catenin phosphorylation. Upon Wnt receptor activation, Dishevelled reinforces this inhibition, and β-catenin accumulates, translocates to the nucleus, and co-activates TCF/LEF transcription factors to drive proliferation and stemness genes. Thus FRAT2 functions as an endogenous amplifier of Wnt/β-catenin signaling at the level of the destruction complex.

## Physiological Function

FRAT2 contributes to the regulation of embryonic patterning, cell fate specification, and tissue homeostasis wherever Wnt signaling operates. Its expression is developmentally regulated, and it helps fine-tune the threshold of Wnt responsiveness in progenitor populations. FRAT2 also intersects with cellular reprogramming contexts, as robust Wnt/β-catenin activity supports the maintenance of [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] and the activity of the [[Yamanaka Factors]] during somatic cell reprogramming.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

FRAT2 was first cloned from a recurrent chromosomal translocation in advanced T-cell [[Lymphoma]], where its rearrangement places it under the control of strong enhancers, driving constitutive Wnt activation and lymphoid transformation. Experimentally, FRAT2 overexpression cooperates with oncogenic lesions to promote tumor growth in several models. Because FRAT2 is dispensable for normal viability in mice but can potentiate Wnt-driven malignancy, it has attracted interest as a relatively tumor-selective node for therapeutic intervention. Small-molecule disruptors of the FRAT2–GSK3β interface are being explored as means to suppress hyperactive Wnt signaling without broadly inhibiting GSK3β, which has pleiotropic physiological roles.
