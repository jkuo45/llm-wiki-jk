## Overview

The nucleolus is the largest and most dynamic structure within the [[Nucleus]], serving as the dedicated site of ribosomal DNA (rDNA) transcription and [[Ribosome Biogenesis|ribosome biogenesis]]. It is organized around tandem arrays of rDNA repeats and assembles around the nucleolar organizer regions.

## Function

Within the nucleolus, [[RNA Polymerase I]] transcribes rDNA into pre-ribosomal RNA, which is then processed and assembled with ribosomal proteins into mature ribosomes. Because ribosomes set cellular protein synthesis capacity, nucleolar activity is a key determinant of cell growth, proliferation, and stress responses. Nucleolar size and activity scale with the growth state of the cell.

## SIRT7 in the Nucleolus

[[SIRT7]] is concentrated in the nucleolus, where it deacetylates [[Histone H3]] at lysine 18 (H3K18) and regulates RNA polymerase I transcription. Its nucleolar residence positions SIRT7 to control rRNA synthesis and, consequently, protein synthetic capacity and growth.

> [!important]
> SIRT7 also deacetylates extra-nucleolar targets such as [[GATA4]] and [[SMAD3]], linking it to cardiac and fibrotic pathways. Deficiency of SIRT7 yields extensive myocardial fibrosis and cardiomyopathy, revealing its importance in stress responses of the heart.

## Pathological Relevance

Nucleolar stress — disruption of rDNA transcription — triggers stabilization of p53 and cell cycle arrest, connecting nucleolar function to tumor suppression. Aberrant nucleolar activity is observed in cancer (hypertrophy) and in aging, where nucleolar function declines. The SIRT7–nucleolus axis links ribosome biogenesis to [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]]-dependent longevity signaling.
