# c-Met

[[c-Met]] (also called the [[MET gene|MET]] proto-oncogene / hepatocyte growth factor receptor, HGFR) is a receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) in the MET family. Encoded by the [[MET gene]] in humans, it is the exclusive high-affinity receptor for [[Hepatocyte Growth Factor]] ([[HGF]]), also known as scatter factor. Upon HGF binding, c-Met initiates a pleiotropic signaling program — variously described as "invasive growth" — that coordinates cell proliferation, survival, motility, morphogenesis, and [[Angiogenesis]]. Deregulated c-Met activity is a well-established driver in numerous human malignancies, making it a major oncology therapeutic target.

## Structure

c-Met is synthesized as a single-chain precursor that is cleaved in the Golgi into a disulfide-linked heterodimer. The extracellular portion comprises a semaphorin (Sema) domain, a plexin–semaphorin–integrin (PSI) domain, and four immunoglobulin-like (IG) domains that mediate HGF recognition. The transmembrane α subunit remains associated with the intracellular β subunit, which contains a juxtamembrane regulatory region, a conserved tyrosine kinase catalytic domain, and a multifunctional carboxy-terminal tail bearing two critical docking tyrosines (Tyr1234/1235 in the activation loop and Tyr1349/1356 in the tail). Autophosphorylation of these residues creates binding sites for adaptor and effector proteins.

## Mechanism of Action

Ligand binding induces HGF-dependent dimerization and trans-autophosphorylation of c-Met. Phosphorylated Tyr1234/1235 fully activate the kinase, while Tyr1349/1356 serve as a "multisubstrate docking site" recruiting effectors via their SH2 or PTB domains. Key downstream pathways include:

- **[[RAS]]–[[MAPK]] / [[ERK]]** — mitogenic and proliferative signaling.
- **[[PI3K]]–[[AKT]]** — survival, anti-apoptosis, and growth.
- **[[STAT]] (especially STAT3)** — proliferation and invasion.
- **[[GAB1]] scaffolding** — amplifies branching morphogenesis and invasive growth signals.
- **[[FAK Signaling]] and [[Integrin]] crosstalk** — motility, invasion, and adhesion turnover.

Non-canonical activation occurs through gene amplification, activating mutations (e.g., in hereditary papillary renal carcinoma), transcriptional upregulation, and ligand-independent mechanisms such as [[Integrin]]-mediated adhesion or [[c-Met]]–[[EGFR]] transactivation. A distinctive feature is c-Met's ability to promote "invasive growth" — the coordinated dissolution of cell–cell contacts, migration, and tubulogenesis recapitulating embryonic morphogenesis.

## Physiological Function

During development, HGF/c-Met signaling orchestrates epithelial–mesenchymal interactions governing organogenesis of the liver, kidney, muscle, and placenta. In adulthood, the axis is largely quiescent but is re-activated during tissue repair — notably [[Liver Regeneration]], wound healing, and the activation of [[Muscle Stem Cell|muscle satellite cells]] (where HGF is a classic mitogen for quiescent satellite cells). c-Met also supports the regenerative competence of [[Mesenchymal Stromal Cells]] and modulates the stem cell niche.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

c-Met is a prototypic [[Oncogene]] when dysregulated. Aberrant activation — through *MET* amplification (gastric, esophageal, non-small cell lung cancer), exon 14 skipping mutations, and overexpression — correlates with poor prognosis, metastasis, and resistance to targeted therapies including [[EGFR]] inhibitors. C-Met is also implicated in [[Cancer]] stemness and in the biology of neuroendocrine tumors. Clinically, inhibitors span monoclonal antibodies ([[Onartuzumab]], [[Emibetuzumab]]), HGF-neutralizing antibodies ([[Rilotumumab]]), and small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors ([[Crizotinib]], [[Cabozantinib]], [[Tepotinib]], [[Capmatinib]]). *MET* exon 14 skipping and high-level amplification are now actionable biomarkers guiding therapy selection in NSCLC. c-Met's links to [[Pluripotency]] networks and [[Reprogramming]] further position it within regenerative and [[Disease Modeling]] contexts using [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]].
