## Overview

Homologous recombination repair (HRR) is the high-fidelity pathway for repairing [[Double-Strand Break|DNA double-strand breaks]] (DSBs), using an undamaged sister chromatid as a template. It is active primarily in S/G2 phase and is essential for [[Genome stability|genome stability]], especially in proliferating tissues.

## Mechanism

At a DSB, the MRN complex recruits [[BRCA1]]. BRCA1 promotes end resection — the generation of 3' single-stranded overhangs — which commits repair to the homologous pathway rather than NHEJ. Resected ends are coated by RPA and then exchanged for [[RAD51]] filaments with the help of [[BRCA2]] and PALB2. RAD51 then invades the homologous duplex, copying the intact sequence to restore the break without error.

> [!info]
> Directionally, BRCA1 sits upstream of high-fidelity DSB repair: loss of BRCA1 cripples homologous recombination, increasing mutation and cancer risk, particularly in breast and ovarian tissue.

## Sirtuin Regulation

[[SIRT2]] supports BRCA1 localization to damage sites, linking sirtuin activity to homologous recombination and genome stability. Other sirtuins modulate repair proteins, suggesting sirtuin status tunes the choice and efficiency of DSB repair during aging.

## Clinical Relevance

The BRCA1 pathway is the basis for synthetic lethality with [[PARP inhibitors]]: BRCA-deficient cancers cannot repair the single-strand lesions converted to DSBs when PARP is inhibited, leading to collapse of replication forks and cell death. This paradigm established the first genetically targeted therapy exploiting repair deficiency.

- [[BRCA2]]: Loads RAD51 onto resected ends together with PALB2.
- [[RAD51]]: Recombinase that executes strand invasion.
- [[Double-Strand Break]]: The DNA lesion repaired by this pathway.
- [[Genome stability]]: The outcome protected by high-fidelity HRR.
- [[PARP inhibitors]]: Drugs synthetically lethal with HRR deficiency.
- [[SIRT2]]: Sirtuin that supports BRCA1 localization to damage sites.
- [[DNA-PKcs]]: Effector of the competing NHEJ pathway.
