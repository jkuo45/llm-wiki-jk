## Overview

UHRF1 (ubiquitin-like with PHD and RING finger domains 1) is a multi-domain accessory protein essential for faithful propagation of [[DNA Methylation]] during cell division. It links replication to methylation maintenance by targeting [[DNMT1]] to the sites where methylation must be restored.

## Mechanism

During S phase, UHRF1 recognizes hemi-methylated CpG sites through its SRA (SET- and RING-associated) domain: it binds the methylated parental strand while leaving the unmodified daughter strand available for methylation.

> [!important]
> This targeting directs DNMT1 to replication foci, where it restores symmetric CpG methylation on the newly synthesized strand, ensuring faithful propagation of epigenetic information across cell divisions. Loss of this targeting mechanism leads to passive demethylation and epigenetic instability.

## Additional Functions

Beyond recruiting DNMT1, UHRF1 is a [[Ubiquitin]] E3 ligase: it ubiquitinates histones (including H3) at replication, further guiding DNMT1 action. It also reads H3K9me3 marks, coupling maintenance methylation to [[Heterochromatin]] propagation.

## Pathological Relevance

UHRF1 is frequently overexpressed in cancer, where it sustains aberrant hypermethylation and silencing of tumor suppressors. Conversely, its inhibition causes global passive demethylation, an attractive strategy for cancer epigenome-targeted therapy. Proper UHRF1 function is also critical for [[Genome stability|genome stability]] during proliferation.
