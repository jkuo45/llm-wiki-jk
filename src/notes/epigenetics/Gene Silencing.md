## Overview

Gene silencing is the heritable repression of [[Gene Expression]] that does not involve changes to the DNA sequence itself. In mammals, the best-characterized mechanism operates through [[DNA Methylation]]: high levels of 5-methylcytosine in promoter regions — often concentrated in [[CpG Island|CpG islands]] — convert an otherwise active promoter into a transcriptionally inert state. Methylated promoters recruit methyl-CpG-binding domain (MBD) proteins and inhibit the binding of transcription factors, compacting the local [[Chromatin]] into a repressive configuration.

## Mechanism

Silencing is enforced by multiple cooperating layers:

- **Promoter methylation** — recruitment of MBD proteins (e.g., MeCP2, MBD2) that attract co-repressor complexes.
- **Repressive histone marks** — [[Histone Methylation]] such as H3K9me3 and H3K27me3, and deacetylation by [[HDAC]]s, which condense [[Nucleosome|nucleosomes]].
- **Heterochromatin formation** — compaction into [[Heterochromatin]] that physically excludes the transcriptional machinery.

> [!info]
> Silencing is not an all-or-nothing switch: promoter methylation density and the interplay with activating marks such as H3K4me3 determine whether a gene is fully silent, poised, or active. This is why CpG island methylation is such a reliable correlate of long-term repression.

## Physiological Functions

Physiologically, gene silencing maintains genomic imprinting (monoallelic expression of imprinted genes), silences [[Transposable Elements|transposable elements]] and endogenous retroviruses, and underpins cell-type-specific differentiation programs such as [[X-Chromosome Inactivation]].

## Pathology

Aberrant silencing is a hallmark of cancer: hypermethylation of tumor suppressor gene promoters (e.g., [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]] CDKN2A) silences growth-regulatory genes, functionally mimicking loss-of-function mutations. Conversely, loss of silencing contributes to reactivation of repeats and genomic instability. Because silencing is reversible, [[DNA Methylation]] inhibitors such as [[5-Azacytidine]] can re-express silenced genes therapeutically.
