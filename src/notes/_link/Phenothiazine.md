# Phenothiazine

[[Phenothiazine]] is a tricyclic heterocyclic compound consisting of two benzene rings fused to a central thiazine ring containing a position-5 sulfur and a position-10 nitrogen. It is the parent scaffold of a large family of derivatives — most famously the antipsychotic [[Chlorpromazine]] and [[Promethazine]], the dye [[Methylene blue]] and [[Toluidine Blue O]], and the antihistamine/antiemetic phenothiazines. It has been called "pharmacology's first lead structure," giving rise to diverse clinical agents across psychiatry, anesthesia, antimicrobial therapy, and mitochondrial medicine [^32].

## Structure & Chemistry

The phenothiazine nucleus presents three substitution positions critical to biological activity:

- **Positions 3 and 7** — typically bear amine/alkylamine side chains in bioactive derivatives (e.g., methyl groups in [[Toluidine Blue O]], dimethylamino groups in [[Methylene blue]]).
- **Position 10 nitrogen** — when free (unsubstituted), the heterocyclic nitrogen is essential for methylene blue-like alternative electron-transfer function; substitution with a bulky side chain (as in [[Chlorpromazine]] and [[Promethazine]]) abolishes this activity [^1].
- **Position 5 sulfur** — required for the electron-carrier phenotype; replacement with nitrogen converts the scaffold to a phenazine (e.g., [[Neutral Red]]) and destroys mitochondrial electron-shuttling [^1].

> [!info] Redox character
> Phenothiazine and its derivatives are redox-active dyes. The oxidized (cationic) form is reduced to a colorless leuco form, enabling electron shuttling. Derivatives such as [[Methylene blue]] and [[Toluidine Blue O]] show negative redox potentials (≈ −0.1 V vs NHE) that correlate with their ability to enhance cellular oxygen consumption [^1].

## Biological & Pharmacological Actions

### Neuroprotection and mitochondrial redox cycling

Phenothiazine itself — without amine side chains — is a potent, direct free-radical scavenger. In HT-22 hippocampal neurons it protects against glutamate, [[Rotenone]], and [[Iodoacetic Acid|iodoacetic acid (IAA)]] neurotoxicity with nanomolar EC50s (viability EC50 ≈ 18.99 nM; ROS EC50 ≈ 57.21 nM) [^1]. Unlike [[Methylene blue]], phenothiazine does **not** enhance cellular oxygen consumption, inhibit anaerobic glycolysis, or act as an alternative electron carrier between [[Respiratory Chain Complex I|Complex I]] and [[Cytochrome c]]; its protection is a direct, mitochondria/NADH-independent antioxidant action [^1].

In *C. elegans* models of [[Parkinson's Disease]], phenothiazine is highly efficacious against rotenone- and MPP+-induced dopaminergic neurodegeneration, outperforming [[Chlorpromazine]] [^38], [^39].

### Other clinical phenotypes

- **Antipsychotic (position-10 substituted):** [[Chlorpromazine]] pioneered the dopamine-receptor blockade treatment of psychosis.
- **Antihistamine / antiemetic:** [[Promethazine]] and related phenothiazines block H1 receptors.
- **Antimalarial & antimicrobial:** [[Methylene blue]] and phenothiazine-derived compounds inhibit parasitic disulfide reductases [^8], [^46].
- **Mitochondrial enhancer:** [[Methylene blue]] and [[Toluidine Blue O]] improve brain oxidative metabolism and memory [^13], [^14], [^16].

## Structure–Activity Relationships (from the MB derivative study)

The study "Neuroprotective Actions of Methylene Blue and Its Derivatives" divided phenothiazine-related compounds into four groups [^1]:

1. **Naked phenothiazine nucleus** ([[Phenothiazine]], [[2-Chlorophenothiazine]]) — nanomolar neuroprotection via direct scavenging; no OCR/electron-shunt effect.
2. **3,7-amine-substituted** ([[Methylene blue]], [[Toluidine Blue O]]) — nanomolar neuroprotection + alternative electron-transfer carrier (bypass of [[Respiratory Chain Complex I|Complex I]]/[[Complex III]]) + OCR enhancement + [[Complex IV]] upregulation.
3. **10-nitrogen side-chain substituted** ([[Chlorpromazine]], [[Promethazine]]) — ~1000-fold potency loss, no electron shunt, pro-oxidative in cell-free assays.
4. **Phenazine (S5→N)** ([[Neutral Red]]) — OCR enhancement but no electron-carrier function and weak neuroprotection.

> [!important] Two structural features define MB-like mitochondrial protection
> A **free 10-nitrogen** and the **position-5 sulfur** are both required for the alternative electron-transfer phenotype. Substituting either destroys the mitochondrial electron-carrier action while often preserving (or converting to) a direct antioxidant effect [^1].
