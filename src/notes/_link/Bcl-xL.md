# Bcl-xL

**Bcl-xL** (BCL2L1) is an anti-apoptotic member of the [[Bcl-2]] family of proteins that regulates programmed cell death by inhibiting mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP) and cytochrome c release. It is a critical survival factor in senescent cells and a key target for [[Senolytic|senolytic]] therapeutics.

## Mechanism

Bcl-xL localizes to the mitochondrial outer membrane where it sequesters pro-apoptotic effectors [[BAX]] and [[BAK]], preventing their oligomerization and the subsequent release of cytochrome c. It also binds BH3-only proteins (BIM, BAD, PUMA), neutralizing their pro-apoptotic signaling. In senescent cells, Bcl-xL is upregulated as part of the senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs), contributing to apoptosis resistance.

## Senolytic Targeting

> [!info] Source: [[task_output_comt_fisetin_senolytic_09_July_2026|COMT × Fisetin Research Evaluation]]
> [[Fisetin]] binds the hydrophobic groove of Bcl-xL with pharmacologically preferred selectivity over Bcl-2. Computational modeling confirms favorable binding energies (Bcl-2:fisetin = −22.2 to −35.1 kcal/mol depending on ionization state). [[PI3K]]-[[Akt]] pathway inhibition by fisetin further suppresses Bcl-xL expression in senescent endothelial cells (Ji et al. 2025, PMID: 38789909). This dual mechanism — direct binding + transcriptional suppression — underlies fisetin's senolytic activity.

Other compounds targeting Bcl-xL include [[Navitoclax]] (ABT-263) and ABT-737, which are BH3 mimetics developed as anticancer agents.
