# RIG-I

**RIG-I** (retinoic acid-inducible gene I; gene *DDX58*) is a cytosolic pattern-recognition receptor of the RIG-I-like receptor (RLR) family that detects short double-stranded RNA bearing a 5′-triphosphate or 5′-diphosphate end. Ligand engagement triggers MAVS-dependent signalling to [[TBK1]] and IKK, activating [[IRF3]]/IRF7 and [[NF-κB]] and inducing [[Type I Interferon|type I interferons]] and inflammatory cytokines. Alongside [[cGAS]], RIG-I is one of the principal cytosolic nucleic-acid sensors, and its RNA-sensing arm provides a route by which cellular RNA species — including retroelement-derived transcripts — can drive sterile [[Inflammation|inflammation]].

## Structure & Domains

RIG-I is a DExH-box RNA helicase with a tripartite architecture: two tandem N-terminal caspase activation and recruitment domains (CARDs), a central helicase domain comprising Hel1, Hel2 and a pincer subdomain, and a C-terminal domain (CTD). In the resting state the CARDs are sequestered by an intramolecular interaction with the helicase domain, holding the receptor autoinhibited. RNA binding at the helicase/CTD interface, together with K63-linked polyubiquitination by TRIM25 and RNF135/RIPLET and binding of unanchored ubiquitin chains, releases the CARDs. The freed CARDs nucleate into filaments that template downstream signalling. Specificity for 5′-triphosphate RNA resides largely in the CTD, which caps the RNA terminus and contributes to discrimination from host capped mRNA.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

CARD filament formation drives oligomerisation of [[MAVS]] (IPS-1) on the mitochondrial outer membrane, mitochondria-associated ER membranes and peroxisomes. MAVS acts as a signalling platform that recruits TRAF3 and the kinases TBK1 and IKKε, which phosphorylate and activate IRF3 and IRF7; TRAF6 and the IKK complex activate NF-κB. The two arms cooperate to induce *IFNB1* and interferon-stimulated genes. Secreted type I interferon acts back on the cell through IFNAR–JAK–STAT signalling, amplifying RIG-I and other antiviral effectors in a positive-feedback loop. Negative regulation includes RNF125-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of RIG-I, deubiquitination by CYLD, and phosphorylation-dependent modulation of CARD activity.

## Physiological Function

RIG-I is a cornerstone of antiviral innate immunity, providing cell-intrinsic detection of RNA viruses including influenza A virus, Sendai virus and flaviviruses. Its expression is itself interferon-inducible, which is the basis of the "retinoic acid-inducible" naming. Beyond infection, RIG-I responds to endogenous RNA ligands generated under cellular stress, coupling RNA metabolism to innate immune output.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Gain-of-function variants in *DDX58* cause autoinflammatory type I interferonopathies (including Singleton–Merten syndrome), illustrating that unrestrained RIG-I signalling is pathogenic. The receptor is a target for pharmacological agonism in cancer immunotherapy, where intratumoural RIG-I activation is intended to convert immunologically cold tumours into interferon-inflamed ones.

## Role in Senescence & SASP

RIG-I is an RNA-sensing input to senescence-associated inflammation: cytosolic RNA species in senescent and damaged cells engage RIG-I to drive interferon and cytokine output. Sources of such RNA include retroelement-derived transcripts, which are de-repressed during senescence and deep senescence, as well as mitochondrial double-stranded RNA released when mitochondrial dynamics or autophagy are perturbed. In the SASP-induction scheme of Birch and Gil, RIG-I is placed on the RNA-sensing arm feeding the secretome, in parallel with the DNA-sensing [[cGAS]]–[[STING]] arm and the [[Inflammasome]].
