# Iduna

**Iduna** (RNF146) is a PAR-dependent E3 ubiquitin ligase. Its catalytic RING domain is paired with a WWE domain that binds iso-ADP-ribose within [[PAR]]; this couples PAR recognition to ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of PARylated proteins. Iduna is best known as a protective brake on [[PAR]]-mediated cell death.

## Mechanism

- The WWE domain binds the iso-ADP-ribose linkage of [[PAR]], recruiting Iduna to PARylated substrates.
- Iduna ubiquitinates PARylated proteins — including the linker histone [[H1.2]], the PAR carrier that exports the polymer from the nucleus — tagging them for [[Ubiquitin]]–proteasome degradation.
- By removing PARylated carriers, Iduna limits cytosolic [[PAR]] accumulation and the downstream [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]]-dependent death cascade of [[Parthanatos]].
- Iduna also degrades tankyrase/AXIN complexes, linking it to Wnt/β-catenin signalling, and participates in the DNA-damage response.

## Physiological and pathological role

- **Neuroprotection:** Iduna protects the brain from glutamate [[Excitotoxicity]] and [[Ischemic Stroke]] by interfering with [[PAR]]-induced cell death (Andrabi et al. 2011, *Nat Med*; Kang et al. 2011, *PNAS*).
- **Cancer:** RNF146 can act as an oncogene in Wnt-driven tumours by destabilizing AXIN; its PAR-dependent activity also positions it in DNA-damage/repair signalling.
- **Parthanatos brake:** pharmacologically augmenting Iduna (or otherwise blocking H1.2-PAR export) is a proposed anti-parthanatos strategy alongside [[PARG]] modulation.
