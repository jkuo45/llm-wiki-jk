# Hydroxyl Radicals

The **Hydroxyl radical** (•OH) is the most reactive and damaging member of the [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) family. It has an extremely short half-life and reacts instantly with almost any biological molecule it encounters.

## Definition

Hydroxyl radicals are the most reactive and destructive species among all reactive oxygen species in biological systems. They have a very short half-life and react instantly with almost any biological molecule they encounter.

## Formation

- **[[Fenton Reaction]]:** Produced from [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] in the presence of transition metals (e.g., Fe²⁺, Cu⁺). This is the predominant source in biological systems.
- **Haber-Weiss Reaction:** Formed from the reaction between [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]; kinetically negligible without iron catalysis but effectively the sum of superoxide-mediated iron reduction and Fenton chemistry.
- **Radiation:** Generated by radiolysis of water (e.g., UV, ionizing radiation).
- **Peroxynitrite Decomposition:** Homolytic cleavage of peroxynitrous acid (ONOOH) yields ~30% hydroxyl radical and nitrogen dioxide.

Hydroxyl radicals react at diffusion-limited rates (k ≈ 10⁹–10¹⁰ M⁻¹s⁻¹), damaging molecules within a radius of only ~1–2 nm from their site of generation. This "site-specific" damage means that iron-binding molecules — including [[DNA]] (histone-associated iron), membrane phospholipids (iron associated with headgroups), and proteins with metal-binding motifs — are preferential targets.

## Role in Adrenochrome Formation

The hydroxyl radical is a potent oxidant that participates in the radical chain process of [[Epinephrine]] [[Oxidation]]. It can drive the formation of [[Adrenochrome]] from adrenaline, especially in environments with high oxidative stress. This creates a positive feedback loop: adrenochrome redox cycling generates superoxide and H₂O₂, which via Fenton chemistry produce more hydroxyl radicals, accelerating further catecholamine oxidation.

## Biological Impact

Because it cannot be detoxified by enzymes (due to its high reactivity), the hydroxyl radical causes non-specific damage to:
- **DNA:** Leading to mutations and strand breaks (e.g., [[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine]] formation, G→T transversions).
- **Lipids:** Initiating [[Lipid Peroxidation]] by abstracting bis-allylic hydrogen from [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids]].
- **Proteins:** Causing oxidation, carbonylation, and loss of function.

## Site-Specific Generation

The Fenton reaction (Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ → Fe³⁺ + •OH + OH⁻) is the predominant source of hydroxyl radicals in biological systems. Under [[Oxidative Stress]], [[Superoxide Radicals]] can mobilize iron from ferritin and from damaged iron-sulfur clusters (e.g., in aconitase), providing the catalytic iron pool needed for sustained Fenton activity. This creates a vicious cycle: superoxide releases iron, iron drives hydroxyl radical formation, and hydroxyl radicals damage additional iron-sulfur proteins, releasing yet more iron.

## Clinical and Pathological Significance

Hydroxyl radicals are implicated in:
- [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] — reintroduction of O₂ after hypoxia provides H₂O₂ that reacts with elevated cytosolic Fe²⁺
- [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]] — iron accumulation in [[Substantia Nigra]] ([[Parkinson's Disease]]) and senile plaques ([[Alzheimer's Disease]]) catalyzes local hydroxyl radical generation
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — iron deposition within arterial wall promotes LDL oxidation
- [[Cancer]] — hydroxyl radical-induced DNA damage drives genomic instability and mutations (e.g., G→T transversions in [[p53]])
- [[Aging]] — accumulated oxidative damage across macromolecules

Therapeutic strategies focus on upstream prevention — reducing H₂O₂ via [[Catalase]] and [[Glutathione Peroxidase]], sequestering transition metals, and limiting [[Superoxide Radicals]] production — since the extreme reactivity of hydroxyl radicals makes direct scavenging impractical.
