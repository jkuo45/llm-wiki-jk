# Th1

**Type 1 helper T cells (Th1)** are a specialized subset of [[CD4+ T Cells]] that orchestrate cell-mediated immune responses against intracellular pathogens, including viruses, intracellular bacteria, and protozoa. Th1 cells are defined by their signature production of **interferon-gamma (IFNγ)** and their ability to activate [[Macrophages]] for enhanced microbicidal activity.

## Differentiation & Regulation

### Polarizing Signals
Th1 differentiation is driven by:
- **[[IL-12]]** — Produced by activated [[Macrophages]] and [[Dendritic Cells]] in response to intracellular pathogens. IL-12 activates **STAT4**, which induces **T-bet** expression.
- **[[IFNγ]]** — Acts in an autocrine and paracrine manner to amplify T-bet expression through **STAT1** activation.
- **IL-18** — Synergizes with IL-12 to enhance IFNγ production.

### Master Transcription Factor: T-bet
**[[T-bet]]** (T-box transcription factor 21, TBX21) is the master regulator of Th1 identity. T-bet:
- Directly activates *IFNG* transcription.
- Induces IL-12Rβ2 chain expression, making cells responsive to IL-12 (positive feedback).
- Reciprocally suppresses [[Th2]] differentiation by inhibiting [[GATA3]].
- Promotes epigenetic remodeling at the *IFNG* locus.

### Cross-Regulation
Th1 differentiation is mutually antagonistic with [[Th2]] and Th17 pathways:
- T-bet inhibits GATA3, suppressing [[Th2]] development.
- T-bet sequesters Runx3 from RORγt, limiting Th17 differentiation.
- [[IL-10]] and TGF-β can restrain Th1 responses to prevent immunopathology.

## Effector Mechanisms

### IFNγ Production
IFNγ is the signature Th1 cytokine with pleiotropic immune effects:
- **Macrophage activation**: Classically activates macrophages (M1 polarization), enhancing phagocytosis, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and nitric oxide (NO) production.
- **MHC upregulation**: Increases [[MHC type I]] and [[MHC type II]] expression on APCs, enhancing antigen presentation.
- **IgG class switching**: Promotes B cell switching to opsonizing IgG subclasses (IgG1, IgG3 in humans).
- **Antiviral state**: Induces antiviral gene expression in neighboring cells.

### TNFα Production
Th1 cells produce [[TNFα]], which contributes to macrophage activation, endothelial upregulation of adhesion molecules, and granuloma formation.

### CD40L Expression
Th1 cells express CD40 ligand (CD40L/CD154), which activates CD40 on macrophages and B cells, enhancing microbicidal activity and antibody responses.

## Biological Functions

### Intracellular Pathogen Defense
Th1 responses are essential for host defense against:
- *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* — Th1-mediated macrophage activation is the primary defense mechanism.
- *Leishmania* species — IFNγ-activated macrophages kill intracellular parasites.
- *Listeria monocytogenes* — Th1 cells coordinate innate and adaptive clearance.
- Viral infections — IFNγ activates antiviral mechanisms and enhances CTL responses.

### Granuloma Formation
Th1 cells drive granuloma formation through IFNγ and TNFα, walling off persistent intracellular pathogens that cannot be fully eliminated.

### Transplant Immunity
Th1 cells mediate acute allograft rejection through direct allorecognition and macrophage activation.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **Autoimmune Disease**: Excessive Th1 responses drive tissue damage in multiple sclerosis (Th1 cells in CNS lesions), rheumatoid arthritis (synovial macrophage activation), and type 1 diabetes (β-cell destruction).
- **Delayed-Type Hypersensitivity**: Th1 cells mediate DTH reactions, including the tuberculin skin test.
- **Tumor Immunology**: Th1 cells and IFNγ are associated with anti-tumor immunity and favorable cancer prognosis. Tumor microenvironments that suppress Th1 responses promote immune evasion.
- **Therapeutic Modulation**: Promoting Th1 responses is a goal in cancer immunotherapy and chronic infections. Suppressing Th1 responses is beneficial in autoimmune diseases.
