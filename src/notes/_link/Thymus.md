# Thymus

The **thymus** is a bilobed mediastinal organ and the primary site of [[T Lymphocyte|T cell]] development. It is composed of two functional compartments: the **thymic epithelial space (TES)**, containing the cortex and medulla where [[Thymopoiesis|thymopoiesis]] occurs, and the **perivascular space (PVS)**, a non-thymopoietic compartment of [[Adipose Tissue|adipocytes]], peripheral lymphocytes, and stroma.

## Structure and Compartments

- **Cortex** — Site of early thymocyte development, including [[T Cell Receptor|T cell receptor]] gene rearrangement and positive selection. Contains cortical [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|thymic epithelial cells (cTECs)]] that present self-peptide/MHC complexes to developing thymocytes.
- **Medulla** — Site of subsequent development where thymocytes undergo negative selection to eliminate auto-reactive clones. Contains medullary TECs (mTECs) that express tissue-restricted antigens via the AIRE transcription factor.
- **Corticomedullary junction** — Entry point for lymphoid progenitors; site where thymocyte migration begins and ends during development.
- **Perivascular space** — Non-epithelial compartment that expands with age, progressively replacing functional TES tissue.

## Age-Related Involution

The thymus begins to involute shortly after birth. The lymphoid component of the TES decreases approximately 3% per year through middle age (35–45 years) and ~1% per year thereafter. By age 70, the TES constitutes less than 10% of total thymic tissue. This [[Thymic Involution|involution]] results in:

- Reduced output of naïve [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]] to the periphery
- Restricted [[T Cell Receptor|TCR]] repertoire diversity
- Increased susceptibility to infection, [[Autoimmune Disease|autoimmunity]], and cancer
- Impaired immune reconstitution following [[Chemotherapy|chemotherapy]] or [[HIV|HIV-1]] infection

> [!info] Evolutionary hypothesis
> Shanley et al. speculate that thymic involution evolved to redirect energy from T cell production to reproduction once a diverse TCR repertoire is established. Dowling and colleagues suggest involution permits peripheral homeostatic proliferation of a well-adapted limited clone repertoire.

## Rejuvenation Strategies

Multiple approaches are under investigation to restore thymic function:

- **[[Sex Steroid Ablation]]** — Removal of sex steroids (surgically or via [[LHRH]] analogues) restores thymic architecture and cellularity
- **Cytokine therapy** — [[IL-7]], [[Keratinocyte Growth Factor|KGF]], and [[IL-22]] promote thymic epithelial cell proliferation and thymocyte survival
- **Hormone therapy** — [[Human Growth Hormone|Growth hormone]] and [[Ghrelin]] stimulate thymopoiesis via [[IGF1|IGF-1]] signaling and direct receptor-mediated effects

> [!important] Stromal microenvironment
> The thymus must function as a whole three-dimensional structure for T cell differentiation. Rejuvenation strategies must consider all cellular and non-cellular components, including the unique microenvironments that nurture and educate T cells (Anderson et al., 1993).

## Key Measurements

- **[[sjTREC]]** — Molecular marker for recent thymic emigrants; shows a three-log decline across an 80-year human lifespan
- **Thymic weight** — Retained thymic weight in aged individuals reflects residual thymocyte-depleted stroma
- **Thymic cellularity** — Direct measure of thymocyte numbers; decreases steadily with age
