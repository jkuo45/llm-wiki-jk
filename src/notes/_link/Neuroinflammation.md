# Neuroinflammation

## Overview

[[Neuroinflammation]] is the immunological response that occurs within the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]], characterized by activation of brain-resident glial and endothelial cells and infiltration of peripheral immune cells. It is a hallmark of multiple neurological disorders, including [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]] (AD), [[Huntington's Disease|Huntington's disease]] (HD), [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] (PD), and acute brain injuries. While acute, well-contained neuroinflammation supports protection and clearance, sustained and dysregulated signaling has deleterious consequences for [[Neuron|neuronal homeostasis]] and accelerates the progression of [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative disease]]. The [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] pathway has emerged as a central driver of chronic neuroinflammation, bridging proteotoxic and mitochondrial stress to innate immune activation.

## Cellular Effectors

Neuroinflammation is orchestrated by both resident and infiltrating immune populations. Brain-resident cells — [[Microglia]], [[Astrocytes]], and [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]] — undergo pro-inflammatory activation, while peripheral [[Monocytes]], [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]], and [[B Cells|B cells]] infiltrate the parenchyma, particularly when the [[Blood-Brain Barrier|blood-brain barrier]] is compromised. Cytokines and [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs)]] produced by these cells act in a paracrine manner on neighboring neurons, amplifying and propagating inflammatory responses. The phenotype of activated glia is heterogeneous and shaped by upstream cues: sustained [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling and [[NF-κB|NF-κB]] activation promote neurotoxic states, whereas resolution programs restore homeostatic functions.

## cGAS-STING as a Central Driver

A unifying insight is that many [[Proteinopathy|proteinopathies]] ultimately generate cytosolic dsDNA — predominantly via [[mtDNA]] leakage from [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial stress]] or from [[DNA Damage|genomic DNA damage]] — that activates [[cGAS]] and [[STING]]. This triggers [[IRF3]]- and [[NF-κB|NF-κB]]-dependent transcription of [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] and pro-inflammatory [[Cytokines]], establishing a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop. Noncanonical activation also occurs: the [[Tau|tau]]–[[PQBP1]] complex engages cGAS through direct protein–protein interaction, bypassing DNA entirely. Although cGAS-STING signaling is predominantly deleterious in chronic disease, it is context dependent — microglial STING preserves hippocampal [[Blood-Brain Barrier|BBB]] integrity during aging, complicating blanket pathway inhibition. Chronic neuroinflammation also intersects with [[Inflammaging]] and [[Senescence|cellular senescence]], contributing to age-related vulnerability.

## Parkinson's Disease & Neuromelanin-Specific Biology

In [[Parkinson's Disease|PD]], neuroinflammation is anatomically restricted to regions enriched in [[Neuromelanin]], implicating the pigment as an active immune stimulus rather than a passive marker. The response is orchestrated by resident and infiltrating populations:

- **Microglia** ([[Microgliosis]]): resident macrophages that, when activated, release [[Cytokines]], [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], and nitric oxide.
- **Astrocytes** (astrogliosis): amplify signaling and form the glial scar.
- **Lymphocytes**: perivascular T cells, potentially engaging [[Major histocompatibility complex class I]] on neurons.
- **Inflammasomes** (e.g., NLRP3): intracellular platforms converting danger signals into IL-1β maturation.

The principal trigger in pigmented nuclei is the release of extracellular [[Neuromelanin]] and [[Alpha-synuclein]] aggregates ([[Lewy Bodies]]) from dying neurons. Neuromelanin acts as a damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP), binding scavenger receptors and priming microglia; mitochondrial [[Complex I]] defects and [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]] further release DAMPs that sustain inflammation. Post-mortem PD brains show microglial activation and MHC-I upregulation confined to the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] and [[Locus Coeruleus]]—the neuromelanin-rich nuclei—while sparing adjacent non-pigmented regions. This spatial coupling suggests pigment drives immune activation, possibly through its bound metals, quinones, or neo-antigens.

Chronic neuroinflammation creates a self-perpetuating loop: activated glia stress neighboring neurons, causing more death, more pigment release, and more inflammation. This cycle is thought to convert acute, toxin-driven injury (e.g., [[MPTP]], [[Manganese]]) into progressive neurodegeneration and may facilitate α-synuclein spread. Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory strategies—NLRP3 inhibitors, microglial phenotype switching, cytokine blockade—are pursued as [[Neuroprotection]]; TSPO-PET and peripheral inflammatory biomarkers are being developed to monitor the response.

## Connections

  - [[cGAS-STING Pathway]]: canonical and noncanonical activation produces IFN-I and cytokines that drive neuroinflammation.
  - [[Microglia]]: primary sensor and effector; chronic activation is a major neurotoxic source.
  - [[Astrocytes]]: adopt neurotoxic A1 states and senesce under cGAS-STING signaling.
  - [[Type I Interferon]]: downstream readout that amplifies glial activation and synapse loss.
  - [[Tauopathy]]: pathogenic tau activates microglial cGAS-STING via PQBP1 and mtDNA.
  - [[Proteinopathy]]: unifying upstream trigger through cytosolic dsDNA generation.
  - [[Blood-Brain Barrier]]: barrier disruption permits leukocyte infiltration, worsening inflammation.
  - [[Inflammaging]]: age-related sterile inflammation that overlaps with neurodegenerative neuroinflammation.
  - [[Parkinson's Disease]]: disease in which neuromelanin-restricted neuroinflammation accelerates progression.
  - [[Neuromelanin]]: pigment that regionally restricts and triggers inflammation as a DAMP.
  - [[Microgliosis]]: glial activation central to the Parkinsonian response.
  - [[Alpha-synuclein]]: aggregate danger signal sustaining inflammation.
  - [[Lewy Bodies]]: inclusions releasing inflammatory stimuli.
  - [[Major histocompatibility complex class I]]: neuronal antigen presentation in PD.
  - [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]]: DAMP source perpetuating inflammation.
  - [[Neuroprotection]]: therapeutic goal of immunomodulation.
