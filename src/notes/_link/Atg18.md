# Atg18

**Atg18** is a peripheral membrane protein of *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* that binds
[[PtdIns3P]] through an N-terminal **PX domain** and is one of the essential effectors
of phagophore expansion during macroautophagy. It is the budding-yeast counterpart of
the mammalian WIPI family (yeast Atg18 is conventionally aligned to human WIPI1/2), and
the naming in the vault — "Atg18 is recruited by PtdIns3P" — is exactly the WIPI/Atg18
axis.

## Domain architecture and binding

The protein carries two defined modules:

- **PX domain (N-terminal).** Binds the head group of [[PtdIns3P]] with specificity; the
  PX domain is the reason Atg18 sits on autophagic membranes at all, since PtdIns3P is
  the membrane landmark set by the class III PI3K complex
  ([[Vps34]]–[[Vps15]]–Beclin 1–Atg14) at the phagophore. Beclin 1 and the
  autophagy-specific Atg14 subunit are named here without links because no separate
  notes exist for them yet.
- **WD40-repeat region (C-terminal).** Propagates along the membrane and, with Atg2,
  sustains membrane expansion.

The PX domain is a general phosphoinositide-binding module, not an autophagy-specific
one: its affinity and localization are tuned by phosphorylation and by partner proteins,
which is how Atg18 avoids being captured indiscriminately by the much larger pool of
PtdIns3P on endosomes.

## Mechanism in autophagosome formation

The accepted order of events is:

1. The phagophore nucleates at the [[Phagophore Assembly Site|PAS]], which is continuous
   with the ER and is where PtdIns3P is generated locally by the Vps34 complex I
   (the Atg14-containing, autophagy-specific complex).
2. PtdIns3P recruits WIPI2 and Atg18 to the phagophore rim.
3. WIPI2 recruits Atg16L1, which brings the E3-like ubiquitin ligase machinery for
   [[LC3]] lipidation (via Atg8 conjugation machinery); Atg18 additionally stabilizes the
   Atg1–Atg17 scaffold and, in yeast, interacts with the septin cytoskeleton to position
   the PAS.
4. Atg18–Atg2 form a complex and drive **phospholipid transfer** from the ER/other
   membranes to the phagophore, supplying the membrane material that lets the cup close
   and the autophagosome reach size. In mammals the analogous transfer is executed by
   the class II PI3K complex III and ATG2A, and by the ATG9A complex.

Atg18 is therefore not simply a *recruiter*: it is part of the mechanism that supplies
membrane, so its loss reduces autophagosome size and impairs flux rather than
eliminating autophagy.

## Other functions

- **Endosomal trafficking.** Atg18 also binds the yeast phosphoinositide [[PtdIns(4,5)P2]]
  and is implicated in endosomal sorting and in vacuole biogenesis, overlapping with the
  Vps34 complex II.
- **Vacuole fusion.** Atg18 interacts with the HOPS complex and the v-SNARE
  [[VAMP8]]-containing fusion machinery, and is reported to be required for
  vacuole–vacuole fusion downstream of autophagosome closure, i.e. a post-closure role
  distinct from its role in expansion.
- **Lipid droplet and peroxisome turnover.** Atg18 and WIPI1/2 have been implicated in
  [[Lipophagy]] and [[Pexophagy]] via PtdIns3P-dependent recruitment.

## Orthology caveat

Atg18 is **not** the orthologue of mammalian WIPI1/2 in a strict one-to-one sense —
yeast Atg18 corresponds to a WIPI/Atg18 family that also includes Atg37 and Atg23-like
partners, and mammalian WIPI3/4 have diverged in PX-domain specificity. The
"yeast Atg18" and "mammalian WIPI" literature is frequently conflated; where a claim is
made about Atg18 in yeast, it should not be silently generalized to WIPI2 in mammals.

## Documents

- [[PtdIns3P]] — the phosphoinositide that Atg18's PX domain binds, generated by Vps34 at
  the phagophore; the recruitment step that places Atg18 on the autophagic membrane.

## Connections

- [[PtdIns3P]] — Atg18 is the effector through which the phagophore's PtdIns3P signal is
  converted into membrane expansion. PtdIns3P is generated and destroyed; Atg18 is what
  the signal acts on, so the pair is best understood as a lipid–protein module.
- [[Vps34]] — the kinase that produces the PtdIns3P Atg18 reads. Vps34 complex I
  (Atg14-containing) is autophagy-specific and localizes to the PAS; perturbing Vps34
  removes Atg18 recruitment and blocks autophagy.
- [[WIPI2]] — the functional mammalian analogue in the same step: WIPI2 is what recruits
  Atg16L1 to the phagophore, whereas yeast has no Atg16L1 and uses WIPI2-independent
  routes — an important difference when comparing the two systems.
- [[Atg16L1]] — in mammals the WIPI2–Atg16L1 axis is the direct route from PtdIns3P to
  LC3 lipidation; in yeast the equivalent function is distributed across WIPI2, Atg12
  and the Atg8 lipidation machinery. Comparing "PX-domain protein" behaviour across
  species without noting this is a common source of error.
- [[Phagophore Assembly Site]] — the site where Atg18 concentrates and where the
  septin-dependent scaffold that positions the PAS acts; not a discrete organelle but a
  dynamic ER subdomain.
- [[Vps15]] — the regulatory subunit of the Vps34 complex, whose kinase-activity
  modulation (e.g. by the ULK1 complex) determines whether PtdIns3P and hence Atg18
  recruitment occurs.
- [[ULK1]] — the upstream kinase complex that positions the autophagy machinery at the
  PAS; its activity is required for Atg18/WIPI2 recruitment to be productive.
- [[LC3]] — the lipidation target. WIPI2 tethers Atg16L1 for LC3-I lipidization and
  deubiquitination; LC3/GABARAP lipidation is what generates the membrane curvature
  Atg18 then helps to expand.
