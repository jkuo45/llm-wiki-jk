# Type 2 Diabetes

## Overview

Type 2 Diabetes (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder defined by peripheral [[Insulin Resistance|insulin resistance]] and progressive decline of pancreatic β-cell function, resulting in chronic [[Hyperglycemia|hyperglycemia]]. It is strongly associated with aging, obesity, and sedentary lifestyle, and represents a major driver of cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, neuropathy, and retinopathy.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

- Multiorgan metabolic derangement: impaired [[Insulin|insulin]]-stimulated glucose uptake in [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] and adipose tissue, unrestrained hepatic gluconeogenesis, and β-cell secretory failure.
- [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|Mitochondrial dysfunction]] is a central pathogenic mechanism linking metabolic overload to diabetic complications; the mitochondrial [[NAD+|NAD+]]-dependent deacetylase [[SIRT3]] is consistently downregulated in diabetic tissues, where its deficiency correlates with insulin resistance and disturbed systemic energy homeostasis[238]-[240].
- In skeletal muscle, SIRT3 deficiency impairs insulin-stimulated glucose translocation, aggravating peripheral insulin resistance, while restoration of SIRT3 via lifestyle interventions or [[FGF21]] signaling improves mitochondrial integrity and myocardial function.

## Clinical Relevance & SIRT3 Connection

- [[Diabetic Cardiomyopathy|Diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM)]] is a major cardiac complication of T2DM in which SIRT3 activation improves mitochondrial respiratory capacity and suppresses oxidative stress (e.g., via the AGO2/cytochrome b axis).
- Chronic hyperglycemia suppresses hippocampal SIRT3, contributing to cognitive deficits through mitochondrial Ca2+ overload and neuronal [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]].
- SIRT3 also preserves pancreatic β-cell viability and mediates the redox-modulating effects of [[Irisin|irisin]] in diabetic periodontitis models, establishing SIRT3 as a convergent therapeutic target across diabetic complications.
