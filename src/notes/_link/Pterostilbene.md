# Pterostilbene

Pterostilbene is a natural stilbenoid [[Polyphenols|polyphenol]], structurally the 3,5-dimethoxy analogue of [[Resveratrol]], found predominantly in blueberries (*Vaccinium* spp.) and, in smaller amounts, in grapes and peanuts. The substitution of two hydroxyl groups with methoxy groups markedly improves its pharmacokinetic profile: oral bioavailability, lipophilicity, and metabolic stability all exceed those of resveratrol, permitting meaningful tissue concentrations at physiological doses.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

As a plant phytoalexin, pterostilbene belongs to the stilbene class of secondary metabolites. Its greater lipophilicity facilitates cellular uptake and mitochondrial accumulation relative to its parent compound.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Pterostilbene is a calorie-restriction mimetic. It directly activates [[Sirtuins|SIRT1]] and upstream energy sensors such as [[AMPK]], increasing [[NAD+]]-dependent deacetylation and metabolic efficiency. Like other [[Xenohormesis|xenohormetic]] compounds, it recapitulates the molecular signature of nutrient scarcity — enhanced fatty-acid oxidation, antioxidant defense via [[NRF2]], and dampened inflammatory signaling. Preclinical studies associate it with improved endothelial function, lipid homeostasis, and cognitive performance.

## Physiological Function & Longevity Relevance

By engaging the same nutrient-sensing axes modulated by [[Caloric Restriction]] (SIRT1, AMPK, mTOR), pterostilbene is investigated as a geroprotective nutraceutical. It lowers markers of [[Oxidative Stress]] and supports [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial health]], situating it within the broader sirtuin–NAD+–autophagy longevity network.

> [!info] Bioavailability Advantage
> The methoxy groups that distinguish pterostilbene from resveratrol are the principal reason for its superior oral absorption and slower clearance.
