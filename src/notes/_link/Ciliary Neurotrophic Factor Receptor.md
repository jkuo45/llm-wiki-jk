# Ciliary Neurotrophic Factor Receptor

**The ciliary neurotrophic factor receptor** is not a single protein but a three-component cell-surface complex. CNTF first binds the ligand-binding α-subunit **CNTFRα** (gene *CNTFR*, a glycophosphatidylinositol-anchored protein), which then recruits the signalling subunits [[gp130]] and **LIFRβ** (leukaemia inhibitory factor receptor, gene *LIFR*). The assembled **1:1:1:1** heterotetramer signals through JAK1/JAK2 to [[STAT3]]. CNTFRα confers the complex its tissue specificity: because neuronal expression of CNTFRα is restricted, CNTF acts predominantly on the nervous system even though gp130 and LIFRβ are broadly expressed.

## Mechanism of Activation

Structural work on the gp130 cytokine family shows an asymmetric assembly. CNTFRα occupies site 1 of the CNTF four-helix bundle, gp130 engages site 2 via its cytokine-binding homology region, and LIFRβ engages the high-affinity site 3. Recruitment is **non-cooperative**, unlike the symmetric hexamer formed by IL-6, IL-6Rα and gp130, because the LIFRβ site-3 contact is stable on its own. Bringing the two signalling chains into proximity trans-phosphorylates their pre-associated Janus kinases, which phosphorylate receptor tyrosines that dock STAT3.

> [!info] Ligand beyond CNTF
> CNTFRα also chaperones secretion of cardiotrophin-like cytokine factor 1 (CLCF1) by binding CLCF1's site 2 and 3, generating a composite cytokine that signals through gp130/LIFRβ. This likely explains why CNTFR-null mice die at birth with profound neuronal deficits while CNTF-null mice survive with only mild adult-onset muscle weakness — CNTFRα has a second, developmentally essential ligand.

## Physiological and Therapeutic Relevance

CNTFRα is expressed on motor neurons and other neurons, oligodendrocytes, and — notably — on humanin-binding cells, where overexpression of CNTFRα or WSX-1 (the LIFRβ paralogue, an IL-27 subunit) enhances humanin binding. The receptor therefore participates in both cytokine and mitochondrial-derived-peptide signalling in the same neuronal context.

CNTFRα has also been developed as a recombinant protein — an IgG–CNTFRα fusion — as a delivery vehicle that extends circulating half-life. Independently, CNTF and CNTF-family signalling is of interest in ALS and other neurodegenerative disease because receptor activation promotes motor neuron survival; the narrow CNS expression of the receptor is what makes the systemic toxicity of CNTF (cachexia and anorexia via hypothalamic signalling) manageable.

> [!warning] Receptor, not cytokine
> This note covers the receptor complex. CNTF, the ligand, does not yet have a note in the vault, and CLCF1 is distinct from CNTF despite using the same receptor.

## Connections
- [[gp130]] — gp130 is the shared signal-transducing subunit of the complex and the only component with a cytoplasmic domain, recruiting JAK1 and transducing activation into STAT3 phosphorylation.
- [[Leukemia Inhibitory Factor]] — LIFRβ is the second signalling chain of the heterotetramer and is also the receptor component used by LIF itself, which signals without any α-subunit.
- [[STAT3]] — STAT3 is the dominant downstream transcription factor; CNTF receptor signalling specificity among gp130 cytokines is set by receptor tyrosine motifs that dock STAT3 rather than STAT1.
- [[Humanin]] — The mitochondrial-derived peptide humanin signals partly through a CNTFR/WSX-1/gp130 trimeric receptor, and CNTFRα overexpression increases humanin binding to neuronal cells.
- [[Motor Neurons]] — Motor neurons are the best-established CNTF target; receptor activation promotes their survival and is the rationale for CNTF-based development in motor neuron disease.
