# Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells

**[[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]]** (iPSCs) are a type of [[Pluripotency|pluripotent]] stem cell generated directly from differentiated adult [[Somatic Cells]] by ectopic expression of defined transcription factors. The technology was pioneered by [[Shinya Yamanaka]] and colleagues in 2006, who demonstrated that four factors — [[Oct4]], [[Sox2]], [[Klf4]], and [[c-Myc]], collectively the [[Yamanaka Factors]] (or [[OSKM]] cassette) — could revert fibroblasts to an embryonic-stem-cell-like state. Yamanaka shared the 2012 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for this discovery. iPSCs self-renew indefinitely and can differentiate into all three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm), paralleling [[Embryonic Stem Cells]] without the ethical constraints of embryo derivation.

In neurodegeneration research, patient-derived iPSC lines carrying disease-associated genotypes are differentiated into human [[Neuron|neurons]], [[Microglia]], [[Astrocytes]], and motor neurons to model cell-autonomous mechanisms of [[Neurodegeneration]] in a human genetic context.

## Mechanism of Reprogramming

Reprogramming is a gradual, stochastic transition requiring sustained transgene expression (typically 1–2 weeks). The factors act as a pioneer-like network: [[Oct4]] and [[Sox2]] form a core heterodimer occupying both active and silent chromatin, recruiting [[Nanog]] and chromatin remodelers such as the [[NuRD complex]]. [[Klf4]] and [[c-Myc]] reinforce proliferation and open chromatin. Mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition (MET), global DNA demethylation, [[Histone]] mark resetting (loss of H3K9me3/H3K27me3 repressive marks, gain of H3K4me3/H3K27ac active marks), and X-chromosome reactivation ultimately establish the pluripotent ground state. Partial reprogramming (e.g., to [[Induced Neural Stem Cells]] or [[Mesenchymal Stromal Cells]]-derived intermediates) is used to study [[Cellular Reprogramming]] without full pluripotency. [[MYCN]] and [[Wnt signaling]] augment efficiency by stabilizing pluripotency-associated factors.

## Physiological & Developmental Significance

iPSCs recapitulate the naive and primed pluripotent states and provide an essentially unlimited source of patient-matched cells. They bridge basic [[Stem Cell Research]] with translational medicine, enabling the study of human lineage specification that is otherwise inaccessible.

## Modeling cGAS-STING in Human Cells

iPSC-derived cultures provide a physiologically relevant platform to interrogate the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] outside the limitations of overexpression mouse models such as [[5xFAD]]. Notably, STING inhibition reduces cell death in TDP-43–treated, iPSC-derived motor neurons, demonstrating a cell-autonomous neuroprotective effect of pathway suppression. This human-cell evidence complements in vivo studies and supports the translational relevance of [[STING Inhibitors]] and [[cGAS Inhibitors]] for [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]] and other proteinopathies.

## Advantages and Limitations

iPSC models capture human-specific biology and genetic risk (e.g., [[APOE4]], [[TREM2]] R47H) that are underutilized in animal studies, while avoiding species differences in innate immunity. However, they lack the full multicellular CNS milieu and peripheral immune interactions, so findings require validation in intact systems. Key technical limitations are incomplete reprogramming, genomic integration from integrating vectors (mitigated by Sendai viral, episomal, and mRNA/[[CRISPR]]-based methods), and residual [[Tumorigenicity]] risk from undifferentiated cells. They remain essential for dissecting neuronal versus glial contributions to [[Neuroinflammation]] and for screening degraders such as [[PROTAC]] approaches targeting cGAS/STING.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

iPSCs have transformed [[Disease Modeling]] for genetic disorders — including [[Frontotemporal Dementia]], [[Olivopontocerebellar Atrophy]], and [[Neurodegeneration]] — by generating isogenic neuronal models from patient fibroblasts. They power [[Drug Discovery]] (toxicity screening, phenotype-based assays), gene-correction strategies, and [[Regenerative Medicine]] applications such as retinal pigment epithelium grafts, cardiomyocyte patches, and dopaminergic neurons in Parkinson's trials. Pluripotency is conventionally validated by the [[Teratoma]] assay (tumor formation in immunodeficient mice containing all three germ layers), a gold-standard readout of differentiation potential. iPSCs also serve as a platform for [[Rejuvenation]] research, where transient [[Reprogramming]] can reset age-associated epigenetic clocks without erasing cell identity.
