# TIMP2

**TIMP2** (Tissue Inhibitor of Metalloproteinases-2) is a soluble secreted protein that inhibits matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and, uniquely, also facilitates pro-MMP-2 activation at the cell surface. This dual role—inhibiting active MMPs while promoting activation of a specific pro-MMP—makes TIMP2 a key regulator of the balance between ECM degradation and remodelling. TIMP2 is widely expressed and plays important roles in vascular homeostasis, development, and cancer.

## Structure & Mechanism

- **Protein:** TIMP2 is a ~21 kDa glycoprotein composed of an N-terminal inhibitory domain (~125 residues) and a C-terminal domain. Like other TIMPs, the N-terminal domain binds the catalytic site of target MMPs in a 1:1 stoichiometric complex.
- **Solubility:** TIMP2 is soluble and diffusible, unlike [[TIMP3]] which is ECM-tethered.
- **Primary targets:** TIMP2 inhibits [[MMP-2]] (gelatinase A), [[MMP-9]] (gelatinase B), [[MMP-1]] (collagenase-1), [[MMP-3]] (stromelysin-1), [[MMP-7]], [[MMP-9]], [[MMP-14]] (MT1-MMP), and [[MMP-16]] (MT3-MMP).

> [!important] Dual Role in MMP-2 Regulation
> TIMP2 is unique among TIMPs in its ability to both inhibit active MMP-2 AND facilitate pro-MMP-2 activation. At low concentrations, TIMP2 bridges MT1-MMP and pro-MMP-2 on the cell surface, forming a ternary complex that enables pro-MMP-2 activation. At higher concentrations, TIMP2 inhibits both MT1-MMP and active MMP-2. This concentration-dependent dual role makes TIMP2 a molecular switch for MMP-2 activity.

## Pro-MMP-2 Activation Mechanism

TIMP2 facilitates pro-MMP-2 activation through a well-characterised cell-surface mechanism:

1. **TIMP2 binds MT1-MMP:** Low concentrations of TIMP2 bind MT1-MMP on the cell surface, forming a MT1-MMP/TIMP2 complex.
2. **Ternary complex formation:** The MT1-MMP/TIMP2 complex recruits pro-MMP-2 via hemopexin domain interactions, forming a trimolecular complex (MT1-MMP/TIMP2/pro-MMP-2).
3. **Pro-MMP-2 cleavage:** Adjacent MT1-MMP molecules (not inhibited by TIMP2) cleave the pro-peptide of pro-MMP-2, generating the active 62-kDa MMP-2 species.
4. **Regulation:** Higher TIMP2 concentrations inhibit both MT1-MMP and active MMP-2, providing negative feedback.

This mechanism is essential for pericellular proteolysis during cell migration, invasion, and tissue remodelling.

## Regulation

- **Transcriptional:** TIMP2 expression is regulated by growth factors (PDGF, EGF, FGF), cytokines, and ECM components. TGF-β upregulates TIMP2 in some cell types.
- **Constitutive expression:** TIMP2 is constitutively expressed in many tissues, unlike TIMP1 which is more inducible.
- **Age-related changes:** TIMP2 levels may decline with age in some tissues, altering the MMP/TIMP balance.

## Physiological Roles

### ECM Homeostasis
TIMP2 maintains ECM integrity by inhibiting a broad spectrum of MMPs. The balance between TIMP2 and its target MMPs determines net ECM turnover in tissues, particularly for collagen IV and gelatin (MMP-2 substrates).

### Development
TIMP2 is essential for embryonic development. TIMP2-null mice have developmental defects in certain tissues and show altered MMP-2 activity.

### Wound Healing
TIMP2 is upregulated during wound healing to limit MMP activity and protect newly deposited ECM during the remodelling phase.

### Vascular Homeostasis
TIMP2 regulates vascular remodelling by controlling MMP-2 and MMP-14 activity in the arterial wall. The TIMP2/MMP-2 balance influences arterial stiffness and remodelling.

## Pathological Roles

### Cancer
TIMP2 has complex roles in cancer:
- **Anti-tumorigenic:** Inhibits MMPs that degrade basement membranes, limiting tumour invasion and metastasis.
- **Pro-tumorigenic:** At low concentrations, facilitates pro-MMP-2 activation that promotes tumour cell invasion. The concentration-dependent dual role makes TIMP2 a context-dependent player in cancer.
- **Angiogenesis:** TIMP2 inhibits MT1-MMP-dependent endothelial cell migration and angiogenesis. High TIMP2 expression in tumour stroma may restrict tumour angiogenesis.
- **Prognosis:** High TIMP2 expression in tumour tissue correlates with better prognosis in some cancers (breast, colorectal) but with poor prognosis in others, reflecting its dual role.

### Cardiovascular Disease
TIMP2 is involved in arterial remodelling and atherosclerosis. Reduced TIMP2 may contribute to plaque instability by permitting excessive MMP-2 activity.

### Fibrosis
TIMP2 contributes to fibrosis by inhibiting MMPs that degrade collagen, promoting net ECM accumulation alongside [[TIMP1]].

### Osteoarthritis
TIMP2 dysregulation contributes to cartilage degradation in osteoarthritis, where the MMP/TIMP balance is shifted toward proteolysis.
