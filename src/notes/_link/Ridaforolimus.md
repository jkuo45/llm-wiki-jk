# Ridaforolimus

**Ridaforolimus** (AP23573, MK-8669, formerly **deforolimus**) is a second-generation rapamycin analogue and allosteric inhibitor of [[mTOR]]. Like other [[Rapalog|rapalogs]], it binds the FKBP12–mTOR FRB domain to allosterically inhibit primarily [[mTORC1]], inducing a starvation-like state that suppresses cell growth, proliferation, and angiogenesis. It differs from [[Rapamycin|sirolimus]] mainly in improved aqueous solubility and oral bioavailability, achieved by chemical modification of the cyclohexyl pipecolate region.

> [!info] Mechanism
> The drug–FKBP12 complex allosterically closes the mTOR kinase gate, reducing mTORC1 kinase activity toward S6K1 and 4E-BP1. Because mTORC1 is the nutrient sensor that restrains [[Autophagy]], its inhibition de-represses autophagic flux — the mechanistic reason rapalogs are studied as geroprotectors. Whether the effect is mTORC1-selective in vivo depends on exposure: chronic dosing also inhibits mTORC2 in some tissues, with associated glucose intolerance.

## Clinical development

Ridaforolimus was co-developed by ARIAD Pharmaceuticals and Merck & Co. under a 2010 agreement. The pivotal **SUCCEED** trial (Sarcoma Multi-Center Clinical Evaluation of the Efficacy of Ridaforolimus) was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III study in 711 patients with metastatic soft-tissue or bone sarcoma who had previously responded to chemotherapy, dosed at 40 mg/day for five of seven days.

SUCCEED met its primary endpoint on independent radiological review: a 28% reduction in the risk of progression or death (HR 0.72, p = 0.0001) with median progression-free survival of 17.7 versus 14.6 weeks. Investigator-assigned analysis was nominally stronger (HR 0.69, median PFS 22.4 versus 14.7 weeks). The overall survival benefit was modest and the FDA was not persuaded the PFS gain translated into a sufficient clinical benefit; the NDA was rejected in 2012, and the EMA application was withdrawn in November 2012. **Ridaforolimus is not an approved drug** and is not in current clinical use.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The SUCCEED PFS benefit was not considered large enough for approval on its own, so ridaforolimus is best treated as a well-characterized but unapproved research compound. It is routinely confused with approved rapalogs ([[Everolimus]], [[Temsirolimus]]); check the identifier before citing it in a protocol.

## Research relevance to geroprotection

Because ridaforolimus shares the rapamycin pharmacophore, much of the aging research on intermittent rapamycin dosing is extrapolated to it, and it is used as a comparator in mTOR-inhibitor screens. Its solubility advantage makes it a convenient tool compound for [[mTORC1]]-selective experiments. The same mTORC2-suppression caveat applies as for all rapalogs: long-term systemic exposure is not free of metabolic cost.
