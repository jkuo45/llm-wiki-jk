# NPC1L1

**NPC1L1** (Niemann-Pick C1-like 1; also *ACAT-like* protein, old alias SLC65A2) is a seven-transmembrane-domain endocytic cholesterol transporter localised to the apical brush border of small-intestinal enterocytes and to the canalicular membrane of hepatocytes. It mediates the cellular uptake of dietary and biliary cholesterol and of phytosterols, and is the molecular target of the cholesterol-absorption inhibitor [[Ezetimibe]].

> [!info] Human genetics
> This is one of the few cholesterol-handling proteins validated by human loss-of-function genetics. In a landmark 2014 *NEJM* analysis, humans carrying inactivating *NPC1L1* mutations had lower LDL cholesterol and roughly a **50% reduction in coronary heart disease risk** relative to non-carriers. That is an unusually large effect for a single lipid-handling allele, and it established reduced intestinal cholesterol absorption as a causally validated therapeutic target rather than a correlational one.

## Structure and internalisation

The protein is a large, heavily glycosylated, ~1300-residue 12-pass (7-transmembrane plus 3 lumenal) structure. Because it is a sterol-sensing receptor-like transporter rather than a classical channel, its activity is regulated less by the lipid bilayer than by **sterol-dependent internalisation**: in the presence of cholesterol, NPC1L1 is concentrated in clathrin-coated vesicles and traffics to endosomes; in sterol-depleted conditions it is retained at the cell surface. Ezetimibe's key action is on this internalisation step — it blocks the sterol-induced endocytosis of NPC1L1 rather than simply competing for substrate — which is why its effect saturates at a modest percentage of absorbed cholesterol. Cryo-EM of the NPC1L1–ezetimibe–NPC1L1 ternary complex (PDB 3QNT) shows the drug sitting at the interface between the two protomers, allosterically locking the "open" conformation.

The name derives from its homology to **NPC1**, the protein mutated in Niemann–Pick type C disease, though the functional relationship between the two is limited to sequence similarity.

## Physiological role

Dietary cholesterol arrives in the duodenum mixed with micellar bile salts, is released, and is imported across the enterocyte brush border by NPC1L1. In the enterocyte, cholesterol and phytosterols are then either re-esterified by ACAT and secreted into chylomicrons, or handled by ABC transporters at the basolateral side. NPC1L1's job is the uptake step, and it works bidirectionally — cholesterol is also effluxed back into the intestinal lumen, which is a recycling valve on net absorption.

In the hepatocyte, NPC1L1 sits at the canalicular (bile-facing) membrane and takes up cholesterol from bile, regulating biliary cholesterol concentration and preventing biliary cholesterol crystal formation. Knockout of hepatic NPC1L1 in mice produces high biliary cholesterol, gallstones, and hepatic free cholesterol accumulation with an increase in plasma aminotransferases; double knockouts with *ABCG5*/*ABCG8* are markedly protected — which is the experimental basis for the clinical use of ezetimibe to reduce gallstone risk in patients undergoing rapid weight loss or with high-risk metabolic profiles.

## Clinical and pharmacological relevance

[[Ezetimibe]] is the sole licensed NPC1L1 inhibitor. It blocks NPC1L1 internalisation and reduces intestinal cholesterol absorption by roughly 50%, lowering LDL by about 15–20% as monotherapy. Because blockade of absorption *lowers hepatic cholesterol*, the hepatocyte upregulates LDL receptor expression and hepatic cholesterol synthesis (via [[SREBP2]]) — a compensatory response that explains both the synergistic pairing with [[Statins]] (which block the synthesis arm) and the occasional paradox of mild transaminase elevation.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Variants in *NPC1L1* are associated with non-response to ezetimibe, and mutations in *NPC1L1*/*ABCG5*/*ABCG8* cause sitosterolemia, with premature atherosclerosis. Separately, NPC1L1 has been identified as an entry factor for hepatitis C virus at the hepatocyte surface, which has been proposed — but not validated in the clinic — as a rationale for repurposing ezetimibe in HCV. The transient early cancer signal in the simvastatin–ezetimibe combination trial was not confirmed in subsequent meta-analyses, so no carcinogenic concern stands.

## Connections

- [[Ezetimibe]] — The selective cholesterol-absorption inhibitor whose molecular target NPC1L1 is; blocks sterol-induced NPC1L1 internalisation at the brush border.
- [[Intestinal Absorption]] — The physiological process NPC1L1 rate-limits: without it, dietary and biliary cholesterol cannot enter the enterocyte.
- [[Cholesterol]] — The substrate; NPC1L1 sets whole-body cholesterol balance by controlling the absorption arm against hepatic synthesis and LDL clearance.
- [[Statins]] — Synergistic partner: ezetimibe lowers hepatic cholesterol and provokes the SREBP2-driven LDL-receptor upregulation that statins also rely on, so the two mechanisms add.
- [[SREBP2]] — The transcriptional response to cholesterol depletion induced by NPC1L1 blockade, driving increased hepatic cholesterol synthesis and LDL receptor expression.
- [[PCSK9]] — The other validated LDL-lowering node; PCSK9 degrades LDL receptors, so blocking it and blocking absorption raise LDL-receptor surface density by two independent routes.
- [[Liver]] — Hepatocyte canalicular NPC1L1 regulates biliary cholesterol and gallstone risk; hepatic loss causes free-cholesterol accumulation and transaminitis.
- [[NPC1]] — The Niemann–Pick C protein NPC1L1 is named for; the functional relationship is limited, but the name anchors the note in the lysosomal cholesterol-handling literature.
- [[Tangier disease]] — The mirror-image inherited cholesterol-transport disorder (ABCA1 loss) that makes the same "which transporter, which direction, which tissue" question concrete.
- [[Membrane Trafficking]] — NPC1L1's activity is regulated by clathrin-mediated internalisation, so its pharmacology is inseparable from endocytic trafficking.
- [[Endocytosis]] — The process that internalises NPC1L1 in response to sterol, and the level at which ezetimibe acts.
- [[Cholesterol]] — Also the metabolite whose cytosolic sensing by SREBPs links NPC1L1 activity to whole-body lipid homeostasis.
- [[Low-Density Lipoprotein|LDL]] — The clinical endpoint NPC1L1 blockade lowers, via hepatic LDL-receptor upregulation.
- [[Hyperlipidemia]] — The indication in which NPC1L1 inhibition is a standard, mechanism-specific therapy.
- [[Insulin Resistance]] — Metabolic-syndrome patients are typically both hyperinsulinemic and hyperabsorptive, which is why ezetimibe is often paired with a statin in this population.
