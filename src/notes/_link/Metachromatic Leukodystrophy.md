# Metachromatic Leukodystrophy

**Metachromatic leukodystrophy (MLD)** is an autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disease caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in *ARSA*, the gene encoding **arylsulfatase A**. The resulting enzyme deficiency blocks the lysosomal degradation of **sulfatides** (cerebroside sulfate), which accumulate in the endolysosomal system of oligodendrocytes, Schwann cells, and — to a lesser degree — kidney and gallbladder epithelium. The name comes from the metachromatic staining of accumulated sulfatide in histological sections.

## Pathophysiology

> [!info] Mechanism
> ARSA initiates sulfatide catabolism by removing the sulfate group from galactosylceramide sulfate to produce galactosylceramide, which is then cleaved by [[GALC|galactocerebrosidase]]. Loss of the first step is sufficient: the substrate accumulates. Sulfatide accumulation disrupts myelin lipid composition, triggers microglial activation, and produces the characteristic white-matter demyelination with relative sparing of gray matter. Notably, a *second* mutation in *PSAP* (encoding saposin B, the activator of GALC) produces an identical biochemical and clinical phenotype — a reminder that sulfatide catabolism depends on both an enzyme and its activator.

Sulfatide also accumulates in urine, which is the basis of the standard diagnostic test (arylsulfatase A activity in leukocytes or fibroblasts plus urine sulfatide levels). The metachromatic granules are visible on cresyl-violet-stained tissue sections.

## Clinical spectrum

MLD is classified by age of onset:

- **Late infantile** (< 30 months) — developmental regression, loss of walking, rapid progression to a vegetative state. Median survival in retrospective natural-history data is roughly 2.7 years.
- **Early juvenile** (30 months – 7 years) — the commonest form; motor decline, ataxia, behavioural change, and later cognitive loss; median survival around 9 years.
- **Late juvenile** (7 – 17 years) — slower motor and cognitive decline, prominent psychiatric presentation possible.
- **Adult** (≥ 17 years) — chronic progressive motor and cognitive decline with long survival, sometimes mistaken for [[Multiple Sclerosis]] or a primary psychiatric disorder.

There is also a distinct *mild* MLD phenotype associated with residual enzyme activity and a *pseudo-deficiency* allele (c.609C>T, p.Arg203Trp) that reduces measured ARSA activity without causing disease — an important pitfall in newborn screening.

## Relationship to other leukodystrophies

MLD sits in a family of sulfatase-related disorders. **Multiple sulfatase deficiency** is caused by mutations in [[SUMF1]] (formylglycine-generating enzyme) and produces deficiency of *all* sulfatases simultaneously, giving MLD plus [[Mucopolysaccharidosis-IIIA|mucopolysaccharidoses]] plus a cholestatic sulphatic aciduria phenotype — MLD features dominate the clinical picture. **Neonatal adrenoleukodystrophy** and **[[X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy|adrenoleukodystrophy]]** are peroxisomal disorders with a different accumulated substrate (very-long-chain fatty acids) but a similar white-matter and adrenal phenotype. **Krabbe disease** is a globoid-cell leukodystrophy caused by galactocerebrosidase deficiency, accumulating galactocerebroside — a downstream substrate of the same pathway.

## Treatment

> [!info] Gene therapy
> **Atidarsagene autotemcel (Libmeldy)** is an autologous ex vivo gene therapy: CD34+ haematopoietic stem cells are harvested, transduced with a functional *ARSA* copy, and reinfused after myeloablative conditioning. Because the transplanted cells are microglia/macrophage-lineage, they supply ARSA enzyme to the CNS independently of blood–brain barrier transport. It is approved in the US, EU, and UK for **presymptomatic** children with late-infantile or early-juvenile MLD, and for early-juvenile patients who still walk independently before cognitive decline. Long-term follow-up of study OTL-200-10 (median ~6.8 years) showed markedly lower risk of severe motor impairment or death than a natural-history cohort, with 100% of treated presymptomatic late-infantile patients free of severe motor impairment at 6 years versus 0% untreated.

Allogeneic **haematopoietic stem cell transplantation** works by the same enzyme-replacement principle without gene modification, and is effective only if performed before symptom onset. Supportive care — seizure control, nutrition, physiotherapy, palliative planning — remains the backbone, especially for late-infantile disease where no disease-modifying therapy is yet licensed.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Efficacy in all of these therapies collapses once symptoms appear. This makes newborn screening and carrier testing of siblings essential, and it also means the diagnosis of *presymptomatic* disease is the time-critical clinical act. Anti-ARSA antibodies (transient, ~15% in the Libmeldy follow-up), febrile neutropenia, and the myeloablation-related morbidity of conditioning are the main safety costs.

## Connections

- [[Lysosomal Storage Diseases]] — MLD is a canonical lysosomal storage disorder, distinguished from the glycosphingolipidoses by the sulfatide substrate.
- [[Saposin-D]] — The activator protein paradigm: saposin B (a different saposin paralogue) activates GALC, and saposin D does the same for sulfatide degradation, which is why saposin mutations can phenocopy the enzyme deficiency.
- [[Myelin]] — Accumulated sulfatide is a myelin lipid; MLD's signature lesion is demyelination with sparing of gray matter.
- [[Oligodendrocytes]] — The CNS cells whose myelin fails in MLD, and the reason the disease is primarily white-matter.
- [[SUMF1]] — The formylglycine-generating enzyme whose deficiency produces multiple sulfatase deficiency, an MLD-plus phenotype.
- [[Multiple Sulfatase Deficiency]] — Closest disease relative, caused by SUMF1 rather than ARSA.
- [[Neonatal Adrenoleukodystrophy]] — Perinatal white-matter disease with adrenal involvement; the key differential for neonatal MLD presentation.
- [[Microglia]] — Transplanted microglia-lineage cells are what deliver ARSA enzyme in gene therapy; the disease is also microglia-driven neuroinflammation.
- [[Apoptosis]] — Neuronal loss in MLD proceeds through accumulated-lipid-induced cell death, contributing to the inexorable terminal decline.
- [[Autophagy]] — Endolysosomal traffic jam in MLD reduces autophagic flux; lysosomal dysfunction and autophagy failure are the same lesion viewed at different resolution.
