# Monocytes

**Monocytes** are circulating mononuclear leukocytes of the innate immune system that serve as precursors to tissue [[Macrophages]] and dendritic cells. They are produced in the bone marrow, circulate in blood for 1–3 days, and then migrate into tissues where they differentiate into specialised macrophage populations. Monocytes are critical effectors of [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] responses, wound healing, tissue remodelling, and immune surveillance. Their dysregulation is implicated in atherosclerosis, neuroinflammation, and the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].

## Ontogeny & Subsets

- **Origin:** Monocytes are derived from common myeloid progenitors in the bone marrow under the control of M-CSF (CSF1) and GM-CSF. They are released into the circulation as immature cells that complete their differentiation in tissues.
- **Circulation time:** Monocytes circulate in blood for 1–3 days before migrating into tissues or undergoing apoptosis.
- **Human subsets:**
  - **Classical monocytes (CD14++CD16−):** ~85–90% of circulating monocytes. High phagocytic capacity, recruited to sites of acute inflammation by [[CCL2]] (MCP-1). Differentiate into inflammatory macrophages.
  - **Intermediate monocytes (CD14++CD16+):** ~5–10%. Produce pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β) and present antigen.
  - **Non-classical monocytes (CD14+CD16++):** ~5–10%. Patrolling behaviour; crawl along endothelium to monitor vascular integrity. Recruited to sites of chronic inflammation.

## Recruitment & Chemotaxis

Monocyte recruitment to tissues is primarily mediated by chemokines:

- **[[CCL2]] (MCP-1):** The principal monocyte chemoattractant. Binds CCR2 on classical monocytes. Produced by endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and macrophages in response to inflammatory stimuli.
- **CCL7, CCL8, CCL13:** Additional CC chemokines that recruit monocytes via CCR2 and CCR3.
- **[[CCL5]] (RANTES):** Recruits monocytes via CCR5, particularly in chronic inflammation.
- **Adhesion molecules:** VCAM-1 and ICAM-1 on activated endothelium mediate monocyte adhesion and transmigration through the vessel wall.
- **Fractalkine (CX3CL1):** Recruits non-classical patrolling monocytes via CX3CR1.

> [!important] MMP-12 and Monocyte Recruitment
> [[MMP-12]] processes CC chemokines (CCL2, CCL7, CCL8, CCL13) into CCR antagonists, dampening monocyte recruitment. This chemokine-processing activity is central to MMP-12's role in inflammation resolution.

## Differentiation & Polarisation

Upon tissue entry, monocytes differentiate into macrophages under the influence of local cytokines and growth factors:

- **M-CSF:** Drives differentiation into M2-like (alternatively activated) macrophages involved in tissue remodelling and resolution.
- **GM-CSF:** Drives differentiation into M1-like (classically activated) macrophages with pro-inflammatory and antimicrobial properties.
- **IL-4/IL-13:** Promote M2 polarisation (alternative activation).
- **IFN-γ/LPS:** Promote M1 polarisation (classical activation).

Differentiation is accompanied by upregulation of phagocytic receptors, pattern recognition receptors, and tissue-specific gene expression programmes.

## Physiological Functions

### Innate Immunity
Monocytes phagocytose pathogens, produce pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6), and generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) via NADPH oxidase. They serve as a first line of defence against bacterial and fungal infections.

### Wound Healing & Tissue Remodelling
Monocyte-derived macrophages are essential for wound healing, clearing debris, producing growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF), and remodelling the [[Extracellular Matrix|ECM]]. They transition from pro-inflammatory (M1) to pro-healing (M2) phenotypes during the resolution phase.

### Antigen Presentation
Intermediate monocytes express MHC class II and co-stimulatory molecules, enabling them to present antigens to T cells and bridge innate and adaptive immunity.

### Vascular Surveillance
Non-classical (CD16+) monocytes patrol the endothelium, monitoring for vascular damage and pathogens. They rapidly respond to vascular injury and infection.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

Monocytes are recruited to senescent tissues by SASP chemokines and contribute to the inflammatory microenvironment:

- **SASP-driven recruitment:** Senescent cells secrete [[CCL2]], GM-CSF, and other chemoattractants that recruit monocytes to the tissue microenvironment.
- **Tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs):** Monocytes recruited by tumour SASP differentiate into TAMs that promote tumour growth, angiogenesis, and immunosuppression.
- **Atherosclerotic plaques:** Monocytes recruited to the arterial wall differentiate into foam cells (lipid-laden macrophages), driving atherosclerotic lesion progression.

## Pathological Roles

### Atherosclerosis
Monocyte infiltration into the arterial intima is an early event in atherogenesis. Monocytes differentiate into macrophages that engulf oxidised LDL, becoming foam cells. This drives fatty streak formation and plaque progression. CCL2/CCR2 signalling is critical for monocyte recruitment to plaques.

### Neuroinflammation
Circulating monocytes can infiltrate the CNS through a disrupted [[Blood-Brain Barrier|BBB]] and differentiate into inflammatory macrophages, contributing to neuroinflammation in Alzheimer's disease, multiple sclerosis, and post-stroke injury.

### Obesity & Metabolic Disease
Obesity increases circulating monocyte counts and promotes monocyte infiltration into adipose tissue, where they contribute to chronic low-grade inflammation ([[Inflammaging]]) and insulin resistance.

### Cancer
Monocytes recruited to the tumour microenvironment differentiate into TAMs that promote tumour growth, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and metastasis. TAM abundance correlates with poor prognosis in many cancers.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Biomarker:** Monocyte count and the monocyte-to-lymphocyte ratio (MLR) are biomarkers of cardiovascular risk, cancer prognosis, and all-cause mortality.
- **Therapeutic target:** CCR2 inhibitors block monocyte recruitment and are under investigation for atherosclerosis, diabetic nephropathy, and cancer.
- **CSF1R inhibitors:** Block monocyte/macrophage differentiation and are used in cancer immunotherapy (e.g., pexidartinib for tenosynovial giant cell tumour).
