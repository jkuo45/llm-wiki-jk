# Protozoa

**Protozoa** is a traditional, non-phylogenetic grouping of heterotrophic unicellular
eukaryotes, most of them motile via cilia, flagella or pseudopodia, typically 10–100 µm
and larger than most bacteria with a correspondingly more complex internal
organisation. It is best understood as a taxon of convenience that historical
morphology-based parasitology used, and that modern sequence phylogeny has dissolved:
"Protozoa" has no single common ancestor beyond the eukaryotic grade, and its members
scatter across Amoebozoa, Excavata and SAR.

> [!info] Taxonomy caveat
> The six-phylum morphological scheme (Sarcomastigophora, Apicomplexa, Ciliophora,
> Microsporidia, and the sporozoan groupings) is a diagnostic framework, not an
> evolutionary one. Contemporary classification places amoeboids in Amoebozoa,
> trichomonads and kinetoplastids in Excavata, and apicomplexans in the alveolates
> within SAR. A protozoan is not a clade, and "primitive" is not a good description —
> Giardia and Trichomonas in particular have among the most reduced genomes of any
> eukaryote while being perfectly well adapted.

## Structure and defining organelles

- **Locomotion.** Pseudopodia (amoeboid), flagella with an undulating membrane
  (trypanosomes, trichomonads), or cilia.
- **Pellicle.** A sufficiently rigid surface layer that maintains shape in
  trypanosomes and Giardia, which otherwise lack a cell wall.
- **Kinetoplast.** A dense mass of mitochondrial DNA within the single large
  mitochondrion of Kinetoplastida; diagnostic for *Leishmania* and *Trypanosoma* in
  microscopy, which is why the amastigote's nucleus-plus-kinetoplast appearance is
  the standard morphological identification of leishmaniasis.
- **Apical complex and apicoplast.** In Apicomplexa, a set of secretory vesicles
  (rhoptries, micronemes, dense granules) that secrete the enzymes needed for host
  cell invasion, plus a non-photosynthetic plastid of secondary endosymbiotic origin
  whose metabolic functions are still not fully resolved but which is required for
  survival.
- **Mitochondrion content.** Kinetoplastids have one mitochondrion per cell, versus
  thousands in a human hepatocyte — a large part of why they are sensitive to drugs
  targeting mitochondrial function.

## Life cycles and transmission

The clinically dominant pattern is a two-host life cycle with sexual reproduction in
the invertebrate vector and asexual multiplication in the human host — true for
*Plasmodium* (mosquito), *Leishmania* and *Trypanosoma* (sandfly, tsetse fly). This
also explains why sexual reproduction, and therefore the generation of drug-resistant
phenotypes, occurs in the vector.

Trophozoite is the motile, feeding, asexual stage; cyst is the dormant, resistant,
transmissive stage in the intestinal protozoa (*Entamoeba*, *Giardia*,
*Cryptosporidium*, *Cyclospora*). Cysts survive chlorination, which is the reason
waterborne protozoal infection is not prevented by standard water disinfection — a
distinctive public-health problem, and one reason water treatment, not chlorine
alone, is the effective control.

| | *Trichomonas vaginalis* | trichomoniasis |

*Entamoeba histolytica* infects an estimated 50–100 million people annually, mostly
asymptomatically, with roughly 10% developing dysentery and fewer than 0.5%
developing the far more lethal amoebic liver abscess. *Toxoplasma* seroprevalence in
the general population is commonly around 30%.

> [!info] Pathogenesis
> Competition for nutrients is rarely what makes protozoa pathogenic. Virulence is
> usually about host-cell invasion and immune evasion: apicomplexans invade actively
> through the apical complex; *E. histolytica* lyses host cells by contact-dependent
> cytolysis as it tunnels through the colonic mucosa; *T. cruzi* escapes the
> parasitophorous vacuole to replicate free in the cytosol. *Leishmania* instead
> survives inside [[Macrophage]]s, using the host cell as both niche and shield.

## Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is species-specific: microscopy of stained smears, antigen detection, serology,
[[Culture]] on defined media, and increasingly [[Polymerase chain reaction|PCR]] —
which is the most sensitive method at low parasite burden and is the tool of choice for
species identification where morphology is uninformative. Cysts are relatively
resistant to the usual antiparasitics, which is why tissue-invasive and luminal
protozoal disease require different agents.

Chemotherapy has distinct liabilities. Chloroquine resistance in *P. falciparum* and
arsenic resistance in *T. rhodesiense* are the canonical examples of drug resistance
emerging in protozoa, in the first case from overuse in agriculture. Many of the
effective agents are toxic: pentavalent antimonials, amphotericin B, [[Miltefosine]],
[[Pentamidine]] and [[Ivermectin]] all have narrow therapeutic indices or prominent
toxicity, which is why most of them also have important non-parasitic research uses in
this vault.

## Documents

- [[Leishmaniasis]] — the vault's worked example of protozoal disease: a kinetoplastid
  infecting macrophages, diagnosed by amastigote morphology, rK39 serology or PCR, and
  treated with antimonials, amphotericin B, miltefosine or paromomycin.

## Connections

- [[Leishmania]] — a kinetoplastid protozoan and the specific genus behind
  leishmaniasis; the single genus through which the vault's other protozoal
  connections run, and the source of the amastigote morphology and rK39 assay described
  in that note.
- [[Leishmaniasis]] — the vault's concrete protozoal disease, providing the clinical
  anchor for the taxonomy, life cycle and treatment liabilities set out above.
- [[Macrophage]] — the replicative niche for *Leishmania*; infecting and surviving
  inside phagocytes is the defining evasion strategy of this phylum's members, and is
  why macrophage biology determines the disease course.
- [[Polymerase chain reaction]] — the highest-sensitivity diagnostic method for
  low-burden protozoal infection and the main route to species-level identification;
  this is the specific assay cited in the Leishmaniasis note.
- [[Ivermectin]] — an antiparasitic whose protozoal activity is incidental; its
  established targets are helminths and ectoparasites, and it is in the vault for its
  separate investigation as an anticancer and autophagy-modulating drug.
- [[Antimicrobial Resistance]] — protozoa are a classic reservoir of acquired drug
  resistance (chloroquine in *P. falciparum*, arsenic in *T. rhodesiense*), and
  resistance arises in the sexual cycle within the vector, which has direct
  implications for how resistance should be managed.
