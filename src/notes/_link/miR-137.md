# miR-137

**miR-137** is a microRNA with tumor-suppressive and neurodevelopmental roles. In [[Melanoma]], miR-137 negatively regulates [[Ferroptosis]] by directly targeting the glutamine transporter [[SLC1A5]]: knockdown of miR-137 promotes ferroptosis, whereas its presence protects melanoma cells from iron-dependent death.

## Biogenesis & Function

Like all miRNAs, miR-137 is processed from pri-/pre-miRNA to a ~22-nt guide that represses target mRNAs via the RISC/Argonaute complex, typically by binding 3'-UTR seed sequences. It regulates differentiation, proliferation, and (in this context) metabolic vulnerability.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review reports that in melanoma, miR-137 negatively regulates ferroptosis by directly acting on the glutamine transporter [[SLC1A5]]; knockdown of miR-137 promotes ferroptosis (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Melanoma"). Glutamine import via SLC1A5 feeds GSH and TCA/anabolic pathways that support antioxidant defense; by repressing SLC1A5, miR-137 would be expected to *promote* ferroptosis—but the observed direction (miR-137 protects) implies SLC1A5 has a ferroptosis-protective role in this context, such that targeting it lowers resistance. The net effect documented is that loss of miR-137 increases ferroptotic death.

> [!info] miRNA as a ferroptosis rheostat
> miR-137/SLC1A5 illustrates post-transcriptional control of ferroptosis: small RNAs tune the glutamine–GSH axis that sets the GPX4 defense threshold in melanoma.

## Clinical Relevance

miR-137 and SLC1A5 are candidate biomarkers/therapeutic nodes in melanoma ferroptosis. Modulating this axis could sensitize resistant melanoma to ferroptosis inducers, complementing mitochondrial complex I–targeted strategies noted in the review.
