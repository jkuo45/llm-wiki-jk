# HMG-CoA reductase

**HMG-CoA reductase** (3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-CoA reductase) catalyzes the committed, rate-limiting step of the **mevalonate pathway** — conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate — and is the target of **statins**. Because the mevalonate pathway supplies both **CoQ10/ubiquinone** and the selenocysteine-tRNA machinery for **GPX4**, HMG-CoA reductase sits at a pivotal ferroptosis-control node.

## Ferroptosis relevance

Inhibition of HMG-CoA reductase (by statins) reduces mevalonate flux, lowering **ubiquinone (CoQ10)** availability. Since ubiquinone is the direct **FSP1 substrate**, this **converges on FSP1**, collapsing its NAD(P)H-driven radical trap and predicting ferroptosis sensitivity — paralleling the GPX4-lowering effect of selenocysteine depletion (see [[Mevalonate pathway]], [[FSP1]], [[Statins]]).

> [!info] Two nodes, one enzyme
> HMG-CoA reductase inhibition hits ferroptosis defense twice: (1) less CoQ10 → weaker FSP1 axis, and (2) impaired selenocysteine tRNA → less GPX4. This dual hit is why statins can both sensitize to ferroptosis and (via CoQ10 depletion) contribute to myopathy.

## Physiological & clinical relevance

- **Cholesterol lowering:** Primary therapeutic action of statins in cardiovascular disease.
- **CoQ10 depletion:** A proposed mechanism of statin-associated muscle symptoms; CoQ10 supplementation is sometimes co-prescribed.
- **Ferroptosis research:** Statin use is exploited experimentally to sensitize cells/tumors to ferroptosis in combination with GPX4 inhibition.
