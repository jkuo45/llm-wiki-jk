# STARD3

## Overview

STARD3, also known as MLN64, is a cholesterol-transport protein studied principally in the context of cholesterol movement from the endoplasmic reticulum to endosomes. At mitochondria–lysosome contacts, STARD3 mediates cholesterol transfer from the lysosomal compartment into mitochondria. Its activity is required for the mitochondrial cholesterol accumulation observed in NPC1-deficient cells, linking the protein to the metabolic consequences of Niemann–Pick type C.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> In NPC1-deficient cells, STARD3 is required for mitochondria–lysosome contacts and for the transfer of lysosomal cholesterol to mitochondria; the buildup of cholesterol in mitochondria in Niemann–Pick type C depends on STARD3. Cholesterol transfer from endo-lysosomes containing NPC2 to mitochondria has also been demonstrated.

## Mechanism

STARD3 contains a START-domain lipid-binding pocket and associates with endosomal and lysosomal membranes. In cells lacking functional NPC1, lysosomes accumulate cholesterol and increase their contact with mitochondria; STARD3 depletion prevents the resultant mitochondrial cholesterol loading. The protein therefore acts at the interface rather than by altering whole-cell cholesterol synthesis. Other factors implicated in the same transfer route include TM4SF5 and HKDC1, a hexokinase-domain protein whose expression is transcriptionally regulated by [[TFEB]] and which is required to maintain mitochondrial and lysosomal homeostasis. These findings raise the possibility that M-L contacts participate in sugar and cholesterol homeostasis, a role that remains incompletely defined.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Mitochondrial cholesterol accumulation in NPC1-deficient cells was shown to be dependent on STARD3. Posterior work showed cholesterol transfer from endo-lysosomes containing NPC2 to mitochondria. HKDC1, a protein with hexokinase activity whose expression is transcriptionally regulated by [[TFEB]], is essential to maintain both mitochondrial and lysosomal homeostasis.

## Pathophysiology

Mitochondrial cholesterol accumulation compromises mitochondrial metabolism in Niemann–Pick type C. Cholesterol loading alters membrane properties and respiratory function; the review notes that lysosomal cholesterol-handling defects increase M-L contacts and promote cholesterol accumulation within mitochondria. Whether STARD3 is the sole transfer route in all cell types, and whether its ER–endosome function is coupled to its M-L contact function, is not established.

## Unresolved Questions

The relation between STARD3-dependent transfer and the NPC2-dependent route remains to be resolved. The contribution of TM4SF5 and HKDC1 to sterol transfer, and whether the proposed role of M-L contacts in sugar metabolism extends beyond cholesterol handling, require further definition.
