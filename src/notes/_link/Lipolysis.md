# Lipolysis

**Lipolysis** is the enzymatic hydrolysis of triacylglycerol (TAG) to free fatty acids (FFA) and glycerol, chiefly in the adipocyte but also in skeletal muscle, liver, and the mammary gland. It is the rate-limiting step releasing energy-dense lipid from [[Lipid Droplet]]-bound stores, and the only meaningful source of circulating FFA during fasting and exercise. Lipolysis is not passive: it is a tightly gated, hormonally and nutrient-sensitively regulated process.

## Enzymatic Cascade

Hydrolysis proceeds in a stepwise fashion through three neutral lipases:

- **[[ATGL]]** (adipose triglyceride lipase, PNPLA2) performs the rate-limiting first step, converting TAG to diacylglycerol (DAG). It is activated by comparative gene identification-58 (ABHD5/CGI-58) and is the dominant adipose lipase; mouse knockouts show reduced fat mass and resistance to diet-induced obesity.
- **Hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL)** preferentially cleaves DAG to monoacylglycerol. Its activity is acutely controlled by phosphorylation via cAMP-dependent protein kinase A ([[PKA]]) and by the activating phosphorylation of the enzyme itself.
- **[[Monoacylglycerol Lipase]] (MGL)** completes the sequence, releasing the final fatty acid and glycerol.

The released fatty acids are either re-esterified into TAG (the futile "lipid cycle", which itself consumes ATP and produces heat) or exported in plasma bound to carrier proteins, taken up by muscle and heart for oxidation.

## Hormonal and Nutrient Control

Catecholamines are the dominant acute stimulator. Sympathetic nerve endings release [[Norepinephrine]] and [[Epinephrine]], which act on [[Beta-Adrenergic Receptor|β-adrenergic receptors]] on adipocytes, raising intracellular cAMP, activating [[PKA]], and promoting both phosphorylation of HSL and translocation of HSL to the [[Lipid Droplet]] surface. [[Glucagon]] acts similarly in liver.

Insulin is the chief brake: it activates phosphodiesterase, lowering cAMP, and activates phosphodiesterase/protein phosphatase cascades that dephosphorylate HSL and PERK/PDK1-dependent inhibition of lipolysis. Feeding state thus suppresses lipolysis and directs fatty acids into re-esterification.

Nutrient-sensing kinases impose longer-timescale control:

- [[AMPK]] phosphorylates and inhibits [[Malonyl-CoA]] synthesis via acetyl-CoA carboxylase, lowering the malonyl-CoA brake on [[ATGL]]; energy depletion therefore promotes lipolysis.
- [[mTOR]] and S6K1 signal nutrient sufficiency and restrain the lipolytic program; [[FOXO]] factors, deacetylated by [[SIRT1]], induce lipolytic genes including ATGL and HSL.

## Physiological Function

- Provides circulating FFA as the dominant fuel for skeletal and cardiac muscle during prolonged exercise and fasting.
- Supports [[Thermogenesis]] in brown and beige adipose tissue, where FFA oxidation is uncoupled from ATP synthesis via [[UCP1]] and is the direct heat source.
- Underpins the fasting adaptation to preserve glucose for obligate glucose users (brain, erythrocyte) by sparing glucose consumption.
- Substrates for hepatic ketogenesis ([[Ketogenesis]]) and re-esterification in [[Liver]]; uncontrolled flux drives steatosis and [[Lipotoxicity]].

## Clinical Relevance

- **Ageing**: adipose lipolytic capacity declines, partly because of reduced [[AMPK]]/[[mTOR]] sensitivity, reduced beta-adrenergic signalling, and loss of functional adipocytes. This contributes to ectopic lipid deposition, insulin resistance, and reduced fat oxidation.
- **Brown/beige adipose**: thermogenesis is entirely dependent on the local lipolysis–FFA oxidation–UCP1 axis; β3-adrenergic agonism fails if lipolysis is blocked, making lipolysis a pharmacological target for metabolic disease.
- **Lipodystrophy**: near-total absence of adipose tissue means no lipid store to mobilise, causing severe insulin resistance and ectopic fat — the mirror image of lipolysis failure.
- **Catecholamine excess** in pheochromocytoma, or β-agonist abuse, drives unrestrained lipolysis, free fatty acid flux and lipid peroxidation.
- **Insulin resistance** involves dysregulated adipose lipolysis; the resulting FFA flux to liver and muscle worsens hepatic steatosis and impaired insulin signalling.
