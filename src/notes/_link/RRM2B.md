# RRM2B

**RRM2B** (ribonucleotide reductase M2 B) encodes **p53R2**, the [[p53]]-inducible small (β′) subunit of human [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]] (RNR). p53R2 is ~83% homologous to [[RRM2]] but is regulated entirely differently: instead of being S-phase-gated and proliferation-linked, p53R2 is induced by [[p53]] after DNA damage and assembles with [[RRM1]] to provision the dNTPs needed for [[DNA Repair]] — and for [[Mitochondrial DNA]] synthesis in non-dividing cells. Pathogenic RRM2B mutations cause severe mitochondrial DNA depletion syndromes (MDS), and p53R2-deficient mice develop lethal renal failure from dNTP pool collapse.

## Structure

- **Gene locus**: chromosome 8q22.3; encodes the ~40 kDa p53R2 protein.
- Small β′ subunit containing the same radical-bearing di-iron/tyrosyl cofactor as [[RRM2]], with a Phe-for-Tyr substitution (vs RRM2) that tunes radical generation and supports a distinct enzymatic profile suited to repair contexts.
- Forms an active RNR with the [[RRM1]] α subunit.

## Regulation & Function

- **p53-inducible**: [[p53]] transactivates RRM2B in response to DNA damage and replication stress; p53R2 competes with [[RRM2]] for the RRM1 partner, biasing RNR output toward repair rather than bulk replication.
- **Repair & mtDNA supply**: p53R2–RNR provides dNTPs for nucleotide excision repair, base-excision repair, and mitochondrial DNA synthesis in G0/G1 and non-dividing tissues — a role RRM2-based RNR cannot fulfill once cells exit the cycle.
- **Constitutive small-subunit function**: p53R2 also supports basal RNR activity in some cell types, complementing the S-phase-restricted [[RRM2]].

## Clinical Relevance

- **Mitochondrial DNA depletion syndromes (MDS)**: hereditary RRM2B mutations cause MDS type 8A — infantile-onset encephalomyopathy, severe renal tubulopathy, hepatopathy, and lactic acidosis; other alleles present with rod-cone dystrophy, sensorineural deafness, and Fanconi-type renal disease. Mechanism: insufficient dNTPs (dATP/dGTP particularly) compromise mtDNA replication, causing muscle weakness, neurological decline, and early mortality.
- **Renal failure in knockout mice**: Rrm2b-null mice die within weeks of renal failure from attenuated dNTP pools (Nat Genet 2003); human 2025 studies (PNAS) confirm dNTP-depletion-driven mtDNA loss.
- **Cancer axis**: p53R2 links the p53 tumor-suppressor network to genome caretaking; deregulation is implicated in tumorigenesis, and p53R2-containing RNR is a target of nucleoside and thiosemicarbazone RNR inhibitors with selectivity considerations vs the proliferative [[RRM2]] enzyme.
