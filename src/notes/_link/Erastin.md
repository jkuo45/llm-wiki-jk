# Erastin

**Erastin** is a synthetic small molecule discovered in 2003 by Dolma et al. as a compound selectively lethal to [[RAS]]-mutant cancer cells through a then-unknown, non-apoptotic mechanism. It was later recognized as the founding member of the ferroptosis inducer class. Erastin kills cells by inhibiting the [[System Xc-]] cystine/glutamate antiporter, collapsing intracellular [[Glutathione]] (GSH), disabling [[GPX4]], and unleashing [[Iron]]-dependent [[Lipid Peroxidation]]—the core execution axis of [[Ferroptosis]].

## Chemistry & Classification

Erastin belongs to the class of compounds termed "FINs" (ferroptosis inducers). It is a small hydrophobic molecule that partitions into membranes. While its canonical target is [[System Xc-]] (specifically the [[SLC7A11]] light chain), evidence also implicates mitochondrial [[VDAC]] (voltage-dependent anion channels) as an additional site of action that amplifies oxidative mitochondrial dysfunction.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

Erastin binds and inhibits system Xc-, blocking cystine import. The resulting cysteine shortage throttles GSH synthesis; with GSH depleted, [[GPX4]] can no longer reduce phospholipid hydroperoxides, and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]-mediated lipid peroxidation propagates (Dixon et al., *Cell* 2012, PMID: 22632970; Yang & Stockwell, *Chem. Biol.* 2008, PMID: 18355723). Labile [[Iron]] (Fe²⁺) fuels the [[Fenton Reaction]], converting lipid hydroperoxides into radical chains that rupture membranes. Erastin-induced death is rescued by [[Ferrostatin-1]], [[Liproxstatin-1]], [[Vitamin E]], and iron chelators such as [[Deferoxamine]].

> [!info] RAS-selective lethality
> Erastin shows synthetic lethality with oncogenic [[RAS]]. RAS-driven cells are particularly dependent on cystine uptake and GSH, making them exquisitely sensitive—an early clue that ferroptosis could be exploited for genotype-selective cancer therapy.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Erastin is primarily a research tool and a lead for ferroptosis-based oncology. It has informed the development of system Xc- inhibitors (e.g., sulfasalazine, imidazole ketones) for therapy-resistant tumors, including [[Pancreatic Cancer]], [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], [[Breast Cancer]], [[Colorectal Cancer]], and [[Lung Cancer]], where the review documents erastin-triggered ferroptosis across multiple models.

> [!tip] Experimental use
> Erastin is the gold-standard positive control in ferroptosis assays; combine with [[RSL3]] (a direct [[GPX4]] inhibitor) to dissect upstream vs. downstream nodes of the ferroptotic cascade.

## Clinical Perspective

Because systemic erastin causes toxicity (normal cells also require cystine), translational efforts focus on tumor-selective delivery and combination with agents that raise cellular iron or suppress GPX4. Erastin itself is not clinically used, but its mechanism underpins several investigational ferroptosis inducers.
