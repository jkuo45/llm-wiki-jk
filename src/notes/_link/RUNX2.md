# RUNX2

**RUNX2** (runt-related transcription factor 2), also called **CBFA1** or core-binding factor subunit alpha-1, is the Runt-domain transcription factor that functions as the master regulator of osteoblast differentiation and of chondrocyte hypertrophy at the growth plate. It sits downstream of [[BMP Signaling Pathway|BMP]]/SMAD signalling and of [[Wnt signaling|Wnt]]/β-catenin, and its output defines the osteoblast phenotype.

## Structure & Domains

RUNX2 belongs to the runt-related family, whose defining feature is the 128-amino-acid **runt homology domain (RHD)**. The RHD mediates both DNA binding (consensus 5′-TGTGGT-3′/ACCACA) and heterodimerisation with the obligate cofactor **CBFβ**. Two isoforms are generated by alternative promoter use: the ubiquitously expressed **type II** isoform is required for osteoblast differentiation, whereas the **type III** isoform is expressed in hypertrophic chondrocytes and is dispensable for bone formation in mouse models. A C-terminal transactivation domain, modulated by the conserved terminal motif, distinguishes RUNX2 from RUNX1 (haematopoiesis) and RUNX3 (T-cell development).

## Mechanism of Action

- **Cooperative DNA binding** — RUNX2/CBFβ binds DNA as a heterodimer, stabilised by the CBFβ tail; the complex recruits co-activators (p300/CBP, [[EP300]]) and co-repressors including [[EZH2]], so RUNX2 acts as a chromatin-organising as well as a sequence-specific factor.
- **Osteoblast gene programme** — drives expression of **osteocalcin**, **ALPL** (alkaline phosphatase), **COL1A1**/type I collagen, sclerostin-axis genes, and downstream regulators of differentiation; it also relieves cell-cycle inhibitors p21 and p27 to allow exit from the cell cycle.
- **Chondrocyte hypertrophy and endochondral ossification** — RUNX2 is required for terminal chondrocyte differentiation, collagen X and MMP13 induction, and vascular invasion of the growth plate.
- **Chondrogenesis** — in the cartilage lineage, RUNX2 acts with [[SMAD4]] and MSX factors; excess RUNX2 activity drives osteogenic transdifferentiation of chondrocytes.

> [!important] Clinical anchor: cleidocranial dysplasia
> Heterozygous loss-of-function or dominant-negative RUNX2 variants cause **cleidocranial dysplasia**, an autosomal dominant disorder of defective intramembranous bone formation — classically delayed closure of fontanelles, clavicular hypoplasia and dental abnormalities. Reported variants include missense (e.g. p.R225Q), frameshift and splice-site changes (e.g. c.685+2T>G), and the RHD is a mutation hotspot associated with severe phenotypes. Roughly two-thirds of CCD cases carry a RUNX2 variant.

## Post-translational Control

RUNX2 protein stability is a therapeutic lever. The SCF^JFK E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (SKP1–CUL1–JFK, JFK also named ZNF622) destabilises RUNX2, and several CCD-associated RUNX2 mutations increase affinity for this complex, accelerating proteasomal degradation and thereby blunting osteoblast differentiation — a plausible explanation for the dominant-negative behaviour of some variants.

## Pathology Beyond the Skeleton

RUNX2 is deregulated in several cancers. It is amplified or overexpressed in osteosarcoma, and in prostate and breast cancer it promotes epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, migration and osteolytic bone metastasis by repressing osteoblastic genes such as osteocalcin — helping to explain why bone metastases are characteristically osteolytic.

## Connections

- [[BMP Signaling Pathway]] — BMP2/4/7 signal through SMAD1/5/8-SMAD4 to induce RUNX2, the principal route by which osteoinductive signals commit mesenchymal cells to the osteoblast lineage.
- [[Osteoporosis]] — Osteoporosis reflects an imbalance in remodelling in which osteoblast number and activity fall; RUNX2 activity is the anabolic side of that balance and its transcriptional loss-of-function produces a distinctly different, developmental skeletal phenotype (cleidocranial dysplasia).
- [[Osteoclast]] — RUNX2 sets osteoblast-derived RANKL, which is the master regulator of osteoclastogenesis; the osteoblast–osteoclast axis couples bone formation to resorption.
- [[Osteoarthritis]] — Chondrocyte hypertrophy, driven in part by RUNX2, is a hallmark of osteoarthritic cartilage, linking developmental endochondral ossification to degenerative joint disease.
- [[EZH2]] — RUNX2 recruits the Polycomb E3 ligase EZH2 to repress osteocalcin and other maturation genes, so that proteasomal/epigenetic control of RUNX2 output is both transcriptional and post-translational.
- [[EP300]] — p300/CBP acetylates and co-activates RUNX2, connecting its activity to the vault's histone acetylation machinery.
