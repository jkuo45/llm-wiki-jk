# Metastatic colonization

**Overview:** **Metastatic colonization** is the ability of disseminated cancer cells to survive, persist, and proliferate at a distant organ — the least efficient, most rate-limiting phase of **[[Metastasis]]**.

## Oxidative-stress constraint

- **[[Oxidative Stress]]** inhibits colonization (Piskounova et al., *Nature* 2015); micrometastases therefore experience high stress and upregulate **[[Selenocysteine]]** biosynthesis.

## Selenocysteine axis

- Efficient Sec insertion — dependent on the **Um34** modification of tRNA[Ser]Sec by **[[FTSJ1]]** — supports translation of antioxidant **[[Selenoprotein|selenoproteins]]** (e.g., **[[Glutathione Peroxidase 4|GPx4]]**, **[[Thioredoxin reductase|TrxR]]**) that buffer **[[Reactive Oxygen Species]]**.
- FTSJ1/Um34 is **necessary and sufficient** for **[[Melanoma]]** colonization in vivo (Nease et al., 2024).

> [!important]
> Source: [[Selenocysteine tRNA methylation promotes oxidative stress resistance in melanoma metastasis]]
> tRNA[Ser]Sec Um34 modification protects cancer cells from oxidative stress and is required for metastasis, identifying FTSJ1/Um34 as a potential metastasis-selective therapeutic target.
