# Mitochondrial ROS

**Mitochondrial ROS (mtROS)** are [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] generated predominantly at [[Complex I]] and [[Complex III|Complex III]] of the [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] electron transport chain. The primary species is superoxide (O2•−), formed when electrons leak from the chain and reduce molecular oxygen; it is rapidly dismutated — by matrix [[Superoxide Dismutase|SOD2]] and other SODs — to hydrogen peroxide, which can diffuse and signal or be scavenged by [[Glutathione]] and [[Peroxiredoxin|Prx]] systems.

## Sources & Determinants

Leak is greatest under high [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] and during reverse electron transport at Complex I (succinate-driven), conditions common in nutrient excess. Thus mtROS production is tightly coupled to metabolic state and substrate supply.

## Dual Roles

At high flux, mtROS inflict oxidative damage to lipids (cardiolipin), proteins, and mtDNA, promoting [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], [[Aging]], and triggering [[Apoptosis]] via [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]] and cytochrome c release. Conversely, low, transient mtROS act as signaling molecules: they stabilize [[HIF-1α]] under [[Hypoxia]], activate [[NRF2]] and [[AMPK]], induce [[PGC-1α]] and the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPRmt]], and underpin [[Mitohormesis]] — the adaptive strengthening of respiratory capacity that supports [[Longevity]].

## Therapeutic Angle

Compounds such as [[Methylene blue]] and [[MitoQ]] modulate mtROS at the source, illustrating the therapeutic value of calibrated (not absolute) ROS suppression.
