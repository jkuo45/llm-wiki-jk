# GATA3

**GATA3** (GATA binding protein 3) is a zinc-finger transcription factor of the GATA family, encoded on chromosome 10p14. It binds GATA motifs in nucleosomal DNA and facilitates chromatin remodelling, functioning as a **pioneer factor** — one that can engage closed chromatin and permit other factors to follow.

## Normal Function

GATA3 is required for normal cell differentiation across several lineages: mammary gland, urothelium, T-helper cell differentiation, inner ear, and epidermis. In the mammary gland it is estimated to be the most abundant transcription factor in luminal epithelial cells, where it directs ductal morphogenesis, alveolar differentiation, and commitment of progenitors to the luminal fate (Qiang et al., *J Clin Pathol* 2023; PMID 37726118).

It also acts in immunity: GATA3 is a master regulator of T-helper type 2 polarization, coordinating expression of IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 together with the transcription factor [[STAT6]]. T-cell receptor signaling and IL-4 both feed into GATA3, and GATA3 then sustains type 2 cytokine production.

## Cancer Biology

**Breast cancer.** GATA3 expression is highly correlated with the luminal transcriptional program, and genomic analyses of human breast tumors reveal **high-frequency GATA3 mutation in luminal tumors**, supporting a driver role rather than a passenger one (Takaku et al., *Gene Expr* 2015; PMID 26637396). GATA3 loss of function removes a differentiation restraint, de-repressing basal-like programs.

**Bladder cancer.** GATA3 expression is a feature of the luminal subtype of urothelial carcinoma, with implications for immune status and for therapeutic response.

**Diagnostic use.** Immunohistochemistry for GATA3 is used in surgical pathology to support breast or urothelial origin in a carcinoma of unknown primary. It is sensitive but **not entirely specific**: labelling is highest in estrogen receptor-positive carcinomas but also occurs in estrogen receptor-negative ones, which is precisely what gives it diagnostic utility in triple-negative breast carcinoma, where other mammary markers are negative (Asch-Kendrick & Cimino-Mathews, *Hum Pathol* 2016; PMID 26772397). Pitfalls exist in squamous cell carcinomas and soft tissue sarcomas, so GATA3 is best interpreted alongside a panel.

> [!info] Transcription factor, forkhead, and GATA
> GATA3 frequently co-occurs with [[FOXA1]] and [[Estrogen Receptor]] binding at shared luminal enhancers — the pioneer factors and the nuclear receptor cooperate in an interdependent loop. It also interfaces with estrogen-driven signaling and, in other settings, with androgen receptor and Wnt/β-catenin programs.
