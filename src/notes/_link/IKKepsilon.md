# IKKepsilon

## Overview
[[IKKepsilon]] (IKKε, IKKi) is an inducible kinase structurally related to the canonical IKK complex but with distinct functions in innate immunity and apoptotic regulation. IKKε, together with [[TBK1]], phosphorylates [[XIAP]] at Ser430 within its RING finger domain, triggering Lys48-linked autoubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. This removes the caspase-inhibitory brake and sensitizes cells to apoptosis.

## Structure & Activation
IKKε contains a C-terminal serine/threonine kinase domain and an N-terminal regulatory region. Unlike the canonical IKKα/IKKβ, IKKε does not directly phosphorylate IκBα. IKKε is induced by viral infection, TLR activation, and inflammatory cytokines, and is activated by autophosphorylation and TBK1-mediated phosphorylation.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **XIAP Degradation**: IKKε and [[TBK1]] phosphorylate [[XIAP]] at Ser430 in the RING finger domain. This triggers Lys48-linked autoubiquitination at Lys322 and Lys328, leading to rapid proteasomal degradation. XIAP degradation liberates [[Caspase-3]], [[Caspase-7]], and [[Caspase-9]] from inhibition, sensitizing the cell to apoptosis.

> [!info] Innate Immunity–Apoptosis Connection
> IKKε-mediated XIAP degradation represents a functional link between innate immune activation (e.g., viral infection) and apoptotic sensitization. Cells that have recently fought viral infection upregulate IKKε, lowering their apoptotic threshold — a potential safeguard against viral persistence.

- **Antiviral Signaling**: IKKε phosphorylates IRF3 and IRF7 to induce type I interferon (IFN-α/β) production.
- **NF-κB Activation**: IKKε can activate NF-κB through phosphorylation of NEMO and other substrates, though this is less prominent than canonical IKKα/IKKβ signaling.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer**: IKKε is overexpressed in certain cancers (breast, ovarian, pancreatic) and can promote either survival (via NF-κB) or apoptosis (via XIAP degradation), depending on context.
- **Autoimmunity**: Constitutive IKKε activity contributes to type I interferon overproduction in lupus and other autoimmune diseases.
- **Viral Infections**: IKKε is a key antiviral kinase; its induction during viral infection simultaneously activates interferon responses and sensitizes infected cells to apoptosis.
