# Peroxiredoxin

**Peroxiredoxins (Prx)** are a ubiquitous family of thiol-dependent peroxidases that detoxify [[Hydrogen Peroxide]], peroxynitrite, and a range of organic hydroperoxides. They are among the most abundant antioxidant proteins in the cell — typical 2-Cys Prx can reach ~0.8% of total soluble protein — and act as both primary [[Hydrogen Peroxide|H2O2]] sinks and refined regulators of [[Redox Signaling]].

## Structure & Mechanism

Mammals express six isoforms (Prx1–Prx6). All use a conserved peroxidatic cysteine that is oxidized to sulfenic acid (−SOH) upon reducing substrate peroxide to water. In the common "2-Cys" Prx (Prx1–Prx4, Prx6 dimeric form), a resolving cysteine on a neighboring subunit then forms a disulfide, which is reduced by [[Thioredoxin]] (Trx) in an NADPH-dependent cycle catalyzed by thioredoxin reductase. Prx6 is a "1-Cys" variant that transfers its disulfide directly to Trx.

## Biology & Regulation

Beyond simple scavenging, Prx are notable for a "floodgate" behavior: mild [[Oxidative Stress]] oxidizes the peroxidatic cysteine only to the disulfide (active), but high peroxide flux over-oxidizes it to sulfinic acid (−SO2H), inactivating the enzyme and revealing its molecular chaperone activity. This over-oxidation is selectively reversed by sulfiredoxin (ATP-dependent) in eukaryotes. Mitochondrial [[Prx3]] and peroxisomal Prx5 protect organelle compartments; cytosolic Prx1/2 buffer signaling H2O2.

## Pathology & Relevance

Prx defend against damage to proteins, lipids, and DNA and cooperate with [[Catalase]], [[Glutathione Peroxidase]] (a [[Glutathione]]-dependent system), and [[Superoxide Dismutase]]. Age-related loss or oxidation of Prx correlates with rising oxidative damage, while Prx1 and Prx6 can act as tumor suppressors yet are frequently up-regulated in cancers to support redox fitness.
