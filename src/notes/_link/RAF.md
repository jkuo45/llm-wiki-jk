# RAF

**RAF** denotes a family of serine/threonine kinases—**ARAF, BRAF, and CRAF (RAF1)**—that transmit signals from [[RAS]] to [[MEK]] in the canonical RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK MAPK cascade. In ferroptosis, the RAF/MEK axis is most relevant as the upstream signaling context in which [[Erastin]] was first shown to kill [[RAS]]-mutant cells, and as a pathway whose inhibition (e.g., by [[Sorafenib]]) can engage ferroptotic death in cancer.

## Structure & Biochemistry

RAF kinases are recruited to the membrane upon RAS-GTP activation, where they phosphorylate MEK1/2, which in turn activates ERK1/2—driving proliferation and survival. BRAF V600E is a common oncogenic mutation; RAF1 (CRAF) also interfaces with apoptosis and survival signaling.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The foundational ferroptosis studies identified erastin as selectively lethal to RAS-mutant cells, and Yagoda et al. (*Nature* 2007, PMID: 17568748) described RAS–RAF–MEK-dependent oxidative cell death involving [[VDAC]] (voltage-dependent anion channels). This oxidative, iron-dependent death preceded the formal naming of ferroptosis and established the RAF/MEK axis as a sensitizing context. In HCC, the [[RAS]]/Raf/[[MEK]] pathway is a ferroptosis-modulating route, and RAF inhibition (sorafenib) induces ferroptosis (review Mechanism/Fig. 2; "Ferroptosis and tumors / HCC").

> [!info] RAF as a ferroptosis-sensitizing context
> Oncogenic RAS–RAF signaling creates a cystine/GSH dependency that erastin exploits. Thus RAF pathway activity is both a proliferation driver and a ferroptosis-vulnerability determinant.

## Clinical Relevance

RAF inhibitors (vemurafenib, dabrafenib) and multi-kinase RAF-pathway drugs (sorafenib) are oncology mainstays. Their ferroptotic component informs combinations with GPX4/system Xc- inhibitors, especially in [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] and [[Melanoma]].
