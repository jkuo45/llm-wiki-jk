## Overview

CD94 is a type II transmembrane C-type lectin-like protein encoded by *KLRD1*. It is not an inhibitory receptor by itself but serves as the obligatory signaling partner of NKG2A (and activating NKG2C/NKG2E) on the cell surface. The **[[CD94-NKG2A]]** heterodimer is the major inhibitory receptor recognizing **[[HLA-E]]**, the non-classical MHC class Ib molecule that presents leader peptides from classical HLA-A/B/C.

## Structure and Signaling

- CD94 provides the transmembrane and ITIM-bearing cytosolic tail; NKG2A contributes the ligand-binding C-type lectin domain.
- Engagement of HLA-E triggers NKG2A ITIM phosphorylation and recruitment of SHP-1, delivering a "stop" signal that suppresses NK-cell and CD8+ T-cell cytotoxicity.

> [!info] CD94 itself is invariant, so the functional specificity of the receptor complex is determined by its NKG2 partner (inhibitory NKG2A vs. activating NKG2C).

## Role in Immune Evasion

Because HLA-E surface stability reports on overall HLA-I expression, CD94/NKG2A acts as a sensor of "missing-self" and of healthy self-MHC. Tumors and **[[Senescent Cells]]** exploit this by up-regulating HLA-E, engaging CD94/NKG2A to blunt killer-cell function — a core axis of **[[Immune Evasion]]** in **[[Cancer]]**.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Blockade of the NKG2A side of the complex (e.g., monalizumab) can restore NK- and T-cell cytotoxicity and is under investigation in combination immunotherapy, paralleling [[PD-1]]/PD-L1 strategies.
