# WR-1065

**WR-1065** is the **free thiol metabolite** of [[Amifostine]] (WR-2721) and the
chemically active species. It is a small, membrane-permeable aminothiol —
2-[(3-aminopropyl)amino]ethanethiol — generated by alkaline phosphatase hydrolysis of the
thiophosphate prodrug at the cell surface. It is the basis of amifostine's clinical use in
reducing cisplatin nephrotoxicity and radiation-induced xerostomia.

## Metabolic logic and tissue selectivity

Amifostine itself is almost inactive; the thiophosphate must be dephosphorylated to expose
the sulfhydryl. Because alkaline phosphatase is abundant on normal tissues and generally
low in tumours, WR-1065 accumulates preferentially in normal tissue — the classic
"selective cytoprotection" rationale. It diffuses passively across membranes, while the
disulfide metabolite **WR-33278** is imported through the polyamine transport system.

> [!warning] The selectivity story is not clean
> Many human tumours ectopically express high alkaline phosphatase, and the four alkaline
> phosphatase classes (tissue-nonspecific TNAP, intestinal, placental, GCAP) are
> distributed unevenly and are themselves inducible. Measured WR-1065 accumulation differed
> only ~2.6-fold between normal fibroblasts and MCF7 cancer cells, which does not obviously
> explain complete absence of radioprotection in the cancer cells. Genetic background and
> cell-type-specific responses to the metabolite mixture probably matter as much as enzyme
> activity. Treat "ALP explains selectivity" as a working assumption, not a settled fact.

## Mechanisms of action

Several operate simultaneously, and none alone accounts for all effects:

**Immediate radical scavenging.** The thiol donates hydrogen to DNA radicals formed during
low-LET irradiation and scavenges hydroxyl radicals. This is short-lived: intracellular WR-1065
falls to background within about an hour of removal. It requires millimolar concentrations —
4 mM protects cells from immediate radiation killing, whereas 40 µM does not.

**Delayed radioprotection via SOD2.** At *low*, non-cytotoxic concentrations (40 µM),
WR-1065 does not protect against cell killing but still markedly reduces delayed genomic
instability in surviving progeny. The mechanism is transcriptional: as a non-protein thiol
it reduces cysteines on the p50/p65 subunits of [[NF-kB]], activating it; NF-κB then drives
*SOD2* transcription via an essential intronic binding element. [[MnSOD]] protein rises
15–20-fold (activity 5.5–6.9-fold) peaking at ~24 h, and persists for ~30 h — far beyond the
compound's own kinetics. Cells transfected with SOD2 siRNA lose the delayed protection
entirely (protection ratio 1.02). Catal rose only ~2.6-fold and glutathione peroxidase was
unchanged, so SOD2 is the specific effector.

> [!info] Same signal, different outcome
> The same NF-κB activation is induced by [[TNF-alpha]] and by other thiols including
> N-acetylcysteine, oltipraz, mesna, captopril and dithiothreitol. The redox potential of
> the sulfhydryl is the determinant of activity — a useful generalisation for thiol drugs.

**Polyamine chemistry.** WR-1065 increases putrescine and spermidine synthesis and
participates in polyamine-mediated chromatin stabilisation, which is proposed to underlie
the anti-mutagenic but not the cytoprotective effect. Oxidation to WR-33278 consumes
dissolved oxygen, producing an hypoxymimetic effect and upregulating hypoxia-inducible
proteins.

**Transcription factor binding.** Labelled WR-1065 binds directly to [[NF-kB]], AP-1 and
[[p53]], enhancing their DNA binding and transactivation. p53 induction is JNK-dependent
and DNA-damage-independent, and WR-1065 directly reduces critical cysteines on p53,
increasing its sequence-specific DNA binding in vitro; it can reactivate some mutant p53
forms. It also catalytically inactivates mammalian topoisomerase II.

**Genomic stability.** WR-1065 reduces both high- and low-LET radiation-induced delayed
genomic instability measured by reporter hyperrecombination, mutation frequency and
micronucleus formation in surviving cells.

## Concentration dependence

The dissociation between cytoprotection and anti-mutagenesis is one of the more useful
facts about this compound:

| Concentration | Protects from immediate cell killing | Protects from mutagenesis / genomic instability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 mM | Yes | Yes |
| 40 µM | No | Yes |

Both doses produce comparable SOD2 induction and comparable delayed radioprotection when
cells are challenged 24 h later.

## Connections

- [[Amifostine]] — the prodrug whose dephosphorylation by alkaline phosphatase generates
  WR-1065; the only FDA-approved radioprotector.
- [[NRF2]] — a distinct but convergent antioxidant transcription factor route; WR-1065's
  documented antioxidant gene induction runs through [[NF-kB]] → SOD2 rather than through
  the Keap1–NRF2 axis, and the two should not be conflated.
- [[MnSOD]] — the effector of delayed radioprotection; SOD2 siRNA abolishes it.
- [[NF-kB]] — the redox-sensitive transcription factor WR-1065 activates by reducing p50/p65
  cysteines, which is what drives SOD2 induction.
- [[p53]] — directly bound and redox-activated by WR-1065 via a DNA-damage-independent
  JNK-dependent pathway.
- [[Cisplatin]] — the cytotoxic whose nephrotoxicity amifostine/WR-1065 reduces by
  detoxifying reactive platinum metabolites.
- [[Topoisomerase]] — mammalian topoisomerase II is catalytically inactivated by WR-1065.
- [[Radiotherapy]] — the treatment context in which amifostine is licensed to reduce
  radiation-induced xerostomia.
- [[Glutathione]] — thiol-redox coupling partner; WR-1065's antioxidant effects are
  expressed through the cellular thiol network.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — the radical species WR-1065 scavenges directly in the
  immediate effect.
- [[Hypoxia]] — the hypoxymimetic consequence of WR-33278 formation and oxygen consumption.
