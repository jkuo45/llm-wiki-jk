# ACLY

**ATP-citrate lyase (ACLY)** is the cytosolic enzyme that cleaves [[Citrate]] into [[Acetyl-CoA]] and oxaloacetate in an ATP- and CoA-dependent reaction. It is the principal enzyme converting mitochondria-derived citrate into the cytosolic acetyl-CoA pool, which feeds both de novo lipogenesis and [[Histone Acetylation]]. In cellular senescence, ACLY is upregulated and required for the epigenetic activation of [[SASP]] genes.

## Overview

ACLY links mitochondrial [[TCA cycle|TCA cycle]] output to the nuclear acetyl-CoA pool. Citrate exported by [[SLC25A1]] is the principal substrate for cytosolic acetyl-CoA production, and ACLY is the enzyme that executes this conversion. Because nuclear [[Histone Acetyltransferase|acetyltransferases]] draw on cytosolic acetyl-CoA, ACLY activity directly couples mitochondrial metabolism to [[Chromatin]] accessibility and [[Gene Expression]].

## Role in Cellular Senescence

- *ACLY* expression is increased across multiple senescence models (irradiation-induced, replicative, doxorubicin-induced)
- Silencing ACLY reduces both [[H3K27ac]] levels and SASP gene expression in irradiation-induced and replicative senescence
- Citrate supplementation increases histone acetylation and induces pro-inflammatory gene expression in proliferating cells, phenocopying the effect of enhanced substrate availability

> [!info] ACLY Is a Central Node of the Citrate–Acetyl-CoA Axis
> Multiple nodes of the pyruvate–citrate–acetyl-CoA axis — from pyruvate import ([[MPC]]), to citrate export ([[SLC25A1]]) and acetyl-CoA generation (ACLY) — converge to control histone acetylation at SASP loci, selectively modulating inflammatory gene expression without affecting the senescence-associated cell cycle arrest.

## Wider Context

Beyond senescence, ACLY is a well-studied metabolic enzyme in cancer biology, where it supports the lipogenic and acetyl-CoA demands of proliferating tumor cells. Its regulation of acetyl-CoA availability also connects nutrient status to the epigenome in diverse physiological contexts.
