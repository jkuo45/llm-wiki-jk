# Gap Junction

## Overview

[[Gap Junction|Gap junctions]] are clusters of intercellular channels formed by connexin proteins that directly connect the cytoplasm of adjacent cells, permitting the passage of ions and small molecules (<~1 kDa). In the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]], connexin channels—notably CX36—link [[Neuron|neurons]] and [[Microglia]], enabling rapid electrical and metabolic coupling.

## Gap Junctions in cGAS-STING Propagation

Gap junctions provide a direct, contact-dependent route for intercellular transfer of [[cGAMP]], the second messenger produced by [[cGAS]] upon DNA sensing. Once cGAMP diffuses through connexin channels such as CX36 into a neighboring cell, it can engage [[STING]] and activate the downstream [[IRF3]] and [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]] programs without that cell having sensed DNA itself. This mechanism allows cGAS-STING signaling to spread network-wide, amplifying [[Neuroinflammation]] far beyond the initiating insult.

## Functional Implications

By coupling cells metabolically and immunologically, gap junctions transform localized cGAS-STING activation into a coordinated tissue response. Modulating connexin channel permeability represents a potential strategy to limit pathological dissemination of cGAS-STING–driven inflammation while preserving physiological coupling.
