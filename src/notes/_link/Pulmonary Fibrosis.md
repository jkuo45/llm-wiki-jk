# Pulmonary Fibrosis

**Pulmonary fibrosis** is a chronic, progressive interstitial lung disease characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix and scar tissue in the lungs, leading to impaired gas exchange and respiratory failure. The most common form is idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), where the cause is unknown.

## Pathophysiology

Pulmonary fibrosis involves dysregulated wound healing following repetitive alveolar epithelial injury. Key processes include epithelial cell apoptosis, fibroblast proliferation and differentiation into myofibroblasts, and excessive collagen deposition. Mitochondrial dysfunction, including excessive [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|mitochondrial fission]] and impaired [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], contributes to alveolar epithelial cell death and fibrotic remodeling.

## Clinical Features

Progressive dyspnea, dry cough, and restrictive ventilatory defects. Median survival for IPF is 3–5 years from diagnosis. Current treatments include antifibrotic agents (pirfenidone, nintedanib) and lung transplantation.
