# p62

## Overview

[[p62]] (sequestosome-1; also written p62/SQSTM1) is a versatile scaffold protein that links polyubiquitinated substrates to the autophagy machinery. It both delivers cargo to the [[Autophagosome]] and is itself degraded by [[Autophagy]], making it a widely used biomarker of autophagic flux. p62 integrates stress, redox, and metabolic signaling through multiple protein–protein interaction domains.

## Structure & Domains

p62 contains an N-terminal PB1 domain (mediating self-oligomerization and association with [[KEAP1]]), a ZZ-type zinc finger, a TRAF6-binding domain, and a C-terminal [[LIR Motif]] (LC3-interacting region) that directly binds LC3-family proteins such as [[LC3]] and [[GABARAPL1]]. An adjacent ubiquitin-association (UBA) domain binds polyubiquitin chains on aggregated proteins and organelles.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Through its UBA domain, p62 recognizes polyubiquitinated protein aggregates, damaged mitochondria, and intracellular pathogens, clustering them into inclusion bodies. Its LIR motif then tethers this cargo to LC3 on the expanding phagophore, enabling selective autophagy. Degradation of p62 within the [[Lysosome]] completes clearance; accumulation of p62 therefore signals impaired autophagic flux. p62 also activates [[NF-kappa B|NF-κB]] via TRAF6 and sequesters [[KEAP1]] to activate [[NRF2]], coupling autophagy to antioxidant and inflammatory programs. These intersections connect it to [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Sirtuins|SIRT1]]-modulated stress responses.

## Physiological Function

p62 maintains proteostasis by clearing misfolded proteins and dysfunctional organelles, protecting against neurodegeneration and metabolic disease. It is essential for mitophagy and aggrephagy.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

p62 accumulation is a hallmark of autosomal dominant inclusion-body myopathy (IBMPFD) and [[Paget disease]], and of many proteinopathies including those in [[Parkinson Disease|Parkinson's disease]]. In [[Cancer]], p62 can act as both tumor suppressor (via proteostasis) and promoter (via NF-κB survival signaling), depending on context.
