# Inflammatory Bowel Disease

**Inflammatory Bowel Disease** (IBD) is a group of chronic, relapsing inflammatory conditions of the gastrointestinal tract, with **ulcerative colitis** and **[[Crohn's Disease|Crohn's disease]]** as the principal forms. In the necroptosis field IBD is a landmark disease context: pathological intestinal-epithelial [[Necroptosis|necroptosis]] is what happens when the [[FADD]]–[[Caspase-8]]–[[c-FLIP]] rheostat that normally suppresses [[RIPK3]] fails.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

- **Genetic loss of the necroptosis brake.** Conditional deletion of [[Caspase-8]] or [[FADD]] from intestinal epithelium causes spontaneous necroptosis and intestinal pathology that is morphologically similar to IBD — resembling Crohn's disease in particular. Crossing these mice onto a [[RIPK3]]-deficient background completely prevents the pathology, demonstrating the cell death is RIPK3-dependent (Günther et al., *Nature* 2011; Welz et al., *Nature* 2011).
- **Deubiquitinase loss.** Ablation of the [[RIPK1]]-deubiquitinase [[A20]] sensitizes mice to lethal colitis driven by TUNEL-positive TNF-mediated death of the intestinal epithelium (interpreted as apoptosis in the original report; necroptosis involvement remains possible).
- **Effector mechanisms.** Two non-exclusive routes link this cell death to disease: the high immunogenicity of necrotic cells ([[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMP]] release) and the loss of barrier function that follows epithelial necroptosis.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]]
> Linkermann & Green frame intestinal-epithelial necroptosis as the molecular core of IBD-like pathology — RIPK3 ablation rescues the spontaneous colitis caused by FADD/caspase-8 loss in the intestinal epithelium.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

Classically presents with relapsing diarrhea, abdominal pain, rectal bleeding, and weight loss, with extra-intestinal manifestations; ulcerative colitis is confined to the colon with continuous mucosal inflammation, whereas Crohn's disease shows transmural, often discontinuous ("skip") lesions anywhere from mouth to anus. Clinical diagnosis uses endoscopy, histology, and imaging; there is no established blood biomarker specific to necroptotic versus apoptotic epithelial loss.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Standard therapy targets inflammation (aminosalicylates, steroids, immunomodulators, anti-TNF biologics). The necroptosis literature suggests a complementary axis: agents that restore the anti-necroptotic brake (caspase-8/c-FLIP function, A20 activity) or block [[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] could limit barrier loss and DAMP-driven inflammation — a hypothesis that remained preclinical as of the 2014 review.

## Connections

- [[Crohn's Disease]] — the IBD subtype most resembling the spontaneous necroptotic ileitis/colitis phenotype.
- [[Necroptosis]] — the RIPK3-dependent lytic program underlying epithelial loss in the genetic models.
- [[FADD]] / [[Caspase-8]] / [[c-FLIP]] — the suppressive complex whose loss licenses intestinal-epithelial necroptosis.

- [[A20]] — RIPK1-deubiquitinase whose ablation sensitizes to lethal TNF-mediated colitis.
