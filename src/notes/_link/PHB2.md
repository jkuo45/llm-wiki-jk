# PHB2

PHB2 (Prohibitin 2, also known as [[BAP37]]) is an inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) protein that functions as a [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] receptor. Under normal conditions, it remains "invisible" to cytosolic [[LC3]], but when [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] are damaged and the outer membrane is ruptured or degraded, PHB2 becomes exposed.

## Mechanism

PHB2 contains a [[LIR Motif|LIR motif]] (Y-V-L-P at positions 121–124) that binds [[LC3]] to initiate [[Autophagy|autophagy]]. Its exposure mechanism ensures that mitophagy is only triggered when mitochondrial damage is sufficiently severe to compromise the OMM, providing a "damage threshold" sensor.

## Role in Parkin-Independent Mitophagy

PHB2-mediated [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] can occur independently of the canonical [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] pathway. When excessive [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|mitochondrial fission]] causes physical rupture of the OMM (e.g., via excessive [[DRP1|Drp1]] activation in the absence of Parkin), exposed IMM proteins like PHB2 can be ubiquitinated by non-Parkin E3 [[Ubiquitin|ubiquitin]] ligases, generating signals that recruit the autophagy machinery to complete mitochondrial clearance.
