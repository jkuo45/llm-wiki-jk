# Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis

**Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT)** is the energy expended on physical activity that is not deliberate exercise: minor movements, posture maintenance, ambulatory activity, fidgeting, and routine daily movements (Levine _et al_. 1999, 2000, 2001; Levine, 2002). Together with the energy cost of deliberate exercise it forms the physical-activity component of [[Energy Expenditure|daily energy expenditure]]; it is the most variable component of the energy budget between individuals.

## Role in Energy Balance

NEAT can differ by hundreds of kilojoules per day between individuals of similar body size and accounts for a meaningful fraction of the divergence between predicted and actual energy requirements. Differences in NEAT contribute to resistance to [[Obesity|fat gain]] in overfeeding studies, and low NEAT (occupational and lifestyle inactivity) has been argued to be a determinant of weight gain in affluent societies (Levine _et al_. 1999).

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> Exercise may raise NEAT indirectly: as individuals become fitter and lose weight they may walk rather than drive or take the stairs rather than lifts, amplifying the energy deficit of the exercise itself. This behavioural amplification, alongside appetite and [[Resting Metabolic Rate|RMR]] effects, reconciles the observation that exercise interventions cause more weight loss than the direct energy cost of the exercise alone predicts.

## Determinants

- **Occupation and lifestyle** — occupational, household, and leisure activity patterns (Salmon _et al_. 2000); mechanisation of domestic labour and increased car use reduce NEAT.
- **Fitness level and body weight** — weight loss and increased fitness are associated with increased spontaneous activity.
- **Socio-economic and ethnic factors** — differing susceptibility to weight gain across groups may reflect NEAT differences (Brownson _et al_. 2000; Weinsier _et al_. 2002).

## Measurement

NEAT is quantified by subtracting RMR, thermic effect of food, and deliberate-exercise costs from total daily energy expenditure measured with [[Doubly-Labelled Water|doubly-labelled water]], often combined with movement sensors (accelerometry, inclinometry) or respiratory chambers (Levine _et al_. 2001).
