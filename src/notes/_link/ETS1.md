# ETS1

**ETS1** (ETS proto-oncogene 1) is the founding member of the ETS family of winged helix-turn-helix transcription factors. It recognizes GGAA/T core motifs and coordinates transcriptional programs for cell invasion, matrix remodeling, angiogenesis, and immune responses. ETS1 is frequently overexpressed in aggressive solid tumors, where it drives epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)-like phenotypes and metastatic behavior, and is also a key effector in T-cell and endothelial biology.

## Regulation by SIRT3 through histone crotonylation

In the context of [[Histone Crotonylation|histone crotonylation]] described in the SIRT3 review, [[SIRT3]] decreases H3K27 crotonylation at metastasis-associated loci such as the ETS1 promoter, effectively constraining neoplastic dissemination[98],[99]. The underlying mechanism involves the long noncoding RNA LINC00922, which decoys SIRT3 from the ETS1 promoter, thereby upregulating H3K27 crotonylation of the ETS1 promoter and facilitating colorectal cancer metastasis (Liao et al., Mol Cancer 2023; doi: 10.1186/s12943-023-01859-y)[15]. ETS1 is therefore both a downstream transcriptional effector and a direct chromatin target of the SIRT3 decrotonylase axis.

## Clinical relevance

Because ETS1 sits at the interface of transcriptional plasticity and metastatic competence, epigenetic restoration of SIRT3-mediated decrotonylation at its promoter is proposed as a strategy to constrain dissemination in ETS1-driven cancers. The ETS1 locus exemplifies how a metabolic enzyme (SIRT3) exerts epigenetic control over an oncogenic transcription factor.
