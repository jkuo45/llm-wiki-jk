# Small Humanin-Like Peptides

**Small Humanin-Like Peptides (SHLPs)** are a family of short, mitochondria-derived peptides encoded within the open reading frame of *MTRNR2L*, the humanin gene on the mitochondrial genome. They are structurally related to [[Humanin]] and to the other mitochondrial-derived peptides (MDPs) such as [[MOTS-c]] and MOTS-a.

## Properties & Proposed Mechanisms

- **Sequence and localisation:** the SHLP family comprises several short peptides (SHLP1, SHLP2, SHLP3) translated from mitochondrial mRNA; like other MDPs they are secreted and circulate in blood, so they are measurable in human plasma.
- **Signalling:** MDPs act in a receptor-independent fashion at low nanomolar concentrations, with proposed receptors on the plasma membrane and in mitochondria; reported effects include attenuation of oxidative stress, improved mitochondrial function, and modulation of [[AMPK]] and [[SIRT1]]-dependent pathways.
- **Metabolic context:** SHLP/Humanin signalling has been linked to [[Insulin]] sensitivity, glucose handling in adipocytes and skeletal muscle, and to protection from [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]-mediated injury in neurons and cardiomyocytes.
- **Humanin as the parent peptide:** [[Humanin]] itself is a 24-residue MDP with anti-inflammatory and glucose-handling effects in rodents; SHLPs are the smaller related species and share parts of that receptor context.

## Clinical Relevance

Plasma MDP levels change with ageing, obesity and metabolic disease, and Humanin/SHLP biology is an active target for longevity-oriented drug development. Evidence in humans remains associative — no interventional trial has yet shown that raising SHLPs improves human healthspan — so the field is best described as promising but unproven.
