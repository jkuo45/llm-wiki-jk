# Interferon

**Interferons (IFNs)** are a family of signaling cytokines that establish an antiviral state and tune innate and adaptive immunity. Three classes: **type I** (IFN-α subtypes, [[IFN-β]]), **type II** ([[Interferon-gamma|IFN-γ]]), and **type III** (IFN-λ1–4). In necroptosis-relevant signaling, IFN — chiefly IFN-β — is the transcriptional output of the [[TLR3]]/[[TRIF]] → [[TBK1]] → [[IRF3]] axis, parallel to the [[NF-κB]] inflammatory branch.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Induction:** dsRNA → [[TLR3]] → [[TRIF]] → [[TRAF3]] → [[TBK1]]/IKKε phosphorylates [[IRF3]] → IRF3 dimer translocates to nucleus → IFN-β transcription. Cytosolic routes (RIG-I/MDA5–MAVS, cGAS–[[STING]]) converge on the same TBK1–IRF3 node.
- **Response:** secreted IFN binds IFNAR (type I) or IFNGR (type II) → JAK1/TYK2 → STAT1/STAT2–IRF9 (ISGF3) → transcription of [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs)]].
- **Necroptosis crosstalk:** [[TRIF]] simultaneously recruits [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]] via [[RHIM]]–RHIM contacts, so a single TLR3/4 stimulus splits into an IFN arm and a [[Necroptosis]] arm; [[MLKL]]-driven [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMP]] release further amplifies IFN and [[IL-6]]/TNF programs.

## Physiological Function

IFNs restrict viral replication, activate NK cells and macrophages, promote antigen presentation, and — when chronic — sustain senescence-associated and sterile inflammation.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Acute IFN is antiviral and antitumoral; chronic type I IFN signatures drive lupus, atherosclerosis, and [[Inflammaging]]. Recombinant IFNs are therapeutics (hepatitis, multiple sclerosis); JAK/TBK1 inhibitors dampen pathogenic IFN.
