# COVID-19

**COVID-19** (coronavirus disease 2019) is an infectious respiratory disease caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). It ranges from asymptomatic infection to severe viral pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, and multi-organ involvement, with systemic inflammation and immune dysregulation as central features.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

COVID-19 results from SARS-CoV-2 infection of angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 ([[ACE2]])-expressing cells, driving inflammatory and oxidative stress pathways. Dysregulated immune responses, lymphopenia, and cytokine release contribute to severe lung injury and extrapulmonary complications.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

- **[[SIRT1]]**: An unbalanced p53/SIRT1 axis impacts lymphocyte homeostasis in COVID-19 — persistently activated p53 due to low SIRT1 may contribute to lymphopenia; increased SIRT1 may alleviate pathogenesis.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Serum SIRT3 levels are associated with clinical outcome/prognosis, markedly lower in severe versus mild/moderate patients, suggesting SIRT3 as a prognostic marker.

## Therapeutic Implications

Sirtuin modulation (particularly SIRT1 activation to restore p53 balance and lymphocyte homeostasis) is proposed as a strategy to mitigate immune dysfunction in COVID-19, while serum SIRT3 may serve as a severity biomarker.
