# S-Nitrosoglutathione Reductase

**S-nitrosoglutathione reductase (GSNOR)** is an enzyme that metabolizes S-nitrosoglutathione (GSNO), thereby regulating cellular protein [[S-Nitrosylation|S-nitrosylation]] levels. By controlling the available GSNO pool, GSNOR tunes the nitric-oxide-derived post-translational modification of target proteins.

## Mechanism

- GSNOR catalyzes the reduction/denitrosylation of GSNO, limiting accumulation of S-nitrosylated proteins. Loss of GSNOR activity prolongs and intensifies S-nitrosylation of substrates.

## Role in Senescence & Mitophagy

- In senescent cells, **GSNOR activity is reduced**, leading to excessive S-nitrosylation of [[Parkin]], the E3 ligase that drives [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]].
- Nitrosylated Parkin is less active, hindering mitophagy. Impaired mitophagy causes accumulation of low-quality mitochondria, raising [[ROS]] production and driving senescence.
- GSNOR thus sits at the intersection of nitrosative stress and mitochondrial quality control in the senescence program.
