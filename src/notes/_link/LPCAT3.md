# LPCAT3

**LPCAT3** (lysophosphatidylcholine acyltransferase 3) is an endoplasmic-reticulum and nuclear-envelope enzyme that remodels phospholipids by acylating lysophospholipid acceptors with polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), especially arachidonate and adrenate. It is a positive regulator of [[Ferroptosis]] because, together with [[ACSL4]], it builds the oxidizable PUFA-containing [[Phosphatidylethanolamine]] (PE) pool that lipoxygenases and the [[Fenton Reaction]] peroxidize to kill cells.

## Structure & Biochemistry

LPCAT3 is an integral membrane protein belonging to the membrane-bound O-acyltransferase (MBOAT) family. It catalyzes the remodeling (deacylation–reacylation, the "Lands cycle") of phospholipids, inserting PUFA moieties at the sn-2 position of glycerophospholipids, thereby determining the peroxidizability of cellular membranes.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

By incorporating PUFAs into phospholipids—critically into PE species—LPCAT3 increases the abundance of PUFA-PE, the preferred substrate for [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and for [[ALOX15]]/[[Lipoxygenase]]-mediated oxidation. Knockdown of LPCAT3 (or [[ACSL4]]) reduces PUFA-PE and protects cells from ferroptosis, whereas its overexpression sensitizes them. LPCAT3 thus operates upstream of the [[GPX4]]-controlled execution step by setting substrate availability (Dixon–Stockwell framework; review Fig. 2).

> [!tip] Substrate gatekeeper
> Because LPCAT3 and ACSL4 govern *how much* oxidizable lipid is present, they set the threshold for ferroptosis independently of GSH/GPX4 levels—relevant in tumors where lipid remodeling drives sensitivity.

## Physiological Function

LPCAT3 maintains phospholipid diversity, membrane fluidity, and lipid-signaling precursor pools; it is highly expressed in intestine and metabolic tissues.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

LPCAT3 is implicated in [[Ferroptosis]] across cancer, neurodegeneration, and [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]]. Its role in generating ferroptosis-prone membranes makes it a candidate sensitizer, though systemic inhibition would perturb essential lipid homeostasis.
