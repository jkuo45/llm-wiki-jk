# IL-10

**Interleukin-10 (IL-10)** is a potent anti-inflammatory cytokine that plays a critical role in limiting excessive immune responses and preventing tissue damage. It is produced by a wide range of immune cells including [[Macrophages]], [[CD4+ T Cells]], regulatory T cells, and [[B Cells]].

## Structure & Receptor

IL-10 is a homodimeric cytokine belonging to the class-II cytokine family. It signals through the IL-10 receptor complex composed of IL-10R1 (ligand-binding chain) and IL-10R2 (accessory chain). IL-10R1 expression is restricted to hematopoietic cells, while IL-10R2 is ubiquitously expressed.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

IL-10 binding to IL-10R1/IL-10R2 activates the [[JAK]]/STAT signaling cascade, primarily phosphorylating **STAT3**. Activated STAT3 translocates to the nucleus and induces transcription of anti-inflammatory genes including *SOCS3* and *BCL3*, which in turn suppress pro-inflammatory cytokine production and inhibit [[NF-κB|NF-κB]] activity. IL-10 also activates STAT1 and STAT5 in certain cell types.

### Key Anti-Inflammatory Effects

- Suppresses production of pro-inflammatory cytokines including [[TNFα]], [[IL-12]], [[IL-6]], and [[IL-1β]].
- Inhibits antigen presentation by downregulating [[MHC type II]] and co-stimulatory molecules on antigen-presenting cells.
- Reduces oxidative burst in [[Macrophages]] by inhibiting NADPH oxidase activity.
- Promotes regulatory T cell differentiation and function.

## Biological Functions

### Immune Homeostasis
IL-10 is essential for maintaining immune tolerance and preventing excessive inflammation in mucosal tissues. Mice deficient in IL-10 or IL-10R develop spontaneous colitis due to unchecked inflammatory responses to commensal microbiota.

### Infection & Pathogen Clearance
While IL-10 limits tissue damage during infection, excessive IL-10 can impair pathogen clearance by suppressing protective Th1 responses. Many pathogens exploit IL-10 to evade immune elimination.

### Tumor Microenvironment
IL-10 can have dual roles in cancer. It limits tumor-promoting inflammation in some contexts but may also suppress anti-tumor immunity by inhibiting [[CD8+ T Cells]] and antigen presentation.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **Inflammatory Bowel Disease**: Polymorphisms in *IL10* and *IL10RA/B* are associated with Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis.
- **Sepsis**: Elevated IL-10 levels correlate with immunosuppression and increased mortality in septic patients.
- **Chronic Infections**: IL-10 is upregulated in chronic HIV, HCV, and tuberculosis, contributing to immune exhaustion.
- **Autoimmune Disease**: Defective IL-10 signaling is implicated in rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, and [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus|systemic lupus erythematosus]].
- **Therapeutic Potential**: Recombinant IL-10 and IL-10-inducing therapies are under investigation for inflammatory and autoimmune conditions.
