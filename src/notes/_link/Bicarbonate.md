# Bicarbonate

**Bicarbonate** (IUPAC: hydrogencarbonate, HCO3−) is an amphiprotic anion of 61.01 Da. It is simultaneously the conjugate base of carbonic acid (H2CO3) and the conjugate acid of carbonate (CO32−), which is what lets it buffer in both directions. It is the dominant form of dissolved inorganic carbon in seawater and most fresh water, and a major sink in the carbon cycle.

## Acid–base chemistry in the body

Roughly 70–75% of CO2 in the body is converted to H2CO3, and carbonic anhydrase catalyses the reversible reaction in both directions:

CO2 + H2O ⇌ H2CO3 ⇌ H+ + HCO3−

The Henderson–Hasselbalch relationship for the bicarbonate buffer is pH = 6.1 + log([HCO3−] / (0.03 × PaCO2)). Together with the lungs (which set PaCO2) and the kidneys (which reclaim filtered bicarbonate and generate new bicarbonate by ammonium excretion), it holds arterial pH near 7.40. This matters most in the [[Blood-Brain Barrier|cerebral compartment]] and in the [[Serum|serum]], where a pH excursion of a few tenths of a unit is not tolerated.

> [!info] Bicarbonate as a metabolite, not just a buffer
> Bicarbonate is also a metabolic substrate. Cytosolic bicarbonate is the carbon source for the carboxylation reactions of the pyruvate carboxylase, acetyl-CoA carboxylase, propionyl-CoA carboxylase and pyrimidine ring synthesis steps, so intracellular bicarbonate concentration is rate-limiting for [[Lipid Synthesis|anabolism]] and nucleotide production. The sodium–bicarbonate cotransporter SLC4A7 supplies the cytosol with extracellular bicarbonate, and [[mTORC1]] signalling was shown to raise its surface abundance and thereby stimulate de novo nucleotide synthesis and proliferation (Ali et al., *Mol Cell*, 2022). [[Carbonic Anhydrase]] inside the cell regenerates CO2 from HCO3− so pyruvate carboxylase has substrate.

## Gastrointestinal and other roles

Pancreatic duct cells secrete bicarbonate in response to [[Secretin]]-type signalling to neutralise gastric acid arriving in the duodenum; the resulting alkaline tide supports pancreatic enzyme activity and duodenal mucosal protection. Bicarbonate also raises intragastric pH after a meal and is the reason [[Sodium Bicarbonate]] works as an antacid and as a leavening agent (CO2 release on heating or on acid contact).

## Diagnostics

Serum bicarbonate is reported as part of a basic metabolic panel alongside chloride, sodium and potassium. The parameter *standard bicarbonate concentration* (SBCe) is the bicarbonate concentration extrapolated to a PaCO2 of 40 mmHg at full oxygen saturation and 36 °C — that is, the bicarbonate after respiratory compensation has been removed — and is used in acid–base diagnostics to distinguish metabolic from respiratory causes of pH change.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Bicarbonate is a poor standalone acid–base marker. A normal bicarbonate with a low chloride and a high anion gap means the bicarbonate is being consumed to buffer an unmeasured acid (a normal-anion-gap acidosis in progress). Interpretation requires PaCO2, and where venous or arterial sampling is unreliable, [[aPTT]] and an acid–base panel should be read together rather than bicarbonate alone.

## Connections

- [[Serum]] — Serum bicarbonate is the standard clinical measure of the bicarbonate buffer pool, reported on every basic metabolic panel and interpreted alongside chloride and the anion gap. Low values indicate metabolic acidosis, high values metabolic alkalosis.
- [[Carbon Dioxide]] — The CO2/H2CO3/HCO3− triad is one reaction in fast equilibrium; ventilation adjusts PaCO2, which is how the buffer line is held against a metabolic acid or base load. This is why a bicarbonate measurement without a PaCO2 is uninterpretable.
- [[Carbonic Anhydrase]] — The zinc metalloenzyme catalysing CO2 hydration/dehydration, present in erythrocytes, renal tubule and gastric parietal cells; it is what makes the buffer system fast enough to respond within seconds.
- [[mTORC1]] — mTORC1 was shown to regulate SLC4A7-dependent bicarbonate uptake, linking nutrient sensing to intracellular bicarbonate supply and thereby to nucleotide synthesis rates.
- [[Calcium]] — Both bicarbonate and calcium are load-bearing in the same carbonate mineral equilibria; the blood–bicarbonate buffer and calcium homeostasis interact wherever alkalosis drives the albumin-bound calcium fraction down.
