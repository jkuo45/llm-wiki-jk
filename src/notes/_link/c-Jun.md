# c-Jun

c-Jun (encoded by the JUN proto-oncogene) is a basic leucine zipper (bZIP) transcription factor and a core subunit of the AP-1 (activator protein-1) complex. In senescence, c-Jun acts as a pioneer factor that opens enhancers at SASP and senescence-related genes; its depletion alone is sufficient to partially reverse the senescence cell-cycle arrest, underscoring its dual role in arrest and secretome control.

## Overview

c-Jun dimerizes with c-Fos, JunB, FosB, and other partners to form AP-1, which binds TPA-response elements (TREs; TGAG/CTCA) and regulates proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation, and stress responses. As a member of the immediate-early gene program, c-Jun is rapidly induced by mitogenic and stress signals, including oncogene activation.

## Structure & Function

The bZIP domain mediates DNA binding and dimerization. c-Jun is regulated by phosphorylation (e.g., by JNK and ERK) that modulates its transcriptional activity and stability. Within AP-1, c-Jun confers pioneer-like ability to engage nucleosomal DNA and initiate chromatin opening at otherwise inaccessible enhancers.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review identifies AP-1 — and specifically the AP-1 superfamily member c-Jun — as a pioneer factor binding enhancers of SASP and other senescence genes, initiating closed-chromatin opening and gene activation in replicative and oncogene-induced senescence (ref. 57). Notably, depletion of c-Jun alone is adequate to partially reverse the cell-cycle arrest, linking AP-1 to the arrest machinery itself. This is balanced by the reader [[HDAC4]], which is recruited to [[H3K27ac]] loci and, through [[HDAC3]], buffers the AP-1 program until degraded upon senescence entry. Thus c-Jun (as AP-1) is the upstream pioneer that opens SASP enhancers later co-occupied by [[BRD4]] and [[CEBPα]] to drive NF-κB-regulated SASP genes.

## Mechanistic Details

- c-Jun/AP-1 pioneer binding de novo activates enhancers (marked by [[H3K4me1]], [[H3K27ac]], eRNAs) at SASP loci.
- c-Jun depletion reduces NF-κB-regulated SASP gene expression and partially releases growth arrest, distinguishing it from HDAC4 (whose depletion enhances SASP).
- The AP-1 → H3K27ac → BRD4/CEBPα axis is a central epigenetic pathway to SASP in the review.

## Therapeutic Relevance

AP-1/c-Jun is a difficult direct drug target, but the downstream readers (BRD4, HDAC4–HDAC3) offer more tractable [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] nodes. BET inhibitors and H3K27ac-axis modulators blunt the AP-1-opened SASP program, preserving arrest while taming inflammation.
