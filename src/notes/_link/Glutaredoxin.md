# Glutaredoxin

**Glutaredoxins** (**Grx**) are a conserved family of small (~9–14 kDa) glutathione-dependent thiol oxidoreductases that reduce protein disulfides and reverse protein S-glutathionylation, passing electrons from reduced [[Glutathione]] (GSH) instead of from [[Thioredoxin reductase]]. They function in parallel with the [[Thioredoxin]]/[[Thioredoxin-1]] system, providing a partially redundant reducing capacity that sustains [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]], [[Peroxiredoxin|peroxiredoxins]], and redox-sensitive signaling proteins, and links the thiol-redox economy directly to the [[Glutathione]]/glutathione-reductase cycle.

## Family Members & Localization

- **GLRX1** (12 kDa, cytosolic, dithiol) — the classical glutaredoxin with the CPYC [[CxxC Motif]]; catalyzes both disulfide reduction and deglutathionylation.
- **GLRX2** (mitochondrial, dithiol) — maintains mitochondrial thiol-redox balance.
- **GLRX3** (monothiol, aka PICOT) — deglutathionylates substrates with a single catalytic cysteine.
- **GLRX5** (monothiol) — essential for mitochondrial and cytosolic iron-sulfur cluster biogenesis.

## Catalytic Mechanism

- **Dithiol mechanism** (disulfide reduction): the nucleophilic active-site cysteine attacks the substrate disulfide to form a mixed disulfide; the resolving cysteine releases the reduced substrate, leaving oxidized Grx; the Grx disulfide is reduced by GSH in two steps (Grx–SG mixed disulfide, then a second GSH), yielding GSSG that [[Glutathione Reductase]] recycles with NADPH.
- **Monothiol mechanism** (deglutathionylation): a single catalytic cysteine attacks protein-*S*-glutathione mixed disulfides, releasing free substrate thiol + GSH.
- Net effect: a **GSH-dependent disulfide-reducing pathway** — the counterpart of the NADPH→[[Thioredoxin reductase]]→[[Thioredoxin-1]] route.

## Physiological Function

- **Reduction of [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]]** — glutaredoxin was discovered through its ability to donate electrons to RNR; it provides a GSH-routed backup to thioredoxin for de novo dNTP synthesis (both systems can sustain ribonucleotide reduction; combined loss is lethal in model organisms).
- **Deglutathionylation / redox signaling** — regenerates protein thiols after S-glutathionylation of kinases, caspases, actin, and transcription factors ([[NF-κB]], Akt), modulating [[Apoptosis]] and stress responses.
- **Peroxiredoxin support** — Grx can regenerate peroxiredoxins in contexts where the thioredoxin route is limiting.
- **Iron-sulfur metabolism** — GLRX5-dependent Fe-S cluster assembly links Grx to mitochondrial function.
- **Compensation for thioredoxin loss** — cells with impaired thioredoxin systems up-regulate or depend on glutaredoxin, explaining partial redundancy in redox defense.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer**: glutaredoxin is up-regulated in many tumors, supporting the redox fitness and drug resistance that mirror thioredoxin-system up-regulation; Grx inhibition is explored as a sensitizing strategy.
- **Inflammation & ischemia**: deglutathionylation of signaling proteins modulates inflammatory and reperfusion injury.
- **Mitochondrial disease**: GLRX5 mutations cause microcytic anemia / sideroblastic anemia with iron-sulfur cluster defects.
- **Pharmacology**: the requirement for GSH makes Grx enzymes sensitive to [[Glutathione]] depletion and to glutaredoxin inhibitors explored for infectious-disease and oncology applications.
