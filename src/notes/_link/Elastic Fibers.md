# Elastic Fibers

**Elastic fibers** are insoluble components of the [[Extracellular Matrix]] in dynamic connective tissues — large arteries, skin, lungs, ligaments and auricular cartilage. They are the structures that let tissues withstand repeated cycles of stretch and recoil across a lifetime: a large artery expands by ~10% of diameter with each systole and must return to baseline during diastole, and only elastic fibers make that reversible. Elastin and fibrillin microfibrils arose at different points in evolution — fibrillin microfibrils are present in early metazoans, whereas elastin and true elastic fibers emerged only in vertebrates, as a requirement for high-pressure circulatory systems.

## Structure & Assembly

- **Two-component architecture.** A core of cross-linked **[[Elastin]]** — an amorphous, hydrophobic, lysine-rich protein — surrounded by and embedded within a scaffold of **microfibrils** made chiefly of fibrillin-1 and fibrillin-2 (fibrillin-3 is developmental). Fibrillins are ~350 kDa multidomain glycoproteins with 43 calcium-binding EGF-like domains, seven eight-cysteine (TB) motifs and two hybrid domains. The eight-cysteine domains are unique to fibrillins and LTBPs, which is why the two families are evolutionarily linked.
- **"Beads on a string."** Fibrillin-1 monomers are ~150 nm long with helical packing that produces a beaded appearance, arranged parallel and unstaggered; the interbead region carries the elasticity.
- **Tissue-specific architecture.** Arterial elastic fibers form concentric, fenestrated **lamellar sheets** (elastic lamellae); dermal elasticity uses thick reticular fibers plus thin oxytalan, elaunin and elastic fiber layers in the papillary dermis; alveolar fibers form fine networks permitting respiratory excursion.
- **Where they sit in the vessel wall.** Lamellar units are defined by elastic sheets with vascular smooth muscle cells and collagen between them. The descending thoracic aorta has ~60 lamellar units in humans; peripheral muscular arteries have fewer than three. The **internal elastic lamina** separates media from intima; an external elastic lamina is present only in large arteries.

Assembly is a four-stage, largely cell-surface process coordinated by ~30 accessory proteins:

1. Microfibrils assemble in the extracellular space, requiring fibrillin processing by furin/PACE convertases and fibronectin–integrin (α5β1, αvβ3) interactions; heparan and chondroitin sulfate glycosaminoglycans align them.
2. Soluble **tropoelastin** coacervates into globules at the cell surface — "microassembly" — with fibulin-4 and fibulin-5 modulating aggregate size and conformation.
3. Globules are transferred onto the microfibril scaffold via fibulins binding both fibrillin-1 and tropoelastin; further lysyl oxidase (LOX/LOXL1) crosslinking gives the mature fiber — "macroassembly".
4. The crosslinked fiber is deposited into the matrix and is extremely long-lived: there is little adult elastic fiber assembly, so the fibers laid down during mid-gestation must serve a lifetime.

> [!info] Two functions, one structure
> Beyond mechanics, microfibrils bind latent TGF-β binding proteins and thereby **sequester latent TGF-β in the matrix**, controlling its activation and bioavailability. LTBP1 interacts specifically with fibrillin-1 microfibrils, and LTBP3 is reduced in fibrillin-1-null aorta. The relationship is bidirectional: elastin accumulation modulates TGF-β availability by changing latent-complex binding, and elastinopathies that alter fiber integrity change TGF-β activity. This makes elastic fibers a signalling platform, not merely a spring.

## Physiological Function

- **Windkessel effect.** Elastic fibers store energy during systole and release it in diastole, dampening pulse pressure and sustaining laminar flow into the microcirculation where gas exchange occurs. Elastin accounts for 40–60% of dry weight in the ascending aorta depending on species.
- **Nonlinear stress–strain behaviour.** Elastin and collagen divide the load: elastase-digested arteries dilate early in the pressure–diameter curve, collagenase-digested arteries only at high pressure. Sequential recruitment of stiff collagen at high stress is what makes the wall stiff in systole yet compliant in diastole.
- **Arterial morphogenesis.** Elastin-null mice die perinatally of arterial obstruction — elastin is required for arterial morphogenesis, not only for elasticity.
- **Lung and airway recoil**, skin extensibility, and ligamentous elasticity all depend on the same structure.
- **Cell signalling.** Microfibrils signal through integrin and syndecan receptors; elastin acts as an autocrine factor controlling the quiescent contractile phenotype of arterial smooth muscle cells.

> [!warning] Elastic recoil and tensile strength are separable
> Genetic dissection shows the two components are not redundant. Elastin haploinsufficiency raises blood pressure and produces smaller, longer, more tortuous vessels with more lamellar units; fibrillin-1 haploinsufficiency instead increases aortic diameter. Elastin provides **elastic recoil**, fibrillin-1 provides **tensile strength** and opposes pressure-induced dilation. Combining both mutations produces roughly additive effects on stiffness with partially overlapping effects on compliance.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **[[Elastin]] gene (*ELN*) mutations** cause **supravalvular aortic stenosis** (arterial narrowing, hypertension) and **autosomal dominant cutis laxa-1** (loose skin, pulmonary emphysema, aortic dilation). Insufficient elastin generally produces narrowing, not aneurysm.
- ***FBN1* mutations** cause Marfan syndrome and Weill–Marchesani — aortic root **dilation** and aneurysm. Loss of TGF-β sequestration in the matrix by fibrillin-1 drives aberrant TGF-β activity and aortic aneurysm.
- ***FBLN4*/*FBLN5* mutations** cause autosomal recessive cutis laxa; *LOX* mutations also cause aneurysm. Disorders of *improper or disorganised* elastic fiber deposition (cutis laxa-1, FBLN4, LOX) associate with **aneurysm**, whereas pure elastin insufficiency (SVAS) does not.
- **Aortic aneurysm:** degraded, fragmented or improperly assembled elastic fibers are the critical lesion. Fragments are more protease-sensitive, permitting progressive dilation, inflammatory cell infiltration, altered TGF-β sequestration, and macrophage activation by released elastin-derived peptides. Aneurysm homogenates show high elastinolytic activity — macrophage elastase (MMP-12) is the major elastinolytic enzyme in lung.
- **[[Atherosclerosis]], [[Hypertension]], [[Diabetes]] and [[Aging]]** all involve age- and risk-associated proinflammatory elastic fiber remodelling: loss of integrity, elasticity, calcification and amyloidosis of the fiber, with increased arterial stiffness — itself a major risk factor for hypertensive and atherosclerotic vasculopathy.
- **Pulmonary:** cigarette smoke–induced emphysema requires macrophage elastase.
- **Skin:** elastin-derived peptides (elastokines) are biomarkers of elastin degradation in COPD, atherosclerosis and skin ageing; MMP-12 inhibition and anti-elastokine strategies are explored as ways to slow age-related elastin loss.

## Connections
- [[Elastin]] — the cross-linked amorphous core of the mature fiber and its most abundant component; the fiber is named for it, and elastin-specific defects (SVAS, cutis laxa-1) versus fibrillin defects (Marfan) map onto narrowing versus dilation.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — the compartment elastic fibers inhabit; fiber assembly depends on ECM glycosaminoglycans, fibronectin and integrins that link matrix to the depositing cell.
- [[TGF-beta]] — sequestered in immature form by LTBP/fibrillin microfibrils, so elastic fiber integrity directly controls TGF-β bioavailability; this is the signalling axis behind Marfan-associated aneurysm.
- [[TGF-β Signaling]] — the downstream pathway whose availability is gated by elastic fiber assembly, making fibrosis and aneurysm two outputs of the same structural defect.
- [[Arterial Stiffness]] — the direct functional consequence of elastic fiber loss or fragmentation; increased stiffness is a major risk factor for hypertensive and atherosclerotic vasculopathy and for left ventricular hypertrophy.
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — shares elastin fragmentation as a lesion feature, and elastic fiber integrity degrades in parallel with plaque development.
- [[Hypertension]] — elastin haploinsufficiency causes it directly, and it in turn drives adaptive arterial remodelling that further stresses the elastic fibers.
- [[Collagen]] — the mechanical partner; collagen provides tensile strength and only engages at high stress, which together with elastin generates the artery's nonlinear pressure–diameter behaviour.
- [[Fibroblast]] — a principal source of elastic fiber components in skin and other tissues, alongside smooth muscle cells in vessels.
- [[MMP]] — matrix metalloproteinases, especially MMP-12 macrophage elastase, degrade elastic fibers and generate elastin-derived peptides, linking fiber destruction to inflammation.
- [[Aging]] — age-associated proinflammatory remodelling of elastic fibers is a core mechanism of arterial stiffening and vascular disease in the elderly.
- [[Diabetes]] — one of the metabolic conditions linked to acquired elastic fiber defects and arterial stiffening.
