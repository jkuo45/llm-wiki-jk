# Complement System

The **complement system** is a network of approximately 30 soluble plasma
proteins, membrane-bound receptors, and regulatory factors that constitutes the
fastest-acting arm of humoral immunity — it can lyse a bacterium within
milliseconds of recognition, long before an antibody response can be mounted.
It is not a single cascade but three pathways converging on a shared terminal
machinery, and it is continuously active at low level in plasma, so its output
is governed almost as much by inhibitors as by activation.

## Architecture: three entry points, one common tail

**Classical pathway.** Initiated by [[Immune Complex|IgG or IgM]] binding C1q
on an antigen-bearing surface. C1q belongs to the [[MHC Class Ib|MHC class I]]
superfamily and its activation is a collaboration with the adaptive system:
the antibody is the recognition element, the complement is the effector. This
is the pathway behind type II and type III [[Hypersensitivity]] reactions.

**Lectin (mannose-binding) pathway.** Initiated when [[Mannose-Binding
Lectin]] or theficolin–MASPs recognise microbial surface mannose, fucose, or
N-acetylglucosamine. No antibody is required, and the discriminating feature
is the sugar repertoire of non-self surfaces — which is why the lectin pathway
attacks microbes and spares host.

**Alternative pathway.** Initiated directly on any surface that lacks
regulatory proteins, principally by spontaneous C3 hydrolysis to C3b. It
needs no pattern recognition at all; it discriminates self from non-self
purely by the density of regulators (factor H, factor I, CD46, CD55) on the
surface.

> [!info] Convergence and the terminal complex
> All three pathways generate C3 convertases — C4b2a, C3bBb, and (in the
> lectin and alternative arms) additional C3 convertase amplification loops.
> Each cleaves C3 into C3a, an anaphylatoxin, and C3b, an opsonin that
> covalently binds the target surface. Bound C3b recruits factor B to form
> C5 convertase, which cleaves C5 into C5a (potent chemoattractant and
> anaphylatoxin) and C5b. C5b assembles the membrane attack complex with C6,
> C7, C8, and polymerised C9 to form a transmembrane pore. The MAC punches a
> hole; it does not rupture cells by osmotic means, and gram-negative bacteria
> with an outer membrane are protected by LPS from the lytic fragment.

> [!info] The effector functions beyond lysis
> - **Opsonisation:** C3b and iC3b coat the target and are recognised by
>   complement receptor 1 on phagocytes. iC3b-driven uptake is
>   arguably the most important complement function in vivo.
> - **Inflammation:** C3a and C5a are anaphylatoxins and C5a is among the most
>   potent chemoattractants known, driving [[Neutrophils]] recruitment and
>   activation. C5a is also a direct driver of [[Pyroptosis]] and
>   [[NETosis]] in the neutrophil.
> - **Immune complex clearance:** C3b-mediated tagging lets erythrocytes carry
>   immune complexes to liver and spleen for clearance via CR1. Loss of this
>   pathway is a major cause of the circulating immune complexes and
>   [[Serum]] sickness seen in [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]].
> - **Antibody response amplification:** C3d-tagged antigen binds CR2/CD21 on
>   B cells, providing the co-stimulatory signal for an affinity-matured
>   response. Complement-deficient humans make poor antibody responses.

## Regulation: why uncontrolled complement is lethal

Because the system is tonically active, a large inhibitory set is essential.
[[Factor H]] and [[Factor I]] inactivate C3b and Bb on host surfaces;
[[C1 Inhibitor]] blocks the classical and lectin entry points; CD46, CD55
(DEC), and CD59 are membrane-anchored protectins. Non-lytic
[[Glutamate-gated Cl- channels|regulators]] such as vitronectin and clusterin
cap soluble C5b-7 and C5b-9.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The clinical interest in complement is overwhelmingly in its *pathology*,
> not its antimicrobial function. C1 inhibitor deficiency and factor H
> deficiency cause hereditary angioedema. C3 glomerulopathy and
> [[Dense Deposit Disease]] are driven by factor H dysregulation.
> Atypical haemolytic uraemic syndrome is treated with eculizumab and
> ravulizumab, C5 antibodies that have transformed a previously fatal
> disease. Paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria is treated the same way, and
> the infectious risk of blocking C5 — notably meningococcal infection — is
> the main cost. Complement activation is also implicated in
> [[Autoimmune Disease]] more broadly, and anti-C1q autoantibodies are a
> biomarker of lupus nephritis.
>
> The [[Retinal Pigment Epithelium]]/[[Macular Degeneration]] connection is
> among the best-validated local complement pathologies: drusen deposits
> contain C3 fragments and C5b-9, and CFH (Y402H) and C3 alleles are the
> strongest common genetic risk factors for [[Age-related Macular
> Degeneration|AMD]] of any class. This is a case where systemic complement
> genotyping predicts a local ocular disease.

## Roles in the vault's other threads

Complement sits directly on the [[Rheumatoid synovial fluid]] and
[[Immune Complex]] notes, where the inflammatory fraction of rheumatoid
disease and of serum sickness is complement-dependent. In the innate-to-
adaptive interface it explains why [[Inflammation]] in
[[Rheumatoid Arthritis]] and [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]] is driven by
immune-complex deposition rather than by direct antibody cytotoxicity. It is
also the reason [[Serum]]'s bactericidal and opsonising capacity is only
partly immunoglobulin: fresh serum owes much of its Gram-negative lytic
activity to the alternative pathway.

## Documents

- [[Immune Complex]] — the classical-pathway trigger; the deposited
  IgG/IgM–antigen complexes of type III hypersensitivity are what recruit C1q.
- [[Immune System]] — places complement as the innate effector arm
  interfacing with antibody, and lists it as an entity the immune-system
  note defers to.
- [[Retinal Pigment Epithelium]] — the RPE/AMD drusen axis, where local C3
  and C5b-9 deposition and CFH/C3 genetics are causal.
- [[Rheumatoid synovial fluid]] — the local inflammatory fluid in which
  complement fragments (C3a, C5a, and the membrane attack complex) are found
  at high concentration and drive neutrophil recruitment.
- [[Serum]] — the circulating compartment in which all complement components
  and regulators are present, and which is assayed for CH50 and AH50.
- [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]] — immune-complex deposition, hypocomplementaemia,
  anti-C1q antibodies, and the failure of CR1-mediated immune-complex clearance.

## Connections

- [[Immune Complex]] — the initiating stimulus for the classical pathway and
  the pathological substrate of lupus nephritis, vasculitis, and serum
  sickness; the classical pathway is the only complement arm that requires an
  antibody.
- [[Immune System]] — complement is the interface between innate and adaptive
  immunity, providing the effector output for antibody and the co-stimulatory
  CR2 signal for B-cell affinity maturation.
- [[Retinal Pigment Epithelium]] — the RPE and Bruch's membrane are a
  chronic complement-activation surface in AMD; this is the clearest example
  of complement as a driver of a degenerative rather than infectious disease.
- [[Rheumatoid synovial fluid]] — the site where complement activation
  products accumulate and where the anaphylatoxins recruit the neutrophils
  that dominate the histological picture.
- [[Serum]] — the source compartment for all complement components, and the
  basis of the CH50/AH50 functional assays used to screen for deficiency.
- [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]] — complement consumption produces
  hypocomplementaemia, which is both a diagnostic marker and a marker of
  activity; the same disease also breaks the CR1 pathway that should be
  clearing the complexes.
- [[Inflammation]] — C3a and C5a are among the most potent inflammatory
  mediators known, and C5a is a direct activator of the neutrophil oxidative
  burst and of [[NETosis]], connecting complement to the neutrophil chemistry
  documented in the [[HOCl-mediated Oxidation]] thread.
- [[Neutrophils]] — recruited by C5a, then activated by it, making complement
  the upstream trigger for the myeloperoxidase/HOCl injury cascade in
  inflammatory tissue.
