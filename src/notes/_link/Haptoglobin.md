# Haptoglobin

**Haptoglobin** is a liver-synthesized plasma glycoprotein (Hp1-1, 1-2, 2-1 allotypes) whose physiological role is to capture hemoglobin released into plasma by intravascular [[Hemolysis]].

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Haptoglobin and [[Hemopexin]] are the two circulating scavengers that keep free hemoglobin and heme non-toxic. Haptoglobin binds hemoglobin with near-irrational affinity; the complex is cleared through CD91 (LRP1)-bearing macrophages without liberating iron, so it acts as the anti-inflammatory counterweight to hemolysis. [[Eryptosis]] limits how much hemoglobin ever escapes in the first place, keeping haptoglobin capacity from saturating.

## Mechanism and clinical use

- Haptoglobin binds oxy/deoxy/met-hemoglobin with 10⁶–10¹⁰ M⁻¹ affinity and stabilizes Hb against oxidation to methemoglobin and heme release.
- Low/absent haptoglobin is a sensitive marker of intravascular hemolysis (alongside elevated LDH, low haptoglobin–hemoglobin complexes, reticulocytosis).
- Haptoglobin is also a natural antioxidant/angiogenic factor being explored in cancer and sepsis; Hp0 genotype (allele-null) is associated with diabetic retinopathy and chronic kidney disease.
- Binding to CD91 reprograms macrophages toward an antiinflammatory, M2-like phenotype — mechanistically the same kind of reprogramming that PS-driven [[Efferocytosis]] of eryptotic cells triggers.
