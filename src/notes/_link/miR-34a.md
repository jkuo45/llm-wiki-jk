miR-34a is a small non-coding RNA of the miR-34 family, best characterized as a direct transcriptional target of the tumor suppressor [[p53]] and a broad tumor suppressor in its own right. It is induced in response to DNA damage, oncogenic stress, and [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]], where it reinforces growth arrest and apoptosis by repressing a wide panel of target mRNAs (e.g., those coding for cell-cycle, MET, NOTCH, and stemness factors).

## miR-34a in the p53–SIRT1 senescence loop

A functionally important relationship links miR-34a to the sirtuin network. Active [[p53|p53]] induces expression of miR-34a; the 3′UTR of the [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] transcript contains a miR-34a-responsive element, so elevated miR-34a represses [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] translation (Grabowska et al., *Biogerontology* 2017). Because [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] normally deacetylates and inhibits [[p53|p53]], this forms a negative feedback circuit: p53 activation → miR-34a ↑ → SIRT1 ↓ → less p53 deacetylation/inhibition. The same miR-34a arm can be engaged by [[NF-κB|NF-κB]] to suppress [[SIRT1|SIRT1]], offering a partial explanation for the fall in [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] observed during [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]].

## Physiological and therapeutic relevance

miR-34a is frequently down-regulated in human cancers, and its restoration (e.g., with miR-34a mimics such as MRX34) has been explored as an anticancer strategy, albeit with notable dose-limiting toxicity in early trials. Within ageing biology, its ability to dampen [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] couples the tumor-suppressive p53 program to the sirtuin–NAD+ axis, positioning miR-34a as a regulatory bridge between genomic stress signaling and metabolic/longevity pathways.
