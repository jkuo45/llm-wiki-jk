# Cyclin B

**Cyclin B** is the G2/M-phase cyclin that forms the Cyclin B–CDK1 (formerly MPF) complex driving entry into mitosis. Its accumulation through G2 and abrupt APC/C-mediated destruction at anaphase enforces the G2/M checkpoint and irreversible commitment to division.

## Structure & Regulation

- **CDK1 partnering**: Cyclin B binding activates CDK1; the complex phosphorylates substrates required for nuclear envelope breakdown, chromosome condensation, and spindle assembly.
- **Degradation**: Ubiquitinated by the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C) at metaphase exit.

## Role in Senescence

- Cyclin B is among the proliferative genes whose mRNA is bound and stabilized by the RNA-binding protein [[HuR]]. In senescent/metabolically stressed cells, activated [[AMPK]] prevents HuR's nuclear-to-cytosolic translocation; reduced cytosolic HuR lowers translation of [[c-Fos]], [[Cyclin A]], and Cyclin B mRNA, helping enforce the permanent [[Cell Cycle|cell-cycle]] arrest of [[Senescence|senescence]].

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: Includes Cyclin B among HuR-stabilized proliferative mRNAs whose reduced cytosolic translation (via AMPK-mediated HuR retention) aids senescence-associated arrest.
