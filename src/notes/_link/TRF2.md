# TRF2

**TRF2** (Telomeric Repeat-binding Factor 2, gene *TERF2*) is a core subunit of the shelterin complex that binds double-stranded TTAGGG telomeric repeats. It is the principal protein protecting chromosome ends from being misrecognized as [[DNA Damage|double-strand breaks]], thereby safeguarding genome stability across the lifespan.

## Structure and Binding

- TRF2 binds double-stranded TTAGGG arrays along the telomeric duplex via its C-terminal Myb/SANT DNA-binding domain.
- Together with TRF1, POT1, TIN2, TPP1, and RAP1 it assembles the shelterin complex at telomeres; TIN2 bridges TRF1/TRF2 to the POT1–TPP1 single-strand binding module.

## Function

- **DDR suppression:** TRF2 prevents activation of the [[ATM]] kinase pathway at chromosome ends and blocks non-homologous end joining by inhibiting [[53BP1]] and the Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer — critically short telomeres lose TRF2, exposing ends as DNA double-strand breaks.
- **End-to-end fusion prevention:** Deletion/dysfunction of TRF2 leads to telomere end-to-end fusions and genomic instability.
- **Replicative senescence:** Progressive telomere attrition with cell division (the end-replication problem) causes loss of the protective TRF2-occupied shelterin cap, activating persistent DDR signaling (DNA-SCARS) that triggers [[Replicative Senescence]].

## Role in Aging and Telomere Homeostasis

TRF2 expression is linked to telomere length maintenance and counteracts [[Telomere Attrition|telomere attrition]] and premature aging. Alongside [[Telomerase]] and other shelterin components, TRF2 is a determinant of [[Telomere|telomere]] integrity and cellular replicative capacity.
