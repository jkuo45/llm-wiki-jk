### Overview
Mitochondrial Integrity describes the continuous maintenance of mitochondrial structure, function, and homeostasis. It is achieved through the balanced opposition of [[Mitochondrial Fusion|fusion]] and [[Mitochondrial Fission|fission]], which govern morphology, distribution, and quality control.

### Mechanisms
Balanced dynamics permit content complementation (via fusion) and selective removal of damaged organelles (via fission and [[Mitophagy]]). Proper ATP production, [[Mitochondrial DNA]] stability, and cristae architecture all depend on this balance.

### Disease Relevance
Defects in integrity underlie a range of diseases, including [[Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease Type 2A]] ([[MFN2]] mutations), [[Dominant Optic Atrophy]] ([[OPA1]] mutations), and [[Left Ventricular Hypertrophy]] (altered DRP1/MFN2). Preserving integrity is a therapeutic target, e.g., with [[Mdivi-1]].
