# FAK

## Overview

[[FAK]] (focal adhesion kinase, PTK2) is a non-receptor cytoplasmic tyrosine kinase that functions as a central integrator of signals from the extracellular matrix (ECM) to the cytoskeleton and nucleus. Encoded by the *PTK2* gene, FAK is enriched at focal adhesions, where it integrates signals from integrins and growth factor receptors to regulate cell adhesion, migration, proliferation, and survival. FAK is also recruited by phosphorylated [[Caspase-8]] as part of the non-canonical, pro-migratory signaling complex that replaces the apoptotic function of Caspase-8.

## Structure & Domains

FAK is organized into an N-terminal FERM (band 4.1, ezrin, radixin, moesin) domain that binds integrin β-subunits and growth-factor receptors, a central catalytic kinase domain containing the autophosphorylation site Tyr397, and a C-terminal FAT (focal adhesion targeting) domain that mediates binding to paxillin, talin, and p130Cas. Autophosphorylation of Tyr397 upon integrin clustering creates a high-affinity binding site for Src family kinases, which then phosphorylate additional residues (Tyr576/577 in the activation loop, Tyr861, Tyr925) that amplify pro-migratory and pro-survival signaling.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

FAK is activated downstream of [[Integrins]], receptor tyrosine kinases ([[EGFR]], [[HER2]]), and [[GPCR]]s. Once phosphorylated, FAK engages several axes:

- **PI3K/Akt pathway**: Promotes survival and growth.
- **MAPK/ERK cascade**: Drives proliferation.
- **Rho GTPase network**: Regulates migration and cytoskeletal remodeling.
- **Caspase-8 scaffolding complex**: When [[Caspase-8]] is phosphorylated by [[Src]] at Tyr380, it loses its apoptotic function and instead acts as a scaffold that recruits FAK, Calpain-2, and [[PI3K]]. This complex promotes cell migration and invasion (via FAK → Rac1 → actin remodeling), focal adhesion dynamics, and survival signaling (NF-κB, mTORC1).
- **Cross-talk**: FAK cooperates with c-Met and [[VEGF]] receptors to coordinate [[Angiogenesis]] and [[Metastasis]].

> [!info] FAK in the Non-Canonical Caspase-8 Complex
> The recruitment of FAK by phosphorylated Caspase-8 represents a gain-of-function switch that couples loss of apoptotic sensitivity with enhanced migratory capacity. This mechanism is particularly relevant in aggressive cancers (colon, glioblastoma) where Caspase-8 phosphorylation is prevalent.

## Physiological Function

In normal tissues FAK is essential for embryonic development, tissue repair, and immune cell trafficking. Its kinase activity is tightly regulated by adhesion-dependent localization, preventing inappropriate proliferation. FAK suppresses anoikis (detachment-induced apoptosis) by activating Akt and inhibiting pro-apoptotic signals.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer**: FAK is overexpressed or hyperactivated in numerous malignancies, including [[Breast Cancer]], [[glioblastoma]], [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], and [[ovarian cancer]], where it drives invasion, anoikis resistance, and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance. Elevated FAK correlates with poor prognosis and advanced stage.
- **Therapeutic Targeting**: FAK is a validated therapeutic target: small-molecule inhibitors (defactinib/PF-562271, GSK2256098, VS-6063) and antisense approaches are in clinical evaluation for mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, and other solid tumors, often combined with [[Immunotherapy]] or cytotoxic agents to overcome resistance. FAK inhibition can also restrain [[Tumor Microenvironment]] stiffness and dissemination.
