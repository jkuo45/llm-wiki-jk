# Macrophage Polarization

**Macrophage polarization** is the functional reprogramming of macrophages into distinct activation states — broadly the proinflammatory M1 phenotype and the anti-inflammatory, tissue-repair M2 phenotype. It is a central determinant of the inflammatory milieu in infection, injury, and fibrosis.

## Sirtuin Regulation

- **[[SIRT6]]** regulates macrophage polarization to alleviate sepsis-induced acute respiratory distress syndrome through both autophagy-dependent and autophagy-independent mechanisms.
- **[[SIRT2]]** aggravates asthma through Th2 responses and macrophage polarization.
- **[[SIRT3]]** deficiency produces more severe acute lung injury via NLRP3 inflammasome activation in macrophages, consistent with SIRT3 driving protective macrophage phenotypes.

## Physiological Function

Polarization governs the balance between host defense and tissue repair. Steering macrophages toward protective phenotypes limits collateral tissue damage in acute and chronic inflammation.

## Clinical Relevance

Modulating macrophage polarization via sirtuins (e.g., SIRT6 activation toward anti-inflammatory states) is a candidate strategy in ARDS, sepsis, and asthma.
