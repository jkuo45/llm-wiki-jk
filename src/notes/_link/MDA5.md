# MDA5

**MDA5** (melanoma differentiation-associated protein 5, gene
**IFIH1**, also called RIG-I-like receptor 5) is a cytosolic
**RIG-I-like receptor (RLR)** that detects viral double-stranded RNA. It is one
of the three RLRs in humans — [[RIG-I]] (RIG-I, DDX58) and
**[[LRRC59|LGP2]]** (RIG-I, LGP2) being the others — and it is the sensor
specialised for **long dsRNA**.

## Structure

MDA5 is a large (~102 kDa, 1025 aa) protein with an N-terminal
**two caspase-recruitment domain (CARD) tandem** that mediates
[[IRF3]]/[[IRF7]] and [[NF-κB]] activation, a central
**ATPase/helicase domain** with two RecA-like lobes, and a C-terminal
**dsRNA-binding domain (CTD)** with two dsRNA-binding motifs. Human MDA5 forms
**filaments** along dsRNA — the long double-stranded RNA acts as a
"ladder" that stimulates filament assembly, and polymerised MDA5 filaments
are the *activatable* form of the sensor. ATP hydrolysis and filament
disassembly set the threshold, which is what allows the sensor to respond to
dsRNA abundance rather than to its absolute concentration.

## Ligand discrimination

> [!info] MDA5 versus RIG-I is a division of labour, not a redundancy
> Both sensors are dsRNA receptors, and the division between them is
> reproducible and mechanistically grounded in filament geometry and
> ligand chemistry:
>
> - **MDA5** responds to **long** dsRNA (kilobase) and to
>   **RNA with unprocessed 5' triphosphate**, and is the primary sensor of
>   **picornaviruses** (rhinovirus, enterovirus, poliovirus, hepatitis A,
>   foot-and-mouth disease virus) whose replication generates dsRNA
>   intermediates in the cytoplasm.
> - **RIG-I** responds to **short** dsRNA and especially to
>   **5'-triphosphate mono-phosphorylated** single-stranded RNA — the
>   PAMPs generated by influenza, [[Paramyxovirus]]virus, [[Hepatitis C]]
>   and rhabdoviruses — and is the primary sensor of flaviviruses such as
>   [[Dengue]] and West Nile virus. RIG-I requires the short, blunt-ended,
>   5'-triphosphate ligands it can bind; MDA5 is insensitive to them.
> - **LGP2** has no CARD and cannot signal, but potentiates both.

The distinction is not absolute: co-infection and dual sensing are common, and
several viruses (dengue, [[Hepatitis B Virus]] replicons) are detected by both
with RIG-I dominant in primary cells.

## Signalling

> [!info] The MAVS filament scaffold
> MDA5's CARD recruits **MAVS** ([[Mitochondrial Antiviral Signaling Protein]]),
> which polymerises into **filaments** on mitochondria and on peroxisomes.
> These filaments are the signalling platform on which [[TRAF3]] and
> [[TRAF6]] assemble, activating:
> - [[TBK1]] → [[IRF3]]/[[IRF7]] → [[Interferon Type I|type I interferon]] (IFN-β, IFN-α)
> - canonical [[NF-κB]] → pro-inflammatory cytokines
> - [[IRF7]] amplification
> - and, downstream of IFN, [[Interferon-Stimulated Gene|interferon-stimulated genes]]
>   including [[PKR]], [[OAS1]]/[[2'-5'-Oligoadenylate Synthetase|2'-5'-oligoadenylate synthetases]]
>   (RNase L) and [[Mx1]].

The resulting type I IFN programme is the principal antiviral defence and
induces a broad antiviral state in neighbouring uninfected cells.

## Disease relevance

> [!warning] Gain-of-function IFIH1 mutations cause a Mendelian interferonopathy
> This is the most clinically established link. Heterozygous missense mutations
> in the **dsRNA-binding domain** of MDA5 — notably **R148H**, **H150R**,
> **G167A** and **R171Q** at the amino acids that contact the ligand — cause
> **Singleton-Merten syndrome**, an autosomal dominant disorder with
> *Singleton-Merten syndrome* features: childhood-onset
> **interferonopathy**, aortic calcification and stenosis, osteopenia,
> psoriasis, and recurrent infections. Mechanistically the mutant MDA5
> filaments are hyper-stable and signal in the *absence* of ligand, so
> constitutive low-level type I IFN is produced from birth.
>
> This is a clean and instructive disease mechanism: a gain-of-function
> mutation in a dsRNA sensor that mimics chronic viral infection, with
> downstream consequences for both the vasculature and the bone.

Other reported associations are less securely established: elevated MDA5
expression in [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]] and in cancer; and a
protective effect of *loss-of-function* MDA5 alleles in a subset of
[[Lupus|autoimmune]] disease. Given the autoimmune classification of
Singleton-Merten syndrome, the SLE association is plausible but the human
genetic evidence for a direct causal role is weaker than for the monogenic
syndrome.

> [!warning] Evidence caveats
> - **Cancer**: MDA5 status in oncology is a genuinely open question.
>   High MDA5 expression sometimes correlates with inflamed
>   [[Tumor Microenvironment|microenvironments]] and better
>   [[Immunotherapy|checkpoint blockade]] response, and sometimes with
>   resistance. The confound is that MDA5 tracks interferon signalling,
>   and interferon-high tumours are a heterogeneous group. Do not present
>   MDA5 as a validated predictive biomarker.
> - In mice, MDA5 is required for the [[Toll-like Receptor]]/MDA5
>   contribution to the response to some sterile inflammatory stimuli, and
>   MDA5 has been implicated in metabolic inflammation and in
>   [[Neurodegeneration|neuroinflammatory disease]] through
>   endogenous dsRNA from dying cells. These are active research
>   areas, not settled biology.
> - A subset of human genetic studies report that common MDA5
>   polymorphisms associate with autoimmune disease, with effect sizes
>   that have often failed to replicate. Treat with caution.

## Documents

- [[DRP1]] — records that MAVS is assembled on **mitochondria** and that
  mitochondrial dynamics machinery participates in RLR signalling
  organisation; the connection is the mitochondrial platform on which
  the MDA5–MAVS signal is transduced, and it is why DRP1 appears
  in the interferon literature at all.
- [[PAMP]] — MDA5 recognises a defined PAMP class (long dsRNA) and is
  one of the [[Pattern Recognition Receptors]] converting that PAMP
  into interferon.
- [[RIG-I]] — the sister RLR; the MDA5/RIG-I division of labour is
  the single most important conceptual content in this note.
- [[Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptor]] — the RLR family
  note of which MDA5 is a member, alongside [[RIG-I]] and LGP2.

## Connections

- [[RIG-I]] — The defining relationship. Long dsRNA and 5'-triphosphate
  ssRNA partition onto MDA5 and RIG-I respectively, which determines
  which sensor dominates the antiviral response to a given virus.
  Both converge on [[MAVS]].
- [[Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptor]] — MDA5 is a
  canonical RLR, one of the cytosolic RNA-sensing branch of the
  [[Innate Immune System|innate immune system]] alongside the
  [[Toll-like Receptor|endosomal TLRs]] and [[NOD]] family.
- [[Interferon]] — Type I interferon is the direct transcriptional
  output of the MDA5 pathway and the effector arm of the antiviral
  response.
- [[PAMP]] — MDA5 detects one specific PAMP (long dsRNA) and, as a
  pattern recognition receptor, converts it into interferon; the
  PAMP/PRR framework is the direct context.
- [[MAVS]] — The obligatory adaptor; MDA5 has no signalling output
  without MAVS filament assembly.
- [[Innate Immunity]] — MDA5 is a core cytosolic RNA sensor and its
  deficiency causes a specific, identifiable loss of antiviral defence
  against picornaviruses.
- [[Mitochondria]] — MAVS filaments form on the mitochondrial outer
  membrane, which is why mitochondrial dynamics factors appear in
  interferon research.
- [[DRP1]] — Mitochondrial fission is not simply a bystander: DRP1
  and other dynamics factors have been implicated in RLR signalling
  organisation and in the metabolic state that determines whether
  MAVS signals.
- [[Innate Immune System]] — The broader program whose effector
  module MDA5 is part of.
- [[Antiviral Response]] — Type I IFN induces the antiviral state;
  MDA5 is the initiating sensor upstream of that whole program.
- [[Toll-like Receptor]] — The other major nucleic acid sensing arm;
  TLR3/7/8/9 sense nucleic acids in [[Endosome|endosomes]] and
  cross-talk between RLR and TLR signalling shapes the response.
- [[AIM2]] and [[cGAS-STING]] — cytosolic DNA sensing; the parallel
  arm of the same "cytosolic nucleic acid surveillance" logic,
  which is a useful contrast because it senses the other nucleic
  acid.
- [[Interferon-Stimulated Gene]] — the downstream effectors; MDA5
  function is largely a means of turning them on.
