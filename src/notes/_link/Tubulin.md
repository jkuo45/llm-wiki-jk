# Tubulin

**Overview:** Tubulin refers to the α/β-heterodimeric protein that polymerizes into **microtubules** — hollow, dynamic polymers that form the mitotic spindle, the centrioles, cilia, and the structural backbone of the cytoskeleton. Microtubule dynamics (polymerization/depolymerization) are essential for chromosome segregation in mitosis, intracellular trafficking, and cell shape. Because rapidly dividing cells depend on a functional spindle, tubulin is one of the most validated anticancer drug targets.

## Structure & Polymerization

Tubulin exists as αβ-heterodimers that add head-to-tail into protofilaments and then close into a 13-protofilament microtubule. The polymer is polarized (plus/minus ends) and exhibits **dynamic instability** — stochastic switching between growth and shrinkage driven by GTP hydrolysis at the β-tubulin E-site. Post-translational modifications regulate microtubule identity: acetylation of **α-tubulin at lysine 40 (K40)** marks stable, long-lived microtubules and is a well-established marker of microtubule stability.

## Drug Interactions (Anticancer Repositioning)

- **Benzimidazole anthelmintics ([[Fenbendazole]], [[Mebendazole]]):** Bind the **colchicine site of β-tubulin**, preventing polymerization and destabilizing microtubules. This disrupts the mitotic spindle → G2/M arrest, mitotic catastrophe, and apoptosis. Fenbendazole is a moderate microtubule-destabilizing agent whose anticancer activity was elaborated by Dogra & Mukhopadhyay (Sci Rep 2018); mebendazole's tubulin-depoylmerizing activity in cancer was shown by Sasaki et al. (Mol Cancer Ther 2002) and Mukhopadhyay et al. (Clin Cancer Res 2002). Because colchicine-site agents show minimal P-glycoprotein-mediated multidrug resistance, benzimidazoles are attractive repurposing candidates.
- **Taxanes ([[Paclitaxel]], docetaxel):** Bind polymerized tubulin and *stabilize* microtubules, the opposite functional effect to benzimidazoles, also arresting mitosis.
- **Vinca alkaloids:** Bind the vinca site and inhibit polymerization.

## SIRT2 & the NAD+ Rheostat

[[SIRT2]] is an **NAD+-dependent deacetylase of α-tubulin K40**; siRNA knockdown of SIRT2 causes tubulin hyperacetylation, establishing SIRT2 as a bona fide tubulin deacetylase (North et al., Mol Cell 2003; PMID 12571239). HDAC6 is a parallel, NAD-independent α-tubulin deacetylase. Because SIRT2 activity is gated by the cellular [[NAD+]] pool, redox/[[NAD+]] stress can shift tubulin acetylation status — a direct mechanistic link between the sirtuin/NAD+ axis and the microtubule node that benzimidazoles attack. This places tubulin at the intersection of the ivermectin–fenbendazole anticancer story: fenbendazole destabilizes tubulin polymer, while SIRT2 (a sirtuin hub in the same network) deacetylates the same α-tubulin subunit.

## Pathological Significance

Altered tubulin isotype expression, tubulin acetylation, and mitotic-spindle defects are implicated in cancer, neurodegeneration, and developmental disorders. Tubulin (and its modifying enzymes) is a biomarker and therapeutic target across multiple clinical contexts, including the repurposed antiparasitic [[Fenbendazole]] and [[Mebendazole]] anticancer strategies.
