# Adrenal medulla

**Overview:** The [[Adrenal medulla]] is the inner parenchyma of the [[Adrenal gland]], a neuroendocrine tissue derived from neural crest that functions as a modified sympathetic ganglion. It is the principal site of systemic [[Catecholamines]] production.

**Cellular Composition:** It is composed of [[Chromaffin cells]]—large, rounded neuroendocrine cells that store catecholamines in dense-core vesicles alongside ATP, dopamine β-hydroxylase, and chromogranins. These cells are directly innervated by preganglionic sympathetic (splanchnic) fibers, so release is governed by the [[Sympathetic Nervous System]] rather than conventional endocrine feedback.

**Physiological Function:** On sympathetic activation, chromaffin cells exocytose [[Epinephrine]] (≈80–85% in humans) and [[Norepinephrine]] into the bloodstream, producing the "fight-or-flight" response: tachycardia, bronchodilation, glycogenolysis, and lipolysis. Epinephrine is synthesized from tyrosine → L-DOPA → dopamine → norepinephrine → epinephrine (the final step by phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase).

**Oxidative Chemistry and Adrenochrome:** Released epinephrine is susceptible to auto-oxidation, forming reactive aminochromes such as [[Adrenochrome]], especially in the presence of oxidants, trace metals, or alkaline pH. This links adrenal medullary catecholamine flux to redox stress.

**Pathology:** Pheochromocytoma (medullary tumor) causes catecholamine excess (hypertension, palpitations). Medullary catecholamine handling is also relevant to [[Catecholamine-induced cardiomyopathy]] under sustained stress.
