# SHP1

## Overview
[[SHP1]] (PTPN6, SH2 domain-containing phosphatase 1) is a non-receptor tyrosine phosphatase that plays critical roles in immune signaling and apoptotic regulation. SHP1 dephosphorylates [[Caspase-8]] at Tyr380, removing the inhibitory phosphate placed by [[Src]] family kinases and restoring the cell's sensitivity to death ligands ([[TRAIL]], [[FasL]]).

## Structure & Activation
SHP1 contains two N-terminal SH2 domains that mediate recruitment to phosphotyrosine-containing motifs on receptors and signaling proteins, a catalytic phosphatase domain, and a C-terminal tail. SHP1 is autoinhibited by intramolecular interaction of the N-SH2 domain with the catalytic domain; binding of phosphotyrosine ligands to the SH2 domains relieves this autoinhibition.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Caspase-8 Dephosphorylation**: SHP1 removes inhibitory phosphate groups from Caspase-8 at Tyr380 (and related Tyr397/Tyr465), restoring Caspase-8's ability to undergo proteolytic cleavage and maturation. This re-engages the extrinsic apoptotic pathway.

> [!important] Epigenetic Silencing of SHP1 in Cancer
> Many tumors epigenetically silence _PTPN6_ (SHP1) via promoter hypermethylation. This maintains Caspase-8 in its pro-migratory, anti-apoptotic phosphorylated state, enabling tumor cells to resist death ligand-induced apoptosis while promoting metastasis through the scaffolding functions of phosphorylated Caspase-8.

- **Immune Regulation**: SHP1 is a negative regulator of signaling through immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs) in T cells, B cells, and NK cells. It limits cytokine signaling and prevents autoimmunity.
- **Other Substrates**: SHP1 dephosphorylates JAK2, STAT3, ERK, and other signaling proteins, modulating inflammatory and growth signaling.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer**: SHP1 is frequently silenced in cancers (colon, glioblastoma, leukemia, breast cancer) through promoter methylation. Loss of SHP1 correlates with poor prognosis and resistance to TRAIL-based therapies.
- **Autoimmunity**: Loss-of-function mutations in SHP1 cause autoimmune-like inflammation in mice (motheaten mice) and potentially in humans.
- **Therapeutic Potential**: Demethylating agents that restore SHP1 expression or small molecules that activate SHP1 could sensitize tumors to death ligand therapy.

- **RIPK1 dephosphorylation.** Tyrosine phosphatase-6 (SHP1/[[PTPN6]]) dephosphorylates tyrosine residues on the [[RIPK1]] protein, gating necroptosis in tumors and inflammatory disease.
