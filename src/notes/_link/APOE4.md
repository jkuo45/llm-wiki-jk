# APOE4

## Overview

[[APOE4]] is the epsilon 4 isoform of apolipoprotein E, the strongest common genetic risk factor for late-onset [[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]]. Produced predominantly by [[Astrocytes]] and acting as a ligand for the [[TREM2]] receptor via ApoE-containing [[Lipid Metabolism]], APOE4 shapes microglial and astrocytic responses to pathology. In the cGAS-STING framework, [[APOE4]] acts as a sensitizing allele that primes microglia and amplifies [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]]–driven neuroinflammation.

## Synergy with TREM2 and Microglial Priming

APOE4 microglia exhibit a primed, inflammatory phenotype. The allele synergizes with the TREM2 R47H risk variant to markedly amplify [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] activation and IFN-I signaling, driving a senescence-like microglial state marked by mitochondrial stress, [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle]] arrest, and a [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]] ([40](#B40)). This cooperative genetic interaction helps explain the steep elevation in AD risk and the heightened vulnerability to [[Proteinopathy|proteinopathies]] observed in carriers.

## Contrast with APOE3 Christchurch

Unlike the deleterious APOE4 allele, the rare [[APOE3 Christchurch]] (R136S) variant confers resilience to AD and dementia despite high [[Amyloid Beta|amyloid]] pathology, even in the presence of autosomal-dominant AD mutations. Pharmacological [[cGAS]] inhibition phenocopies the protective effect of APOE3 Christchurch, suggesting that the resilience axis operates upstream of, or in parallel to, cGAS-STING suppression ([41](#B41)). This dichotomy makes the APOE isoform landscape a powerful genetic lens for dissecting cGAS-STING contributions to [[Neurodegeneration]].
