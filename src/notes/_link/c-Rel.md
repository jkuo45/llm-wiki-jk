# c-Rel

**c-Rel** is a member of the [[NF-κB]] transcription factor family, encoded by the *REL* proto-oncogene, that dimerizes with RelA (p65) and NF-κB1 (p50) to regulate inflammatory, survival and differentiation programmes. It shares the N-terminal Rel homology domain with the rest of the family, but its C-terminal transactivation domain makes it an unusually potent transcriptional activator.

## Overview

c-Rel is one of five transcription factors of the [[NF-κB]] superfamily ([[RELA|RelA/p65]], NF-κB1/p50, NF-κB2/p52, RelB and c-Rel), each of which shares an N-terminal Rel homology domain and, except for the p50/p52 precursors, a C-terminal transactivation domain. c-Rel was originally identified as a cellular homolog of the avian v-rel oncogene, and it occupies a distinctive middle position in the family: less ubiquitous than RelA, but with the strongest intrinsic transactivating capacity, and it functions both as an inflammatory driver and as a transcriptional repressor in tolerance settings.

## Structure & Domains

- **Rel homology domain (RHD):** N-terminal ~300 aa, containing the DNA-binding motif and the dimerization surface. RHDs are interchangeable, so c-Rel can be forced into any dimer by engineered fusion or by partner availability.
- **Nuclear localization sequence:** a basic stretch at the RHD C-terminus, regulated by phosphorylation and by redox state.
- **Transactivation domain:** C-terminal glutamine-rich region absent from p50 and p52; it recruits co-activators (p300/CBP) and drives strong transcriptional output. c-Rel is the only family member with an intrinsic transactivation domain other than RelA and RelB.
- **Ankyrin repeat:** a small internal ankyrin repeat region binds and masks the NLS, preventing constitutive nuclear localization.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **IκB-dependent (canonical) activation:** In resting cells, c-Rel dimers are sequestered in the cytoplasm by [[IκBα]]. Stimuli such as [[TNF-alpha|TNF]], IL-1, or [[Toll-like Receptor]] ligation activate the IKK complex (IKK1/IKK2/NEMO), which phosphorylates IκBα for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation via the [[Proteasome]] and [[Ubiquitination]] machinery. Freed c-Rel/p50 and c-Rel/RelA dimers translocate to the nucleus.
- **Non-canonical activation:** [[NOD-like Receptors]] can engage the alternative IKKα-dependent pathway, which processes p100 to p52 and allows p52/RelB (and p52/c-Rel) dimers to accumulate.
- **Redox control:** c-Rel is uniquely sensitive to the intracellular thiol redox state; reducing conditions (high [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]-driven oxidation of Keap1) potentiate its DNA binding, coupling NF-κB output to oxidative metabolism.
- **Targets:** cytokines (IL-2, IL-6, TNF, GM-CSF), chemokines, anti-apoptotic genes ([[Bcl-2]], Bcl-xL, [[Survivin]]), and master regulators such as [[MYC]].

## Physiological Function

c-Rel is required for a distinctive, non-redundant subset of NF-κB outputs:

- **B- and T-cell activation and proliferation:** c-Rel-deficient lymphocytes fail to respond to mitogens and to sustain IL-2 production; c-Rel is dispensable for initial T-cell development but essential for expansion.
- **Immunological tolerance:** c-Rel, together with RelB, is induced by regulatory T cells and B cells and acts as a transcriptional repressor of inflammatory genes, restraining autoimmunity.
- **Antigen presentation and dendritic cell maturation:** c-Rel-deficient dendritic cells fail to upregulate MHC class II and co-stimulatory molecules.
- **Survival and proliferation:** c-Rel supports cell-cycle entry and anti-apoptotic programmes that cooperate with [[Apoptosis]] evasion.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Lymphoma:** the *REL* locus at 11p13 is amplified in a substantial fraction of [[Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma]] and Hodgkin lymphoma, and *REL* rearrangements (e.g. *REL*–*BACH2* in t(4;11)-like acute lymphoblastic leukaemia) generate constitutively nuclear, dimerization-competent c-Rel. On its own, *REL* amplification is an initiating event but is insufficient for lymphoma; cooperating lesions (e.g. *NOTCH1*, *MYD88*, BCL2 translocation) are required — consistent with the "two-hit" model of c-Rel oncogenesis.
- **Autoimmunity and inflammation:** excessive c-Rel activity contributes to [[Inflammation]] in [[Adaptive Immune System]] contexts and to chronic inflammatory disease; c-Rel–deficient mice are resistant to several autoimmune models, making c-Rel a therapeutic target.
- **Targeting:** peptide inhibitors disrupting c-Rel dimers, and IKKβ inhibitors, are in preclinical and early clinical development.
