NBS1 (Nijmegen breakage syndrome 1, also called nibrin or p95) is a scaffold protein and core subunit of the **MRE11–RAD50–NBS1 (MRN) complex**, the principal sensor of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs). Through its C-terminal motifs it recruits and activates the ataxia-telangiectasia mutated (ATM) kinase, orchestrating DSB recognition, checkpoint activation (including the S-phase checkpoint), and initiation of homologous recombination repair. Germline loss-of-function causes Nijmegen breakage syndrome, characterized by genome instability, radiosensitivity, immunodeficiency, and cancer predisposition.

## Regulation by SIRT1

[[SIRT1|SIRT1]] interacts directly with NBS1 and maintains it in a hypoacetylated state, which permits phosphorylation of NBS1 on Ser343—an event required for an efficient [[DNA Repair|DNA damage repair]] response and activation of the S-phase checkpoint (Yuan et al.). By keeping NBS1 deacetylated, [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] sustains MRN complex function at break sites, coupling sirtuin-dependent chromatin control to high-fidelity DSB repair and genome stability. This places NBS1 among the direct DNA-repair substrates through which [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] opposes [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]] and genomic instability.
