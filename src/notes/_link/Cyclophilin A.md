# Cyclophilin A

**Cyclophilin A** (CypA, encoded by *PPIA*) is a ubiquitously expressed peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase (PPIase) and the founding cyclophilin. It is the intracellular target of [[Cyclosporine A]] and a secreted pro-inflammatory factor, and it has been proposed as an alternative nuclease-complex partner for [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] in [[Parthanatos]].

## Overview

- PPIase activity catalyses cis-trans isomerization of prolyl peptide bonds, assisting protein folding; it is inhibited by cyclosporine A (which also engages the calcineurin pathway).
- Secreted CypA acts extracellularly through receptors such as CD147 (basigin) to drive chemotaxis and [[Inflammation]], and is elevated in inflammatory and cardiovascular disease.
- Involved in several cell-death settings, including caspase-independent death and viral restriction.

## Role in parthanatos

Two models have been proposed for the nuclease that executes large-scale DNA fragmentation after [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] nuclear translocation:

- [[MIF]]/PAAN, recruited by cytoplasmic AIF — the leading, pharmacologically validated model (inhibited by [[PAANIB-1]]).
- An AIF–Cyclophilin A–histone [[H2A.X]] complex in which AIF itself provides the nuclease activity, proposed to degrade chromatin during caspase-independent death.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Parthanatos Moura 2024 molecular mechanisms more questions than answers|Moura et al. 2024]]
> The review presents the AIF–cyclophilin A–H2AX complex as the second, less pharmacologically developed model of AIF-induced DNA cleavage, alongside the MIF/PAAN nuclease.
