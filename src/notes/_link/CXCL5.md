# CXCL5

CXCL5 (C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 5), also known as ENA-78 (epithelial-derived neutrophil-activating peptide 78), is an ELR+ C-X-C chemokine that binds [[CXCR2]] (and weakly CXCR1). It is a neutrophil chemoattractant and a SASP component that is notably secreted by blastocysts derived from aged oocytes, implicating it in reproductive aging (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptor binding**: CXCL5 engages [[CXCR2]] (high affinity) and CXCR1 (low affinity), activating Gαi-coupled PLCβ/IP3/Ca2+, PI3K/Akt, and [[p38 MAPK]]/ERK cascades in target cells.
- **Cell sources**: Produced by epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and endothelial cells under inflammatory, hypoxic, or genotoxic stress; in the reproductive context it is secreted by the pre-implantation blastocyst.
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Driven by [[NF-κB]] and [[AP-1]], with [[p38 MAPK]] feed-forward; expression overlaps the core SASP network.
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Recruits and activates [[Neutrophils]], promoting chemotaxis, elastase release, and ROS generation; in embryos it exerts paracrine toxicity on neighboring pre-implantation cells.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CXCL5 is a member of the ELR+ CXC SASP chemokine family and is co-induced with [[CXCL1]], [[CXCL2]], and [[CXCL3]] in senescent cells.
- In reproductive aging, blastocysts arising from aged oocytes secrete CXCL5, which is detrimental to pre-implantation embryo development and contributes to the age-related decline in embryo quality used for IVF in older women.
- More broadly, chronic CXCL5/CXCR2 signaling in aged or diseased tissue promotes [[Inflammaging]] and tissue dysfunction through sustained neutrophil recruitment.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CXCL5 is a potential biomarker for embryo quality in IVF and for senescent-cell burden in inflammatory disease.
- Elevated CXCL5 is found in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, COPD, and multiple cancers, where CXCR2 signaling supports neutrophil infiltration and tumor progression.
- CXCR2 antagonism is being explored to improve fertility outcomes in older women and to blunt SASP-driven inflammation.
