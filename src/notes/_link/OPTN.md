# OPTN

Optineurin (OPTN) is a primary [[Autophagy|autophagy]] receptor in [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]]-mediated [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] and a key regulator of anterograde axonal [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] transport. It is recruited to ubiquitin-coated [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] and, through its [[LIR Motif|LC3-interacting region (LIR) motif]], binds to [[LC3]] proteins on the phagophore.

## Mechanism in Mitophagy

After [[Parkin]] ubiquitinates outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM) proteins, OPTN is recruited to the ubiquitin-coated mitochondrial surface. Its binding affinity for ubiquitin chains is significantly enhanced by phosphorylation mediated by the kinase [[TBK1]]. OPTN functions together with [[NDP52]] as one of the two primary autophagy receptors in the [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] pathway.

## Role in Mitochondrial Transport

Beyond its role in [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], OPTN directly interacts with the microtubule and mitochondrial transport complex [[TRAK1]]/[[KIF5B]] in a C-terminal-dependent manner to stabilize them for proper anterograde axonal mitochondrial transport. Overexpression of OPTN/[[TRAK1]]/[[KIF5B]] prevents neurodegeneration induced by OPTN truncation and protects against damage from high intraocular pressure while promoting optic nerve regeneration.

## Role in Neurodegeneration

OPTN mutations have been identified as pathogenic in [[ALS|amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)]]. Since mitophagy critically depends on OPTN and its kinase [[TBK1]], loss of OPTN function impairs both mitochondrial clearance and axonal transport. In [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]], OPTN expression is significantly reduced in the [[Hippocampus|hippocampus]] and [[Prefrontal Cortex|prefrontal cortex]], mediated by [[GSK3|GSK-3β]] phosphorylation at the Ser473 site promoting its [[Ubiquitin|ubiquitin]]-mediated degradation.
