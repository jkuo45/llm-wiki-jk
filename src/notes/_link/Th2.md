# Th2

**Type 2 helper T cells (Th2)** are a specialized subset of [[CD4+ T Cells]] that orchestrate humoral immunity, anti-helminth defense, and allergic inflammatory responses. Th2 cells are defined by their signature production of **[[IL-4]]**, **IL-5**, and **[[IL-13]]**, which collectively drive IgE production, eosinophil recruitment, and mucus hypersecretion.

## Differentiation & Regulation

### Polarizing Signals
Th2 differentiation is driven by:
- **[[IL-4]]** — The primary polarizing cytokine. IL-4 activates **[[STAT6]]**, which induces **GATA3** expression. Early IL-4 sources include basophils, mast cells, and NKT cells.
- **IL-2** — Promotes T cell proliferation and survival during differentiation.
- **TCR signal strength** — Moderate TCR signaling favors Th2 commitment.

### Master Transcription Factor: GATA3
**GATA3** is the master regulator of Th2 identity. GATA3:
- Directly activates transcription of the *IL4*, *IL5*, and *IL13* gene cluster on chromosome 5q31.
- Induces and maintains its own expression through an auto-regulatory loop.
- Reciprocally suppresses [[Th1]] differentiation by inhibiting [[T-bet]] and *IL12RB2* expression.
- Remodels chromatin at the Th2 cytokine locus to establish stable lineage commitment.

### Cross-Regulation
Th2 differentiation is mutually antagonistic with [[Th1]] and Th17 pathways:
- GATA3 inhibits T-bet, suppressing [[Th1]] development.
- GATA3 antagonizes RORγt, limiting Th17 differentiation.
- [[IL-10]] from regulatory T cells can dampen excessive Th2 responses.

## Effector Mechanisms

### IL-4 Production
[[IL-4]] drives:
- **IgE class switching** in B cells — the hallmark of allergic immune responses.
- **Th2 self-amplification** through autocrine STAT6/GATA3 activation.
- **M2 macrophage polarization** — alternative activation associated with tissue repair and fibrosis.
- **Eotaxin production** — recruiting eosinophils to sites of inflammation.

### IL-5 Production
IL-5 is the primary regulator of eosinophil biology:
- **Eosinophil differentiation** and maturation in bone marrow.
- **Eosinophil activation** and survival at tissue sites.
- **Eosinophil recruitment** to mucosal surfaces.

### IL-13 Production
[[IL-13]] drives:
- **Goblet cell hyperplasia** and mucus hypersecretion in airways.
- **Airway hyperresponsiveness** through smooth muscle contractility.
- **Tissue fibrosis** via TGF-β and direct pro-fibrotic signaling.
- **M2 macrophage polarization** — synergizes with IL-4.

### IL-9 Production
IL-9 promotes mast cell proliferation and survival, amplifying type 2 inflammatory responses.

## Biological Functions

### Anti-Helminth Immunity
Th2 responses are the primary defense against parasitic helminth infections. The coordinated action of IL-4 (IgE production), IL-5 (eosinophilia), and IL-13 (mucus, smooth muscle contraction) creates a "weep and sweep" response that expels worms from mucosal surfaces.

### Allergic Immunity
The same effector mechanisms that defend against helminths drive pathological allergic responses when directed against innocuous environmental antigens (allergens):
- IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation → immediate hypersensitivity.
- Eosinophilic inflammation → chronic allergic tissue damage.
- Mucus hypersecretion and airway remodeling → asthma.

### Tissue Repair
Th2 cytokines promote wound healing and tissue repair through M2 macrophage activation, fibroblast proliferation, and collagen synthesis. This reparative function can become pathological when chronically activated, leading to fibrosis.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **Asthma**: Th2 cells are the central drivers of eosinophilic (T2-high) asthma. IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13 are therapeutic targets: **dupilumab** (anti-IL-4Rα), **mepolizumab** (anti-IL-5), **benralizumab** (anti-IL-5Rα), and **tezepelumab** (anti-TSLP).
- **Atopic Dermatitis**: Th2-polarized inflammation dominates skin lesions. Dupilumab is first-line biologic therapy.
- **Allergic Rhinitis**: Th2 cells drive nasal eosinophilia and IgE production.
- **Eosinophilic Esophagitis**: IL-13-driven epithelial remodeling with eosinophilic infiltration.
- **Helminth Infections**: Protective Th2 responses are essential for worm expulsion.
- **Fibrotic Disease**: Chronic Th2 activation contributes to pulmonary fibrosis, liver fibrosis, and systemic sclerosis.
- **Cancer**: Th2-dominant tumor microenvironments may suppress anti-tumor immunity by inhibiting [[Th1]] responses.
