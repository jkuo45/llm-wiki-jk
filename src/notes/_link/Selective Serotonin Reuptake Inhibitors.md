# Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors

**Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs)** are a major class of antidepressant that block the [[Serotonin]] transporter (SERT), increasing synaptic serotonin availability. Common members include fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram, and fluvoxamine.

## Pharmacodynamics
SSRIs bind the presynaptic serotonin transporter, preventing reuptake and prolonging serotonergic neurotransmission at [[Serotonin Receptor]]s. Therapeutic antidepressant effects emerge over weeks and are attributed to downstream neuroplastic and receptor-sensitivity changes rather than the acute increase in synaptic serotonin. SSRIs are also used for anxiety disorders, obsessive–compulsive disorder, and some neuropathic pain states.

## Adverse Interactions
Because SSRIs raise synaptic serotonin, combining them with a [[Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor]] can cause [[Serotonin Syndrome]] — a potentially life-threatening syndrome of hyperthermia, clonus, autonomic instability, and altered mental status. A washout period is required when switching between classes. Notably, [[Methylene blue]] possesses MAO-A–inhibitory activity at the doses used clinically, so concurrent SSRI use is contraindicated and can precipitate serotonin syndrome.

> [!warning] Drug Interaction
> Methylene blue's MAO-A inhibition makes co-administration with SSRIs (and SNRIs) dangerous; avoid combination or observe strict washout.
