# Anaplerosis

**Anaplerosis** (from Greek "to fill up") refers to biochemical reactions that replenish [[TCA cycle]] intermediates that are consumed or extracted for biosynthetic purposes. In rapidly proliferating cells, many TCA cycle intermediates are diverted to synthesize amino acids, fatty acids, sterols, and nucleotides, making anaplerotic input essential for continued cycle function.

## Primary Anaplerotic Routes

- **Glutamine anaplerosis**: The dominant route in cancer cells. [[Glutamine]] is converted to [[Glutamate]] by [[Glutaminase]], then to [[α-Ketoglutarate]] (α-KG) via transamination or glutamate dehydrogenase. α-KG enters the [[TCA cycle]] to replenish intermediates.
- **Pyruvate carboxylation**: Pyruvate is converted to oxaloacetate by pyruvate carboxylase. Important in gluconeogenic tissues and some cancers.
- **Aspartate and malate**: Can feed into the cycle at various entry points.

## Role in Cancer

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]]
> In [[MYC]]^high^ cancer cells, glutamine anaplerosis becomes the primary carbon source for the [[TCA cycle]]. [[MYC]] enhances [[Glutaminolysis]], promoting influx of glutamine-derived [[α-Ketoglutarate]] into the TCA cycle. This is essential because TCA intermediates are continuously extracted for biosynthesis in rapidly proliferating cells. The sustained TCA cycle generates reducing equivalents (NADH, FADH2) that drive the [[Electron Transport Chain|respiratory chain]] and biosynthetic precursors for lipids and nucleotides.

## Coupling to Complex I

Anaplerosis via glutamine is coupled to [[Complex I]] function: the TCA cycle dehydrogenase enzymes require [[NAD+]] as a hydride acceptor, and NAD+ is regenerated by Complex I. Therefore, Complex I inhibition collapses the glutamine-fueled TCA cycle by depleting NAD+, even though glutamine-to-α-KG conversion itself is not blocked.
