# DNA Polymerase

**DNA polymerases** synthesize DNA in the 5′→3′ direction from a template strand. Replicative polymerases (Pol δ, Pol ε in eukaryotes) duplicate the genome in [[S Phase]], while specialized polymerases handle repair (Pol β), mitochondrial replication (Pol γ), and translesion synthesis across damaged bases.

## Therapeutic Targeting

Polymerases are inhibited by nucleoside analogs that incorporate and terminate chain elongation ([[Clofarabine]], cytarabine, gemcitabine) and stalled indirectly by dNTP depletion ([[Hydroxyurea]] via [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]]). Thymidine analogs such as [[BrdU]] compete for incorporation and mark proliferating cells.

## Documents

- [[BrdU]] — competes with endogenous thymidine for incorporation by DNA polymerase during replicative synthesis; basis of proliferation labeling.
- [[Clofarabine]] — triple threat on DNA metabolism: inhibits ribonucleotide reductase, poisons DNA polymerases, and incorporates to terminate chains.
- [[Hydroxyurea]] — depletes dNTP pools via ribonucleotide reductase inhibition, stalling DNA polymerase at replication forks.
