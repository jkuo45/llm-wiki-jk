# Bone Marrow

**Bone Marrow** is a semi-solid tissue found within the spongy (trabecular) portions of bones. It is the primary site of [[Hematopoiesis]] — the production of all blood cell lineages — in adult mammals.

## Types

- **Red Marrow** — hematopoietic tissue containing [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cells]], [[Progenitor Cells]], and developing blood cells. Found in the axial skeleton ([[Sternum]], [[Pelvis]], [[Vertebrae]]) in adults.
- **Yellow Marrow** — dominated by [[Adipose Tissue]] (adipocytes); can convert to red marrow under high hematopoietic demand.

## Function

Bone marrow produces approximately 500 billion blood cells per day, including [[Erythrocytes]], [[Platelets]], [[Granulocytes]], [[Monocytes]], and [[Lymphocytes]]. The [[Bone Marrow Microenvironment]] (niche) comprising [[Stromal Cells]], [[Osteoblasts]], and [[Endothelial Cells]] regulates [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell]] self-renewal, quiescence, and differentiation.

## Clinical Relevance

Bone marrow is the target of [[Bone Marrow Transplantation]] (hematopoietic stem cell transplantation) for [[leukemia]], [[Lymphoma]], [[Multiple Myeloma]], and [[Aplastic Anemia]]. Bone marrow suppression is a common dose-limiting toxicity of [[Chemotherapy]] and [[Radiation Therapy]].
