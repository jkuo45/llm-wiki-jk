# γH2AX

[[γH2AX]] is the phosphorylated (Ser139) form of histone H2AX, formed rapidly at sites of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) by [[ATM]], [[ATR]], and [[DNA-PK]]. It serves as a sensitive biomarker of genomic instability and chemotherapy-induced DNA damage.

## Role as a Therapeutic Biomarker

Elevated γH2AX foci indicate successful induction of DNA damage in tumor cells and are commonly used to quantify the cytotoxicity of [[Microtubule|microtubule-targeting agents]] and genotoxic drugs.

> [!info] Source: [[Oral Fenbendazole for Cancer Therapy in Humans and Animals]]
> In A375 skin cancer cells, fenbendazole (1, 2, 4 μ M, 24-48 h) increased γH2AX levels, indicating DNA damage, alongside antiproliferative activity, microtubule disruption, G2/M arrest, and p53 activation via MDM2/MdmX down-regulation (35).
