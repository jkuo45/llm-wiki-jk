# Leptin

**Biochemical properties:** Leptin is an endogenous metabolite involved in cellular energy metabolism, redox reactions, or biosynthetic pathways. Its cellular concentration reflects metabolic flux and energy status.

**Metabolic role:** Leptin participates as a substrate, intermediate, or product in key metabolic pathways including glycolysis, the TCA cycle, oxidative phosphorylation, or one-carbon metabolism.

**Regulation:** Intracellular levels of Leptin are maintained through biosynthesis, catabolism, and transport mechanisms. Its concentration serves as a metabolic signal that regulates enzyme activity and gene expression.

**Clinical significance:** Altered levels of Leptin are observed in metabolic disorders, mitochondrial diseases, and nutritional deficiencies. Supplementation or pharmacological modulation of this metabolite has therapeutic potential.

## Energy Balance Role

Leptin is the principal adipocyte-derived signal communicating energy stores to the hypothalamus. Circulating leptin rises with fat mass and falls during energy deficit, reducing appetite and stimulating [[Energy Expenditure|energy expenditure]]. In [[Obesity]], elevated leptin is accompanied by central leptin resistance, blunting its catabolic actions.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review notes that residual variation in [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]] (that not explained by [[Fat-Free Mass|fat-free mass]]) is heritable and correlates with physiological and genetic factors, including circulating [[Leptin|leptin]] levels (Toth _et al_. 1999).
