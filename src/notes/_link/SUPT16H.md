# SUPT16H

**SUPT16H** (suppressor of Ty 16 homolog; gene symbol for the protein **SPT16**, also called **hSPT16/FACT-P140/CDC68**) encodes the **140 kDa large subunit of the [[FACT complex]]**, the histone chaperone that enables transcription of chromatin templates. It was found on human chromosome 14q11.2 within the region deleted in Williams–Beuren syndrome, though that deletion's phenotype is attributable to the many other genes in the interval rather than to SUPT16H.

> [!info] Mechanism
> FACT is a heterodimer of SPT16 and [[SSRP1]]. SPT16 provides the second dimer contact, complementing the HMG box of SSRP1, and together the two subunits transiently **remove the H2A–H2B dimer** from the entry face of a nucleosome, let the transcription or replication machinery pass, and **reassemble it on the exit face**. SPT16 alone has no chromatin-remodeling activity — the FACT chaperone function is a property of the heterodimer, which is why the two subunits are always discussed together.

## Biological roles

FACT was identified by Orphanides, LeRoy and Reinberg in 1998 as a factor that permits transcript elongation through nucleosomes, and the human composition as SPT16 plus SSRP1 was resolved the following year. Minimal transcription systems containing only the general transcription factors and RNA polymerase II can transcribe naked DNA but **cannot** transcribe chromatinized templates, which is the experimental fact that establishes FACT as a general accessibility factor. Additional characterized roles:

- **Elongation.** FACT relieves DSIF/NELF-mediated pausing of RNA polymerase II and works cooperatively with H2B monoubiquitination to set elongation rates.
- **Replication.** FACT cooperates with the MCM helicase to facilitate initiation of chromatin DNA replication.
- **DNA damage response.** SPT16 assembles with SSRP1 and casein kinase 2 into a **CK2–hSpt16–SSRP1 complex** that phosphorylates [[p53]] at **Ser392**, and this assembly is induced by UV.
- **Splicing and transcript processing.** SPT16-containing FACT complexes have been reported in alternative splicing regulation alongside SRrp86.
- **Transcription initiation.** SPT16 interacts functionally with the general transcription factor TFIIE.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> SPT16 is a core, essential chromatin factor, so loss-of-function is not a recognized human Mendelian disease. Human SUPT16H variation has been studied mainly as a cancer and developmental-deficit candidate; the Williams–Beuren locus association should not be read as SPT16 haploinsufficiency disease.

## Connections

- [[SSRP1]] — SPT16's obligate heterodimer partner. SSRP1's HMG box and SPT16's N-terminal domain form the two halves of the H2A–H2B chaperone, and FACT is not functional without both.
- [[FACT complex]] — SUPT16H encodes one of exactly two FACT subunits. The elongation-through-nucleosomes function and the TFEB/TFE3 regulatory link are properties of the complex, not of SPT16 alone.
- [[Nucleosome]] — the substrate. SPT16's role is to make the nucleosome physically traversable by disassembling and reassembling its H2A–H2B dimers around a moving polymerase.
- [[p53]] — SPT16 participates in the CK2–hSpt16–SSRP1 kinase complex that phosphorylates p53 at Ser392 in response to UV damage, connecting FACT to the damage response.
- [[Chromatin Remodeling]] — FACT occupies a distinct category from ATP-dependent remodelers such as SRCAP or INO80: it is a histone chaperone that transiently disassembles and reassembles dimers rather than sliding or ejecting whole nucleosomes.
- [[Autophagy]] — FACT inhibition increases autophagy by altering the epigenetic landscape at autophagy gene promoters; this is recorded in the vault's FACT note and is the entry point for connecting SPT16 to autophagic gene expression.
- [[Cisplatin]] — FACT and its SSRP1 partner engage cisplatin- and UV-modified DNA, and this engagement is proposed to be part of cisplatin's cytotoxic mechanism; SPT16 is required for the complex to be present.
