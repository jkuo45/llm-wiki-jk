# Nematode

**Nematodes** (roundworms, phylum Nematoda within Ecdysozoa) are unsegmented, bilaterally symmetric, pseudocoelomate animals with a cylindrical body, a complete digestive tract, and a tough collagenous cuticle that must be molted to allow growth. They are among the most successful animals on earth: nearly every soil, sediment, and host is inhabited by them, and the total biomass of nematodes in soil rivals that of all other soil animals combined. The phylum splits broadly into free-living lineages (clades III–V) and the parasitic **roundworms** (clade IV, which includes the vertebrate-parasitizing *Chromadorea*).

## Body plan and biology

> [!info] Structure
> Nematodes are pseudocoelomates: the body cavity is a fluid-filled **pseudocoel** not fully lined by mesoderm, which serves as a hydrostatic skeleton. Circular muscles pushing against this fluid pressure against the inextensible cuticle is the design that produces the characteristic sinusoidal thrashing locomotion — efficient in soil and in degrading tissue, inefficient in open water. The body is therefore a simple, repeated tube: mouth, pharynx, intestine, anus (or cloaca), with longitudinal cords, gonads, and excretory canals, and no circulatory or respiratory system. Oxygen is obtained by diffusion.

Feeding depends almost entirely on the **buccal cavity** — a cuticle-lined, often muscular pharynx whose internal cuticular ornamentation is species-specific and taxonomically diagnostic. This is why so much of nematode taxonomy and identification rests on buccal morphology, and it is why *Strongyloides*, hookworm, *Ascaris*, and trichurids can be told apart by mouthparts alone.

The cuticle is a collagen-rich **extracellular matrix** produced by the hypodermis and rebuilt four times across the life cycle (egg → L1 → L2 → L3/adult molts). Because molting requires the old cuticle to be shed, nematodes are transiently vulnerable targets, and the cuticle is also a major drug and vaccine target. The process is hormonally orchestrated, using ecdysteroids and the "dauer" diapause decision in free-living species.

## [[Caenorhabditis elegans]] as a model

*C. elegans* (the type species) made nematodes foundational to cell and developmental biology. Around 1 mm long, transparent, with a ~3-day generation time, ~959 somatic cells in the adult hermaphrodite (a near-complete lineage known since Sulston, Horvitz, and Brenner), a fully sequenced genome, and a facile RNAi pathway (originally discovered in worms, which is why the [[RNA Interference|RNAi]] field began where it did). It is a model for [[Aging|aging]] research, since its lifespan is short, its developmental programme fully described, and mutants of conserved genes often have human disease counterparts.

Its limits as a model for parasitic nematodes are real and worth stating: *C. elegans* is a free-living soil/microbivorous species, its genome is unusually compact and lacks many gene families present in parasites, and long-range neuronal organisation differs. It is excellent for conserved mechanism and a poor stand-in for stage-specific parasite biology.

## The parasitic nematodes

Human-parasitic species are organised largely by their transmission route, which is also the reason they need an arthropod intermediate host:

**Soil-transmitted gastrointestinal nematodes** — enter the host by ingestion of embryonated eggs (*Ascaris lumbricoides*, *Trichuris trichiura* — both adult worms in the gut) or by skin penetration of filariform larvae (hookworms *Necator*/*Ancylostoma*, *Strongyloides stercoralis*). These need no vector and are treated by mass administration of [[Albendazole]] or [[Mebendazole]].

**Filarial nematodes** — long-lived, vector-transmitted, and obligate endosymbiont-dependent. The adults live in the lymphatic system (*Wuchereria bancrofti*, *Brugia malayi*, *Brugia timori*) producing microfilariae, or in subcutaneous nodules (*Onchocerca volvulus*). The females are viviparous, releasing microfilariae into the bloodstream or skin. Because the adults live for **a decade or more** in the host, curative therapy is impossible and control is transmissibility-based: kill or sterilise the microfilariae, the adult worms survive but the chain to the next host is broken. This is the entire logic of [[Ivermectin]]-based MDA in filariasis and onchocerciasis.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Two features make *O. volvulus* uniquely dangerous. First, adult worms sit in nodules and are essentially untreatable — the drug reaches the microfilariae, not the adults. Second, rapid killing of a heavy microfilarial load (as with ivermectin, or acutely after [[Suramin]]) provokes an exaggerated [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] response in the eye — **ocular onchocerciasis**, or "river blindness" — driven by dying microfilariae releasing antigen into ocular tissue. Moxidectin and ivermectin–albendazole combination MDA are both strategies designed around this constraint.

## Why the arthropod host matters

Filarial development requires molts inside the vector, and the filarial worms are obligate endosymbionts of [[Wolbachia]]. So the transmission chain has three obligatory links — human, vector, and bacterium — and each is a drug target in turn. The other parasitic nematode with an arthropod requirement is *Thelazia* (a filarial of the eye, no Wolbachia dependence reported, transmitted by *Musca* and *Sicus*).

## Connections

- [[Caenorhabditis elegans]] — The type species and the foundational model organism; established nematodes as tractable experimental systems and made the phylum a reference for the phylum.
- [[Ascaris]] — The giant gut nematode of humans and livestock, ingested as embryonated eggs; the model for soil-transmitted helminths.
- [[Trichuris]] — Whipworm, whose anterior thread-like end embeds in colonic mucosa, making it one of the few worms that does not simply occupy the lumen.
- [[Trichuriasis]] — The disease caused by *Trichuris*; notable because albendazole–ivermectin combination MDA outperforms either drug alone here.
- [[Onchocerca volvulus]] — The nodule-dwelling filarial worm of onchocerciasis, obligate Wolbachia symbiont, vectored by *Simulium* blackflies; cause of river blindness.
- [[Onchocerciasis]] — The disease; one of the NTDs with an elimination strategy and the hardest MDA-safety problem of the filarial group.
- [[Brugia malayi]] — Filarial nematode transmitted by *Mansonia*/*Anopheles* mosquitoes, cause of lymphatic filariasis in South and Southeast Asia.
- [[Wuchereria bancrofti]] — The major cause of lymphatic filariasis globally, with different vector genera in Africa, South Asia, and the Pacific.
- [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] — The disease, one of the most successful MDA elimination programmes; *Nigerian* and *Sri Lankan* precedents.
- [[Wolbachia]] — Obligate endosymbiont of filarial nematodes, and therefore a drug target against the worm itself.
- [[Ivermectin]] — The single most important drug in nematode control; kills microfilariae rather than adults, which is why transmission reduction rather than cure is the goal.
- [[Albendazole]] — The MDA drug for soil-transmitted helminths, and the partner in combined ivermectin–albendazole MDA for filariasis and onchocerciasis.
- [[Mebendazole]] — Alternative MDA drug for soil-transmitted helminthiasis.
- [[Moxidectin]] — A newer macrocyclic lactone for onchocerciasis with improved microfilaricidal activity and a longer interval between doses.
- [[Strongyloides]] — The soil-transmitted worm distinguished by autoinfection and hyperinfection, including a life-threatening disseminated form in the immunosuppressed.
- [[Strongyloidiasis]] — The disease of autoinfection, a genuine hazard for patients on steroids or with HTLV-1.
- [[Neglected Tropical Diseases]] — The programme under which the mass drug administration that controls most of these species is delivered.
- [[Mosquito]] — Intermediate host for the lymphatic filariasis species; [[Simulium]] for onchocerciasis.
- [[Suramin]] — The drug whose rapid microfilarial killing in onchocerciasis triggers the severe ocular inflammatory reaction.
- [[Inflammation]] — The immune effector responsible for both granuloma-mediated pathology and drug-induced inflammatory flares in filarial disease.
- [[Model Organisms]] — *C. elegans* is the vault's canonical free-living nematode, in contrast to the parasitic species above.
