# High-fat Diet

A high-fat diet is a **defined experimental intervention**, not a natural state, and the single most useful thing to know about it is that "high-fat diet" covers a range of recipes that produce materially different phenotypes. The most common rodent version supplies **~60% of metabolizable calories as fat**, usually lard, with the balance from carbohydrate and protein; a milder "Western-style diet" (WD) supplies ~43% kcal fat (21% butter fat) plus 34% sucrose, reproducing the Research Diets D12079B formulation. Diets in the literature range from 40% to 70% kcal fat, with the source of fat varying (lard, beef tallow, coconut oil, milk fat, butter, fish oil).

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The fat content is not the only variable that matters, and results do not transfer between formulations. The Jackson Laboratory's guidance is blunt: it is "critically important to know what diet was used in any publication you are trying to reproduce, as differences in nutrient content can have significant effects on metabolic outcomes." A controlled comparison of five lard-based HFDs differing in fat source and carbohydrate content found **heterogeneous effects on phenotype, metabolic outcome, and even the hepatic proteome signature** in BL/6 male mice. The 60%-lard diet is also increasingly criticised as "rather artificial" relative to the fat-and-sugar-rich diets humans actually eat, which is the argument for the WD formulation.

## Phenotype

Standard HFD feeding of C57BL/6J mice for 12–24 weeks produces:

- **Obesity**, typically 25–45% excess body weight, predominantly as [[Adipose Tissue|adipose]] rather than lean mass; C57BL/6J are more prone than AKR/J, and males more than females
- **Insulin resistance** and [[Hyperglycemia|hyperglycaemia]], with hyperinsulinaemia early and eventual β-cell decompensation
- **Dyslipidaemia**: raised circulating triglyceride, raised [[LDL]] and reduced [[HDL]]
- [[Hepatic Steatosis|hepatic steatosis]] — the most reproducible single finding
- Mild **steatohepatitis** (inflammation) but **minimal fibrosis**, which is the HFD model's central limitation for liver research
- Low-grade systemic inflammation, elevated [[TNFα]], [[IL-6]], and [[NF-κB]] activity — a state often described as [[Inflammaging|metabolic inflammation]]
- Cardiovascular phenotype: [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]], [[Arterial Stiffening|arterial stiffness]], [[Atherosclerosis]] in susceptible strains (ApoE⁻/⁻, LDLr⁻/⁻)

> [!info] Model selection
> | Model | Produces | Limitation |
> |---|---|---|
> | Standard HFD (45–60% kcal fat) | Metabolic syndrome, obesity, steatosis, mild steatohepatitis | Minimal fibrosis |
> | Western diet (fat + sucrose) | More severe metabolic and inflammatory phenotype, NASH in rats | Cost, variability |
> | Fast-food diet (fat + cholesterol + fructose) | More severe disease **with fibrosis** | Less standard |
> | Choline-deficient HFD (CDHFD) | Accelerated steatohepatitis and fibrosis | Removes the metabolic-syndrome context |
> | Methionine-choline-deficient (MCD) diet | Rapid steatohepatitis | **No obesity, no insulin resistance** — poor model of human NASH pathogenesis |
> | CDAA diet | Steatohepatitis, fibrosis | Same caveat as MCD |
>
> The recurring lesson is that MCD and CDAA models produce histological NASH **without** the metabolic syndrome that drives human disease, which is why HFD-based and WD-based models dominate despite being weaker histologically.

## Ageing context

> [!info] Why this matters for the longevity literature
> HFD is the standard model for **metabolic ageing**, and the phenotypes it induces — reduced [[AMPK]] and [[mTORC1]] signalling, increased [[NF-κB]] and SASP-adjacent inflammation, impaired [[Autophagy]] flux, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]], [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] — are largely the same phenotypes the ageing field is trying to reverse. HFD-induced acceleration of ageing phenotypes is therefore a workhorse model for testing geroprotectors.
>
> This is also where [[SRT1720]] enters: it was originally developed as a [[Sirtuins|SIRT1]] activator and its early characterisation was performed on diet-induced obesity models. Its HFD context is a reminder that most "sirtuin activator" pharmacology was done in obese, inflamed, short-lived animals — which limits how far the results transfer to metabolically healthy, long-lived subjects.

## Connections

- [[Metabolic Syndrome]] — HFD is the rodent route to the same phenotype cluster: central obesity, insulin resistance, [[Hypertension|hypertension]] and dyslipidaemia. It is the most-used model in the vault for this disease.
- [[Obesity]] and [[Adipose Tissue]] — the adipocyte hypertrophy and hyperplasia story; the shift from subcutaneous to visceral fat storage is what makes HFD metabolic rather than cosmetic.
- [[Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease|NASH]] / [[NAFLD]] — the hepatic arm; the reason HFD dominates NASH modelling despite modest fibrosis.
- [[Hepatic Steatosis]] and [[Lipotoxicity]] — the direct consequence of hepatic triglyceride accumulation and of the lipid species that actually drive lipotoxicity, as distinct from inert stored triglyceride.
- [[Insulin Resistance]] — via [[Adiponectin]]/leptin dysregulation, ectopic lipid in liver and skeletal muscle, and low-grade inflammation; [[Akt]] and [[AMPK-PGC-1α Pathway|AMPK-PGC-1α]] sit downstream.
- [[Inflammaging]] and [[SASP]] — HFD raises the inflammatory tone that the ageing field is trying to lower, which is the mechanistic overlap that makes HFD a pro-gero model rather than just a metabolic one.
- [[SRT1720]] and [[Resveratrol]] — sirtuin activators whose original pharmacology was done on diet-induced obesity models; the obesity context confounds interpretation of their geroprotective claims.
- [[Aging]] and [[Longevity]] — HFD shortens lifespan and healthspan in mice and is a standard counterfactual in the caloric-restriction and [[Intermittent Fasting]] literature.
- [[Gut Microbiome]] and [[Dysbiosis]] — diet is the largest single determinant of the mouse gut microbiome; HFD produces a reproducible dysbiotic shift, another route by which the diet reaches systemic inflammation.
- [[Lifespan]] and [[Healthspan]] — the endpoints HFD is used to move, in either direction.
