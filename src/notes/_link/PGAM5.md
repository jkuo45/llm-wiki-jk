# PGAM5

**PGAM5** (phosphoglycerate mutase family member 5) is a mitochondrial inner-membrane serine/threonine phosphatase with a split personality: the same protein serves **mitophagy** (cell preservation) and **necroptosis** (cell destruction), depending on which complex recruits it. It is the molecular hinge between [[Mitophagy]] and [[Necroptosis]].

## Role in mitophagy

- PGAM5 **stabilizes [[PINK1]]** on the damaged mitochondrial outer membrane and can attach to [[LC3]] proteins, supporting PINK1/[[Parkin]]-mediated mitophagy via a [[DRP1]]-dependent route.
- The **RIPK3–PGAM5–[[DRP1]] pathway** induces mitochondrial fission, which is a prerequisite for segregating damaged mitochondria and enacting mitophagy — the cell-preservation choice under moderate damage.
- PGAM5 is accordingly described as an important regulator of PINK1-dependent mitophagy that protects cells from necroptosis (e.g., ischemia-reperfusion heart models, where PGAM5 inhibition reduces necroptosis via Drp1 suppression).

## Role in necroptosis

- In the necroptosis cascade, PGAM5 **acts as an anchor for the necrosome** ([[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] complex), facilitating pore formation and lytic death.
- Downstream of phosphorylated [[RIPK3]] (e.g., ischemia-reperfusion injury), PGAM5 activates [[DRP1]] — driving the mitochondrial fission that accompanies necroptotic rupture.
- PGAM5 is a direct [[RIPK3]] substrate and bifunctional [[DRP1]] regulator: it dephosphorylates Drp1-Ser637 to release autoinhibition and facilitates Ser616 phosphorylation, jointly driving fission, MMP collapse, ATP depletion, and mtROS burst.
- A second execution arm is [[CypD]]-mPTP: [[RIPK3]]-upregulated PGAM5 enhances CypD phosphorylation, prolongs mPTP opening, and triggers necroptosis in parallel to [[MLKL]] pores (cardiac microvascular I/R, bromocriptine models).
- PGAM5 also converges diverse necrosis triggers beyond TNF-a (t-butyl hydroperoxide, H2O2, calcium ionophore A23187) and promotes RIPK1-PANoptosome activity via [[RIPK1]] phosphorylation in subarachnoid hemorrhage models.

> [!important]
> Direction depends on damage severity: mild stress → PGAM5 supports PINK1/Parkin mitophagy and cell survival; severe stress or specific stimuli → the same PGAM5–Drp1 axis serves necrosome-mediated lysis. Some studies contest the PGAM5–Drp1 link, so the exact split remains under investigation.

> [!warning]
> Source: Exp Mol Med 2025 Qi et al.; iScience 2024 He et al. 111539; Mitochondrial Dysfunction at Crossroads of Necroptosis 2025
> Species and context limits: PGAM5 knockdown does not affect TNF-a necroptosis in murine BMDMs (Moriwaki et al.) or FADD-deficient Jurkat with Drp1 inhibition (Maeda et al.); protection is strong in human epithelial, hepatocyte, cardiac, endothelial, and neuronal models but weak in murine immune cells. Proposed explanation is requirement for the human-only short splice form PGAM5-S. Alternative Drp1 routes (TREM-1/mTOR-Ser616, FUNDC1-mitophagy-MLKL) can bypass PGAM5.

## Supplemental research (2024–2026)

- Diabetic podocytopathy (Kang et al. 2024): RIPK3-PGAM5-Drp1 drives mitochondrial dysfunction and albuminuria.
- LPS acute lung injury (Zhu et al. 2022): NR4A1 inhibits Opa1-mediated fusion and activates PGAM5-related necroptosis.
- Aluminum-induced PC12 necroptosis (Zhang et al. 2023): RIP3-PGAM5-Drp1 axis confirmed.
- Lipid-overload renal tubular necroptosis (Sang et al. 2022): PGAM5-DRP1 fission/mitophagy flux.
- Vascular dementia PC12/rat (2025): PGAM5 hyperactivation drives excess Drp1 fission and RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL signaling; inhibition restores fusion and cognition.
- First specific inhibitor (J Adv Res 2026): Plantainoside D binds PGAM5, inhibits phosphatase activity and oligomerization, restores mitochondrial homeostasis, blocks necroptosis; protective in acute pancreatitis acinar necrosis, autoimmune hepatitis, acute kidney injury, myocardial I/R, lung fibrosis, and high-IOP retinal ganglion injury.
- Ferroptosis crosstalk (Cell Death Dis 2026 Ma et al.): PGAM5 dysregulation upregulates NRF2-FSP1 via AMPK, protecting against ferroptosis — U-shaped dosing caution for inhibitors.
