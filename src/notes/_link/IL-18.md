## Overview

Interleukin-18 (IL-18) is a pro-inflammatory cytokine belonging to the IL-1 superfamily. It is produced as an inactive precursor (pro-IL-18) and cleaved to its mature form by caspase-1 downstream of NLRP3 inflammasome activation, typically in macrophages and other innate immune cells.

## Biological Role

- **Inflammasome effector:** IL-18 is a key output of the NLRP3/caspase-1 axis, promoting IFN-γ production by NK and T cells and amplifying type 1 immunity.
- **Senescence & SASP:** IL-18 is a component of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) and contributes to inflammaging.
- **Cross-talk:** Synergizes with IL-12 in Th1 responses and with IL-1β in inflammatory cascades.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Dysregulated IL-18 is implicated in chronic inflammation, autoimmune disease, metabolic syndrome, and age-related inflammaging. It is a candidate target for inflammasome inhibitors (e.g., [[MCC950]] class compounds) in senescence- and inflammation-driven pathology.
