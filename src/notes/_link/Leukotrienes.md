# Leukotrienes

**Leukotrienes** are eicosanoid lipid mediators built from
[[Arachidonic acid]] through the 5-lipoxygenase pathway. They are named for
their original discovery as factors produced by leukocytes, and they fall into
two functional groups: **LTB4**, a neutrophil and monocyte chemoattractant, and
the **cysteinyl leukotrienes** (LTC4, LTD4, LTE4), which are bronchoconstrictors
and increase vascular permeability.

> [!info] The clinically important products are LTB4 and the CysLTs
> Leukotriene A4 is the branch-point intermediate. LTA4 hydrolase converts it to
> LTB4, while LTC4 synthase conjugates glutathione to make LTC4. The pathway is
> unusual in having two competing fates for one substrate, and that branch point
> is a point of pharmacological control.

## Biosynthesis

Substrate [[Arachidonic acid]] — released from membrane phospholipids by
phospholipase A2 — is oxygenated by **5-lipoxygenase** (ALOX5), which requires
FLAP, to LTA4. 5-lipoxygenase translocation to the nuclear membrane is the
regulated, activation-dependent step. LTA4 is then diverted:

- **LTB4** via LTA4 hydrolase.
- **LTC4** via LTC4 synthase, which adds glutathione. LTC4 is exported by
  transporters and is rapidly converted in the extracellular space to LTD4 by
  γ-glutamyl transpeptidase and then to LTE4 by dipeptidase — so LTD4 and LTE4
  are largely extracellular metabolites of LTC4.

Unlike the [[Cyclooxygenase|prostaglandin]] branch, the 5-lipoxygenase pathway
is heavily restricted to a small number of cell types — neutrophils,
eosinophils, basophils, and [[Macrophage|macrophages]] — which is why leukotriene
effects are concentrated in inflammatory and airway contexts.

## Receptors and Effects

**LTB4** acts at BLT1 and BLT2 receptors. Its functions are chemoattraction and
aggregation of neutrophils, enhanced adhesion and degranulation, and
stimulation of cytokine production. It contributes to neutrophil influx in
[[Inflammation]] and to the acute inflammatory response generally.

**Cysteinyl leukotrienes** act at **CysLT1** and **CysLT2**, both G-protein
coupled:

- CysLT1 mediates bronchoconstriction, increased bronchial mucosal blood flow
  and vascular permeability, mucus secretion, and eosinophil recruitment.
- CysLT2 is more widely expressed (including airway smooth muscle and immune
  cells) and mediates largely vasodilatory effects; its human contribution is
  less well defined.

CysLT1 antagonism with montelukast, zafirlukast, and pranlukast is a
first-line-adjacent asthma therapy, and 5-lipoxygenase inhibition with
zileuton is an alternative. Leukotriene receptor antagonists were among the
first therapies that could be taken orally without steroid-like systemic
effects, which explains their persistence in guidelines.

> [!warning] Cysteinyl leukotrienes are bronchoconstrictors, so they matter in
> asthma, but LTB4 largely does not
> The two branches are frequently lumped together under "leukotriene
> antagonists", yet the asthma evidence is for CysLT1 blockade. LTB4's minor
> role in asthma pathogenesis is often overstated in review articles.

## Beyond the Airway

CysLT signalling is not confined to asthma. Leukotrienes appear in
[[Neurodegeneration|neurodegenerative]] and [[Neurodegeneration|neuroinflammatory]]
contexts, in cardiovascular disease via effects on vascular
permeability and leukocyte recruitment, and in tumour microenvironments where
CysLT1 signalling has been linked to tumourigenic signalling cascades. Because
leukotrienes are highly reactive and lipid-derived, they also overlap
mechanistically with lipid peroxidation chemistry — the same
[[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and electrophilic-stress axis that other
oxidised lipids engage.

## Connections
- [[Arachidonic acid]] — the substrate for the whole pathway; the same pool that feeds the cyclooxygenase branch, which is why NSAID-type COX inhibition can shunt arachidonic acid toward leukotriene production.
- [[Cyclooxygenase]] — the competing branch point on arachidonic acid; the two pathways share substrate but use different enzymes and different cell types.
- [[Inflammation]] — leukotrienes are among the earliest and most potent lipid mediators of acute inflammation, acting upstream of and alongside cytokines.
- [[Asthma]] — the main clinical indication for CysLT1 antagonists, and the disease in which leukotriene overproduction is best documented.
- [[Lipoxygenase]] — the enzyme class that produces the leukotriene intermediate; it is also the source of lipoxins and hepxins, which resolve inflammation.
- [[Macrophage]] — one of the restricted cell types expressing 5-lipoxygenase and LTC4 synthase, and a major source of leukotriene production in tissue.
- [[Histamine]] — the other mast-cell-derived preformed mediator; histamine and leukotrienes are released together in the same immediate hypersensitivity response and act on overlapping airway endpoints.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — leukotrienes are reactive lipids, so they participate in the same electrophilic and oxidative stress chemistry as other oxidised membrane lipids.
