### Overview
The Inner Mitochondrial Membrane (IMM) is the highly folded inner membrane of the mitochondrion that invaginates to form cristae, dramatically expanding the membrane surface area available for energy production. It encloses the [[Mitochondrial Matrix]] and houses the electron transport chain and [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] (OXPHOS) complexes responsible for ATP generation.

### Structure and Dynamics
The IMM is markedly less permeable than the outer membrane, containing the [[Mitochondrial DNA]] and the machinery of oxidative metabolism within the matrix. During mitochondrial dynamics, IMM fusion is mediated by [[OPA1]] in concert with [[Cardiolipin]], a dimeric phospholipid unique to the IMM that stabilizes membrane curvature and fusion events.

### Fission and Contact Sites
IMM constriction during [[Mitochondrial Fission|fission]] is Ca2+-dependent and occurs at [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]]–mitochondria contact sites, where localized calcium transfer triggers the membrane remodeling required for division. The integrity of the IMM is therefore central to both the energetic and morphological homeostasis of the organelle.
