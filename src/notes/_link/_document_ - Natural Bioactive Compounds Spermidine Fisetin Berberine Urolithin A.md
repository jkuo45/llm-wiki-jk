# Natural Bioactive Compounds Targeting Key Hallmarks of Aging: Functional Food Potential of Spermidine, Fisetin, Berberine, and Urolithin A

Rzeski W., Rzeska W. *Nutrients* 2026, 18, 2511. [https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18152511](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu18152511). CC BY. Received 13 Jul 2026, accepted 25 Jul 2026, published 3 Aug 2026.

## Abstract

Naturally derived bioactive compounds that modulate [[Aging]]-associated mechanisms have attracted growing research interest, yet few reviews examine how such compounds might act together. This narrative review examines four mechanistically distinct compounds — [[Spermidine]], [[Fisetin]], [[Berberine]], and [[Urolithin A]] — as emerging functional food ingredients with scientifically supported effects on key hallmarks of human [[Aging]]. [[Spermidine]], a dietary polyamine from wheat germ and fermented foods, induces [[Autophagy]] through [[EP300]] inhibition and is associated with reduced all-cause mortality in prospective studies. [[Fisetin]], a flavonoid from strawberries and apples, exerts [[Senolytic]] activity by selectively eliminating senescent cells via [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] and [[Bcl-2]]/[[Bcl-xL]] inhibition, with emerging clinical evidence. [[Berberine]], an isoquinoline alkaloid from *Berberis* species, modulates metabolic dysfunction via [[AMPK]] activation and reshapes gut microbiota composition through direct high intraluminal exposure, with the most extensive clinical dataset of the four. [[Urolithin A]], a gut-microbiome-derived postbiotic from ellagitannins in pomegranates and nuts, induces [[Mitophagy]] via [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] and has been evaluated in a growing number of registered human clinical trials. Together they target distinct but complementary pathways ([[Autophagy]], senolysis, metabolic regulation, [[Mitophagy]]), suggesting rational potential for combined functional food formulations. Limited direct evidence for combined use and the need for dedicated co-administration studies are discussed, alongside bioavailability, safety, and regulatory considerations.

**Keywords:** functional food, bioactive compounds, [[Spermidine]], [[Fisetin]], [[Berberine]], [[Urolithin A]], [[Autophagy]], senolysis, [[Mitophagy]], [[Aging]], [[Healthspan]]

> [!important]
> Four compounds, four non-redundant hallmarks — [[Spermidine]] (bulk [[Autophagy]] via [[EP300]]), [[Fisetin]] (senolysis via [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] + [[Bcl-2]]/[[Bcl-xL]]), [[Berberine]] (metabolic + microbiome via [[AMPK]]), [[Urolithin A]] (selective [[Mitophagy]] via [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]]). No completed trial has tested all four together; the combination hypothesis is mechanistically grounded but prospectively unvalidated.

## Introduction

The global population is [[Aging]] at an unprecedented rate (2.1 billion aged 60+ projected by 2050). The hallmarks of [[Aging]] framework (López-Otín 2013, expanded 2023 to twelve processes: genomic instability, telomere attrition, epigenetic alterations, loss of proteostasis, disabled macroautophagy, deregulated nutrient-sensing, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular [[Senescence]], stem cell exhaustion, altered intercellular communication, chronic [[Inflammation]], and [[Dysbiosis]]) identifies tractable targets for interventions delaying biological [[Aging]].

Functional foods — foods providing health benefits beyond basic nutrition through bioactive constituents — offer a practical route for translating [[Aging]] biology into dietary recommendations. This review addresses four compounds selected for (i) natural dietary origin or derivation from dietary precursors, (ii) mechanistically characterized effects on defined hallmarks, (iii) at least preliminary human clinical data, and (iv) mechanistic complementarity. [[Resveratrol]] and [[Quercetin]] were excluded because their principal mechanisms substantially overlap with compounds already represented ([[AMPK]]/sirtuin signaling shared with [[Berberine]]; [[Senescence]]/[[Inflammation]] modulation shared with [[Fisetin]]). No review has previously examined all four within a unified functional-food framework; the contribution is integrative rather than mechanistic.

## Materials and Methods

Literature identified through PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar (through 2025). Review articles, RCTs, prospective cohorts, and mechanistic in vitro/animal studies included; grey literature and conference abstracts excluded except trial registries. Narrative (not PRISMA systematic) review, following conventions for synthesizing heterogeneous evidence across compound classes.

## Spermidine: The Dietary Autophagy Inducer

### Natural Sources and Dietary Availability

[[Spermidine]] is a triamine of the polyamine family (with putrescine and spermine), occurring in virtually all living organisms. Wheat germ is the richest common dietary source (~337 mg/kg dry weight); other notable sources include soybeans (~207 mg/kg), mushrooms, green peas, lentils and other legumes, corn, broccoli, cauliflower, mature cheeses (aged cheddar, brie), natto, and fermented sausages. Systemic availability comes from diet, endogenous biosynthesis from putrescine via spermidine synthase, and intestinal microbiome synthesis — all three declining with age. Average Western intake is ~8–12 mg/day, higher in Mediterranean/Japanese patterns.

### Molecular Mechanisms

[[Spermidine]] primarily induces [[Autophagy]] through inhibition of the acetyltransferase [[EP300]], which normally keeps cytoplasmic [[Autophagy]]-related proteins (ATG5, ATG7, ATG12, LC3) acetylated and inactive; inhibition derepresses autophagic flux. This is mTOR-independent (distinct from [[Rapamycin]]). Additional targets include [[AMPK]] activation, [[SIRT1]] upregulation, eIF5A hypusination (supporting mitochondrial respiratory complex synthesis), and suppression of [[NLRP3]] inflammasome activation and [[NF-κB]]-mediated transcription, reducing [[SASP]] and systemic low-grade [[Inflammation]]. Together these position [[Spermidine]] as a pleiotropic [[Caloric Restriction]] mimetic.

### Preclinical Evidence

[[Spermidine]] extends lifespan in yeast, *C. elegans*, *Drosophila*, and mice through [[Autophagy]]-dependent mechanisms (abolished by *atg* knockouts). In aged mice it reduced cardiac hypertrophy, preserved diastolic function, and improved mitochondrial respiration (absent in cardiomyocyte-specific *Atg5* knockouts); reduced systolic pressure in hypertensive rats; reduced tau hyperphosphorylation and amyloid-β accumulation in murine [[Alzheimer's Disease]] models; and enhanced T-cell anticancer immunosurveillance.

### Human Clinical Evidence

Strongest epidemiological evidence: the prospective Bruneck Study cohort (n = 829, 20-year follow-up) — higher dietary [[Spermidine]] intake independently associated with reduced all-cause mortality (40.5 → 23.7 → 15.1 deaths/1000 person-years across intake thirds; ~10 percentage-point lower 20-year cumulative mortality top vs bottom tertile), replicated in the Salzburg cohort. RCT evidence focuses on cognition: SmartAge phase II (wheat-germ extract 1.2 mg/day, 3 months, n = 30) established safety/tolerability with preliminary memory improvement; SmartAge phase IIb (0.9 mg/day, 12 months, n = 100) showed no significant mnemonic or biomarker benefit — noting trial doses (0.9–1.2 mg/day) sit well below habitual intake (8–12 mg/day). Limitations: small single-center studies in at-risk populations; no long-term or hard-endpoint data. The ongoing POLYCAD trial (Denmark, NCT06186102) evaluates [[Spermidine]] in coronary artery disease over 48 weeks.

### Potential in Functional Food Formulations

Strategies: wheat-germ enrichment of cereals, natto extracts in fermented foods, legume-enriched beverages, standardized wheat-germ supplements. Constraint: heat-induced degradation during processing requires optimization; age-related decline in intake and synthesis supports fortified products for older adults.

## Fisetin: The Senolytic Flavonoid

### Natural Sources and Dietary Availability

[[Fisetin]] (3,3′,4′,7-tetrahydroxyflavone) is a flavonol present in strawberries (richest source, ~160 mg/kg fresh weight), persimmons (~10.5 mg/kg), apples (~26 mg/kg), grapes, kiwi, peaches, cucumbers, and onions — localized in outer layers/skins, susceptible to peeling/processing losses. Bioavailability from food is severely limited by poor aqueous solubility, rapid phase II glucuronidation/sulfation, and first-pass hepatic metabolism (plasma levels nanomolar vs micromolar needed for senolysis) — the principal translational challenge.

### Molecular Mechanisms

[[Fisetin]] exerts [[Senolytic]] activity primarily through inhibition of the [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] pro-survival axis (constitutively active in senescent cells), plus direct inhibition of anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2]] family proteins ([[Bcl-2]], [[Bcl-xL]]), lowering the mitochondrial apoptotic threshold selectively in senescent cells. Senomorphic effects include suppression of [[NF-κB]] and downstream [[SASP]] components ([[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], MMP-1, MMP-3); additional mechanisms: [[NRF2]] activation, [[mTOR]]/[[AMPK]] modulation, [[NLRP3]] inhibition, [[SIRT1]] activation. Neuroprotective effects include tau inhibition via [[GSK3β]] modulation, reduced amyloid-β aggregation, and ERK1/2 activation.

### Preclinical Evidence

Yousefzadeh et al. (2018) screened ten flavonoids and identified [[Fisetin]] as the most potent [[Senolytic]] in vitro (25–50% senescent-cell clearance at 1–10 µM); dietary [[Fisetin]] (500 mg/kg diet) from 85 weeks extended median and maximum lifespan in mice, and acute gavage (100 mg/kg/day, 5 days) reduced senescent burden across tissues. Murray et al. (2025) showed intermittent dosing (1 week on / 2 off / 1 on) improved [[Frailty]] scores and grip strength comparably to genetic p16-positive cell clearance or ABT-263.

### Human Clinical Evidence

> [!warning]
> Unlike [[Berberine]] (46-RCT meta-analysis), [[Fisetin]] has no completed phase III trials and only early-phase studies — safety and plausibility established, clinical senolytic efficacy in humans not demonstrated.

Tavenier et al. (2024, with Mayo Clinic senolytics group) concluded [[Fisetin]] kills senescent cells in some (not all) cell types in vitro, reduces burden in animals, and is safe in early human studies; pharmacokinetics and validated circulating senolysis [[Biomarkers]] remain barriers. The STOP-Sepsis trial (NCT05758246, elderly sepsis, phase II) is the most rigorous ongoing assessment; additional trials cover peripheral arterial disease (NCT06399809), [[Frailty]], and post-COVID condition. Tolerated at 100–1000 mg/day; GI effects at high doses.

### Bioavailability Challenges and Functional Food Strategies

Poor bioavailability (logP ~2.9–3.2, rapid glucuronidation, short half-life). Galactomannan-fiber complexation (fenugreek-derived) raised bioavailability >25-fold in a crossover study; liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles, SEDDS, and co-crystallization under investigation. Intermittent high-dose paradigms (rather than daily intake) may suit food design: strawberry-based formulations, enriched beverages, standardized capsules.

## Berberine: The Metabolic and Microbiome Modulator

### Natural Sources and Functional Food Context

[[Berberine]] is an isoquinoline alkaloid in roots, rhizomes, bark, and stems across multiple families. Quantitatively significant sources include *Berberis vulgaris* (barberry), *Berberis aristata* (Indian barberry), *Coptis chinensis* (Chinese goldthread), *Hydrastis canadensis* (goldenseal), and *Phellodendron amurense* (Amur cork tree), at <1% to >8% dry weight depending on species, part, and growing conditions. Unlike [[Spermidine]] and [[Fisetin]], [[Berberine]] is not present in common Western foods at relevant concentrations; its relevance comes from traditional Chinese/Ayurvedic use and its status as a regulated supplement and functional ingredient (standardized extracts, capsules, beverages).

### Molecular Mechanisms

Primary mechanism: [[AMPK]] activation via mitochondrial complex-I inhibition (ATP↓, AMP:ATP↑, Thr172 phosphorylation), driving GLUT4 translocation, fatty-acid β-oxidation, [[SREBP-1c]] downregulation, and autophagic flux through [[mTORC1]] suppression — paralleling caloric-restriction/exercise adaptations. Anti-inflammatory actions: [[NF-κB]] suppression, [[NLRP3]] inhibition, TNF-α/[[IL-6]] reduction, M1→M2 macrophage polarization, [[SIRT1]] upregulation, and [[NRF2]]-ARE antioxidant activation.

> [!info]
> The berberine–microbiome axis — [[Berberine]]'s low oral bioavailability (1–5%) produces high intraluminal concentrations that enrich [[Akkermansia muciniphila]], [[Bifidobacterium]] spp., and SCFA-producing [[Lactobacillus]] spp. while reducing conditionally pathogenic taxa tied to metabolic endotoxemia. This creates systemic anti-inflammatory and metabolic effects independent of direct cellular actions, supporting gut barrier integrity and immune tone.

### Preclinical Evidence

In *C. elegans*, [[Berberine]] extended mean lifespan ~27% via [[DAF-16]]/[[FOXO]], HSF-1, and SKN-1/NRF2. In diabetic rodent models it improved glucose tolerance, lowered fasting glycemia, triglycerides, and LDL cholesterol, and attenuated hepatic steatosis. In naturally aging rats (6 months), it improved cognition, reversed muscle [[Insulin Resistance]], and raised mitochondrial biogenesis markers (p-[[AMPK]], [[SIRT1]], [[PGC-1α]]). It also attenuated oxidative-stress-induced premature [[Senescence]] in human fibroblasts via [[SIRT1]]-mediated p53 deacetylation.

### Human Clinical Evidence

[[Berberine]] carries the strongest clinical dataset of the four. Yin et al. (2008) randomized 36 newly diagnosed [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] patients to [[Berberine]] or [[Metformin]] (500 mg TID, 3 months) with comparable HbA1c, fasting/postprandial glucose, and triglyceride reductions. Guo et al. (2021) meta-analyzed 46 RCTs: HbA1c −0.73%, fasting glucose −0.86 mmol/L, 2-h postprandial glucose −1.26 mmol/L, HOMA-[[Insulin Resistance]] −0.71, plus total cholesterol −0.64, LDL-C −0.86, triglycerides −0.50 mmol/L and CRP/[[IL-6]]/TNF-α reductions; corroborated by a 2023/2024 umbrella meta-analysis. The [[PREMOTE]] trial (~400 newly diagnosed T2D patients, multi-center) showed ~1% HbA1c reduction at 12 weeks with measurable gut microbiota and metabolite shifts, directly linking metabolic improvement to microbiome mechanisms in humans.

### Bioavailability and Delivery Systems

Low systemic bioavailability (1–5%; P-glycoprotein efflux, first-pass metabolism) favors microbiome effects with conventional formulations, while systemic [[AMPK]] action needs enhanced absorption: dihydroberberine (reduced form, re-oxidized in tissues), phospholipid complexes, nanoparticle encapsulation, hydrophilic solid dispersions, and berberine-ursodeoxycholic acid salt (recent NASH+T2D trials). Formulation choice should follow the goal: conventional [[Berberine]] for microbiome reshaping, enhanced forms for systemic metabolic modulation.

## Urolithin A: The Dietary Postbiotic and Mitophagy Activator

### Ellagitannins as Dietary Precursors and Food Sources

[[Urolithin A]] (3,8-dihydroxy-6H-dibenzo[b,d]pyran-6-one) is not present in food but produced endogenously by colonic microbiota from [[Ellagitannins]] → [[Ellagic acid]] (hydrolyzed in small intestine) → sequential dehydroxylation to urolithin A. Precursor sources: pomegranate (densest, punicalin/punicalagin), walnuts, pecans, almonds, raspberries, strawberries, blackberries, oak-aged wines, certain teas. The terms describe sequential stages, not interchangeable substances; "postbiotic" is used per common field usage though a purified metabolite does not strictly fit the ISAPP definition.

> [!info]
> The converter consortium — principally [[Gordonibacter urolithinfaciens]] and [[Ellagibacter isourolithinifaciens]], with contributions from select [[Enterocloster]] species — converts [[Ellagic acid]] through urolithin M6/C to terminal [[Urolithin A]] (urolithin B arises in a subset). See [[Metabotypes]] for converter stratification.

### Gut Microbiome Conversion and Inter-Individual Variability

Individuals fall into metabotype A (UA producers, ~25–55% depending on population), metabotype B (urolithin B/isourolithin A, ~10%), and metabotype 0 (non-producers, ~10–50%). Dietary ellagitannin strategies therefore fail for most people — the fundamental constraint distinguishing [[Urolithin A]] from the other three compounds — driving direct [[Urolithin A]] supplementation for consistent exposure independent of microbiota.

### Molecular Mechanisms

[[Urolithin A]] was the first natural compound shown to induce [[Mitophagy]] in vitro and in vivo after oral intake (Ryu 2016): stabilization of [[PINK1]] on depolarized mitochondria → activation of E3 ligase [[Parkin]] → ubiquitination of outer-membrane proteins → recognition by autophagy receptors (p62/SQSTM1, NDP52, OPTN) → autophagosomal engulfment and lysosomal degradation.

### Preclinical Evidence

In *C. elegans*, [[Urolithin A]] (50 µM) extended median lifespan 45.4% via [[Mitophagy]] (abolished by *dct-1*/BNIP3L or *bec-1* ablation), preserving pharyngeal pumping and mobility. Rodent work showed improved exercise capacity and muscle mitochondrial function (Duchenne model) and reversed cardiac hypertrophy/remodeling in HFpEF models via cardiomyocyte [[Mitophagy]].

### Human Clinical Evidence

A 2024 systematic review identified five completed trials (n = 250) — the largest dataset among natural mitophagy inducers. Andreux et al. (2019, first-in-human phase I): safe at 250–2000 mg single / 500–1000 mg/day × 4 weeks, with plasma acylcarnitine shifts and mitochondrial gene induction in muscle biopsies (target engagement). The [[ATLAS]] RCT (Singh 2022, n = 88, 500/1000 mg/day × 4 months): ~12% hamstring-strength gain (500 mg), improved peak VO2 and 6-minute walk, reduced acylcarnitines/CRP. Liu et al. (2022, older adults, 1000 mg/day × 4 months): improved hand/leg muscle endurance plus mitochondrial-health biomarkers. A 2025 *Nature Aging* RCT (1000 mg/day × 4 weeks): remodeled peripheral immunity (naive-T/TSCM expansion, T-cell OXPHOS reprogramming).

### Dietary Strategy vs. Direct Supplementation

Ellagitannin-rich foods reliably deliver [[Urolithin A]] only to metabotype A individuals; others need direct purified supplementation (e.g., Mitopure) for dose-controlled exposure. Hybrid prebiotic + ellagitannin strategies supporting *Gordonibacter*/*Ellagibacter* growth remain understudied.

## Complementary Mechanisms and the Combination Hypothesis

### Mechanistic Complementarity Within the Hallmarks of Aging Framework

[[Spermidine]] (bulk [[Autophagy]] via [[EP300]]) and [[Urolithin A]] (selective [[Mitophagy]] via [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]]) are complementary: impaired [[Mitophagy]] with intact general [[Autophagy]] still yields ROS overproduction and cGAS-[[STING]] [[Inflammation]]. The autophagy–[[Senescence]] threshold model (Bahar 2026) rationalizes sequencing: [[Spermidine]] prevents [[Senescence]] initiation pre-threshold; [[Fisetin]] eliminates established senescent cells post-threshold. [[Berberine]] restores metabolic homeostasis ([[AMPK]], microbiota) countering nutrient-sensing dysregulation and gut-derived inflammatory signaling that amplify other hallmarks.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[Spermidine]] | General [[Autophagy]] induction | Proteostasis loss; disabled macroautophagy; chronic [[Inflammation]] | [[EP300]] down; [[AMPK]] up; [[SIRT1]] up; [[mTORC1]] down | Wheat germ, soy, mushrooms, fermented foods |
| [[Fisetin]] | Senolysis + [[SASP]] suppression | Cellular [[Senescence]]; chronic [[Inflammation]]; altered intercellular communication | [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] down; [[Bcl-2]]/[[Bcl-xL]] down; [[NF-κB]] down; [[NRF2]] up | Strawberries, apples, persimmons, onions |
| [[Berberine]] | Metabolic + microbiome modulation | Deregulated nutrient-sensing; [[Dysbiosis]]; chronic [[Inflammation]] | [[AMPK]] up; [[mTORC1]] down; [[NF-κB]] down; microbiota reshaping | *Berberis* spp., *Coptis* spp. (supplement) |
| [[Urolithin A]] | Selective [[Mitophagy]] induction | Mitochondrial dysfunction; chronic [[Inflammation]]; immunosenescence | [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] up; [[SIRT1]]/[[PGC-1α]] up; [[NLRP3]] down | Postbiotic from [[Ellagitannins]] (pomegranate, berries, walnuts) |

### Evidence for Combined Use: Pairs and Subsets

No peer-reviewed direct evidence for all-four co-administration exists. The most direct clinical evidence is NCT06990256 (registered, quadruple-blind, placebo-controlled, n = 80 aged 45–70; [[Urolithin A]] 500 mg and [[Fisetin]] 500 mg single/combination arms incl. UA 300 mg + [[Fisetin]] 200 mg; endpoints sleep quality, [[DNA Methylation]] age, inflammatory markers, [[BMAL1]]/circadian proteins, HOMA-[[Insulin Resistance]], 12 weeks) — the only registered trial combining any two of the four. Mechanistic reviews support [[Spermidine]] + [[Urolithin A]] (broad [[Autophagy]] + specific [[Mitophagy]]) and [[Spermidine]] + [[Berberine]] ([[Caloric Restriction]] mimetics via histone-deacetylation/[[Autophagy]] vs [[AMPK]]–microbiota axes) as additive rather than redundant. Network-medicine logic (multi-node modulation) supports the hypothesis, but pharmacokinetics, disease stage, and individual variability may dominate outcomes; ablation of any single pathway produces distinct aging phenotypes, supporting additivity.

### Practical Considerations for Multi-Compound Formulations

Human trial dose ranges: [[Spermidine]] 0.9–3.3 mg/day (wheat-germ extract); [[Fisetin]] 100–1000 mg/day (intermittent preferred); [[Berberine]] 900–1500 mg/day (divided); [[Urolithin A]] 500–1000 mg/day. Food-matrix delivery of [[Fisetin]]/[[Berberine]] faces bioavailability constraints. Pharmacokinetic interactions are unstudied — critical gap: [[Berberine]] inhibits [[CYP3A4]] and P-glycoprotein, potentially modulating [[Fisetin]]/[[Urolithin A]] absorption (dedicated study needed before multi-compound clinical use); [[Spermidine]] shows no interaction signal at dietary doses. [[Metabotypes]] heterogeneity (A ~25–55%) means broad-coverage formulas should use direct [[Urolithin A]] supplementation, while ellagitannin + prebiotic formulas suit metabotype-A-enriched or screened populations.

> [!warning]
> Berberine–fisetin interaction caution — [[Berberine]] ([[CYP3A4]]/P-gp inhibitor) plus [[Fisetin]] ([[CYP3A4]] substrate and partial inhibitor) may mutually elevate plasma levels. Interim mitigation: temporal separation ([[Berberine]] AM / [[Fisetin]] PM); avoid co-administration with narrow-therapeutic-index [[CYP3A4]] substrates.

### Priority Directions for Future Research

(1) Co-administration RCTs in defined aging populations (metabolic syndrome, [[Frailty]], accelerated biological aging) with composite multi-hallmark biomarker panels. (2) Co-administration pharmacokinetics ([[CYP3A4]]/P-gp focus). (3) Stratification by urolithin [[Metabotypes]] and microbiome composition. (4) Validated composite aging-biomarker indices ([[Autophagy]], [[Senescence]], mitochondrial, metabolic). (5) Preclinical proof-of-concept: aged mice randomized to single-compound, pairwise, four-compound, and vehicle arms with flux/burden/mitochondrial/[[Inflammation]] readouts. (6) Pharmacogenomics ([[CYP3A4]]*3, [[CYP2D6]] poor-metabolizer alleles affecting [[Berberine]] exposure; metabotype-determining microbiome signatures; PRKAA2 variants preliminary).

## Safety, Bioavailability, and Regulatory Considerations

### Safety Profiles

**[[Spermidine]].** Inherent dietary safety basis; 90-day GLP rat study of spermidine trihydrochloride showed no adverse effects up to 728–829 mg/kg/day. Human RCTs (0.9–3.3 mg/day) reported no attributable adverse events. Theoretical concern: polyamine support of pre-existing cancer proliferation — undemonstrated in human trials.

**[[Fisetin]].** Well tolerated at 100–1000 mg/day; mild GI effects at high doses. Low bioavailability may paradoxically limit off-target risk while constraining efficacy. Theoretical [[CYP3A4]]/CYP1A2 inhibition at high concentrations — not clinically documented at trial doses.

**[[Berberine]].** Extensive Asian clinical record at 900–1500 mg/day (divided); mostly mild transient GI effects. Interacts via [[CYP3A4]], [[CYP2D6]], P-glycoprotein inhibition (statins, warfarin, cyclosporine, antidiabetics); contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenicity/neonatal jaundice signals). EFSA safety assessment (EFSA-Q-2022-00803): NDA Panel draft opinion endorsed Jan 2026, public consultation closed May 2026, finalization pending — outcome will shape EU standing. Practitioners should separate [[Berberine]]/[[Fisetin]] dosing and avoid combining with narrow-therapeutic-index [[CYP3A4]] substrates pending interaction studies.

**[[Urolithin A]].** Most rigorous safety evaluation of the four: phase I safe to 2000 mg single / 1000 mg/day × 4 weeks; no attributable serious adverse events across completed/ongoing RCTs; no significant drug interactions identified.

> [!caution]
> Common safety gap — virtually no prospectively designed drug-interaction studies and no toxicology beyond ~90 days (animals) or ~12 months (humans). Current conclusions rest on short observation windows, especially for any long-term multi-compound formulation.

### Bioavailability Comparison

[[Spermidine]] (small water-soluble polyamine) absorbs efficiently via polyamine transporters, though rapid spermine interconversion complicates plasma PK; systemic exposure at dietary doses appears sufficient for peripheral [[Autophagy]] modulation. [[Urolithin A]] (purified supplement) shows dose-proportional PK (Tmax 4–6 h, half-life ~20 h; once-daily dosing). [[Fisetin]] (poor solubility, rapid phase II conjugation, short/poorly characterized half-life) and [[Berberine]] (1–5% systemic; P-glycoprotein efflux, first-pass metabolism) face major constraints — though [[Berberine]]'s intraluminal levels independently drive microbiome effects.

| [[Spermidine]] | Moderate; diet-equivalent | ~1–2 | Variable | 0.9–3.3 mg/day (extract) | Wheat germ extract; standardized foods |
| [[Fisetin]] | Low (<10%) | ~1–2 | Not well characterized | 100–1000 mg/day (intermittent) | Galactomannan complex; liposomes; nanoparticles |
| [[Berberine]] | Very low (1–5% systemic) | ~1–2 | ~3–5 | 900–1500 mg/day (divided) | Dihydroberberine; phospholipid complex; SEDDS |
| [[Urolithin A]] | Good (direct supplement); variable from diet | ~4–6 | ~20 | 500–1000 mg/day | Purified supplement (Mitopure); microbiome optimization |

### Regulatory Status

| Compound | USA (FDA) | EU (EFSA/EC) | Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Spermidine]] | Dietary supplement; GRAS self-affirmation (synthetic) | Food supplement (wheat germ extract); no novel food required | None identified |
| [[Fisetin]] | Dietary supplement | Food supplement (plant extract); no novel food currently required | Low; clinical advance may trigger review |
| [[Berberine]] | Dietary supplement | EFSA assessment ongoing (2023–2026); novel-food authorization terminated Apr 2025 | Moderate–High; EU uncertainty pending opinion |

[[Spermidine]] (wheat-germ) needs no novel-food authorization; synthetic trihydrochloride via GRAS self-affirmation. [[Fisetin]] (plant extracts, e.g., *Rhus succedanea*, strawberry) currently needs none. [[Berberine]] is most complex: Commission Implementing Decision C(2025)2494 (Apr 2025) terminated novel-food authorization for a berberine–silymarin preparation from *Berberis aristata*, reflecting safety uncertainty. [[Urolithin A]] is most advanced: FDA GRAS 2018 plus ongoing EFSA novel-food review — the most legally straightforward EU ingredient of the four.

## Conclusions and Future Directions

The four compounds address non-overlapping targets — [[Autophagy]] induction ([[Spermidine]]), senolysis ([[Fisetin]]), metabolic/microbiome regulation ([[Berberine]]), selective [[Mitophagy]] ([[Urolithin A]]) — interconnected within the hallmarks network, providing a rational multi-target dietary strategy. Evidence maturity varies: [[Berberine]] strongest (46-RCT meta-analysis) though metabolically focused; [[Urolithin A]] the richest mitophagy clinical dataset (safety, target engagement, strength/endurance, immune remodeling) though mostly surrogate endpoints and converter heterogeneity; [[Spermidine]] has Bruneck mortality linkage with inconclusive cognition RCTs; [[Fisetin]] earliest-stage (preclinical + early human). Direct combination evidence is limited to one registered trial (NCT06990256, [[Urolithin A]] + [[Fisetin]]). The threshold model ([[Spermidine]] pre-threshold prevention, [[Fisetin]] post-threshold clearance), [[Berberine]]'s gut-derived inflammatory-input reduction, and [[Urolithin A]]'s organelle quality control together form the conceptual core.

A Mediterranean-style pattern (wheat germ, strawberries/apples, pomegranate/walnuts) plus standardized [[Berberine]] supplementation could deliver several compounds within a coherent dietary framework — testable without novel-product complexity — while [[Urolithin A]] delivery must account for [[Metabotypes]] heterogeneity. Key open questions: multi-compound co-administration trials with composite endpoints (NCT06990256 results first), co-administration PK ([[CYP3A4]]/[[CYP2D6]]/P-gp), metabotype stratification, validated composite biomarker indices.

> [!tip]
> Practical takeaway — food-first delivery works for [[Spermidine]] and partially [[Fisetin]]/[[Urolithin A]] (metabotype A only); [[Berberine]] requires supplementation; enhanced-absorption forms matter only when systemic (not gut) action is the goal.
