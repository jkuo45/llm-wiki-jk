# Triiodothyronine

**Triiodothyronine (T3)** is the principal active thyroid hormone. The thyroid gland secretes predominantly the prohormone thyroxine (T4), which is converted to T3 by 5'-deiodinases in the liver, kidney, and skeletal muscle. T3 binds nuclear thyroid-hormone receptors to regulate transcription of genes controlling metabolism, including mitochondrial biogenesis and sodium–potassium ATPase expression.

## Role in Resting Metabolic Rate

- T3 is a major endocrine determinant of basal metabolic rate; hypothyroidism lowers RMR and hyperthyroidism raises it.
- Plasma T3 (and T3:T4 ratio) is among the physiological correlates of residual variation in [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]] — individuals with higher T3 tend toward higher RMR at the same body composition (Kaiyala _et al_. 2002).

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review lists circulating T3 among the physiological correlates of residual RMR variance, alongside [[Protein Turnover|protein turnover]], β-adrenergic markers, and [[Mitochondrial Uncoupling|mitochondrial proton leak]].

## Regulation

T3 secretion is controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis: TRH → TSH → thyroid hormone release, with negative feedback. Fasting, calorie restriction, and severe illness lower T3 (low-T3 syndrome), contributing to the reduction in RMR during energy deficit.

## Clinical Relevance

Thyroid status is routinely assessed for metabolic complaints; T3 or levothyroxine replacement normalises RMR in hypothyroidism, while over-replacement can cause unwanted weight loss and cardiac strain.
