# Deferoxamine

**Deferoxamine (DFO)** is a clinically used iron chelator (derived from *Streptomyces*) that binds Fe³⁺ with high affinity, removing labile iron from cells and plasma. Because ferroptosis is iron-dependent, DFO is a classic **ferroptosis inhibitor**: by stripping the redox-active iron that fuels the [[Fenton Reaction]], it prevents lipid peroxide propagation and rescues cells from ferroptotic death.

## Chemistry & Pharmacology

DFO is a trihydroxamate siderophore analog that forms a stable 1:1 complex with ferric iron (ferrioxamine), excreted renally. It penetrates poorly across membranes and has a short plasma half-life, requiring prolonged infusion; newer oral chelators (deferiprone, deferasirox) are alternatives.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

Ferroptosis requires Fe²⁺ to convert lipid hydroperoxides into radical chains via the [[Fenton Reaction]]. DFO chelates extracellular/accessible iron, shrinking the labile iron pool and blocking this chemistry. It rescues erastin-, [[RSL3]]-, and [[Artesunate]]-induced ferroptosis and is listed among the key ferroptosis inhibitors alongside [[Ferrostatin-1]], [[Liproxstatin-1]], and [[Vitamin E]] (review Introduction/Mechanism). Beyond ferroptosis, DFO treats iron overload (thalassemia, hemochromatosis) and is being studied in [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] and neurodegeneration.

> [!info] DFO in the clinic of ferroptosis
> In the review, DFO improves cardiac function when applied at reperfusion in I/R models and alleviates oxidative stress/motor symptoms in early [[Parkinson's Disease]]—effects consistent with iron-chelation suppression of ferroptosis.

## Clinical Relevance

DFO is established for iron-overload disease. Its ferroptosis-inhibitory action makes it a research and potential therapeutic tool in stroke, TBI, AKI, myocardial I/R, and Parkinson's—though systemic iron stripping limits chronic use. It is the canonical positive control for demonstrating iron-dependence of a death program.

> [!tip] Use in assays
> If a death is blocked by DFO (and by Fer-1/Liproxstatin-1) but not by apoptosis/necroptosis inhibitors, iron-dependence and likely ferroptosis are established.
