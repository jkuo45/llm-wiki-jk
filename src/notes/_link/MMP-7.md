# MMP-7

**MMP-7** (matrix metalloproteinase 7; matrilysin) is the smallest member of the matrix metalloproteinase family, a secreted zinc-dependent endopeptidase with broad substrate selectivity. Unlike most MMPs, MMP-7 lacks a hemopexin-like domain and is constitutively secreted by epithelia. It degrades [[Extracellular Matrix|ECM]] components (type IV collagen, laminin, fibronectin, proteoglycans), processes growth factors and receptors, and is a well-established mediator of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), tissue remodeling, and tumor invasion. Elevated MMP-7 is a clinical biomarker of fibrotic disease (e.g., idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis).

## MMP-7 in renal fibrosis downstream of SIRT3

In chronic kidney disease, [[SIRT3]] deacetylates [[β-Catenin|β-catenin]], a pivotal transcriptional coactivator in fibroblast activation, facilitating the expression of MMP-7 and [[PAI-1|plasminogen activator inhibitor-1]] — two mediators implicated in ECM remodeling via EMT induction[308],[296]. In the tubular epithelium, SIRT3 suppresses [[EMT|epithelial-mesenchymal transition]] through [[FOXO3a]]-dependent antioxidant gene induction, and the β-catenin/MMP-7 arm represents a parallel profibrotic program that SIRT3 restrains. Loss of SIRT3 thus derepresses MMP-7-driven matrix remodeling that accelerates the AKI-to-CKD transition.

## Clinical relevance

MMP-7 couples matrix degradation to EMT and fibrogenesis across kidney, lung, and intestinal disease. As a secreted enzyme, it is a tractable biomarker for monitoring fibrotic progression and the efficacy of SIRT3-restoring interventions.
