# Helminth

Helminths ("worms") are multicellular, non-microscopic parasitic organisms divided into three major assemblages: **Nematoda** (roundworms — unsegmented, tube-like, separate sexes, a complete gut and a fluid pseudocoel acting as a hydrostatic skeleton), **Trematoda** (flukes — flat, hermaphroditic, living in blood vessels, bile ducts, gut or airways) and **Cestoda** (tapeworms — flat, segmented, no gut at all, absorbing nutrients across their tegument). Nearly 300 species infect humans, and it has been estimated that more than a third of the world's population carries at least one.

What makes helminths biologically distinct from other pathogens is not their taxonomy but their **lifespan and their modulation of the host response**. Adult worms live months to years, lay thousands to millions of eggs, and have co-evolved mechanisms that blunt the killing response that would otherwise clear them. The result is that helminth disease is usually a chronic, low-grade, non-lethal state, with morbidity — anaemia, malnutrition, blood loss, obstruction, tissue remodelling — rather than acute mortality as the signature.

> [!info] Immunomodulation
> Helminths induce a stereotyped **T helper 2 / regulatory** response: IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, eosinophilia, [[IgE]] and mast cell / basophil activation, and — critically — expansion of [[Regulatory T Cell|regulatory T cells]] and IL-10. This is the ancestor of the mechanism behind the **hygiene hypothesis**: helminth colonisation was near-universal in humans until the early 20th century, and their disappearance coincides with the rise of [[Asthma|asthma]], allergic disease and [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease|IBD]].
>
> The proposed clinical applications run in both directions: live attenuated helminths (e.g. *Trichuris suis* ova, *Necator americanus*) have been trialled as therapy for Crohn's disease and multiple sclerosis, and helminth-derived molecules are being developed as immune-modulating drugs.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The translational record is weak and the caveats are real. Randomised trials of *Trichuris suis* ova in Crohn's disease were **negative or equivocal**; a *Necator americanus* trial in MS produced clinical benefit with no change on MRI. The field's dominant honest framing is that the mechanism is real and the intervention is not yet effective. Two further points matter for a longevity-focused reader: (i) helminth infection in humans is associated with **poorer** vaccine responses and with altered glucose homeostasis, so extrapolating immunological benefits is not straightforward; and (ii) this vault's antihelminthic drugs — [[Albendazole]], [[Ivermectin]], [[Fenbendazole]] — are also being explored as anti-cancer and anti-ageing agents, which is precisely why getting the immunomodulatory biology right matters.

> [!info] Major human helminths
> - **Nematodes**: *Ascaris lumbricoides* ([[Ascariasis]]), *Trichuris trichiura* ([[Trichuriasis]]), hookworms (*Necator americanus*, *Ancylostoma duodenale*), *Strongyloides stercoralis* ([[Strongyloidiasis]]), *Enterobius vermicularis*, and the filariae — *Wuchereria bancrofti* ([[Lymphatic Filariasis]]), *Brugia malayi*, *Onchocerca volvulus* ([[Onchocerciasis]]), *Loa loa*, *Mansonella*. *Gnathostoma spinigerum* ([[Gnathostoma]]) is a tissue-migrating tissue nematode with an arthropod intermediate host.
> - **Cestodes**: *Taenia saginata* and *Taenia solium* (neurocysticercosis, the leading cause of acquired epilepsy worldwide), *Diphyllobothrium latum*, *Echinococcus* (hydatid cyst, cysticercosis).
> - **Trematodes**: *Schistosoma* spp. (blood flukes; the most significant helminth pathogens globally by morbidity), *Clonorchis sinensis* and *Opisthorchis viverrini* (cholangiocarcinoma), *Fasciola hepatica*, *Paragonimus westermani*.

> [!info] Diagnostic note
> Diagnosis is overwhelmingly by detection of eggs in stool (or [[Microfilariae]] in blood or skin snips), which has the direct consequence that the vault's helminth notes are host-organism pairs: the worm is defined by the disease it causes in a given tissue, not by phylogeny. Serology matters only where eggs are not produced or not yet mature (e.g. schistosomiasis, filariasis, *Gnathostoma*).

## Connections

- [[Ascariasis]], [[Trichuriasis]] and [[Gnathostomiasis]] — the three vault notes that reach for the parent term; each is a distinct helminth disease with a distinct organ, lifecycle and immune phenotype.
- [[Nematode]] — the phylum containing the majority of clinically important helminths, and the vault's own lifecycle notes ([[C. elegans]], *Drosophila* work) belong to the same phylum.
- [[Immune System]] / [[Innate Immune System]] — helminths do not stimulate [[NLRP3 Inflammasome|NLRP3]] the way bacteria or crystals do; they polarise adaptive immunity towards a regulatory phenotype instead. The interesting question in the longevity field is whether that polarity is protective or mildly pro-tumour-promoting.
- [[Inflammation]] and [[Anti-inflammatory]] — helminth-induced immunomodulation is the strongest real-world example of physiological, chronic, low-grade anti-inflammatory signalling, which is the same shape of intervention as [[Fasting]] and [[Spermidine]].
- [[Eosinophils]] and [[Eosinophil Peroxidase]] — eosinophilia is the haematological signature of helminth infection and of tissue-invasive worms in particular; the eosinophil is a double-edged actor here, since the same response that damages the worm damages the host.
- [[Albendazole]] and [[Ivermectin]] — the two effective chemotherapeutics, both mitochondria-toxic in the worm (they potentiate [[Glutamate]]-gated chloride channels, which exist only in invertebrates — the basis of their selectivity). [[Fenbendazole]] and [[Moxidectin]] are the same class of thinking.
- [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]] and [[Asthma]] — the diseases the hygiene hypothesis attributes to *loss* of helminths; the strongest observational support is the inverse relationship between helminth prevalence and atopic disease, which is robust geographically but has not converted into a working therapy.
- [[Obesity]] and metabolic disease — an active and genuinely unsettled area: some studies associate helminth infection with *worse* glucose tolerance, others with a leaner phenotype, and the direction depends on species (Trichuris vs hookworm differ).
