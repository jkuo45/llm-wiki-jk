# Enzalutamide

**Overview:** Enzalutamide (MDV3100, brand name Xtandi) is a second-generation **androgen receptor (AR) signaling inhibitor** approved for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) and non-metastatic CRPC. Unlike first-generation antiandrogens, enzalutamide competitively inhibits AR binding of androgens, blocks AR nuclear translocation, and prevents AR-coactivator interactions, thereby suppressing AR-driven transcription in tumor cells.

## Mechanism of Action

- **Androgen-receptor pathway blockade:** Enzalutamide binds the AR ligand-binding domain with high affinity, preventing nuclear translocation and DNA binding of the receptor complex, leading to growth arrest and apoptosis of AR-dependent prostate cancer cells.
- **Resistance:** Acquired enzalutamide resistance emerges through AR amplification, AR splice variants (e.g., AR-V7), and bypass signaling, posing a major therapeutic limitation.

## Interaction with Ivermectin (Repurposing Context)

In the ivermectin anticancer-repositioning literature, enzalutamide resistance serves as a clinically relevant resistance model. Preclinical reports indicate that [[Ivermectin]] **overcomes enzalutamide resistance** in prostate cancer models and shows **synergy with docetaxel** (a tubulin-stabilizing taxane), supporting the rationale for repurposing antiparasitic agents in AR-pathway-driven tumors. This connects the [[Ivermectin]] node to the [[Prostate Cancer]]/[[Docetaxel]]/[[Androgen Receptor]] axis.

## Pharmacodynamics & Clinical Use

Enzalutamide is administered orally; it is extensively metabolized by CYP2C8 and CYP3A4. It is a cornerstone of CRPC therapy but is limited by resistance and cross-resistance with other AR-axis agents.
