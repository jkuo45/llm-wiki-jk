# Lithium

**Chemical properties and classification:** **Lithium** is the lightest alkali metal, administered clinically as Li+ salts (carbonate, citrate). Despite its simple ionic form, it exerts complex, therapeutically useful biology.

**Pharmacodynamics:** The best-characterized target is inhibition of [[GSK3]] (both α and β isoforms), mimicking Wnt/β-catenin signaling and influencing neuroplasticity. Lithium also depletes brain inositol via inhibition of inositol monophosphatase, dampening phosphoinositide signaling, and promotes [[Autophagy]] and mitochondrial protection. These actions converge on reduced [[Inflammation]] and enhanced neuronal resilience.

**Applications:** Lithium is a first-line mood stabilizer for bipolar disorder and augments antidepressants; it reduces suicide risk. Preclinical and epidemiological work links long-term lithium exposure to [[Alzheimer's Disease]] risk reduction and potential [[Aging]]/longevity benefits via autophagy and stress-resistance pathways.

**Safety and toxicity:** Lithium has a narrow therapeutic index; toxicity manifests as tremor, hypothyroidism, and nephrotoxicity ([[Kidney|chronic kidney disease]]). Monitoring of serum levels and renal/thyroid function is mandatory.
