# Radiation Therapy

[[Radiation Therapy]] (radiotherapy) uses ionizing radiation to damage DNA and kill cancer cells. It is one of the three pillars of cancer treatment alongside surgery and chemotherapy. Molecular oxygen acts as a potent chemical radiosensitizer, making hypoxic tumor cells resistant to radiation.

## Hypoxia and Radiation Resistance

Solid tumors develop regions of severe [[Hypoxia]] due to disorganized vasculature. Hypoxic cells can survive radiation regimens that eradicate fully aerobic populations, causing tumors to recur. Strategies to overcome this include combining radiation with hypoxic cell sensitizers, hypoxia-selective cytotoxins, or agents that improve tumor oxygenation.

## Relevance to Fenbendazole

> [!warning] Source: [[Fenbendazole as a Potential Anticancer Drug]]
> Fenbendazole did not alter the radiation response of aerobic or hypoxic EMT6 cells in vitro, nor did it alter the growth or radiation response of EMT6 tumors in vivo when given as three daily i.p. injections (50 mg/kg/day) or in diet (150 ppm) (Duan et al. 2013).
