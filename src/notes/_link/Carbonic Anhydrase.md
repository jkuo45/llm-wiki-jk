# Carbonic Anhydrase

**Carbonic anhydrase** (CA) is a zinc-containing enzyme that catalyzes the near-diffusion-limited hydration of CO2: CO2 + H2O ⇄ H2CO3 ⇄ H+ + HCO3−. In [[Erythrocytes]] it is the enzyme half of the CO2 transport system, working together with the membrane exchanger [[Anion Exchanger 1|AE1/Band 3]].

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> CO2 diffuses into the erythrocyte, is hydrated by carbonic anhydrase, and the resulting HCO3− is exported to plasma by AE1 in exchange for Cl−. In lung capillaries the reactions run in reverse and gaseous CO2 leaves via the alveoli. Hemoglobin carries only ~23% of CO2 directly; most of the rest travels as bicarbonate made possible by this enzyme.

## Isoforms and pharmacology

- CA II is the abundant erythrocyte isoform (~200 mg/g hemoglobin); CA I is also present. Both are Zn2+-dependent, sulfonamide-sensitive.
- CA II deficiency (marble brain disease) causes osteopetrosis, renal tubular acidosis, and cerebral calcification; erythrocyte carbonic anhydrase content/activity also rises in some anemias.
- Acetazolamide and other sulfonamides inhibit CA; inhibition alters renal acid handling and, in RBCs, CO2 handling and intracellular pH — the reason CA activity is relevant to the pH-sensitive steps of regulated red-cell death.
