## Overview

Neurotoxicity denotes the capacity of a chemical, drug, or endogenous metabolite to injure or kill neurons, disrupting central nervous system structure and function. Within this knowledge graph, neurotoxicity arises in two distinct contexts: (1) as a risk of **SIRT2 overactivity**, where excessive deacetylation impairs neuronal protein-homeostasis and microtubule defenses, and (2) as a hypothetical concern tied to **adrenochrome-derived oxidative pressure** in senotherapeutic strategies.

## Mechanism / Structure

> [!info] SIRT2 overactivity and neuronal damage
> Direct SIRT2 activation is generally avoided therapeutically because overactivity is linked to neurotoxicity in neurodegenerative contexts. Excessive SIRT2-dependent deacetylation may compromise microtubule stability and protein-homeostasis (proteostasis) networks that neurons depend on heavily. Consequently, **SIRT2 inhibitors** — rather than activators — are pursued for Parkinson's disease and Huntington's disease, contrasting with the activation-seeking strategy used for most other sirtuins such as [[SIRT1]] or [[SIRT3]].

This distinguishes SIRT2 from its siblings: whereas SIRT1/SIRT3 activation is broadly sought for metabolic and cardioprotective benefit, SIRT2 sits on the opposite side of the risk–benefit curve because of its neuronal liabilities.

## Role in Senotherapeutics

A separate, more speculative thread connects neurotoxicity to adrenochrome. A mechanistic hypothesis proposes adrenochrome or its derivatives as senotherapeutic agents that selectively stress or eliminate [[Senescent Cells]], which — with altered redox and detoxification capacity — may be differentially vulnerable to adrenochrome-derived oxidative pressure. Because adrenochrome and its aminochrome oxidation products are redox-active and can generate reactive species, their senotherapeutic use must be weighed against potential neurotoxic and oxidative side effects.

> [!warning] Speculative status
> The adrenochrome-as-senotherapeutic hypothesis is mechanistic and not yet clinically validated. Neurotoxicity remains a key safety consideration that would need to be excluded or managed before any such strategy advanced.

## Clinical & Research Significance

Neurotoxicity is a critical safety axis for any CNS-penetrant longevity or senotherapeutic intervention. The SIRT2 case illustrates a general principle: sirtuin-targeted therapies must be tuned by isoform, since pro-survival activation of one isoform can be harmful in another tissue context. Monitoring for oxidative and proteostatic neuronal damage is essential when repurposing redox-active catechols.
