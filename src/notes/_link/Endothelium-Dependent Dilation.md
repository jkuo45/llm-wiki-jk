# Endothelium-Dependent Dilation

**Endothelium-dependent dilation (EDD)** is the relaxation of a conduit artery in response to a physiological stimulus — increased flow or shear stress — that requires an intact vascular endothelium to release vasoactive mediators. It is the functional counterpart of the endothelial dysfunction that underlies hypertension, atherosclerosis and cardiovascular ageing.

## Mechanism

- **Nitric oxide (dominant pathway):** shear stress activates endothelial [[Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase|eNOS]] via [[Akt]]-dependent phosphorylation and [[Calcium]]-calmodulin; NO diffuses to adjacent smooth muscle, activates soluble guanylyl cyclase, raises cGMP and lowers smooth-muscle calcium, producing dilation. NO is inactivated by reactive oxygen species, so oxidative stress converts dilation to constriction.
- **Prostacyclin (PGI2):** arachidonic-acid metabolism through COX-2 contributes a vasodilatory and anti-platelet component.
- **EDHF (endothelium-derived hyperpolarizing factor):** a largely non-NO, non-prostaglandin pathway via potassium channels and myoendothelial gap junctions, which becomes proportionally more important when NO bioavailability is reduced.
- **Endothelium-independent dilation:** responses to pharmacological vasodilators (e.g. nitroglycerin) bypass the endothelium and are therefore used as the control in flow-mediated dilation (FMD) testing.

## Measurement & Clinical Relevance

- FMD of the brachial artery after reactive hyperaemia is the standard non-invasive readout; a lower FMD predicts cardiovascular events and tracks with arterial stiffness and left-ventricular mass.
- EDD declines with age, with hypertension, diabetes, smoking, and with elevated [[Homocysteine]] or low [[Nitric Oxide]] bioavailability — making EDD one of the earliest vascular markers of biological ageing.
- The NOS/NO axis couples EDD to exercise: shear-stress-driven NO release during repeated flow increases endothelial eNOS expression, which is one mechanism of the vascular benefit of aerobic training.
