# Energy Balance

**Energy Balance** is the equilibrium between metabolizable [[Energy Expenditure|energy intake]] and [[Energy Expenditure|energy expenditure]]. Because stored fat represents the difference between intake and output, chronic positive energy balance — intake exceeding expenditure — produces weight gain, whereas negative balance produces weight loss. Energy balance is regulated by interrelated adjustments of appetite, [[Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis|spontaneous activity]], and [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolism]] (Blundell _et al_. 2003).

## Components

- **Energy intake** — metabolizable energy from food; the heat increment of feeding ([[Thermic Effect of Food|thermic effect of food]]) is a fixed processing cost tied to intake.
- **Energy expenditure** — [[Resting Metabolic Rate|RMR]] (largest fraction), [[Thermic Effect of Food|thermic effect of food]], and physical-activity energy expenditure ([[Exercise|exercise]] + [[Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis|NEAT]]).
- **Adaptive components** — appetite suppression, behavioural NEAT changes, and resting-metabolism adjustments modify the net effect of a given exercise or dietary perturbation.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> Speakman & Selman quantify the energy-balance arithmetic of the obesity epidemic: if Western food intake has been stable or declined, then reduced physical activity (PAL ≈ 1·66) must have tipped the budget positive. They further show that exercise alone adds only ~5 % to weekly expenditure, so its weight-loss effects must be amplified through NEAT, appetite, and RMR — otherwise a small appetite adjustment would fully offset the exercise cost.

## Clinical Relevance

Most obesity management relies on tilting energy balance negative by increasing expenditure and/or reducing intake. The "amplifier" mechanisms (NEAT, EPOC, lean-mass-driven RMR) explain why exercise interventions outperform their modest direct energy cost, and why adaptive reductions in expenditure oppose long-term weight-loss maintenance.
