# MUC1

**MUC1** encodes a heavily glycosylated transmembrane mucin; its cytoplasmic tail fragment **MUC1-C** is an oncogenic signaling protein. In triple-negative [[Breast Cancer]] (TNBC), MUC1-C is highly expressed and, analogous to the [[SLC7A11]] (xCT) light chain of [[System Xc-]], it plays a key role in maintaining [[Glutathione]] (GSH) levels and redox balance, thereby suppressing [[Ferroptosis]].

## Structure & Biochemistry

MUC1 is a heterodimer of an extracellular mucin subunit and a transmembrane C-terminal subunit (MUC1-C) with a short cytoplasmic tail engaging numerous signaling partners (β-catenin, NF-κB, p53, etc.).

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review notes that MUC1-C is highly expressed in TNBC and, like xCT, helps maintain GSH and redox balance. [[MUC1|MUC1-C]] forms a complex with xCT and [[CD44]] variants (CD44v), interacting with xCT to control GSH levels. Inhibition of the MUC1-C/xCT signaling pathway induces ferroptosis in TNBC cells, killing tumor cells or reducing their self-renewal ability (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Breast Cancer").

> [!info] MUC1-C/xCT/CD44v axis
> This complex is a ferroptosis-resistance node in TNBC: disrupting it starves GSH synthesis and commits cells to ferroptosis—a druggable vulnerability distinct from hormonal/receptor targets absent in TNBC.

## Clinical Relevance

MUC1-C is a promising target in TNBC, where few targeted options exist. Blocking MUC1-C/xCT/CD44v to trigger ferroptosis complements system Xc- inhibition. It connects mucosal/''cancer'' biology to the core ferroptotic GSH axis.
