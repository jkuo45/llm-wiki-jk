# Hyaluronic Acid

**Hyaluronic acid** (hyaluronan) is a non-sulfated glycosaminoglycan composed of repeating glucuronic acid and N-acetylglucosamine disaccharides. It is a major component of the [[Extracellular Matrix]] in skin, where its high water-binding capacity underpins tissue hydration and turgor. Hyaluronic acid content declines with chronological aging and UV exposure, contributing to dryness and loss of firmness.

## Role in Skin and Wound Repair

- **Hydration and firmness.** HA binds large quantities of water, giving the dermis viscoelastic properties; it is a common topical cosmetic active, although topically applied HA primarily acts at the surface rather than replacing dermal HA.
- **[[Wound Healing]].** HA is abundant in early repair matrices and supports cell migration and proliferation through interactions with CD44 and RHAMM.
- **Aging readout.** Dermal HA immunostaining is used as an ECM-quality marker in reconstructed skin models.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Topical application of urolithin A activates mitophagy and enhances mitochondrial function to promote skin longevity|Topical urolithin A skin study]]
> In a full-thickness skin model reconstructed from an aged donor, 1 μM [[Urolithin A]] for 25 days significantly increased hyaluronic acid staining alongside [[Collagen|collagen III]], [[Elastin]], and collagen VII. The authors interpret the HA increase as improved dermal ECM quality and hydration-relevant matrix support.
