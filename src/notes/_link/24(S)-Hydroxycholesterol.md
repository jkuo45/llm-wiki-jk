# 24(S)-Hydroxycholesterol

**24(S)-Hydroxycholesterol** (24S-OHC) is the brain's principal cholesterol-clearance oxysterol, formed in neurons by CYP46A1 (cholesterol 24-hydroxylase). Unlike [[Cholesterol]] itself, it crosses the blood–brain barrier, coupling cerebral [[Cholesterol]] turnover to hepatic elimination, and it is an endogenous ligand of [[LXRα]]/LXRβ driving reverse-cholesterol-transport genes ([[ABCA1]], [[ABCG1]], [[ApoE]]). Rising CSF/plasma levels mark neuronal damage, and it is studied as a possible biomarker of [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]].

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Necroptosis a regulated inflammatory mode of cell death|Dhuriya & Sharma 2018]]
> 24S-OHC is biphasic in neurons: its ester form inhibits amyloid-β production at physiological concentration, while at high concentration it induces non-apoptotic programmed death in neuronal cells with low [[Caspase-8]] expression — so controlling its level may prevent onset of progressive neurodegeneration. This positions 24S-OHC at the [[Apoptosis]]/[[Necroptosis]] fate boundary set by [[Caspase-8]] availability.
