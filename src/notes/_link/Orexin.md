# Orexin

**Orexin** (also known as **hypocretin**) is a family of neuropeptides produced by a small cluster of neurons in the lateral and perifornical hypothalamus. It is best known for stabilizing wakefulness and arousal, and it also regulates feeding behavior, energy balance, reward, and thermogenesis. Orexin signals through two G-protein-coupled receptors: **OX1R (HCRTR1)** and **OX2R (HCRTR2)**.

## Biology & Signaling

- **Production:** prepro-orexin is cleaved into orexin-A and orexin-B; neurons project widely to the cortex, brainstem, and limbic system.
- **Receptors:** [[OX2R]] is enriched in the tuberomammillary nucleus and dorsomedial hypothalamus and is the primary receptor for wakefulness; loss of orexin neurons causes [[Narcolepsy]].
- **Metabolic role:** orexin neurons are glucose-sensing; orexin signaling promotes arousal-associated energy expenditure and physical activity.

## Link to Sirtuins and Longevity

- In the brain-specific [[SIRT1]] overexpression model (BRASTO), SIRT1 upregulates [[OX2R]] in the dorsomedial and lateral [[Hypothalamus]], increasing physical activity and extending median lifespan ~11% ([[_document_ - Sirtuins Guardians of Mammalian Healthspan|Sirtuins: Guardians of Mammalian Healthspan]], Giblin et al., 2014).
- Orexin-induced arousal and activity connect sleep–wake regulation to metabolic health, positioning the SIRT1–OX2R–orexin axis as a potential mediator of healthspan.
