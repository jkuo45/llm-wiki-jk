# Erythrocyte Senescence

**Erythrocyte senescence** is the slow, aging program of red blood cells over their ~120-day lifespan. It is a *clearance phenotype*, not a cell-death program: senescent cells are recognized over **days**, whereas [[Eryptosis]]-positive cells are engulfed within **minutes**.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Senescent erythrocytes show loss of surface area and volume, increased density, reduced deformability, cation loss, membrane desialylation, enzymatic dysregulation, oxidative stress, hemichrome accumulation, elevated glycated hemoglobin, reduced CD47, and an altered protein 4.1a/4.1b ratio. Aging-related conformational change of [[Anion Exchanger 1|AE1/Band 3]] creates a senescence-specific autoantigen. Senescent cells cannot show a senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), because they are terminally differentiated and cannot synthesize protein — which is why distinguishing senescence from eryptosis in RBCs is harder than distinguishing senescence from apoptosis in nucleated cells.

## Hallmarks vs eryptosis

| Feature | Senescence | [[Eryptosis]] |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clearance time | days | minutes |
| PS externalization | only late/oxidized | defining, with Ca2+ rise |
| Gardos channel / calpain | Ca2+-linked, not defining | defining execution |
| Trigger | chronological aging | energy depletion, ROS, osmotic shock, ceramide |

- Aged cells are nonetheless *more* susceptible to oxidation-induced eryptosis, so a senescent cell exposing PS should be scored as eryptotic rather than merely old.
- Desialylation is the classical senescent "eat-me" cue; neuraminidase treatment experimentally drives PS exposure, bridging the two programs.

## Clearance

- Immune route: germline-encoded naturally occurring IgG autoantibodies (NOAbs) bivalently bind neo-antigens on old cells — frequently [[Anion Exchanger 1|AE1]] — driving macrophage clearance.
- Histone route: extracellular histones promote erythrocyte aggregation, loss of deformability, splenic retention and lysis with ghost removal.
- [[CD47]] loss accelerates clearance by removing the "don't-eat-me" signal.
