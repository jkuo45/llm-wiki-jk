α-tubulin is one of the two subunits (with β-tubulin) that form the αβ-tubulin heterodimer, the building block of microtubules and the mitotic spindle. Its post-translational acetylation—classically at Lys40—is a well-established mark of stabilized, long-lived microtubules and is dynamically regulated by tubulin acetyltransferases (e.g., αTAT1) and deacetylases.

## Deacetylation by SIRT2

[[SIRT2|SIRT2]] is the predominantly cytoplasmic sirtuin and the major deacetylase of α-tubulin. By removing acetyl groups from α-tubulin, SIRT2 tunes microtubule dynamics, cytoskeleton organization, and mitotic fidelity. Its activity peaks at the G2/M transition, where α-tubulin deacetylation contributes to chromosome condensation and faithful chromosome segregation. Because an elevated SIRT2 level is observed in senescent (but not quiescent or apoptotic) cells, α-tubulin deacetylation by SIRT2 is part of the molecular signature that makes SIRT2 a useful cellular-senescence marker.

## Broader significance

SIRT2-dependent α-tubulin deacetylation also bears on neuronal health (microtubule stability in axons/myelinating glia) and on the balance between differentiation and proliferation, extending the functional reach of this cytoplasmic sirtuin beyond chromatin-based regulation.
