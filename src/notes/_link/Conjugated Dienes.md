# Conjugated Dienes

**Conjugated dienes** are products of [[Lipid Peroxidation]] formed when polyunsaturated fatty acids in lipoproteins (especially [[LDL]] and [[HDL]]) are oxidized. They are measured by monitoring absorbance at 234 nm and serve as early markers of oxidative modification.

## Formation

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Conjugated diene formation in the lipid fraction is measured to determine the extent of LDL peroxidation. The kinetic profile of oxidation is characterized by an initial lag phase followed by a propagation phase (where the rate of conjugated diene formation is maximal) and then a decomposition phase. Resveratrol (0–1 µM) inhibits conjugated diene formation, extending the lag phase and reducing V_max.

- Conjugated dienes are early products of lipid peroxidation in lipoproteins
- They are formed during copper- or radical-induced oxidation of LDL and HDL
- Formation is measured at 234 nm (extinction coefficient ε234 nm = 27,000 M⁻¹/cm)

## Kinetic Parameters

- **Lag phase**: Initial period before appreciable peroxidation; extended by antioxidants
- **V_max**: Maximum rate of conjugated diene formation (propagation phase); reduced by resveratrol
- **OD_max**: Maximum absorbance; unaffected by resveratrol except at high concentrations

## Clinical Relevance

- Conjugated dienes are markers of [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Lipid Peroxidation]]
- Increased conjugated dienes in LDL correlate with [[Atherosclerosis]] progression
- HDL oxidation (measured by conjugated dienes) decreases HDL's capacity to mediate [[Cholesterol Efflux]]
