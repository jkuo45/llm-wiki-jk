# Translocase of the Outer Mitochondrial Membrane

The translocase of the outer mitochondrial membrane (TOM complex) is the principal entry gate for nuclear-encoded proteins targeted to mitochondria. It recognizes [[Mitochondrial Targeting Sequence|mitochondrial targeting sequences]] and conducts precursor proteins across the [[Mitochondria|outer mitochondrial membrane]] into the intermembrane space, handing them to the [[Translocase of the Inner Mitochondrial Membrane]] for further import.

According to [[PINK1]], PINK1 is imported through the TOM (and TIM) complexes. Under healthy conditions this import is completed, allowing PINK1 to be processed by [[Mitochondrial Processing Peptidase]] and [[PARL]] and degraded, preventing mitophagy activation. When membrane potential is lost, import via TOM stalls, and PINK1 accumulates on the outer membrane to recruit [[Parkin]] and trigger [[Mitophagy]].

The TOM complex is therefore central to the import-versus-signal decision that controls PINK1-dependent mitochondrial quality control.
