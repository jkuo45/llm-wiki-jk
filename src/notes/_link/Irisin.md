# Irisin

**Irisin** is a proteolytically cleaved, secreted peptide derived from the type I membrane protein fibronectin type III domain-containing protein 5 (FNDC5). It is released from [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] during exercise and from adipose tissue and other sources, and was first characterized for its ability to induce browning of white adipose tissue via [[UCP1|UCP1]] expression, thereby increasing energy expenditure. Beyond thermogenesis, irisin exerts pleiotropic effects on bone metabolism (promoting osteoblast differentiation and inhibiting osteoclastogenesis), neuronal function, and cellular redox homeostasis.

## SIRT3 dependence in diabetic periodontitis

Under conditions of hyperlipidemia and inflammation, irisin exerts antioxidative and osteoprotective effects in diabetic [[Periodontitis|periodontitis]] models, and these actions require [[SIRT3]]. Lentivirus-mediated SIRT3 silencing abolishes irisin's osteoprotective efficacy against osteoclastic alveolar bone resorption and [[ROS|ROS]] overproduction, establishing SIRT3 as the obligatory signaling node for irisin-mediated redox homeostasis and bone preservation[240],[248]. The axis therefore connects exercise biology to mitochondrial deacetylase function in the diabetic bone microenvironment.

## Clinical relevance

Because irisin couples physical activity to metabolic, neuronal, and skeletal benefits, its SIRT3 dependence identifies SIRT3 activation as a pharmacological strategy to recapitulate or amplify irisin's redox and osteoprotective effects in metabolically compromised bone disease.
