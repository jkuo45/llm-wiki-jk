# Irisin

**Irisin** is a proteolytically cleaved, secreted peptide derived from the type I membrane protein [[FNDC5]]. It is released from [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] during exercise and also produced by [[Adipose Tissue|adipose tissue]], brain, and heart. First characterized in 2012 for its ability to drive browning of white adipose tissue via [[UCP1|UCP1]] expression and thermogenesis (Böström et al. 2012, *Nature*), irisin has since been recognized as a pleiotropic exercise hormone with effects spanning metabolism, bone remodeling, neuroprotection, cardiovascular health, and redox homeostasis. Over 2,000 studies have been published on irisin since its discovery.

## Discovery and the PGC-1α–FNDC5–Irisin Axis

Irisin was identified through a screen for [[PGC-1α|PGC-1α]]-responsive genes in [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]]. Forced expression of PGC-1α in mouse muscle or endurance exercise both induced the transmembrane precursor protein FNDC5. The extracellular ectodomain of FNDC5 is proteolytically cleaved and released into the circulation as irisin (~12 kDa, 112 amino acids in mice; ~14 kDa glycosylated). Irisin was shown to induce a brown-fat-like gene program in subcutaneous white adipose tissue, increase total energy expenditure, protect against diet-induced obesity, and improve glucose tolerance in mice (Böström et al. 2012).

> [!important] Exercise–irisin–browning axis
> The pathway from exercise to metabolic benefit runs: contractile activity → [[PGC-1α]] upregulation → [[FNDC5]] transcription → irisin cleavage and secretion → [[UCP1]] induction in white adipose tissue → thermogenesis and increased energy expenditure. This axis represents a core mechanism by which physical activity combats obesity and metabolic syndrome.

## Structure, Biosynthesis, and Secretion

### FNDC5 Precursor

[[FNDC5]] is a type I transmembrane protein (209 amino acids in mice; 209 in humans) comprising:
- **Signal peptide** (aa 1–28) — directs ER translocation and is co-translationally cleaved
- **Fibronectin type III (FNIII) domain** (aa 29–140) — corresponds to the irisin domain
- **Linker region** (aa 141–169)
- **Transmembrane segment** (aa 170–188)
- **Cytoplasmic tail** (aa 189–209)

FNDC5 is expressed in skeletal muscle, heart, brain, adipose tissue, and liver. In skeletal muscle, exercise and [[PGC-1α]] activation are the primary transcriptional drivers. [[AMPK]] and [[p38 MAPK]] signaling upstream of PGC-1α mediate exercise-induced FNDC5 upregulation.

### Proteolytic Cleavage

The protease (sheddase) responsible for cleaving the FNDC5 ectodomain to release irisin has not been definitively identified. Cathepsin B has been proposed as a candidate, but the precise cleavage site and enzymatic machinery remain areas of active investigation. After cleavage, irisin enters the bloodstream and acts as an endocrine signal on distant tissues.

### Glycosylation and Molecular Weight

Irisin has two N-linked glycosylation sites. Nonglycosylated recombinant irisin (produced in *E. coli*) runs at ~13 kDa on SDS-PAGE, while glycosylated irisin (produced in mammalian cells) runs as a broad band at ~18–25 kDa with a peak near 20 kDa. Complete deglycosylation requires a cocktail of enzymes (PNGase F plus O-glycosidase, neuraminidase, β1-4 galactosidase, and β-N-acetylglucosaminidase), not PNGase F alone (Jedrychowski et al. 2015).

> [!warning] The ATA start codon debate
> The human *FNDC5* gene carries an ATA (instead of ATG) at the annotated translation start site, leading early critics to argue that human FNDC5 is a non-coding pseudogene (Raschke et al. 2013; Albrecht et al. 2015). However, a 2024 study identified a conserved upstream start codon (cATG) supported by ribosome profiling data, demonstrating that FNDC5 is translated primarily from this cATG in both humans and mice. The N-terminal extension is co-translationally cleaved by the signal peptide, yielding a protein of similar size to the downstream-start product. This finding effectively resolves the "pseudogene" concern, though quantitative translation efficiency in vivo remains under investigation (Kurgan et al. 2024).

## Receptor and Signaling

### Integrin αVβ5 as the Irisin Receptor

Irisin binds to integrin receptors of the αV class, with integrin [[Integrin|αVβ5]] identified as the primary high-affinity receptor (Perakakis et al. 2018, *Cell*). Biophysical studies mapped the interacting surfaces between irisin and the αVβ5 integrin heterodimer. Chemical inhibition of αV integrins blocks irisin's signaling and function in both osteocytes and adipocytes.

### Two-Step Activation Mechanism

A 2023 cryo-EM study revealed a two-step activation mechanism: extracellular heat shock protein 90α (eHsp90α), which is secreted by muscle during exercise, primes integrin αVβ5 and enables high-affinity irisin binding and signaling through an Hsp90α/αVβ5 ternary complex (Liu et al. 2023, *Molecular Cell*). This explains why irisin efficacy may depend on the co-secretion of eHsp90α during exercise.

### Downstream Signaling Pathways

Irisin activates multiple intracellular pathways depending on cell type:
- **p38 MAPK and ERK** — drive UCP1 induction and adipocyte browning
- **PI3K/Akt** — promotes glucose uptake, osteoblast survival, and cell proliferation
- **AMPK/mTOR** — regulates glucose metabolism and autophagy
- **FAK/eNOS** — mediates endothelial NO production and BDNF upregulation in hippocampus
- **JNK, Wnt/β-catenin, RANKL/RANK/OPG** — regulate osteoclast and osteoblast differentiation
- **PI3K/Akt–Snail–EMT** — modulates cancer cell migration and invasion

## Physiological Functions

### 1. Browning of White Adipose Tissue and Thermogenesis

Irisin's founding function: induction of UCP1 and the thermogenic gene program in subcutaneous white adipose tissue (iWAT), converting energy-storing white adipocytes into energy-dissipating beige adipocytes. In mice, adenoviral overexpression of FNDC5 increased energy expenditure, reduced body weight, and improved glucose tolerance on a high-fat diet. Cold exposure, a potent browning stimulus, also induces FNDC5/irisin in muscle.

> [!info] IL-33–ST2 axis in browning
> A 2026 study in *Nature Metabolism* demonstrated that chronic irisin treatment in obese mice promotes thermogenesis through an IL-33–ST2 axis in white adipose tissue, operating largely through ST2+ regulatory T cells. This reduces fat content without loss of muscle mass and improves glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity (Nature Metabolism 2026). This newly identified mechanism adds an immunological dimension to irisin's metabolic effects.

### 2. Glucose Metabolism and Insulin Sensitivity

Irisin improves glucose homeostasis through multiple mechanisms:
- **Direct glucose uptake** — activates AMPK-dependent GLUT4 translocation in skeletal muscle cells
- **Insulin signaling** — upregulates PI3K/AKT pathway and enhances insulin receptor substrate function
- **Hepatic effects** — reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis
- **Adipokine modulation** — increases adiponectin and improves adipose tissue insulin sensitivity

Clinical data are complex: circulating irisin is often lower in established type 2 diabetes compared to controls or prediabetic individuals, and is inversely correlated with HOMA-IR, fasting glucose, and HbA1c. Some studies interpret this as a compensatory response — irisin rises in early metabolic stress and declines as PGC-1α activation fails. Pharmacological agents including [[Metformin]], sitagliptin, exenatide (GLP-1 agonist), and omega-3 PUFAs have been shown to elevate circulating irisin levels in T2DM patients, potentially contributing to their metabolic benefits.

### 3. Bone Metabolism

Irisin plays a dual role in bone biology, acting on all major cell types of the bone remodeling unit:

**Osteoblast effects (pro-anabolic):**
- Promotes osteoblast proliferation, differentiation, and mineralization via ERK, p38 MAPK, and AMPK pathways
- Enhances Wnt/β-catenin signaling in bone marrow stromal cells, promoting osteogenic over adipogenic lineage commitment
- In aging models, irisin restores Wnt/β-catenin activity in senescent bone marrow stromal cells, reducing marrow fat deposition and attenuating bone loss
- Prevents and restores bone loss in hindlimb unloading models (Colaianni et al. 2017)

**Osteoclast effects (complex):**
- Some studies show irisin inhibits osteoclastogenesis via RANKL/RANK/OPG regulation
- Conversely, a *eLife* study (Giro et al. 2020) demonstrated that irisin directly stimulates osteoclast progenitor differentiation and bone resorption in vitro and in vivo, upregulating "clastokines" and increasing bone turnover. *FNDC5*-transgenic mice showed lower bone mass at multiple ages
- Dose, duration, and species context likely determine whether irisin favors formation or resorption — a critical unresolved question

**SIRT3-dependent osteoprotection:** In diabetic [[Periodontitis|periodontitis]] models, irisin's osteoprotective and antioxidative effects require [[SIRT3]]. Lentivirus-mediated SIRT3 silencing abolishes irisin's protection against osteoclastic alveolar bone resorption and [[ROS|ROS]] overproduction, establishing SIRT3 as the obligatory signaling node for irisin-mediated redox homeostasis in the diabetic bone microenvironment (Li et al. 2023).

### 4. Neuroprotection and Cognitive Function

Irisin crosses the blood-brain barrier (mechanism under investigation) and exerts potent neurotrophic effects:

- **BDNF upregulation** — irisin induces [[BDNF]] expression in the hippocampus through a mechanism dependent on endothelial αVβ5 integrins and the FAK/eNOS signaling pathway. Exercise-induced FNDC5 in the hippocampus stimulates BDNF and mediators of hippocampal function including Npas4, c-Fos, and Arc (Wrann et al. 2013)
- **Amyloid-β clearance** — irisin promotes the release of eHsp90α, which activates microglial phagocytosis of amyloid-β. This represents a cellular and molecular mechanism by which exercise-induced irisin attenuates Aβ pathology in Alzheimer's disease (Lourenco et al. 2023, *Neuron*)
- **Hippocampal neurogenesis** — recombinant irisin stimulates hippocampal cell proliferation by 70–80% and may act through the STAT3 signaling pathway via the motor–irisin–BDNF axis
- **Cognitive improvement** — irisin treatment improves spatial learning and memory in APP/PS1 Alzheimer's disease mouse models, associated with increased BDNF and reduced Aβ burden

> [!tip] Clinical significance
> Decreased circulating irisin has been associated with cognitive decline in aging populations and AD patients. Irisin's ability to simultaneously enhance BDNF, promote Aβ clearance, and support neurogenesis positions it as a key molecular mediator of exercise-induced neuroprotection and a potential therapeutic target for Alzheimer's disease.

### 5. Cardiovascular Protection

Irisin exerts broad cardiovascular effects:
- **Endothelial function** — stimulates NO production via AMPK-eNOS pathway, promoting vasodilation and inhibiting atherosclerotic plaque formation
- **Anti-inflammatory** — suppresses TNF-α, IL-6, VCAM-1, ICAM-1, and E-selectin expression in endothelial cells, reducing leukocyte adhesion and vascular inflammation
- **Anti-atherosclerotic** — reduces lipid deposition in arterial walls and retards plaque progression in ApoE-deficient mice; aerobic exercise-induced irisin secretion is associated with improved endothelial function and reduced atherosclerosis
- **Anti-hypertensive** — promotes endothelium-dependent vasorelaxation and reduces vascular resistance
- **Cardiac protection** — improves left ventricular ejection fraction and reduces cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis in heart failure models; protects cardiomyocytes from ischemia-reperfusion injury by inhibiting mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening
- **Vascular calcification** — inhibits VSMC osteoblastic transformation via AMPK/Drp1 signaling and modulates the FNDC5/irisin–DnaJ/Hsp40–SIRT6 axis to suppress vascular smooth muscle cell senescence

### 6. Anti-Inflammatory and Antioxidant Effects

Irisin has intrinsic anti-inflammatory and redox-modulating properties:
- Inhibits NF-κB signaling and NLRP3 inflammasome activation
- Increases antioxidant enzyme activity (SOD, catalase)
- Reduces ROS production in multiple cell types
- In the diabetic context, its redox effects are partially mediated through SIRT3-dependent pathways

## Measurement Controversy and Assay Limitations

> [!warning] Major methodological caveat
> The irisin field has been significantly complicated by measurement issues. Commercial ELISA kits based on polyclonal antibodies show extensive cross-reactivity with non-specific serum proteins. Albrecht et al. (2015) demonstrated that four commercial polyclonal antibodies all showed prominent cross-reactivity, and no immune-reactive band of the expected size was detected in human or animal sera by Western blot. Reported irisin levels span orders of magnitude across studies (50 pg/mL to >10 μg/mL), and even comparisons of the same samples with different ELISAs or different lots of the same kit show significant discrepancies.

**Mass spectrometry** (Jedrychowski et al. 2015, *Cell Metabolism*) is considered the gold standard: targeted proteomics with AQUA (absolute quantification) heavy peptides identified human irisin at ~3.6 ng/mL in sedentary individuals, increasing to ~4.3 ng/mL with aerobic interval training. This study used proper deglycosylation and confirmed that human irisin is mainly translated from the non-canonical ATA start codon (now understood to be downstream of the cATG; see above).

**FNDC5 knockout mice** provide the most reliable functional data. Xiong et al. (2019) showed that *Fndc5* loss-of-function attenuates exercise-induced browning of white adipose tissue in mice, providing genetic evidence that FNDC5/irisin is dispensable for muscle development and basal metabolism but essential for exercise-induced metabolic benefits. *FNDC5*-transgenic mice overexpressing irisin show altered bone mass, supporting irisin's direct role in bone remodeling.

**Current consensus:** Recombinant irisin treatment and genetic models (overexpression/knockout) produce consistent, reproducible results supporting irisin's biological functions. The major uncertainty remains in correlating endogenous circulating levels measured by ELISA with physiological states. Newer immunoassays and mass spectrometry approaches are gradually resolving this, and the recent resolution of the cATG translation start site strengthens the case for irisin as a genuine human hormone.

## Clinical Potential and Therapeutic Implications

- **Exercise mimetic** — irisin mediates many benefits of exercise; pharmacological FNDC5/irisin activation could benefit individuals unable to exercise
- **Obesity and T2DM** — irisin reduces fat mass and improves insulin sensitivity; combination with GLP-1 agonists or metformin may be synergistic
- **Osteoporosis** — exogenous irisin prevents bone loss in animal models; SIRT3 activation could amplify irisin's osteoprotective effects
- **Alzheimer's disease** — irisin-enhanced BDNF and Aβ clearance suggest disease-modifying potential
- **Cardiovascular disease** — endothelial protection and anti-atherosclerotic effects position irisin as a candidate for cardiometabolic risk reduction
- **NAD+ precursors** — NMN and NR supplementation upregulate FNDC5/irisin expression, potentially mediating some metabolic benefits of NAD+ boosting therapy
