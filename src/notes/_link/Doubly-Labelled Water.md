# Doubly-Labelled Water

**Doubly-Labelled Water (DLW)** is a stable-isotope technique that measures the total [[Energy Expenditure|daily energy expenditure]] (DEE) of free-living subjects over days to weeks. The subject drinks water labelled with the stable isotopes ²H (deuterium) and ¹⁸O. ²H is eliminated only as water, while ¹⁸O is eliminated both as water and as CO₂ (via rapid isotopic equilibration between body water and bicarbonate). The difference between the two elimination rates therefore reports CO₂ production, from which energy expenditure is calculated using the respiratory quotient (Schoeller _et al_. 1986).

## Advantages and Validation

- Non-invasive and does not interfere with normal behaviour, making it the criterion method for free-living energy expenditure in humans and animals.
- Validated against direct and indirect calorimetry in humans (Schoeller _et al_. 1986; Speakman, 1997).
- Deployed in classic comparative energetics datasets (Black _et al_. 1996) establishing a DEE:[[Resting Metabolic Rate|RMR]] ratio (~1·66) for Western adults.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> Speakman & Selman combine DLW with respirometry to separate flight costs from post-flight [[Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption|EPOC]] in bats, and use DLW-derived DEE:RMR values to argue that modern humans expend far less energy than allometric predictions for their body size.

## Applications

- Establishing physical activity levels ([[Physical Activity Level|PAL]] = DEE:RMR) in human populations and wild animals (Black _et al_. 1996; Nagy _et al_. 1999; Speakman, 2000).
- Measuring the energetic cost of flight in bats and birds (Speakman & Racey, 1991).
- Validating energy-intake and body-composition approaches to energy balance in obesity research.
