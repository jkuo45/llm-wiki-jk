# Platelet

A **platelet** is a 2–3 µm, anucleate cytoplasmic fragment released from bone-marrow
megakaryocytes, with a lifespan of roughly 8–10 days. Platelets are not inert
particles: they retain mitochondria, residual mRNA and translation capacity, three
types of secretory granule, and a surface of ~60 distinct receptors, enough to make
them metabolically active signalling cells. Their sole physiological function is to
seal a breach in the vasculature in under a minute and then to be cleared.

This note covers the cell itself; the process it performs is described separately in
[[Platelet aggregation]], and the multisystem view in [[Platelets]].

## Adhesion

The initiating event is exposure of subendothelial [[Collagen]] and platelet-activating
factor at a site of injury. Plasmatic [[von Willebrand Factor]] binds collagen, and
its A1 domain then engages platelet glycoprotein Ib (the GPIb-IX-V complex). Because
GPIb is tethered to the actin cytoskeleton through its filaggrin-binding cytoplasmic
tail, mechanical force on the vWF-GPIb bond is transduced directly into inside-out
signalling: the vWF A1 domain spontaneously switches conformation under shear,
unmasking the binding site, and platelet signalling is initiated without any soluble
agonist.

> [!info] Mechanism
> Kroll et al. (1991) showed that vWF binding to GPIb drives breakdown of PIP2,
> production of phosphatidic acid, [[Protein Kinase C|PKC]] activation, a rise in
> cytosolic calcium and thromboxane synthesis — and that none of this was blocked by
> anti-GPIIb/IIIa antibody, so adhesion signalling and aggregation signalling are
> mechanistically separate.

## Activation and the positive-feedback loops

Adhesion is amplified by three autocatalytic loops, all of which recruit further
platelets:

- **Thromboxane A2.** Newly synthesised from arachidonic acid via platelet
  [[Cyclooxygenase]]-1, acting on TP receptors on neighbouring platelets. This is the
  step irreversibly blocked by low-dose [[Aspirin]].
- **ADP.** Released from dense granules, acting on P2Y1 and P2Y12 receptors; P2Y12 is
  the target of clopidogrel, prasugrel and ticagrelor.
- **Thrombin.** Generated by the coagulation cascade, acting on PAR1 and PAR4.
  Thrombin is the most potent platelet activator known, which is what couples primary
  to secondary haemostasis.

Dense granules also release [[Serotonin]], which is taken up by the plasma membrane
5-HT transporter, amplifies vasoconstriction, and maintains the platelet store;
α-granules release vWF, [[Fibrinogen]], [[Platelet-Derived Growth Factor]] and
endostatin. Dense bodies are lysosome-like, rich in ATP, ADP, serotonin and calcium.

## Aggregation

All three loops converge on a single step: the conformational activation of integrin
αIIbβ3 (GPIIb/IIIa), which binds soluble fibrinogen bridging two adjacent platelets.
Weak agonists such as ADP only activate the surface pool; stronger agonists such as
thrombin and collagen additionally externalise the internal α-granule pool, raising
surface receptor density by as much as 50%. The sufficiency of this step was
established by [[Glanzmann thrombasthenia]] — loss of αIIbβ3 gives bleeding with
absent aggregation despite entirely normal activation.

## Procoagulant surface

Activated platelets expose phosphatidylserine, which binds factor Va and supports
assembly of the [[Prothrombinase Complex]], accelerating conversion of [[Prothrombin]]
to [[Thrombin]] roughly a thousand-fold. This is a positive-feedback loop: thrombin
recruits and activates more platelets. Platelets also carry CD63 and P-selectin on the
surface, linking coagulation to leukocyte recruitment and to complement.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Platelets are Janus-faced. The same reactions that stop bleeding drive occlusive
> arterial thrombosis, and dual antiplatelet therapy buys its mortality benefit at the
> cost of bleeding. P-selectin-driven leukocyte interaction means platelet activation
> is also a systemic inflammatory stimulus, which is why platelet hyper-reactivity is
> being pursued as a target in inflammatory and atherosclerotic disease.

## Clinical correlates

Quantitative or functional platelet defects present as mucocutaneous bleeding. The
degree of impairment separates the diagnoses: [[Thrombocytopenia]] gives petechiae
and bruising with prolonged bleeding time but normal function in the remaining
platelets, whereas [[Glanzmann thrombasthenia]] gives a normal count with absent
aggregation. [[von Willebrand Disease]] is the commonest inherited bleeding disorder
and is a defect of adhesion, not of the platelet. Heparin-associated
[[Thrombocytopenia]] is the paradoxical exception — a low count with a strongly
prothrombotic state.

## Documents

- [[Serum]] — serum is prepared by letting whole blood clot, so its composition is
  defined by what platelets release on activation; platelet-derived growth factors and
  dense-granule mediators are part of why serum is preferred for some assays and
  platelet-poor plasma for others.
- [[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate]] — cGMP is the brake on platelet activation, via
  protein kinase G inhibition of calcium mobilisation; this is the target of PDE
  inhibitors used as antiplatelet agents in some settings.

## Connections

- [[Platelet aggregation]] — the terminal, αIIbβ3-dependent step that the platelet's
  activation state exists to enable; every agonist converges on it, which is why
  αIIb/IIIa antagonists (abciximab, eptifibatide, tirofiban) block aggregation
  regardless of which receptor initiated it.
- [[Thrombin]] — the most potent platelet activator, acting through PAR1/PAR4, and
  itself a product of the platelet surface; the thrombin–platelet loop is the
  mechanistic link between primary and secondary haemostasis.
- [[Thromboxane A₂]] — the COX-1-derived autocatalytic loop that recruitment depends
  on, and the one aspirin irreversibly removes for the platelet's whole 8–10 day life.
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — platelet adhesion and activation at a disrupted plaque cap is
  the proximate event of myocardial infarction and ischaemic stroke, which is the
  entire rationale for antiplatelet therapy in secondary prevention.
- [[Serum]] — since serum is the product of clot formation, its content of
  platelet-derived mediators is inseparable from platelet biology, and the choice
  between serum and platelet-poor plasma for a given analyte follows from it.
- [[Endothelial Cells]] — endothelium and platelets are opposing arms of primary
  haemostasis: intact endothelium produces nitric oxide and prostacyclin to suppress
  platelet activation, and its loss is what permits it.
- [[Thrombocytopenia]] — the count-level counterpart to platelet function; a low
  count with normal function bleeds differently from a normal count with defective
  function, which is how the two are told apart clinically.
