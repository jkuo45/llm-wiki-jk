# Opportunistic infection

An **opportunistic infection** is a disease caused by a bacterium, fungus, virus, or parasite that ordinarily does not cause illness in a healthy host, but which becomes pathogenic when the host's defences are impaired. The term is functional rather than taxonomic: the same pathogen can be opportunistic in one host and fully pathogenic in another. It is worth being explicit that "opportunistic" describes the *host state*, not a fixed property of the organism — and that the label says nothing about severity or treatability.

## Thresholds and Risk

The classical organising idea is a **threshold dependent on the specific immune deficit**. In HIV the thresholds are well documented: below **200 CD4 cells/µL**, *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia and toxoplasmic encephalitis become likely; below **100 cells/µL**, cryptococcosis, CMV retinitis, and disseminated MAC; below **50 cells/µL**, CMV and disseminated *Mycobacterium avium* complex. Risk rises steeply in the weeks to months after CD4 falls below each threshold and falls again promptly with effective therapy.

> [!info] The threshold is specific, not general
> The CD4 cut-offs are useful precisely because they are *organism-specific*: 200 cells/µL protects against PJP and not against CMV, which needs a far deeper deficit. Collapsing these into a single "immunocompromised" number has caused both over- and under-treatment.

## The expanding causes

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The epidemiology has shifted substantially. Advanced HIV remains the paradigm, but the majority of new opportunistic infections in high-income settings now occur in people who are **not** HIV-positive: transplant recipients, patients on high-dose [[Glucocorticoids]] or [[Calcineurin]] inhibitors, oncology patients with neutropenia, people on biologics such as anti-TNF agents and natalizumab, and — increasingly and most importantly — **older people with [[Aging|age-related]] immune dysfunction**. The common thread is impairment of cell-mediated immunity, but the degree and the specific arm of immunity impaired determine which pathogen gets through.

The age link deserves emphasis because it is the least obvious. Immunosenescence is a low-grade, chronic, T-cell-centred impairment: reduced naïve T-cell output from the thymus, accumulation of late-differentiated memory and exhausted T cells, reduced proliferative capacity, and a blunted response to new antigens. This is the immune substrate of [[Inflammaging]] — simultaneously too little new anti-pathogen response and too much chronic inflammatory output. An older adult is therefore simultaneously more susceptible to new infection and more prone to inflammatory damage from the infections they do acquire.

## The specific pathogens

Grouped by the immune function that fails:

- **Cell-mediated immunity defect** — *Pneumocystis jirovecii*, *Toxoplasma gondii*, *Cryptococcus neoformans*, CMV, HSV, VZV, *Mycobacterium* (both TB and MAC), *Histoplasma*, *Talaromyces*, *Nocardia*
- **Neutrophil/number defect** — bacterial and fungal infections, invasive aspergillosis, and the febrile neutropenia syndromes of oncology care
- **Barrier/colonisation defect** — *Clostridioides difficile* colitis, candidiasis, *Pneumocystis*, and inborn errors of immunity generally
- **Complement and antibody defects** — encapsulated bacteria, principally *Neisseria*

## Clinical Significance

> [!warning] Diagnostic caveat
> The practical difficulty is that OIs present non-specifically: fever, malaise, breathlessness, and raised inflammatory markers overlap heavily with each other and with the underlying condition's own symptoms. In advanced HIV, an unexplained fever or a focal neurological sign is an OI until proven otherwise. In the transplant and oncology settings the diagnosis is made empirically early, at the cost of overtreating colonisation — notably *Aspergillus* in the air and *Pneumocystis* carriage, where a positive PCR without compatible disease is not by itself an indication to treat.

**Secondary prevention.** The largest single intervention in OI mortality is not the treatment of the infection but the restoration of immune function plus prophylaxis: TMP-SMX prevents the majority of PJP cases, azithromycin prevents MAC, and fluconazole prevents cryptococcosis. In HIV this is now largely obsolete in the era of effective [[HIV|ART]], which is the clearest demonstration that the immune deficit, not the pathogen, is the primary problem.

**Interaction with ageing.** OIs are a leading cause of functional decline and mortality in older adults, independent of the pathogen load itself. The vulnerability of an ageing immune system is a leading and underappreciated contributor to the mortality and frailty gradient in this vault's longevity literature.

## Documents

- [[HIV-1]] — the paradigm document for this concept. The defining feature of AIDS is not any single pathogen but the susceptibility to this whole class of infections, and the CD4 threshold framework above is derived from the HIV literature.

## Connections

- [[HIV-1]] — HIV provides the best-characterised clinical scale of opportunistic disease, the CD4 threshold framework, and the historical demonstration that restoring immunity (ART) is what removes the risk, not treating each infection. It is also the origin of the term's modern clinical use.
- [[AIDS]] — the AIDS-defining illnesses are a defined subset of opportunistic infections plus specific malignancies; the relationship is definitional rather than causal.
- [[Immune System]] — the entire concept is defined by a gap in this system. Every immune arm that fails determines which pathogens appear, which is why "opportunistic infection" is really a set of several distinct syndromes that happen to share a name.
- [[Aging]] — age-related immune decline, [[Immunosenescence]], is now one of the leading non-HIV drivers of opportunistic disease. This is the connection most worth building out, because it connects OI directly to the longevity framing the rest of the vault is organised around.
- [[Immunosenescence]] — the specific mechanism by which ageing impairs cell-mediated immunity: thymic involution, T-cell repertoire narrowing, reduced proliferative response. The immunosenescence–infection link is well documented and under-used as evidence for the geroscience argument.
- [[Inflammaging]] — the same age-related immune change produces the opposite phenotype in chronic inflammation. Having both impaired pathogen clearance and elevated baseline inflammatory tone is the paradox that makes immunosenescence a geroscience target rather than merely an infectious-disease problem.
- [[Chronic Granulomatous Disease]] — a congenital phagocyte defect that produces a characteristic, restricted opportunistic infection profile. It is a clean demonstration that the pattern of infection maps onto the specific pathway lost.
- [[Glucocorticoids]] — a leading iatrogenic cause of OIs through T-cell suppression, and a model for the iatrogenic category that has become central to modern OI epidemiology.
- [[Microglia]] — the brain-resident phagocytes; the opportunistic infections with CNS involvement (toxoplasmic encephalitis, cryptococcal meningitis, PML) all depend on the integrity of CNS immune surveillance, making this the direct neurological connection.
- [[SASP]] — senescent cells secrete an inflammatory SASP that alters the tissue environment around infections. Combined with impaired pathogen clearance, this is the mechanistic bridge between cellular senescence and infection susceptibility in the elderly.
