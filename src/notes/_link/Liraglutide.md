# Liraglutide

## Overview

Liraglutide is a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist used clinically for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and obesity. Beyond its metabolic effects, liraglutide exerts cardioprotective actions by promoting [[Mitophagy]], which reduces cardiac fibrosis, inflammatory responses, and myocardial cell death.

## Mechanism of Action

Liraglutide acts through several pathways:

- **GLP-1 receptor activation** — Enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppresses glucagon release, and promotes satiety
- **Mitophagy promotion** — Stimulates selective mitochondrial clearance through mitophagy, removing damaged mitochondria
- **Anti-fibrotic effects** — Reduces cardiac fibrosis through modulation of TGF-β signaling and extracellular matrix remodeling

## Therapeutic Applications

Liraglutide is used for:

- **Type 2 diabetes** — Improves glycemic control through insulin secretion enhancement
- **Obesity** — Reduces appetite and promotes weight loss
- **Cardiovascular protection** — Reduces major adverse cardiovascular events in high-risk patients

## Cardiac Benefits

Liraglutide's cardioprotective effects include:

- **Reduced cardiac fibrosis** — Promotes mitophagy to clear damaged mitochondria that drive fibrotic signaling
- **Anti-inflammatory effects** — Reduces myocardial inflammatory responses
- **Myocardial protection** — Prevents cardiomyocyte death through improved mitochondrial quality control

## Relationship to Mitophagy

Liraglutide promotes mitophagy as a key mechanism of cardiac protection:

- Enhances clearance of dysfunctional mitochondria
- Reduces mitochondrial ROS production that drives fibrosis
- Maintains mitochondrial quality in cardiomyocytes under stress
