# ROCK

**ROCK** (Rho-associated coiled-coil containing protein kinase) is the family of serine/threonine kinases — **ROCK1** and **ROCK2**, 130–138 kDa homologues with ~90% identity — that serve as the principal downstream effectors of [[RhoA]]. ROCK converts the RhoA GTPase switch into cellular force generation by phosphorylating myosin light chain and, indirectly, actin-regulatory substrates.

> [!info] The effector arm of Rho signalling
> Where RhoA decides *that* the cytoskeleton should be reorganised, ROCK supplies the mechanism. It is activated by binding GTP-loaded RhoA, which relieves ROCK's autoinhibitory pseudokeeper loop–N-terminal helix interaction and exposes the kinase domain. Both isoforms are widely expressed, with ROCK1 relatively enriched in non-neural tissues and ROCK2 higher in the central nervous system and in proliferative cells.

## Substrates and effects

Direct and indirect substrates converge on three outputs:

- **Myosin activation.** ROCK phosphorylates myosin light chain at Ser19/Thr18 and relieves the myosin phosphatase (MYPT1) brake by phosphorylating its regulatory subunit, producing sustained myosin activation and therefore contractility. ROCK can also phosphorylate and inhibit MYPT1 directly.
- **Cytoskeletal architecture.** ROCK promotes [[Actin]] filament assembly through activation of formin-like effectors and the LIM-kinase/cofilin axis, forming stress fibres and focal adhesions. This is the mechanism behind the fibrotic phenotype of constitutively active ROCK.
- **Cell polarity and division.** ROCK drives the actomyosin cortex required for cleavage furrow constriction during [[Cytokinesis]], and for planar cell polarity and migration.

ROCK additionally phosphorylates [[PKC]]-family kinases at their regulatory sites, and engages membrane proteins through PH domains, so its output is broader than the classic smooth-muscle contractility role.

## Disease relevance

**Fibrosis.** TGF-β-driven myofibroblast activation requires RhoA/ROCK signalling, and ROCK inhibition reduces [[Fibrosis]] after myocardial infarction, pressure overload, and renal injury.

**Neurodegeneration.** Elevated ROCK1 activity has been implicated in Alzheimer-type phenotypes, and ROCK inhibition or ROCK1 haploinsufficiency ameliorates pathology in APP/PS1 mouse models.

**Vascular disease.** ROCK sustains vasoconstriction, so ROCK inhibition is a vasodilator strategy.

**Cancer.** ROCK supports invasion, proliferation, and vasculogenic mimicry; the inhibitor fasudil suppresses vasculogenic mimicry in B16 melanoma cells in vitro and in vivo.

> [!warning] Pan-ROCK inhibitors are not isoform-selective
> Fasudil and Y-27632 are widely described as ROCK inhibitors but are pan-ROCK and, at typical in vivo doses, inhibit additional kinases including PKA and PKC. Knockout studies show that fasudil or genetic depletion of either isoform alone produces anxiety-like behaviour, and it is genuinely difficult to assign a pan-inhibitor phenotype to ROCK1 versus ROCK2. Interpret isoform-specific claims with caution.

## Inhibition

- **Fasudil** (HA-1077, an isoquinoline sulfonamide) — the first ROCK inhibitor approved for clinical use, for cerebral vasospasm; also licensed for glaucoma in some regions. IC₅₀ roughly 0.8–2.7 µM for both isoforms.
- **Y-27632** — the standard selective research tool, Ki ≈ 140 nM (ROCK1) and 300–800 nM (ROCK2), reversible and ATP-competitive. Widely used to promote cell survival in dissociated stem cells, since the ROCK-dependent actomyosin stress fibres that drive dissociation-induced anoikis are relaxed by its inhibition.
- **Netarsudil**, **ripasudil**, and local-tissue fasudil formulations extend the class across glaucoma.

## Connections
- [[RhoA]] — the upstream GTPase whose GTP-bound form activates ROCK; the RhoA→ROCK→myosin light chain axis is the canonical contractility pathway.
- [[Cytokinesis]] — cleavage-furrow constriction is driven by RhoA/ROCK-activated actomyosin; ROCK inhibition blocks it, which is why Y-27632 rescues embryonic stem-cell dissociation.
- [[Actin Cytoskeleton]] — ROCK remodels the actin network through formin effectors and the LIM-kinase/cofilin axis, producing stress fibres and focal adhesions.
- [[Myosin]] — ROCK phosphorylates myosin light chain and inhibits myosin phosphatase, giving sustained myosin activation and contractile force.
- [[TGF-beta]] — TGF-β is the dominant upstream activator of the RhoA/ROCK pathway in myofibroblasts, which is why ROCK inhibition is antifibrotic.
- [[PKC]] — PKC is activated downstream of ROCK phosphorylation, and PMA-driven PKC signalling is a common tool for probing the same axis.
