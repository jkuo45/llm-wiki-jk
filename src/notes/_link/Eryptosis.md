# Eryptosis

**Eryptosis** is the apoptosis-like regulated death program of red blood cells (RBCs). Because mature erythrocytes are anucleate and lack mitochondria, eryptosis proceeds without caspase-dependent nuclear fragmentation: the core axis is Ca2+ influx → calpain and phospholipid scramblase activation → phosphatidylserine (PS) exposure on the outer membrane leaflet, accompanied by cell shrinkage and membrane blebbing. PS-positive remnants are cleared silently by splenic macrophages, mirroring [[Apoptosis]] without its nuclear machinery.

## Mechanism

Triggers converge on cytosolic Ca2+ elevation: oxidative stress, osmotic shock, energy depletion (ATP loss impairs Ca2+ extrusion), and [[Ceramide]] accumulation. Ca2+ activates calpain proteases and scramblases while inhibiting flippases, externalizing PS — the Annexin V–positive "eat-me" signal. Caspases are present in RBCs but dispensable, so eryptosis is classified as caspase-independent.

> [!info] Triggers
> Oxidative stress, hyperosmotic shock, glucose/ATP depletion, [[Ceramide]], prostaglandin E2, and xenobiotics. Pathological contexts include [[Chronic Kidney Disease]], [[Sepsis]], diabetes, and hemolytic anemias.

## Detection and blockade

Detection: Annexin V binding (PS), Fluo-3 Ca2+ rise, reduced forward scatter (shrinkage), [[Ceramide]] staining. Blockade: extracellular Ca2+ chelation, calpain inhibitors; [[Erythropoietin]] is reported protective (inferred, not established as a direct eryptosis inhibitor).
