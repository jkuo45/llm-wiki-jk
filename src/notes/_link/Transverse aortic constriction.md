# Transverse aortic constriction

**Transverse aortic constriction (TAC)** is a surgical model that acutely constricts the transverse aorta of a mouse (banding between the brachiocephalic trunk and the left common carotid artery), imposing chronic pressure overload on the left ventricle. It is one of the most widely used experimental models to induce [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]] and its transition to [[Heart Failure|heart failure]].

## Procedure and Physiology

- A suture is placed around the exposed transverse aorta and tied against a blunt needle of a defined gauge, which is then removed to yield a reproducible stenosis.
- The fixed afterload increase triggers compensatory left-ventricular hypertrophy within days to weeks, which over time progresses to chamber dilation, fibrosis, and contractile dysfunction — recapitulating pressure-overload cardiomyopathy.
- Time course and severity scale with stenosis diameter and strain background (e.g., FVB, C57BL/6).

## Use in Sirtuin and Longevity Research

TAC is frequently used to probe the cardioprotective roles of sirtuins and stress-response factors:

- **[[SIRT1]] upregulation:** Sirt1 is significantly upregulated ~5.5× (2 weeks) to ~8.8× (4 weeks) in the mouse heart after TAC, a stress-adaptive response studied in [[_document_ - Sirt1 Regulates Aging and Resistance to Oxidative Stress in the Heart|Alcendor et al., Circ Res 2007]].
- **[[SIRT6]]:** cardiac SIRT6 overexpression increases survival of TAC-induced heart-failure mice (in part via telomerase / TERT, TRF1).

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Sirt1 Regulates Aging and Resistance to Oxidative Stress in the Heart|Alcendor et al., Circ Res 2007]] — TAC pressure overload upregulates cardiac SIRT1 5.5–8.8×, the basis for the hormetic dose-response and stress-resistance findings.
