# Entacapone

**Entacapone** is a competitive, peripherally-acting [[COMT Inhibitors|COMT inhibitor]] of the nitrocatechol class. It is used clinically as an adjunct to [[Levodopa]]/carbidopa therapy in [[Parkinson's Disease]] to prolong levodopa's half-life and reduce "off" time.

## Mechanism

Entacapone inhibits COMT with an IC₅₀ of ~0.23 µM via competitive (nitrocatechol) mechanism. It primarily acts in the periphery (liver, gut), reducing the conversion of levodopa to 3-O-methyldopa and thereby increasing levodopa's bioavailability to the brain. Unlike tolcapone, entacapone does not significantly cross the [[Blood-Brain Barrier|blood-brain barrier]] and therefore does not inhibit central COMT.

## Comparison with Fisetin

> [!info] Source: [[task_output_comt_fisetin_senolytic_09_July_2026|COMT × Fisetin Research Evaluation]]
> [[Fisetin]] has a COMT IC₅₀ of 2.6–5.8 µM — approximately 10–50× weaker than entacapone (~0.23 µM). However, fisetin is a mixed-type inhibitor (competitive + noncompetitive) rather than purely competitive, and it is also a COMT substrate that generates the active metabolite [[Geraldol]]. Fisetin's additional senolytic, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties create a multi-pharmacological profile that entacapone lacks.

## Adverse Effects

Entacapone's most notable side effect is orange-brown discoloration of urine. Diarrhea, nausea, and dyskinesias are also common. Blocking peripheral COMT can theoretically increase catecholamine levels and shift metabolism toward [[Aminochromes|aminochrome]] formation under oxidative conditions, though this remains a theoretical concern at clinical doses.
