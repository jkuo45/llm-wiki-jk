# RRM1

**RRM1** (ribonucleotide reductase M1) encodes the large (α) subunit of human [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]] (RNR), the rate-limiting enzyme of de novo dNTP synthesis. The RRM1 homodimer carries the catalytic site where ribonucleoside diphosphates (NDPs) are reduced to deoxyribonucleoside diphosphates (dNDPs), together with the two allosteric regulatory sites and the redox-active disulfide that must be re-reduced between each catalytic cycle. Its gene (chromosome 11p15.4) is expressed constitutively, contrasting with the S-phase-gated small subunit [[RRM2]].

## Structure

- **~90 kDa monomer** that homodimerizes to form the α₂ (R1) core; assembly with the [[RRM2]] or [[RRM2B]] (p53R2) β₂ dimer yields the active α₂β₂ RNR holoenzyme.
- **Active site**: binds NDP substrates (CDP, UDP, ADP, GDP) and contains the cysteine pair reduced by the thioredoxin/glutaredoxin systems; radical-driven C2′ chemistry is initiated by the tyrosyl radical housed in the small subunit.
- **Overall-activity (A) site**: an N-terminal ATP-cone domain that binds ATP (activator) or dATP (inhibitor), gating oligomeric state and total activity.
- **Substrate-specificity (S) site**: binds effector nucleotides (dATP/dTTP/dGTP) to adjust which NDP is reduced, balancing the four dNTP pools.

## Catalytic Role

- Converts NDPs to dNDPs in a thiyl-radical mechanism; after each turnover the active-site cysteines form a disulfide that reduced [[Thioredoxin-1]] (or [[Glutaredoxin]]) restores using NADPH.
- Together with RNR small subunits, RRM1 is required for de novo dNTP supply supporting [[DNA Replication]] and [[DNA Repair]].

## Clinical Relevance

- **Chemoresistance biomarker**: RRM1 is overexpressed in many solid tumors (non-small-cell lung, pancreatic, bladder); high RRM1 is associated with reduced gemcitabine benefit and poor prognosis, and RRM1 transcript/protein levels are evaluated as a predictive marker in NSCLC.
- **Drug target**: the α subunit is the site of action of nucleoside-analog RNR inhibitors — [[Gemcitabine]]'s diphosphate (dFdCDP) irreversibly inactivates R1, and [[Clofarabine]]'s diphosphate inhibits it; RRM1 up-regulation is therefore a principal mechanism of gemcitabine resistance.
- **Genome stability**: deregulated RRM1/RNR activity skews dNTP pools and elevates mutation rates, contributing to tumor evolution.
