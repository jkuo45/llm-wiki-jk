# Metabolic Flexibility

## Overview

**Metabolic flexibility** is the capacity of cells and organisms to efficiently switch between different fuel sources—primarily [[Glycolysis|glucose]] and [[Fatty acid oxidation|fatty acids]]—in response to nutrient availability, hormonal signals, and energy demand. This adaptive capacity is essential for maintaining energy homeostasis and is a hallmark of metabolic health.

## Mechanisms of Metabolic Flexibility

### Mitochondrial Regulation

- **Sirtuin network:** [[SIRT1]], [[SIRT3]], and [[SIRT6]] are key regulators of metabolic flexibility
- **PGC-1α activation:** [[SIRT1]]-mediated [[Deacetylation|deacetylation]] of [[PGC-1α]] drives mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative capacity
- **AMPK signaling:** [[AMPK]] activation promotes fatty acid oxidation and glucose uptake

### Substrate Switching

- **Fed state:** Insulin promotes glucose uptake and glycolysis; lipogenesis is active
- **Fasted state:** Glucagon and catecholamines promote lipolysis and fatty acid oxidation
- **Exercise:** AMPK activation shifts metabolism toward fatty acid oxidation
- **Caloric restriction:** [[NAD+]] elevation activates sirtuins, promoting oxidative metabolism

### Key Enzymes

- **Pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH):** Gatekeeper between glycolysis and TCA cycle; regulated by [[SIRT3]]
- **Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC):** Controls malonyl-CoA levels; regulated by AMPK
- **Carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT1):** Rate-limiting step for mitochondrial fatty acid import

## Role of Sirtuins

Sirtuins are central regulators of metabolic flexibility:

- **[[SIRT1]]:** Promotes fatty acid oxidation via PGC-1α and PPARα; suppresses lipogenesis via SREBP-1c
- **[[SIRT3]]:** Enhances mitochondrial oxidation through deacetylation of metabolic enzymes; loss leads to PDH hyperacetylation and impaired glucose oxidation
- **[[SIRT6]]:** Represses glycolysis via HIF-1α inhibition; promotes oxidative metabolism
- **[[SIRT4]]:** Counterbalances catabolic metabolism; inhibits fatty acid oxidation
- **[[SIRT7]]:** Context-dependent; may promote lipogenesis and energy conservation

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - The Sirtuin Network Linking NAD+ Metabolism, Mitochondrial Function, and Metabolic Homoeostasis]]
> SIRT1 promotes metabolic flexibility, allowing cells to shift from glucose utilisation toward increased fatty acid oxidation and mitochondrial respiration during energy deprivation. SIRT3 coordinates mitochondrial substrate use, respiratory competence, and oxidative stress resistance. Loss of SIRT3 leads to PDH hyperacetylation, reduced glucose oxidation, pyruvate/lactate accumulation, and impaired metabolic flexibility in skeletal muscle.

## Pathology: Metabolic Inflexibility

Impaired metabolic flexibility is a hallmark of metabolic disease:

- **[[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]]:** Inability to switch from fatty acid to glucose oxidation in the fed state
- **[[Obesity]]:** Ectopic lipid accumulation due to impaired fatty acid oxidation
- **[[Metabolic Syndrome]]:** Chronic metabolic inflexibility drives insulin resistance
- **[[Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease]]:** Impaired hepatic fatty acid oxidation contributes to steatosis
- **[[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]:** Impaired mitochondrial capacity limits substrate oxidation

### Molecular Mechanisms of Impaired Flexibility

- **Reduced [[NAD+]] availability:** Impairs sirtuin activity across all compartments
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction:** Limits oxidative capacity and substrate oxidation
- **Chronic inflammation:** [[NF-κB]] activation suppresses oxidative gene expression
- **Lipotoxicity:** Ectopic lipid accumulation impairs insulin signaling and mitochondrial function

## Clinical Relevance

- **Biomarker:** Metabolic flexibility can be assessed by respiratory quotient (RQ) measurements
- **Therapeutic target:** Interventions improving metabolic flexibility include:
  - [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Fasting]]
  - [[Exercise]] training
  - [[Sirtuin Activators]] (e.g., resveratrol)
  - NAD+ precursors ([[Nicotinamide Riboside]], [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide]])
  - [[AMPK]] activators (e.g., metformin)
