# CD44

**CD44** is a cell-surface glycoprotein and hyaluronan receptor involved in adhesion, migration, and cancer stemness. In triple-negative [[Breast Cancer]] (TNBC), alternatively spliced **CD44 variant (CD44v)** isoforms are part of the [[MUC1]]-C/xCT complex that controls [[Glutathione]] (GSH) levels and ferroptosis resistance.

## Structure & Biochemistry

CD44 has a standard isoform (CD44s) and multiple variant isoforms (CD44v) generated by alternative splicing of variable exons. CD44v isoforms localize to membrane signaling clusters and interact with a range of partners including xCT.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review states that in TNBC, [[MUC1|MUC1-C]] forms a complex with xCT ([[SLC7A11]]) and [[CD44|CD44v]] variants, interacting with xCT to control GSH levels. Disruption of this MUC1-C/xCT/CD44v signaling induces ferroptosis in TNBC cells, reducing tumor growth and self-renewal (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Breast Cancer"). CD44v thus helps lock in the cystine→GSH supply that keeps [[GPX4]] active.

> [!info] CD44v as a ferroptosis-resistance scaffold
> CD44v is not merely an adhesion molecule—in TNBC it is an active node of the redox-protective complex. Targeting CD44v (or its interaction with xCT) is a route to trigger ferroptosis.

## Clinical Relevance

CD44v is a cancer-stem-cell and chemoresistance marker. Its role in the MUC1-C/xCT ferroptosis-resistance axis adds a mechanistic rationale for CD44v-targeted ferroptosis therapy in TNBC and potentially other CD44v-high tumors.
