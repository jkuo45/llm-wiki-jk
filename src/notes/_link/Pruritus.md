# Pruritus

**Pruritus** (itch) is a distinct sensory modality with its own peripheral
pathways, its own receptors and its own central representation, separable from pain
and from touch. It is carried almost entirely by unmyelinated C fibres and thinly
myelinated A-delta fibres, both of which are polymodal, which is why the same fibres
signal pain, temperature and itch. Its distinguishing feature is that scratching,
unlike withdrawal, is a *desirable* response — the itch-scratch cycle can itself become
the disease.

Pruritus is not a diagnosis. Chronic pruritus (typically >6 weeks) is a presenting
symptom of dozens of systemic diseases, and the practical task in medicine is almost
always to find the underlying cause rather than to treat the sensation.

## Perceptual classification

Three mechanisms, useful clinically because they imply different workup:

- **Pruritoceptive** — a pruritogen acts in the skin (contact dermatitis,
  insect bite, scabies, xerosis). Histamine often involved.
- **Neuropathic** — pathology in the afferent pathway itself: postherpetic
  neuralgia, diabetic neuropathy, uraemic neuropathy, spinal cord lesions. Itching
  without visible skin change, and often with allodynia.
- **Pruritogenic** — a circulating pruritogen acts on otherwise intact skin, with no
  primary skin lesion: uraemia, cholestasis, polycythaemia vera, HIV, iron
  deficiency, pregnancy.

## Neuronal signalling

Peripheral pruritogen receptors on C-fibre terminals are GPCRs, chiefly the
Mas-related family (MRGPRX1, MRGPRX2, MRGPRD) plus serotonin and neuropeptide
receptors, and cytokine receptors including [[IL-31RA]]. These signal
through Gq, Gi, calcium and, decisively, through TRP channels.

> [!info] Mechanism
> TRPV1 and TRPA1 are the required downstream nodes for essentially every itch
> pathway, histaminergic or not. Knockout studies show that histamine-, chloroquine- and
> IL-31-induced scratching are all markedly reduced in TRPV1- or TRPA1-deficient mice.
> This is why TRP channel blockade is one of the few mechanistically general
> antipruritic strategies: it sits below the receptor level and above the spike
> generator, rather than blocking one ligand at a time.

**Histaminergic itch.** [[Histamine]] acts on H1 and H4 receptors on C fibres,
driving calcium influx and TRP channel opening, and also produces the wheal, flare
and itch of an immediate hypersensitivity reaction. Antihistamines work here.

**Non-histaminergic itch.** The majority of chronic pruritus is histamine-independent
and unresponsive to antihistamines — cholestatic, uraemic, neuropathic, and most
atopic dermatitis. Key nodes: TRPA1 (activated downstream of MRGPRX1), [[PAR2]]
(activated by mast cell tryptase), IL-31RA (T-cell derived), and [[Interleukin 33|IL-33]] and thymic stromal lymphopoietin, which act upstream by expanding and
sensitising the afferent population.

**IL-31** is the best-validated of these. It is produced predominantly by Th2 cells
and to a lesser extent by mature dendritic cells, and acts on a small
IL-31RA-positive, TRPV1-positive, TRPA1-positive subpopulation of dorsal root
ganglion neurons — around 4% of them. Its receptor is a heterodimer of IL-31RA and
the oncostatin M receptor, and its signalling requires ERK1/2 phosphorylation. IL-31
also elongates nerve fibres directly. This dual neuronal and epidermal action is why
anti-IL-31R blockade (nemolizumab) is effective in atopic dermatitis and in
cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, where malignant T cells are themselves the IL-31 source.

Central processing adds a further layer: dorsal horn neurons, the spinothalamic
tract, and thalamic and cortical representation, with descending serotonergic and
opioid modulation. Affective and affective-sensory components ("pleasant itch") are
processed differently, which is why antidepressants help in some chronic pruritic
disease.

## Specific clinical syndromes

- **Uraemic pruritus** — affects 25–62% of peritoneal dialysis and 38–84% of
  haemodialysis patients. Mechanism unresolved. Plasma histamine does not correlate
  with severity and antihistamines are largely ineffective, which is itself evidence
  against a histaminergic mechanism. Serum [[IL-31]] correlates with
  intensity. Elevated CRP, IL-2 and IL-6, and an increased Th1 proportion, have been
  reported — an inflammatory signature in what is classically taught as metabolic.
- **Cholestatic pruritus** — historically attributed to retained bile salts,
  bilirubin progesterone metabolites or endogenous opioids; none of the single-agent
  accounts has held up. Bile acids activate MRGPRX4 at physiological concentrations,
  and the proenkephalin-derived peptide BAM8-22 acting at MRGPRX1 and TRPA1 has been
  proposed as a contributor. Serum IL-31 correlates with pruritus severity in
  primary biliary cholangitis and primary sclerosing cholangitis, and rises with FXR
  agonism in NASH — so the "cholestasis" label describes a multi-pathway syndrome
  rather than bile acid accumulation.
- **Aquagenic pruritus of polycythaemia vera** — affects 30–50% of patients, and in
  many precedes the diagnosis by years (mean ~2.9 years in one 441-patient cohort), so
  it is a presenting sign rather than an incidental one. The strong response of this
  subtype to aspirin, and the SSRIs' efficacy, implicate platelet, serotonin and
  prostaglandin signalling.
- **Atopic dermatitis** — Th2-driven, with the IL-31/TSLP/IL-33 axis; barrier
  dysfunction and nerve fibre elongation both contribute.
- **Scabies** — see the Scabies note; the itch is markedly delayed 2–6 weeks after
  the initial infestation and can persist for weeks after successful treatment, an
  immune rather than parasitic phenomenon.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Chronic pruritus with no skin findings should prompt a search for systemic
> disease: iron deficiency, polycythaemia vera, cholestatic liver disease, chronic
> kidney disease, thyroid disease, HIV, and haematological malignancy. Nalpagan
  and gabapentinoid-class agents are commonly used off-label for neuropathic itch, and
  the evidence base for most chronic pruritic conditions remains thin — treatment is
  largely guided by the mechanism, and the mechanism is often incompletely settled.
> For aquagenic pruritus specifically, treating the underlying myeloproliferative
> disease (phlebotomy, cytoreduction) resolves symptoms in a substantial minority
> where antihistamines do not.

## Connections

- [[Histamine]] — the classical pruritogen and the reason antihistamines work at all;
  H1 and H4 receptor signalling on C fibres is the well-understood, and now
  increasingly, *minority* pathway in chronic pruritus.
- [[Serotonin]] — acts on 5-HT receptors on pruriceptive fibres and provides the
  descending serotonergic modulation of itch; also the mechanistic rationale for SSRIs
  in cholestatic pruritus and in aquagenic pruritus.
- [[Scabies]] — a pruritic infestation in which the timeline of the itch relative to
  the infestation and to treatment is itself diagnostic, making itch course rather
  than skin findings the clinical signal.
- [[IL-31]] — the best-characterised neuronal cytokine pruritogen,
  acting on a small TRPV1-positive dorsal root ganglion subpopulation; the target of
  the first biologic approved specifically for pruritus in atopic dermatitis.
- [[Inflammation]] — pruritus in its non-histaminergic form is frequently an
  inflammatory signal with an immune-cell source, and Th2 cytokines are upstream
  sensitisers of the afferent terminal.
- [[Neurodegeneration]] — the neuropathic pruritus mechanism overlaps with the
  pathology of small-fibre neuropathies, in which itch and burning dysaesthesia are the
  presenting complaints.
- [[Inflammatory Pain]] — pruritus and pain share C-fibre afferents and central
  pathways, which is why TRP channel blockers and many agents used for neuropathic
  pain act on both.
