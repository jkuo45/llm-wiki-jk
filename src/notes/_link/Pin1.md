# Pin1

## Overview
[[Pin1]] (peptidyl-prolyl cis/trans isomerase NIMA-interacting 1) is a unique enzyme that specifically recognizes phosphorylated Ser/Thr-Pro (pSer/pThr-Pro) motifs and catalyzes cis/trans isomerization of the peptide bond. This conformational switch can dramatically alter the function, stability, and interactions of its substrate proteins. Pin1 acts as a critical node integrating kinase and phosphatase signals to determine cellular outcomes.

## Structure & Activation
Pin1 contains an N-terminal WW domain (phospho-Ser/Thr binding) and a C-terminal PPIase (peptidyl-prolyl isomerase) catalytic domain. Pin1 itself is regulated by phosphorylation (e.g., at Ser16 by PKA, which inactivates it) and by ubiquitination-mediated degradation.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways
Pin1 recognizes pSer/pThr-Pro motifs and isomerizes the peptide bond, inducing conformational changes in substrate proteins. This can have dramatically different effects depending on context:

> [!info] BAX Thr167: A Pin1-Dependent Survival Switch
> In response to survival cytokines (e.g., [[GM-CSF]]), [[ERK2]] phosphorylates [[BAX]] at **Thr167**, creating a pThr-Pro motif recognized by Pin1. Pin1 binding catalyzes a conformational change that **locks Bax in an inactive state**, preventing its translocation to mitochondria and oligomerization. This protects cells from apoptosis. However, under stress, [[JNK]] or [[p38 MAPK]] can target the same site to promote apoptosis, making Pin1 the critical switch determining the pro-survival vs. pro-death outcome.

- **Oncogenic substrates**: Pin1 isomerizes and stabilizes many oncoproteins (cyclin D1, c-Myc, β-catenin) and inactivates tumor suppressors (p53, p27).
- **Cancer**: Pin1 is overexpressed in many cancers and is considered an oncogene. Its inhibition triggers apoptosis in cancer cells.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Cancer**: Pin1 overexpression correlates with poor prognosis in breast, lung, and prostate cancers. Pin1 promotes tumor progression by stabilizing oncoproteins and inactivating tumor suppressors.
- **Neurodegeneration**: Pin1 regulates Tau phosphorylation and is implicated in Alzheimer's disease pathology.
- **Therapeutic Targeting**: Pin1 inhibitors (e.g., juglone, PiB) are under investigation as potential anticancer agents.
