# Kynurenic Acid

**Kynurenic acid** (KynA) is a neuroactive metabolite of the [[Kynurenine Pathway]] produced from [[Kynurenine]] by kynurenine aminotransferase (KAT) enzymes (KAT I, II, III). It functions as a broad-spectrum antagonist of excitatory amino acid receptors.

## Receptor Targets

- **NMDA receptor antagonist:** KynA is an endogenous antagonist at the glycine co-agonist site of the [[NMDA receptor]], providing neuroprotection against glutamate-mediated excitotoxicity.
- **Alpha-7 nicotinic receptor antagonist:** KynA also antagonizes α7nAChR, which may modulate cognition and [[Inflammation]].

## Physiological Role

KynA is considered **neuroprotective** and serves as a counterbalance to [[Quinolinic Acid]] (an NMDA agonist and neurotoxin). The KynA/QA ratio is a critical determinant of neuronal health:
- High KynA/QA → neuroprotection
- Low KynA/QA → excitotoxicity and [[Neurodegeneration]]

## Clinical Relevance

Elevated KynA is implicated in [[Schizophrenia]] (via α7nAChR antagonism impairing cognitive function). Reduced KynA contributes to excitotoxicity in [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Huntington's disease]], and [[Depression|major depression]]. Kynurenine aminotransferase inhibitors are being explored to modulate KynA levels.
