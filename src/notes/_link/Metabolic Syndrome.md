# Metabolic Syndrome

**Metabolic syndrome** is a cluster of interconnected metabolic abnormalities — central obesity, insulin resistance, hypertension, and dyslipidemia — that collectively increase the risk of [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Cardiovascular Disease]], and [[Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease]]. Its development involves genetic, environmental, and lifestyle risk factors. Chronic [[Inflammation]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] underpin its metabolic dysregulation.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Metabolic syndrome arises from the combination of visceral adiposity, insulin resistance, and dysregulated lipid and glucose metabolism, creating a state of systemic low-grade inflammation and oxidative stress. These disturbances drive progression to diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Downregulated in metabolic syndrome. Post-transcriptional stabilization by the RNA-binding protein HuR represses inflammation and hyperglycemia to counter metabolic syndrome. SIRT1 modest overexpression protects from hepatic steatosis (via MnSOD/Nrf1 induction, PGC-1α activation, NF-κB downregulation).
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Downregulated in lipid metabolism-related diseases; overexpression improves hypercholesterolemia (FoxO3 recruitment to the SREBP2 promoter → H3K9/H3K56 deacetylation).

## Therapeutic Implications

Enhancing SIRT1/HuR stability and SIRT6 activity are candidate strategies to combat metabolic syndrome by dampening inflammation, hyperglycemia, and dyslipidemia.
