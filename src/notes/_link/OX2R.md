# OX2R

[[OX2R]] (orexin receptor type 2, encoded by *HCRTR2*) is a G-protein-coupled receptor that mediates signaling of the hypothalamic neuropeptides orexin A and B (hypocretins). It regulates arousal, wakefulness, feeding behavior, and energy homeostasis via projections from the [[Dopamine|hypothalamus]] to arousal and reward circuits.

## SIRT1 and the BRASTO lifespan phenotype

In the dorsomedial and lateral hypothalamic nuclei (DMH/LH), [[SIRT1]] deacetylates the transcription factor NKX2-1 to upregulate brain-specific OX2R expression. The resulting increase in neural activity raises physical activity and whole-body oxygen consumption, preserves [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] morphology in skeletal muscle, and promotes body temperature homeostasis — phenotypes underlying the ~11% median lifespan extension and delayed cancer-related death of the BRASTO SIRT1-overexpressing mouse ([[_document_ - Sirtuins Guardians of Mammalian Healthspan|Sirtuins: Guardians of Mammalian Healthspan, Giblin et al. 2014]], ref 32). Mice on a calorie-restricted diet show the same SIRT1/OX2R signature in the DMH/LH.

> [!important] Hypothalamic hub for longevity
> SIRT1 → NKX2-1 → OX2R in the hypothalamus recapitulates calorie-restriction physiology (activity, thermoregulation, muscle mitochondria), linking sirtuin activity to lifespan.
