# Ca2+ overload

**Ca2+ overload** is the sustained rise of cytosolic free calcium from ~100 nM toward micromolar levels via reverse-mode Na+/Ca2+ exchange, voltage-gated channels, and ER release after ATP-dependent extrusion fails. It is a point-of-no-return intermediate: calcium activates [[Calpain]] cysteine proteases, opens the [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore]], stimulates phospholipases, and loads mitochondria until membrane potential collapses.

In the comparison-table context, Ca2+ overload sits second in the necrosis core-machinery chain (pump failure → overload → calpains → mPTP/LMP) and also feeds excitotoxic parthanatos upstream of nNOS/PARP-1 (NMDA → Ca2+ → peroxynitrite). Chelation protects renal ischemia-reperfusion and H2O2 models but not MNNG parthanatos, marking it as context-obligatory rather than universal.
