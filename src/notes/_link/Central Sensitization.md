# Central Sensitization

Central sensitization is the increased responsiveness of central nociceptive neurons to afferent input that follows sustained or repeated nociceptive signalling. It is a form of synaptic plasticity, not a distinct disease, and it is the mechanistic backbone of the modern [[Nociplastic Pain|nociplastic pain]] framework: pain that is disproportionate to, and in the limit independent of, actual tissue damage.

## What defines it

Three features together constitute central sensitization:

- **Hyperalgesia** — increased pain from a normally painful stimulus.
- **Allodynia** — pain from a stimulus that does not normally hurt (light touch, brushing, bedding contact).
- **Expanded receptive fields and reduced spatial summation thresholds** — inputs are amplified and recruit wider neuronal populations.

Alongside these, patients show impaired **conditioned pain modulation**: descending inhibition, the brainstem-and-spinal-circuit brake that normally dampens nociceptive transmission, is weakened. Temporal summation ("wind-up") of repeated C-fibre stimulation is enhanced, and second pain is larger and lasts longer. In [[Fibromyalgia]] these psychophysical abnormalities are reproducible in patients and are among the better-supported findings in the disorder.

> [!info] Mechanism
> Central sensitization is best understood as a shift in the excitation/inhibition balance of dorsal horn and supraspinal circuits. Excitatory drive rises: C-fibre terminals release more [[Glutamate]] and [[Substance P]] onto [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] and AMPA receptors, with PKA-, [[PKC]]-, Src- and MAPK-dependent phosphorylation removing the voltage-dependent Mg2+ block so that a modest glutamate release produces a large depolarisation. Inhibitory control falls: [[GABA]] and glycine signalling, adenosine, endogenous [[Opioids|opioid]] and cannabinoid tone are all reduced, and descending serotonergic and noradrenergic [[Serotonin]]-[[Norepinephrine]] descending inhibition from the midbrain and rostral ventromedial medulla is attenuated. A-fibres also acquire the neurochemical features of C-fibres, so innocuous light-touch input acquires the capacity to drive the sensitised circuit. A transcriptional wave follows the early post-translational changes, so once established the state is maintained by altered gene expression.

## Neuroinflammation as a driver

Central sensitization is not purely a circuit phenomenon; [[Neuroinflammation]] is a sufficient driver of it. Microglia and [[Astrocytes]] in the spinal cord and brain are activated by ATP, chemokines, substance P and calcitonin gene-related peptide released from nociceptors, and shift toward a pro-inflammatory phenotype. They release [[Cytokines]] and chemokines — most consistently TNF-alpha, [[IL-1β]], [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]] — plus glutamate and nitric oxide, all of which raise neuronal excitability and lower thresholds.

> [!info] Mechanism
> The [[Fractalkine]] axis is a well-characterised example. Nerve injury upregulates the microglial receptor [[CX3CR1]], and cathepsin S released from dorsal horn neurons cleaves the neuronal chemokine CX3CL1 (fractalkine) to release CX3CL1. This activates microglia, which release further pro-inflammatory mediators; blocking CX3CR1 attenuates mechanical allodynia and the phenotype is abolished in knockout animals. This gives a concrete circuit by which immune signalling and synaptic plasticity become a single process.

Glial-polarisation state also matters: in reserpine-induced and chronic-pain models microglia shift toward the M1 phenotype with rising iNOS and falling arginase-1, and similar M1-skewed signatures are reported in CRPS spinal cord.

## Clinical contexts

Central sensitization is documented in [[Fibromyalgia]] (the prototypical condition) and in other functional pain disorders — irritable bowel syndrome, temporomandibular disorder, vulvodynia, interstitial cystitis — which share sleep, fatigue, memory and mood complaints alongside pain. It is also a real contributor to the pain of [[Osteoarthritis]], [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]] and psoriatic arthritis, where it can explain pain that does not track with imaging or with markers of inflammation. Long COVID, post-chemotherapy pain, and chronic low back pain of unclear source are other candidates.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Central sensitization is a mechanism, not a diagnosis, and it is easy to over-apply it. In fibromyalgia especially, the honest position is that patients sit on a continuum: some are predominantly peripherally driven, some predominantly centrally driven. Several studies also report reduced epidermal nerve fibre density in fibromyalgia skin biopsies, which supports a peripheral component. Claims that fibromyalgia is a purely central disorder are not well supported.

## Identifying and treating it

Practical identification relies on quantitative sensory testing (pressure pain thresholds, conditioned pain modulation, temporal summation) rather than on any single biomarker; PET imaging of glial activation and CSF/serum cytokine panels are research tools. Proposed phenotypes to guide "precision pain medicine" are actively being worked on but are not yet clinically validated.

Treatments that follow the mechanism are mostly indirect. Drugs that restore the inhibitory/excitatory balance — SNRIs, gabapentinoid agents, low-dose naltrexone — address the neurotransmission side. Ketamine and [[Dextromethorphan]] as [[NMDA receptor|NMDA]] antagonists are used for selected patients, with limited evidence and real side-effect burden. Non-pharmacological management — sleep, graded physical activity, education, cognitive behavioural therapy and related approaches — is the keystone of care and is consistently rated as the most effective component. Directly targeting glia has only preclinical support so far: minocycline and other microglial inhibitors attenuate pain in fibromyalgia-like and chronic pain models, but there is no trial evidence in patients.

## Connections

- [[Fibromyalgia]] — Central sensitization is the leading mechanistic model for fibromyalgia, and the psychophysical abnormalities it predicts are among the most reproducible findings in the disorder. It is also the condition in which the framework is most contested, since peripheral pathology has been documented alongside the central changes.
- [[NMDA receptor]] — The NMDA receptor is the effector that converts repeated C-fibre activity into lasting synaptic change; removing its voltage-dependent magnesium block is the single most cited step in the initiation of central sensitization.
- [[Neuroinflammation]] — Glial activation supplies the cytokines, chemokines and glutamate that sustain the sensitised state, and neuroinflammation is now understood as a sufficient rather than merely permissive cause.
- [[Microglia]] — Spinal and supraspinal microglia are the resident immune cells that drive the cytokine and fractalkine signalling described above, and their polarisation state tracks with pain chronicity in animal models.
- [[Substance P]] — Elevated in fibromyalgia CSF and released from C-fibre terminals, substance P both drives the sensitised dorsal horn directly and stimulates microglial release of further pro-inflammatory mediators.
- [[Glutamate]] — Excess presynaptic glutamate release onto NMDA and AMPA receptors is the excitatory half of the excitation/inhibition imbalance, and CSF glutamate correlates with fibromyalgia severity.
- [[GABA]] — Reduced GABAergic inhibition is the inhibitory half of the same imbalance; the paired glutamate-high/GABA-low pattern is the standard neurochemical signature of nociplastic pain.
- [[Allodynia]] — Pain from normally innocuous input is a defining clinical output of central sensitization, and the principal endpoint in the animal models used to study the phenomenon. The Allodynia note is currently a stub and should be expanded to carry the psychophysical testing detail.
- [[Inflammatory Pain]] — Chronic inflammatory pain is the commonest route by which central sensitization is established, which is why removing the peripheral driver sometimes improves central pain and sometimes does not.
- [[Hyperalgesia]] — Increased pain from painful input; the other of the two defining psychophysical signs, alongside allodynia.
- [[Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS)]] — ME/CFS and fibromyalgia overlap substantially in patients and are both now framed in part as nociplastic conditions with central sensory amplification.
- [[P2X3]] — Purinergic receptor upregulation after nerve injury is one route by which ATP released from damaged or sensitised neurons drives microglial hyperactivity and mechanical allodynia. The P2X3 note is a stub; it should be expanded to cover sensory nerve fibre hyperexcitability, not only bladder pain.
- [[Ketamine]] — The best-known clinical attempt to block the NMDA-dependent initiation of central sensitization; efficacy in fibromyalgia and chronic pain is real but limited by psychotomimetic and other side effects.
