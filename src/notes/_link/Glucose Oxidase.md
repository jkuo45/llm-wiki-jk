# Glucose Oxidase

[[Glucose Oxidase]] (GOx, notatin) is a flavoenzyme that oxidizes β-D-glucose to D-glucono-δ-lactone while reducing O<sub>2</sub> to hydrogen peroxide (H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub>) [^29]. It is widely used experimentally to impose a defined, extracellular source of oxidative stress.

## Role as a neurotoxicity insult

In the methylene blue derivative study, glucose oxidase (2 U) was added to HT-22 hippocampal-neuron media to generate extracellular H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> and test whether MB and its derivatives act as conventional antioxidants [^1]. Glucose oxidase caused ~80% cell death after 3 h. Crucially, **none** of the methylene blue-related compounds ([[Methylene blue]], [[Toluidine Blue O]], [[Phenothiazine]], [[2-Chlorophenothiazine]], [[Chlorpromazine]], [[Promethazine]], [[Neutral Red]]) provided protection, whereas pyruvate — a positive antioxidant control (4 mM) — did [^1].

> [!warning] Key discriminating assay
> The glucose oxidase result establishes that MB and its derivatives are **not** direct extracellular ROS scavengers. Their neuroprotection operates upstream on mitochondrial electron flow, not by chain-breaking neutralization of externally generated H<sub>2</sub>O<sub>2</sub> [^1].
