# Osteoporosis

**Osteoporosis** is a systemic skeletal disease characterized by low bone mass and deterioration of bone microarchitecture, leading to increased bone fragility and fracture risk. It arises from an imbalance between osteoclastic bone resorption and osteoblastic bone formation. Its development involves genetic, environmental, and lifestyle risk factors.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Osteoporosis results from excessive bone resorption relative to formation, driven by aging, estrogen deficiency, oxidative stress, and chronic inflammation. Impaired osteoblast osteogenesis and increased osteoclast activity reduce bone anabolism and density.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Knockout mice exhibit a low-bone-mass phenotype; overexpression increases osteoblast osteogenesis via FoxO3a deacetylation and oxidative-stress inhibition; deacetylates sclerostin → activates Wnt/β-catenin → increased bone anabolism; resveratrol activation restores alkaline phosphatase and osteocalcin via [[NF-κB]] inhibition.
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Inhibits age-related bone loss by stabilizing estrogen receptor alpha in preosteoblastic cells.

## Therapeutic Implications

SIRT1 activation (e.g., via [[Resveratrol]]) supports osteoblast function and bone anabolism, while SIRT6 stabilization of estrogen receptor signaling counters age-related bone loss — candidate strategies for osteoporosis therapy.
