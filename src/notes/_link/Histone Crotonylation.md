# Histone Crotonylation

**Histone crotonylation** is a reversible [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] modification in which crotonyl groups derived from crotonyl-CoA are covalently attached to lysine residues (Kcr) on histone tails. Unlike [[Acetylation|acetylation]], the crotonyl moiety contains an unsaturated carbon–carbon bond that allows extended conformations, and Kcr marks are generally associated with active transcription — particularly at promoters, enhancers, and recently activated genes. The mark is deposited by writer complexes (e.g., p300/CBP) and erased by erasers including the sirtuin deacylases, adding a layer of acyl-selective control over gene expression plasticity.

## SIRT3 as a decrotonylase

Beyond its canonical mitochondrial deacetylase activity, [[SIRT3]] possesses substantial lysine decrotonylase capacity, selectively removing crotonyl groups from specific histone substrates[89]. This activity expands SIRT3's epigenetic repertoire under both homeostatic and stress conditions, linking mitochondrial NAD+ metabolism to sustained histone acylation states[90],[91].

## Role in cancer biology

The review highlights SIRT3-mediated control of H3K27 crotonylation at metastasis-associated loci such as [[ETS1]], effectively constraining neoplastic dissemination[98],[99]. Mechanistically, the long noncoding RNA LINC00922 can decoy SIRT3 away from the ETS1 promoter, upregulating local H3K27 crotonylation and driving colorectal cancer metastasis (Liao et al., Mol Cancer 2023; doi: 10.1186/s12943-023-01859-y)[15]. These findings position histone crotonylation as a context-dependent rheostat of oncogenic transcription that SIRT3 actively opposes.
