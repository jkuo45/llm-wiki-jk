# Motor Neurons

**Motor neurons (MNs)** are the efferent neurons of the motor system and the **final common pathway** through which all central and peripheral motor commands converge on muscle. The term covers a heterogeneous population, and two distinctions carry most of the clinical weight: **upper** versus **lower** motor neurons, and within lower motor neurons, the **alpha, beta and gamma** classes defined by which muscle fibres they innervate.

## Upper vs Lower Motor Neurons

| | Upper MN | Lower MN |
|---|---|---|
| Soma | Premotor and primary motor cortex ("motor strip") | Brainstem motor nuclei; ventral horn (anterior horn cells) of spinal cord |
| Neurotransmitter | Glutamate | Acetylcholine |
| Target | Other motor neurons/interneurons, within the CNS | Skeletal muscle, via the neuromuscular junction; outside the CNS |
| Tracts | Corticonuclear (corticobulbar), corticospinal (lateral and ventral), rubrospinal | Peripheral nerves |
| Lesion signs | Spasticity, hyperreflexia, ataxia, loss of superficial reflexes, clonus, Babinski sign | Flaccid paresis, [[Muscle Atrophy]], hyporeflexia, fasciculations, fibrillation potentials on EMG |

> [!info] "Motor neuron" is a shared misnomer
> Upper and lower MNs share only a name. Upper MNs are best defined as neurons confined to the CNS whose axons project *within* it; lower MNs are cholinergic neurons that extend out of the CNS to innervate muscle directly. Using one term for both invites exactly the diagnostic confusion the distinction exists to prevent. Some authors have proposed renaming upper neurons (e.g. "imbuo-neurons"/"didactic-neurons") to break the ambiguity.

## Subtypes of Lower Motor Neurons

Lower MNs in brainstem and spinal cord fall into somatic, special visceral efferent (branchial) and general visceral groups. **Somatic** MNs — the largest group, in Rexed lamina IX — subdivide by target:

- **Alpha (α) MNs** innervate **extrafusal** muscle fibres and are the effectors of contraction. They have large somata and heavily myelinated Aα axons conducting up to ~100 m/s, and they are the neurons that receive monosynaptic input from sensory afferents in the monosynaptic stretch reflex. Within a motor pool they are ~one third of motor neurons and subdivide into **SFR** (slow-twitch fatigue-resistant), **FFR** (fast-twitch fatigue-resistant) and **FF** (fast-twitch fatigable) types, matching type I, IIA and IIB muscle fibres respectively. Conduction velocities are roughly 85 m/s for SFR versus 100 m/s for FF.
- **Beta (β) MNs** innervate **both** extrafusal and intrafusal fibres, and are the exception to motor-unit homogeneity; fewer and smaller, and the least characterised class.
- **Gamma (γ) MNs** innervate the **intrafusal** fibres of muscle spindles and control spindle sensitivity — they do **not** directly initiate contraction. They fire spontaneously, receive only indirect sensory input, and are **α–γ coactivated** to maintain spindle sensitivity during contraction. They subdivide into **dynamic** γ-MNs (bag1 fibres, regulating muscle length during locomotion) and **static** γ-MNs (bag2 and nuclear chain fibres, involved in posture). γ-MNs have thinner, less myelinated Aγ axons, lower conduction velocity, lower discharge threshold, higher firing rates and lower input resistance than α-MNs.

## Organisation, Diversity & Recruitment

- **Motor units.** A motor unit is one MN plus all the fibres it innervates. Motor units are *homogeneous* — an MN innervates fibres of a single type — which implies selectivity in neuromuscular connectivity and coordinated MN–fibre maturation. FF units contain 300–500 fibres (up to ~2000 in large trunk and limb muscles); S units ≤200.
- **Motor pools and columns.** MNs group into columns and pools matching their targets; a pool innervating a single muscle contains a mix of α (SFR/FFR/FF) and γ and β types.
- **Henneman's size principle.** Recruitment follows soma size and intrinsic excitability: smaller SFR neurons have higher input resistance, lower thresholds and simpler dendritic arbors, so they fire first; larger FF neurons follow. This makes graded force production economical — sustained activity (standing, walking) recruits S units, while powerful bursts (running, jumping) recruit FF units.
- **Selective vulnerability.** In ALS, SMA and aging, **fast-fatigable** motor units degenerate early, while motor neurons innervating slow muscle and those serving eye movement and pelvic sphincter function are strikingly preserved. Oculomotor and Onuf's nucleus MNs have lower rheobase and higher intrinsic excitability, and their survival is why eye-tracking remains a communication channel late in ALS.

## Physiology & Modulators

Motor neurons receive convergent input from upper MNs, sensory neurons and interneurons, plus monoaminergic and peptidergic modulation (noradrenaline, serotonin, [[BDNF]]-family trophic factors). GDNF acts on MN connectivity in a pool-specific, column-specific, axonal-growth and subtype-specific-survival manner — specificity at multiple scales from one ligand. α-MN axons also synapse on inhibitory Renshaw cells, providing recurrent inhibition that limits activity and protects the muscle.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis** ([[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)]]) selects α-MNs, particularly fast-fatigable motor units; the preserved oculomotor and Onuf's populations define the disease's characteristic clinical selectivity.
- **Poliomyelitis** specifically destroys ventral horn MNs, giving flaccid paralysis without upper motor neuron signs.
- **Spinal muscular atrophy** and spinal cord injury produce lower-motor-neuron deficits; denervated muscle atrophies both from disuse and loss of trophic support supplied by the axon.
- **Energy vulnerability.** MNs are among the most metabolically demanding cells, with high mitochondrial content and axonal transport load; their reliance on [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial function]] explains vulnerability to [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], [[Excitotoxicity]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] across ALS, PD and ageing.
- **Glial and non-neuronal contributions.** MN degeneration involves extrinsic factors — trophic factor withdrawal, glial and immune paracrine signalling — alongside cell-autonomous ones, so targeting glia is a live therapeutic strategy.
- **Degenerative-context resistance.** Extrinsic and intrinsic mechanisms conferring resistance (notably on oculomotor and sphincter MNs) are promising targets for currently incurable disease.

## Connections
- [[Neuron]] — motor neurons are a defined class within the vault's general neuron note; the classification here (upper/lower, somatic/visceral, α/β/γ) is the motor-system specialisation of general neuronal taxonomy.
- [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)]] — the defining disease of motor neuron loss; its selective vulnerability gradient (FF units early, oculomotor and sphincter spared) is the clearest demonstration that "motor neuron" denotes a diverse population.
- [[Muscle Atrophy]] — the direct consequence of losing MN innervation, both from disuse and from withdrawal of axonal trophic support, making it the clinical readout of MN loss.
- Neuromuscular junction — the cholinergic synapse where lower MN output meets muscle; MN degeneration expresses as junction failure before axonal loss in several conditions.
- [[Huntington's Disease (HD)]] — the other major disease in the vault characterised by motor dysfunction, though via upper-MN/indirect and basal ganglia circuits rather than MN loss, making the contrast with ALS instructive.
- [[Spinal Cord Injury (SCI)]] — destroys the descending upper-MN pathways and interrupts supraspinal control of lower MNs, separating the two halves of the motor circuit.
- [[Neurodegeneration]] — motor neuron disease is one of the core neurodegenerative categories, alongside cognitive and autonomic forms.
- [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] — MNs have high energetic demand and axonal transport load; bioenergetic failure is a recurring mechanism of selective MN vulnerability.
- [[Excitotoxicity]] — chronic glutamatergic overstimulation injures motor neurons and is one proposed non-cell-autonomous driver of selective MN degeneration.
- [[Oxidative Stress]] — the shared downstream injurer in ALS, SMA and aging-related MN loss, linking this note to the vault's redox biology.
- [[Autophagy]] — autophagic failure in MNs contributes to aggregate accumulation in ALS and SMA, and is the target of several approved and pipeline therapies.
- [[SIRT1]] — NAD+-dependent deacetylase activity in MNs modulates ageing-related motor decline, tying the motor system to the vault's sirtuin biology.
- [[BDNF]] — neurotrophin signalling regulates MN survival, axon growth and synaptic plasticity, and is one of the trophic inputs the note above describes.
- [[BDNF|Neurotrophins]] — broader trophic-factor family (GDNF, NGF) providing the specificity-distinct survival cues that MN subtypes rely on.
- [[Creatine]] and energy-pathway notes — high ATP demand at the NMJ makes MN terminals metabolically vulnerable, which is the rationale behind creatine supplementation strategies in ALS.
- [[Aging]] — MN populations are selectively lost with age, with fast-fatigable units declining preferentially, contributing to sarcopenia.
