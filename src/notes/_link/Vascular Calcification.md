# Vascular Calcification

**Vascular calcification (VC)** is the pathological deposition of calcium phosphate in the arterial wall, driven by osteogenic transdifferentiation of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs). It is markedly prevalent in chronic kidney disease (CKD) and aging and is an independent predictor of cardiovascular mortality.

## Sirtuin Axis

- **[[SIRT6]]**: Markedly downregulated in CKD/VC patients; suppresses osteogenic transdifferentiation of VSMCs via runt-related transcription factor 2. MSC exosomes inhibit high-phosphate aortic calcification through SIRT6–HMGB1 deacetylation.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Soluble epoxide hydrolase destabilizes SIRT3, accelerating VSMC calcification; preservation of SIRT3 suppresses calcification.

## Clinical Relevance

Maintaining the anti-osteogenic sirtuin barrier (SIRT6, SIRT3) in VSMCs is a candidate strategy to prevent arterial calcification and its cardiovascular consequences in CKD and aging.
