# UPRmt

**UPRmt** (mitochondrial unfolded protein response) is a mitochondria-to-nucleus **retrograde signaling** pathway activated when unfolded or misfolded proteins accumulate in the mitochondrial matrix, or when there is an imbalance between nuclear- and mitochondrial-encoded [[Electron Transport Chain]] subunits. It restores organellar [[Proteostasis]] by transcriptionally upregulating mitochondrial chaperones and proteases.

## Mechanism

- **In *C. elegans*:** proteotoxic stress impairs import of the transcription factor **[[ATFS-1]]**, which normally would be imported and degraded. Excluded from mitochondria, ATFS-1 traffics to the nucleus and, with DVE-1 and UBL-5, induces chaperones (HSP-60, HSP-6/mtHSP70) and proteases (e.g. LONP).
- **In mammals:** the [[Integrated Stress Response]] axis ([[ATF4]], [[ATF5]] where present, [[CHOP]]) drives an analogous transcriptional program; the [[OMA1]]–[[DELE1]]–[[HRI]] pathway links inner-membrane stress to [[eIF2α]] phosphorylation.

## Physiological Importance

> [!important] Mitohormesis and longevity
> Mild mitochondrial stress that activates UPRmt can extend lifespan — a hallmark of [[Mitohormesis]]. Transient ETC perturbation during development induces a persistent, epigenetically maintained UPRmt that promotes longevity in worms. UPRmt cooperates with [[Mitophagy]] and [[Mitokines]] (e.g. [[GDF15]], [[FGF21]]) to preserve mitochondrial network health; chronic activation, however, can be maladaptive and permit propagation of deleterious mtDNA.

Dysregulated UPRmt is implicated in neurodegeneration, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], and metabolic disease.
