# Myocardial Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury

**Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion (MI/R) injury** is the paradoxical worsening of cardiac damage that occurs when blood flow is restored to a previously ischemic myocardium. Reperfusion triggers an oxidative burst, calcium overload, mitochondrial permeability transition, and inflammatory cell infiltration that together expand the zone of infarction.

## Sirtuin Axis

Sirtuins are largely cardioprotective against MI/R injury:

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Cardiac-specific knockout increases infarct size; overexpression upregulates FoxO1/MnSOD antioxidant pathways and downregulates pro-apoptotic caspase-3/Bax. Upstream regulation includes miR-132 inhibition → PGC-1α/Nrf2 and lncRNA Oip5-as1 acting via the SIRT1/AMPK/PGC-1α axis.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Deficiency exacerbates MI/R injury; as the primary mitochondrial deacetylase it sustains OXPHOS and mitochondrial quality control during reperfusion stress.
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Protective via FoxO3α antioxidant defense and attenuation of CHMP2B accumulation.
- **[[SIRT4]]**: Downregulated after MI/R; overexpression decreases infarct size through mitochondrial preservation and reduced apoptosis.
- **[[SIRT5]]**: Loss increases infarct size via protein succinylation modulation.
- **[[SIRT2]]** and **[[SIRT7]]**: Few studies reported.

## Clinical Relevance

MI/R injury is central to outcomes in reperfusion therapy for acute myocardial infarction and cardiac surgery. The protective sirtuin network (SIRT1/3/6) is a target for conditioning strategies that reduce infarct size.
