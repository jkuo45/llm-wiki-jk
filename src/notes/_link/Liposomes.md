# Liposomes

**Liposomes** are self-assembled, spherical vesicles composed of natural or synthetic phospholipids forming one or more lipid bilayers around an aqueous core. Their amphiphilic nature allows encapsulation of both hydrophobic (within the bilayer) and hydrophilic (within the core) compounds, providing solubility enhancement, protection of labile molecules, and controlled release.

## Variants

- **Conventional liposomes** — DOPC/DODA-PEG<sub>2000</sub> fisetin liposomes gave a 47-fold AUC increase (i.p.) vs. free fisetin in LLC-bearing mice.
- **Solid Lipid Nanoparticles (SLNs)** — solid-lipid matrix for controlled release (see [[Solid Lipid Nanoparticles]]).
- **Spherulites** — multilamellar onion-like liposomes; 5× higher fisetin entrapment than conventional liposomes.
- **Nanocochleates** — rolled lipid sheets; 141-fold AUC gain (i.p.) for fisetin.
- **Ethosomes / transethosomes / glycerosomes / niosomes** — skin-penetrant derivatives; fisetin binary ethosomes reduced UVB-skin-cancer incidence to 49% vs. 96% in UV-only mice.

> [!info] Fisetin liposomal performance
> i.p. fisetin liposomes delayed LLC tumor growth 3.3 days vs. 1.6 days for free fisetin and achieved a 47-fold AUC improvement; co-encapsulation with [[Cisplatin]] showed additive cytotoxicity in glioblastoma (U-87 MG).
