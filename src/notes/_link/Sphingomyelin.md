# Sphingomyelin

**Sphingomyelin (SM)** is a phosphosphingolipid composed of [[Ceramide|ceramide]] linked to phosphocholine. It is a major structural lipid of the [[Plasma Membrane|plasma membrane]] and [[Myelin|myelin]], and serves as a reservoir for [[Ceramide|ceramide]] generation through the action of [[Acid Sphingomyelinase|sphingomyelinases]].

## Metabolism

Most SM is synthesized by sphingomyelin synthases that transfer phosphocholine from phosphatidylcholine to [[Ceramide|ceramide]]. In the Soriano-Castell et al. (2026) study of replicative [[Senescence|senescence]], SM species were present at higher levels in [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] than proliferative cells; knockdown of [[Acid ceramidase]] did not substantially change SM levels (a slight increasing trend in proliferative cells), indicating SM is not the primary lipid altered by the ACase–ferroptosis axis.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Acid_ceramidase_modulates_the_lipid_profile_and_ex|Acid ceramidase modulates the lipid profile… (Soriano-Castell et al., 2026)]]
> Although [[Acid ceramidase]] knockdown raised [[Ceramide|ceramide]] (its substrate), it only modestly altered [[Sphingomyelin|sphingomyelin]] levels, suggesting the liberated free fatty acids from ceramide breakdown are preferentially channeled into [[Phospholipid|phospholipid]] PUFA pools rather than back into SM synthesis.

## Connections

- [[Ceramide]] — Direct precursor of SM via sphingomyelin synthases.
- [[Acid ceramidase]] — Does not strongly alter SM; its main ferroptosis effect runs through ceramide → free FA → PUFA-PL.
- [[Phospholipid]] — SM is a sphingolipid analog of glycerophospholipids; the glycerophospholipid PUFA pool is what ACase remodeling primarily affects.
- [[Senescent Cells]] — SM is elevated in senescence but is not the key ACase-linked ferroptosis mediator.
- [[Acid Sphingomyelinase]] — Enzyme that hydrolyzes SM back to ceramide.
