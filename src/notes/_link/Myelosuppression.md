# Myelosuppression

**Myelosuppression** is the reduction in blood-cell production caused by
damage to hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells in the [[Bone Marrow]]. It
is the dose-limiting toxicity of most cytotoxic chemotherapy and the dominant
practical constraint on delivering full-dose, on-schedule regimens.

## Presentation

Three lineages are affected, usually with staggered kinetics:

- **Neutropenia** — the earliest and most clinically serious, because the
  neutrophil pool turns over rapidly. Severe neutropenia plus fever is
  **febrile neutropenia**, a medical emergency with substantial mortality in
  high-risk patients. Monocytopenia precedes neutropenia by roughly three days.
- **Anaemia** — later onset, cumulative, and a major driver of fatigue and
  reduced quality of life rather than of acute mortality.
- **Thrombocytopenia** — intermediate kinetics, risks bleeding and forces dose
  reduction.

Nadir timing and depth are functions of regimen intensity — the type, dose and
number of myelosuppressive agents — so schedules can be predicted
quantitatively from a model of granulopoiesis for agents such as paclitaxel,
carboplatin, gemcitabine and doxorubicin.

> [!warning] The cost is schedule, not just symptoms
> Myelosuppression drives chemotherapy dose reductions and delays, which lowers
> relative dose intensity and worsens treatment outcomes. Managing the
> myelosuppression is therefore directly dose-preserving, not just
> symptom-relieving.

## Management

**Granulocyte colony-stimulating factors (G-CSFs)** are the mainstay.
Filgrastim and its pegylated form pegfilgrastim shorten the duration and
severity of neutropenia and reduce febrile neutropenia; the pegylated version
has a long half-life (15–80 h versus 3–4 h), switches to self-regulating
neutrophil-mediated clearance, and is given once per cycle — a single 6 mg
dose given at least 24 h after chemotherapy. Meta-analyses show G-CSF
prophylaxis reduces all-cause mortality, especially with dose-dense
schedules, and long-acting agents outperform short-acting ones for grade 3–4
neutropenia and febrile neutropenia.

Guidelines (ASCO, EORTC, NCCN) reserve primary prophylaxis for patients whose
febrile-neutropenia risk exceeds roughly 20%, with intermediate-risk decisions
weighted by age, prior chemotherapy or radiation, persistent neutropenia and
tumour involvement of marrow. Routine primary prophylaxis for every patient on
every myelosuppressive regimen is not warranted.

**Erythropoiesis-stimulating agents** and platelet support address anaemia and
thrombocytopenia. Interleukin 11 is approved in the US for prevention of severe
thrombocytopenia after myelosuppressive chemotherapy. Broad-spectrum
antibiotics are the mainstay of febrile neutropenia, with G-CSF as an adjunct
in many regimens.

For neutropenia, timing matters: CSFs are generally started 24–72 h after
chemotherapy and continued until an absolute neutrophil count of ~10,000/µL,
though earlier discontinuation can be reasonable and cost-effective.

## Class Associations

Myelosuppression is the signature toxicity of agents whose targets are shared
with proliferating marrow precursors: [[Alkylating Agent|alkylating agents]]
(where the same nondiscrimination against proliferating normal tissue causes
myelosuppression, infertility and teratogenicity), anthracyclines, taxanes,
platinum agents, antimetabolites, and monoamine oxidase inhibitors. The
mechanistically distinct inhibitors of the vault's targeted-therapy families —
CDK inhibitors, MEK inhibitors, PARP inhibitors — generally produce
less marrow toxicity than cytotoxic chemotherapy, which is part of their
therapeutic appeal.

## Connections

- [[Bone Marrow]] — the tissue whose haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells
  are the direct target; all three affected lineages descend from it.
- [[Chemotherapy]] — the exposure that causes myelosuppression in the great
  majority of cases, and whose doses it limits.
- [[Neutrophils]] — the lineage whose rapid turnover makes neutropenia the
  earliest and most dangerous manifestation.
- [[Anemia]] — the erythroid component of myelosuppression and a dominant
  quality-of-life burden.
- [[Platelet]] — the megakaryocyte-derived lineage lost in thrombocytopenia.
- [[Erythropoietin]] — the target of erythropoiesis-stimulating agents used to
  counter anaemia.
- [[Interleukin 11]] — approved to prevent severe thrombocytopenia after
  myelosuppressive chemotherapy.
- [[Alkylating Agent]] — the archetypal cytotoxic class, where myelosuppression
  is inseparable from antitumour effect.
- [[Leukopenia]] — the broader term for reduced white-cell counts encompassing
  neutropenia.
- [[Mucositis]] and [[Stomatitis]] — fellow dose-limiting toxicities of
  cytotoxic regimens that compound the nutritional cost of myelosuppression.
- [[Cancer]] — the disease context in which dose intensity, and therefore
  myelosuppression, is managed.
