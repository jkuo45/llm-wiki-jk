# Protein Degradation

**Protein degradation** (proteolysis) is the enzymatic hydrolysis of proteins into smaller polypeptides and ultimately amino acids. It is the principal mechanism by which protein half-life is set, and therefore a *regulatory* mechanism of gene expression rather than merely a disposal route: the abundance and timing of cyclins, transcription factors, inhibitors of apoptosis, and metabolic enzymes are set by how fast they are destroyed. Uncatalysed peptide-bond hydrolysis at physiological pH takes centuries, so all cellular proteolysis is enzyme-driven.

## Major Pathways

- **Ubiquitin–proteasome system (26S proteasome).** Proteins tagged with a polyubiquitin chain by an E1 (activation), E2 (conjugation) and E3 (ligase) cascade are recognised, deubiquitinated and unfolded by the 19S regulatory ATPase ring, then fed to the 20S catalytic core. This is the pathway for short-lived, soluble, nuclear and secretory-pathway regulatory proteins. See [[Ubiquitin]], [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]], [[Ubiquitination]], [[26S Proteasome]].
- **Ubiquitin-independent 20S proteasomal proteolysis.** Oxidised, intrinsically disordered or otherwise "unstructured" proteins bypass the 19S cap and are degraded directly by the 20S core. This is a major route for clearing oxidatively damaged species that no longer present a structured degron — see [[Ubiquitin-dependent 20S proteasome]].
- **Lysosomal degradation.** Bulk, organelle-sized material, and aggregated/misfolded protein that exceeds proteasomal capacity, are delivered to [[Lysosome|lysosomes]] — by macroautophagy (see [[Autophagy]] and [[Autophagosome]]), by [[Chaperone-Mediated Autophagy]] via LAMP-2A receptor binding of KFERQ-like motifs, and by endocytosis/phagocytosis. Lysosomal proteases are largely [[Cathepsins|cathepsins]] active at acidic pH.
- **Non-proteasomal proteases.** [[Calpain]]s (calcium-dependent, cytosolic), granzyme B, and mitochondrial/intermembrane proteases (OMA1, PARL, YME1L1) regulate specific substrates rather than bulk turnover.

> [!important] Degradation closes the loop with synthesis
> Because degradation returns amino acids to the pool, it is inseparable from proteostasis as a whole: the balance of synthesis and breakdown sets steady-state abundance, and cells couple the two through nutrient-sensing pathways (mTORC1, eIF2α/GCN2, the integrated stress response). Autophagy also serves as the principal supply route during fasting, when it mobilises intracellular protein for [[Ketogenesis]] and gluconeogenesis.

## Regulation and Quality Control

Substrate choice is not random. Degrons and post-translational modifications (phosphorylation, ubiquitination, lysine acetylation, oxidation) license selective destruction. Chaperone and co-chaperone systems triage folded versus unfolded substrate, and the machine-readable outcome of triage is degradation. Ubiquitin-chain topology (K48 for proteasomal turnover, K63 for signalling, linear/M1 chains for autophagy and [[NLRP3 Inflammasome|innate-immune signalling]]) determines which downstream reader engages — so the same molecule can be routed to disposal or to a signal depending on the chain attached.

## Disease and Therapeutic Leverage

Loss of degradation capacity is a core feature of [[Aging]]: reduced 20S/26S activity, autophagic flux decline, lysosomal insufficiency and impaired turnover all cause accumulation of ubiquitinated inclusions, lipofuscin, and carbonylated proteins. Conversely, retaining degradative capacity is now an active drug-design strategy — [[Bortezomib]] and related proteasome inhibitors in [[Multiple Myeloma]], deubiquitinase inhibitors (USP7, USP30), autophagy inducers ([Rapamycin]-class mTORC1 inhibition, spermidine, [[Trehalose]]), lysosomal enhancers (TFEB activation), and targeted protein degraders that hijack the E3-ligase/ubiquitin machinery to remove a single disease protein.

## Connections

- [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]] — The UPS is the principal selective proteolytic pathway for soluble regulatory proteins, and the clearest demonstration that degradation is signal-controlled rather than bulk disposal.
- [[Proteasome]] — The proteasome is the catalytic machine for both ubiquitin-tagged and ubiquitin-independent degradation, and the reason misfolded protein and oxidative damage can be cleared at all.
- [[Ubiquitin-dependent 20S proteasome]] — This route degrades oxidised and disordered proteins without ATP or ubiquitin and is directly impaired by glycation, connecting protein degradation to the dicarbonyl/AGE chemistry of aging.
- [[Autophagy]] — Autophagy is the degradation route of last resort for bulky, aggregated and organelle material; its decline with age is a principal cause of inclusion-body accumulation and of impaired mitoproteostasis.
- [[Lysosome]] — Lysosomes deliver the bulk degradative capacity downstream of autophagy, chaperone-mediated autophagy and endocytosis, and their acidification-dependent cathepsin activity is the terminal step of most degradation routes.
- [[Mitochondrial Quality Control]] — Selective removal of damaged mitochondria, and of mitochondrial precursor proteins that fail to import, is a specialised application of the same ubiquitin/lysosome machinery that governs bulk protein turnover.
- [[Proteostasis]] — Protein degradation is the terminal step of proteostasis; without it, chaperone folding capacity and quality-control triage have no output.
- [[p62]] — p62 is the archetypal selective-autophagy receptor that couples polyubiquitin-tagged cargo to autophagosomal degradation, and a signalling node for oxidative and stress responses.
- [[Bortezomib]] — Proteasome inhibition exploits the fact that myeloma cells depend on the proteasome for survival, converting a degradation pathway into a therapeutic target.
