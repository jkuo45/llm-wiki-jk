# Lipoxygenase

**Overview:** [[Lipoxygenase]] enzymes (LOXs; gene symbol *ALOX*) are a family of non-heme, iron-containing dioxygenases that catalyze the stereospecific insertion of molecular oxygen into [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|polyunsaturated fatty acids]] (notably arachidonic and linoleic acid), yielding hydroperoxy fatty acids (HPETEs/HpODEs).

**Isoforms and products:** Mammalian isoforms include 5-LOX, 12-LOX, and 15-LOX. 5-LOX generates leukotrienes—potent [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] mediators of asthma and allergy—while 12/15-LOX produce lipoxins and oxidize membrane phospholipids. All can initiate enzymatic [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

**Mechanism and relevance:** LOX-derived hydroperoxides can be reduced to alcohols or rearranged into oxylipins that regulate vascular tone, platelet function, and immunity. 12/15-LOX activity on membrane [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids]] contributes to [[Ferroptosis]] by generating peroxidized phospholipids when GPX4 defense is overwhelmed. LOX pathways are implicated in atherosclerosis, neurodegeneration, and cancer.

**Therapeutic landscape:** 5-LOX inhibitors (e.g., zileuton) treat asthma; broader LOX modulation is explored for cardiovascular and neurodegenerative disease.
