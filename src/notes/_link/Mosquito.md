# Mosquito

**Mosquitoes** are hematophagous (blood-feeding) insects of the family Culicidae within the order Diptera. Females of most species require a blood meal to support egg development, and it is during that meal that they transmit the pathogens responsible for the major vector-borne diseases of humans. Three genera carry essentially the entire global burden: **Aedes** (dengue, chikungunya, Zika, yellow fever), **Anopheles** (malaria, lymphatic filariasis), and **Culex** (West Nile virus, Japanese encephalitis, lymphatic filariasis). Blackflies of the genus *Simulium* (family Simuliidae, not Culicidae) transmit onchocerciasis, and sandflies (family Psychodidae) transmit [[Leishmaniasis]].

## Transmission biology

> [!info] Mechanism
> A mosquito is not simply a syringe. Pathogen acquisition depends on the parasite or virus reaching the midgut and then crossing the midgut barrier, replicating, and disseminating to the salivary glands. This is a genuine biological bottleneck: most ingested pathogens are destroyed by digestive proteases and by the innate immune response, and only a small fraction of the blood meal reaches the salivary glands. The vector's competence — its intrinsic ability to support this full cycle — is therefore a separate variable from vector abundance, and interventions differ depending on which one they target.

Beyond vector competence, the timing of biting determines epidemiology. *Anopheles* bites at night (anthropophilic, indoor-resting) and so is well controlled by insecticide-treated bed nets. *Aedes aegypti* bites by day and prefers human blood (anthropophilic, peridomestic) and is driven by container-breeding, which makes it tractable by source reduction and by the same nets. *Culex* is opportunistic and zoophilic, biting mixed.

Filariasis transmission illustrates the layering: the mosquito is not a passive host but an **obligate intermediate host** in which the parasite undergoes molts and develops to the infective L3 stage over roughly 10–14 days. Temperature and humidity accelerate this, so vector competence and vector density both feed directly into transmission intensity.

## The filarial connection

> [!info] Obligate endosymbiont
> Filarial nematodes in the genera *Wuchereria*, *Brugia*, *Onchocerca*, *Loa*, and *Mansonella* are all **obligate intracellular endosymbionts of [[Wolbachia]]** — a maternally transmitted bacterium, in most cases required for worm fertility and often for worm development. This has a striking translational consequence: killing the bacterium kills the worm, and several anti-filarial approaches in trials are Wolbachia-directed (including rifampicin-based macrolide combinations and doxycycline). Wolbachia also occurs in many mosquito species, where it is not always obligate, and it is the basis of a completely different vector-control strategy (below).

## Vector control as intervention

- **Insecticide-treated nets and indoor residual spraying** — the historic core of malaria control, aimed at night-biting anophelines.
- **Source reduction and larval control** — removing or treating standing water containers; disproportionately effective against *Aedes*.
- **Wolbachia population replacement** — releasing *Wolbachia*-infected *Aedes* into a population displaces the local *Wolbachia*-free type. Because the virus-blocking *Wolbachia* strains block replication of dengue, chikungunya and Zika in the mosquito, this converts the mosquito population itself into the control agent. This is the basis of The World Mosquito Program and has shown a real, durable effect on dengue incidence in trial sites.
- **Antimicrobial targeting of vector *Wolbachia*** — for filarial disease.
- **Vaccination of the vector, and gene drive** — under investigation.

> [!warning] Caveat
> The "neglect" in the vector-borne disease framing is a real and often misread point: mosquito-borne diseases are not neglected diseases. The 20 diseases on the [[Neglected Tropical Diseases]] WHO list include malaria and dengue/chikungunya, but the majority of global mosquito-borne burden falls outside that framing and outside that funding envelope.

## Vault context

The vault's mosquito content is parasitic-nematode-centred. *Brugia malayi* and *Wuchereria bancrofti* use *Mansonia* and *Anopheles* vectors, and *Onchocerca volvulus* uses *Simulium* blackflies. For those lifecycles, the mosquito is the site of larval molts, developmental arrest, and the stage at which vector competence is determined — which is why [[Wolbachia]] depletion is lethal to the parasite mid-vector, a mechanism with no mammalian analogue.

## Connections

- [[Brugia malayi]] — A filarial nematode for which the mosquito is an obligatory intermediate host and where vector competence is a determinant of transmission intensity.
- [[Wuchereria bancrofti]] — The other major lymphatic filariasis species, mosquito-transmitted across its range with different vector genera in Africa, South Asia, and the Pacific.
- [[Wolbachia]] — Obligate endosymbiont of the filarial nematodes (making it a drug target for the parasite) and a population-replacement tool for controlling the mosquito itself.
- [[Onchocerca volvulus]] — Filarial nematode transmitted by *Simulium* blackflies rather than mosquitoes; the same Wolbachia dependence, a different vector.
- [[Simulium]] — The blackfly genus that vectors onchocerciasis, and the one NTD whose vector control (larviciding river rapids) has been more successful than drug delivery.
- [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] — The disease for which mosquito-borne filarial transmission and mass drug administration combine; one of the NTDs targeted for eradication.
- [[Leishmaniasis]] — Vector-transmitted by sandflies, not mosquitoes, but shares the [[Neglected Tropical Diseases]] and MDA treatment framework.
- [[Neglected Tropical Diseases]] — The programmatic frame under which much mosquito-borne disease control is funded and delivered.
- [[Ivermectin]] — The MDA drug for filariasis and onchocerciasis; its "indirect" community effects (reducing transmission, benefitting non-participants) illustrate why MDA works as a herd-immunity intervention.
