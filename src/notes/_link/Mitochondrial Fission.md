# Mitochondrial Fission

**Mitochondrial fission** is the process by which a [[Mitochondria|mitochondrion]] divides into two, coordinated in mammals by the cytosolic dynamin-like GTPase [[DRP1|Dynamin-Related Protein 1 (DRP1)]]. Fission partitions mitochondria during [[Cell Cycle|cell division]], enables proper subcellular distribution, releases [[Cytochrome C|cytochrome c]] during [[Apoptosis]], and — critically — segments damaged organelles for removal by [[Mitophagy]]. Loss of fission yields elongated mitochondria from unbalanced [[Mitochondrial Fusion|fusion]]; loss of fusion yields fragmented mitochondria.

## Molecular Machinery

1. **ER-mediated constriction.** The initial constriction step occurs where [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]] tubules contact the mitochondrion, reducing diameter from ~300–500 nm to ~150 nm to permit DRP1 ring formation. The ER-bound formin [[INF2|Inverted Formin 2 (INF2)]] cooperates with mitochondria-anchored Spire1C to assemble actin at these sites; Arp2/3, cofilin, and cortactin also contribute.
2. **DRP1 recruitment.** [[DRP1|DRP1]] is recruited from the [[Cytosol|cytosol]] to the [[Outer Mitochondrial Membrane|outer membrane]] by adaptor proteins [[MFF]], [[MID49]], [[MID51]], and [[FIS1]], forming a ring-like oligomer that enhances constriction.
3. **Membrane scission.** GTP hydrolysis by DRP1 constricts and severs the membrane. [[DNM2|Dynamin 2 (DNM2)]] is then recruited to the constriction site to complete division, though studies show DRP1 alone can constrict and sever, making DNM2 dispensable.
4. **Inner membrane constriction.** [[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane|IMM]] constriction is [[Calcium|Ca2+]]-dependent at ER–mitochondria contact sites and is promoted by [[Cardiolipin]], which binds DRP1 to drive its oligomerization and stimulate GTPase activity.

## Regulation of DRP1

- **[[CDK1]]/[[Cyclin B]]** phosphorylates DRP1 at Ser585 during mitosis, promoting fission.
- **[[ERK|ERK2 (MAPK1)]]** phosphorylates DRP1 at Ser616, activating fission — a key node in [[MAPK|MAPK]]-driven [[Cancer|tumor growth]].
- **[[Protein Kinase A]]** phosphorylates DRP1 at Ser637, retaining it in the cytosol and inhibiting fission during nutrient deprivation.
- **[[Calcineurin]]** dephosphorylates Ser637, allowing DRP1 translocation to mitochondria and fission.
- **SUMOylation** by MAPL (mitochondrial-associated protein ligase) stabilizes ER–mitochondrial contacts and promotes cytochrome c release during apoptosis.
- **S-nitrosylation** by [[Nitric Oxide|nitric oxide]] and **O-GlcNAcylation** both increase fission activity; O-GlcNAc transferase drives DRP1 to mitochondria.
- **[[Ubiquitination]] by [[Parkin]]** targets DRP1 for proteasomal degradation.

## Physiological Role

Fission assures equal segregation of mitochondrial contents to daughter cells during mitosis and isolates damaged mitochondria for [[Mitophagy]], protecting the network. It also supports [[Calcium|calcium]] signaling and [[Apoptosis|apoptotic]] cytochrome c release.

## Pathological Associations

- **[[Parkinson's Disease]]** — increased fission via OPA1 long-isoform cleavage and DRP1 hyperphosphorylation; DRP1 inhibition rescues deficits in sporadic PD cybrids.
- **[[Atherosclerosis]]** — PDGF lowers [[MFN2]], increasing fission in vascular smooth muscle; [[Mdivi-1]] or DRP1 inhibition reduces lesion progression.
- **[[Left Ventricular Hypertrophy]]** — DRP1 up / MFN2 down; [[Mdivi-1]] ameliorates dysfunction.
- **[[Cancer]]** — excessive fission (often via ERK–DRP1 Ser616) promotes tumorigenesis, metabolic repositioning to lamellipodia, and [[Metastasis|metastasis]] (hypoxia-driven; [[Mdivi-1]] attenuates).
