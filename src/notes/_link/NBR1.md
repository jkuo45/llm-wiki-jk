# NBR1

## Overview

NBR1 (neighbor of BRCA1 gene 1) is a cytoplasmic selective autophagy receptor that plays a role in [[Mitophagy]] alongside other autophagy receptors including [[p62]], [[NDP52]], and [[Optineurin]]. These receptors share functional domains that allow them to bridge ubiquitinated cargo with the autophagosomal membrane.

## Mechanism of Action

NBR1 mediates selective autophagy through several key interactions:

- **Ubiquitin binding** — NBR1 contains ubiquitin-binding domains that recognize ubiquitinated substrates, including damaged mitochondria
- **LC3 interaction** — Through its [[LIR Motif]], NBR1 binds [[LC3]] on the autophagosomal membrane, tethering cargo for degradation
- **Cooperative function** — NBR1 works in concert with other selective autophagy receptors to ensure efficient substrate recognition and clearance

## Physiological Function

NBR1 contributes to cellular quality control by mediating the selective removal of:

- Damaged mitochondria (mitophagy)
- Protein aggregates
- Other ubiquitinated cellular components

## Relationship to Other Autophagy Receptors

NBR1 functions alongside several other selective autophagy receptors:

- [[p62]] — the prototypical selective autophagy receptor
- [[NDP52]] — involved in xenophagy and mitophagy
- [[Optineurin]] — linked to glaucoma and ALS through autophagy dysfunction

These receptors provide redundancy and specificity in substrate recognition, allowing cells to fine-tune their quality control responses.
