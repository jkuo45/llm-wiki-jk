## Overview

Lamivudine (3TC) is a nucleoside analog and competitive inhibitor of reverse transcriptase, developed as an antiretroviral for HIV and as a therapy for chronic hepatitis B. In the senescence literature it has been **repurposed as a senomorphic agent**: rather than killing senescent cells, it dampens their pro-inflammatory secretome (the [[SASP]]) by interfering with the retrotransposon arm of SASP induction. The key mechanistic insight is that lamivudine blocks reverse transcription of the LINE-1 (L1) retroelement, lowering the burden of cytosolic retroelement cDNA that would otherwise trigger innate immune signaling.

## Mechanism / Structure

Lamivudine is a cytidine analog in which the normal 2'-deoxyribose ring is constrained into a oxathiolane (five-membered) ring. After intracellular phosphorylation to its triphosphate form, it competes with natural dCTP for incorporation by reverse transcriptase, terminating viral cDNA chain elongation. The same inhibitory principle applies to the endogenous reverse transcriptase activity encoded by autonomous [[LINE-1]] elements.

> [!info] Retrotransposon–SASP linkage
> Senescent cells experience derepression of normally silenced repetitive elements. LINE-1 retrotransposition produces cytosolic cDNA, which is sensed by the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] and reinforces [[NF-κB]]–driven inflammation, feeding the SASP. By suppressing L1 reverse transcription, lamivudine reduces this cDNA pool and the downstream inflammatory cascade.

The inhibition of [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] by lamivudine is **indirect**: lamivudine does not bind cGAS or STING directly but removes their upstream trigger (retroelement cDNA). Studies reporting reduced SASP upon L1 inhibition support this pathway.

## Role in Senescence

As a senomorphic (SASP-suppressing) rather than senolytic (cell-killing) strategy, lamivudine targets a fundamentally different vulnerability than agents such as navitoclax or dasatinib+quercetin. It is attractive because it leaves the senescent cell in place while neutralizing its deleterious paracrine and systemic inflammatory output.

> [!tip] Repurposing rationale
> Antiretrovirals already validated for human safety can be repositioned against age-related inflammation. Lamivudine illustrates how an off-patent, well-tolerated drug may be redeployed to blunt the retrotransposon component of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype.

## Clinical & Research Significance

The translational status of lamivudine as a senomorphic remains **investigational**, and confidence in the SASP-suppressing effect is currently moderate. Open questions include optimal dosing, tissue penetration, and whether chronic LINE-1 suppression has unintended consequences on genomic plasticity. Nevertheless, it represents a mechanistically attractive, low-hanging candidate for clinical repurposing trials targeting inflammation of aging.
