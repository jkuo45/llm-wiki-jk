# Horvath Clock

The Horvath clock is a seminal epigenetic clock built by Steve Horvath that estimates chronological age from DNA [[Methylation]] at 353 CpG sites, using elastic-net penalized regression. A defining feature is its pan-tissue applicability: it performs accurately in nearly all healthy human tissues and cell types, from blood and brain to breast and kidney, making it the first broadly generalizable age estimator.

## Construction & Properties

The clock was trained on methylation data spanning many tissues, so it reports "chronological" rather than "phenotypic" age. It nonetheless reveals accelerated epigenetic aging in diseased and aged tissues, and its residuals ("age acceleration") associate with [[Aging]] and [[Epigenetic Alterations]].

## Relation to Other Clocks

The Horvath clock is the reference against which later clocks are compared, including the blood-focused [[Hannum Clock]], the mortality-linked [[PhenoAge]], and the rate-based [[DunedinPACE]]. It helped establish [[Epigenome|epigenetic]] drift as a quantifiable hallmark of aging.

## Longevity Relevance

Because it is tissue-general, the Horvath clock is used to assess whether longevity interventions — [[Caloric Restriction]], [[Rapamycin]], [[Senolytics|senolytics]] — reset the epigenetic state, and it underpins research into [[Senescence]] and [[Telomere]] attrition relationships.

> [!note] Generality vs. Specificity
> The Horvath clock's strength (universal applicability) is also its limitation: it is less tuned to morbidity than phenotype-driven clocks such as PhenoAge.
