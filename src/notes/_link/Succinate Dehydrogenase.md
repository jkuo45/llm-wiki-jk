# Succinate Dehydrogenase

**Succinate dehydrogenase** (SDH; succinate-ubiquinone oxidoreductase, respiratory **complex II**) is a mitochondrial enzyme that couples the [[TCA cycle|TCA cycle]] to the [[Electron Transport Chain|electron transport chain]]: it catalyzes the oxidation of succinate to fumarate (FAD-linked), reducing ubiquinone to ubiquinol. Because it performs both a metabolic and a respiratory function, SDH is a unique control point where substrate flux and membrane-coupled electron transfer are cross-regulated. SDH is anchored to the inner mitochondrial membrane, with its catalytic subunits (SDHA/SDHB) exposed to the matrix, making it directly accessible to matrix-localized enzymes such as [[SIRT3]].

## Regulation by SIRT3

The SIRT3 review identifies SDH as a critical acetylation node within the mitochondrial matrix: [[SIRT3]] regulates succinate dehydrogenase activity, mediating crosstalk between metabolic flux control and mitochondrial energy transduction systems[63],[64]. Through modulation of SDH enzymatic activity, SIRT3 fine-tunes substrate utilization efficiency while maintaining electron transfer chain synchronization, thereby preserving metabolic homeostasis. Reduced SIRT3 activity is expected to leave SDH hyperacetylated, compromising the coordination between TCA cycle turnover and [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|oxidative phosphorylation]].

## Pathophysiological relevance

Given SDH's position at the metabolic–respiratory interface, SIRT3-dependent regulation of SDH contributes to the mitochondrial bioenergetic phenotype across aging, metabolic disease, and ischemia-reperfusion injury. SDH is also a well-known source of mitochondrial [[ROS|reactive oxygen species]] when electron flow is imbalanced, linking its acetylation state to oxidative stress outcomes.
