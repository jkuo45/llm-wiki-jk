# Mast Cells

**Mast cells** are resident granular cells of connective tissue — the tissue-resident counterpart of circulating basophils — that sit at boundaries of blood vessels, epithelia and nerves. They are the effector cells of immediate hypersensitivity, but also long-lived sentinels that link innate immunity, [[Adaptive Immune System|adaptive immunity]] and tissue repair. Their lifespan, and the quality of their signalling, deteriorate with age, which makes them a recurring node in the inflammaging literature.

## Overview

Mast cells develop from bone marrow progenitors, migrate into tissues and mature locally. Their defining features are a large granule complement rich in [[Histamine]] and proteases (tryptase, chymase), a surface high-affinity IgE receptor (Fc-epsilonRI) that couples to the kinases Lyn and Syk, and a repertoire of Toll-like receptors ([[Toll-like Receptor]], including [[TLR2]] and [[TLR4]]) that lets them respond directly to microbial products without antibody. They are found in skin, mucosa of the respiratory and gastrointestinal tract, and in the brain, bone marrow and joint tissues.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **IgE-dependent activation.** Cross-linking of Fc-epsilonRI-bound IgE by multivalent allergen triggers trans-phosphorylation by Lyn, recruitment and activation of Syk, and the activation of phospholipase Cγ, which raises intracellular calcium and triggers exocytosis of granules and the synthesis of lipid mediators ([[Arachidonic acid]] → [[Leukotrienes]], [[Prostaglandins]]) and cytokines (including TNF-α).
- **Non-IgE activation.** Stem-cell factor (c-Kit) signalling sustains survival; complement receptors, neuropeptides such as substance P, and TLR ligation trigger activation without IgE — a route relevant to infection and to chronic inflammation.
- **Granule products.** Histamine raises vascular permeability and drives [[Inflammation]]; tryptase and chymase are proteases that activate PAR receptors, cleave complement and can degrade [[Extracellular Matrix]] components; eosinophil chemotactic factor and TNF-α recruit [[Eosinophils|Eosinophils]] and [[Neutrophils]].
- **Anaphylaxis.** Systemic, sudden mast-cell degranulation — whether IgE-mediated, complement-mediated, or triggered directly by physical stimuli (exercise, NSAIDs) — causes vasodilation, capillary leak and bronchospasm, i.e. [[Anaphylaxis]]. Adrenaline and the epinephrine auto-injector ([EpiPen]) act as physiological antagonists.
- **Tissue repair and remodelling.** Mast cells are sources of VEGF, FGF and MMPs and are required for wound healing, angiogenesis and matrix remodelling; their protease output shapes connective tissue.

## Physiological Function

Mast cells defend against venoms and helminths, contribute to immune surveillance of tissue-resident pathogens, and are required for the development of [[IgE]]-mediated memory and for the recruitment of other leukocytes. In the gut they maintain epithelial barrier integrity and interact with the gut microbiome; in bone they regulate remodelling and are abundant in osteoarthritic lesions.

With age, mast cells show reduced degranulation thresholds, impaired signal transduction, altered cytokine profiles and reduced numbers in some tissues, while the surviving population shifts toward a more inflammatory, "senescence-associated secretory" phenotype. This combination — fewer cells, but more inflammatory output — contributes to [[Inflammaging]] and to impaired vaccine responses and wound healing in the elderly.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Allergy and anaphylaxis.** IgE-mediated allergic rhinitis, asthma, urticaria and food allergy are mast-cell diseases; [[Asthma]] in particular is associated with airway mast-cell hyperplasia.
- **Mastocytosis and mast cell tumours.** Clonal expansion of mast cells (urticaria pigmentosa, systemic mastocytosis) driven by activating KIT mutations causes mediator release and, in aggressive forms, organ damage — the rationale for KIT tyrosine kinase inhibitors.
- **Chronic inflammation and ageing.** The [[Histamine Intolerance]] spectrum, chronic urticaria, and inflammatory bowel disease all involve sustained mast-cell activation; mast-cell contribution to inflammaging is a live research topic.
- **Sepsis and trauma.** Massive activation in sepsis and in trauma drives the "mast cell degranulation" component of systemic inflammatory response, and tryptase is used as a biomarker of anaphylaxis.
- [[Histamine Intolerance]] and comorbidities including chronic fatigue and long COVID have been linked by some authors to persistent mast-cell activation, though the evidence is mixed.
- **Therapeutic approaches.** H1/H2 antihistamines, leukotriene-modifying agents, omalizumab (anti-IgE), and KIT inhibitors (e.g. midostaurin) are the mainstays; [[Rapamycin]] and other agents that modulate mTOR-dependent mast-cell numbers are under investigation for longevity-oriented applications.
