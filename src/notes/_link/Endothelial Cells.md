# Endothelial Cells

## Overview

Endothelial cells (ECs) are the specialized cells that line blood vessels, forming the innermost layer of the vasculature. In the brain, cerebrovascular endothelial cells are the principal cellular constituent of the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]], joined by tight junctions and working together with [[Astrocytes]] and [[Pericytes]] to restrict paracellular permeability. ECs are not merely passive barriers; they are active participants in [[Inflammation]], hemostasis, and immune surveillance, and they express the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] components [[cGAS]] and [[STING]].

## cGAS-STING Activation Disrupts the BBB

In endothelial cells, cGAS-STING signaling is activated by damaged [[mtDNA]] and other cytosolic DNA triggers. Across models of irradiation, infection, and intracerebral hemorrhage, endothelial cGAS-STING activation induces [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling and [[Pyroptosis|pyroptotic]] cell-death pathways, leading to [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] disruption and increased leukocyte infiltration into the brain. Activated ECs also adopt a pro-inflammatory and pro-thrombotic phenotype, amplifying [[Neuroinflammation]] and promoting vascular dysfunction within the [[Central Nervous System]].

## Contrast with Microglial STING

Importantly, the role of STING in barrier integrity is context dependent and cell type specific. Whereas endothelial STING activation is deleterious to the BBB, [[Microglia|microglial]] STING signaling appears protective, preserving hippocampal BBB integrity during aging. This divergence — endothelial activation disrupting the barrier while microglial activation maintains it — exemplifies the dual, context-dependent nature of cGAS-STING signaling in the neurovascular unit and complicates blanket pharmacological inhibition of the pathway.

## Senescence and Endothelial Dysfunction

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Senolytic Treatment With Fisetin Reverses Age‐Related Endothelial Dysfunction Partially Mediated by SASP Factor CXCL12|Mahoney et al. 2025]]
> [[Endothelial Cells|Endothelial cells (ECs)]] are the most [[Senescence|senescence]]-susceptible vascular cell type, with senescent EC burden increasing 10.7-fold with aging in the aorta. [[Fisetin]] (a [[Senolytic|senolytic]]) selectively eliminates senescent ECs in vivo.

Key aging-related changes in ECs:
- **Senescent EC signature**: Identified via [[Single-cell RNA-seq]]; found 12 EC subclusters, with subcluster 10 representing the senescent EC population expressing *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]*, *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]*, and [[SASP]] factors
- **SASP production**: Senescent ECs secrete [[CXCL12]] as the most highly upregulated SASP factor — this chemokine drives [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] in an autocrine/paracrine manner
- **EC as both source and target**: ECs function as both a source of circulating [[CXCL12]] and a target of its detrimental effects
- **[[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EndoMT)**: Senescent ECs lose endothelial markers ([[CDH5]], [[PECAM1]]) and gain mesenchymal markers ([[TGFB1]], [[ACTA2]]) — a transdifferentiation process driven by the [[SASP]] and [[CXCL12]]
- **Functional consequences**: Senescent ECs exhibit reduced [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] production (−24%) and increased [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] superoxide (3.5-fold)
- **Receptor changes**: Senescent ECs show altered [[CXCR4]] (increased) and [[ACKR3]] (decreased) expression, shifting CXCL12 signaling patterns

> [!info] EC Susceptibility to Senescence
> Among all aortic cell types (endothelial, [[Macrophages|macrophage]], neutrophil-like, [[Fibroblast|fibroblast]], [[T Cell|T cells]], [[B Cell|B cells]], [[Smooth Muscle Cells|smooth muscle]]), [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] were the most affected by [[Senolytic|senolytic]] [[Fisetin]] treatment, with the greatest reduction in [[SenMayo]] senescence signature — reinforcing their role as key senolytic targets in vascular aging.
