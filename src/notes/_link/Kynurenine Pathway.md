# Kynurenine Pathway

The **kynurenine pathway** (KP) is the major catabolic route for [[Tryptophan]] (Trp) degradation, accounting for approximately 95% of Trp metabolism outside protein synthesis and [[Serotonin]] production. It serves as the primary de novo biosynthetic route for [[NAD+]] in mammals.

## Pathway Overview

The KP proceeds through sequential enzymatic steps:

1. **[[IDO1]]** or **[[TDO]]** (tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase) catalyzes the rate-limiting first step: oxidative cleavage of the indole ring of [[Tryptophan]] to produce [[N-Formylkynurenine]].
2. Kynurenine formamidase (also known as arylformamidase) deacylates [[N-Formylkynurenine]] to **[[Kynurenine]]** (Kyn).
3. [[Kynurenine]] is then metabolized through three branch points:
   - **Kynurenine aminotransferase (KAT)** — produces [[Kynurenic Acid]] (a neuroprotective NMDA receptor antagonist)
   - **Kynurenine 3-monooxygenase (KMO)** — produces [[3-Hydroxykynurenine]] (3-HK), a downstream precursor of [[Quinolinic Acid]]
   - **Kynureninase** — directly produces anthranilic acid (a minor branch)
4. [[3-Hydroxykynurenine]] is converted by kynureninase to 3-hydroxyanthranilic acid (3-HAA).
5. 3-Hydroxyanthranilic acid is oxidized by 3-hydroxyanthranilate 3,4-dioxygenase (HAAO) to form **[[Quinolinic Acid]]**.
6. Quinolinic acid is converted to nicotinic acid mononucleotide (NaMN) by quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase (QPRT), entering the Preiss-Handler pathway to ultimately produce **[[NAD+]]**.

## Physiological Significance

The KP is the only de novo [[NAD+]] biosynthetic pathway in mammals (alongside the salvage pathway via [[NAMPT]] and [[NMNAT]]). It bridges immune regulation, neurotransmission, and cellular energetics.

### Immune Regulation
- [[IDO1]] induction by [[Interferon-gamma]] links KP activation to [[Inflammation]] and immune tolerance.
- [[Kynurenine]] activates the [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor]] (AhR), promoting [[Regulatory T cell|Treg]] differentiation.
- Tryptophan depletion via IDO1 suppresses effector [[T cell|T-cell]] proliferation through [[GCN2]] activation.

### Neuroactive Metabolites
- **[[Kynurenic Acid]]** — NMDA receptor antagonist; neuroprotective
- **[[Quinolinic Acid]]** — NMDA receptor agonist; excitotoxic and neurotoxic
- **[[3-Hydroxykynurenine]]** — redox-active; generates [[Oxidative Stress]]

## Clinical Relevance

**[[Cancer]]:** KP activation in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]] suppresses antitumor immunity. [[Epacadostat]] and other [[IDO1]] inhibitors have been investigated as immunotherapies.

**[[Neurodegeneration]]:** Imbalance in the KynA/QA ratio contributes to excitotoxicity in [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]], and [[Depression|major depression]].

**Diabetes:** Altered KP flux is observed in both [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|type 1]] and type 2 diabetes, linked to [[IDO1]] genotype, [[Inflammation]], and disrupted [[NAD+]] homeostasis.

**[[Inflammation]]:** KP activation is a hallmark of chronic inflammatory states, with [[Interferon-gamma]] and [[TNF-α]] driving [[IDO1]] upregulation.
