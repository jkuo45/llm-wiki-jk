# Necrosis

**Necrosis** (from the Greek *nekros*, 'dead') is a form of cell death characterized by loss of membrane integrity and release of intracellular contents into surrounding tissue. It results in premature death of cells in living tissue by [[Autolysis]].

Unlike [[Apoptosis]], which is a tightly regulated, energy-dependent program, necrosis is traditionally viewed as an unregulated, passive process triggered by overwhelming stress (trauma, extreme heat, [[Ischemia]], toxins). Specific forms of regulated necrosis (e.g. [[Necroptosis]]) blur this distinction.

## Necrosis versus apoptosis

| Feature | Necrosis | Apoptosis |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | Severe injury, hypoxia, toxins | Physiological signals, mild stress |
| Energy requirement | ATP-depleted | ATP-dependent |
| Cell size | Swelling ([[Oncosis]]) | Shrinkage |
| Membrane integrity | Early rupture | Preserved (blebbing) |
| DNA fragmentation | Random, smeared | Inter-nucleosomal (ladder) |
| Inflammation | Prominent (pro-inflammatory) | Minimal (anti-inflammatory) |
| Fate | Phagocytosis by scavengers | Apoptotic body clearance |

**Secondary necrosis**: occurs when apoptotic cells are not efficiently phagocytosed (e.g. in vitro) and subsequently lose membrane integrity.

## Oncosis and autolysis

**[[Oncosis]]** (from Greek *onkos* = swelling) is the pre-lethal swelling phase preceding necrotic death — cellular swelling, organelle edema, bleb formation from ion-pump failure. **[[Autolysis]]** is the post-mortem degradation by lysosomal hydrolytic enzymes after membrane rupture. Oncosis is the process leading to necrotic death; autolysis is the post-mortem consequence.

Morphological hallmarks: cell swelling with water influx and vacuolation; plasma membrane rupture; organelle breakdown (mitochondrial swelling, lysosomal leakage, nuclear pyknosis to karyorrhexis to karyolysis); release of [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns]] (DAMPs) triggering [[Inflammation]].

## Types of necrosis

| Type | Tissue | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Coagulative necrosis** | Heart, kidney, liver (ischemia) | Denatured proteins preserve architecture; eosinophilic cytoplasm; nuclear loss |
| **Liquefactive necrosis** | Brain, abscesses | Enzymatic digestion liquefies tissue; pus; cystic cavitation |
| **Caseous necrosis** | [[Mycobacterium tuberculosis]] | Soft, friable, cheese-like; granuloma with epithelioid macrophages |
| **Fat necrosis** | Pancreas, breast, omentum | Lipases hydrolyze triglycerides to free fatty acids + calcium to saponification deposits |
| **Gangrenous necrosis** | Limbs, GI tract | Coagulative + liquefactive; secondary bacterial infection; dry (ischemic) or wet (infected) |
| **Fibrinoid necrosis** | Arteries ([[Hypertension]], vasculitis) | Fibrin-like deposition in vessel walls; immune-complex damage |

## Mechanisms

### ATP depletion and ionic collapse

[[Ischemia]] or mitochondrial poisons deplete [[Adenosine Triphosphate]] (ATP), disabling the [[Na+/K+-ATPase]] pump. Intracellular sodium and water accumulate, causing oncosis. [[Calcium]] enters via reverse-mode [[Na+/Ca2+ exchange]] and voltage-gated channels, precipitating [[Ca2+ overload]].

### Ca2+ overload and calpain activation

Cytosolic [[Calcium]] overload activates [[Calpains]] — Ca2+-dependent cysteine proteases cleaving [[Cytoskeleton]] components (spectrin, talin), membrane proteins, and [[Bid]] (apoptosis crosstalk). Prolonged calpain activity causes blebbing and integrity loss.

### ROS and mPTP opening

[[Reactive Oxygen Species]] from leaky mitochondria, [[NADPH Oxidase]], and [[Xanthine Oxidase]] attack lipids, proteins, DNA. Mitochondrial permeability transition pore ([[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore|mPTP]]) opening collapses membrane potential, halting ATP synthesis and releasing [[Cytochrome c]] and other pro-death factors.

### Lysosomal membrane permeabilization

Lysosomal membrane permeabilization ([[LMP]]) releases hydrolytic [[Cathepsins]] (B, D, L) into cytosol. [[Cathepsin B]] and [[Cathepsin D]] amplify death by cleaving [[Bid]] to tBid, activating [[Caspase]] cascades, and degrading structures — a point of no return.

## Pathophysiology: DAMP release and sterile inflammation

Rupture releases DAMPs activating innate immunity:

- **[[HMGB1]]** — binds [[TLR2]]/[[TLR4]] and [[RAGE]], promoting NF-kB inflammation.
- **[[ATP]]** — acts on [[P2X7 Receptor]] to trigger [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] assembly and [[IL-1beta]] secretion.
- **[[Uric Acid]]** — activates [[NLRP3]] as a danger signal.
- **[[DNA]]** — engages [[TLR9]] and [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], driving type I [[Interferon]] responses.
- **Other DAMPs** — [[S100 proteins]], [[Heat Shock Proteins]], [[Calreticulin]], [[Histones]].

This defines **sterile inflammation** — inflammation without infection — underlying [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], [[Myocardial infarction]], and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]].

## Role in cancer

While apoptosis is the desired therapy outcome, tumor necrosis produces a proinflammatory environment that can paradoxically support progression and [[Metastasis]].

## Adrenochrome relevance

[[Adrenochrome]], the oxidative metabolite of [[Epinephrine]], can directly induce necrosis in [[Cardiomyocytes]] via converging mechanisms:

- **[[Oxidative Stress]]** — redox cycling generating [[Superoxide anion]], depleting [[Glutathione]].
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction** — inhibits [[Complex I]] of the [[Electron Transport Chain]], collapses membrane potential, promotes [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore|mPTP]] opening.
- **[[Ca2+ Overload]]** — impairs [[SERCA]] and [[Na+/Ca2+ exchanger]], elevating diastolic calcium.
- **[[Cardiotoxicity]]** — chronic exposure in catecholamine-rich states (e.g. [[Pheochromocytoma]], [[Septic Shock]], [[Myocardial infarction]]) drives transition from reversible injury to frank necrosis.

The link between adrenochrome-driven necrosis and the [[Adrenochrome Hypothesis]] of neuropsychiatric disorders remains speculative, but necrotic debris and DAMP release in brain parenchyma could perpetuate [[Neuroinflammation]].

## Sex bias

Necrosis per se shows no verified intrinsic sex bias; observed male–female infarct and AKI differences track the regulated executors — male-biased [[Necroptosis]] RIPK3/MLKL output in renal IRI and estrogen-restrained [[NLRP3]] priming versus female-biased [[Gasdermin D]] systemic scores in trauma (see [[Pyroptosis]]). Accidental necrosis remains the sex-neutral parent process; dimorphism lives downstream. Distinction: the vault's core XX/XY paradigm states males preferentially undergo PARP-1/AIF-dependent (regulated) necrosis over caspase apoptosis — cell-autonomous, protective PARP-1 inhibition in males only (McCullough et al. 2005; Liu et al. 2009); that claim belongs to regulated necrosis, not accidental rupture.
