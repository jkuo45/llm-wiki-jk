# Huntington's Disease (HD)

**Huntington's disease (HD)** is an autosomal dominant [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative disorder]] caused by a CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion in the *HTT* gene, encoding an abnormally long polyglutamine tract in the [[Huntingtin|huntingtin protein]]. It leads to selective degeneration of striatal medium spiny neurons and cortical atrophy.

## Clinical Features

- Involuntary movements ([[Chorea]])
- [[Cognitive Decline|cognitive decline]] and [[Executive Function|executive dysfunction]]
- Psychiatric disturbance
- Progressive [[Motor Neurons|motor]] and functional impairment

## Energy Metabolism and Creatine

HD is associated with impaired cellular [[Energy Metabolism|energy metabolism]] and mitochondrial dysfunction, making the [[Creatine Phosphate Shuttle]] and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|PCr]] buffering system a therapeutic target. According to [[Creatine in Health and Disease]], animal and early human studies of [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine supplementation]] in [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]] were encouraging; in the large Bender et al. multicenter trial (creatine 9.5 g/day, up to 5 years; n=1687 across PD, HD, ALS), there was *some evidence* that creatine attenuated [[Brain]] atrophy in HD patients — the most positive signal among the three diseases studied.

## Management

Symptomatic (tetrabenazine, antipsychotics); no cure. Energy-supportive and disease-modifying strategies remain active research areas.
