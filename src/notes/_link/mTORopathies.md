# mTORopathies

[[mTORopathies]] are a group of neurologic and developmental disorders in which hyperactivation of the [[mTOR]] (mechanistic target of rapamycin) pathway is the dominant, actionable molecular driver. Paradigmatic examples include [[TSC]] (tuberous sclerosis complex, caused by *TSC1*/*TSC2* loss), focal cortical dysplasia, and GATOR1-related epilepsies (e.g., *NPRL3* loss) ([Iffland & Crino, 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28135561/); [Iffland et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35136953/)).

## Why mTORopathies respond to rapamycin

Because a single, highly penetrant mutation drives mTOR hyperactivation, the pathway is an exceptionally clean therapeutic target. [[Rapamycin]] and [[Rapalog|rapalogs]] prevent seizures, reduce mortality, and rescue cortical architecture in TSC mouse models and human trials, whereas their efficacy in the multifactorial process of [[Aging]] is far less consistent ([Zeng et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18389497/); [Roark & Iffland, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12226543/)). The EXIST-3 trial established everolimus as FDA-approved therapy for TSC-associated seizures, though benefit is suppressive rather than curative—seizures often recur on withdrawal.

> [!info] Lessons for aging research
> The success of rapamycin in monogenic mTORopathies—where etiology is clear and biomarkers (e.g., phospho-[[Ribosomal Protein S6]]) exist—contrasts with the inconsistent aging data, highlighting the need for genetic stratification and validated pharmacodynamic biomarkers in longevity trials.
