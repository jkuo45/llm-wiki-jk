# Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation

## Overview

Liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) is a biophysical process through which proteins and nucleic acids coalesce into dense, membraneless condensates within the cell, analogous to oil droplets forming in water. LLPS is driven largely by multivalent, low-affinity interactions mediated by intrinsically disordered regions (IDRs) and is fundamental to the formation of diverse nuclear and cytoplasmic bodies. In the context of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], LLPS has emerged as a central mechanism underlying the noncanonical, STING-independent functions of [[cGAS]].

## cGAS LLPS via Its Intrinsically Disordered Region

Human [[cGAS]] contains an N-terminal [[Intrinsically Disordered Region|intrinsically disordered region]] (IDR) that promotes LLPS with DNA, enhancing nonspecific DNA binding and catalytic activity in the cytosol. More importantly for its noncanonical role, this IDR enables cGAS to drive phase separation in the [[Nucleus]], where it scaffolds a broad network of interacting proteins. Through LLPS, nuclear cGAS assembles condensates that recruit [[RNA-Binding Proteins]], components of the [[Spliceosome]], and the [[SWI-SNF Complex]], positioning cGAS as an architectural organizer of chromatin and RNA metabolism. The IDR thus converts cGAS from a simple enzyme into a hub that concentrates regulatory machinery.

## Pathological Phase Separation in Neurodegeneration

LLPS also underlies the aggregation of pathogenic proteins in [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]]. Misfolded proteins such as [[Tau]] and polyglutamine (polyQ)-expanded huntingtin form phase-separated droplets that mature into stable aggregates, sequestering [[RNA-Binding Proteins]] and disrupting RNA processing. These pathological condensates thus compete with and corrupt normal phase-separated assemblies, linking the biophysics of LLPS to [[Neuroinflammation]] and neuronal dysfunction. Understanding LLPS therefore bridges the noncanonical cGAS scaffold function with the proteinopathy-driven pathology of diseases like [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Huntington's Disease]].
