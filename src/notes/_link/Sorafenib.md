# Sorafenib

**Sorafenib** (Nexavar) is an orally active multi-kinase inhibitor targeting RAF ([[RAF]]/[[MEK]]/ERK), VEGFR, and PDGFR kinases, approved as first-line therapy for advanced [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] (HCC) and certain renal/thyroid cancers. Beyond anti-angiogenic and proliferative blockade, a major mechanism of its anti-tumor activity is the induction of [[Ferroptosis]] in cancer cells.

## Pharmacology & Targets

Sorafenib inhibits multiple receptor and intracellular tyrosine kinases as well as RAF serine/threonine kinases, dampening the [[RAS]]–[[RAF]]–[[MEK]]–ERK proliferative signaling axis. It suppresses tumor angiogenesis (VEGFR/PDGFR) and promotes apoptosis at high doses. Critically, in HCC it engages ferroptotic cell death.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

Studies (e.g., Louandre et al.) showed that sorafenib-induced cell death in HCC is ferroptotic: it depletes [[Glutathione]] and inactivates [[GPX4]], leading to [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and iron-dependent death; this is rescued by [[Ferrostatin-1]] and [[Liproxstatin-1]]. Loss of the retinoblastoma (Rb) protein sensitizes HCC cells to sorafenib ferroptosis, and negative regulators of ferroptosis in HCC—including [[NRF2]], metallothionein-1G, [[CISD1]], and [[p53]]—confer resistance. The [[RAS]]/Raf/[[MEK]] pathway is thus both a sorafenib target and a ferroptosis-modulating route (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / HCC").

> [!info] Ferroptosis explains sorafenib sensitivity
> The ferroptotic component helps explain why some HCC subtypes respond better to sorafenib and why ferroptosis inducers can overcome resistance—a finding with direct therapeutic implications.

## Clinical Relevance

Sorafenib remains a cornerstone in HCC, but resistance is common.Strategies to enhance its ferroptotic mechanism—combining with GPX4 inhibitors, iron donors, or blocking NRF2/CISD1—are active investigation areas. It also informs ferroptosis-based regimens in [[Renal Cell Carcinoma]] and [[Melanoma]].

> [!tip] Resistance markers
> High NRF2, MT-1G, CISD1, or p53 activity predicts sorafenib ferroptosis-resistance; targeting these could resensitize refractory HCC.
