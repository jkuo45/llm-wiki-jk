# UK5099

**UK5099** (α-cyano-β-(1-phenylindol-3-yl)acrylate) is a selective small-molecule inhibitor of the [[MPC|mitochondrial pyruvate carrier]] (MPC1/MPC2), the transporter that imports [[Pyruvate]] into the mitochondrial matrix. It is widely used experimentally to divert glucose-derived pyruvate away from mitochondrial oxidation toward [[Lactate]].

## Mechanism

UK5099 binds the MPC and blocks pyruvate uptake, reducing TCA-cycle substrate supply and mitochondrial [[Acetyl-CoA]] production. Cells treated with UK5099 show increased lactate secretion and reduced TCA intermediates, consistent with impaired pyruvate import. It inhibits pyruvate-dependent oxygen consumption with an IC50 of ~50 nM and was originally synthesized in the 1970s as an analogue of α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamate; the MPC1/MPC2 heterodimer was only identified as its target in 2012.

> [!warning] Not MPC-selective at high doses
> Selective MPC inhibition occurs in a narrow low-dose window (roughly 2–5 µM; only <10 µM is generally considered MPC-selective). At higher concentrations UK5099 also impairs glutamate oxidation, mitochondrial membrane potential, OXPHOS, and [[HIF-1α]] stabilization — and it inhibits the [[NLRP3]] [[Inflammasome]] and [[Pyroptosis]] **independently of MPC** (Ran et al., *Adv Sci* 2024). Conclusions about MPC biology drawn from UK5099 alone therefore require genetic confirmation (MPC1/MPC2 knockdown or knockout).

## Use in Senescence Research

In senescent cells, MPC inhibition with UK5099 suppresses [[SASP|SASP]] gene expression and secretion across multiple senescence models without affecting the cell-cycle arrest (p16/p21, EdU incorporation, DNA damage foci), establishing mitochondrial pyruvate entry as a druggable node of the SASP metabolic checkpoint.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP|Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP]]
> UK5099 treatment reduced senescence-associated TCA intermediates and SASP output, linking [[Pyruvate]] import to the mitochondrial citrate–[[Acetyl-CoA]] axis that sustains [[H3K27ac]] at SASP loci.

## Role in Necroptosis

[[Necroptosis]] is a [[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]]-driven lytic death whose mitochondrial arm depends on [[Pyruvate]] oxidation. [[RIPK3]] phosphorylates the E3 subunit of the [[Pyruvate Dehydrogenase]] complex (PDC) at Thr135, increasing aerobic respiration and mitochondrial [[ROS]], which feed back on the necrosome to promote [[RIPK1]] autophosphorylation and RIPK3 recruitment. Because [[MPC]]-mediated pyruvate import feeds this axis, blocking mitochondrial pyruvate uptake suppresses TNF-induced necroptosis.

- Depletion of pyruvate, inhibition of mitochondrial pyruvate transport, or blockade of PDC all inhibit TNF-induced necroptosis (Yang et al., *Nat Cell Biol* 2018).
- The Han laboratory's commentary explicitly summarizes this as inhibited pyruvate transport/carrier function blunting necroptosis (Qiu, Zhang & Han, *Cell Death Differ* 2018).

> [!warning] Context-dependence and selectivity caveats
> The anti-necroptotic effect is not universal. In hypoxia- and chemotherapy-induced necroptosis of colorectal cancer cells, glucose/pyruvate protection was **not** reversed by UK5099 because cytosolic pyruvate scavenges mitochondrial superoxide non-enzymatically, without entering mitochondria (Huang et al., *Cell Death Dis* 2013). Likewise, widespread mitochondrial depletion via [[Mitophagy]] does not always compromise necroptosis (Tait et al., *Cell Rep* 2013). Given UK5099's MPC-independent effects (see above), attributing necroptosis suppression specifically to MPC inhibition requires genetic validation.
