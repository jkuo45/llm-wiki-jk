# Calpain

**Calpain** is a family of calcium-activated, non-lysosomal cysteine proteases. The ubiquitous isoforms calpain-1 (μ-calpain, activated by micromolar Ca2+) and calpain-2 (m-calpain, millimolar Ca2+) cleave substrates involved in cytoskeletal remodelling, signalling, and cell death. Calpain is held in check by calpastatin, and its dysregulation by sustained Ca2+ overload is implicated in [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], [[Neurodegeneration]], and [[Necrosis|necrotic]] cell death.

## Mechanism

Calpain activation requires Ca2+-dependent displacement of the inhibitory calpastatin and autoproteolytic processing. Its substrates include cytoskeletal and membrane proteins, [[Bid]] (to tBID), BAX, and [[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]]. Because it is Ca2+-gated, calpain is positioned to translate the Ca2+ influx of [[TRPM2]] gating or [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] release into downstream death signalling.

## Role in parthanatos

[[Apoptosis-Inducing Factor|AIF]] release after [[PARP1]] hyperactivation is thought to proceed mainly via direct [[PAR]] binding, but calpain I is a competing candidate:

- Calpain I cleaves and releases AIF from isolated mitochondria, generating the 62 → 57 kDa truncated form; this proteolysis is a plausible release mechanism downstream of TRPM2/ER Ca2+.
- Calpain cleaves [[Bid]] to tBID, which activates [[BAX]] to permeabilize mitochondria and release AIF — a caspase-independent, Ca2+–calpain–BID–BAX–AIF axis.
- **Evidence against a central role:** calpain inhibition or knockout still permits AIF translocation and death in some MNNG models, so calpain is likely a context-dependent amplifier rather than an obligatory step.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Parthanatos Moura 2024 molecular mechanisms more questions than answers|Moura et al. 2024]]
> The review frames AIF release as unresolved, with calpain I, the [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore]], and direct [[PAR]] binding as non-exclusive candidates. Which route operates — and whether calpain is required — remains an open question; Ca2+ chelation protects in H2O2/renal-I/R but not MNNG models, so the calpain arm is insult-specific.
