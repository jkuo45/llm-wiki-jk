# Choline

**Choline** (trimethylammonioethanol, historically "vitamin B4") is a
quaternary ammonium compound that the human body cannot synthesise de novo and
must obtain from diet. It sits at the intersection of three large metabolic
demands: [[Phospholipid]] synthesis, neurotransmitter synthesis, and
one-carbon metabolism.

## Three fates

Choline enters cells through specific choline transporters (CHT/SLC5A3 and
CTP/phosphocholine cytidylyltransferase-coupled uptake) and is immediately
committed to one of three pathways.

**Phosphatidylcholine and sphingomyelin.** The Kennedy pathway phosphorylates
choline to phosphocholine via choline kinase, then to CDP-choline. When
phosphatidylcholine is broken down by [[Phospholipase C]]-mediated signalling,
the choline head group is reclaimed by the
[[Arginine Glycine Amidinotransferase|PCYT2]] salvage enzyme rather than being
oxidised, which makes choline demand unusually sensitive to membrane
turnover. Choline is the limiting substrate for
[[Sphingomyelin]] synthesis, and sphingomyelin-rich lipid rafts in
[[Plasma Membrane|membrane]] microdomains are a structural, not merely
metabolic, requirement.

**Acetylcholine.** Choline is taken up by the high-affinity
choline transporter CHT1 at cholinergic nerve terminals, where
[[Phospholipase C|phospholipase C]]-coupled receptor signalling activates it;
the enzyme choline acetyltransferase then transfers the acetyl group from
[[Acetyl-CoA]] to form acetylcholine. Because neurons cannot synthesise
choline, its availability sets the ceiling on acetylcholine synthesis —
which is why choline reuptake is such a well-validated Alzheimer's disease
target, though the clinical trial results for cholinergic precursor
supplementation have been mixed.

**Betaine and the methylation cycle.** In liver and kidney, choline is oxidised
by the mitochondrial enzyme betaine dehydrogenase to [[Betaine]], which
participates in the [[Methylation Cycle]] as an alternative methyl donor to
[[Methylfolate]] for the remethylation of [[Homocysteine]] to
[[Methionine]]. This remethylation reaction is catalysed by
[[Methionine Synthase]] and requires cobalamin ([[Cobalamin]]) as a cofactor.
When folate-dependent remethylation is compromised — for example in folate
deficiency — the choline-to-betaine route is the only remaining way to keep
homocysteine in check, which is why folate deficiency causes a
fatty liver phenotype that responds to choline.

> [!info] TMAO
> The gut microbial enzyme TMA-lysine metyltransferase converts dietary
> choline, carnitine, and betaine into trimethylamine, which the liver oxidises
> to [[Trimethylamine N-oxide|TMAO]]. Elevated circulating TMAO is robustly
> associated with cardiovascular and renal disease in observational cohorts,
> though whether it is a cause or a marker remains contested — the
> causality evidence is considerably weaker than the association.

## Requirements and deficiency

US adequate intake is 550 mg/day for adult men and 425 mg/day for adult women,
raised during pregnancy and lactation. Roughly 90–95% of the requirement is
disbursed by the de novo phosphatidylcholine synthesis pathway, which is why
liver and muscle phospholipid content falls within days of a choline-deficient
diet. Deficiency is rare outside of parenteral nutrition without choline
supplementation, TPN-induced liver disease, and chronic alcohol use, where it
contributes to hepatic steatosis. Rodent models show a distinctive
phenotype: phosphatidylcholine depletion, mitochondrial dysfunction with
altered cardiolipin, hepatocellular apoptosis, and — after weeks —
spontaneous hepatocellular carcinoma.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The most-cited human data (Framingham Offspring cohort analyses linking
> choline intake to plasma TMAO and to all-cause and cardiovascular mortality)
> are observational and subject to residual confounding by overall diet
> quality. Choline intakes in Western populations
> commonly fall short of the adequate intake, but no trial has established that
> raising intake improves clinical outcomes in the general population. Choline
> supplementation also produces a distinct fishy body odour through TMAO
> excretion, which limits dose and adherence.

## Connections

- [[Betaine]] — the direct oxidative product of choline by betaine
  dehydrogenase, and the form in which choline enters the methylation cycle
  as a methyl donor. The two are functionally interchangeable as methyl-group
  sources but not as nutrients: betaine cannot back-synthesise
  phosphatidylcholine.
- [[Phospholipase C]] — hydrolyses phosphatidylcholine to yield
  diacylglycerol and phosphocholine, recycling the choline head group and
  linking membrane signalling to choline demand. This is the loop that makes
  choline a signalling-responsive nutrient rather than a static one.
- [[Cobalamin]] — methylcobalamin is the obligate cofactor for the
  methionine synthase reaction that betaine feeds. Choline status and B12
  status are therefore coupled, and either deficiency impairs the same
  remethylation step.
- [[Trimethylamine N-oxide]] — the gut-microbial oxidation product of
  choline-derived trimethylamine, the main reason high-choline diets attract
  cardiovascular scrutiny despite choline's protective effects in liver.
- [[Methyl Donor]] — defines choline's role as one input into the methylation
  cycle and connects it to epigenetic machinery via [[SAMe]] and
  [[DNA Methylation]].
- [[Sphingomyelin]] — a choline-containing sphingolipid whose synthesis is
  choline-limited and whose loss destabilises membrane lipid rafts.
