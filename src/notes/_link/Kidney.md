## Overview

The kidneys are paired retroperitoneal organs that perform filtration, reabsorption, and secretion to maintain fluid, electrolyte, and acid–base homeostasis. They also serve endocrine roles (erythropoietin, renin, activation of vitamin D) and clear metabolic waste and advanced glycation end products (AGEs).

## Biological Role

- **Filtration & excretion:** Glomerular filtration removes waste while preserving proteins and cells.
- **Hormonal regulation:** Renin release modulates blood pressure; erythropoietin stimulates erythropoiesis.
- **Redox & glycation clearance:** Impaired renal function slows AGE clearance, linking kidney health to systemic glycation and aging.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) accelerates vascular calcification, AGE accumulation, and cardiovascular risk, and is a major comorbidity of aging. Renal dysfunction intersects with the adrenochrome/glycation axis through impaired AGE clearance. Dialysis and transplantation are definitive therapies for end-stage disease.
