# DMT1

**DMT1** (divalent metal-ion transporter 1), encoded by **SLC11A2**, is the principal importer of Fe²⁺ (and other divalent cations such as Mn²⁺, Zn²⁺, Cd²⁺) from endosomes into the cytosol. It is the gateway that releases transferrin-derived iron into the labile iron pool (LIP), the redox-active reservoir that powers the [[Fenton Reaction]] and thus [[Ferroptosis]].

## Structure & Biochemistry

DMT1 is a proton-coupled symporter of the SLC11/NRAMP family, with multiple splice isoforms differing in their C-terminal tails and subcellular itineraries. It transports Fe²⁺ down its electrochemical gradient in exchange for H⁺, coupling iron release to endosomal acidification.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

After [[Transferrin]]–Fe³⁺ is endocytosed via [[Transferrin receptor 1]] and Fe³⁺ is reduced to Fe²⁺ by [[STEAP3]], DMT1 transports Fe²⁺ across the endosomal membrane into the cytosol, expanding the LIP. This iron fuels Fenton chemistry that peroxidates PUFA-phospholipids; when [[GPX4]]/[[FSP1]] antioxidant capacity is exceeded, ferroptosis is executed (review "Role of iron metabolism in ferroptosis"). DMT1 therefore sits squarely on the iron-supply side of the ferroptotic cascade.

> [!info] Coupling to transferrin cycle
> DMT1 is the downstream effector of the transferrin cycle for cytosolic iron delivery; blocking it (or upstream TFR1/STEAP3) starves the Fenton reaction and protects against erastin-induced ferroptosis.

## Physiological Function

DMT1 is essential for dietary iron absorption (duodenum), erythroid iron supply, and recycling from macrophages. It also handles other metals, linking it to metal homeostasis and toxicity.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

DMT1 dysfunction underlies microcytic anemia (DMT1 loss) and contributes to iron loading when up-regulated. In ferroptosis, enhanced DMT1-mediated iron import sensitizes cells/tumors; it is a potential node for modulating iron-dependent cell death in [[Cancer]] and neurodegeneration.
