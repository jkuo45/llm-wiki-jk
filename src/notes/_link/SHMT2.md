# SHMT2

**SHMT2** (serine hydroxymethyltransferase 2) is a mitochondrial [[Vitamin B6|PLP]]-dependent enzyme that catalyzes the reversible conversion of [[Serine]] to glycine, transferring a one-carbon unit from serine to tetrahydrofolate (THF) to form 5,10-methylene-THF. This reaction is the primary entry point for **one-carbon metabolism** in the mitochondrial compartment and is essential for nucleotide biosynthesis, [[NADPH]] regeneration, and methylation reactions in proliferating cells.

## Structure & Mechanism

- SHMT2 is a homotetrameric enzyme; each subunit binds one PLP (vitamin B6) cofactor and one THF molecule.
- The reaction: **L-Serine + THF → Glycine + 5,10-methylene-THF + H₂O**
- SHMT2 is the mitochondrial isoform; its cytoplasmic counterpart is **SHMT1**. The two isoforms differ in their kinetic properties and physiological roles — SHMT2 is the predominant isoform upregulated in cancer.
- 5,10-methylene-THF produced by SHMT2 feeds into the mitochondrial folate cycle, connecting serine catabolism to nucleotide synthesis, methylation, and redox balance.

## One-Carbon Metabolism

> [!important]
> SHMT2 sits at the critical junction between serine catabolism and the mitochondrial folate cycle. In proliferating cells — especially cancer cells — SHMT2-driven one-carbon metabolism provides:
> - **One-carbon units** (5,10-methylene-THF) for *de novo* purine and thymidylate synthesis
> - **NADPH** (via downstream MTHFD2) for reductive biosynthesis and redox defense
> - **Glycine** for protein synthesis and glutathione production

One-carbon metabolism occurs in both mitochondria and the cytoplasm, but mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism (driven by SHMT2 → MTHFD2 → MTHFD1L) is now recognized as the dominant source of one-carbon units in cancer cells. The mitochondrial pathway accounts for ~75% of total one-carbon production in rapidly dividing cells.

## Role in Cancer

### Upregulation and Prognosis

- SHMT2 is significantly upregulated in colorectal cancer (CRC), breast cancer, lung cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and other malignancies.
- High SHMT2 expression correlates with **poor patient survival** in CRC, consistent with its role in fueling nucleotide biosynthesis and tumor growth.

### SIRT3-Mediated Deacetylation

> [!info]
> Source: Wei et al., *Nature Communications* 2018;9:4468 (PMID: 30367038; cited 187×)
> [[SIRT3]] deacetylates SHMT2 at **Lys95**, enhancing its enzymatic activity. Acetylation at K95 inhibits SHMT2 activity and promotes its degradation via macroautophagy. SIRT3-mediated deacetylation of SHMT2 **promotes colorectal carcinogenesis** by increasing serine consumption and NADPH levels.

- This is a key oncogenic mechanism: SIRT3 deacetylation of SHMT2 increases serine-to-glycine conversion, boosting NADPH levels and nucleotide precursor availability for CRC cell proliferation.
- SHMT2-K95-Ac (the acetylated, inactive form) **decreases** CRC cell proliferation and tumor growth *in vivo* through attenuation of serine consumption and reduction in NADPH levels.
- This mechanism positions SIRT3 as an oncogene in CRC through its metabolic reprogramming of serine metabolism.

### SHMT2 and Chemoresistance

- 5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) is a cornerstone of CRC chemotherapy. 5-FU-resistant CRC cells display a strong **serine dependency** — they upregulate SHMT2 to compartmentalize one-carbon metabolism in mitochondria, supporting purine biosynthesis and DNA damage repair.
- SHMT2-driven mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism is therefore both a driver of CRC progression and a mechanism of **chemoresistance** to 5-FU.
- Targeting serine availability or SHMT2 activity can revert 5-FU resistance in preclinical models.

### Other PTM Regulation of SHMT2

- **SIRT5** desuccinylates SHMT2 at **Lys280**, also promoting its activity and cancer cell proliferation (demonstrated in hepatocellular carcinoma).
- **HDAC11** defatty acylates SHMT2 at K95, regulating type I interferon signaling in immune cells.
- These competing modifications on the same residue (K95) illustrate the complexity of SHMT2 regulation by the PTM landscape.

### Interactions with Other One-Carbon Enzymes

- **MTHFD2**: The mitochondrial bifunctional enzyme that processes 5,10-methylene-THF further to 10-formyl-THF; also a deacetylation substrate of SIRT3. Co-upregulated with SHMT2 in many cancers.
- **MTHFD1L**: Produces formate from 10-formyl-THF in mitochondria, which is exported to the cytoplasm for nucleotide synthesis via MTHFD1.
- **MTHFR**: Converts 5,10-methylene-THF to 5-methyl-THF for methionine/homocysteine remethylation.
