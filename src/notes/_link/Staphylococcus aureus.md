# Staphylococcus aureus

Staphylococcus aureus is a Gram-positive bacterium. [[TLR2]] is required for [[Vital NETosis]] during murine in vivo skin infection with S. aureus, and TLR2 or [[TLR4]] blockade suppresses NET formation induced by in vitro exposure (Yipp et al. 2012; Wan et al. 2017). Staphylococcal protein A (SpA) suppresses neutrophil [[Phagocytosis]] of S. aureus but in doing so promotes [[NETosis]], while extracellular adherence protein (Eap) suppresses NET formation and function (Hoppenbrouwers et al. 2018; Eisenbeis et al. 2018).

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> A selective [[PAD4]] inhibitor abrogated vital NETosis induced by [[Ionomycin]] or S. aureus (Lewis et al. 2015), and [[GSDMD-p30]] is reported to target bacterial membranes to kill extracellular S. aureus (Ding et al. 2016; Liu et al. 2016).
