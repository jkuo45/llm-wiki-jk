# CTPI2

**CTPI2** (citrate transport inhibitor 2) is a third-generation pharmacological inhibitor of the mitochondrial citrate carrier [[SLC25A1]]. It binds SLC25A1 with high affinity and specificity, blocking the export of [[Citrate]] from the mitochondrial matrix to the cytosol. By limiting cytosolic citrate, CTPI2 reduces [[Acetyl-CoA]] production by [[ACLY]] and thereby histone acetylation at [[SASP]] loci.

## Overview

CTPI2 was developed as a selective tool to interrogate mitochondrial citrate export. By inhibiting SLC25A1, CTPI2 reduces the availability of the substrate for cytosolic acetyl-CoA generation, providing a pharmacological handle on the mitochondrial citrate–acetyl-CoA axis. It is orally bioavailable and has been administered to mice by gavage.

## Mechanism of Action

- Binds SLC25A1 with high affinity and specificity (third-generation inhibitor)
- Reduces cytosolic (but not mitochondrial) acetyl-CoA levels, as confirmed by subcellular fractionation and mass spectrometry
- Decreases global nuclear acetylation (pan-acetylated lysine, [[H3K27ac]], [[H3K9ac]])
- Reduces [[Chromatin]] accessibility and H3K27ac at SASP loci
- Does not broadly impair mitochondrial function: OXPHOS gene expression, ATP production, coupling efficiency, mitochondrial ROS, and mitochondrial morphology are largely unchanged
- Metabolomics shows reduced citrate, isocitrate, and *cis*-aconitate in whole-cell extracts

## Effects on the SASP

In vitro, CTPI2 suppresses SASP gene expression and secretion in a dose-dependent manner across multiple senescence models (irradiation-, doxorubicin-, and replicative-induced), both when initiated shortly after senescence induction and in fully established senescent cells — without altering cell cycle arrest, p16/p21, HMGB1 localization, or DNA damage foci.

## In Vivo Effects in Aged Mice

> [!important] CTPI2 Improves Healthspan in Aged Mice
> Aged (19-month-old) C57BL/6 mice treated with CTPI2 (50 mg kg^-1^, 3×/week for 3 months) showed:
> - More lustrous fur, reduced alopecia and fur greying
> - Delayed onset of [[Frailty]] (31-parameter frailty index)
> - Improved neuromuscular performance: coordination/balance (tightrope test), strength (hanging and grip strength tests), preserved muscle integrity
> - Reduced SASP gene expression in muscle, liver, and heart; reduced circulating inflammatory cytokines; reduced SenMayo senescence signature in liver
> - Reduced pro-inflammatory [[CCR2]]^+^ [[Macrophages|macrophage]] markers in female hearts
> - Unchanged p16/p21 and unchanged senescent cell abundance — SASP modulation rather than senescent cell clearance
> - No broad metabolic reprogramming (glucose tolerance, body composition, metabolic rate, lipid metabolism largely unchanged)

CTPI2 was previously shown to improve metabolic dysfunction in diet-induced [[Steatohepatitis]] (reverting steatosis, glucose intolerance, and inflammation in NAFLD/NASH models).
