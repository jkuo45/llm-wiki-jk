# p53
[[p53]] (encoded by the [[p53|TP53]] gene; the gene and protein are consolidated as one entity in this vault) is a crucial tumor suppressor protein often referred to as the "guardian of the genome." It coordinates the cellular response to DNA damage by inducing cell cycle arrest, [[Apoptosis]], or [[Senescence]].
**p53** (also known as tumor protein P53 or TP53) is a crucial tumor suppressor protein that regulates the cell cycle and prevents cancer formation. It is often referred to as the "guardian of the genome."
## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency
In [[Cellular Reprogramming]], [[p53]] acts as a major roadblock to the generation of [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSCs). The process of viral transduction commonly used to deliver the [[Yamanaka Factors]] (OSKM) triggers an [[Innate Immunity|innate immune response]], which generates [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) and causes oxidative DNA damage. This damage activates [[p53]], which in turn initiates [[Apoptosis]] or [[Senescence]] in the transduced cells, significantly reducing the efficiency of iPSC induction.
While inhibiting [[p53]] can dramatically increase the efficiency of reprogramming, it also poses significant risks. iPSCs generated in the absence of [[p53]] are more susceptible to chromosomal aberrations and genomic instability, which increases the risk of [[Tumorigenicity|tumorigenesis]] in therapeutic applications. Therefore, finding ways to transiently modulate the [[Innate Immunity|innate immune response]] to avoid [[p53]] activation without permanently disabling it is a key area of research in optimizing iPSC technology.
## Role in Cancer
- **Mutation**: Mutations in the *TP53* gene are among the most common genetic alterations in human cancers.
- **Claudin-low T11**: A murine breast cancer model harboring a p53 mutation, used to study [[melittin]] selectivity.
## SIRT1–p53 Regulatory Axis
SIRT1 and p53 are intimately linked — they physically associate and mutually regulate each other's activity:
### SIRT1 Deacetylates p53
[[SIRT1]] deacetylates multiple lysine residues of p53:
- **Mouse p53**: Lys³¹⁷, Lys³⁷⁰, Lys³⁷⁹
- **Human p53**: Lys³²⁰, Lys³⁷³, Lys³⁸²
**Consequence of deacetylation**: Inhibits p53 transactivation activity and **suppresses apoptosis** in response to oxidative stress and DNA damage.
### HIC1–SIRT1–p53 Feedback Loop
A complex regulatory circuit governs this interaction:
1. Under normal conditions: **HIC1 (hypermethylated in cancer 1)** represses SIRT1 expression → promotes p53 activity and apoptosis under stress
2. Recovery from DNA damage: **p53 down-regulates HIC1** → induces SIRT1 transcription → SIRT1 deacetylates p53 → promotes cell survival
3. During aging: HIC1 hypermethylation → increased SIRT1 expression → p53 deacetylation → **increased cellular risk for neoplastic transformation**
This represents a SIRT1–HIC1–p53 feedback loop with potential implications for age-related cancer risk.
## p53 and Cellular Senescence
[[SIRT1]] can rescue mouse embryonic fibroblasts from PML-mediated premature cellular senescence by inhibiting p53's pro-apoptotic activity (via PML nuclear body recruitment).
Conversely, in SIRT1-deficient MEFs:
- Extended replicative potential
- Greater proliferative capacity following chronic sublethal stress
- This phenotype requires **p19ᴬᴿᶠ** and its downstream target p53
## p53 in SIRT1 Knockout Phenotype
**SIRT1-deficient mice** show:
- p53 hyperacetylation after DNA damage
- Increased sensitivity of thymocytes to ionising radiation
- However, elimination of p53 in SIRT1 knockout mice does **not** relieve the knockout phenotype, suggesting p53 hyperacetylation is not the primary cause of the SIRT1 knockout effects
## p53 in Cancer Contexts
### Hepatocellular Carcinoma
In hepatocarcinoma cells (HepG2), **p33ᴵᴺᴳ¹ᵇ** interacts with SIRT1, inhibiting its ability to deacetylate p53 → negatively regulates transcription of AFP (α-fetoprotein), a diagnostic marker of hepatocellular carcinoma.
### Prostate Cancer
FOXO1 deacetylation by SIRT1 (enhanced by FHL2) in prostate cancer cells has been proposed to promote tumorigenesis in response to increased aging-related stress — representing a context where SIRT1/p53 pathway may have oncogenic implications.
### Kidney Disease
SIRT1 diminishes mesangial cell apoptosis induced by oxidative stress **via p53 deacetylation**, protecting against glomerular disease.
### Cardiac Protection
SIRT1 protects cardiac myocytes from programmed cell death (via serum starvation or PARP-1 activation) in a **p53-dependent manner**.
## Regulatory Complexity
The SIRT1/p53 pathway involves complex mechanisms beyond simple p53 deacetylation, including:
- Discrepant reports on whether SIRT1 impairs thymocyte/splenocyte survival following radiation
- Context-dependence of whether SIRT1 acts as tumour suppressor or oncogene
- Mouse transgenic studies now critical for resolving _in vivo_ roles

## Sex Differences

> [!important] p53 is regulated by sex hormones and the X chromosome
> The p53 pathway shows pervasive sex-dependent regulation: estrogen bidirectionally crosstalks with p53 (activation + sequestration), testosterone reduced p-p53 in a C2C12 oxidative model, and X-linked miR-504 (hypothesized dosage effect) provides an additional layer. These differences contribute to sex-disparate cancer incidence, therapeutic response, and aging trajectories.

### Estrogen–p53 Bidirectional Regulation

Estrogen bidirectionally crosstalks with p53 — transcriptional activation via CTF-1/NF-κB/ERα but also E₂–ERα physical sequestration inhibiting BAX/PUMA/NOXA. Context-dependent outcomes. Specific ERα-element counts need primary citation (prior “4 elements / Desai Cells 2025” unverified; Huang et al., *Oncotarget* 2015).

### Testosterone Inhibits p53

- In skeletal muscle C2C12 oxidative model, testosterone counteracts apoptosis by inactivating p53 via FoxO3a (Pronsato et al., *Steroids* 2017 — narrow in vitro, do not generalize systemically).
- In prostate cancer, p53 and androgen receptor (AR) reciprocally inhibit each other's functions: p53 binds the AR promoter and disrupts AR N-to-C interaction; DHT decreases p53 levels. This bidirectional inhibition is lost during progression to castration-resistant disease.

### X Chromosome and p53 Pathway Regulation

- The X chromosome encodes **~10% of human miRNAs**. X-linked miR-504 (Xq26.3) targets TP53 3'-UTR (Hu *Mol Cell* 2010); female dosage advantage via escape is hypothesis (no evidence miR-504 escapes XCI).
- **KDM6A/UTX** (escapes X-inactivation) enhances p21 transcription, providing females with a unique dosage advantage in p53-mediated senescence.
- Females have a **"Non-Expressed Mutation" barrier**: X-linked p53 regulators can mutate but remain unexpressed in one allele, providing a buffer against loss-of-function that males lack.

### TP53 Mutation Frequency by Sex

- Males have **higher population frequency of TP53-mutant cancers** across 12/13 non-reproductive types, correlating with poorer survival (Haupt et al., 2019; Dunford et al., *Nature Genetics* 2017) — incidence + per-tumor rate combined.
- Females with TP53 mutations in NSCLC show **better immunotherapy response** (6.1 vs 2.6 months PFS), suggesting sex-specific clinical implications.
- TP53 mutation frequency is higher in male tumors partly because females have biallelic expression of X-linked p53 regulators, providing redundancy.

### SNP72 (Pro72Arg) — Sex-Specific Effects

- Pro72 promotes senescence over apoptosis → weaker tumor suppression but **+3 years median survival** in a Danish cohort.
- Longevity benefit was **female-exclusive** in a German cohort.
- Sex-specific cancer risk associations reported (needs primary verification; prior “Desai Cells 2025” unverified).

### p53 and Sex-Specific Senescence Arms

- Female cells favor **p53→p21→senescence** (cell-cycle arrest); male cells favor **p53→apoptosis** (cell death).
- In utero ovarian programming biases females toward p21-dependent senescence; KDM6A mediates this through X-linked epigenetic regulation.
- This sex difference in senescence arm selection has implications for cancer therapy (senescence-inducing treatments may be more effective in females) and aging (males accumulate more apoptotic cell loss).

### Trp53⁻/⁻ Mouse Phenotype by Sex

- Standard phenotype: thymic lymphomas in both sexes.
- When lymphoma is prevented: **females develop more osteosarcomas**.
- Female Trp53R210X/R210X mice are poor breeders and smaller, suggesting sex-specific developmental roles for p53.

### Cross-Link: Unified Sex-Specific Cell-Death Framework

- p53 sits at the **decision point** of the vault's unified cell-death framework ([[Apoptosis]] ↔ [[Caspases]] ↔ p53 ↔ [[Bcl-2]]): estrogen drives p53 toward pro-apoptotic BAX/PUMA/NOXA targets in some tissues while sequestering it (anti-apoptotic) in others, and sex hormones bias the p53-dependent choice between [[Apoptosis]] (via [[Caspases]]) and [[Senescence]].
- Females favor the p53→p21→senescence arm (see above), while male cells bias toward the [[PARP1]]-dependent necrotic arm of the same p53/AIF axis — see [[Apoptosis]] and [[Caspases]] for the full XX-caspase / XY-PARP-1 physiology.
