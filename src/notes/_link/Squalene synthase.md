# Squalene synthase

**Squalene synthase** (encoded by **FDFT1**) catalyzes the first committed step of sterol biosynthesis in the [[Mevalonate pathway]]: it condenses two molecules of farnesyl diphosphate into squalene. Although best known for its role in cholesterol production, squalene synthase sits at a metabolic branch point that influences ferroptosis vulnerability by governing the flux of isoprenoids and downstream sterols/CoQ10 precursors relevant to [[GPX4]] and [[FSP1]]–[[Coenzyme Q10]] antioxidant capacity.

## Structure & Biochemistry

Squalene synthase is an ER-membrane enzyme with two catalytic activities (condensation and NADPH-dependent reduction) that convert farnesyl diphosphate to squalene, the precursor to lanosterol and ultimately cholesterol. It is the target of cholesterol-lowering squalene synthase inhibitors under investigation for dyslipidemia.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

By committing isoprenoids to the sterol branch, squalene synthase modulates the balance between sterol and non-sterol isoprenoid outputs of the MVA pathway. Because the MVA pathway controls selenocysteine tRNA maturation (affecting [[GPX4]]) and CoQ10 synthesis (used by [[FSP1]]), perturbations at the squalene synthase node can indirectly shift ferroptosis thresholds. Inhibition that diverts flux away from sterols toward other branches may alter the cellular antioxidant landscape.

> [!info] Branch-point significance
> Squalene synthase marks the divergence of "sterol vs. non-sterol" fates of mevalonate. Where the pathway funnels into cholesterol, less substrate may remain for CoQ10 and selenoprotein maturation—linking sterol metabolism to ferroptotic resilience.

## Physiological Function

Essential for cholesterol and downstream steroid/hormone biosynthesis; a central node of lipid homeostasis.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Squalene synthase inhibitors (e.g., lapaquistat) were developed for hypercholesterolemia. Their effect on the ferroptotic set-point is indirect but mechanistically plausible via MVA-branch rerouting. Exploring squalene synthase modulation as a sensitizer to ferroptosis in [[Cancer]] remains largely preclinical.
