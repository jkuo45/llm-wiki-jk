# Guanylate Cyclase

**Overview:** [[Guanylate Cyclase]] (GC) enzymes convert GTP to [[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate]] (cGMP), a second messenger that activates protein kinase G (PKG) and regulates smooth-muscle tone, platelet aggregation, and neuronal signaling. Two families exist: the **soluble** isoform ([[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]], sGC) and **particulate** receptor isoforms (pGC) activated by natriuretic peptides.

**Mechanism and regulation:** sGC is the principal receptor for [[Nitric Oxide]] (NO); NO binds the enzyme's heme, markedly increasing cGMP production. cGMP is degraded by phosphodiesterases, terminating the signal. The NO→sGC→cGMP→PKG axis is the basis of nitrate vasodilators and endogenous vascular homeostasis.

> [!note] Methylene blue target
> [[Methylene blue]] inhibits guanylate cyclase (specifically sGC), blocking NO→cGMP signaling — the basis of its vasoconstrictive effect in [[Vasoplegic Shock]].

**Physiological and clinical relevance:** cGMP signaling mediates [[Vasodilation]], penile erection, and renoprotection. Excessive NO/cGMP in distributive shock causes refractory hypotension, rescued by methylene blue's GC inhibition. Particulate GCs respond to atrial natriuretic peptide to promote natriuresis. Dysregulation is implicated in hypertension and erectile dysfunction.
