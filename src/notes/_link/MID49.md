# MID49

## Overview

MID49 (mitochondrial dynamics protein 49, also known as MIEF2) is an outer mitochondrial membrane protein that serves as an adaptor for [[DRP1]] recruitment during [[Mitochondrial Fission]]. Along with [[MID51]], it forms part of the receptor complex that tethers DRP1 to the mitochondrial surface, working in concert with [[MFF]] and [[FIS1]] to mediate fission.

## Mechanism of Action

MID49 physically interacts with the DRP1 complex, including MFF, to facilitate DRP1 oligomerization and GTPase-dependent membrane constriction. The regulation of MID49 levels has a dose-dependent effect on mitochondrial morphology:

- **Low MID49 levels** enhance fission by allowing more efficient DRP1 recruitment and activity
- **Overexpression of MID49** paradoxically leads to mitochondrial elongation by recruiting inactive DRP1 in a non-productive complex, sequestering it from functional fission sites

This bidirectional regulation highlights MID49's role as a fine-tuning regulator of the fission-fusion balance.

## Physiological Function

MID49 contributes to maintaining [[Mitochondrial Integrity]] by regulating organelle number, distribution, and quality control through fission. Proper MID49 expression is essential for:

- Mitochondrial quality control via segregation of damaged portions for [[Mitophagy]]
- Cell division requiring mitochondrial partitioning
- Metabolic adaptation through organelle morphology changes

## Pathology

Epigenetic dysregulation of MID49 (and [[MID51]]) has been implicated in [[Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension]]. Aberrant expression disrupts the fission-fusion balance, contributing to mitochondrial dysfunction in pulmonary vascular cells.
