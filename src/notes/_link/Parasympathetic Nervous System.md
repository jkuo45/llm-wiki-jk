# Parasympathetic Nervous System

The **[[Parasympathetic Nervous System|parasympathetic nervous system (PNS)]]** is the "rest, digest, and repair" branch of the [[Autonomic Nervous System|autonomic nervous system]]. Its cranial (vagal) and sacral outflows slow the heart, stimulate digestion and anabolism, and promote cellular repair. Because the PNS permits greater beat-to-beat fluctuation, its activity *raises* [[Heart Rate Variability|HRV]].

## Vagus Nerve Dominance

The [[Vagus Nerve|vagus nerve]] (cranial nerve X) is the principal parasympathetic efferent to the heart. High "vagal tone" — strong, responsive parasympathetic drive — is the physiological signature of a recovered, adaptable system and the foundation of a high HRV.

## Role in Mitohormetic Recovery

After an acute stressor engages the [[Sympathetic Nervous System|sympathetic branch]], the PNS must rebound for [[Mitohormesis|mitohormetic]] adaptation to occur. This rebound pushes HRV back to or above baseline (supercompensation). If parasympathetic recovery is blocked by stacked stressors or poor sleep, HRV fails to rebound and mitochondrial quality control ([Mitophagy], [Mitochondrial Biogenesis]) stalls.
