# Kidney Fibrosis

**Kidney fibrosis** is the pathological accumulation of extracellular matrix in the renal tubulointerstitium and glomeruli, the final common pathway of nearly all progressive kidney diseases. Activation of myofibroblasts and fibroblasts — driven by TGF-β, oxidative stress, and metabolic dysfunction — replaces functional nephrons with scar tissue, causing progressive loss of filtration.

## Sirtuin Axis

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Overexpression abolishes TGF-β1-induced apoptosis and fibrosis via CTGF suppression; SRT1720 attenuates UUO-induced fibrosis; SIRT1 also represses HIF2α. Downregulation may additionally inhibit fibroblast activation via EGFR/PDGFR-β signaling.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Knockdown/chemical inhibition attenuates TGF-β1-induced fibroblast activation (MDM2); downregulation inhibits interstitial fibroblast activation.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Knockout mice are susceptible to severe fibrosis via PDH E1α hyperacetylation at K385.
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Proximal tubule-specific knockout aggravates UUO fibrosis; the MDL-800 activator mitigates it.

## Clinical Relevance

Fibrosis is the key determinant of progression to end-stage kidney disease. The sirtuin network — SIRT1/SIRT3/SIRT6 protective, SIRT2 context-dependent — offers multiple anti-fibrotic intervention points.
