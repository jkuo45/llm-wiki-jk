# Clostridium sporogenes

**Clostridium sporogenes** is an anaerobic, spore-forming, Gram-positive gut commensal and the best-characterized bacterial producer of [[Indole-3-propionic acid|indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]]. It is a model "auxotrophic" gut anaerobe: it cannot synthesize most amino acids and instead scavenges host and dietary amino acids — including reducing dietary [[Tryptophan]] to indole derivatives via the indole-pyruvate pathway.

## Metabolic role

- Reduces tryptophan → indole-3-lactate → **IPA**, a circulating radical-scavenging metabolite that acts on host [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor|AhR]] and [[Pregnane X Receptor|PXR]] signalling.
- In a 5xFAD Alzheimer's mouse model, oral administration of *C. sporogenes* (with xylan to enhance gut delivery) increased IPA levels and IPA-producing bacteria, improved cognition, reduced Aβ pathology, and restored gut-barrier integrity.

## Relevance to host physiology

- IPA produced by *C. sporogenes* mediates the microbiota–heart axis in HFpEF: cardiac [[SIRT3]] restoration and NAD-salvage enhancement depend on this metabolite.
- Reduced proportions of IPA-producing bacteria (including Clostridia) are observed in type 2 diabetes and aging, linking loss of this organism's metabolic output to cardiometabolic and neurodegenerative risk.
