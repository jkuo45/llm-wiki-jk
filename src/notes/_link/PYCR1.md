# PYCR1

**PYCR1** (pyrroline-5-carboxylate reductase 1) is a mitochondrial matrix enzyme that catalyzes the final and rate-limiting step of [[Proline]] biosynthesis — the NAD(P)H-dependent reduction of Δ1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate (P5C) to L-proline. PYCR1 is increasingly recognized as an oncogenic metabolic enzyme whose upregulation fuels tumor cell proliferation, migration, and drug resistance.

## Structure & Mechanism

- PYCR1 is a 319-amino-acid enzyme (~33.4 kDa) localized to the mitochondrial matrix.
- Crystal structures reveal that PYCR1 monomers assemble into a **pentamer of dimers** (decameric complex, >350 kDa). Each monomer has an N-terminal Rossmann-fold domain (NAD(P)H binding) and a six α-helix C-terminal dimerization domain.
- The substrate P5C binds at the interface between two dimers, and the enzyme is thought to function cooperatively as a decamer.
- Humans have three PYCR isoforms: **PYCR1**, PYCR2, and PYCR3 (also called PYCRL). PYCR1 is the predominant proline biosynthetic enzyme and is the isoform most consistently upregulated in cancer.
- PYCR1 uses NADH or NADPH as the reducing cofactor. The reaction is:
  **P5C + NAD(P)H + H⁺ → L-Proline + NAD(P)⁺**

## Role in Cancer

> [!important]
> PYCR1 is overexpressed in a broad range of human cancers — including breast cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, colorectal cancer, melanoma, and esophageal cancer — and high expression consistently correlates with **poor patient prognosis** and increased tumor aggressiveness.

### Proline-Driven Proliferation

- Tumor cells that overexpress PYCR1 divert glutamate into proline synthesis, providing both amino acid building blocks for protein synthesis and reducing equivalents for redox balance.
- Proline biosynthesis consumes NADH in the mitochondria, which can paradoxically shift the NAD⁺/NADH ratio and alter mitochondrial metabolism. This may support the **Warburg effect** by reducing the drive for OXPHOS.
- Proline itself feeds into the TCA cycle via proline catabolism (proline → P5C → glutamate), creating a metabolic loop that can generate ATP under nutrient stress.
- PYCR1-driven proline accumulation also supports **collagen biosynthesis** in the tumor microenvironment, facilitating invasion and metastasis.

### Signaling Pathways

PYCR1 modulates multiple oncogenic signaling axes:

- **JAK/STAT pathway**: PYCR1 knockdown inhibits proliferation, migration, and invasion via suppression of JAK/STAT signaling (demonstrated in lung adenocarcinoma).
- **STAT3-mediated p38 MAPK and NF-κB**: PYCR1 knockdown inhibits proliferation, drug resistance, and EMT in colorectal cancer through regulation of these pathways.
- **Epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT)**: PYCR1 promotes EMT, a key step in metastatic dissemination.

### SIRT3-Mediated Deacetylation

> [!info]
> Source: Chen et al., *Neoplasia* 2019;21(7):665–675
> [[SIRT3]] deacetylates PYCR1 at **Lys228**, enhancing its enzymatic activity and promoting proline biosynthesis. This is a critical oncogenic axis: SIRT3-mediated PYCR1 deacetylation directly facilitates tumor cell proliferation. This represents a context where SIRT3 acts as an **oncogene** rather than a tumor suppressor.

- The SIRT3–PYCR1 axis connects mitochondrial [[NAD+]] metabolism to amino acid biosynthesis in cancer. Under conditions where SIRT3 is upregulated (e.g., certain breast cancers, cervical cancer), the enhanced PYCR1 activity drives proline-dependent proliferation.
- This mechanism helps explain the dual (Janus) role of SIRT3 in cancer — the same deacetylase activity that protects normal cells from oxidative stress (via [[SOD2]], [[IDH2]]) can promote tumor growth when directed at metabolic enzymes like PYCR1.

### Therapeutic Implications

- PYCR1 inhibition reduces proliferation in liver, skin, and esophageal tumor models *in vitro* and *in vivo*.
- Fragment-based approaches to PYCR1 inhibition are under active investigation.
- Targeting the SIRT3–PYCR1 axis could simultaneously suppress proline-driven tumor metabolism and restore SIRT3's tumor-suppressive activities.

## Role in Aging

- [[Proline]] levels in plasma, muscle, and skin decline with age. PYCR1 expression changes may contribute to age-related shifts in proline homeostasis.
- Proline supplementation extends lifespan in yeast and *C. elegans*, partly through ROS scavenging. Whether PYCR1 activity changes with age in mammals remains under investigation.
