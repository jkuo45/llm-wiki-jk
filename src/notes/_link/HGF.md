# HGF

## Overview

Hepatocyte growth factor is the archetypal **hepatocyte mitogen** — a serum
factor discovered independently as a hepatocyte mitogen, as *hepatopoietin-A*,
and as *scatter factor* (an activity that dispersed Madin-Darby canine kidney
epithelial cells). It is the only known ligand for **[[c-Met]]/[[MET]]**
(UniProt P14210), a receptor tyrosine kinase that is one of the most
oncogenically activated receptors in human cancer.

HGF stands out among growth factors because it is produced almost exclusively
as an **inactive single-chain zymogen**. Proteolytic activation is the
regulatory step, and the activating proteases are themselves tightly
compartmentalised.

## Structure and domains

HGF is a 92 kDa glycoprotein precursor of 728 residues, structurally
belonging to the **plasminogen/apolipoprotein (PAI-1) family** of serine
protease zymogens.

- **N-terminal region** (residues ~1–31) — the **HGF/NK1 domain** ("HGF
  first-domain," also called NK1). Binds the N-terminal **Sema domain** of
  MET; this fragment alone is a competitive MET antagonist and has been
  developed as a drug lead (NK1, and the longer NK1/NK2 constructs).
- **N-terminal region** (residues ~37–123, ~128–206, ~211–288) — four
  **sushi (PCI/PDZ-like) domains** ("NLD1"–"NLD4"), a kringle-like module
  that supports the beta-chain as a whole and interacts with additional
  partners including [[uPA]] and HSPG-bound ECM components.
- **Serine protease (hepsin) domain** (residues ~391–469) — catalytically
  incompetent; substitutes a histidine for the usual serine at the active
  site, so HGF cannot cleave substrates. Its real function is as a binding
  surface that couples the α- and β-chains.
- **Chain cleavage sites** — a furin site at ~Arg294 and a second site at
  ~Arg374 generate the **α chain (32–494)** and the **β chain (495–728)**,
  which remain disulfide-linked. Both chains are required for full MET
  activation; the αβ heterodimer is the active species.
- **Six alternative splice isoforms** — including **HGF/NK1** and **HGF/NK2**,
  which contain only subsets of the sushi domains and act as partial agonists
  or antagonists, and **HGF/ΔN1** and **HGF/ΔN2** isoforms (via exon skipping
  at the furin site) that require trypsin rather than furin for activation.
- **Serine protease (hepsin) domain in the β chain** (residues 495–721),
  which supplies the MET-binding β-chain surface.

> [!info] The domain duplication is functional
> The fact that the MET-binding surface is split across two NLD domains of the
> α chain plus the protease-like domain of the β chain means that splice
> isoforms retaining only some NLDs act as partial agonists or antagonists
> rather than as simple "less HGF." This is the basis of the HGF/MET
> isoform biology used in oncology and in regenerative medicine.

## Mechanism and activation

1. **Transcription and secretion.** *HGF* transcription is driven by
   injury-responsive signals — reactive oxygen species, inflammatory
   cytokines, and endotoxin — in fibroblasts, stromal cells, and several
   non-parenchymal cell types.
2. **Binding and activation.** HGF binds the extracellular region of MET
   (semaphorin, plexin, IPT, and cysteine-rich domains), promoting MET
   kinase-domain dimerisation.
3. **Proteolytic activation.** In its biologically important routes, the
   single-chain form is converted to the two-chain form by
   **HGF activator (HGFAC)**, a trypsin-like serine protease produced
   endogenously. Alternatively, **TMPRSS13 (matriptase-2/EpCAM)** cleaves
   pro-HGF and activates MAPK signalling; TMPRSS13 activity is itself
   negatively regulated by **HGF activator inhibitor-1 (HAI-1)**.
4. **Downstream signalling.** Activated HGF–MET signals through:
   - **[[PI3K]]–[[Akt]]** → survival, growth, protein synthesis, and
     anti-apoptotic effects;
   - **[[RAS]]–[[MEK]]–[[ERK]] (MAPK)** → proliferation, and the
     "scattering" motility phenotype (via [[E-cadherin]] downregulation);
   - **[[STAT3]]** via the JAK/STAT arm — crucial for tumour transformation;
   - **[[JAK]]**/STAT, and c-Jun N-terminal kinase signalling.

## Physiological roles

- **Liver regeneration.** After partial hepatectomy, hepatic stellate and
  other stromal cells release HGF, which drives hepatocyte proliferation
  through MET. HGF is required for the full regenerative response; mice
  lacking HGF show markedly impaired liver regeneration after injury.
- **Embryonic development.** HGF–MET signalling is essential for the
  development of the liver, kidney, lung, skeletal muscle, and gonads; mice
  with *Met* or *Hgf* disruption die in utero with multiple organ defects.
- **Stem cell and progenitor biology.** In skeletal muscle injury, HGF is
  activated and released from the surrounding [[Extracellular Matrix]] and
  primes quiescent [[Muscle Stem Cell|satellite cells]] for proliferation
  before the injury resolves. Similar ECM-sequestered HGF releases activate
  stem cells in other tissues.
- **Wound healing, angiogenesis, and tissue repair** — HGF is one of the
  broadest-spectrum growth factors known, acting on hepatocytes, renal, lung,
  and neural progenitors, [[Endothelial Cells]], and epithelial cells of
  many organs.
- **Hearing loss.** Biallelic *HGF* variants cause **deafness, autosomal
  recessive, 39 (DFNB39, MIM 608265)** — a form of profound prelingual
  sensorineural hearing loss. This is direct evidence that HGF–MET signalling
  is required for inner-ear development and hair-cell survival.

## Pathology and clinical relevance

> [!important] MET addiction and MET-targeted therapy
> The HGF–MET axis is one of the most frequently and diversely activated
> receptor tyrosine kinase axes in cancer:
> - **Ligand overexpression and autocrine/paracrine loops:** HGF is
>   overproduced by tumour and stromal cells in essentially all
>   HGF/MET-addicted tumours.
> - **MET gene amplification** in gastric cancer, lung cancer (especially
>   EGFR-mutant NSCLC), and glioblastoma.
> - **MET exon 14 skipping mutations**, which impair CBL-mediated
>   degradation and prolong MET activation. This is now an actionable
>   alteration; **capmatinib** and **tepotinib** are approved in
>   MET exon 14-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer.
> - **Fusion events** (EML4–MET, TFG–MET, CDMN–MET) and **receptor
>   antibodies** (EML4 immunogenic fusions) also activate the axis.
>
> MET inhibition has real but modest single-agent activity; combination with
> EGFR, KRAS, or PARP inhibition is under active study, and resistance is
> commonly driven by on-target MET amplification.

> [!warning] HGF is context-dependent in regeneration
> In some settings HGF–MET is *pro*-fibrotic rather than regenerative,
> because MET signalling on hepatic stellate cells promotes collagen
> deposition. That is why the same factor that enables liver regeneration
> also participates in liver fibrosis.

## Connections

- [[c-Met]] — HGF is the only known ligand; HGF binding promotes MET dimerisation and kinase activation, and its entire biological identity is defined by this interaction.
- [[MET]] and [[MET gene]] — MET amplification and exon 14 skipping are the principal oncogenic lesions of the axis and the direct targets of capmatinib and tepotinib.
- [[Liver Regeneration]] — stromal HGF release after partial hepatectomy is the canonical physiological demonstration that HGF drives hepatocyte proliferation.
- [[Liver]] — the organ whose name HGF carries and whose regeneration HGF is required for; also the organ in which HGF–MET is also pro-fibrotic.
- [[Receptor Tyrosine Kinases]] — HGF–MET is the textbook example of an RTK/growth-factor pair, and one of the few where the ligand is itself a pseudoprotease zymogen.
- [[Muscle Stem Cell]] — ECM-sequestered HGF released on injury primes quiescent satellite cells for proliferation, making HGF a niche-controlled mitogen.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — HGF is stored and activated in the ECM; the niche holding HGF back is what maintains quiescence, so ECM turnover is the switch.
- [[PI3K]] and [[Akt]] — the pro-survival arm of HGF–MET signalling; [[RAS]]–[[MEK]]–[[ERK]] drives the proliferative and "scattering" arms, and [[STAT3]] is required for transformation.
- [[uPA]] — urokinase-type plasminogen activator also interacts with the N-terminal HGF sushi domains and participates in the proteolytic network that liberates active growth factors.
- [[Angiogenesis]] — HGF promotes endothelial cell proliferation and motility and is one of the principal HGF-driven angiogenic factors in wound healing and tumour angiogenesis.
- [[FKBP12]] — a documentation-only connection: GLMN (glomulin), the sole documented geminin-interacting protein, binds FKBP12 — see [[Geminin]] for the naming collision.
