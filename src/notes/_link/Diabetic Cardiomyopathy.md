# Diabetic Cardiomyopathy

## Overview

Diabetic Cardiomyopathy (DCM) is a distinct myocardial pathology in [[Type 2 Diabetes|diabetes]] characterized by diastolic dysfunction, left ventricular remodeling, and eventual heart failure, occurring independent of coronary atherosclerosis and hypertension. Mitochondrial dysfunction, [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], [[Inflammation|inflammation]], and metabolic inflexibility (impaired glucose-fatty acid substrate switching) are core pathogenic drivers.

## Pathophysiology & SIRT3 Connection

- SIRT3 activation improves mitochondrial respiratory capacity and suppresses oxidative stress in DCM, primarily through modulation of the AGO2/cytochrome b (CYTB) signaling pathway, which uncouples excessive glucose levels from [[Electron Transport Chain|ETC]] dysfunction.
- SIRT3 downregulation breaks this axis, resulting in impaired ETC activity and accelerated DCM progression.
- [[Melatonin|Melatonin]] counteracts adverse left-ventricle remodeling and cardiac dysfunction in DCM by promoting [[Autophagy|autophagy]], reducing [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]], and mitigating mitochondrial dysfunction — effects attributed to inhibition of [[Mst1|Mst1]] phosphorylation and upregulation of SIRT3 expression.
- SIRT3-mediated mitochondrial quality control (e.g., [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], SOD2 deacetylation) preserves [[Cardiomyocytes|cardiomyocyte]] viability under diabetic stress.

## Clinical Relevance

- DCM is a major contributor to heart failure burden in the aging diabetic population; SIRT3 loss parallels hyperacetylation-induced mitochondrial proteome dysfunction in failing hearts.
- Preclinical restoration of SIRT3 (via lifestyle interventions or [[FGF21]] signaling) improves mitochondrial integrity and myocardial function, supporting SIRT3 agonism as a translational strategy.
