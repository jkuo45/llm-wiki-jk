# Leucine

## Overview

**Leucine** is an essential branched-chain amino acid and the primary nutritional activator of [[mTORC1]], making it a key anabolic signal for [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]]. It is one of the three [[Branched-Chain Amino Acids|BCAAs]] (with isoleucine and valine).

## Mechanism of Action & Physiology

Leucine (and its metabolite α-ketoisocaproate) activates the Rag GTPase complex and upstream regulators to recruit [[mTORC1]] to the lysosome, stimulating translation initiation and muscle protein synthesis—counteracting [[Muscle Atrophy]]. It also modulates insulin sensitivity and energy metabolism.

## Leucine and Dietary Restriction

Leucine is the most potent amino-acid activator of [[mTORC1]], so leucine restriction is a candidate driver of [[Protein Restriction]] benefits. In model organisms, dietary leucine restriction alone recapitulates many hallmarks of protein restriction, including suppressed mTORC1 signaling, reduced hepatic steatosis, and improved metabolic health. Its restriction phenotype overlaps with but is less complete than restriction of the other branched-chain amino acids.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - The-hallmarks-of-protein-and-amino-acid-restriction|The Hallmarks of Protein and Amino Acid Restriction]]
> Among individual EAAs, leucine (with isoleucine and valine) is one of the key drivers of the protein-restriction response. Decreased leucine in the diet acts primarily through suppression of mTORC1, whereas the effects of isoleucine and valine restriction depend more on amino-acid sensing pathways such as [[GCN2]].

## Applications

Used clinically and as a supplement to support muscle mass in aging, exercise recovery, and catabolic states. Often paired with [[Creatine]] or [[Urolithin A]] in muscle-health strategies.
