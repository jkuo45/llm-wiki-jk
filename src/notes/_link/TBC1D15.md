# TBC1D15

## Overview

TBC1D15 is a RAB7-specific GTPase-activating protein (GAP) that disassembles [[Mitochondria-Lysosome Contact Site|mitochondria–lysosome contacts]]. It is recruited to the mitochondrial outer membrane by [[Fis1]], where it converts [[Rab7]]-GTP to RAB7-GDP and thereby terminates the RAB7-dependent tether. Beyond untethering, TBC1D15 recruits [[DRP1]] to contact sites to drive asymmetric mitochondrial fission. These activities place TBC1D15 at the junction of contact dynamics, organelle division, and quality control.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> [[Fis1]] on the outer mitochondrial membrane recruits TBC1D15, a RAB7 GAP, promoting conversion of RAB7-GTP into RAB7-GDP and consequent M-L contact untethering. TBC1D15 also recruits [[DRP1]] to M-L contact sites to drive asymmetrical mitochondrial fission.

## Mechanism

TBC1D15 acts through the nucleotide state of RAB7 rather than by displacing a structural tether. Lysosomal RAB7-GTP promotes tethering to an unidentified mitochondrial partner; TBC1D15-dependent hydrolysis of GTP terminates the contact. The same GAP activity is used in multiple disease settings to normalize contact duration: in GBA1-mutant dopaminergic neurons, excessive tethering and defective resolution are rescued by TBC1D15 overexpression; during seizures, extended contact duration is shortened by the same manipulation.

## Tissue Injury and Neuroprotection

In acute myocardial infarction, mitochondria–lysosome contacts show decreased distance and increased duration; TBC1D15 overexpression reverses these changes and improves myocardial morphology and systolic function. Mechanistically, TBC1D15 recruits DRP1 to contacts to maintain organelle quality control through asymmetric fission. In seizure models, TBC1D15 overexpression reduces mitochondrial Ca²⁺ overload and restores mitochondrial function, indicating a neuroprotective effect. These are rodent and cellular injury-model findings and have not been established in humans.

## Unresolved Questions

The review does not resolve whether TBC1D15 acts primarily through contact resolution or through DRP1-dependent fission in each injury model. Whether augmenting TBC1D15 activity is beneficial across chronic as opposed to acute states remains to be established, given that acute and chronic stress have opposite effects on the AMPK–TFEB axis.
