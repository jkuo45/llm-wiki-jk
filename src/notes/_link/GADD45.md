# GADD45

**GADD45** (Growth Arrest and DNA Damage-inducible) is a family of stress-response proteins (α, β, γ) encoded by *GADD45A/B/G*. They act as sensors that couple genotoxic/oxidative signals to [[DNA Repair]], cell-cycle checkpoints, and [[Cellular Senescence|senescence]], and are classic FOXO/p53 transcriptional targets.

## Function

- **DNA repair and genome stability:** GADD45α promotes nucleotide excision repair by facilitating chromatin access (interaction with PCNA) and is required for efficient recovery from DNA damage.
- **Cell-cycle checkpoint:** Induces G<sub>2</sub>/M growth arrest via Cdc2/cyclin B1 inhibition, giving cells time to repair damage.
- **Stress-response integration:** Upregulated by [[p53]] and by [[FOXO]] family transcription factors under [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], linking nutrient/energy and redox cues to genomic defense.

## Regulation by the SIRT1–FOXO Axis

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates forkhead transcription factors, restoring their transactivation potential so they can drive antioxidant and DNA-repair programs:

- **[[FOXO4]] deacetylation by SIRT1** restores FOXO4 activity and induces **GADD45** expression ($\alpha$-isoform), enhancing cellular defenses against [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]].
- GADD45 thus sits downstream of the SIRT1–FOXO antioxidant loop that shifts output from [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] toward repair and survival (e.g., GADD45 as a FoxO target that regulates antioxidant expression and DNA damage repair).

## Context in Aging and Senescence

As a mediator of DNA-damage-induced growth arrest, GADD45 links stress to cell-cycle control and is part of the broader p53/[[p21 CIP1|p21]]-coupled anti-proliferative response, with roles in the decision between repair, senescence, and death.
