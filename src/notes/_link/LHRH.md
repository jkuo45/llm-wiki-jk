# LHRH

**Luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone (LHRH, GnRH)** is a decapeptide hormone produced by the hypothalamus that stimulates the release of luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) from the anterior pituitary. It is the upstream regulator of sex steroid production and a target for [[Sex Steroid Ablation|sex steroid ablation]] therapy.

## Biology

- **Source:** Hypothalamic neurons
- **Target:** Anterior pituitary gonadotrophs
- **Function:** Stimulates LH and FSH release → [[Testosterone]] and [[Estrogen]] production
- **Pulsatility:** Physiological pulsatile release is required for normal gonadotropin secretion

## Pharmacological Analogues

LHRH agonists (e.g., **leuprolide/Lupron**) desensitize LHRH receptors, leading to:

- Decreased LH and FSH secretion
- Reduced [[Testosterone]] and [[Estrogen]] production
- Effective medical castration without surgery

## Thymic Rejuvenation Applications

### Mechanism
- LHRH analogues achieve [[Sex Steroid Ablation]] pharmacologically
- Removal of sex steroid inhibition restores [[Thymus|thymic]] architecture
- Increases thymic weight and reverses age-related thymic structural defects

### Pre-clinical evidence
- LHRH analogue treatment in aged rats restored thymic weight and cellularity (Greenstein et al., 1987)
- LHRH agonists influence absolute levels of lymphocyte subsets in male mice (Rao et al., 1996)

### Clinical trials
- **Lupron/leuprolide** in allogeneic [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cell]] transplant (NCT01338987)
- Leuprolide augmentation of immune responses to melanoma vaccine (NCT00254397, closed)

> [!info] Clinical advantage
> Unlike surgical castration, LHRH analogues offer a reversible, pharmaceutical approach to sex steroid ablation that is more likely to be tolerated in clinical settings.
