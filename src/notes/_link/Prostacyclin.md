# Prostacyclin

**Prostacyclin (PGI₂)** is the prostaglandin produced from [[Prostaglandin H₂]] by prostacyclin synthase in vascular endothelium. It is the body's principal endogenous inhibitor of platelet aggregation and a potent vasodilator.

## Formation and Chemistry

Prostacyclin synthase (PTGIS), a cytochrome P450-type enzyme, isomerizes the endoperoxide bridge of [[Prostaglandin H₂]] to form an enol ether bridge characteristic of PGI₂. Prostacyclin is chemically unstable and hydrolyzes to 6-keto-PGF₁α within minutes at physiological pH — the stable metabolite measured in clinical samples.

PGI₂ synthesis is concentrated in endothelial cells lining the vascular wall, where prostacyclin synthase is physically coupled to COX-2 (and to COX-1 in some vascular beds). Because PGI₂ has a short half-life, its action is strictly local — it acts on the platelet and smooth-muscle cell immediately adjacent to its site of synthesis.

## Mechanism of Action

Prostacyclin binds the **IP receptor** (a Gs-coupled prostaglandin receptor), raising intracellular cAMP. In platelets this inhibits activation, shape change, and aggregation; in vascular smooth muscle it relaxes the vessel wall. The result is a locally anti-thrombotic, vasodilated surface.

The physiological counterweight is [[Thromboxane A₂]], produced from the same [[Prostaglandin H₂]] pool in platelets and acting on the TP receptor to promote aggregation and vasoconstriction. The **PGI₂/TXA₂ ratio** is the key determinant of thrombotic tendency at the vascular wall.

> [!info] Aspirin and the PGI₂/TXA₂ balance
> Low-dose aspirin irreversibly acetylates COX-1 in platelets, suppressing [[Thromboxane A₂]] for the platelet's ~10-day lifespan. Endothelial [[Prostacyclin]] is largely spared because endothelium is nucleate and can resynthesize COX, and because low dose preferentially inhibits the platelet pool. This selectivity is the mechanistic basis of low-dose aspirin prophylaxis. Non-selective NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors shift the balance toward thrombosis, which is why COX-2 inhibitors carry cardiovascular risk.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Haemostasis**: PGI₂ is the dominant endogenous restraint on platelet aggregation in intact vessels.
- **Vascular tone**: maintains organ blood flow and limits vasospasm; deficiency contributes to hypertension and Raynaud's phenomenon.
- **Atherosclerosis**: [[Oxidized LDL]] and lipid peroxides inhibit prostacyclin synthase, lowering PGI₂ and shifting the balance toward thrombosis — a direct link between [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and thrombotic risk.
- **Pulmonary arterial hypertension**: prostacyclin deficiency is central to pathogenesis; prostacyclin analogues (epoprostenol, iloprost, treprostinil) are mainstay therapy.
- **Renal**: PGI₂ maintains glomerular filtration and renal blood flow, particularly under volume depletion.
- **Cardioprotetection**: PGI₂ release during ischaemia limits platelet deposition and microvascular occlusion during reperfusion.
