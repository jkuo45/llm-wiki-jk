# Macrophages

**Overview:** Macrophages are specialized immune cells derived from monocytes that play critical roles in tissue homeostasis, immune surveillance, and inflammation. In [[Atherosclerosis]], macrophage cholesterol accumulation and [[Foam Cells]] formation are hallmarks of early atherogenesis.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Macrophages (J774, THP-1, and mouse peritoneal macrophages) take up and oxidize LDL, leading to significant cholesterol accumulation. Oxidative stress (Fe/ascorbate) impairs ABCA1-mediated cholesterol efflux. Resveratrol (0–25 µM) restores HDL3-mediated cholesterol efflux under oxidative stress in a dose-dependent manner.

## Cholesterol Homeostasis in Macrophages

- **Cholesterol accumulation**: Macrophages accumulate cholesterol via LDL uptake, oxidation, and increased biosynthesis
- **Foam cell formation**: Excessive cholesterol accumulation transforms macrophages into foam cells — the hallmark of early atherosclerotic lesions
- **Cholesterol efflux**: The protective mechanism by which HDL removes excess cholesterol from macrophages; occurs via three pathways:
  1. Aqueous diffusion
  2. [[SR-BI]]-mediated bidirectional flux
  3. [[ABCA1]]-mediated transfer to lipid-poor [[Apolipoprotein A1|apoA-1]]
- **Oxidative stress effects**: Fe/ascorbate impairs ABCA1 expression and cholesterol efflux; [[Resveratrol]] restores this function

## Cell Lines Used in Research

- **J774**: Mouse macrophage cell line; commonly used for cholesterol efflux studies
- **THP-1**: Human monocyte-derived macrophages; differentiated with PMA
- **MPM**: Mouse peritoneal macrophages; primary cells harvested from thioglycollate-treated mice
