# Neglected Tropical Diseases

**Neglected tropical diseases (NTDs)** are a WHO-defined portfolio of 20 parasitic, fungal, and ectoparasitic infections that occur predominantly in tropical and subtropical regions, disproportionately affect the poorest populations, and have historically attracted little research funding or commercial incentive. They are common in roughly 149 countries, affect more than 1.4 billion people (including over 500 million children), and caused an estimated 142,000 deaths in 2013 — down from 204,000 in 1990. The "neglect" refers to their position in global health policy, not to their biological importance: together their burden in sub-Saharan Africa is comparable to that of malaria and tuberculosis, and NTD co-infection makes [[HIV]] and [[Tuberculosis]] more lethal.

## Why they are neglected

> [!info] Structural drivers of neglect
> The neglect is a product of four things: (1) **poverty** — the diseases affect the poorest countries and, within rich countries, the poorest people; (2) **asymptomatic or chronic presentation** with long incubation, so the link between an NTD and later death is not visible; (3) **geographic isolation** of endemic foci, which makes treatment and surveillance logistically hard; and (4) **absence of economic incentive** — of 1,393 new chemical entities marketed between 1975 and 1999, only 16 were for tropical diseases or TB, and there was a 13-fold greater chance a new drug targeted CNS disease or cancer.

## The WHO 20

The current WHO list groups the diseases by pathogen class:

| Group | Diseases |
| --- | --- |
| **Protozoan** | Chagas disease, human African trypanosomiasis, [[Leishmaniasis]] |
| **Helminthic** | Dracunculiasis, echinococcosis, foodborne trematodiases, [[Lymphatic Filariasis]], [[Onchocerciasis]], schistosomiasis, soil-transmitted helminthiasis (ascariasis, hookworm, trichuriasis, strongyloidiasis) |
| **Bacterial** | Buruli ulcer, leprosy, trachoma, yaws |
| **Fungal** | Mycetoma and other deep mycoses |
| **Viral** | Dengue and chikungunya, [[Rabies]] |
| **Ectoparasitic** | [[Scabies]] and other ectoparasites |
| **Snakebite envenoming** | Added 2017, non-infectious |

The 2017 addition of scabies, deep mycoses, and snakebite is a useful marker of how the definition has widened beyond helminths and protozoa toward ectoparasites and toxin exposures. Note that what the WHO calls NTDs and what individual researchers count differ — some lists have 12 "core" NTDs, some 13, some 20 — so any count should be attributed.

## Control strategy: MDA, WASH, and disease-specific tools

> [!info] The MDA paradigm
> Roughly half of the WHO NTD portfolio is controlled by **mass drug administration** — treating an entire endemic population, regardless of infection status, at regular intervals. The logic is transmission-based, not cure-based: for worms that live for years in the host, killing the juvenile or microfilarial stages stops the transmission chain even though adult worms survive. Triple-drug MDA (ivermectin + albendazole + azithromycin) is deployed for integrated programmes, and safety data support the combination.

The MDA packages are:
- **Ivermectin** for onchocerciasis, lymphatic filariasis (with albendazole), scabies, and strongyloidiasis — donated in billions of tablets through the Mectizan Donation Program.
- **Albendazole** for soil-transmitted helminths, and in combination with ivermectin for filariasis and onchocerciasis.
- **Azithromycin** for trachoma.
- **Praziquantel** for schistosomiasis and foodborne trematodiases.

The drugs are cheap, often donated, and given a handful of times a year. What is not cheap is the delivery: this is why MDA requires the pharmaceutical donation programmes and the implementation infrastructure of the [[World Health Organization]], [[TDR]] (the WHO Special Programme for Research and Training in Tropical Diseases), and the [[African Programme for Onchocerciasis Control]]. Where MDA alone cannot deliver — brucellosis-free chocolate challenges aside, control also needs water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) integration for schistosomiasis, vector control, snail control, case detection and treatment for trypanosomiasis and leprosy, and one Health/zoonotic programmes for echinococcosis.

> [!info] Elimination, not eradication
> Several NTDs now have elimination as a formal target: dracunculiasis (Guinea worm) is the only human disease ever eradicated, and lymphatic filariasis, onchocerciasis, trachoma, schistosomiasis, soil-transmitted helminthiasis, yaws, and echinococcosis have elimination roadmaps. The distinction matters — elimination is interruption of local transmission in a defined geography, which is a far weaker and more achievable claim than global eradication.

## Research and development problem

The pipeline for NTDs is close to empty, and the gap has widened as donor funding has contracted. A small number of high-value drugs came from repurposing existing agents rather than de novo design: miltefosine (originally an oncology/dermatology agent), [[Ivermectin]] (a natural-product-derived macrocyclic lactone), and the current attraction of Wolbachia-depleting antibiotics against filarial worms. The current incentive toolkit — priority review vouchers, advance market commitments, milestone-based prizes, and open-source/consortium models — exists precisely because the classic patent-and-profit model does not work for diseases of the poorest.

## Connections

- [[TDR]] — The WHO Special Programme for Research and Training in Tropical Diseases; the research and product-development arm of the NTD effort.
- [[African Programme for Onchocerciasis Control]] — The APOC partnership delivering community-directed ivermectin distribution; the most successful NTD elimination programme in practice.
- [[Ivermectin]] — The most consequential NTD drug: onchocerciasis, filariasis, scabies, strongyloidiasis, plus incidental benefit for malaria transmission and for soil-transmitted helminths in combination.
- [[Onchocerciasis]] — The NTD with the most successful elimination trajectory and the hardest safety problem (ocular inflammation from rapid microfilarial killing).
- [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] — The NTD with the longest continuous MDA record and an established elimination verification framework.
- [[Leishmaniasis]] — The NTD with the best oral drug (miltefosine) and a growing resistance problem.
- [[Trichuriasis]] — Soil-transmitted helminthiasis where ivermectin–albendazole combination MDA outperforms either alone.
- [[Strongyloidiasis]] — A soil-transmitted helminthiasis with a distinctive autoinfection lifecycle and hyperinfection risk in the immunosuppressed.
- [[Scabies]] — Added to the NTD list in 2017 and curable with single-dose ivermectin, making it the most tractable of the newly added diseases.
- [[World Health Organization]] — Defines the disease list, sets the roadmaps, issues the MDA guidelines, and certifies elimination.
- [[Mosquito]] — The vector for several NTDs (dengue/chikungunya, malaria where counted, filariasis) and the target of vector-control components of NTD programmes.
- [[Nematode]] — The parasite phylum behind the largest share of the NTD portfolio.
- [[HIV]] — NTD co-infection increases HIV progression and mortality; the co-epidemic is a core rationale for integration.
- [[_document_ - Ivermectin, a potential anticancer drug derived from an antiparasitic drug|Repurposing]] — The broader theme that NTD drug discovery feeds back into mainstream pharmacology; the vault's ivermectin-as-oncology documents are direct instances of an NTD drug being repurposed, and also instances of contested claims.
- [[Moxidectin]] — A newer onchocerciasis drug developed to overcome operational limits of ivermectin, illustrating both the innovation problem and its partial solution.
