# PAK2

## Overview
[[PAK2]] (p21-activated kinase 2) is a serine/threonine kinase belonging to the PAK family, activated by binding to active [[Rac GTPase|Rac1]] and [[Rac GTPase|Cdc42]]. PAK2 plays dual roles in cell biology: promoting cytoskeletal dynamics, cell migration, and survival signaling, while also being cleaved and constitutively activated during apoptosis. In cancer, PAK2's survival functions dominate, particularly through its inhibition of apoptotic caspases.

## Structure & Activation
PAK2 contains an N-terminal regulatory domain with a Cdc42/Rac interactive binding (CRIB) domain and a C-terminal catalytic domain. Autoinhibition is relieved by binding of active Rac1 or Cdc42, or by caspase-mediated cleavage during apoptosis (generating a constitutively active C-terminal fragment).

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Caspase-7 Inhibition**: PAK2 directly binds and phosphorylates [[Caspase-7]] at three residues:
  - **Ser30** (prodomain): Blocks interaction with [[Caspase-9]], preventing activation
  - **Thr173**: Contributes to inhibitory conformation
  - **Ser239** (near active site): Sterically hinders substrate binding

> [!warning] Dual-Layered Inhibition of Caspase-7
> PAK2 employs a two-pronged strategy: Ser30 phosphorylation prevents Caspase-7 activation by Caspase-9, while Ser239 phosphorylation neutralizes even already-active Caspase-7 molecules. This represents one of the most complete known mechanisms for caspase inhibition by a kinase.

- **Survival Signaling**: PAK2 activates [[NFKB]], [[mTORC1]], and [[ERK2]] pathways to promote cell survival and proliferation.
- **Cytoskeletal Regulation**: PAK2 phosphorylates LIMK, filamin, and other cytoskeletal regulators to promote cell migration and invasion.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance
- **Breast Cancer**: PAK2 is frequently overexpressed in invasive ductal carcinoma. The PAK2–Caspase-7 axis is a major driver of resistance to DNA-damaging chemotherapeutics (e.g., staurosporine, doxorubicin).
- **Other Cancers**: PAK2 overexpression is implicated in glioblastoma, ovarian cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **Therapeutic Targeting**: PAK2 inhibitors are under development as potential chemosensitizers.
