# CD47

**CD47** is a 50 kDa, five-pass transmembrane receptor of the immunoglobulin superfamily,
encoded on chromosome 3q13.12. It is one of the most ubiquitously expressed membrane
proteins in the human body — present on red cells, platelets, leukocytes, endothelium,
neurons and fibroblasts — which is what makes it simultaneously such an effective shield
and such a difficult drug target.

## Structure

CD47 has a small extracellular N-terminal IgV domain followed by five transmembrane
segments and a short C-terminal cytoplasmic tail. Four alternatively spliced isoforms
differ only in cytoplasmic tail length: form 2 is ubiquitous, form 4 is enriched in brain
and peripheral nerve, form 1 is largely restricted to keratinocytes. The tails are
conserved between mouse and man, but their distinct functions remain incompletely
characterised — the dominant signalling output of CD47 is carried by a membrane-proximal
G-protein-coupled motif rather than by a classical enzyme domain.

## Ligands

> [!info] Mechanism: the "don't eat me" signal
> On myeloid cells, CD47 engages [[SIRPalpha|SIRPα]], whose cytoplasmic ITIM motifs
> recruit SHP-1 and SHP-2 phosphatases. The resulting dephosphorylation of
> Vav-family and other substrates inhibits actin rearrangement at the phagocytic cup,
> so the macrophage does not engulf the target. In the adaptive arm, CD47 on antigen-presenting
> cells binds SIRPγ on T cells, and CD47 ligation also transduces signals into the CD47-bearing
> cell via integrins, chiefly αvβ3.
>
> The physiological purpose is protection of self: CD47-null red cells and
> [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cells]] are cleared from the circulation
> within minutes by splenic macrophages, and stem cell progenitors transiently upregulate
> CD47 during their migratory phase to avoid being eaten in transit.

CD47 also binds [[Thrombospondin-1]] (TSP-1), a secreted anti-angiogenic glycoprotein.
TSP-1 signalling through CD47 inhibits nitric oxide signalling at several levels in
vascular cells, suppresses endothelial migration and tube formation, and — through
integrin co-recruitment — drives migration of neutrophils and monocytes across endothelium.
CD47 also cooperates with several [[Integrins|integrins]] to set adhesion, spreading and
migration thresholds.

## Functions

- **Self-recognition** — the dominant role; the reason CD47-null mice and humans' CD47-deficient
  cells are rapidly cleared.
- **Migration and adhesion** — CD47 blockade inhibits neutrophil and monocyte transendothelial
  migration and reduces leukocyte recruitment to inflamed tissue in knockout animals.
- **Apoptosis** — ligation by certain anti-CD47 antibodies or by TSP-1 induces apoptosis or
  autophagy in T cells, CLL cells and RAS-transformed cells respectively. The effect is
  epitope-dependent, which is why not all anti-CD47 antibodies are interchangeable.
- **Stemness** — TSP-1/CD47 signalling represses c-MYC and other stem cell transcription
  factors and inhibits self-renewal; CD47 loss deregulates them.
- **Insulin secretion** — CD47 receptor signalling inhibits insulin release from human and
  mouse pancreatic β cells, and the signal can be pharmacologically blocked to raise
  secretion.

## Clinical significance

Tumours over-exploit the self-recognition axis. CD47 is elevated across essentially all
tumour types — acute myeloid leukaemia, chronic myeloid leukaemia, ALL, non-Hodgkin
lymphoma, [[Multiple Myeloma]], bladder cancer and other solid tumours — and is
enriched on cancer stem-like cells. Critically, high CD47 can override even a strong
pro-phagocytic signal such as surface [[Calreticulin]]: the inhibitory signal wins.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat — the field has cooled
> Anti-CD47 monotherapy and the leading combination programmes (anti-CD47 plus rituximab
> in lymphoma; anti-CD47 plus rituximab and magrolimab-based combinations in AML) have
> not delivered the preclinical promise. The recurring problems are anaemia from
> on-target red-cell and platelet destruction, which required Fc-silent and
> haematopoietic-stem-cell-sparing antibody engineering, and disappointing
> single-agent clinical activity. CD47 blockade has had substantially better results in
> fibrotic disease than in haematological malignancy. Any therapeutic claim should be
> checked against current trial readouts rather than the 2011–2015 enthusiasm.

## Connections

- [[Thrombospondin-1]] — TSP-1 is CD47's high-affinity extracellular ligand, linking CD47 to the anti-angiogenic arm of the TSP axis. Through CD47, TSP-1 blocks NO-stimulated responses in endothelial and smooth muscle cells and restrains self-renewal programmes.
- [[SIRPalpha]] — SIRPα on macrophages and dendritic cells is the inhibitory receptor whose engagement by CD47 generates the "don't eat me" signal. This axis is the single most exploited CD47 interaction therapeutically.
- [[Integrin]] — CD47 forms functional complexes with integrins, particularly αvβ3, and these complexes set adhesion, spreading and migration thresholds. CD47 blockade reduces chemotaxis to collagen IV in melanoma, prostate and ovarian cancer cells.
- [[Angiogenesis]] — TSP-1 signalling through CD47 inhibits endothelial migration and tube formation and reduces blood-flow recovery after hindlimb ischemia. It is an anti-angiogenic brake rather than a pro-angiogenic driver, the opposite of the common assumption.
- [[Nitric Oxide]] — CD47 is necessary for TSP-1's inhibition of nitric-oxide-stimulated vascular cell responses, acting at multiple levels rather than on NO synthase directly.
- [[Macrophage]] — The macrophage is the cell whose behaviour CD47 governs: SIRPα engagement suppresses phagocytic cup formation. Blocking CD47 restores phagocytosis of tumour cells.
- [[Stem Cells]] — CD47 expression is transiently upregulated by migrating hematopoietic progenitors to prevent macrophage engulfment, and CD47 loss derepresses c-MYC-driven stemness.
- [[Insulin Secretion]] — CD47 signalling is a negative regulator of pancreatic β-cell insulin release in both species tested, and is blockable pharmacologically — an underappreciated metabolic role.
