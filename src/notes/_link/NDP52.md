# NDP52

NDP52 (also known as [[CALCOCO2]]) is a primary [[Autophagy|autophagy]] receptor in [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]]-mediated [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]]. It is recruited to ubiquitin-coated [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] and, through its [[LIR Motif|LC3-interacting region (LIR) motif]], binds to [[LC3]] proteins on the expanding phagophore, guiding the isolation membrane to enwrap damaged mitochondria.

## Mechanism of Action

In the [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] pathway, after [[Parkin]] ubiquitinates outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM) proteins, NDP52 is recruited to the ubiquitin-coated mitochondrial surface. Its binding affinity for ubiquitin chains is significantly enhanced by phosphorylation mediated by the kinase [[TBK1]]. This creates a positive feedback loop that accelerates mitochondrial engulfment. NDP52 works in concert with [[OPTN]] as the two primary autophagy receptors in this pathway.

## Regulation

- [[TBK1]] phosphorylates NDP52, enhancing its binding affinity for ubiquitin chains on damaged mitochondria
- Functions redundantly with [[OPTN]] to ensure robust mitophagic clearance
- Ubiquitin chain recognition is necessary but not sufficient for mitophagy — downstream receptors like NDP52 must efficiently recognize these chains

## Role in Neurodegeneration

NDP52-mediated [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] is critical for maintaining [[Mitochondrial Quality Control|mitochondrial quality control]] in [[Neuron|neurons]]. Defects in this pathway contribute to the accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria in [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]].

## Connections

- [[PINK1]] — Upstream kinase that initiates the ubiquitin signal recognized by NDP52
- [[Parkin]] — E3 ligase that generates ubiquitin chains on mitochondria for NDP52 recruitment
- [[OPTN]] — Partner autophagy receptor; both are phosphorylated by TBK1
- [[TBK1]] — Kinase that phosphorylates NDP52 to enhance ubiquitin binding
- [[LC3]] — Direct binding partner via LIR motif
