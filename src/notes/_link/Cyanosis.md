# Cyanosis

**Overview:** [[Cyanosis]] is a bluish or grayish discoloration of the skin, lips, mucous membranes, and nail beds caused by an increased concentration of deoxygenated [[Hemoglobin]] (or of abnormal hemoglobin variants such as [[Methemoglobin]]) in the small blood vessels near the surface. It is a clinical sign rather than a disease itself, reflecting inadequate oxygenation of the blood or tissues.

**Pathophysiology:** The color arises because deoxygenated hemoglobin absorbs light differently from oxygenated hemoglobin. Cyanosis becomes clinically visible when the absolute amount of deoxygenated hemoglobin in capillaries exceeds roughly 3–5 g/dL, which is why anemic patients may not appear cyanotic despite significant [[Hypoxia]], whereas polycythemic patients become cyanotic more readily. In [[Methemoglobinemia]], the iron of hemoglobin is oxidized from the ferrous (Fe²⁺) to the ferric (Fe³⁺) state, producing methemoglobin that cannot bind oxygen and imparts a characteristic slate-gray or chocolate-brown discoloration that does not improve with supplemental oxygen.

**Classification:** Central cyanosis affects the lips, tongue, and mucous membranes and reflects arterial desaturation from cardiac (right-to-left shunt), pulmonary (impaired gas exchange), or hemoglobin abnormalities. Peripheral cyanosis is confined to the extremities and results from reduced local blood flow or increased oxygen extraction (e.g., cold exposure, vasoconstriction, shock) with normal arterial saturation.

**Diagnosis and management:** Evaluation includes pulse oximetry (which may read falsely normal or low in methemoglobinemia), arterial blood gas analysis, and co-oximetry to distinguish hemoglobin species. Management targets the underlying cause—oxygen and support for cardiopulmonary causes, warming and perfusion restoration for peripheral cyanosis, and [[Methylene blue]] as antidotal therapy for symptomatic methemoglobinemia.
