# FPRL1

FPRL1 (formyl-peptide receptor-like-1), also known as FPR2, is a seven-transmembrane, G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) originally identified as a pattern recognition receptor of the innate immune system. It has since been recognized as a functional receptor for [[Humanin]], mediating its neuroprotective and cytoprotective effects.

## Structure and Signaling

FPRL1 is a class A GPCR that signals through pertussis toxin-sensitive Gi/o proteins. Activation induces:

- **Ca²⁺ mobilization**: Rapid intracellular calcium release
- **[[ERK]]1/2 activation**: MAPK pathway activation promoting cell survival
- **PI3K/Akt signaling**: Pro-survival metabolic regulation
- **p38 MAPK activation**: Stress response regulation

> [!info] Humanin as an FPRL1 Agonist
> Both the 42-amino acid β-amyloid peptide (Aβ42) and [[Humanin]] can activate FPRL1 in neuroblasts. However, while Aβ42 is cytotoxic, humanin is cytoprotective. Humanin's neuroprotective effects may be achieved by competitively binding to FPRL1, thereby blocking Aβ42-induced cytotoxicity. This competitive interaction suggests a potential therapeutic mechanism for humanin in [[Alzheimer's Disease]].

## Ligands

FPRL1 has a remarkably diverse ligand repertoire:
- **N-formylated peptides**: Including formylated [[Humanin]] (which has enhanced affinity)
- **Lipoxin A4 (ALX)**: Anti-inflammatory lipid mediator
- **Annexin A1**: Anti-inflammatory protein
- **Serum amyloid A (SAA)**: Acute-phase protein
- **β-amyloid peptides**: Including Aβ42
- **Host defense peptides**: Including antimicrobial peptides

## Biological Roles

FPRL1 mediates:
- Innate immune responses and chemotaxis
- Resolution of [[Inflammation]]
- Neuroprotection (via [[Humanin]] signaling)
- Vascular homeostasis

## Disease Relevance

- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]: FPRL1 mediates both Aβ toxicity and humanin protection
- [[Inflammation]]: FPRL1 activation by lipoxin A4 promotes inflammation resolution
- [[Atherosclerosis]]: FPRL1 modulates vascular inflammatory responses
