# vFLIP

**vFLIPs** (viral FLICE-inhibitory proteins) are the viral originals of the rheostat story: discovered
in 1997 by Thome et al. in gammaherpesviruses and poxviruses, these DED-tandem proteins mimicked by
(and ancestrally related to) cellular [[c-FLIP]] block host [[Apoptosis]] at the [[DISC]] while
frequently *activating* NF-κB — keeping the infected cell alive and transcriptionally productive.

## Members & Mechanisms

- **KSHV/HHV-8 K13 (ORF71):** the flagship vFLIP; DED-mediated [[FADD]]/caspase-8 engagement blocks
  Fas/TRAIL killing, while direct [[NEMO]] binding (same site as [[p22-FLIP]]) drives constitutive
  [[IKK complex]] → NF-κB activity essential for primary effusion lymphoma survival and viral latency.
- **Molluscum contagiosum MC159/MC160:** MC159 potently inhibits [[Caspase-8]] processing and,
  like [[c-FLIP|FLIP_S]], promotes [[RIPK1]]-dependent [[Necroptosis]] under IAP-antagonist pressure
  (Feoktistova 2011) — a viral phenocopy of the short-isoform rheostat setting.
- **Other herpes/poxviruses (EHV-2 E8, HVS ORF71, VACV F1):** variations on DED competition at the
  [[DISC]] plus lineage-specific NF-κB/autophagy modulation.

## Relationship to c-FLIP

Genomic adjacency (*CFLAR*–*CASP8*–*CASP10* at 2q33) and DED homology argue ancient gene capture:
viruses stole a host DED module and convergently re-derived both the caspase-8-blocking and the
NEMO-activating faces that cellular [[c-FLIP]] splits across its isoforms and fragments
([[p43-FLIP]], [[p22-FLIP]]).

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Viral persistence:** vFLIP expression is a latency linchpin (KSHV, HVS); deleting it collapses
  infected-cell survival and aborts transformation.
- **Drug template:** the MC159–[[Caspase-8]] and K13–[[NEMO]] interfaces inform stapled-peptide and
  small-molecule efforts to modulate the human rheostat — including DR5-binding-domain peptides that
  displace [[c-FLIP|FLIP_L]] (Jin et al.).
- **Oncovirus logic:** constitutive vFLIP→NF-κB signaling is a direct tumor-maintenance addiction in
  PEL and multicentric Castleman disease, targetable via IKK/NF-κB inhibition.
