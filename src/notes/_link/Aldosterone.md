# Aldosterone

**Aldosterone** is the principal mineralocorticoid hormone, produced exclusively by the zona glomerulosa of the [[Adrenal Cortex]]. Its job is to set whole-body sodium content, extracellular volume and potassium distribution — and, as a consequence, arterial blood pressure. Cortisol circulates 100–1000-fold higher than aldosterone yet the mineralocorticoid receptor ([[Nuclear Receptor|mineralocorticoid receptor]], MR) binds both with near-identical affinity; the specificity is supplied by local enzyme gating, not by the receptor.

## Mechanism of Action

In the late distal tubule and collecting duct, aldosterone acts on principal cells:

- **Increases apical ENaC expression and activity** — the epithelial sodium channel reabsorbs luminal Na⁺.
- The reabsorbed Na⁺ is pumped basolaterally by the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, and K⁺ moves in the opposite direction, so **urinary potassium excretion rises** and plasma K⁺ falls.
- **Stimulates H⁺ secretion** by α-intercalated cells, via enhanced H⁺-ATPase and H⁺/K⁺-ATPase activity, tending to raise plasma bicarbonate.
- **Increases Na⁺ reabsorption in colon and sweat glands**, minor contributions with the same mechanism.

> [!info] Why aldosterone and not cortisol activates the receptor
> 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2 (11β-HSD2) in the kidney converts active cortisol to inactive cortisone, keeping the effective cortisol concentration at the MR far below its plasma level. When 11β-HSD2 is inhibited (licorice/glycyrrhetinic acid) or saturated, cortisol gains access to the receptor and produces **apparent mineralocorticoid excess** — hypertension with hypokalaemia and suppressed renin in a patient who is not secreting excess aldosterone (MDPI IJMS 2025).

## Regulation

Two stimuli dominate, and they are largely independent:

- **Angiotensin II** via [[Angiotensin II Type 1 Receptor]] — raised by low renal perfusion pressure, sympathetic β1 stimulation, and macula densa sodium sensing. This is the dominant driver in hypovolaemia and heart failure.
- **Plasma potassium** — a direct, largely angiotensin-independent stimulation of glomerulosa cells. Hyperkalaemia itself stimulates aldosterone, which is why it is a homeostatic loop rather than an acute regulator.

ACTH has a permissive role; the zona glomerulosa expresses ACTH receptors but angiotensin II and potassium dominate. Aldosterone feeds back negatively on renin release and on its own synthesis.

> [!important] Aldosterone escape
> In states of sustained aldosterone excess (primary aldosteronism, heart failure, resistant hypertension), renal sodium retention plateaus and natriuresis resumes — "aldosterone escape." Volume expansion then persists while the *non-volume* actions of aldosterone continue: endothelial dysfunction, myocardial and vascular fibrosis, and pro-inflammatory and pro-oxidant effects. This explains why aldosterone blockade (spironolactone, eplerenone, MR antagonists) helps beyond blood-pressure lowering.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Primary aldosteronism** — adrenal adenoma or bilateral hyperplasia causing autonomous aldosterone excess. Screening rests on an elevated aldosterone-to-renin ratio. Classically resistant hypertension, hypokalaemia (present in only a minority), and metabolic features including glucose intolerance and bone loss. Chronic aldosterone excess is associated with left ventricular hypertrophy and atrial arrhythmias independent of blood pressure.
- **Secondary hyperaldosteronism** — appropriately raised aldosterone in heart failure, cirrhosis with ascites, and nephrotic syndrome; a marker of effective volume depletion.
- **Addison disease / adrenal insufficiency** — mineralocorticoid deficiency causing sodium wasting, hyperkalaemia, hypotension and volume depletion.
- **Pseudohyperaldosteronism** — MR overactivity without aldosterone excess, from 11β-HSD2 deficiency, Liddle syndrome (gain-of-function ENaC), or CRAC channel blockade by macrolides.
- **Innate immune training** — aldosterone acting on the MR reprograms human monocytes toward fatty acid synthesis rather than glycolysis; blocking fatty acid synthesis abolishes the trained phenotype (van der Heijden et al., *Cardiovasc Res* 2020).

## Connections

- [[Adrenal Cortex]] — the zona glomerulosa is the only site of aldosterone synthesis, and its CYP11B2 expression is what makes the hormone aldosterone rather than cortisol.
- [[Angiotensin II]] — the dominant humoral stimulus for aldosterone secretion, acting through the AT1 receptor on glomerulosa cells and completing the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone axis.
- [[Potassium]] — the second independent stimulus; hyperkalaemia directly drives aldosterone, which then lowers potassium via renal excretion.
- [[Hypertension]] — the principal clinical consequence of aldosterone excess, and the reason MR antagonism is a first-line agent in resistant hypertension.
- [[Cardiovascular Disease]] — chronic aldosterone excess promotes vascular and myocardial fibrosis and endothelial dysfunction independently of blood pressure.
- [[Angiotensin II Type 1 Receptor]] — the receptor through which angiotensin II stimulates aldosterone synthesis and through which ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower it.
