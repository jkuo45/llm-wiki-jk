# GCN2

**GCN2** (General Control Nonderepressible 2, gene symbol *EIF2AK4*) is a serine/threonine protein kinase that functions as a sensor of amino acid availability. It is one of four mammalian eIF2α kinases (along with PERK, PKR, and HRI) that phosphorylate eukaryotic initiation factor 2α ([[eIF2α]]) in response to cellular stress.

## Mechanism

GCN2 is activated by uncharged tRNA accumulating during amino acid starvation. The kinase domain phosphorylates [[eIF2α]] at Ser51, which attenuates global protein synthesis while paradoxically promoting translation of specific stress-responsive mRNAs (e.g., [[ATF4]]). This integrated stress response (ISR) redirects cellular resources toward adaptation.

## Role in IDO1-Mediated Immunosuppression

[[IDO1]]-mediated [[Tryptophan]] depletion activates GCN2 in effector [[T cell|T cells]]. GCN2 activation leads to:
- Cell-cycle arrest at G₁ phase
- Induction of [[Apoptosis|apoptotic]] programs in activated T cells
- [[T cell]] anergy and functional inactivation
- [[Regulatory T cell|Treg]] differentiation

GCN2 is thus a critical effector of [[IDO1]]'s immunosuppressive function, translating Trp depletion into T-cell growth arrest.

## Broader Functions

- Amino acid sensing in the liver and brain
- Regulation of [[Autophagy]] under nutrient stress
- Synaptic plasticity and memory consolidation

## Role in Dietary Amino-Acid Restriction

GCN2 is the primary sensor of amino-acid deprivation during dietary restriction, translating low amino-acid availability into the [[Integrated Stress Response]]. It is required for the metabolic benefits of several amino-acid–restricted diets:

- Protein-restricted and amino-acid–restricted diets activate GCN2 via uncharged tRNAs.
- GCN2 mediates the metabolic adaptation to diets low in specific [[Essential Amino Acids|essential amino acids]] (notably [[Tryptophan]] and [[Valine]]), inducing adaptive hepatic gene programs.
- Reduced leucine in the diet suppresses [[mTORC1]], whereas restriction of other amino acids acts largely through GCN2-dependent amino-acid sensing — providing complementary, amino-acid-specific signaling arms.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - The-hallmarks-of-protein-and-amino-acid-restriction|The Hallmarks of Protein and Amino Acid Restriction]]
> In a hallmark-based framework of protein/amino-acid restriction, GCN2 is the archetypal amino-acid-sensing kinase: it responds to decreased amino-acid levels with eIF2α phosphorylation and ATF4 induction, and its activation is necessary for the full metabolic response to certain amino-acid restrictions.
