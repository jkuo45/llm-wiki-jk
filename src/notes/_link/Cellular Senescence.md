# Cellular Senescence

**Cellular Senescence** is a complex stress-responsive state characterized by stable [[Cell Cycle]] arrest, resistance to [[Apoptosis]], and the [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP)]]. It is driven by [[Replicative Senescence|replicative exhaustion]], [[DNA Damage]], [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|oncogene activation]], and other stressors.

## Acid Ceramidase Connection

> [!info] Source: Soriano-Castell et al., 2026, *Cell Death Dis*
> [[Acid ceramidase]] is over-expressed 5- to 20-fold in [[Replicative Senescence|replicatively senescent]] cells, driving a pro-[[Ferroptosis|ferroptotic]] membrane lipid profile via the [[Lands cycle]]. This creates a novel vulnerability that can be therapeutically targeted with ACase inhibitors ([[ARN14794]]).

## Sex Differences

Senescent-cell burden and the dominant arrest arm are **sex-dependent** (see [[Senescence]] for the full treatment): males accumulate more senescent cells earlier in life and skew toward the p53/p21 arm, whereas females carry a higher p16-positive load (especially in liver) and derive the greatest benefit from p16+ cell clearance. Sex-hormonal status gates SASP burden (estrogen suppresses [[NF-κB]]-driven SASP) and senolytic responses (fisetin male-beneficial vs D+Q female-detrimental in some models; see [[Senolytics]]). Both senescence arms — and the SASP they produce — should therefore be interpreted sex-stratified.
