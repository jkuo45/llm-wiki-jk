## Overview

Platelets (thrombocytes) are small, anucleate cell fragments produced by megakaryocytes in the bone marrow and released into the circulation. Despite lacking a nucleus, platelets are metabolically active, possess mitochondria and granules, and are central to hemostasis, vascular integrity, and innate immunity.

## Biological Role

- **Primary hemostasis:** Upon vascular injury, platelets rapidly adhere (via von Willebrand factor and collagen receptors) and activate, undergoing shape change and degranulation.
- **Platelet aggregation:** Activated platelets express P-selectin and expose phosphatidylserine, recruiting additional platelets and providing a surface for coagulation cascade assembly.
- **Inflammation & immunity:** Platelets store and release cytokines, interact with leukocytes, and modulate inflammatory and aging-related processes.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Platelet hyperreactivity contributes to atherothrombosis and cardiovascular events. Platelet biology intersects with aging (platelet senescence/function changes) and with redox and inflammatory pathways relevant to the broader knowledge base. They are also a practical research model for aggregation assays.
