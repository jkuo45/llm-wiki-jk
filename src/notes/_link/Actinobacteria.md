# Actinobacteria

**Overview:** Actinobacteria is a phylum of Gram-positive bacteria distinguished by a high guanine–cytosine (G+C) content in their genomic DNA (typically >55%, often 70% or more). They are among the most abundant phyla in soil and are also important members of the human [[Gut Microbiome]], where they include commensal genera such as [[Bifidobacterium]].

**Phylogeny and Diversity:** Actinobacteria encompasses both filamentous and coccoid forms. The class Actinomycetia includes soil-dwelling saprophytes and symbionts, while [[Bifidobacterium]] and related genera colonize the gastrointestinal tract from birth. Their high G+C genome is reflected in codon usage and in the stability of their secondary structures.

**Ecological and Biomedical Roles:** In the environment, Actinobacteria are premier decomposers of complex plant polymers (cellulose, chitin, lignin) and are the source of the majority of clinically used antibiotics (e.g., streptomycetes producing tetracyclines, macrolides, and aminoglycosides). In the gut, Bifidobacteria ferment dietary fiber into [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] (notably acetate and lactate), acidify the lumen, competitively exclude pathogens, and help train the neonatal immune system.

**Physiological Function and Longevity:** A healthy Actinobacterial (particularly bifidobacterial) abundance is associated with [[Longevity]] and is enriched in [[Centenarians]] relative to younger adults. Reduced Bifidobacterium with age ("dysbiosis") is linked to inflammaging. Alongside [[Firmicutes]], Actinobacteria constitute the dominant culturable gut phyla whose balance influences metabolic and immune homeostasis.

**Pathology:** While mostly beneficial, some Actinobacteria (e.g., *Mycobacterium*, *Corynebacterium*, *Actinomyces*) are opportunistic pathogens causing tuberculosis, diphtheria, and actinomycosis.
