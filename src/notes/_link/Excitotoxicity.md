# Excitotoxicity

**Definition:** [[Excitotoxicity]] is a form of neuronal injury in which overactivation of excitatory neurotransmitters—principally glutamate—overwhelms receptor signaling and triggers cell death. It bridges physiological excitatory neurotransmission and pathological degeneration.

**Mechanism:** Excessive synaptic glutamate activates ionotropic receptors, causing sustained Ca2+ entry. Intracellular Ca2+ overload activates calpains, phospholipases, and endonucleases, and drives massive production of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] within [[Mitochondria]], collapsing membrane potential and ATP synthesis. This oxidative and energetic crisis engages [[Apoptosis]] and necrotic cell death.

**Physiological importance:** Tightly regulated glutamate signaling is essential for learning, memory, and synaptic plasticity. Excitotoxicity represents the failure of that regulation under metabolic or traumatic stress.

**Regulation and dysregulation:** Endogenous defenses include astrocytic glutamate uptake, antioxidant buffering, and [[Autophagy]] that clears damaged organelles. Dysregulation contributes to ischemic stroke, traumatic brain injury, status epilepticus, and chronic neurodegeneration in [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]], amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and Huntington's disease. Modulating NMDA receptor activity and bolstering mitochondrial and antioxidant defenses are active neuroprotective strategies.
