# ELK1

[[ELK1]] is the founding member of the ternary complex factor (TCF) subfamily of [[ETS]]-domain transcription factors (alongside ELK3 and [[ELK4]]/SAP-1). It is a major nuclear target of the [[RAS]]/[[ERK|MAPK/ERK]] cascade: ERK-mediated phosphorylation converts ELK1 into a transcriptional activator controlling immediate-early genes such as *FOS* and *EGR1*.

## Structure & Regulation

- Contains a conserved ETS DNA-binding domain (GGAA/T core motifs) and an SRF interaction domain that allows cooperative binding to the serum response element (SRE) of *c-fos*.
- Phosphorylated by ERK1/2 at a C-terminal transcriptional activation domain, driving rapid induction of immediate-early genes; also sumoylated, which can convert it into a transcriptional repressor.

## Function & Pathology

- Controls proliferation, differentiation, and the early transcriptional response to growth-factor and stress signals.
- Deregulated ELK1 activity contributes to oncogenic transcription programs downstream of constitutive MAPK signaling in cancers.
- As a TCF member, it shares the DNA-binding and SRF-coupling architecture used by [[ELK4]] in chromatin-recruitment contexts.

## Connection to Sirtuin Biology

The ELK1-related TCF [[ELK4]] was reported to stabilize [[SIRT7]] at target gene promoters, enabling H3K18 deacetylation and repression of tumor-suppressor genes. ELK1 itself is a useful comparator for the ETS-TCF family in chromatin-regulatory studies.
