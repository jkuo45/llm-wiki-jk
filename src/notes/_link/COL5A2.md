# COL5A2

*COL5A2* (2q32.2, immediately adjacent to [[COL3A1]] in tail-to-tail orientation on
chromosome 2) encodes the alpha-2(V) chain of **type V collagen**, one of the
low-abundance fibrillar collagens. Type V is a heterotypic collagen: its molecules
contain two alpha-1(V) chains and one alpha-2(V) chain (or, in some tissues, the
alpha-1(V)/alpha-2(V)/alpha-3(V) combination shared with type XI).

## Function: the nucleation template

Type V collagen is present at only 2–5% of the abundance of type I, and that low
abundance is the point. It is not a major load-bearing fibre; it is a **nucleation
template** that controls the assembly of type I:

- Type V co-assembles with type I to form **heterotypic fibrils**, rather than
  forming fibres of its own.
- It nucleates type I fibril formation and regulates the resulting **fibril diameter**.
- In skin, type V and type I appear in parallel arrays; tendon and cornea are dominated
  by type I; cornea and intervertebral disc are the tissues where type XI substitutes
  for type V with an almost identical chain architecture.

Type V and type XI are homologous enough that they are sometimes described as a single
collagen type with tissue-specific chain combinations. The distinction is functional,
not fundamental.

> [!info] Physiological consequence of loss
> Removing type V does not remove tensile strength so much as it removes the control
> on fibre geometry. That is why classical Ehlers-Danlos syndrome — in which
> *COL5A1* and *COL5A2* variants are the cause — presents with skin hyperextensibility,
> atrophic scarring and joint hypermobility rather than with catastrophic rupture. The
> vasculature is relatively spared. This contrasts sharply with the vascular (type IV)
> phenotype caused by *COL3A1*, where fragility of the arterial wall is the defining
> feature.

## Clinical significance

Heterozygous mutations in *COL5A2* (and more commonly *COL5A1*, which accounts for the
majority of cases) cause **classical Ehlers-Danlos syndrome** (formerly types I and II).
The characteristic phenotype is skin hyperextensibility and fragility with atrophic
"papery" scarring, easy bruising, joint hypermobility, and in the more severe form
muscle hypotonia and early-onset spinal deformity. Classical EDS is usually not
life-limiting, though a small subset develops arterial rupture.

Recurrent *COL5A2* variants include a structural allele producing a shortened alpha-2(V)
chain that is over-represented in joint hypermobility cohorts, and haploinsufficiency
alleles. Because the type V trimer tolerates some chain imbalance, a single mutant
alpha-2(V) chain is diluted into a trimer of which two thirds are wild type — the
mechanism of haploinsufficiency in a structural collagen gene.

## Relationship to TGF-β signalling

Like other fibrillar collagens, *COL5A2* expression is induced by the canonical
[[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway|TGF-β pathway]] — [[TGF-beta1|TGF-β1]] → [[TGFBR1]] →
[[SMAD2]]/[[SMAD3]]/[[SMAD4]] complex → nucleus. Elevated *COL5A2* and *COL3A1*
transcript levels in the same tissue are therefore read as a paired readout of TGF-β
pathway activity, and both are standard fibrosis markers measured alongside
[[TIMP1]] and the [[Matrix Metalloproteinases|MMP]] family in fibrotic tissue.

## Connections

- [[SMAD3]] — Phosphorylated SMAD3 in the SMAD2/3/4 complex occupies the COL5A2 promoter, making SMAD3 occupancy a direct readout of type V collagen transcription. SMAD3 is the shared node linking the type V and type III collagen notes.
- [[COL3A1]] — COL5A2 sits immediately adjacent to COL3A1 on 2q32.2 in tail-to-tail orientation, and both genes are TGF-beta1/SMAD3-responsive. The two are therefore physical and regulatory neighbours in the genome and in fibrosis biology.
- [[Collagen]] — Type V collagen is a member of the fibrillar collagen family, defined by its long uninterrupted triple-helical domain and Gly-X-Y repeat, but functionally distinct because it is present at low abundance and regulates other collagens rather than carrying load.
- [[Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome]] — Classical Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (types I and II) is caused by mutations in COL5A1 and COL5A2, producing skin hyperextensibility, atrophic scarring and joint hypermobility. COL5A2 is one of the two causal genes.
- [[Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome, Classic Type]] — The classical subtype is the specific disease entity caused by COL5A2 (and COL5A1) variants, distinct from the vascular subtype caused by COL3A1.
- [[TGF-beta1]] — TGF-β1 signalling through TGFBR1 and SMAD2/3/4 is the transcriptional driver of COL5A2 expression and therefore of the type V contribution to fibrotic matrix.
- [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway]] — The pathway whose nuclear output includes COL5A2 transcription. COL5A2 transcript level is used as a downstream marker of pathway activation.
- [[TIMP1]] — TIMP1 restrains Matrix Metalloproteinases; the TIMP:MMP ratio determines whether TGF-beta-driven collagen synthesis results in net accumulation or net turnover of the type V-rich heterotypic fibril network.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — Type V collagen is a nucleation and control element of the extracellular matrix. Its abundance sets type I fibril calibre, so it is a regulator of matrix architecture rather than a bulk structural component.
- [[Wound Healing]] — Type V collagen is abundant in early granulation tissue and in fetal and keloid skin, where it is progressively replaced by type I during scar maturation and in hypertrophic remodelling.
- [[Fibrosis]] — Excess type V collagen deposition, driven by TGF-beta1 and SMAD3, is a histological hallmark of fibrosis. The ratio of type V to type I and the TIMP:MMP balance together determine the outcome.
