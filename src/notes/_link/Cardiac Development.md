# Cardiac Development

Cardiogenesis — the prenatal development of the heart — is the first functional organ
system to form in a vertebrate embryo. Because the heart must function immediately and
without interruption, every one of its developmental steps is tightly timed, and each is
a point at which a genetic or teratogenic lesion produces a congenital heart defect.
Congenital heart disease is the commonest birth defect class, so this pathway is a
principal explanation of human developmental disease.

## Induction

The heart derives from the mesodermal germ layer. Induction occurs in the anterior
mesoderm during gastrulation, and is driven by signals from the adjacent endoderm
(extra-embryonic and definitive), principally endogenous inhibitors of [[Wnt]]
signalling such as DKK1, which stabilise β-catenin and drive a cardiogenic gene program
in the anterior lateral plate mesoderm. In the mouse, the second heart field (SHF) is
the major contributor to the right heart, atria, outflow tract and part of the
myocardium, and is induced later and more posteriorly than the first heart field (FHF);
its formation depends on FGF signalling and on stage-specific [[Notch]]-mediated
inhibition of [[GATA4|GATA4]]-independent differentiation.

The canonical cardiac master transcription factors are NKX2-5, GATA4, [[MEF2C]],
TBX5 and Hand1/Hand2. They act combinatorially rather than redundantly: no single one is
sufficient, and disease genes typically knock out one component of a cooperation
(hand-in-hand pocket on DNA). The chamber identity code is set by chamber-specific
factor combinations — Hand1 and Hand2 for ventricular versus atrial specification,
Nkx2-5 plus Hand2 for right versus left ventricular myocardium, and Pitx2 for left-sided
identity, which establishes left-right asymmetry.

## Tube formation

Around day 19 in the human (embryonic stage 7), two endocardial tubes form on either
side of the developing neural plate within the cardiogenic mesenchyme. As embryonic
folding brings the embryo into the thoracic cavity, the two tubes converge and fuse —
completed at about day 22 — by a process that uses programmed cell death as well as
cell migration, producing the **primitive heart tube**. Five regions become recognisable
from cranial to caudal: truncus arteriosus, bulbus cordis, primitive ventricle, primitive
atrium and sinus venosus.

The myocardium thickens and secretes a hyaluronan-rich extracellular matrix that
separates it from the endothelium; the pericardium forms from the mesothelium and the
myocardial cells migrate outward to form most of the epicardium.

## Looping and septation

The first visible sign of left-right asymmetry is **rightward looping of the heart tube**
on about day 23. Cephalic (bulbar) looping brings the bulbus cordis to the right and
caudal (atrial) looping brings the atria leftward; by day 28 the loop is complete. The
ventricle–bulbus junction then forms the primary interventricular foramen.

- **Aorticopulmonary septum** — neural crest cells migrate into the truncus arteriosus
  and form a spiral ridge that divides it into the ascending aorta and pulmonary trunk.
  Failure produces transposition of the great arteries; the neural crest contribution
  also explains why conotruncal defects associate with [[22q11.2 deletion]]-type syndromes
  and with maternal diabetes.
- **Endocardial cushions** — the atrioventricular canal cushions form by
  [[Endocardial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndMT]] of endocardial cells under the influence
  of [[Has2]]-mediated hyaluronan signalling; they divide the atrioventricular canal,
  contribute to the atrioventricular septum and to the mitral and tricuspid valve leaflets,
  and their malformation produces AV septal defects and the common atrioventricular
  canal of Down syndrome.
- **Outflow tract remodelling** — neural crest smooth muscle occupies the outflow tract
  and the aortic root, in a process tightly dependent on [[Hes1 and Hey2]] and on
  semaphorin–neuropilin and ephrin–[[Ephrin Type A Receptor 2|EphA2]] guidance. This
  dependence is why [[Hes1 and Hey2]] double-null mice die with a persistent truncus
  arteriosus.

## Chamber maturation and the conduction system

After septation, the primitive ventricle is divided by the interventricular septum into
morphological left and right ventricles. The embryonic sinus venosus is incorporated into
the right atrium as the sinoatrial node and coronary sinus. Myocardial proliferation and
maturation follow, and the [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|haematopoietic]] progenitors
temporarily colonise the outflow tract before seeding the liver and marrow.

The conduction system is built by a second wave of cells that delaminate from the
myocardium, marked by [[Hes1 and Hey2]] for the atrioventricular node and by Islet1 for
the peripheral Purkinje network — an example of the same Notch effectors used for
valve and outflow morphogenesis being reused for a different tissue.

## Postnatal maturation

Fetal cardiomyocytes are proliferative; after birth they exit the cell cycle almost
completely, and adult cardiomyocyte turnover is on the order of a fraction of a percent
per year. The newborn heart also shifts its energy substrate preference: the fetal
phenotype relies heavily on glycolysis and lactate, whereas the adult heart depends
predominantly on fatty acid oxidation — the metabolic shift that is reversed in heart
failure. Ventricular wall thickness, collagen deposition and [[Titin]] isoform expression
all continue to change through the first year.

## Modelling and study

Cardiac development is studied in the mouse (where the timeline above maps almost
directly onto mouse embryonic days), in zebrafish, in *Drosophila*, in human
[[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells|pluripotent stem cells]] differentiated to
[[Cardiomyocyte|cardiomyocytes]] and assembled into [[Embryoid Bodies|embryoid bodies]],
[[Organoids|heart organoids]], and engineered heart muscle. Lineage tracing in the mouse
established the first-heart-field versus second-heart-field division of labour; human
iPSC differentiation protocols exploit the same BMP/WNT/retinoic acid modulation
sequences.

## Connections

- [[GATA4]] — GATA4 is a master cardiac transcription factor of the second heart field and cooperates hand-in-hand with NKX2-5 on chamber identity genes. Heterozygous loss-of-function causes septal defects and, in mouse, embryonic lethality from defective looping.
- [[Hes1 and Hey2]] — The Hes1 and Hey2 bHLH repressors are the Notch transcriptional output that specifies the atrioventricular node, the outflow tract and the atrioventricular cushions. Their loss produces a persistent truncus arteriosus, one of the classic models of a great-vessel septation failure.
- [[Wnt]] — Inhibition of Wnt signalling in the anterior mesoderm is the earliest instructive step: DKK1-mediated Wnt inhibition is what commits the cardiac field at all. Later, reactivation of Wnt in the outflow tract is required for neural crest invasion and cushion maturation.
- [[Notch]] — Notch signalling acts twice: to delay epicardial differentiation so the myocardial and endocardial programmes can run, and later to specify cushions, valves and the conduction system. Aberrant Notch dosage produces bicuspid aortic valve, tying this note to [[Bicuspid Aortic Valve]].
- [[Endocardial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] — EndMT is the mechanism by which endocardial cells become cushion mesenchyme and the moving valve leaflets of the atrioventricular and semilunar valves. It is also a pathological route to fibroblast-like cells in adult fibrosis.
- [[Cardiomyocytes]] — Cardiomyocytes are the output of cardiac development: FHF-derived from the first heart field, Nkx2-5+ second-heart-field-derived for the rest. The fetal proliferative-to-terminal state transition after birth is what makes adult heart failure a problem of hypertrophy rather than regeneration.
- [[Endocardial Cushion]] — The cushions are the shared embryological precursor of the atrioventricular septum, the atrioventricular valve leaflets and the membranous interventricular septum, so cushion malformation produces a spectrum of septal and valvular defects.
- [[Cell Signaling]] — Cardiac development is the paradigm case of signalling in a morphogenetic process: Notch, Wnt, BMP and FGF signals pattern a field, and transcription factor codes read those signals to specify identity.
- [[Bicuspid Aortic Valve]] — A congenital defect of valvulogenesis, the terminal step of cardiac development, arising from abnormal endocardial cushion remodelling. Its best-validated gene is NOTCH1.
- [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] — Human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes and heart organoids are the principal human model of cardiac development, since human embryonic tissue at these stages is not accessible.
- [[Mesenchymal-to-Epithelial Transition]] — EMT/MET pairs are the workhorse transitions of cardiogenesis: EndMT in cushion formation, and EMT for the epicardium-to-mesenchymal and neural crest contributions that build the outflow tract.
