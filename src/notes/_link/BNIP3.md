# BNIP3

**Overview:** BNIP3 (BCL2 Interacting Protein 3) is a single-pass transmembrane protein located on the outer mitochondrial membrane, belonging to the [[Bcl-2 family|BH3-only subfamily]] of the BCL-2 family. It functions as a receptor for ubiquitin-independent [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]].

**Structure and Function:** BNIP3 directly interacts with the autophagy-related protein [[LC3]] via its [[LIR Motif|LIR motif]], triggering the specific clearance of [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]]. It is induced by [[HIF-1α]] under hypoxic stress and acts as an important receptor-mediated autophagy receptor.

**Role in Hypoxia-Induced Mitophagy:** Following severe metabolic stress (such as [[Hypoxia|hypoxia]]/[[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury|ischemia]]), [[HIF-1α]] within neurons transcriptionally activates BNIP3. BNIP3 then inserts into the mitochondrial membrane, directly causing the loss of [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential|mitochondrial membrane potential]] and the opening of the [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore|mitochondrial permeability transition pore]]. This BNIP3-mediated cell death is delayed neuronal death. Knocking out the BNIP3 gene can significantly improve neuronal survival in damaged brain regions.

**Pathological Significance:** In [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]], BNIP3 expression is significantly lower in brain tissues compared to normal controls, contributing to mitochondrial clearance disorders. Excessively prolonged activation of BNIP3-mediated mitophagy disrupts neuronal energy metabolism and triggers [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]. Inhibiting BNIP3 represents a potential therapeutic target for preventing neuronal degenerative death.
