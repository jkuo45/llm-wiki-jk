# Clear cell renal cell carcinoma

**Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC)** is the most common subtype of [[Renal Cell Carcinoma]], arising from proximal tubular epithelial cells with loss of the VHL tumor suppressor. A defining metabolic feature—and the basis of its ferroptosis link—is an exceptionally high dependence on [[Glutathione]] (GSH) synthesis and the [[GPX4]] antioxidant axis to prevent [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and cell death.

## Molecular Context

ccRCC cells are highly sensitive to depletion of glutamine and cystine, both required for GSH synthesis. They rely heavily on the GSH/GPX4 pathway to neutralize lipid ROS. This fragility stems from their reprogrammed redox metabolism and lipid-rich phenotype (accumulation of neutral lipids gives the characteristic "clear" cytoplasm).

## Ferroptosis Connection

The review states that ccRCC cells are highly sensitive to glutamine and cystine depletion and rely heavily on the GSH/GPX4 pathway to prevent lipid peroxidation. Inhibiting GSH synthesis in ccRCC induces [[Ferroptosis]] and suppresses tumor growth (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Clear cell renal cell carcinoma"). Because cystine uptake depends on [[System Xc-]] ([[SLC7A11]]) and GSH on glutamine, dual targeting of these inputs is a rational ferroptotic strategy.

> [!info] Why ccRCC is ferroptosis-prone
> Its GSH addiction means any hit to cystine import (system Xc-) or glutamine metabolism collapses GPX4 defense, tipping cells into iron-dependent death—making ccRCC a prime candidate for ferroptosis-based therapy.

## Clinical Relevance

Ferroptosis induction (system Xc- inhibitors, GPX4 inhibitors, glutaminolysis blockers) is an attractive approach for ccRCC, including [[Cisplatin]]-resistant and VHL-mutant disease. The Sold/glutamine axis and lipid peroxidation threshold are active therapeutic targets. Related renal pathology overlaps with [[Acute Kidney Injury]] and [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] ferroptosis.
