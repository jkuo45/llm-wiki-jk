# ChREBP

**ChREBP (carbohydrate-responsive element-binding protein; gene MLXIPL)** is a glucose-activated transcription factor that induces the enzymes of de novo lipogenesis and glycolysis in the liver. In response to high carbohydrate intake, ChREBP shifts hepatic metabolism toward lipid synthesis, linking nutrition to fatty liver disease.

## Sirtuin Regulation

- **[[SIRT1]]** and **[[SIRT6]]** deacetylate ChREBP (alongside [[SREBP-1c]]) to maintain hepatic lipid homeostasis and oppose fatty liver disease.
- Hepatocyte-specific [[SIRT6]] knockout elevates fibrosis and oxidative stress in NASH mice, in part through loss of ChREBP/SREBP-1c control.

## Physiological Function

ChREBP integrates glucose sensing with lipogenic gene expression, balancing carbohydrate disposal against ectopic lipid accumulation. Its acetylation status is a key regulatory node modulated by sirtuins.

## Clinical Relevance

SIRT1/SIRT6-mediated deacetylation of ChREBP suppresses hepatic lipogenesis, representing an epigenetic brake on NAFLD and metabolic syndrome.
