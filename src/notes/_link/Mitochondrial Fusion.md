# Mitochondrial Fusion

**Mitochondrial fusion** is the dynamin-related GTPase–mediated process by which two [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] merge their outer and inner membranes, creating a single, elongated, interconnected network. Fusion is essential for maintaining a healthy mitochondrial pool: it permits the exchange of [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]], proteins, and metabolites between organelles, complements defective components, and supports optimal [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS)]]. An imbalance favoring either [[Mitochondrial Fusion|fusion]] or [[Mitochondrial Fission|fission]] underlies [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative]], [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular]], and [[Cancer|neoplastic]] diseases.

## Molecular Machinery

Mitochondrial fusion occurs in two coordinated steps — outer membrane (OMM) and inner membrane (IMM) fusion — driven by three dynamin-related GTPases.

### Outer Membrane Fusion — Mitofusins

[[MFN1|Mitofusin 1 (MFN1)]] and [[MFN2|Mitofusin 2 (MFN2)]] are large integral GTPases of the [[Outer Mitochondrial Membrane|outer mitochondrial membrane]] and are the mammalian homologs of the drosophila protein [[Fzo|Fzo]]. (Separately, [[OPA1|OPA1]] is the mammalian ortholog of the yeast protein [[Mgm1|Mgm1]], which drives fusion in yeast.) Tethering of two OMMs is achieved through oligomerization of the GTPase domains of the mitofusins; GTP binding and hydrolysis drive a conformational change that brings the two [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] into docking and fusion. A newer topology model places both MFN1 and MFN2 with a single transmembrane domain, positioning the N-terminal GTPase and HR1 in the [[Cytosol|cytosol]] and the C-terminal HR2 domain in the [[Mitochondrial Intermembrane Space|intermembrane space]].

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial Fusion and Fission The fine-tune balance for cellular homeostasis|Mitochondrial Fusion and Fission review]]
> Redox signaling regulates OMM fusion: increased oxidized [[Glutathione|glutathione]] oxidizes two cysteine residues in the HR2 domains of MFN molecules, forming disulfide bonds that drive MFN oligomerization; adding GSH reverses the oligomers.

[[MFN2|MFN2]] also localizes to the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]] membrane, where it tethers ER to mitochondria and enables [[Calcium|mitochondrial calcium uptake]].

### Inner Membrane Fusion — OPA1 and Cardiolipin

[[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane|Inner membrane]] fusion is mediated by [[OPA1|Optic Atrophy Protein 1 (OPA1)]], a GTPase anchored to the IMM, together with the lipid [[Cardiolipin]]. OPA1 exists as eight splice isoforms processed into long (L-OPA1, anchored) and short (S-OPA1) forms; efficient IMM fusion requires both L- and S-OPA1 working together. [[Cardiolipin|Cardiolipin]] binds L-OPA1 on liposomes and is absolutely required for fusion — removal of cardiolipin abolishes membrane merging even when L-OPA1 is present on both membranes.

## Regulation

- **[[ERK|ERK]]** phosphorylates [[MFN1|Mfn1]] in HR1, inhibiting fusion and promoting [[Apoptosis]].
- **[[HDAC6|HDAC6]]** deacetylates [[MFN1|Mfn1]], activating it and promoting fusion during [[Glucose|glucose]] deprivation.
- **[[JNK]]** phosphorylates [[MFN2|MFN2]] under stress, recruiting an E3 [[Ubiquitination|ubiquitin ligase]] that degrades MFN2, causing [[Mitochondrial Fission|fragmentation]] and apoptosis.
- **[[PINK1]]** phosphorylates [[MFN2|MFN2]], leading to [[Ubiquitination|ubiquitination]] by [[Parkin]] and eventual [[Mitophagy]].
- **[[SIRT3]]** deacetylates [[OPA1|OPA1]] at Lys926/Lys931, elevating its GTPase activity and enhancing fusion.
- **[[YME1L]]** and **[[OMA1]]** proteolytically process OPA1 (S2/S3 and S1 sites, respectively), controlling the L/S-OPA1 balance.

## Physiological Role

Fusion is upregulated by inhibitors of protein synthesis, [[mTOR|mTOR]] inhibition–induced [[Autophagy|autophagy]], and starvation, protecting mitochondria from autophagic degradation. It allows complementation of damaged mtDNA and equalizes membrane potential across the network. However, its role is context-dependent: in pancreatic adenocarcinoma, forced fusion (MFN2 overexpression or [[DRP1|DRP1]] inhibition) increased [[Mitophagy]], shrinking mitochondrial mass and reducing OXPHOS.

## Pathological Associations

- **[[Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease Type 2A]]** — heterozygous [[MFN2]] mutations.
- **[[Dominant Optic Atrophy]]** — [[OPA1]] missense mutations impair IMM fusion.
- **[[Cancer]]** — both excessive fusion (e.g., liver tumor metabolism via OPA1/MFN1) and excessive fission can promote tumor growth depending on context.

## Connections

- [[MFN1]] — OMM GTPase; oligomerization drives outer-membrane tethering and fusion.
- [[MFN2]] — OMM GTPase; also ER tether; mutations cause CMT2A.
- [[OPA1]] — IMM GTPase; with cardiolipin mediates inner-membrane fusion.
- [[Cardiolipin]] — IMM lipid required for OPA1-mediated fusion.
- [[DRP1]] — Counterbalancing fission GTPase; inhibition promotes fusion.
- [[SIRT3]] — Deacetylates and activates OPA1.
- [[Mitochondrial Fission]] — The opposing process; balance of the two defines network morphology.
