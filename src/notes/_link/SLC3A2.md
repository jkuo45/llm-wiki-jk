# SLC3A2

**SLC3A2** (solute carrier family 3 member 2), also known as **4F2 cell-surface antigen heavy chain (4F2hc)** or **CD98**, is the heavy-chain subunit of several heterodimeric amino acid transporters, most relevantly the [[System Xc-]] cystine/glutamate antiporter. SLC3A2 does not itself bind cystine but is essential for the proper folding, disulfide-linked assembly, and plasma-membrane localization of its catalytic light-chain partner [[SLC7A11]] (xCT). Through this role it indirectly governs cellular [[Glutathione]] (GSH) supply and therefore ferroptosis susceptibility.

## Structure & Domains

SLC3A2 is a type II single-pass transmembrane glycoprotein with a large extracellular N-terminal domain containing the disulfide-bond motif that covalently tethers the light chain. Its intracellular tail participates in trafficking and signaling scaffolds. It heterodimerizes not only with SLC7A11 (system Xc-) but also with multiple SLC7 family light chains (e.g., LAT1/2, y+LAT1/2), giving 4F2hc broad roles in amino acid transport and integrin signaling.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

By chaperoning [[SLC7A11]] to the plasma membrane, SLC3A2 enables the antiporter to import cystine in exchange for [[Glutamate]]. Cystine reduction to cysteine feeds GSH biosynthesis, sustaining [[GPX4]] activity and suppressing [[Lipid Peroxidation]]-driven [[Ferroptosis]]. Loss of SLC3A2 destabilizes SLC7A11, curtailing cystine uptake and sensitizing cells to ferroptosis, while abundant 4F2hc supports ferroptosis resistance in tumors.

> [!info] Beyond transport
> 4F2hc/CD98 signals through integrins to influence cell proliferation, migration, and survival—functions separable from its role in system Xc-. This dual identity complicates interpretation of CD98-targeted therapies.

## Physiological Function

SLC3A2 is ubiquitously expressed and critical during development, immunity (activated lymphocyte amino-acid sensing), and tissue homeostasis. Its transport complexes regulate mTORC1 activation via amino-acid availability.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

CD98 is upregulated in many cancers and is being explored as an immunotherapy target. In the ferroptosis context, its role is primarily supportive of [[System Xc-]]; therapies that disrupt 4F2hc–SLC7A11 assembly could mimic [[Erastin]]-like cystine-deprivation. Its role in [[Breast Cancer]] and [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] ferroptosis resistance is partly mediated through xCT stability.
