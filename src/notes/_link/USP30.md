# USP30

USP30 (Ubiquitin Specific Peptidase 30) is a mitochondrial deubiquitinase that opposes [[Parkin]]-mediated [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] by removing [[Ubiquitin|ubiquitin]] chains from outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM) proteins. It functions as a negative regulator of mitophagy, counterbalancing the ubiquitination activity of [[Parkin]] and [[PINK1]].

## Mechanism

While [[PINK1]] and [[Parkin]] orchestrate the clearance of dysfunctional [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] by inducing ubiquitylation of OMM proteins, this process is antagonized by USP30-mediated deubiquitylation. By removing ubiquitin chains, USP30 prevents the recruitment of autophagy receptors (such as [[OPTN]] and [[NDP52]]) to damaged [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]], thereby inhibiting mitophagy initiation.

## Therapeutic Potential

Loss of USP30 in [[Knockout mouse|knockout mice]] prevents behavioral deficits, increases mitophagy, and reduces both phosphorylated S129 [[Alpha-synuclein|α-Syn]] levels and α-Syn-induced [[Dopaminergic Neurons|dopaminergic neuron]] loss. [[MTX115325]], a potent, selective, and brain-penetrant USP30 inhibitor, has shown therapeutic efficacy in [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] models. USP30 knockdown also reduces lipid ROS by about 40% and reduces neuronal death, linking USP30 inhibition to [[Ferroptosis|ferroptosis]] suppression.

## Role in Neurodegeneration

As a negative regulator of [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], USP30 represents an attractive therapeutic target for diseases where impaired mitophagy contributes to pathology, including [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] and potentially other [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]].
