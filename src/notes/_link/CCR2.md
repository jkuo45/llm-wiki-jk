# CCR2

**CCR2** (C-C chemokine receptor type 2) is a G-protein-coupled receptor for the chemokine [[CCL2|CCL2 (MCP-1)]] (and related ligands CCL7, CCL8, CCL13). It is expressed on [[Monocytes]], [[Macrophages]], [[Microglia]], and subsets of [[T Cell|T cells]], and directs monocyte egress from bone marrow and recruitment to inflamed tissues.

## Role in Inflammation and Aging

CCR2 is a central mediator of inflammatory cell trafficking. In aged tissues, the [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]] attracts CCR2+ inflammatory cells; SASP-driven immune recruitment through CCR2 contributes to cardiac and other tissue aging phenotypes. Targeting CCR2 (e.g., with antagonists such as [[Monalizumab|maraviroc-related compounds]] or PF-04178903) is investigated for chronic inflammatory and fibrotic diseases.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP|Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP]]
> In aged mice, [[CTPI2]]-mediated suppression of the SASP reduced pro-inflammatory CCR2+ macrophage markers in female hearts — consistent with reduced SASP-driven immune recruitment during cardiac aging.
