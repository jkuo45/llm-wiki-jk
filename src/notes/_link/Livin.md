# Livin

**Overview:** [[Livin]], also known as BIRC7 or ML-IAP (melanoma inhibitor of apoptosis protein), is a member of the inhibitor of apoptosis (IAP) family. It is normally expressed during development and in certain immune cells but is broadly silent in most adult tissues.

**Structure and domains:** Like other IAPs, Livin contains a baculoviral IAP repeat (BIR) domain that directly binds and inhibits effector [[Caspases]] (caspase-3, -7, and -9), and a C-terminal RING finger that confers E3 ubiquitin-ligase activity, targeting apoptotic proteins for [[Proteasome|proteasomal]] degradation.

**Cellular function:** By suppressing caspase activation, Livin blocks the execution phase of [[Apoptosis]], enhancing cell survival under stress. Its expression can be induced by survival cytokines and oncogenic signaling.

**Pathological significance:** Livin is overexpressed in diverse cancers—including melanoma, lung, breast, and bladder carcinomas—where it confers resistance to chemotherapy and radiation and correlates with poor prognosis. It is investigated both as a diagnostic/prognostic biomarker and as a therapeutic target (smac mimetics, [[XIAP|-targeted]] IAP antagonism). Its silencing sensitizes tumors to death pathways.
