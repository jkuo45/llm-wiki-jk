# Phosphatidylethanolamine

**Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE)** is one of the most abundant phospholipids of mammalian membranes, particularly the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane and mitochondrial membranes. In ferroptosis, PE species esterified with arachidonic acid (AA) or adrenic acid are the **key peroxidation substrates** whose oxidation triggers lethal membrane damage. PE is thus the structural and biochemical stage on which [[Ferroptosis]] is executed.

## Biochemistry & Distribution

PE consists of a glycerol backbone, two fatty acyl chains, a phosphate, and an ethanolamine head group. Its sn-2 position is frequently occupied by long-chain PUFAs. The generation of oxidizable PUFA-PE is governed by [[ACSL4]] (which activates free PUFAs to acyl-CoAs) and [[LPCAT3]] (which re-esterifies them into phospholipids). PE hydroperoxides (PE-OOH) are the proximal lethal species in ferroptosis.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

When [[GPX4]] (and the [[FSP1]]–[[Coenzyme Q10|CoQ10]] axis) fail to reduce lipid peroxides, PUFA-PE undergoes radical-chain peroxidation propagated by [[Iron]] via the [[Fenton Reaction]]. [[Lipoxygenase|Lipoxygenases]] such as [[ALOX15]] directly oxygenate PUFA-PE. Accumulation of PE-OOH disrupts membrane integrity, leading to rupture and cell death. PUFA-PE is therefore both the substrate and the signal carrier of ferroptosis (review "Regulation of the lipid metabolism pathway").

> [!info] Why PE, not other phospholipids?
> PE—especially in the inner leaflet—has the highest density of peroxidizable double bonds and is uniquely positioned to translate lipid oxidation into catastrophic membrane collapse. Its peroxidation is more lethal than that of phosphatidylcholine.

## Physiological Function

PE supports membrane curvature (via its conical shape), mitochondrial fusion, and protein anchoring. Its peroxidation threshold is normally guarded by GPX4.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

The PUFA-PE pool size, set by [[ACSL4]]/[[LPCAT3]], is a major determinant of ferroptosis sensitivity in [[Cancer]], [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], and neurodegeneration. Therapeutics that shrink (e.g., [[Acid ceramidase]] inhibition) or expand the PUFA-PE pool modulate ferroptotic vulnerability.
