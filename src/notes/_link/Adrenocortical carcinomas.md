# Adrenocortical carcinomas

**Adrenocortical carcinomas (ACCs)** are rare, highly malignant endocrine cancers of the adrenal cortex with poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options. The ferroptosis literature identifies ACC as a tumor with elevated [[GPX4]] expression and consequently heightened sensitivity to ferroptosis, raising the possibility that ferroptosis-inducing therapy could replace or augment the toxic standard agent [[Mitotane]].

## Molecular Context

ACC exhibits dysregulated steroidogenesis, frequent CTNNB1/TP53 alterations, and an aggressive biology. Its high GPX4 level is notable: GPX4 is the master negative regulator of [[Ferroptosis]], so abundant GPX4 normally protects cells—but it also signifies a dependency: when GPX4 is inhibited or GSH depleted, ACC cells are primed to die by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation.

## Ferroptosis Connection

The review reports significantly increased expression of [[GPX4]] and sensitivity to ferroptosis in ACCs, suggesting that inducing ferroptosis may be a promising treatment approach. Moreover, ferroptosis inducers "may be more effective and less toxic than mitotane" in treating ACC patients (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Adrenocortical carcinomas").

> [!important] GPX4 as a vulnerability, not just a protector
> In ACC, high GPX4 marks ferroptosis sensitivity: the same protein that defends the cell becomes the node whose inhibition is lethal. GPX4 inhibitors ([[RSL3]], FINs) or upstream cystine-starvation ([[Erastin]]) are candidate ACC therapies.

## Clinical Relevance

Mitotane remains standard but is toxic and often ineffective. Ferroptosis-targeted strategies—GPX4 inhibition, iron loading, [[System Xc-]] blockade—represent an investigational, potentially safer avenue justified by ACC's ferroptosis-sensitive, GPX4-high phenotype. Related [[Cancer]] ferroptosis paradigms (e.g., [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], [[Renal Cell Carcinoma]]) inform combinatorial design.
