# Myelopoiesis

Myelopoiesis is the generation of myeloid cells — [[Neutrophils]], monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells — from [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cells]] via progenitor intermediates (LS−K, CMP, GMP). It is one arm of [[Hematopoiesis]], normally balanced against lymphopoiesis.

## Age-Associated Myeloid Skewing

- Aging shifts [[Hematopoiesis]] toward the myeloid lineage: expanded HSC and GMP fractions, contracted common lymphoid progenitors, elevated circulating and tissue-infiltrating [[Neutrophils]], and an increased neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) — a strong predictor of all-cause mortality across age-associated diseases.
- Established niche mechanisms include adrenergic nerve degeneration, IL-1β-driven stromal inflammation, IGF1 decline, vascular niche remodeling, and adipocyte accumulation.
- A T cell–BM axis adds to these: [[Cytotoxic CD4+ T Cells]] accumulating in the aged [[Bone Marrow]] secrete [[CCL5]], which signals through [[CCR5]] upregulated on aged HSCs and GMPs to selectively drive myeloid output (Gabandé-Rodríguez et al., 2026, *Nat Aging*). T cell-infiltration-driven myelopoiesis also occurs in multiple sclerosis and acute stress.
- Aged ("CXCR4^hi^CD62L^lo") neutrophils display an activated, pro-inflammatory profile (ICAM-1^hi^, TLR-4^hi^, CD11b^hi^, CXCR2^lo^).

## Clinical Significance

- Elevated NLR predicts mortality in the general population and virtually all age-associated diseases (Rotterdam Study, Baltimore Longitudinal Study on Aging).
- Pharmacological suppression of myelopoiesis ([[Maraviroc]]/CCR5 blockade; myeloid-biased HSC depletion; 4-oxo-retinoic acid after myocardial infarction) improves immune function and healthspan outcomes.
