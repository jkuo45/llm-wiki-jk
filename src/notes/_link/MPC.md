# MPC

The **mitochondrial pyruvate carrier (MPC)** is a heterodimeric complex composed of MPC1 and MPC2 subunits in the inner [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] membrane. It imports [[Pyruvate]] (derived from [[Glycolysis]]) from the cytosol into the mitochondrial matrix, where it is converted to acetyl-CoA by the [[Pyruvate Dehydrogenase]] complex to enter the [[TCA cycle]]. MPC thereby links glycolysis to mitochondrial oxidative metabolism.

## Overview

The MPC is the rate-limiting entry point for glucose-derived carbon into the TCA cycle. By controlling pyruvate import, the MPC determines the balance between mitochondrial oxidation and cytosolic [[Lactate]] production, and ultimately the supply of [[TCA cycle]] intermediates that feed downstream biosynthetic and epigenetic programs.

## Role in Cellular Senescence

- Expression of MPC1 and MPC2 is increased across multiple senescence models (irradiation-induced, doxorubicin-induced, replicative)
- Pharmacological inhibition of MPC with [[UK5099]] reduces TCA cycle intermediates (upregulated in senescence) and increases lactate levels, consistent with impaired mitochondrial pyruvate import
- MPC inhibition suppresses SASP gene expression and secretion without affecting cell cycle arrest, p16/p21 levels, EdU incorporation, or DNA damage foci
- CRISPR–Cas9-mediated deletion of MPC1 reduces expression of SASP factors such as *IL6* and *IL8*

> [!info] MPC Is an Upstream Node of the Citrate–Acetyl-CoA Axis
> MPC-dependent pyruvate import feeds the TCA cycle, which generates the citrate exported by [[SLC25A1]] and converted to [[Acetyl-CoA]] by [[ACLY]]. Inhibition at any of these nodes (MPC, SLC25A1, ACLY) suppresses histone acetylation at SASP loci and the SASP programme.
