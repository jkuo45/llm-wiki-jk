# GATA4

**GATA4** (GATA Binding Protein 4) is a zinc-finger transcription factor of the GATA family that recognizes the GATA motif (WGATAR) in gene promoters. It is essential for [[Cardiac Development]], [[Cardiomyocyte]] differentiation, and the hypertrophic response to pressure overload and neurohumoral stimuli, and is expressed predominantly in the heart as well as liver, lung, gut, and gonads, where it governs tissue-specific gene programs. In the context of cellular senescence, GATA4 is a stress-inducible factor that acts alongside [[NF-κB]], [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]], and DNA-damage signaling to sustain the inflammatory secretome of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].

## Structure & Domains

GATA4 contains two conserved zinc finger domains (N-terminal ZnF1 and C-terminal ZnF2). The C-terminal finger is the principal DNA-binding module, while the N-terminal finger stabilizes the interaction and mediates cofactor recruitment. Between and flanking the fingers lie transactivation domains that engage partners including [[NKX2-5]], [[TBX5]], and [[MEF2C]] within a core cardiac transcriptional network. GATA4 also harbors regulatory acetylation and phosphorylation sites — including regulatory serines — whose modification state controls its activity. GATA4 binds the consensus motif (A/T)GATA(A/G) via its two zinc fingers and activates or represses target genes involved in differentiation, stress response, and survival.

## Physiological Role

During embryogenesis GATA4 is required for formation of the primitive heart tube, endocardial cushion development, and ventricular septation. In the adult heart, GATA4 maintains expression of contractile and ion-handling genes and orchestrates adaptation to stress. Hypertrophic stimuli — [[Angiotensin II]], [[Endothelin-1]], phenylephrine, and mechanical stretch — drive GATA4 acetylation and nuclear accumulation, activating fetal gene reprogramming ([[ANP]], [[BNP]], β-[[Myosin Heavy Chain]]) that underlies pathological [[Cardiac Hypertrophy]]. GATA4 is stabilized under cellular stress and is a nodal mediator linking tissue injury and inflammatory gene programs.

## SIRT7 Regulation

[[SIRT7]] directly deacetylates GATA4, ameliorating stress-induced [[Cardiac Hypertrophy]]. SIRT7-mediated deacetylation suppresses GATA4 transcriptional activity at hypertrophic gene promoters, including those for [[ANP]], [[BNP]], and β-[[Myosin Heavy Chain]] (β-MHC). This anti-hypertrophic action positions SIRT7 as a cardioprotective sirtuin that counteracts pathological cardiac remodeling. SIRT7-deficient mice develop exaggerated hypertrophy and heart failure after pressure overload, underscoring the physiological importance of this axis.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review of the senescent epigenetic landscape lists GATA4 among the transcription factors and upstream regulators — together with DNA-damage signaling, [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]], [[p38 MAPK]], [[mTOR]], [[JAK|JAK-STAT]], and [[Notch]] — that control [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] (SASP) expression. GATA4 contributes to the NF-κB- and C/EBPβ-driven inflammatory program, helping maintain expression of SASP cytokines and chemokines. Its induction under genotoxic and oncogenic stress positions GATA4 as a stress-integrating transcription factor that converts DNA-damage response ([[DNA Damage Response|DDR]]) and inflammatory signaling into sustained SASP output, complementing the master regulator [[NF-κB]].

## Mechanistic Details

- GATA4 integrates stress signals (DNA damage, oxidative stress) with inflammatory transcription factors to enforce SASP.
- It cooperates with C/EBPβ and NF-κB at SASP enhancers, reinforcing the feed-forward inflammatory loop.
- GATA4 stabilization/accumulation in senescent cells parallels the enhancer activation ([[H3K27ac]] gain) at SASP loci described in the review.
- GATA4 can be degraded by autophagy (e.g., upon nutrient stress via FOXO/autophagy) and stabilized by stress, making its protein level a sensor of cellular damage.

## Therapeutic & Clinical Relevance

Dysregulated GATA4 activity contributes to cardiomyopathy, congenital heart defects (loss-of-function variants), and heart failure. The SIRT7–GATA4 axis is a candidate therapeutic target: strategies that preserve or enhance SIRT7 deacetylase activity may limit maladaptive remodeling. In the senescence setting, because GATA4 sits upstream of SASP, its modulation is a candidate [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] approach — strategies that lower GATA4 stabilization (e.g., restoring autophagic clearance) may dampen SASP without eliminating senescent cells. GATA4 is also a biomarker of senescence in several tissues. [[Resveratrol]] and other sirtuin-modulating compounds may indirectly affect GATA4 acetylation through [[NAD+]]-dependent sirtuin activation, though SIRT7-specific pharmacology remains nascent.
