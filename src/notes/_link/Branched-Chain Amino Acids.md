# Branched-Chain Amino Acids

The **branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs)** are the three [[Essential Amino Acids]] with aliphatic side-chains containing a branch: [[Leucine]], [[Isoleucine]], and [[Valine]]. Together they constitute roughly 35% of the essential amino acids in muscle proteins and ~20% of total dietary protein. Unlike most amino acids, BCAAs are catabolized primarily in skeletal muscle rather than the liver, making them uniquely responsive to dietary intake and central to the regulation of [[mTORC1]] and systemic metabolism.

## Overview

High circulating BCAA levels are associated with [[Obesity]], [[Insulin Resistance]], and mortality in humans, and BCAA levels predict incident type 2 diabetes. High-BCAA diets promote obesity, insulin resistance, inflammation, and decreased lifespan in rodents. Conversely, dietary restriction of BCAAs promotes metabolic health, reduces [[Frailty]], and extends lifespan, recapitulating many of the benefits of [[Protein Restriction]]. BCAA restriction reduces [[mTORC1]] signaling, induces *Fgf21*, and reduces hepatic [[Cellular Senescence]].

## Metabolism

BCAAs share a common catabolic pathway. The first step, transamination by [[BCAT2|branched-chain amino acid transaminase]], produces branched-chain α-ketoacids, which are then decarboxylated by the branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase (BCKDH) complex. The end-products feed distinct metabolic pools:

- [[Leucine]] → acetyl-CoA and acetoacetate (ketogenic)
- [[Isoleucine]] → acetyl-CoA and succinyl-CoA (ketogenic + glucogenic)
- [[Valine]] → succinyl-CoA, entering the [[TCA cycle]] to produce ATP (glucogenic)

## Role in Aging and Metabolism

BCAAs act as potent nutrient signals. [[Leucine]] is a strong [[mTORC1]] activator that stimulates skeletal muscle anabolism; leucine signals to mTORC1 in specific neurons by binding to and activating a specific voltage-gated calcium channel (Cav3.1). Each individual BCAA plays distinct roles in aging — [[Isoleucine]] restriction is exceptionally powerful, while [[Valine]] and [[Leucine]] have more context-dependent effects.

> [!tip] Individual BCAAs Differ
> While BCAAs are often considered as a group, each individual BCAA plays distinct roles in aging. See the separate notes for [[Leucine]], [[Isoleucine]], and [[Valine]].

Human studies show BCAA restriction improves insulin sensitivity, glucose/insulin homeostasis, and increases circulating [[FGF21]]. BCAA restriction also improves [[Alzheimer's Disease]] pathology and cognition in mouse models.
