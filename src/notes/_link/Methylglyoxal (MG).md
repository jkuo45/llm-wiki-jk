# Methylglyoxal (MG)

**Methylglyoxal (MG)** is a reactive α-oxoaldehyde produced mainly as a side product of [[Glycolysis]] (from triose phosphates). It is detoxified by the [[Glyoxalase System|glyoxalase system]] (GLO1/GLO2) using [[Glutathione]]. When unchecked, MG glycates proteins, lipids, and DNA to form [[Advanced Glycation End-products (AGEs)|advanced glycation end-products (AGEs)]], contributing to [[Diabetes]] complications and [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Anticancer Properties

Paradoxically, MG is selectively cytotoxic to many [[Cancer]] cells, which often have elevated glycolysis and impaired detoxification. As detailed in [[Creatine in Health and Disease]]:

- Patra et al. showed that the efficacy of MG as an [[Anticancer Medication|anticancer agent]] is significantly augmented in the presence of [[Creatine]], and combined [[Creatine]] + MG + [[Ascorbic Acid|ascorbic acid]] eliminated visible [[Tumor Growth|tumor growth]].
- Pal et al. confirmed MG efficacy improved with co-administration of creatine and ascorbic acid in [[Sarcoma]] models (in vitro and in vivo).
- The mechanism involves depletion of cellular energy/[[Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)|ATP]] and disruption of [[Cancer]] cell metabolism, with creatine enhancing the effect.

Thus [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine supplementation]] has been proposed as an [[Adjunctive Anticancer Therapeutic Intervention|adjunctive anticancer intervention]] alongside MG.

## Relevance

Connects [[Glycolysis]], [[Cancer]] metabolism, and the therapeutic potential of [[Creatine]] described in [[Creatine in Health and Disease]].
