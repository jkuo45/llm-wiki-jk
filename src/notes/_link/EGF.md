# EGF

**Epidermal growth factor (EGF)** is a 53-residue, ~6 kDa secreted peptide discovered in 1961 by Stanley Cohen in mouse submandibular gland during nerve growth factor work — the discovery that, with its receptor, founded the receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) field. It is the prototypic member of the seven ligands that activate the EGFR/ErbB family, and the founding member of the structurally conserved **EGF module**.

## Structure and Processing

The functional core is a ~40-residue **EGF module** of six cysteines in fixed spacing (CX7 CX4-5 CX10-13 CXCX8 C) forming three disulphide bridges; a single "hinge" residue between loops B and C lets the two segments move. The mature peptide is released from a much larger precursor (>1200 residues) by **ADAM** metalloprotease ectodomain shedding, chiefly [[ADAM17]] and [[ADAM10]].

> [!info] Four signalling modes from one precursor
> Because shedding is regulated rather than constitutive, EGF-family ligands signal in four modes: **endocrine**, **paracrine**, **autocrine**, and **juxtacrine** (uncleaved membrane-anchored precursor contacting an adjacent cell's receptor). Ligand identity partly determines the mode — uncleavable HB-EGF in mice causes severe heart failure and valve enlargement, resembling full knockout.

EGF is abundant in saliva, milk, bile, urine and seminal fluid (50–500 ng/mL) and low in plasma. It is required for normal morphogenesis of teeth, skin, gastrointestinal tract, brain and reproductive tract, and is the basis of the classic sialadenectomy and bulbectomy rodent experiments.

## Mechanism of Action

EGF binds the extracellular domain of [[EGFR]] (ErbB1/HER1), stabilising a conformation that exposes the receptor's dimerisation arm. Ligand-induced homo- or heterodimerisation (with [[HER2]], ErbB3 or ErbB4) produces an **asymmetric kinase dimer** in which the activator kinase allosterically stimulates the receiver kinase, which then trans-phosphorylates C-terminal tyrosines. Those phosphotyrosines dock adaptor and effector proteins, launching [[MAPK Signaling]], [[PI3K-Akt Signaling]] and JAK-STAT output.

> [!important] Ligand identity sets receptor fate
> EGF is not interchangeable with its siblings. Because ligand-receptor complexes sort differently in endosomes — EGF-bound EGFR is degraded, TGF-alpha-bound EGFR is recycled — the same receptor yields different signalling amplitudes depending on which ligand bound it. This is the basis of "functional selectivity" among EGFR ligands.

Upregulated EGF signalling is implicated in [[Lung Cancer]], [[Colorectal Cancer]], [[Glioblastoma]] and [[Pancreatic Cancer]], and also in non-malignant disease including psoriasis, Alzheimer's disease and schizophrenia.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Ligand abundance and receptor kinase activity are separable, giving two anti-EGF strategies: **ligand blockade** and **receptor blockade**. Antibodies such as [[Cetuximab]] compete at the ectodomain, while small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors — [[erlotinib]], gefitinib — occupy the intracellular ATP site. EGFR overexpression is also exploited indirectly: [[miR-7]] restrains proliferation chiefly by repressing EGFR translation. EGF itself has a long clinical history as a wound-healing agent, and urinary EGF has been reported as an independent risk factor for chronic kidney disease progression.

> [!warning] EGF in senescence
> EGF is also a component of the [[SASP]]. Senescent fibroblasts secrete EGF alongside [[VEGF]], [[Amphiregulin]] and HGF, and the SASP is uniformly **pro-angiogenic** — angiostatic factors are conspicuously absent. This is one route by which senescent cells, though no longer dividing, still drive tumour vascularisation.

## Connections
- [[EGFR]] — EGF's cognate receptor and the founding member of the ErbB RTK family; ligand binding converts the receptor from an autoinhibited monomer into an active asymmetric kinase dimer.
- [[Amphiregulin]] — AREG is the other EGF-module ligand in the SASP and the best-characterised EGFR ligand in senescence; it activates the same receptor but with distinct sorting and lower affinity.
- [[ADAM17]] — The metalloprotease performing most ectodomain shedding of EGF-family precursors, so it indirectly controls how much soluble growth factor is available.
- [[Receptor Tyrosine Kinases]] — The EGF/EGFR pair is the archetype that established the whole RTK signalling paradigm; most RTK notes generalise from it.
- [[Cortactin]] — Cortactin's own link list includes EGF signalling among the upstream inputs converging on actin remodelling, alongside VEGF and PAK1.
- [[SASP]] — Senescent cells secrete EGF as part of their secretory phenotype, which is how quiescent cells promote angiogenesis and paracrine proliferation in neighbours.
- [[ADAM10]] — The second major ectodomain sheddase; loss of either protease phenocopies partial ligand deficiency in mice.
