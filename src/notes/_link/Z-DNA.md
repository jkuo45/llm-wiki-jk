# Z-DNA

## Overview

Z-DNA is a noncanonical, left-handed conformation of double-stranded DNA that contrasts with the right-handed B-DNA helix. It forms transiently at sequences enriched in alternating purine–pyrimidine repeats and at sites of active transcription or topological stress. Although historically viewed as a structural curiosity, Z-DNA has emerged as a biologically relevant recognition motif: the DNA sensor [[cGAS]] and the Z-nucleic-acid sensor [[ZBP1]] both bind Z-DNA (and Z-RNA), linking this conformation to innate immune signaling and cellular stress responses within the [[Nucleus]] and [[Cytosol]].

## cGAS Recognition of Z-DNA

[[cGAS]] can recognize [[Z-DNA]] (and Z-RNA) structures at chromatin, a function spatially and mechanistically separable from its canonical role of synthesizing [[cGAMP]] from cytosolic dsDNA. Structural and mutational studies show that cGAS Z-DNA–binding mutants decouple chromatin binding from DNA-sensing activity: the protein still localizes to chromatin but fails to mount a productive [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] response. This dissociation reveals that chromatin tethering and enzyme activation are governed by distinct molecular interfaces, underscoring the importance of conformation-specific recognition in regulating nuclear cGAS. This noncanonical Z-DNA interaction is central to understanding how cGAS exerts STING-independent functions in [[Genomic Instability|genome stability]] and transcription.

## ZBP1 and Z-Nucleic-Acid Sensing

[[ZBP1]] (also known as DAI) is the prototypical Z-nucleic-acid sensor and detects Z-DNA/Z-RNA to trigger cell death programs, including PANoptosis, frequently through coordination with [[STING]] and MAVS signaling. In the context of viral encephalitis (e.g., HSV-1), ZBP1-mediated sensing drives [[RIPK3]]-dependent cell death. Together with cGAS, ZBP1 exemplifies how non-B DNA structures serve as danger signals that recruit innate immune effectors, contributing to [[Inflammation]] and [[Neuroinflammation]] when dysregulated.
