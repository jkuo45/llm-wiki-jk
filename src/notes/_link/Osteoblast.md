# Osteoblast

**Osteoblast** is the bone-forming cell of the [[Extracellular Matrix|extracellular matrix]]: a mesenchymal-derived cell that secretes type I collagen-rich osteoid and controls its mineralisation, and that is embedded in that matrix as a bone-lining or osteocyte precursor. Osteoblast number and activity are the limiting factor in bone mass, which is why osteoblast failure underlies osteoporosis even when osteoclast activity is normal.

## Overview

Osteoblasts arise from mesenchymal stem cells under the sequential control of [[RUNX2]] (master transcription factor of osteoblast differentiation) and osterix (SP7), downstream of which lie osteocalcin, bone sialoprotein and alkaline phosphatase. They are short-lived in the osteoid surface, and most either become [[Bone Marrow|bone-lining]] cells, differentiate into osteocytes (the cells embedded in mineralised matrix), or undergo [[Apoptosis]].

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Wnt/β-catenin signalling.** [[Wnt-beta-catenin]] signalling through [[β-Catenin]] stabilises β-catenin, which enters the nucleus with TCF/LEF factors and drives RUNX2 expression; conditional deletion of β-catenin or of the Wnt receptor in osteoblasts causes severe low bone mass. LRP5, sclerostin ([[Sclerostin]]), and the Wnt antagonist DKK1 are major modulators of this axis.
- **Osteoblastogenesis versus osteoclastogenesis.** Bone resorption and formation are coupled: osteoblasts express [[RANKL]], which engages [[RANK]] on [[Osteoclast]] precursors and drives their differentiation; they also secrete osteoprotegerin as a decoy RANKL receptor that restrains osteoclastogenesis. This RANKL/OPG balance, and the "osteoblast–osteoclast coupling" it creates, is the control point of bone remodelling.
- **Sclerostin and the endocrine axis.** Sclerostin, produced by osteocytes, inhibits Wnt signalling; the antiresorptive antibody romosozumab neutralises it to increase bone formation. Endocrine signals — oestrogen ([[Estrogen]]), growth hormone, and adiponectin ([[Adiponectin]], which acts on osteoblasts and promotes bone formation) — also act on this cell.
- **Matrix production and mineralisation.** Osteoblasts deposit osteoid (type I [[Collagen]] and [[COL1A1]]-rich matrix), express alkaline phosphatase, and promote hydroxyapatite deposition; they also control mineralisation through inhibitors such as sclerostin and osteopontin. Matrix metalloproteinases ([[Matrix Metalloproteinase]]) are needed to relieve the mechanical constraint before mineralisation.
- **Intercellular signalling.** Osteocytes and osteoblasts signal to the osteoclast via RANKL, M-CSF and semaphorin 3A, and to distant tissues via osteocalcin, which links bone metabolism to glucose handling and to the [[Adiponectin]]/insulin axis.

## Physiological Function

- Skeletal growth, cortical and trabecular modelling during development, and lifelong bone turnover (≈5–10% of the adult skeleton per year).
- Fracture repair: osteoblast proliferation and matrix deposition form the hard callus.
- Calcium and phosphate homeostasis as a reservoir, and endocrine output via osteocalcin and FGF23.
- Maintenance of bone quality depends on osteoblast survival and on the balance with osteoclast resorption; when resorption outpaces formation, as in post-menopausal or senile bone loss, microstructural deterioration follows.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Osteoporosis.** Reduced osteoblast number and function, plus increased osteoclast survival, underlie senile and post-menopausal [[Osteoporosis]]. Ageing reduces osteoblast responsiveness to mechanical loading and to anabolic signals, and osteoblast-lineage cells undergo [[Cellular Senescence]].
- **The osteoblast senescence axis.** Senescent osteoblasts and osteocytes accumulate with age; they secrete a pro-inflammatory, pro-resorptive SASP (see [[SASP]]) and produce less matrix, so senescent-cell accumulation in bone actively drives bone loss. This links bone fragility to the general senescence programme and to [[Inflammaging]] — and is why senolytic strategies have been explored for osteoporosis.
- **Osteogenesis imperfecta and other matrix disorders.** Defective type I collagen synthesis impairs osteoblast function and matrix quality.
- **Skeletal metastasis and cancer bone disease.** Tumour cells disrupt the RANKL/OPG balance and co-opt osteoblasts to build a permissive niche; prostate and breast cancer metastases preferentially seed sclerotic or osteolytic lesions depending on the tumour type.
- **Sclerosteosis and high bone mass.** Loss of sclerostin function in sclerosteosis and van Buchem disease produces extreme bone overgrowth via unrestrained Wnt signalling in osteoblasts.
- **Therapeutic targets.** Teriparatide (PTH analogue), romosozumab, and the anabolic agents act on osteoblast number and activity; denosumab targets the RANKL axis on the resorption side.
