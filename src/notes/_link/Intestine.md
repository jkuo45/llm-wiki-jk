# Intestine

The **intestine** is the terminal segment of the [[Gastrointestinal Tract]] and
the largest immunological organ in the body. It performs four functions that
are usually collapsed into "digestion": mechanical transit, enzymatic
absorption, endocrine secretion, and immune surveillance of a lumen that
contains roughly 10¹⁴–10¹⁵ organisms.

> [!info] Scale
> The small intestine is ~6.5 m in cadavers (3–5 m in vivo), subdivided into
> duodenum (~25 cm), jejunum (~2.5 m), and ileum (~3 m). Its mucosa, folded
> into plicae, villi, and microvilli, presents ~30 m² of absorptive surface.
> The large intestine is ~1.5 m and reclaims water and electrolytes.

## Structure and regional specialisation

- **Duodenum** — receives chyme plus pancreatic enzymes and bile; contains
  Brunner's glands, whose bicarbonate-rich alkaline secretion neutralises gastric
  acid. Iron and calcium are preferentially absorbed here.
- **Jejunum** — the major site of absorption of sugars, amino acids, and fatty
  acids; has the tallest villi and the highest blood flow per unit length.
- **Ileum** — specialised for vitamin B12–intrinsic factor uptake via cubilin
  receptors and for bile salt reclamation via the ileal sodium-bile acid
  co-transporter. Loss of terminal ileum causes B12 deficiency and bile salt
  diarrhoea.
- **Large intestine** — recovers water and electrolytes, houses the resident
  microbiota, and compacts and stores faeces.

Villus epithelium is composed of absorptive enterocytes, mucus-secreting goblet
cells, enteroendocrine cells, and [[Dendritic cells]] sampling the lumen; the
epithelium turns over every 3–5 days from [[Intestinal Stem Cell]]s in the
crypts. Below the epithelium, Peyer's patches house IgA-producing plasma cells.

## The microbiome

The gut lumen contains the densest and most metabolically important microbial
community in the body, dominated by [[Firmicutes]] and [[Bacteroidetes]] phyla,
with [[Akkermansia]] and others at lower abundance. Fermentation of
indigestible fibre produces [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] — [[Butyrate]],
[[Propionate]], and acetate — which are not merely waste products:

- [[Butyrate]] is the primary fuel for colonocytes and is a strong HDAC
  inhibitor, connecting gut microbial metabolism to the vault's
  [[Epigenetics]] and [[Sirtuins]] themes.
- SCFAs signal through GPR41/GPR43 (FFAR3/FFAR2) on enteroendocrine and immune
  cells, modulating [[Gut-Brain Axis]] signalling and [[Neuroinflammation]].
- Disruption of the community — [[Dysbiosis]] — is associated with
  [[Inflammatory Diseases]], metabolic syndrome, and [[Colon Cancer]].

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The microbiome literature is unusually prone to over-claim. Associations
> between dysbiosis and disease are well replicated; the claim that a specific
> species causes a specific disease generally is not. Fecal microbiota
> transplant is standard only for recurrent *Clostridioides difficile*
> colitis; its use for inflammatory and metabolic conditions remains
> experimental.

## Barrier function and immune signalling

The [[Intestinal Barrier]] is a single epithelial layer held together by tight
junctions, with mucus layers above and a dense lamina propria of
[[Macrophages]], [[Dendritic cells]], [[Regulatory T cells]], and IgA plasma
cells below. Leaky gut — increased paracellular permeability — is a shared
feature of [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]], [[Alcoholism]], and
[[NAFLD]], and is a plausible route by which endotoxin drives
[[Inflammaging]].

Barrier disruption is bidirectional: inflammation loosens the barrier, and a
loose barrier drives inflammation. In disease states such as sepsis this loop
is self-sustaining, and it is one of the few places in physiology where a
structural leak and an immunological state are causally inseparable.

## The intestine as a nutrient-sensing and endocrine organ

Enteroendocrine cells lining the gut constitute the largest diffuse endocrine
system in the body. They secrete:

- **GLP-1** and **GIC/PYY** — satiety signalling, and the basis of
  [[Glucagon]]-like peptide-1 receptor agonists.
- **CCK** — digestive enzyme and gallbladder control.
- **Ghrelin** — preprandial hunger signalling, from X/A-like cells.
- **5-HT** — a majority of the body's serotonin is produced by enterochromaffin
  cells, and the gut is the main source of peripheral serotonin.

This makes the intestine a nutrient sensor upstream of the
[[Insulin]]/[[Insulin Receptor]] axis rather than a passive tube.

## Parasitology

The intestine is the residence of the soil-transmitted helminths. In this
vault's existing notes:

- **[[Ascaris]] / [[Ascariasis]]** — *Ascaris lumbricoides* is the largest
  common intestinal nematode of humans. Transmission is by ingestion of
  embryonated eggs from soil. Eggs are excreted in faeces; infective larvae
  hatch in the small intestine and undergo hepatic–pulmonary migration before
  being swallowed back into the intestine, where they mature. Adult worms can
  obstruct the biliary or pancreatic duct. Diagnosis is by eggs on faecal
  microscopy; treatment is [[Albendazole]] or [[Mebendazole]].
- **[[Trichuris]] / [[Trichuriasis]]** — *Trichuris trichiura* (whipworm)
  establishes anterior-end attachment in the caecum and colon, where the
  thin anterior end sits in the mucosa and the posterior end protrudes into
  the lumen. Heavy infection causes chronic dysentery, rectal prolapse, and
  anaemia; diagnosis is by characteristic barrel-with-a-bristle ova on stool
  microscopy, treatment with mebendazole or albendazole.

Both are classic [[Helminth]] examples, and both illustrate the same
ecology: a soil-transmitted stage, a luminal adult, and a mucosa-adjacent
lifestyle that sustains chronic blood loss and [[Inflammation]].

> [!info] Life-cycle lesson
> Ascariasis and trichuriasis diverge in exactly one clinically important way.
> *Ascaris* is an intestinal luminal worm with a larval migratory phase and is
> therefore an obstruction and hepatobiliary disease. *Trichuris* is
> mucosa-anchored throughout, with no tissue migration, and is therefore a
> bleeding and protein-losing disease. The morphology of the adult determines
  the pathology.

## Connections

- [[Ascariasis]] and [[Ascaris]] — The largest common intestinal nematode;
  adult worms occupy the small intestine and can obstruct the biliary tree.
  Both stub inbound links are retained in the Documents section.

- [[Trichuriasis]] and [[Trichuris]] — Caecal/colonic whipworm infection with
  mucosal attachment; causes chronic blood loss, anaemia, and rectal prolapse in
  heavy infection. Both stub inbound links are retained.

- [[Gut Microbiome]] — The resident community is the largest metabolic
  contribution to the intestinal lumen, producing SCFAs and competing with
  pathogens. Dysbiosis links the intestine to systemic inflammatory and
  metabolic disease.

- [[Intestinal Barrier]] — Tight junctions, mucus, and the immune lamina
  propria form the barrier whose failure is central to inflammatory bowel
  disease and endotoxin-driven systemic inflammation.

- [[Intestinal Stem Cell]] — Crypt stem cells replenish the villus epithelium
  every few days, and are themselves maintained by Wnt and Notch signalling.
  They are also a model system for regeneration and for
  [[Cellular Reprogramming]].

- [[Autophagy]] — Intestinal epithelium is exposed to constant mechanical and
  microbial stress; basal autophagy in Paneth cells and enterocytes is
  required for barrier integrity, and Paneth cell granule morphology is a
  standard readout of autophagy function in mice.

- [[Gastrointestinal Tract]] — The parent organ system; the intestine is its
  absorptive and immunological terminal segment.

- [[Gut-Brain Axis]] — Vagal and enteroendocrine routes plus microbial SCFAs
  connect intestinal state to brain function, relevant to the vault's
  neurodegeneration notes.

- [[Helminth]] — The broader category of parasitic worms; the two documented
  intestinal infections here are soil-transmitted nematodes.

- [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]] — A barrier and immune-dysregulation disease
  of the intestine, and the clearest clinical expression of the
  barrier–immunity loop described above.
