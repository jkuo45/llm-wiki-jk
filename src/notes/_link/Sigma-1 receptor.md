# Sigma-1 receptor

**Sigma-1 receptor (S1R)**, encoded by **SIGMAR1**, is an endoplasmic-reticulum-resident chaperone and ligand-operated receptor implicated in neuroprotection, calcium signaling, and lipid metabolism. In the ferroptosis context, S1R is abundantly expressed in hepatocytes, and its inhibition promotes ferroptosis in [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] (HCC) cells.

## Structure & Biochemistry

S1R is a 223-aa transmembrane protein in the ER/mitochondria-associated membrane that interacts with the chaperone BiP/GRP78 and modulates ER stress, calcium homeostasis, and lipid droplet dynamics. It binds diverse ligands (neurosteroids, cocaine, various drugs).

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review notes that S1R is abundantly expressed in hepatocytes and that inhibition of this receptor promotes ferroptosis in HCC cells (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / HCC"). While the precise downstream route is under study, S1R's roles in lipid metabolism and ER stress position it as a suppressor of the lipid-peroxidation cascade; removing its activity lowers the threshold for [[GPX4]] failure and [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

> [!info] S1R as an HCC ferroptosis brake
> Because S1R is enriched in liver, it is a liver-selective vulnerability: S1R inhibitors could trigger ferroptosis preferentially in HCC while sparing other tissues—an attractive therapeutic window.

## Clinical Relevance

S1R modulators (agonists/antagonists) are investigated in neurodegeneration and psychiatry. Repurposing S1R antagonists to induce ferroptosis in HCC is a plausible oncology strategy, potentially combinable with [[Sorafenib]] or [[GPX4]] inhibitors.
