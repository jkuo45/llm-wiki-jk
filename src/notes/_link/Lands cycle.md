# Lands cycle

The **Lands cycle** (also known as the deacylation-reacylation cycle) is a phospholipid remodeling pathway in which free fatty acids are exchanged into the *sn-2* position of membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]]. It serves as the mechanistic link between [[Acid ceramidase]] activity and the pro-[[Ferroptosis|ferroptotic]] membrane lipid profile observed in [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]].

## Role in the ACase-Ferroptosis Axis

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Acid_ceramidase_modulates_the_lipid_profile_and_ex|Acid ceramidase modulates the lipid profile… (Soriano-Castell et al., 2026)]]
> [[Acid ceramidase]] over-expression in replicatively [[Senescent Cells|senescent]] cells breaks down [[Ceramide|ceramide]], releasing free saturated (SFA) and monounsaturated (MUFA) fatty acids. These enter the Lands cycle and are exchanged into the *sn-2* position of membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]], enriching them with [[PUFA|polyunsaturated fatty acids]] — the preferred substrates for [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

## Connections

- [[Acid ceramidase]] — ACase-liberated free fatty acids enter the Lands cycle for membrane PL-PUFA enrichment
- [[Phospholipid]] — The Lands cycle remodels membrane PLs at the sn-2 position
- [[PUFA]] — PUFAs are the species preferentially incorporated into PLs via Lands cycle exchange
- [[Ceramide]] — Ceramide catabolism by ACase provides the free fatty acid substrate pool
- [[Ferroptosis]] — The Lands cycle links ceramide metabolism to the lipid peroxidation substrate pool
- [[ACSL4]] — Activates fatty acids for incorporation into PLs via the Lands cycle
