# Ribosomal Protein S6

[[Ribosomal Protein S6]] (rpS6, RPS6) is a constituent of the 40S ribosomal subunit. Its phosphorylation at multiple serine residues by [[S6K1]] (itself activated by [[mTORC1]]) is the classic readout of mTORC1 signaling activity.

## Role as an mTOR Biomarker

Phosphorylated ribosomal protein S6 (phospho-S6) is widely used as a surrogate marker of [[mTORC1]] activation in both resected tissues and preclinical models—for example, to confirm mTOR pathway hyperactivation in [[TSC]] and other [[mTORopathies]] ([Meikle et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18495876/); [Crino, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25833943/)).

> [!warning] Inconsistent clinical biomarker
> Although phospho-S6 is a robust research biomarker, it has not proven consistent as a clinical biomarker in humans, complicating the correlation between mTOR inhibition depth, drug blood levels, and therapeutic effect—especially in the heterogeneous context of [[Aging]] ([Roark & Iffland, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12226543/)).

Standardizing phospho-S6 and other pharmacodynamic measurements is considered essential for rigorous translation of rapamycin from models to human longevity trials.
