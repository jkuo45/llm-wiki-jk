# Endostatin

[[Endostatin]] is a 20 kDa [[Collagen]] XVIII-derived fragment generated by proteolytic cleavage of the non-collagenous C-terminal (NC1) domain. First identified as a potent endogenous inhibitor of angiogenesis, it blocks endothelial proliferation, migration, and vessel formation, restraining tumor growth and metastasis.

## Structure & Origin

- Derived from the C-terminal NC1 domain of [[Collagen]] type XVIII (basement-membrane heparan-sulfate proteoglycan).
- Forms a compact globular fold stabilized by disulfide bonds; binds heparin, integrins, and matrix components.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Anti-angiogenesis**: Inhibits endothelial cell survival/sprouting by interfering with [[VEGF]] signaling, integrin α5β1, and [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|MMP]] activity at the endothelial [[Extracellular Matrix]] interface.
- **Tumor restraint**: Starves neoplasms of vascular supply, limiting expansion and [[Metastasis]]; synergizes with the body's endogenous anti-tumor programs.
- **Broader biology**: Modulates chondrogenesis and epithelial integrity; implicated in tissue remodeling during development and [[Aging]].

> [!info] Therapeutic precedent
> Recombinant endostatin (endostar) is approved in some regions as an anti-angiogenic cancer therapy, validating endogenous fragments as druggable angiogenesis inhibitors.

## Physiological Function

As a cleavage product of a ubiquitous basement-membrane collagen, endostatin provides endogenous braking on pathological neovascularization while participating in matrix homeostasis.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer**: A natural anti-angiogenic barrier; its levels and activity are relevant to tumor vascularity.
- **Ocular & inflammatory angiogenesis**: Relevant to retinopathy and chronic inflammation where aberrant vessel growth occurs.
