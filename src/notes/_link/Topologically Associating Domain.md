# Topologically Associating Domain

A topologically associating domain (TAD) is a contiguous genomic region — typically tens to hundreds of kilobases up to a few megabases — within which DNA sequences physically contact one another far more frequently than with sequences outside the domain. TADs are fundamental units of higher-order chromosome folding and constrain enhancer–promoter communication. In senescence, TAD-scale rewiring of the 3D genome reshapes [[Enhancer-Promoter Looping|enhancer-promoter looping]] to drive [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] expression, as reviewed in the npj Aging survey of the senescent epigenetic landscape (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2).

## Definition

TADs are operationally defined by Hi-C and related chromosome-conformation-capture methods as domains of elevated contact probability bounded by sharp drops in inter-domain contacts. Their borders are often anchored by CTCF/cohesin binding and by active or repressive histone marks. Within a TAD, looping brings enhancers into proximity with cognate promoters.

## Formation and Mechanism

TADs are formed and maintained by loop-extrusion mechanisms driven by the cohesin complex and anchored by CTCF. Architectural proteins such as [[HMGB1]]/[[HMGB2]] (HMG-box factors) and boundary elements help define domains. Senescence induces transcriptional-dependent cohesin repositioning that rewires loops (Olan et al., 2020, PMID 33177495), and the [[METTL3]]/METTL14 m6A complex redistributes genome-wide to promote senescence-associated contacts in an enzymatic-activity-independent manner (Liu et al., 2021, PMID 33712796).

## Role in SASP

The SASP is regulated by rewiring the enhancer–promoter (EP) network within TADs: hyper-connected enhancers to SASP promoters correspond to increased SASP transcription (Olan et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023, bioRxiv). Upon senescence entry, enhancers marked by [[H3K27ac]] undergo global remodeling at the TAD scale in replicative (RS), oncogene-induced (OIS) and therapy-induced (TIS) senescence across human and murine fibroblasts (Guan et al., 2020, PMID 33004516; Tasdemir et al., 2016, PMID 27193494). Boundary-defining HMGB1/2 and the METTL3–METTL14 complex collectively reshape topology and accessibility to drive SASP.

## Mechanistic Details

TAD-level organization determines which enhancers can reach which promoters. In senescence, de novo activation of enhancers from previously unmarked chromatin (Martinez-Zamudio et al., 2020, PMID 32439934) creates new intra-TAD loops that engage SASP genes. Disruption of this topology (e.g., HMGB2 loss, CTCF clustering) alters SASP output. TADs thus translate the epigenetic landscape into spatially restricted transcriptional programs.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

Because TAD rewiring is upstream of SASP, factors that organize TADs (METTL3/14, HMGB1/2, cohesin) are candidate [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] targets. Long-read sequencing of multi-way interactions promises deeper insight into TAD formation in senescence (see [[KDM4]], [[BRD4]], [[DOT1L]]).
