# Head and neck cancer

**Head and neck cancer (HNC)**, predominantly head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), arises in the mucosal surfaces of the upper aerodigestive tract. It is a tractable model for ferroptosis-based therapy: HNC cells are killed by [[GPX4]] inhibitors and by [[Artesunate]], and ferroptosis sensitivity is governed by the [[NRF2]]-ARE axis and mitochondrial iron regulators such as [[CISD2]].

## Ferroptosis Connection

The review reports that two GPX4 inhibitors, (1S,3R)-RSL3 and ML-162, induce ferroptosis in HNC cells to varying degrees. [[Artesunate]] also induces ferroptosis in HNC, with GSH depletion and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] accumulation; inhibiting the [[NRF2]]-ARE pathway abolishes resistance to ART and GPX4 inhibitors, reversing HNC ferroptosis resistance. Additionally, inhibition of the [[CISD2]] gene in HNC cells increases mitochondrial iron and lipid ROS accumulation, promoting ferroptosis (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Head and neck cancer").

> [!info] NRF2 as a resistance switch
> NRF2-ARE up-regulation protects HNC from ferroptosis inducers; its inhibition restores sensitivity—highlighting NRF2 as a biomarker and combinatorial target in HNC ferroptosis therapy.

## Molecular Modulators

- **CISD2:** Mitochondrial iron-sulfur protein; its loss raises mitochondrial Fe²⁺ and lipid ROS → ferroptosis.
- **NRF2:** Drives antioxidant (including [[GPX4]]-supporting) programs that confer resistance.
- **Artesunate / RSL3 / ML-162:** Inducers with demonstrated HNC activity.

## Clinical Relevance

Ferroptosis induction offers a route against HNC, including cisplatin-resistant disease. Combining GPX4 inhibitors or ART with NRF2 suppression (or CISD2 targeting) is a rational strategy. Links to [[Melanoma]] and other ferroptosis-sensitive tumors suggest shared therapeutic principles.
