# GLUT4

**Overview:** [[GLUT4]] (encoded by *SLC2A4*) is a facilitative glucose transporter expressed predominantly in [[Adipose Tissue]], cardiac and skeletal muscle, and heart. Unlike basal glucose transporters, it is largely sequestered in intracellular vesicles and mobilized in response to insulin.

**Mechanism of translocation:** Insulin binding to its receptor activates IRS/PI3K/Akt signaling, prompting Rab-GTPase-mediated trafficking of GLUT4 vesicles to the plasma membrane, where GLUT4 mediates stereospecific, insulin-dependent glucose uptake. [[AMPK]] activation (by [[Exercise]] or metformin) and muscle contraction independently stimulate GLUT4 translocation through AMPK/AS160 pathways.

**Physiological function:** By clearing glucose from the bloodstream after meals, GLUT4 is central to whole-body glucose homeostasis and glycogen/lipid synthesis.

**Clinical relevance:** Impaired GLUT4 translocation or expression—driven by [[Obesity]], ectopic lipid accumulation, and [[Inflammation]]—produces [[Insulin Resistance]] and type 2 diabetes. Enhancing GLUT4 trafficking via exercise, weight loss, and AMPK activation is a cornerstone of metabolic therapy. Genetic variation in *SLC2A4* influences diabetes risk and, indirectly, drug response.
