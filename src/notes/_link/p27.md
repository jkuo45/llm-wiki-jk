# p27

**p27** (UniProt P46527; **CDKN1B**; 198 residues; human locus 12p13.1) is the prototype member of the **Cip/Kip family** of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors, alongside p21 and p57. It was cloned in 1994 as "a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor and a potential mediator of extracellular antimitogenic signals" (Polyak et al., *Cell*).

> [!info]
> **p27 is dual-function, and that is not an oddity.** It is classically a *tumour suppressor* because it blocks G1→S progression. It is also a *driver of motility* when mislocalised to the cytoplasm, and it is simultaneously *assembled into* the very cyclin D–CDK4/6 complexes it is supposed to inhibit. Any accurate account of p27 has to hold all three at once.

## Structure and domains

p27 is unusually small and has no catalytic activity. What it has instead is an **N-terminal kinase-binding domain (approximately residues 1–91)**, which is the whole functional core, and a poorly conserved **C-terminal region (85–198)** that does not inhibit CDKs but determines p27's *stability*.

- Residues **28–79 alone** retain substantial cyclin A/CDK2 inhibitory activity — everything C-terminal is dispensable for the kinase block.
- The inhibitory action is **conformational**: p27 inserts the N-terminal helix into the cyclin–CDK active site, sterically excluding ATP, rather than competing with substrate.
- The C-terminal region carries the motifs that control fate — the Thr187 phospho-acceptor recognised by the SCF ubiquitin ligase, the Ser10 nuclear-export signal, the Thr198 site for [[14-3-3]] binding, and the Tyr74/Tyr88/Tyr89 tyrosine-phosphorylation sites used by [[Src]].

## Mechanism of action

p27 binds cyclin–CDK complexes and blocks them.

- **Cyclin E–CDK2** and **cyclin A–CDK2:** potent inhibition. p27 forms a ternary CCNE1/CDK2/CDKN1B complex; binding to cyclin E is inhibited once CDK2 phosphorylates Thr187.
- **Cyclin D–CDK4:** p27 acts here as an **assembly factor** rather than a pure inhibitor — it is required for stable formation of the cyclin D1/CDK4 complex, and phosphorylation at Tyr88/Tyr89 (by [[Src]]-family kinases) is *required* for CDK4 binding while having no effect on CDK2 binding. So the same post-translational modification converts p27 from an inhibitor into a chaperone.

Because CDK2 activity must fall below a threshold for a cell to become quiescent, and because p21 and p27 are the two dominant inhibitors setting that threshold, loss of either one alone permits, and loss of both synchronously forces, cell-cycle re-entry.

## Regulation: four independent levels

p27 protein abundance is a poor proxy for p27 activity, because p27 is regulated at four separate steps.

| Level | Mechanism |
| --- | --- |
| Transcription | Activated by FoxO proteins downstream of [[PI3K-Akt Signaling|PI3K–Akt]]; repressed by mitogens, and by c-Myc |
| Translation | Maximal in quiescence and early G1. The 5′ UTR carries an IRES bound by PTB (activating) and by HuR/ELAVL1 and ELAVL4 ([[HuR]]) (repressive) |
| Nuclear export | Ser10 phosphorylation allows CRM1/XPO1 binding and cytoplasmic export — the earliest step in G1 entry, and independent of proteolysis |
| Proteolysis | KPC ubiquitin ligase complex acts in early G1 on cytoplasmic p27; SCF-Skp2 (with Skp1, CKS1B) acts on nuclear p27 after Thr187 phosphorylation |

> [!warning]
> **14-3-3 sequestration.** Phosphorylation at **Thr198** (by AKT and RSK1) creates a 14-3-3 binding site. The resulting complex translocates p27 to the cytoplasm, where it is inactive, cannot inhibit CDK2, and is eventually degraded. Phosphorylated p27 is also ubiquitinated through a TRIM21-containing SCF(Skp2)-like complex, and separately routed to lysosomal degradation via interaction with SNX6 at endosomes.

A notable extra layer: in quiescent cells, hypophosphorylated [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] binds APC-C–CDH1 and targets **Skp2** for degradation, which stabilises p27 — a mechanism that couples the Rb and p27 brakes. Loss of APC-C–CDH1 activity in hepatocytes causes cell-cycle re-entry and liver failure.

## Clinical relevance

- **MEN4.** Heterozygous germline *CDKN1B* mutations cause multiple endocrine neoplasia type 4, a MEN-like syndrome overlapping MEN1 and MEN2 with primary hyperparathyroidism and pituitary adenomas (OMIM 610755). Testing for *CDKN1B* is recommended in MEN-suspected patients who are negative for *MEN1* and *RET*.
- **Prognosis.** Low nuclear p27 tracks with higher tumour grade, larger size and metastatic propensity across colorectal, breast, prostate, melanoma, glioma, ovarian and lung cancers. In ovarian cancer p27-negative tumours progressed in ~23 months versus ~85 months for p27-positive tumours; in colorectal carcinoma p27-negative patients had median survival of 69 versus 151 months.
- **Cytoplasmic p27 predicts metastasis specifically.** Roughly 70% of metastatic melanomas show cytoplasmic p27, with benign melanomas retaining nuclear localisation. Cytoplasmic p27 acts on the cytoskeleton — in hepatocellular carcinoma it colocalises with actin fibres and acts on Rac to promote migration; in breast cancer it reduces RhoA activity; RSK1-mediated Thr198 phosphorylation both mislocalises it and inhibits RhoA, reducing stress fibres and focal adhesions.
- **Treatment response.** Low p27 predicts poor response to platinum chemotherapy in non-small-cell lung cancer and to adjuvant chemotherapy in breast cancer.
- **Cochlear regeneration.** Disruption of *CDKN1B* releases cochlear hair cells from cell-cycle arrest and drives their proliferation in adult mice — mammalian hair cells normally do not regenerate, so this is a concrete target for hearing-loss therapy.
- **Prognostic use caveat.** All prognostic data for p27 are retrospective and no standardised scoring system exists.

## Connections

- [[p21 CIP1]] — the other dominant Cip/Kip inhibitor. Together they set the CDK2 activity threshold for quiescence, and combined loss is far more permissive than either alone.
- [[CDK2]] — the primary target. p27 blocks cyclin E/A–CDK2 by inserting its N-terminal helix into the ATP site; Thr198/Akt/RSK1 signalling disables it.
- [[Cyclin D]] — p27 is paradoxically required to assemble cyclin D–CDK4 complexes, and Tyr88/89 phosphorylation is what enables that switch from inhibitor to chaperone.
- [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] — hypophosphorylated Rb activates APC-C–CDH1, degrading Skp2 and thereby stabilising p27; the two brakes are wired together.
- [[Quiescence]] — p27 abundance peaks in quiescence and early G1 and falls as cells commit to S phase, which is why mVenus-p27 constructs are used as quiescence reporters.
- [[HuR]] — binds the p27 IRES to repress p27 translation; depleting HuR or ELAVL4 causes G1 arrest.
- [[PI3K-Akt Signaling]] — AKT phosphorylates p27 at Thr198 and drives 14-3-3-dependent cytoplasmic sequestration, one of the best-characterised mechanisms of oncogene-induced p27 inactivation.
- [[Metastasis]] — cytoplasmic, rather than lost, p27 is the metastasis-promoting form; this dual role is why p27 levels alone are an ambiguous prognostic marker.
