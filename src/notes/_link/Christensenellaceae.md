# Christensenellaceae

**Overview:** Christensenellaceae is a family of anaerobic bacteria within the phylum [[Firmicutes]], order Clostridiales. The type genus *Christensenella* was only formally described in 2012, yet has rapidly become a focus of microbiome–aging research because of its unusually strong host genetic control.

**Heritability:** Christensenellaceae is among the most heritable taxa in the human [[Gut Microbiome]]—its abundance is strongly correlated between twins and tracks host genotype more than most other microbes. This makes it a candidate "keystone" genus whose presence shapes community composition.

**Association with Lean Phenotype and Aging:** Higher Christensenellaceae abundance is consistently associated with a lower [[Body Mass Index|BMI]] and lean body composition, whereas reduced levels appear in obesity. In aging cohorts, Christensenellaceae is enriched in [[Centenarians]] and is considered a signature of [[Successful Aging]]. Its members co-occur with other beneficial taxa (e.g., *Akkermansia*, [[Bifidobacterium]]) and with [[Methanogens]] that together modulate short-chain-fatty-acid economy.

**Functional Role:** *Christensenella* species are saccharolytic and produce [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]], particularly acetate and butyrate, supporting colonocyte energy and anti-inflammatory tone. Experimental work shows that Christensenellaceae can reduce weight gain in germ-free mouse models, pointing to direct metabolic effects.

**Transplantation Studies:** Fecal microbiota transplantation and gnotobiotic studies indicate Christensenellaceae can restructure the wider community toward a healthier profile, raising interest in its use as a next-generation probiotic or "age-protective" taxon.
