# APE1

**APE1** (APEX1; also called **Redox factor-1, Ref-1**) is a bifunctional enzyme central to the cellular response to [[Oxidative Stress]]: it combines **DNA-repair** activity (Apurinic/Apyrimidinic Endonuclease 1, the major abasic-site endonuclease in [[Base Excision Repair]]) with a **redox-signaling** function in which it is regulated by [[Thioredoxin-1]] to activate transcription factors.

## DNA-Repair Function (Apurinic/Apyrimidinic Endonuclease)

- APE1 cleaves the DNA backbone 5′ to abasic (AP) sites generated by spontaneous base loss or by DNA glycosylases such as [[OGG1]]. This creates a 3′-OH primer that is processed by DNA polymerase β and ligated by LIG3/XRCC1, completing base-excision repair of oxidative lesions such as 8-oxo-dG.
- It also removes 3′-blocking groups (e.g., 3′-phosphoglycolate) left by AP-lyase activities, enabling repair completion.
- APE1 is therefore a rate-limiting hub in the repair of oxidative DNA damage; its loss sensitizes cells to oxidative and genotoxic stress.

## Redox-Signaling Function (Redox factor-1 / Ref-1)

- APE1/Ref-1 possesses a distinct, non-overlapping redox activity in which cysteine residues are maintained in the reduced state by **[[Thioredoxin-1]]** (Trx1).
- Reduced Ref-1 then activates a panel of redox-sensitive transcription factors by reducing critical cysteine residues — notably **AP-1** (Fos/Jun), **[[NF-κB]]** (p50/p65), [[p53]], and the glucocorticoid receptor — modulating inflammatory, survival, and stress-response gene programs.
- The Trx1 → Ref-1 → transcription-factor cascade is a canonical route by which the thioredoxin system converts redox status into transcriptional decisions.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer therapy**: APE1 is frequently overexpressed in cancers to support both DNA-damage tolerance and survival signaling, making it (and its partner Trx1) an anticancer target; APE1 redox inhibitors (e.g., E3330) are being explored.
- **Inflammation and neurological disease**: Ref-1-dependent NF-κB/AP-1 activation links oxidative stress to inflammatory gene expression in neurodegeneration and other age-related conditions.
- **Dysregulation** of APE1 activity is implicated in metabolic disorders, oxidative-stress pathologies, and cancer; pharmacological modulation is under investigation.
