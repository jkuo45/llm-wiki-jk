# Pregnane X Receptor

The **Pregnane X Receptor (PXR; NR1I2)** is a ligand-activated nuclear receptor classically known as the master regulator of xenobiotic and bile-acid detoxification (CYP3A4, transporters). It is expressed in intestine, liver, endothelium, and muscle, and has emerged as one of the two principal host receptors for microbiota-derived tryptophan metabolites — most prominently [[Indole-3-propionic acid|IPA]].

## Ligands and activation

- **Endogenous microbial ligand**: [[Indole-3-propionic acid]] activates PXR, upregulating the barrier gene **B3galt5** and reinforcing mucus/tight-junction defences.
- Like the [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor|AhR]], PXR translates microbial chemistry into host transcriptional programs, but with distinct gene sets (detoxification/transport vs. xenobiotic-stress and immune modulation).

## Physiological roles

- **Intestinal barrier**: PXR activation by IPA inhibits [[NF-κB|NF-κB]], suppresses CD4⁺/CD25⁺/Th1 responses, and maintains mucin expression — a mechanism relevant to inflammatory bowel disease.
- **Vascular**: PXR and IPA jointly regulate **endothelium-dependent vasodilation** (Venu et al., 2019).
- **Blood–brain barrier**: IPA's PXR-mediated signalling protects BBB integrity in models of oxidative stress and neonatal hypoxia–ischemia.
- **Bone**: IPA's AhR/PXR activation indirectly protects bone by modulating immune/inflammatory regulation of osteoclastogenesis.
- **Detoxification**: canonical induction of CYP enzymes and transporters; IPA's minimal hepatic metabolism is consistent with a favourable PXR-linked pharmacokinetic profile.
