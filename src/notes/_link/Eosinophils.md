# Eosinophils

Eosinophils are terminally differentiated [[Granulocyte|granulocytes]] of
myeloid lineage, comprising roughly 1-6% of circulating leukocytes. They are
named for the red-orange (eosinophilic, acidophilic) staining of their specific
granules with eosin, which reflects the overwhelmingly **cationic** protein
content of those granules.

## Development and trafficking

Production is driven almost entirely by [[IL-5]], with [[IL-3]] and GM-CSF
contributing. IL-5 is the target of the therapeutic monoclonal antibodies
mepolizumab, reslizumab, and benralizumab, and of [[Dexamethasone]]-based
therapies in eosinophilic disease - the existence of three approved biologics
is itself a measure of how central this axis is.

Roughly 80% of the body's eosinophils sit in the [[Bone Marrow]] at any moment;
the rest patrol the gastrointestinal tract, particularly the ileocolic junction.
Circulating half-life is short, on the order of 8-18 hours, and the cells are
programmed to apoptose within about five days unless IL-5 rescues them. Because
they are short-lived and terminally differentiated, the eosinophil count at any
moment reflects production, trafficking, and survival signals acting together.

Recruitment into tissue is driven by [[Eotaxin]] (CCL5-family chemokines CCL11,
CCL24, CCL26), which signal through [[CCR3]]. CCR3 is broad-spectrum and
responsive to several ligands, which is why the same receptor is used by
eosinophils, [[Basophils]], and [[Mast Cells]] alike.

## Granule contents and effector mechanisms

Specific granules are crystalloid, with a dense core that resists dissolution
during degranulation - a design that allows slow, sustained release rather than
the explosive release of a soluble granule.

| Component | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Major basic protein (MBP), ~14 kDa, the most abundant granule protein | Non-enzymatic membrane attack; disrupts helminth tegument and damages airway epithelium |
| Eosinophil cationic protein (ECP), ~18-21 kDa | Ribonuclease; induces necrosis (not apoptosis) of human bronchial epithelium |
| Eosinophil peroxidase (EPO) | Generates oxidant species, including [[Hypobromous Acid]] and hypochlorous acid, via H2O2 + halide |
| Eosinophil-derived neurotoxin (EDN) | RNase; cytotoxic to epithelium |
| Lysophospholipases, histaminase, arylsulfatase, collagenase | Matrix and lipid degradation |

Crystalloid also carries the lipid mediators - [[Leukotriene]] C4, PAF, and
prostaglandins - which are generated rather than merely stored.

> [!info] Hypobromous acid and iodide
> EPO uses the same peroxidase chemistry as [[Neutrophils|neutrophil]]
> myeloperoxidase but has a much higher preference for bromide over chloride.
> Because the human body concentrates iodide in the thyroid and secretes it,
> eosinophil-derived EPO can generate HOBr in vivo. This is the reason
> [[Hypobromous Acid]] appears in the vault's oxidative-damage chemistry and
> in the thyroid axis.

Two granule populations are not yet released: specific granules and
eosinophil granules are joined by a smaller pool of "secretory" vesicles and by
the granule-associated matrix, which acts as a slow-release store released only
by [[Piezo1]]-mediated mechanical forces in the lung.

## Protective and pathogenic roles

> [!info] Helminth defence
> Mouse knockouts lacking either MBP or EPO lose protective immunity against
> [[Strongyloides]] infection, and both are needed against
> [[Trichuris]]. The mechanism is not a direct killing reaction but coating of
> the parasite tegument followed by damage that exposes it to other effector
> mechanisms. This is direct evidence that granule proteins are not
> decorative.

The same arsenal is indiscriminate against host tissue. MBP and ECP are
cytotoxic to airway epithelium, EPO generates oxidative damage, and EDN
degrades epithelium - which is why eosinophilic asthma, eosinophilic
oesophagitis, and eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis are diseases of
granule-mediated tissue injury rather than of abnormal numbers alone.

Clinical convention has formalised this into **absolute eosinophil count**
thresholds: >500 cells/uL historically, >1500 cells/uL for hypereosinophilia
with organ involvement, and >10^9/L as the level at which tissue infiltration
is essentially certain.

## Bone, homeostasis, and the wider biology

Eosinophils are not only parasites-versus-allergy. They are sources of
[[TGF-beta1|TGF-β]], osteopontin, and periostin - the exact profibrotic and
osteogenic cytokines that drive [[Fibrosis]] and [[RANKL]]-dependent bone
removal. This is one of the clearest mechanistic explanations for why
persistent eosinophilia associates with both progressive fibrotic lung disease
and accelerated bone loss. Conversely, [[IL-13]], which eosinophils produce
abundantly, drives alternative macrophage activation and epithelial goblet cell
metaplasia in [[Th2]]-polarised settings.

Eosinophils also secrete cytokines (including IL-4, and in some settings
IL-13) that bias [[T Cell Receptor|T cell]] differentiation, and contribute to
local [[IgE]] production.

## Connections

- [[Eosinophil Peroxidase]] — the granule peroxidase that generates
  hypohalous acids; loss of it, alongside major basic protein, abolishes
  protective immunity against Strongyloides in mice.
- [[Hypobromous Acid]] — the halogenating oxidant EPO produces from
  hydrogen peroxide and bromide; a direct bridge from eosinophil degranulation
  to oxidative tissue damage and to thyroid-axis chemistry.
- [[Strongyloides]] and [[Strongyloidiasis]] — the best-characterised
  human helminth for which eosinophil granule proteins are demonstrably
  required; the basis for the claim that these cells are not merely correlates.
- [[IL-5]] — the master survival and differentiation cytokine; the target of
  every approved eosinophil-directed biologic.
- [[TGF-beta1]] and [[Fibrosis]] — eosinophils are a non-resident source of
  profibrotic cytokines, contributing to tissue remodelling in lung, gut and
  skin disease.
- [[RANKL]] and [[Osteoporosis]] — eosinophil-derived RANKL and cytokines
  promote osteoclast differentiation and bone resorption.
- [[Th2]] and [[IgE]] — the adaptive axis that mobilises and sustains
  eosinophils in allergic disease.
- [[Asthma]] — the archetypal eosinophil-driven disease; blood eosinophil
  count now predicts response to type-2-targeted biologics.
- [[IL-13]] — produced in bulk by eosinophils and the dominant effector cytokine
  in type-2 airway disease.
- [[Innate Immunity]] — eosinophils are a core innate effector against
  helminths while also operating in the adaptive [[Th2]] circuit.
- [[Neutrophils]] — the other granulocyte family; shares the peroxidase
  chemistry and, with mast cells and basophils, the granule-release
  architecture.
- [[Homeostasis]] — eosinophils contribute tissue remodelling and repair, not
  only defence; their profibrotic output is normal physiology in the wrong dose.
