## Overview

The mitochondrial free radical theory of aging (MFRTA), proposed by [[Denham Harman]] in 1972, refines the original [[Free Radical Theory of Aging]] by specifically implicating [[Mitochondria]] as both the primary source of damaging [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] and a key target of oxidative damage. The theory posits a positive feedback loop — a "vicious cycle" — in which ROS-mediated damage to [[mtDNA]] and mitochondrial components impairs respiratory chain function, leading to further ROS production and cascading cellular damage.

## Experimental Evidence

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease]]
> The MFRTA has been tested through comparative studies of ROS production across species, antioxidant supplementation, and genetic manipulation of antioxidant enzymes. Results have been mixed: while longer-lived species generally produce less ROS, antioxidant interventions have not consistently extended lifespan. The field now recognizes ROS as having both damaging and signaling roles, and the MFRTA has evolved into a broader paradigm of mitochondrial dysfunction as one of several interconnected [[Hallmarks of Aging|hallmarks of aging]].
