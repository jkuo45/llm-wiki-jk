# Stanniocalcin-1

**Stanniocalcin-1 (STC1)** is a secreted glycoprotein hormone originally identified in fish (where it regulates calcium/phosphate homeostasis) and conserved in mammals, where it is widely expressed and functions as a cytoprotective and anti-inflammatory factor. STC1 is induced by cellular stress, promotes mitochondrial uncoupling, and is known for its ability to suppress ROS-driven apoptosis and inflammation — activities that have generated interest in its therapeutic potential in ischemia-reperfusion injury, neurodegeneration, and kidney disease.

## Hormetic AMPK/SIRT3 axis

Stanniocalcin-1 orchestrates a hormetic [[AMPK]]/[[SIRT3]] axis that mitigates oxidative stress and apoptosis[388]. In diabetic renal injury, STC1 inhibits the expression of [[BNIP3|Bnip3]] through the AMPK/SIRT3 pathway, ameliorating kidney damage in diabetic murine models[389]. The "hormetic" description reflects STC1's pattern of effect: mild mitochondrial stress/uncoupling engages AMPK, which in turn supports SIRT3-dependent mitochondrial quality control, rather than a simple linear activation. Suppression of BNIP3 (a pro-apoptotic, mitophagy-linked BH3-only protein) is a downstream effector of the cytoprotective program.

## Clinical relevance

STC1 and its analogs are being explored as therapeutic agents for acute kidney injury, cardiac ischemia, and retinal degeneration. Its AMPK/SIRT3-dependent mechanism aligns STC1 with the growing class of SIRT3-activating biologicals for metabolic kidney disease.
