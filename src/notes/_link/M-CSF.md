# M-CSF

**Macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF, CSF-1)** is a dimeric, cysteine-knotted secreted glycoprotein of the CSF-1 family that is the principal trophic and survival factor for cells of the monocyte–macrophage lineage. It signals exclusively through the CSF-1 receptor (CSF1R, CD115), a receptor tyrosine kinase, and is required for monocyte egress from bone marrow, macrophage differentiation in every tissue, and the maintenance and phenotype of resident macrophages including microglia.

## Overview

M-CSF (also called CSF-1) is the founding member of the colony-stimulating factor family and the principal regulator of monocyte and macrophage biology. It acts through a single receptor, CSF1R, and controls monocyte egress from bone marrow, the differentiation of macrophages in every tissue, the survival of tissue-resident macrophages (including microglia), and the conditioning of macrophages for antigen presentation and for tumour-supporting phenotypes.

## Structure & Domains

- **Gene:** *CSF1* on chromosome 5q33; alternative splicing and proteolytic processing generate secreted, membrane-bound and biologically active intracellular forms.
- **Homodimer:** two identical subunits held by an interchain disulfide bond; each monomer has an N-terminal Ig-like domain, a cysteine-knot (PDGF-like) growth factor domain, and a C-terminal fibronectin type III domain.
- **CSF-1R:** a single-pass type III receptor tyrosine kinase with an extracellular immunoglobulin-like domain containing the CSF-1 binding surface, a transmembrane helix, and an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. The receptor is also activated by IL-34, and the two ligands share downstream signalling but differ in cell-type specificity.
- **Proteolytic processing:** the transmembrane form can be cleaved to release a soluble, highly active factor.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- CSF-1 binding induces CSF1R dimerization and autophosphorylation; the receptor is then internalized and trafficked to the lysosome, where signalling persists. Binding of CSF-1 to the lysosomal domain of CSF1R is a key regulatory step for the "lysosomal transcription axis" that couples receptor trafficking to gene expression.
- **Downstream:** PI3K–AKT (survival, metabolic reprogramming), RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK (proliferation and differentiation), and STAT3 (self-renewal and anti-apoptotic programmes). AKT and mTORC1 activation drive the macrophage shift toward a glycolytic, scavenger-receptor-high phenotype.
- **Transcriptional output:** induction of macrophage identity genes, including PU.1-dependent enhancers and the transcription factors that enforce the phagocytic programme.
- CSF-1 also acts on [[Monocytes]] and precursors in the marrow, and on dendritic cell progenitors at lower potency.

## Physiological Function

- **Monopoiesis:** CSF-1 promotes monocyte differentiation; mice lacking CSF-1R have near-complete absence of tissue macrophages.
- **Tissue-resident macrophages:** Kupffer cells of the liver ([[Kupffer Cell]]), alveolar macrophages ([[Alveolar Macrophages]]), splenic marginal zone and red pulp macrophages, microglia ([[Microglia]]), Langerhans cells and kidney-resident macrophages are all CSF-1R dependent for their maintenance.
- **Maternal and fetal development:** placental and embryonic macrophage proliferation.
- **Bone remodelling:** [[Osteoclast]] differentiation runs through the M-CSF–CSF1R axis, which also drives osteoclast survival and activity.
- **Immune regulation:** CSF-1 conditions macrophages for antigen presentation and for the resolution phase of [[Inflammation]]; it is one of the canonical macrophage-polarising cytokines alongside IL-4 and IL-13.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Tumour-associated macrophages:** many tumours overexpress CSF-1 and CSF1R, and CSF-1 recruits and polarises protumour macrophages in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]]. CSF-1 signalling supports tumour growth, angiogenesis, immune evasion and [[Metastasis]]; [[Tumor-Associated Macrophage|TAMs]] are a major component of the immunosuppressive niche.
- **Inflammatory disease:** excess CSF-1 contributes to the macrophage expansion of rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, atherosclerosis ([[Foam Cells]]) and [[Inflammaging]]; CSF1R is a marker of pathogenic inflammatory macrophages.
- **Bone disease:** excess M-CSF signalling contributes to osteoclast-driven bone loss, including in rheumatoid arthritis and postmenopausal osteoporosis.
- **Oncogenic signalling:** CSF1R is a recurrent mutation target in AML, and CSF1R translocation defines a subset of histiocytic neoplasms.
- **Therapeutics:** CSF1R inhibitors (pexidartinib, BLZ945, axatilimab and other monoclonal antibodies) are in clinical trials in cancer, and in inflammatory and fibrotic disease. Because CSF-1 cooperates with [[TNF-alpha]] and IL-6 in driving inflammatory macrophages, dual blockade is being explored.
