# PANoptosis

**PANoptosis** is an inflammatory programmed-cell-death paradigm in which the hallmarks of [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[Necroptosis]] occur together, coordinated by a multiprotein assembly called the **[[PANoptosome]]**. Its defining property is non-redundancy: blocking any single pathway does not prevent death, because the three arms are co-assembled rather than sequentially compensating.

## The PANoptosome

Canonical assembly is nucleated by innate-immune sensors — classically the Z-nucleic-acid sensor **[[ZBP1]]** during viral infection, or loss of **TAK1** kinase activity — which recruits [[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]], [[Caspase-8]], and [[Caspase-1]] (with FADD and ASC) into one complex. The result combines:

- gasdermin pore formation (pyroptotic arm),
- executioner caspase activation (apoptotic arm), and
- MLKL phosphorylation and membrane rupture (necroptotic arm).

ZBP1–RIPK3 recruitment can activate the [[NLRP3]] inflammasome directly, tying PANoptosis to interferon-driven and viral DNA-sensing contexts; TAK1 inhibition (e.g., by Yersinia effectors) converts the same complex into a death switch.

## Why it matters

PANoptosis explains death phenotypes that are neither rescuable by single-pathway blockade nor adequately described by one morphology — severe infectious (influenza, Yersinia), inflammatory, and ischemia-reperfusion contexts. It also explains why pharmacological inhibitors of individual arms (z-VAD, necrostatin-1) fail to protect in these settings, motivating combined or upstream targeting.
