# Defibrillation

**Defibrillation** is the delivery of an unsynchronised transthoracic (or transvenous) electrical shock intended to terminate a shockable cardiac arrhythmia — **ventricular fibrillation (VF)** and **pulseless ventricular tachycardia (VT)**. It is the definitive treatment for the arrest rhythms that account for the large majority of sudden cardiac death, and it is the only intervention that restores rhythm in a patient with no output.

## Mechanism

A shock is a very high-voltage, very brief depolarising pulse delivered across the chest between two paddles or adhesive pads. It does not "reset" the heart in the electrical sense: it depolarises a critical mass of myocardium simultaneously, extinguishing the reentrant wavelets that sustain VF/VT. When the shock ends, the tissue has passed through a refractory period and the myocardium is uniformly excitable, allowing the sinoatrial node to re-establish organised sinus depolarisation.

> [!warning] Not every arrest is shockable
> Defibrillation does not work in **asystole** or **pulseless electrical activity** — there is no organised electrical activity to reset, and no circulation to restart. Those rhythms are managed with high-quality chest compressions, adrenaline and reversal of the reversible causes (see the chain of survival and CPR). Survival falls by roughly 7–10% for every minute without shock.

## Waveforms and Energy

- **Monophasic** shocks deliver a single unidirectional current pulse and require high energies (traditional 360 J). Largely superseded.
- **Biphasic** shocks deliver current in two opposite phases, which is more efficient at defibrillating and lowers myocardial injury. Randomised and observational data favour biphasic over monophasic waveforms for out-of-hospital shockable arrest; defibrillators therefore dose by **energy**, with biphasic adult shocks commonly started at **120–200 J** and escalated. Clinical trials and bench work (optical mapping, virtual-electrode models, impedance-based truncated-exponential delivery) address why the same energy at different impedance or phase settings gives different outcomes — a reason the field debates energy-based versus **current-based** defibrillation.
- **Paediatric shocks** are energy-based from 2 J/kg and escalated.
- **Automatic external defibrillators** analyse the rhythm and deliver a pre-programmed shock if the rhythm is shockable; lay and non-expert use is explicitly endorsed in guidelines.

## Unscheduled Versus Scheduled

Defibrillation is by definition **unsynchronised**. Electrical cardioversion treats a patient *with* a pulse and *is* synchronised (R-wave timed) so the shock avoids the vulnerable T-wave period. Implantable cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) deliver a low-energy internal shock for sustained ventricular arrhythmias in patients at risk who already have a pulse or who have survived a prior arrest.

## Practical Management

- **Compressions first, shock early** — minimise the pause between chest compressions and shock; resume compressions immediately after the shock without a rhythm or pulse check.
- **Energy escalation** — increase energy with each unsuccessful shock.
- **Post-shock care** — reverse the causes (hypoxia, acidosis, hypo-/hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, hypovolaemia) and prevent re-arrest; adrenaline, anti-arrhythmics and, where indicated, coronary revascularisation follow the chain of survival.
- **Safety** — the shock is delivered synchronously with the compressor's rhythm in most protocols; the operator's risk is chiefly from failed pads, wet skin, jewellery or a flammable oxygen-enriched environment.

## Connections

- [[Cardiac arrest]] — Defibrillation is the definitive shockable-rhythm intervention inside the out-of-hospital chain of survival, and time-to-first-shock is one of the strongest predictors of survival and of good neurological outcome.
- [[Arrhythmias]] — Arrhythmia classification decides shockability: VF and pulseless VT are defibrillated, whereas asystole and pulseless electrical activity are not.
- [[Atrial Fibrillation]] — Unstable AF with a pulse is treated by synchronised cardioversion rather than defibrillation, so defibrillation sits at the boundary of arrhythmia management.
- [[Myocardial infarction]] — Because ischaemic VT/VF arises on acute coronary occlusion, early defibrillation is usually followed by urgent revascularisation, making reperfusion the practical partner of shock.
- [[Heart Failure]] — Reduced ejection fraction and scar set the substrate for re-entrant ventricular arrhythmias, which is why ICDs are implanted for secondary and selected primary prevention.
