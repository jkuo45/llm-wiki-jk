# Glutaminolysis

**Glutaminolysis** is the catabolic pathway through which [[Glutamine]] is sequentially converted to [[Glutamate]] (by [[Glutaminase]]) and then to [[α-Ketoglutarate]] (via transamination or glutamate dehydrogenase), enabling entry into the [[TCA cycle]]. This pathway is the dominant [[Anaplerosis|anaplerotic]] route in rapidly proliferating cancer cells.

## Pathway

1. **Glutamine → Glutamate**: Catalyzed by [[Glutaminase]] (GLS/GLS2), the committed step
2. **Glutamate → α-Ketoglutarate**: Via glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH) or aminotransferases (GOT1/2, GPT)
3. **α-KG → TCA cycle**: Enters at the α-ketoglutarate node, feeding oxidative and biosynthetic branches

## Role in Cancer

Many cancers exhibit "glutamine addiction" — a dependence on exogenous glutamine for survival driven by enhanced glutaminolysis.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]]
> [[MYC]] enhances [[Glutaminolysis]], promoting influx of glutamine-derived [[α-Ketoglutarate]] into the [[TCA cycle]]. In MYC^high^ cells, the TCA cycle shifts from glucose-derived to glutamine-derived carbon as its primary fuel. This renders MYC^high^ cells selectively vulnerable to combined inhibition of glutaminolysis ([[CB-839]]) and [[Complex I]], which collapses the glutamine-fueled TCA cycle by simultaneously blocking anaplerotic input and [[NAD+]] regeneration.

## Pharmacological Inhibition

- **[[CB-839]]** (telaglenastat): Glutaminase inhibitor in clinical trials; limited single-agent efficacy in unselected populations but promising in MYC^high^ combination strategies
- **BPTES**: Research-grade glutaminase inhibitor
- **DON** (6-diazo-5-oxo-L-norleucine): Broad glutamine antagonist
