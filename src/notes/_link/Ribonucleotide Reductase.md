# Ribonucleotide reductase

**Ribonucleotide reductase** (**RNR**) is the only enzyme capable of the *de novo* conversion of ribonucleoside diphosphates (NDPs — ADP, GDP, CDP, UDP) into deoxyribonucleoside diphosphates (dNDPs), the immediate precursors of the dNTPs required for [[DNA Replication]] and [[DNA Repair]]. As the rate-limiting, allosterically regulated committed step of de novo dNTP synthesis, RNR couples nucleotide supply to cell-cycle progression and the [[DNA Damage Response]], balancing genome stability against mutagenesis. Its central role makes it a validated anticancer drug target ([[Hydroxyurea]], [[Gemcitabine]]).

> [!important]
> RNR is the archetypal **thioredoxin-1 client**. After every catalytic cycle its active-site cysteines are oxidized to a disulfide, and the enzyme is re-reduced by reduced [[Thioredoxin-1]] (regenerated in turn by [[Thioredoxin reductase]] + NADPH) or by glutaredoxin (via [[Glutathione]]). Through this link the thioredoxin system directly fuels genome duplication — which is why RNR appears throughout this vault's thioredoxin and redox-biology notes.

## Structure & Subunits

Mammalian RNR is a **Class Ia** enzyme assembled as an active α₂β₂ heterotetramer (with higher-order assemblies) from two homodimeric subunits:

- **RRM1 (α, large subunit, ~85 kDa monomer)** — binds substrates and allosteric effectors. Contains the catalytic site (three conserved cysteines, including the residue that forms the transient thiyl radical), the **substrate-specificity (S)** allosteric site, and the **overall-activity (A)** allosteric site.
- **RRM2 (β, small subunit, ~45 kDa monomer)** — carries the **diferric-tyrosyl radical** cofactor that initiates catalysis. The stable tyrosyl radical (Tyr122-equivalent) is generated by O₂-dependent assembly of the di-iron center. In expressed cells it is **S-phase restricted**, giving RNR maximal activity during [[S Phase]].
- **RRM2B / p53R2 (β′)** — a p53-inducible alternative small subunit with ~83% homology to RRM2. It assembles with RRM1 when [[p53]] is activated by DNA damage, provisioning dNTPs for repair and mitochondrial DNA synthesis in non-dividing cells.
- **Radical taxonomy**: RNRs across kingdoms use different radical initiators — class Ia/Ib (metal–tyrosyl radical, eukaryotes), class II (adenosylcobalamin, many prokaryotes), class III (glycyl radical, anaerobes) — all ultimately reducing NDPs with the same radical-based ribose chemistry.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

1. **Substrate reduction**: An NDP binds the RRM1 active site. The thiyl radical formed at the catalytic cysteine abstracts a hydrogen from the ribose 3′, a ketyl radical intermediate expels water from C2′ (C–O bond cleavage), and two active-site cysteines reduce the product, generating a dNDP and an enzyme disulfide.
2. **Long-range radical transfer**: The tyrosyl radical in RRM2 is relayed ~32–40 Å to the RRM1 active site through a conserved proton-coupled electron-transfer (PCET) pathway (Tyr· → conserved tryptophan/hydrogen-bond network → catalytic cysteine).
3. **Reductant regeneration**: After each turnover the RRM1 disulfide must be re-reduced. Reduced [[Thioredoxin-1]] is the dominant physiological electron donor in proliferating cells; the thioredoxin pathway ([[Thioredoxin reductase]] + NADPH) recycles it. Glutaredoxin provides a parallel donor routed through reduced [[Glutathione]] (glutathione reductase + NADPH). Both pathways converge on NADPH.
4. **Product flow**: dNDPs are converted to dNTPs by nucleotide kinases; balanced dNTP pools are essential — deficits or imbalances drive mutagenesis, replication stress, and cell death.

## Regulation

- **Allosteric overall activity (A site)**: ATP activates; **dATP inhibits** (end-product feedback), toggling oligomeric states between active and inactive conformations.
- **Allosteric substrate specificity (S site)**: ATP/dATP favor pyrimidine reduction (CDP, UDP), dTTP directs GDP, dGTP directs ADP — a homeostatic mechanism that keeps the four dNTP pools balanced.
- **Cell cycle & transcription**: RRM1 is constitutively expressed, while **RRM2 is transcriptionally restricted to S phase** via [[E2F]] and its protein is degraded in quiescent/G1 cells. Holoenzyme activity is therefore highest during DNA replication.
- **DNA damage & p53**: [[p53]] transactivates **RRM2B (p53R2)** so that repair synthesis can proceed in arrested or non-cycling cells; [[ATR]]/[[ATM]] checkpoint signaling further couples RNR capacity to replication-stress sensing.

## Physiological Function

- **De novo dNTP biosynthesis** — the only de novo route to DNA building blocks; proliferating cells depend absolutely on RNR activity.
- **DNA replication** ([[S Phase]]), **repair synthesis** (BER/NER/MMR via p53R2), and **mitochondrial DNA synthesis** in non-dividing tissues — p53R2-deficient mice develop kidney failure and mtDNA depletion within weeks.
- **Genome stability**: RNR fidelity (substrate specificity, balanced pool output, and cell-cycle gating) minimizes replication errors; insufficient or imbalanced dNTP supply causes mutagenesis and chromosome breakage.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Anticancer drug target**: Because transforming and proliferating cells depend on de novo dNTP synthesis, RNR is among the most exploited targets in onco-pharmacology:
  - **[[Hydroxyurea]]** — quenches the RRM2 tyrosyl radical; used in myeloproliferative neoplasms and as an immunosuppressive; canonical inducer of [[DNA Replication Stress]] and [[Senescence]].
  - **[[Gemcitabine]]** — its diphosphate (dFdCDP) irreversibly inactivates RRM1 and depletes dNTP pools (especially dCTP), self-potentiating its own incorporation into DNA.
  - Other inhibitors: clofarabine and cytarabine as nucleoside analogues, and the iron-chelating thiosemicarbazone **triapine** (3-AP) that dismantles the radical cofactor.
- **Chemoresistance**: RRM1/RRM2 overexpression is a major mechanism of gemcitabine resistance in pancreatic and non-small-cell lung cancers; RRM1 expression is evaluated as a predictive biomarker.
- **Replication stress & senescence axis**: RNR inhibition → dNTP insufficiency → stalled replication forks → [[ATR]]/[[ATM]] checkpoint activation → [[Apoptosis]] or [[Senescence]]. This dNTP-catastrophe axis is exactly how hydroxyurea and related agents impose cell-cycle arrest — a mechanistic link to the vault's senescence and senolytic themes.
- **Inherited defects**: pathogenic RRM2B (p53R2) mutations cause **mitochondrial DNA depletion syndromes** (encephalomyopathic/mtDNA-depletion presentations with renal tubulopathy), and defective RNR in lymphoid lineages underlies severe replication-dependent immunodeficiencies.
