# Genotype

**Overview:** A **genotype** is the part of the genetic makeup of a cell, and therefore of any individual, which determines one of its characteristics. It comprises the specific inherited DNA sequence—the allelic variants at loci across the genome.

**Interaction with environment:** The phenotype is the physical, biochemical, and behavioral expression of the genotype, shaped by environmental exposures, lifestyle, and stochastic factors. Identical genotypes can yield different phenotypes under differing conditions, underscoring gene–environment interplay.

**Relation to epigenetics and instability:** Genotype sets the constitutional sequence, but reversible [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] marks (DNA methylation, histone modification) modulate its readout without altering the code. Conversely, acquired mutations and [[Genomic Instability]]—an accumulation of DNA damage and chromosomal aberrations—distort the genotype over the lifetime and drive aging and cancer.

**Relevance to longevity and precision medicine:** Polymorphisms (e.g., in drug-metabolizing enzymes) explain interindividual variation in treatment response and risk. Characterizing genotype informs stratified prevention and [[Aging]]-related risk stratification, though it acts in concert with epigenetic and environmental modifiers rather than in isolation.
