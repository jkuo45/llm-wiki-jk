# CXCL14

CXCL14 (C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 14), also known as BRAK (breast and kidney-expressed chemokine), is a constitutively expressed C-X-C chemokine that, unlike most CXC ligands, lacks the ELR motif and does not signal through classical CXCR1/2/3 receptors. It is a component of the early, p53/p21/Rb-dependent SASP program that places stressed or senescent cells under immune surveillance (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptor/contact**: CXCL14 does not bind the major ELR+ CXC receptors; it is retained on endothelial glycosaminoglycans and is thought to act via an as-yet-unidentified receptor (proposed orphan GPCR/atypical pathways), often cooperating with other chemokines presented on the endothelium.
- **Cell sources**: Broadly expressed in normal epithelium, skin, lung, and lymphoid tissues; upregulated early after senescence-inducing stress.
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Expression is supported by the [[p53]] tumor suppressor and the [[p21 CIP1|p21]]–[[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] axis, marking it as part of the p53-dependent "early" SASP profile rather than the later NF-κB-driven inflammatory wave.
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Recruits and activates [[Macrophages]], dendritic cells, NK cells, and T cells (but not neutrophils), promoting immune recognition and killing of damaged cells.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CXCL14 belongs to the early senescence secretome induced before the full NF-κB/IL-6 inflammatory SASP matures, acting as an "eat-me/come-find-me" signal for immunosurveillance.
- By recruiting cytotoxic/antigen-presenting cells, it helps the immune system clear senescent cells, supporting tumor suppression and tissue homeostasis.
- Its p53-dependence means CXCL14 is often lost in p53-mutant cancers, removing a surveillance cue and favoring immune evasion.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- CXCL14 is a candidate biomarker of p53-intact senescence and of immune-active tumors.
- It has context-dependent tumor-suppressive roles (immune surveillance) but can be hijacked in some cancers to drive angiogenesis or immune exclusion.
- Restoration of CXCL14 expression is being explored as an immuno-oncologic strategy to reinstate senescence surveillance.
