## Overview

IGF–Akt signaling is a conserved pro-growth, pro-survival axis in which insulin-like growth factor ([[IGF1]]) receptor engagement activates phosphoinositide 3-kinase and the serine/threonine kinase [[Akt]]. Akt drives anabolic growth, cell survival, and hypertrophy. Within this knowledge graph the pathway is positioned downstream of — and repressed by — [[SIRT6]], which blocks it to forestall maladaptive cardiac hypertrophy.

## Mechanism / Structure

> [!info] Directionality
> SIRT6 blocks IGF–Akt signaling (SIRT6 is upstream of repression). SIRT6 acts by targeting c-Jun, interrupting a pro-hypertrophic survival pathway and thereby protecting the heart from maladaptive growth.

The canonical flow of the axis:
1. **IGF-1 binding** to its receptor triggers PI3K-dependent PIP3 generation.
2. **Akt is recruited and phosphorylated** (by PDK1/mTORC2), becoming active.
3. **Akt phosphorylates downstream targets** (e.g., mTOR, GSK3β, FOXOs) to promote growth, survival, and protein synthesis.
4. In cardiomyocytes, sustained activation pushes **cardiac hypertrophy** — increased cell size and fetal gene program re-activation.

SIRT6 opposes this by dampening the pathway, complementing its separate anti-fibrotic actions in cardiovascular tissue.

## Role in Cardiovascular and Aging Biology

The IGF–Akt axis is a double-edged sword: physiologically essential for growth and survival, but chronically overactive in maladaptive hypertrophy and aging-related disease. SIRT6-mediated repression illustrates a cardioprotective sirtuin mechanism distinct from the AMPK/PGC-1α metabolic axis, highlighting how individual sirtuins deploy tissue-specific programs.

> [!tip] Sirtuin context
> Whereas [[SIRT3]] promotes metabolic fitness via AMPK/PGC-1α, SIRT6 guards the heart by restraining IGF–Akt–driven growth. Together they exemplify how sirtuins calibrate distinct downstream pathways for cardiovascular health.

## Clinical & Research Significance

Modulating IGF–Akt signaling is relevant to heart failure, cardiomyopathies, and the broader biology of growth-axis aging (where reduced IGF–Akt activity is associated with longevity in model organisms). SIRT6 activation or IGF–Akt inhibition represents a candidate strategy against pathological hypertrophy, though systemic effects on growth and metabolism must be carefully balanced.
