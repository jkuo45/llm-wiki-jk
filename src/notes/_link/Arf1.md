# Arf1

**Arf1** (ADP-ribosylation factor 1) is a ~21 kDa small GTPase of the RAS
superfamily and the founding member of the ARF family. It is the master
regulator of coat recruitment at the Golgi: Arf1-GTP on a Golgi membrane is
what brings [[COPI]] coatomer there in the first place.

## Structure & Family

ARFs fall into three classes: class I (ARF1, ARF2, ARF3), class II (ARF4,
ARF5) and class III (ARF6). Arf1 is N-terminally myristoylated, which is what
couples its GDP- and GTP-bound pools reversibly to membranes — the myristoyl
switch. Like all small GTPases it cycles between an inactive GDP-loaded
cytosolic state and an active GTP-loaded membrane-anchored state, driven by
Sec7-domain guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GBF1, BIG1, BIG3 at ER-Golgi
intermediate compartment and Golgi) and terminated by ARF GAPs such as ARFGAP1.

## Mechanism of Action

1. Arf-GEFs recruit Arf1 to membrane, aided by PI4P and Rab1.
2. The Sec7 domain of the GEF triggers GDP-GTP exchange, inserting the
   GTP-bound Arf1 into the membrane.
3. Membrane-inserted Arf1-GTP recruits the soluble heptameric **coatomer**
   en bloc, providing the outer shell of the [[COPI]] vesicle.
4. Arf1 and coatomer cooperate to bud the vesicle and mediate its release.
5. ARFGAP1-driven GTP hydrolysis releases Arf1 from the membrane; COPI
   persists briefly to allow uncoating.

Arf1-GTP also activates phospholipase D (no note yet), generating phosphatidic
acid that shapes the membrane and recruits further coat components.

Reconstitution experiments established the core requirement directly: salt-washed
Golgi membrane plus coatomer plus GTPγS is sufficient for stable COPI
recruitment, whereas GTP (hydrolysable) will not sustain it, and ARFGAP1
addition promotes vesicle formation. Reconstitution with mitotic cytosol or
purified coatomer plus wild-type Arf1 — but not the inactive mutant — converts
Golgi membranes into COPI vesicles.

> [!info] Cargo selection
> COPI vesicles are selected for dilysine-tagged cargo (KKXX motifs on
> membrane proteins such as ER-resident retrograde cargo). Arf1 sets where the
> coat assembles; coatomer and the cargo receptors decide what goes in.

## Cellular Functions

- **Intra-Golgi and retrograde transport.** Arf1 is localized to the Golgi and
  has a central role in intra-Golgi transport, moving cargo both anterograde
  between cisternae and retrograde to the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]].
- **Golgi structure.** Live imaging shows Arf1 and COPI bind and release
  continuously at Golgi membranes, letting those sites recruit cargo, alter
  phospholipid composition and grow into larger phase-separated domains.
- **Mitotic Golgi fragmentation.** Arf1-GTP-driven COPI budding fragments the
  Golgi during mitosis.
- **Beyond membrane traffic.** ARFs regulate phospholipid metabolism, actin
  at membrane surfaces, secretion, endocytosis, phagocytosis, cytokinesis and
  tumour cell invasion.

## Disease & Clinical Relevance

- **Neurodevelopmental disease.** ARF1 haploinsufficiency causes periventricular
  nodular heterotopia with variable clinical expressivity.
- **Golgi system disruptors.** M-COPA syndrome arises from COPA mutants, which
  destabilise ARF1–COPI coupling.
- **Viral pathogenesis.** ARFs were first identified as the host factors that
  enhance cholera toxin's ADP-ribosyltransferase activity, and many bacterial
  toxins exploit ARF-GEF or ARF-GAP recruitment. HIV-1 Nef binds Arf1-GTP
  directly and recruits it to endosomal membranes to sequester trafficking
  components.
- **Metabolic signalling.** Proximity labelling identifies Arf1 as a regulator
  of mTORC2 via RICTOR.

## Connections

- [[COPI]] — the heptameric coat that Arf1-GTP recruits; Arf1 is the switch
  that puts coatomer on the Golgi membrane.
- [[Golgi apparatus]] — the organelle where Arf1 concentrates and where the
  class I ARF function is defined.
- [[Membrane Trafficking]] — the system Arf1 organises by alternating coat
  assembly and disassembly.
- [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] — the destination of COPI retrograde transport and
  the site of the GBF1-driven anterograde step.
- [[Clathrin]] — the other major coated-vesicle system, built on a different
  logic (pit formation, adaptor proteins) but intersecting with ARF6 at the
  plasma membrane and endosomes.
- [[Phospholipase D]] — activated by Arf-GTP to generate phosphatidic acid.
- Mitotic Golgi fragmentation — Arf1-dependent COPI budding is what breaks the
  Golgi up during cell division.
