# Carmofur

**Carmofur** (1-hexylcarbamoyl-5-fluorouracil, HCFU) is a 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) prodrug that has been clinically used in Japan since 1981 for the treatment of [[Colorectal Cancer]]. It was identified as a highly potent inhibitor of [[Acid ceramidase]] (IC50 29 nM), a property essential to its anti-proliferative effects and distinct from 5-FU activity.

## Pharmacological Properties

- **ACase IC50:** 29 nM (rat recombinant); ~100 nM (cellular)
- **Covalent mechanism:** Carbamoylates the catalytic Cys143 of ACase
- **BBB penetration:** Crosses the blood-brain barrier, making it relevant for [[Glioblastoma]]
- **Additional targets:** Also inhibits FAAH, NAAA, and SARS-CoV-2 Mpro
- **Clinical status:** Approved in Japan for colorectal cancer; investigated for glioblastoma given its GSC-targeting activity

## Role in Senolytic Research

> [!info] Source: Realini et al., 2013, *Sci Rep*; Doan et al., 2017, *Oncotarget*
> Carmofur kills [[Glioblastoma]] stem-like cells (GSCs) with IC50 11–104 µM — >50-fold more potent than temozolomide (IC50 >750 µM). As an existing approved drug that targets [[Acid ceramidase]], it is a candidate for repurposing as a [[Senolytic]] therapy.
