# SUMO

**SUMO** (Small Ubiquitin-like Modifier, also called **sentrin**) is a family of small (~11 kDa, ~100 amino acid) [[Ubiquitin]]-like proteins that are covalently attached to lysine residues of target proteins by [[SUMOylation]] and removed again by [[SENP]] proteases. Four functional paralogues exist in humans: **SUMO1, SUMO2, SUMO3, and SUMO4**. SUMO2 and SUMO3 are nearly identical at the amino acid level and cannot be distinguished by antibodies; SUMO1 shares only about 50% similarity with them. In budding yeast the homologue is **SMT3** ("suppressor of mif two 3").

> [!info] Mechanism
> The SUMO fold is nearly superimposable on the ubiquitin fold despite less than 20% sequence identity, with one distinctive addition: an extended, unstructured N-terminal region of 10–25 residues that SUMO1/2/3 use to build polySUMO chains. Mature SUMO is generated by cleavage of a C-terminal peptide to expose a di-glycine motif, which forms an isopeptide bond to a substrate lysine — usually within the **Ψ-K-x-D/E** consensus motif, where Ψ is hydrophobic. Substrates bind SUMO non-covalently through **SUMO-interacting motifs (SIMs)**, short hydrophobic stretches flanked by acidic residues.

## The E1/E2/E3 cascade

Mature SUMO is activated by the heterodimeric **SAE1/SAE2** (SUMO-activating enzyme) in an ATP-dependent step that forms a SUMO–AMP intermediate and a thioester-linked SUMO–SAE2, then transferred to the sole E2, **Ubc9**. Unlike ubiquitination, the E2 is sufficient for conjugation when a consensus motif is present; E3 ligases such as the **PIAS** family and **RanBP2** improve efficiency and can direct non-canonical, motif-independent sites. All human SUMO proteins are expressed as fusions to large sentrin-specific protease substrates and are processed by the SENP family.

## Functional consequences

SUMOylation is dynamic and typically affects only a small fraction of any given protein at any moment. Its most reproducible effects are:

- **Localization.** The first identified substrate, **RanGAP1**, is redirected from the cytosol to the nuclear pore complex by SUMO-1 modification.
- **Transcription.** SUMOylation of transcriptional regulators usually correlates with inhibition of transcription; deSUMOylation is the release valve.
- **Stability and degradation.** SUMO is not itself a degradation signal, but a polySUMOylated protein can be recognized by SUMO-targeted ubiquitin ligases (STUbLs) and then ubiquitinated and destroyed.
- **DNA damage response.** SUMO acts as a molecular glue that facilitates assembly of large repair complexes at foci, and SUMOylation participates in base excision repair, nucleotide excision repair, NHEJ, and homologous recombination.
- **Cell cycle.** SUMO-2/3 localize to centromeres and condensed chromosomes during mitosis and exclusively modify topoisomerase II; SUMO-1 instead localizes to the spindle and midzone. SUMO4 is not processed and conjugated under normal conditions but is used for modification under starvation stress.

## Nuclear bodies

**PML** itself is SUMOylated, and that modification is required for the maturation of **PML nuclear bodies** — a SUMO-rich nuclear compartment that concentrates client proteins for post-translational modification. SUMO-1 terminates polySUMO chains because it lacks the internal SUMO consensus sites present in SUMO2/3, and SUMO-1 Ser2 is itself phosphorylated, a "modified modifier" that further complicates chain architecture.

## Biomedical relevance

SUMO pathway imbalance is implicated in cancer, cardiovascular disease, neurodegeneration, and infection. In cancer, SUMOylation stabilizes [[MDM2]] and can keep [[p53]] out of the nucleus; in hypoxic tumors, deSUMOylation of HIF-1α by SENP3/SENP7 promotes survival. Under disturbed blood flow, p90RSK phosphorylates SENP2 at Thr368 and exports it, producing the SUMOylation of p53 and ERK5 that drives endothelial apoptosis and inflammation. In the yeast cell cycle, polySUMOylation of Tof2 releases Cdc14 to permit mitotic exit — the 2024 polySUMOylation-axis work that re-framed polySUMO as a regulated state rather than an autoregulatory byproduct.

**Biotechnology.** SUMO is widely used as a solubility tag for recombinant proteins expressed in *E. coli*, cleaved afterwards with Ulp1 protease.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> No agent that selectively modulates SUMO conjugation or deSUMOylation is an approved therapy. SUMOylation effects are paralogue-specific and site-specific, so "SUMO" without a paralogue or site index is often not a sufficiently specified claim.

## Connections

- [[SUMOylation]] — SUMO is the substrate and defining component of the SUMOylation cascade. The modification is what makes SUMO's functional effects; without it SUMO only binds non-covalently via SIMs.
- [[SENP]] — the SUMO-specific proteases that both generate mature SUMO precursors and reverse the modification, thereby setting steady-state SUMO levels.
- [[Ubiquitin]] — SUMO is a structural and mechanistic sibling of ubiquitin with less than 20% sequence identity but a near-identical fold; the two systems compete for the same substrate residues and are coupled through STUbL-mediated polySUMO-to-polyubiquitin conversion.
- [[PML]] — SUMOylated PML nucleates PML nuclear bodies, the paradigmatic SUMO-rich compartment and a platform for further client modification.
- [[Post-translational Modification]] — SUMOylation is a core member of the vault's PTM set, distinguished from most others by being typically non-degradative and by having a dedicated, specific reversing protease family.
- [[p53]] — a heavily SUMOylated substrate whose sumoylation affects nuclear localization and MDM2 feedback, making SUMO balance a determinant of p53 activity.
- [[Proteasome]] — the downstream fate of polySUMOylated substrates once STUbLs append ubiquitin; SUMO itself does not target proteins for destruction.
- [[RanGAP1]] — the first identified SUMO substrate and the reference example for SUMO-driven relocalization.
- [[Autophagy]] — via the SUMOylation of TFEB, ULK1, and GABARAP, the SUMO state of autophagy regulators sets the autophagic threshold.
