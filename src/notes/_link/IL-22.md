# IL-22

**Interleukin 22 (IL-22)** is a member of the IL-10 gene family that mediates cellular inflammatory responses and epithelial regeneration after tissue injury. It has emerged as a key cytokine of interest for [[Thymus|thymus]] tissue rejuvenation.

## Production and Signaling

- **Producers:** [[TH17|T helper 17 cells]] and innate lymphoid cell subsets (ILCs)
- **Receptor:** IL-22R on epithelial cell surfaces
- **Signaling:** JAK1/TYK2 → STAT3 → proliferation and survival genes
- **Primary role:** Mucosal surface barrier maintenance via innate antimicrobial molecules

## Thymic Rejuvenation

Groundbreaking work from the van den Brink laboratory established IL-22 as a thymic regeneration factor:

### Key findings (Dudakov et al., 2012)
- IL-22 signals through IL-22R on [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|thymic epithelial cells]]
- Promotes proliferation and survival of thymocytes and TEC subsets
- Administration to wild-type mice after total body irradiation significantly increased thymic cellularity
- IL-22-deficient mice showed impaired immune recovery after irradiation

### Mechanism
- Absence of CD4/CD8 double-positive thymocytes triggers upregulation of IL-23
- IL-23 promotes innate lymphoid cells (ILCs) to produce IL-22
- IL-22 then drives TEC proliferation and [[Thymopoiesis|thymopoiesis]] recovery

> [!important] Clinical potential
> IL-22 therapy represents a promising approach for rejuvenating aged or damaged thymus. As of 2013, no clinical trials were registered for IL-22 therapy in aged thymus rejuvenation, but pre-clinical proof-of-concept studies strongly support further investigation.

## Other Biological Roles

- Mucosal barrier defense (gut, lung, skin)
- Epithelial cell proliferation and tissue repair
- Pro-inflammatory mediator in [[Inflammation|Crohn's disease]] and psoriasis
- Antimicrobial peptide induction (RegIIIγ, RegIIIβ, β-defensins)
