# CISD2

**CISD2** (CDGSH iron sulfur domain 2), also known as **NAF-1** (nutrient-deprivation autophagy factor-1), is a mitochondrial/ER-membrane [2Fe-2S] protein related to [[CISD1]] (mitoNEET). It is a negative regulator of [[Ferroptosis]]; its suppression increases mitochondrial iron and lipid ROS, driving ferroptotic cell death—demonstrated in [[Head and neck cancer]].

## Structure & Biochemistry

CISD2 contains a CDGSH domain coordinating a labile [2Fe-2S] cluster and localizes to mitochondrial outer membrane and ER membranes. Like CISD1, it participates in iron handling and redox signaling.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

Inhibition of the [[CISD2]] gene in HNC cells increases the accumulation of mitochondrial iron and lipid ROS, promoting ferroptosis (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Head and neck cancer"). By contrast, intact CISD2 limits mitochondrial Fe²⁺ availability for the [[Fenton Reaction]], protecting membranes from peroxidation. Its role parallels [[CISD1]] in liver cancer, establishing the CISD family as a conserved mitochondrial ferroptosis brake.

> [!info] CISD1 vs CISD2
> Both are [2Fe-2S] mitochondrial proteins that restrain ferroptosis by limiting mitochondrial iron; CISD1 is prominent in HCC, CISD2 in HNC—tissue-distinct but mechanistically unified.

## Clinical Relevance

CISD2 is a ferroptosis-resistance factor and potential target to sensitize HNC and other tumors. Its modulation could complement [[GPX4]] inhibition or iron loading in ferroptosis-based regimens.
