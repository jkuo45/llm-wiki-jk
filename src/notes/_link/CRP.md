# CRP

**CRP** (C-reactive protein) is a member of the pentraxin family — the most
frequently measured inflammatory biomarker in clinical practice, and the
reference acute-phase reactant against which other inflammatory markers are
judged. Its assay form, [[hs-CRP]], is covered separately; this note covers
the molecule itself.

## Structure

CRP circulates predominantly as a **pentamer** of five identical 23 kDa
subunits associated non-covalently in cyclic symmetry, giving it the
disc-like shape responsible for its name (reaction with the C-polysaccharide
of *Streptococcus pneumoniae*). It is a calcium-dependent protein: calcium
binding is required for its two best-described effector interactions, classical
complement activation through C1q and phagocyte engagement via Fcγ receptors.
Under inflammatory conditions it can also dissociate into **monomeric CRP**,
which has distinct, often pro-inflammatory, tissue-binding and complement-fixing
properties — a conformational shift that underlies the "native versus modified
CRP" debate in cardiovascular biomarker literature.

## Regulation of Synthesis

CRP is produced predominantly by hepatocytes, and its transcription is driven
by the cytokine network rather than by the protein itself:

- **IL-6 is the dominant stimulus.** It signals through membrane-bound IL-6R
  and gp130 to activate [[STAT3]], NF-κB and C/EBPβ. A complex of c-Fos,
  STAT3 and hepatocyte NF-1α binds the CRP promoter and upregulates transcription.
- **Interleukin 1β** acts through complementary pathways via NF-κB and raises
  hepatic CRP production further, synergistically.
- [[TNF-alpha]] amplifies IL-6-driven output but is not the primary signal.

Because synthesis is transcriptional, plasma CRP rises over hours (typically
peaking at ~48 h after an inflammatory stimulus) and falls with a long tail of
~19–20 h biological half-life that shortens with inflammation. Baseline
concentrations are low but not zero, and are modulated by BMI, smoking,
age and sex.

## Clinical Use

CRP is the workhorse of low-grade inflammation monitoring and of cardiovascular
risk stratification, where bands of <1, 1–3 and >3 mg/L are used, with ≥2 mg/L
conventionally defining residual inflammatory risk.

> [!warning] Marker, not cause
> Mendelian randomisation and neutral CRP-infusion studies indicate CRP
> **predicts** cardiovascular events but is unlikely to be directly causal in
> atherosclerosis — in contrast to IL-6 and IL-1β, for which the causal
> evidence is strong. CRP is therefore a readout placed downstream of IL-6,
> not a therapeutic target in itself. Consistent with this, the CANTOS
> IL-1β inhibition trial reduced events in patients selected by hs-CRP ≥2 mg/L
> without touching lipids.

Recent analyses suggest [[IL-6]] outperforms hs-CRP as a predictor of
atherosclerotic, heart-failure and fatal outcomes: in MESA, hs-CRP's
association with major adverse cardiovascular events lost significance after
adjustment for IL-6 while IL-6's did not; in the STABILITY trial, hs-CRP
associations did not survive multivariable adjustment whereas IL-6 did.
Practical reading: CRP quantifies the inflammatory state, but IL-6 is the
better-specified upstream mediator, and the two correlate only modestly
(Rho ~0.5).

## Role Beyond Measurement

CRP is not inert. It participates directly in complement activation, opsonization
and clearance of apoptotic cells and necrotic debris, and it modulates
endothelial function and the NLRP3 inflammasome axis. The functional
consequence for biomarker interpretation is that CRP is a participant in the
process it reports on.

## Connections

- [[hs-CRP]] — the high-sensitivity assay for the same analyte, and the form
  used for cardiovascular risk stratification; low concentrations of the same
  protein.
- [[IL-6]] — the principal upstream cytokine driving hepatic CRP transcription,
  and increasingly the better prognostic inflammatory marker.
- [[Interleukin 1β]] — synergizes with IL-6 to amplify hepatic CRP production.
- [[TNF-alpha]] — amplifies but does not initiate CRP synthesis.
- [[STAT3]] — the transcription factor through which IL-6 signalling reaches
  the CRP promoter.
- [[Inflammation]] — CRP is its standard circulating proxy.
- [[Hepatocyte]] — the cell that makes almost all circulating CRP.
- [[Liver]] — the organ determining CRP kinetics.
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — the disease whose residual risk is stratified by CRP
  band, though CRP is a marker here rather than a driver.
- [[Inflammaging]] — chronically elevated CRP is both a component and a driver
  of inflammaging phenotypes.
- [[SASP]] — senescent cells raise IL-6, which raises CRP, which is one reason
  CRP tracks biological age.
- [[Biomarker]] — CRP is the vault's reference inflammatory biomarker.
