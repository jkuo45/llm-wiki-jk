# Apelin

**Apelin** is an endogenous peptide hormone and the ligand of the G-protein-coupled receptor APLNR (APJ), encoded by the *APLN* gene. It exists as several active isoforms (apelin-13, -17, -36) and regulates cardiovascular function, fluid homeostasis, insulin sensitivity, and neuroprotection.

## Necroptosis relevance

- In streptozotocin-induced diabetic rats, apelin treatment reversed hippocampal necroptosis ([[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] axis), alleviated cognitive impairment, and preserved neuronal viability.
- As an endogenous modulator, apelin is a proof-of-concept that necroptosis can be restrained pharmacologically or peptidergically in metabolic/neurological injury, complementing small-molecule [[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] inhibitors.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Regulatory complexity and therapeutic targeting of the necroptosis network|Niu et al. 2026]]
> The review cites apelin as a non-pharmacological/endogenous-peptide intervention that reversed hippocampal necroptosis and cognitive impairment in diabetic rats, illustrating multi-modal therapeutic potential beyond conventional inhibitors.
