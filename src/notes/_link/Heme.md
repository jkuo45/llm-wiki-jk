# Heme

**Heme** is a coordination complex of an iron ion held inside a tetraporphyrin ring (protoporphyrin IX). In its ferrous (Fe²⁺) state the complex is electrically neutral and is called heme; oxidised to Fe³⁺ it is *hemin*. Heme is the prosthetic group of an unusually large number of hemoproteins and is synthesised in eight enzymatic steps from [[Glycine]] and succinyl-CoA, with the rate-limiting first step catalysed by 5-aminolevulinate synthase and the final iron insertion by ferrochelatase.

## Hemoprotein Functions

- **Oxygen transport and storage** — heme b in [[Hemoglobin]] binds O₂ reversibly; oxidation to Fe³⁺ produces [[Methemoglobin]], which cannot carry oxygen.
- **Electron transport** — heme a and heme c are integral to respiratory complexes II, III and IV, shuttling electrons along the [[Electron Transport Chain]] to drive [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]].
- **Xenobiotic metabolism** — the prosthetic group of the [[Cytochrome P450]] monooxygenases.
- **Reactive oxygen species handling** — heme modulates the two-to-one water/oxygen decomposition of H₂O₂ by [[Catalase]], and is an obligatory cofactor of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]].
- **Gas sensing** — the same iron centre also binds carbon monoxide and nitric oxide, making heme a gasotransmitter sensor as well as an oxygen carrier.

> [!info] Signal molecule, not just a cofactor
> Beyond its catalytic roles, heme regulates antioxidant defence, circadian rhythm via Bach1/Bach2 repression of the HO-1 promoter, ion channel activity, glucose utilisation and red blood cell production. The leucine-rich repeat domains of nuclear proteins such as Bach1 bind free heme directly as a physiological sensor.

## Degradation and Toxicity

Catabolism proceeds through [[HO-1]] (inducible) and heme oxygenase-2, which open the porphyrin ring to release [[Bilirubin]] precursor biliverdin, carbon monoxide and Fe²⁺. The released iron is exported by [[Ferroportin]], oxidised by [[Ceruloplasmin]] and loaded onto [[Transferrin]], or sequestered by [[Ferritin]] and [[HFE]] to keep it out of the Fenton reaction.

Free heme is a potent damage-associated molecular pattern. It drives macrophage inflammatory reprogramming through SYK-dependent reactive oxygen species generation, and it can drive [[Ferroptosis]] by expanding the labile iron pool that [[GPX4]] normally restrains. Heme is also trafficked and sensed by dedicated membrane receptors that either import it into the cytosol or transduce signalling without degradation, so a given heme signal can be either pro-death or pro-repair depending on which receptor engages and on the redox state of the cell.

> [!warning] Dual role
> Heme deficiency (as in sideroblastic anaemias and porphyrias) and heme excess are both cytotoxic. Copper deficiency phenocopies heme deficiency because copper is required for ferrochelatase activity.

## Connections
- [[Hemoglobin]] — Heme b is the oxygen-binding prosthetic group of each of the four globin chains; oxidation to the ferric state yields Methemoglobin and loss of oxygen carriage.
- [[HO-1]] — Heme oxygenase-1 is the rate-limiting enzyme of heme catabolism and the main route for converting heme's pro-oxidant iron into recyclable, ferritin-sequestered pools.
- [[Methemoglobin]] — Ferric heme within hemoglobin is the defining lesion of methemoglobinemia and links heme chemistry to adrenochrome-type oxidation injury.
- [[Ferroptosis]] — Heme-derived labile iron feeds the Fenton reaction that phospholipid peroxidation depends on, making heme handling a determinant of ferroptotic sensitivity.
- [[Iron-Sulfur Clusters]] — Heme and Fe-S cluster synthesis draw on the same mitochondrial iron pool, so mitophagy defects compromise both prosthetic groups at once.
