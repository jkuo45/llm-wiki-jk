## Overview

Cyclophilin D (CypD) is a mitochondrial matrix protein encoded by the *PPIF* gene that functions as a [[Peptidyl-Prolyl Isomerase|peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase]]. It is the primary positive regulator of the [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore|mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP)]], binding to the pore and promoting its opening in response to matrix [[Calcium|Ca^2+^]] overload and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]].

## Role in Cellular Senescence

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease]]
> CypD was recently identified as a senolytic target. Senescent cells display increased mPTP activity and frequent transient mPTP opening ("flickering") that serves to offload excessive Ca^2+^ from the matrix, preventing [[Apoptosis]]. Inhibiting CypD in senescent cells leads to mitochondrial Ca^2+^ accumulation and cell death. Additionally, [[SIRT3]] deacetylates CypD to inhibit mPTP opening, linking NAD^+^ metabolism to mitochondrial Ca^2+^ handling.

## Apoptotic gatekeeper — SIRT3 deacetylation

CypD deacetylation by [[SIRT3]] diminishes its peptidyl-prolyl isomerase activity and dissociates it from ANT, raising the MPT pore-opening threshold (Teodoro et al. 2018). Consequences: Sirt3⁻/⁻ cardiomyocytes show age-dependent mitochondrial swelling from spontaneous pore opening (Hafner et al. 2010), and SIRT3 activation attenuates neuronal apoptosis in sepsis-associated encephalopathy (Sun et al. 2017). Because MPT-driven permeabilization governs [[Cytochrome c]] release upstream of the caspase cascade, CypD is the mitochondrial gate through which sirtuins throttle apoptosis — a core node of the [[Sirtuin-Caspase Crosstalk]].

## Role in necroptosis — PGAM5-CypD-mPTP axis

- Upstream [[RIPK3]] upregulates [[PGAM5]], which enhances CypD phosphorylation, prolongs mPTP opening, and drives endothelial necroptosis in cardiac microvascular ischemia-reperfusion and bromocriptine models — in parallel to [[MLKL]] plasma-membrane pores.
- This positions CypD as the mitochondrial execution arm of necroptosis, complementary to the [[PGAM5]]-[[DRP1]] fission arm (Ser637 dephosphorylation plus Ser616 facilitation). Both arms converge on MMP collapse, ATP depletion, and mtROS burst.
- I/R-driven ROS via ER-Ca2+–xanthine oxidase signaling activates mPTP and the necroptotic program; [[RIPK3]] knockdown blocks this route. [[RIPK1]] may also regulate mPTP independently of [[RIPK3]].

> [!note]
> Source: [[PGAM5]] supplemental research 2024–2026 (Exp Mol Med 2025 Qi et al.; iScience 2024 He et al. 111539; Mitochondrial Dysfunction at Crossroads of Necroptosis 2025)
> PGAM5-enhanced CypD phosphorylation prolongs mPTP open time and establishes PGAM5 as a positive regulator of mPTP-dependent necroptosis, distinct from SIRT3 deacetylation control of the same pore.
