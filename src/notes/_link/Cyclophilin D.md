## Overview

Cyclophilin D (CypD) is a mitochondrial matrix protein encoded by the *PPIF* gene that functions as a [[Peptidyl-Prolyl Isomerase|peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase]]. It is the primary positive regulator of the [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore|mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP)]], binding to the pore and promoting its opening in response to matrix [[Calcium|Ca^2+^]] overload and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]].

## Role in Cellular Senescence

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease]]
> CypD was recently identified as a senolytic target. Senescent cells display increased mPTP activity and frequent transient mPTP opening ("flickering") that serves to offload excessive Ca^2+^ from the matrix, preventing [[Apoptosis]]. Inhibiting CypD in senescent cells leads to mitochondrial Ca^2+^ accumulation and cell death. Additionally, [[SIRT3]] deacetylates CypD to inhibit mPTP opening, linking NAD^+^ metabolism to mitochondrial Ca^2+^ handling.
