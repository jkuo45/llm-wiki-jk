# Creatine Supplementation

**Creatine supplementation** is the exogenous intake of [[Creatine]] — overwhelmingly as creatine monohydrate, the gold-standard form on the basis of efficacy, safety, and cost. Because roughly half of the body's creatine pool comes from diet (meat, fish) and the rest from endogenous synthesis ([[Arginine Glycine Amidinotransferase|AGAT]] → [[Guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase|GAMT]]), supplementation raises total stores beyond what diet alone provides — saturating muscle and brain [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)]] reserves and widening the [[Creatine Phosphate Shuttle]]-mediated energy buffer.

## Protocols

- **Loading**: ~20 g/day (4 × 5 g) for 5–7 days → muscle total creatine rises ~20–40%, after which stores can be maintained on lower doses.
- **Maintenance/no-load**: 3–5 g/day reaches saturation over ~3–4 weeks; dosing is commonly scaled to lean mass (~0.1 g/kg/day).
- **Form**: monohydrate remains the benchmark; alternative salts offer no confirmed advantage. Response magnitude is largest in individuals with low baseline stores (vegetarians, low meat intake).

## Evidence-backed applications

- **Exercise performance**: improved repeated-sprint capacity, strength, and training volume — indirectly amplifying hypertrophy and strength gains when combined with resistance exercise.
- **Sarcopenia and aging**: counters age-related declines in muscle phosphocreatine and pool size; combined protein + creatine + exercise trials show preserved lean mass and function in older adults.
- **Brain and neuroprotection**: the brain consumes [[Creatine Transporters (CRTR)]]-mediated uptake for its own PCr buffer; supplementation shows benefit in sleep-deprivation cognition, [[Depression]] adjunct therapy, and traumatic brain injury models, with investigation in [[Huntington's Disease (HD)]], [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)]], and [[Fibromyalgia]].
- **Special populations**: studied in [[Pregnancy]] (fetal growth), [[Skin Health]], and [[Fertility]].

## Safety

Extensive trials report no consistent adverse effect on kidney function in healthy individuals at recommended doses; transient water retention during loading is the main nuisance effect. Creatine's methyl-group demand does draw on the one-carbon pool (GAMT methylation consumes [[SAM]]), linking long-term supplementation to methylation flux.
