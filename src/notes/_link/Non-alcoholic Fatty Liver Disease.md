# Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease

**Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)** is the most common liver disease, with a worldwide prevalence of ~25%. It encompasses a spectrum from simple hepatic steatosis to non-alcoholic steatohepatitis ([[NASH]]), fibrosis, and cirrhosis in the absence of significant alcohol consumption. Its development involves genetic, environmental, and lifestyle risk factors.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

NAFLD arises from lipid metabolic dysregulation, oxidative stress, inflammation, and fibrosis. Key drivers include de novo lipogenesis, impaired fatty acid oxidation, insulin resistance, and mitochondrial dysfunction. Lipid metabolic homeostatic factors such as PPARα, PGC-1α, and NF-κB play protective roles.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

Sirtuin expression is decreased in NAFLD/fibrosis patients (SIRT1–3, SIRT5, SIRT6):

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Protective — deacetylates ChREBP and SREBP-1c to maintain hepatic lipid homeostasis; antagonizes liver fibrosis by blocking hepatic stellate cell activation. However, intestinal SIRT1 can be harmful by intensifying hepatic ferroptosis/inflammation via gut microbiota imbalance.
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Deacetylates ChREBP/SREBP-1c; hepatocyte-specific knockout elevates fibrosis and oxidative stress in NASH mice.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Prevents NAFLD by deacetylating hepatocyte nuclear factor 4α; deleterious in hepatic fibrosis via the SIRT2/ERK/c-Myc axis.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Improves mitochondrial function via deacetylation of mitochondrial trifunctional proteins and LCAD.
- **[[SIRT5]]**: Systemic knockout impairs medium-chain fatty acid oxidation → periportal macrovascular steatosis.
- **[[SIRT4]]**: Upregulation inhibits HFD-induced lipid accumulation/inflammation/fibrogenesis via the SIRT4/Smad4 axis; but can be harmful in NAFLD by deacetylating/destabilizing mitochondrial trifunctional protein-α.
- **[[SIRT7]]**: High expression in alcoholic hepatitis/NAFLD is harmful.

## Therapeutic Implications

NAD⁺-sirtuin axis modulation (e.g., SIRT1/SIRT6 activators) represents a candidate strategy for restoring hepatic lipid homeostasis and limiting progression to NASH and fibrosis.
