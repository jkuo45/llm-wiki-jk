# PNMT

**PNMT** (phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase, EC 2.1.1.28) is the terminal
enzyme of catecholamine biosynthesis. It catalyses the
S-adenosyl-L-methionine-dependent N-methylation of norepinephrine to
epinephrine (adrenaline), making it the switch point that decides whether a
cell produces norepinephrine or epinephrine.

## Structure & Classification

PNMT is a soluble, monomeric ~30 kDa enzyme belonging to the class I-like
SAM-binding methyltransferase superfamily (NNMT/PNMT/TEMT family), defined by
an N-terminal domain and a C-dependent catalytic domain with a conserved
SAM-binding motif. The human protein is 282 amino acids; the gene (PNMT,
chromosome 10q31, Gene ID 5409) is biased in expression to adrenal gland,
with lower expression in fat and brain. Alternative transcript variants exist,
and the enzyme also has β-carboline 2N-methyltransferase activity — relevant
to the vault's interest in β-carboline chemistry.

Km values are low-micromolar for SAM (~3.4 µM) and higher for phenylethanolamine
(~99 µM), consistent with SAM being the saturating co-substrate in vivo.

## Mechanism & Regulation

PNMT sits in the vesicular lumen-facing compartment of [[Chromaffin cells]],
which is where the preceding steps of the pathway run:

tyrosine → L-DOPA → dopamine → norepinephrine → **epinephrine**

The enzyme's abundance, not its catalytic rate, is the regulated variable.
PNMT activity is controlled almost entirely at the level of gene
transcription:

- **Glucocorticoids.** Cortisol diffusing from the adjacent adrenal cortex
  binds classical glucocorticoid receptors (Kd ~1 nM) and raises PNMT
  transcription. Glucocorticoid-receptor sites at −759 and −773 bp cooperate
  with the immediate-early transcription factor Egr-1 and the neural-crest
  developmental factor AP-2. Dexamethasone or hydrocortisone raises PNMT
  activity within days in cultured chromaffin cells, and the increment is
  not blocked by cycloheximide — consistent with transcriptional induction.
- **Splanchnic innervation.** Acetylcholine acting at muscarinic and nicotinic
  receptors induces PNMT; the induction is blocked by actinomycin D and
  cycloheximide, again implicating new transcription rather than activation
  of existing enzyme.
- **Developmental and glucocorticoid programming.** Prenatal glucocorticoid
  exposure programs later PNMT expression and adult hypertension risk, making
  this enzyme a node where developmental stress exposure becomes a
  cardiovascular phenotype.

> [!info] Anatomical consequence
> Because epinephrine is made only where PNMT is expressed, "the adrenal
> medulla" and "the epinephrine system" are close to synonyms. Norepinephrine
> is the pervasive neuronal transmitter; epinephrine is the restricted
> endocrine output of the adrenal medulla.

## Clinical & Pharmacological Relevance

- **Blood pressure.** Adrenal PNMT activity differs more than fivefold between
  inbred rat strains with distinct kinetic properties, and human PNMT
  variants are biomarkers of hypertension and hypoglycaemia.
- **Asthma susceptibility.** Epistatic interactions between PNMT and
  β2-adrenergic receptor genotype associate with extracellular epinephrine
  levels and asthma susceptibility.
- **Neurodegenerative disease.** The human orthologue is implicated in
  Alzheimer's disease and multiple sclerosis.
- **Pheochromocytoma / paraganglioma.** PNMT underexpression is associated
  with malignant behaviour in these tumours — consistent with a low-epinephrine
  phenotype.
- No clinically used PNMT inhibitor exists. PNMT knockout mice are viable but
  lack epinephrine entirely and die of catecholamine deficiency; the enzyme is
  therefore not a tolerated drug target in the way [[COMT]] is.
