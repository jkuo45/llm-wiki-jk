# Hypothalamus

**Hypothalamus** is a small but densely interconnected region of the diencephalon that serves as the master homeostatic center of the brain. It integrates signals from the periphery and the CNS to regulate feeding, energy expenditure, sleep–wake cycling, thermoregulation, stress responses, and reproduction, largely through [[Neuroendocrine Cells]] and projections to the pituitary and brainstem.

## Anatomy & Key Nuclei

- **Dorsomedial hypothalamus (DMH)** and **lateral hypothalamus (LH)** — sites where [[SIRT1]] overexpression in the brain-specific BRASTO mouse upregulates [[OX2R]], linking sirtuin activity to longevity phenotypes.
- Other major nuclei: ventromedial (satiety), arcuate (leptin/ghrelin sensing), paraventricular (CRH/oxytocin/vasopressin), suprachiasmatic (circadian pacemaker).

## Role in Aging & Metabolic Regulation

- The hypothalamus integrates nutrient and [[Adipose Tissue]]-derived signals ([[Leptin]], ghrelin, insulin) to set systemic metabolic state.
- Hypothalamic [[NF-κB]]-mediated [[Inflammation]] increases with age and drives age-related hormonal and metabolic decline (Zhang et al., 2013), positioning the hypothalamus as a potential pacemaker of systemic aging.
- SIRT1-dependent upregulation of [[OX2R]] in the DMH/LH promotes wakefulness and increases physical activity, contributing to the lifespan and healthspan benefits of the [[BRASTO]] model.

## Connections

  - [[OX2R]]: Orexin receptor upregulated by SIRT1 in the DMH/LH; mediator of BRASTO lifespan extension
  - [[SIRT1]]: Overexpressed in the hypothalamus in the BRASTO model
  - [[Orexin]]: Neuropeptide ligand for OX2R produced by neurons of the lateral hypothalamus
  - [[NF-κB]]: Age-related hypothalamic inflammation via NF-κB contributes to systemic aging
  - [[Inflammation]]: Hypothalamic inflammatory signaling drives metabolic and hormonal aging
