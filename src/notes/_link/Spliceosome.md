# Spliceosome

## Overview

The spliceosome is a large ribonucleoprotein complex that catalyzes the removal of introns from pre-mRNA, generating mature messenger RNAs. It assembles dynamically on nascent transcripts and is essential for eukaryotic gene expression. Like the [[SWI-SNF Complex]], the spliceosome was identified among the major nuclear interaction partners of [[cGAS]], indicating that noncanonical cGAS function extends into the regulation of RNA processing within the [[Nucleus]].

## Recruitment and Regulation by Nuclear cGAS

Noncanonical nuclear [[cGAS]] recruits spliceosome components through its [[Intrinsically Disordered Region|intrinsically disordered region]], which mediates [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|phase separation]] with chromatin and RNA-processing machinery. The cGAS IDR directly binds spliceosome components, suggesting that phase-separated cGAS condensates concentrate splicing factors and influence alternative splicing decisions. By scaffolding the spliceosome, cGAS may shape the transcriptome in ways independent of its canonical [[cGAS-STING Pathway|STING-dependent]] interferon output, adding an RNA-metabolism layer to its nuclear roles.

## Convergence with RNA-Binding Proteins and Disease

The recruitment of the spliceosome by cGAS parallels its scaffolding of [[RNA-Binding Proteins]], reinforcing the theme that cGAS is an organizational hub for nuclear RNA metabolism. Given that splicing defects and RBP mislocalization (e.g., [[TDP-43]]) are hallmarks of [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]], cGAS-mediated regulation of splicing could intersect with disease pathology. Disruption of this noncanonical axis may therefore contribute to the [[Neuroinflammation]] and transcriptomic dysregulation characteristic of chronic brain disorders, an area ripe for further investigation.
