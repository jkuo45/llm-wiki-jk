# IL-7

**Interleukin 7 (IL-7)** is a 25 kDa glycoprotein cytokine produced by stromal cells in the [[Thymus|thymus]] and [[Bone Marrow|bone marrow]]. It is essential for [[T Lymphocyte|T cell]] survival, differentiation, and homeostatic proliferation in both thymic and peripheral compartments.

## Structure and Receptor

- **Molecular weight:** 25 kDa glycoprotein
- **Receptor:** IL-7R (composed of IL-7Rα/CD127 and γc/CD132)
- **Expression:** IL-7R is expressed on lymphoid precursors, CD3⁻CD4⁻CD8⁻ (triple negative) thymocytes, CD4⁺CD8⁺ (double positive) thymocytes, and single-positive CD4 or CD8 T cells
- **Signaling:** JAK1/JAK3 → STAT5 → Bcl-2 upregulation (anti-apoptotic)

## Biological Functions

### Thymic effects
- Promotes survival and differentiation of immature triple-negative thymocytes
- Supports positive selection in the cortex
- Essential for TCR gene rearrangement during early [[Thymopoiesis|thymopoiesis]]

### Peripheral effects
- Regulates naïve and memory [[CD4+ T Cells|CD4]] and [[CD8 T cells|CD8]] T cell homeostasis
- Maintains T cell survival via Bcl-2 expression
- Expansion of both naïve and memory T cell pools upon exogenous administration

## Therapeutic Applications

Recombinant human IL-7 (**CYT 99 007**) has been tested in clinical settings:

- **Low toxicity** with dose-dependent sustained expansion of naïve and memory T cells
- Restored [[Thymopoiesis|thymopoiesis]] in IL-7-deficient mice
- Expanded CD8⁺ and CD4⁺ T cells in human trials (Rosenberg et al., 2006)
- No open trials focused specifically on thymus rejuvenation as of 2013

> [!info] Cross-talk network
> IL-7 production by [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|TECs]] is stimulated by [[IGF1|IGF-1]], linking [[Human Growth Hormone|growth hormone]] signaling to thymic function. This positions IL-7 as a downstream effector of GH-mediated thymic rejuvenation.
