# STX17

**STX17** is a single-helical Qa-type [[SNARE proteins|SNARE]] (syntaxin) encoded on human chromosome 9q31.1. It is the only mammalian syntaxin with a **double-hairpin C-terminal transmembrane (tail) anchor** rather than a single-pass membrane anchor, and that unusual topology lets it flip between two compartments without relocalizing its SNARE domain.

> [!info] Mechanism
> STX17 sits in the SNARE complex with [[SNAP29]] as the Q-SNARE and the lysosomal VAMPs (VAMP1/2, also called synaptobrevin) as R-SNAREs, forming the four-helix trans-SNARE bundle that pulls autophagosome and lysosome membranes into contact. SNARE zippering is not by itself sufficient to fuse membranes: it must be gated by the HOPS/Mon1–Ccz1–Bull and VARP complexes, which use STX17–SNAP29 as a landing site to position the complex for tethering and lipid mixing.

## Autophagosome maturation

The Itakura/Mizushima lab showed in 2012 that STX17 forms a **hairpin-type tail-anchored SNARE** that targets specifically to autophagosomes, fusing them with endosomes/lysosomes, and that STX17 is required for autophagosome maturation. Its recycling is controlled: cleavage at the N-terminus releases a soluble STX17 fragment that then regulates the reassembly of SNARE complexes on the lysosome. Because this fusion event is the rate-limiting step of [[Autophagic Flux]], STX17 is functionally equivalent to an autophagosome-lysosome gatekeeper — loss of STX17 produces autophagosome accumulation without a corresponding increase in cargo degradation.

## Other trafficking roles

STX17 was originally identified in 1998 in a screen for novel syntaxin/SNAP-25 family proteins and is abundant in steroidogenic cells, where it is implicated in smooth endoplasmic reticulum membrane dynamics. It cycles between the rough ER and the ER-Golgi intermediate compartment (ERGIC) and is required to maintain ERGIC and [[Golgi apparatus|Golgi]] architecture, working with syntaxin 5 in the HOPS complex for intra-Golgi and ER-to-Golgi vesicle fusion. It is also a component of COPII vesicle membranes and the mitochondrial-associated membrane.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> STX17 has no established monogenic human disease association. It was initially reported as a GWAS candidate in alopecia areata, and a follow-up study found no association between STX17 polymorphisms and cutaneous melanoma; neither claim survived as a causal finding. In horses, a duplication in intron 6 of STX17 has been associated with progressive graying, which is a genuine but species-specific finding.

## Connections

- [[SNAP29]] — SNAP29 is STX17's obligate Q-SNARE partner on the autophagosome and lysosome membranes. Knocking out either partner blocks autophagosome-lysosome fusion identically, which is why the two are usually discussed together.
- [[SNARE proteins]] — STX17 is one specific Qa-SNARE within the larger family, unusual for its hairpin anchoring and for recycling between two membrane compartments by the same protein.
- [[Autophagosome]] — STX17 localizes to the autophagosome membrane and provides the v-SNARE side of the fusion reaction with the lysosome.
- [[Lysosome]] — the fusion partner in autophagosome maturation; STX17 is recruited to lysosomes as the assembly site for the fusion machinery.
- [[Autophagy]] — STX17 is required for autophagosome maturation, so it is required for the completion, not the initiation, of the autophagic process.
- [[Autophagic Flux]] — the specific flux step STX17 gates. Flux readouts that count autophagosomes alone will not register an STX17 loss; lysosomal degradative readouts will.
- [[Golgi apparatus]] — STX17 cycles between ERGIC and Golgi with syntaxin 5, so loss of STX17 disrupts basal secretory architecture in addition to autophagy.
- [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] — the ER is one of STX17's two home compartments, and its function there includes smooth ER membrane dynamics in steroidogenic cells.
