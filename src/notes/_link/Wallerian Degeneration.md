# Wallerian Degeneration

**Wallerian degeneration** is the stereotyped program of axonal fragmentation that occurs distal to a nerve injury, after the axon is severed from its soma. It is the canonical readout of [[SARM1]]-dependent axonal degeneration.

## Mechanism

Axonal injury disrupts [[NMNAT2]] transport and triggers its proteasomal degradation (half-life ~30 min). NMN accumulates, raising the NMN/[[NAD+]] ratio, which relieves [[SARM1]] autoinhibition. Activated SARM1 cleaves NAD⁺, collapsing axonal NAD⁺ from ~400 μM to near zero within minutes. This halts [[Glycolysis]] (via GAPDH) and mitochondrial respiration, causing loss of membrane potential, calcium influx, and axonal fragmentation within 30–60 minutes.

## Modulation

- **SARM1 knockout** renders axons profoundly resistant — they remain intact for days to weeks after injury.
- **SARM1 inhibitors** ([[Disulfiram]], [[DSRM-3716]], [[GSK-428]], [[NB-4746]]) preserve axonal NAD⁺ and improve functional recovery in sciatic nerve crush/transection models.
- The [[CD38]] inhibitor 78c and NR supplementation also show axon-protective effects in CD38 KO models (Takaso et al. 2020).
