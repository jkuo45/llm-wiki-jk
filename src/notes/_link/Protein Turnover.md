# Protein Turnover

**Protein Turnover** is the dynamic steady state between protein synthesis and protein degradation that maintains [[Proteostasis|proteostasis]] across the body. Because protein synthesis and degradation are energetically expensive (each peptide bond consumes ATP equivalents), the aggregate rate of whole-body protein turnover is a meaningful component of resting energy metabolism.

## Relation to Resting Metabolic Rate

- Whole-body protein turnover correlates with [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]] beyond what [[Fat-Free Mass|lean mass]] predicts (Badaloo _et al_. 1996), and is one of the physiological correlates of the residual (heritable) variance in RMR.
- Skeletal muscle contributes disproportionately: muscle mass and muscle protein turnover dominate the total-body protein flux.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review lists protein turnover among the physiological and genetic factors correlating with residual variation in resting metabolic rate (Badaloo _et al_. 1996).

## Regulation

Protein turnover is regulated by nutrient availability (particularly amino acids), anabolic hormones (insulin, growth hormone, IGF-1), catabolic signals (glucocorticoids, cytokines), and physical activity — resistance exercise transiently increases both muscle protein synthesis and degradation, with synthesis favoured under adequate amino-acid supply.

## Clinical Relevance

Chronic disease, catabolic states, and calorie restriction alter protein turnover; maintaining protein intake and resistance training preserves muscle protein turnover and helps sustain RMR during weight loss.
