# Creatine Synthesis Deficiency

**Creatine synthesis deficiency** (also termed cerebral creatine deficiency syndrome, CCDS) refers to a family of rare, autosomal recessive inborn errors that reduce the body's ability to synthesize or transport [[Creatine]] into cells, especially the [[Brain]]. Three principal forms are recognized:

- **AGAT deficiency** — mutation in [[Arginine Glycine Amidinotransferase|arginine:glycine amidinotransferase (AGAT)]], the first committed step producing [[Guanidinoacetate (GAA)]].
- **GAMT deficiency** — mutation in [[Guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase|guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase (GAMT)]], blocking methylation of GAA to creatine; accumulates toxic GAA.
- **Creatine transporter deficiency (CRTR / SLC6A8)** — mutation in the [[Creatine Transporters (CRTR)|creatine transporter (SLC6A8)]], impairing cellular uptake; accounts for ~2% of intellectual disability in boys.

## Pathophysiology

Endogenous [[Creatine]] synthesis supplies roughly half of daily creatine needs; the remainder comes from diet (red meat, [[Fish]]). When synthesis or transport fails, tissue — particularly [[Brain]] — creatine and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|PCr]] levels fall. Because the [[Creatine Phosphate Shuttle]] is central to neuronal and muscle energy buffering, depletion produces:

- Intellectual disability / developmental delay
- [[Speech Development|Speech and language delay]]
- [[Epilepsy]]
- [[Autism]]-like features
- [[Movement Disorders|Movement disorders]] and [[Muscle Myopathies|myopathies]] (weakness)

## Clinical Management

High-dose, long-term [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine monohydrate supplementation]] (0.3–0.8 g/kg/day) is the standard therapy for AGAT and GAMT deficiencies and can normalize brain creatine and improve or stabilize neurodevelopmental outcomes — especially when initiated early in life. SLC6A8 (transporter) deficiency responds poorly to oral creatine because cellular uptake is blocked, though some benefit and adjunctive strategies are under study.

## Relevance

Detailed in [[Creatine in Health and Disease]], which concludes that long-term high-dose supplementation in synthesis-deficient individuals increases brain creatine/PCr and reduces deficit severity.
