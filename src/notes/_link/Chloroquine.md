# Chloroquine

## Overview

Chloroquine is a pharmacological agent (4-aminoquinoline) that functions as an [[Autophagy]] inhibitor by raising lysosomal pH and blocking autophagosome-lysosome fusion. It is used therapeutically in combination cancer regimens and as an [[Cancer|antimalarial]] drug, primarily for the prevention and treatment of malaria. In research settings, chloroquine is a widely used tool for studying autophagy-dependent processes including [[Mitophagy]]. Clinically, it is also identified as a potential trigger for acquired [[Methemoglobinemia]].

## Mechanism of Action

Chloroquine inhibits autophagy through several mechanisms:

- **Lysosomal alkalinization** — Chloroquine accumulates in lysosomes and raises their pH, impairing acid-dependent degradation
- **Autophagosome-lysosome fusion block** — Prevents the maturation of autophagosomes by inhibiting their fusion with lysosomes; this leads to accumulation of LC3-II and [[p62]] (SQSTM1) as autophagic flux is halted
- **Off-target effects** — When combined with [[Mdivi-1]], chloroquine can affect the [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|electron transport chain]]

> [!info] Autophagic flux readout
> Inhibition of autophagosome-lysosome fusion with chloroquine results in the accumulation of LC3-II and SQSTM1/p62, providing an experimental readout for blocked autophagic degradation.

## Adverse Effects

- **[[Methemoglobinemia]]** — A rare but possible side effect of chloroquine, which is listed among antimalarial medications that can induce acquired methemoglobinemia
- **[[G6PD deficiency]]** — Patients with this deficiency are at higher risk of complications from chloroquine

## Therapeutic Applications

### Cancer Therapy

Chloroquine is used in combination with other agents for enhanced anti-cancer effects:

- **Breast cancer** — In combination with isorhamnetin, enhances apoptosis in triple-negative [[Breast Cancer]] cells
- **Pancreatic cancer** — Used in combination with trametinib for [[Pancreatic Cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma]]
- **General oncology** — Inhibits protective autophagy that cancer cells use to survive chemotherapy

### Antimalarial Use

Chloroquine remains a first-line treatment for malaria in many regions, though resistance has limited its efficacy in some endemic areas.

## Research Applications

Chloroquine is widely used experimentally to:

- Assess the role of autophagy in cellular processes
- Evaluate autophagy-dependent cell death mechanisms
- Study mitophagy flux and mitochondrial quality control
