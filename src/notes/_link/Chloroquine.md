# Chloroquine

## Overview

Chloroquine is a pharmacological agent that functions as an [[Autophagy]] inhibitor by raising lysosomal pH and blocking autophagosome-lysosome fusion. It is used therapeutically in combination cancer regimens and as an antimalarial drug. In research settings, chloroquine is a widely used tool for studying autophagy-dependent processes including [[Mitophagy]].

## Mechanism of Action

Chloroquine inhibits autophagy through several mechanisms:

- **Lysosomal alkalinization** — Chloroquine accumulates in lysosomes and raises their pH, impairing acid-dependent degradation
- **Autophagosome-lysosome fusion block** — Prevents the maturation of autophagosomes by inhibiting their fusion with lysosomes
- **Off-target effects** — When combined with [[Mdivi-1]], chloroquine can affect the [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|electron transport chain]]

## Therapeutic Applications

### Cancer Therapy

Chloroquine is used in combination with other agents for enhanced anti-cancer effects:

- **Breast cancer** — In combination with isorhamnetin, enhances apoptosis in triple-negative [[Breast Cancer]] cells
- **Pancreatic cancer** — Used in combination with trametinib for [[Pancreatic Cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma]]
- **General oncology** — Inhibits protective autophagy that cancer cells use to survive chemotherapy

### Antimalarial Use

Chloroquine remains a first-line treatment for malaria in many regions, though resistance has limited its efficacy in some endemic areas.

## Research Applications

Chloroquine is widely used experimentally to:

- Assess the role of autophagy in cellular processes
- Evaluate autophagy-dependent cell death mechanisms
- Study mitophagy flux and mitochondrial quality control
