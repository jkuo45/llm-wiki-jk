# Age-related Hearing Loss

## Overview

Age-related Hearing Loss (AHL, presbycusis) is the most prevalent form of auditory impairment among older adults. It is a multifactorial, progressive condition involving cumulative [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]], and degeneration of sensory [[Hair Cells|cochlear hair cells]] and spiral ganglion neurons, manifesting as high-frequency hearing loss, speech discrimination difficulty, and social isolation.

## Pathophysiology & SIRT3 Connection

- Cumulative oxidative damage in [[Cochlea|cochlear]] cells is a core driver; [[Caloric Restriction|caloric restriction (CR)]] alleviates AHL by reducing this oxidative damage.
- The protective effect of CR is abolished in [[SIRT3|SIRT3]]-deficient mice, establishing SIRT3 as indispensable for CR-induced auditory resilience.
- Mechanistically, SIRT3 enhances [[IDH2|IDH2]] activity under CR, boosting mitochondrial [[NADPH|NADPH]] production and efficient [[ROS|ROS]] clearance — an antioxidant defense that preserves cochlear hair cell integrity and delays hearing loss progression.

## Clinical Relevance

- No effective interventions currently reverse AHL; SIRT3 activation is a promising therapeutic strategy to recapitulate CR benefits in the cochlea.
- SIRT3 is under investigation as a biomarker to predict AHL susceptibility.
