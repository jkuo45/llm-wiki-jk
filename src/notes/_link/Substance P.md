# Substance P

**Substance P** is an undecapeptide (11 residues) neuropeptide, sequence **Arg-Pro-Lys-Pro-Gln-Gln-Phe-Phe-Gly-Leu-Met-NH2** with a C-terminal amide, encoded by the human **TAC1** (preprotachykinin-1) gene on chromosome 7. It is a member of the **tachykinin** family, whose other mammalian members are neurokinin A (NKA) and neurokinin B, alongside the amphibian and avian peptides physalaemin and Kassinin.

It was discovered in 1931 by von Euler and Gaddum as a tissue extract causing intestinal contraction — the "P" stands for *preparation* or *powder* — and its structure was solved only in 1971. It is co-transmitted in vesicles with the excitatory amino acid [[Glutamate]].

## Receptor and signalling

The endogenous receptor is the **neurokinin-1 receptor (NK1R)**, a GPCR coupled principally to Gq and Gs. Binding activates phospholipase C, IP₃ and calcium, and adenylyl cyclase; PKC and MAPK (p38) are engaged downstream, giving cytokine induction via both NF-κB-dependent and independent routes.

> [!info] Agonist–receptor trafficking
> NK1R internalises after binding via the clathrin-dependent pathway to acidified endosomes, where SP dissociates and is degraded, and the receptor is re-expressed at the surface. The receptor's internalisation state is why SP-induced vasodilation in human skin **declines during continuous infusion** — tachyphylaxis to a locally released mediator.

SP acts at lower affinity on NK2R and NK3R, the receptors for the other tachykinins. Cytokines such as [[Interleukin 1β]] and [[TNFα]], and neurotrophic factors, induce both SP expression and NK1R transcription, and SP in turn induces the same cytokines — a reciprocal loop, which is the mechanistic reason SP is described as amplifying inflammatory signalling.

## Functions

**Nociception.** SP is the canonical transmitter of pain at the first synapse of the spinal dorsal horn. It is released from small-diameter, capsaicin-sensitive Aδ and C fibres in skin, muscle and joint, and it excites second-order dorsal horn neurons, either directly or via interneuron excitation. This transmission is glutamatergic-dependent: blocking NK1R alone does not block nociception because the same vesicles carry glutamate.

**Neurogenic inflammation.** Peripheral release of SP from sensory nerve terminals causes the axon-reflex **wheal-and-flare** — local vasodilation (endothelium-dependent, via [[Nitric Oxide]]) and plasma extravasation — without any mast cell degranulation required. It also drives bronchoconstriction via the non-adrenergic, non-cholinergic vagal pathway. SP is a direct trigger of mast cell degranulation and of TNFα/IL-6 production, so the two neurogenic and classical inflammatory pathways converge.

**Vasodilation.** SP is a potent vasodilator acting on endothelial NK1R with NO dependence; desensitisation with continued infusion is documented in human skin microdialysis.

**Emesis.** SP and NK1R are present in the area postrema, the chemoreceptor trigger zone of the medulla, and the SP–NK1R axis is the final common pathway for emesis from drugs, toxins, motion and radiation. This is the one indication where NK1R antagonism is fully established therapeutically.

**Other roles.** SP is involved in mood, anxiety, reinforcement, reward, respiratory rhythm generation, neurogenesis, dendritic arborisation, and long-term potentiation of aversive stimuli. In the hypothalamus, tachykinin release mediates defensive rage and aggressive biting, an effect conserved in *Drosophila* male-specific lunging behaviour. SP promotes cell proliferation, angiogenesis and — notably — tumour migration and metastasis.

## Clinical significance

SP levels and NK1R density are elevated in a striking number of conditions: sickle cell crisis (rising further during vaso-occlusive crisis), inflammatory bowel disease, major [[Depression]], fibromyalgia and other rheumatologic pain states, HIV/AIDS, respiratory syncytial virus infection, atopic [[Pruritus]] in atopic dermatitis, and several cancers including glioma, breast and hepatoblastoma. NK1R is overexpressed in breast cancer associated with bone marrow metastasis.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The translational record on NK1R antagonists is a cautionary tale. Despite a strong preclinical rationale, NK1R antagonists **failed** as analgesics in well-conducted clinical trials, failed in depression (osanetant), failed in irritable bowel syndrome for pain endpoints (though they did reduce anxiety in one trial), and failed in chronic pruritus. The approved drugs — aprepitant, netupitant, rolapitant and fosnetupitant — are all emetics. Serum SP concentrations cannot yet be used to diagnose disease or gauge severity, and the direction of causality (cause vs. consequence of disease) is unresolved in essentially every condition where SP is elevated.

## Connections

- [[Inflammatory Pain]] — SP is the afferent transmitter that carries nociceptive and inflammatory signal from the periphery into the spinal cord, where it drives the central sensitisation state that this note describes. SP is one of the molecular handles on the process, and the failed clinical track record of NK1R antagonists is the process's caution about single-target analgesia.
- [[Magnolol]] — Magnolol and honokiol (biphenyl neolignans from *Magnolia*) have been reported to reduce SP release and SP-mediated neurogenic inflammation in vitro, partly via TRP channel blockade. The evidence is preclinical and the mechanism attribution is indirect.
- [[Honokiol]] — Honokiol's GABAergic, TRPV1-antagonist and neuropeptide-modulating actions converge with SP biology in the same pain and neuroinflammation literature; the vault's honokiol document is the natural-product side of this.
- [[_document_ - Neuro-Modulating Effects of Honokiol A Review]] — This review supplies the honokiol neuro-modulation framing that this note's document list is built around, and specifically treats SP among the neuropeptides whose release honokiol is reported to modulate.
- [[Histamine]] — Histamine is the co-transmitted peptide in the same small-diameter nociceptive afferents as SP, and both act at NK1R on trigeminal and spinal second-order neurons. The CGRP/SP pair is the clinically successful half of this system (CGRP antagonists work for migraine; NK1R antagonists do not work for pain).
- [[TRPM2]] — TRPM2, a nonselective cation channel coupled to [[Ca2+]] signalling, is activated by ADP-ribose and oxidative stress and is on the same afferent terminals that release SP, and the "capsaicin-sensitive" designation of SP fibres is functionally defined by TRPV1. TRPV1 is the upstream sensor whose activation triggers SP release.
- [[Nitric Oxide]] — SP-induced vasodilation is endothelium-dependent and NO-requiring, making the axon reflex wheal-and-flare a neuropeptide→NO→vasodilation chain.
- [[Interleukin 1β]] — Pro-inflammatory cytokines induce both SP expression and NK1R transcription, while SP induces those same cytokines. This bidirectional loop is the mechanistic reason SP is a hub in chronic inflammation rather than a peripheral messenger.
- [[TNFα]] — Another cytokine that induces SP and NK1R; SP in turn drives TNFα production through NF-κB, completing the amplification loop.
- [[Neuroinflammation]] — SP is a well-documented amplifier of neuroinflammation, so neurogenic and mast-cell-mediated inflammation converge on the same effector cells. It is one of the routes by which the SP system recruits classical inflammatory cells.
- [[Glutamate]] — SP is co-packaged with glutamate in the same synaptic vesicles of primary afferents, and both act on the same dorsal horn second-order neurons. This co-transmission is the structural reason NK1R antagonism alone is a weak analgesic.
- [[Central Sensitization]] — Sustained SP-driven C-fibre input is one of the mechanisms that wind up dorsal horn neurons, making this note the transmitter-level entry point to central sensitisation. Its clinical manipulation has been thoroughly tried and largely failed as analgesia, which remains an active puzzle rather than a settled question.
