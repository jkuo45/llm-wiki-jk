# LIPIN1

**LIPIN1** encodes **lipin-1**, a ~89 kDa protein with two apparently unrelated
functions that are the reason it is interesting: it is a lipid enzyme *and* a
transcriptional coactivator. The gene produces three isoforms — lipin-1α,
lipin-1β, and lipin-1γ — differing in C-terminal length and tissue
distribution, produced by alternative splicing and transcription start sites.

## The catalytic function

Lipin-1 is **phosphatidic acid phosphatase 1** (PAP1), the enzyme that converts
phosphatidic acid to diacylglycerol (DAG) in the final step of
[[Lipid Synthesis|glycerolipid synthesis]], immediately before
triacylglycerol assembly. It is located in the cytosol and on the cytosolic
face of the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]], recruited to membranes by its
interaction with the small G-protein small G-protein regulators and by
SREBP-dependent transcription
induction. It has a C-terminal phosphatase domain with the
`D(X)2D(X)2D` motif characteristic of haloacid dehalogenase superfamily phosphatases, plus an N-terminal coactivator domain separated by a
proteolytically sensitive linker.

> [!info] The dual-function problem
> Lipin-1's two functions are separable experimentally. N-terminal fragments
> lacking the catalytic domain retain coactivator activity, and catalytic-dead
> mutants can still coactivate transcription. This is unusual and is why
> LIPIN1 is a textbook example of a protein with a catalytic and a scaffolding
> role in the same molecule.

## The coactivator function

Lipin-1 is a coactivator of **[[PPAR-alpha]]**, working with
[[PGC-1α]] to drive transcription of fatty acid oxidation genes
(acyl-CoA oxidase, medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase, carnitine
palmitoyltransferase) and mitochondrial biogenesis genes. It binds PPARα
without binding DNA directly — it is a coactivator recruited by the receptor
rather than a sequence-specific transcription factor. It also coactivates
[[SREBP1]]-driven lipogenic transcription and interacts with
[[FOXO]] and [[eIF2α]]-linked stress-response pathways.

> [!warning] The paradox that defines LIPIN1 biology
> Lipin-1 is **both** a positive regulator of triglyceride synthesis *and* a
> positive regulator of fatty acid oxidation — pathways that are normally
> reciprocally opposed. Its level, and its subcellular localisation, appear to
> be the switch. Membrane-associated, hyper-phosphorylated lipin-1 functions as
> PAP1 and promotes triglyceride synthesis; cytosolic, dephosphorylated lipin-1
> acts as the PPARα coactivator. Loss of the phosphorylation that governs this
> redistribution removes both functions simultaneously, and this is the
> mechanistic core of the `fld` (fatty liver dystrophy) mouse phenotype.

## Model systems and human disease

- **The `fld` mouse** — a spontaneous mutation in LIPIN1 producing a
  truncated, non-functional protein. Phenotype: severe hepatic steatosis,
  insulin resistance, reduced adipose mass, myopathy, and a shortened
  lifespan. Lipin-1 is therefore non-redundant.
- **Congenital generalized lipodystrophy type 6** — biallelic *LIPIN1*
  mutations produce generalised loss of adipose tissue from birth, with
  extreme hypertriglyceridaemia, hepatomegaly, and early-onset diabetes.
- **Familial partial lipodystrophy type 6** — heterozygous variants give
  limb-girdle fat loss with relative sparing of the face and distal limbs.
- **Rhabdomyolysis** — reported in severe biallelic cases, consistent with the
  myopathy seen in `fld` mice.

> [!info] Uncertain territory
> LIPIN1 has been reported as an oncogene upregulated in breast, prostate, and
  pancreatic cancer, where increased lipid synthesis supports proliferation,
> and lipin-1 phosphorylation status is proposed as a prognostic marker. Much
  of this rests on correlative human tumour tissue data and on model systems;
> causal evidence and clinical validation are limited, and the same protein
  that promotes lipogenesis in tumours promotes fatty acid oxidation in
  normal fasting liver.

## Connections

- [[SREBP1]] — Sterol regulatory element-binding protein 1 is the master
  transcriptional regulator of de novo lipogenesis, and lipin-1 both
  coactivates SREBP1-driven transcription and executes the final
  phosphatase step of the pathway it induces. The feedback between the
  transcription factor and the enzyme it controls is what allows lipogenic flux
  to be coordinated rather than merely switched. The stub's sole inbound
  document link is retained in the Documents section.

- [[SREBP-1c]] — The specific isoform mediating glucose-responsive
  lipogenesis in liver and adipose. Lipin-1 is induced downstream of it, so
  LIPIN1 is both a target of the lipogenic programme and a feed-forward
  amplifier of it.

- [[PPAR-alpha]] — The nuclear receptor whose coactivation by lipin-1 drives
  fatty acid oxidation. This is the flip side of the same molecule: the
  oxidation arm of lipid catabolism, opposing the synthesis arm.

- [[PGC-1α]] — Works with lipin-1 as a coactivator of mitochondrial
  biogenesis and oxidative metabolism genes. Together they are the
  fasting-state transcriptional module.

- [[Lipolysis]] — The catabolic counterpart of lipin-1's function. Lipolysis
  releases fatty acids from [[Lipid Droplet|triglyceride stores]]; lipin-1's
  PAP1 activity runs the *anabolic* direction. The fasting/feeding state
  determines which dominates, and lipin-1 is one of the switch points.

- [[Lipophagy]] — Selective autophagy of lipid droplets delivers triglycerides
  to lysosomes for lipolysis. Lipin-1 sits upstream of the pool that lipophagy
  mobilises, so droplet flux and lipin-1 activity are two coupled variables.

- [[Adipose Tissue]] — Lipin-1 is required for adipocyte differentiation and
  lipid storage competence. Its loss causes lipodystrophy — the absence of
  functional adipose storage, which paradoxically causes severe
  hypertriglyceridaemia because lipid is displaced into blood and liver.

- [[Insulin Resistance]] — The `fld` mouse is insulin resistant with hepatic
  steatosis, and human lipodystrophy patients are severely insulin resistant.
  This makes LIPIN1 a rare example where a lipogenic enzyme's loss causes
  insulin resistance, which is a useful counterpoint to the usual direction of
  the lipogenesis–insulin resistance association.

- [[NAFLD]] — Hepatic steatosis is the central phenotype of the `fld` model.
  Dysregulated lipid deposition in liver is a primary event in NAFLD, not only a
  consequence of insulin resistance.

- [[Metabolism]] — LIPIN1 is a compact node in the fasting/feeding
  metabolic switch: it is a transcription factor coactivator in the fasted
  state (oxidation) and a biosynthetic enzyme in the fed state
  (synthesis), switching by subcellular localisation and phosphorylation.
