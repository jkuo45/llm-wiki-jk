# Indole-3-propionic acid

**Indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)** is a tryptophan-derived metabolite produced **exclusively by commensal gut bacteria** — predominantly [[Clostridium sporogenes]] — which reduces ~4–6% of dietary tryptophan via the indole-pyruvate pathway. Absorbed by intestinal epithelial cells, it undergoes minimal hepatic metabolism and reaches the systemic circulation at ~1 µM in humans (~5 µM in rodents). IPA is one of the most potent endogenous radical scavengers known (effective at blocking lipid peroxidation from hydroxyl radicals), and acts as a ligand of the [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor|AhR]] and the [[Pregnane X Receptor|PXR]] — the two receptors through which most of its transcriptional effects are mediated.

> [!important]
> IPA is not merely an antioxidant: its receptor-mediated (AhR/PXR) actions regulate mitochondrial function, epithelial barrier integrity, immune tolerance, and — critically — the expression of [[SIRT3]] in the heart.

## Origin and pharmacokinetics

- **Producer organisms**: [[Clostridium sporogenes]] (best characterized), with contributions from *Lactobacillus reuteri*, *Akkermansia muciniphila*, and other Clostridia.
- **Transport**: absorbed by intestinal epithelium → portal circulation → minimal first-pass metabolism → systemic levels ~1 µM (human) / ~5 µM (rodent).
- **Diet/lifestyle modifiers**: Mediterranean diet, dietary fibre, and mulberry **increase** plasma IPA; diabetes, obesity, dyslipidaemia, and atherosclerosis **decrease** it. IPA levels also decline with age.
- Unlike other microbial tryptophan derivatives (indoxyl sulfate, indole-3-acetic acid), IPA does not accumulate in renal failure — consistent with a favourable safety profile.

## Receptor-mediated signalling: AhR and PXR

- **[[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor|AhR]]**: IPA binding → cytosolic complex dissociation → nuclear translocation → transcriptional regulation of xenobiotic-defence, anti-inflammatory, and (in heart) **Sirt3** gene expression. AhR activation by IPA also inhibits [[NF-κB|NF-κB]]/[[NLRP3]] inflammasome signalling in multiple tissues.
- **[[Pregnane X Receptor|PXR]]**: IPA activation upregulates barrier genes (e.g. **B3galt5**), suppresses NF-κB, inhibits Th1 responses, and mediates endothelium-dependent vasodilation and BBB protection.
- Net outcomes are cell-type- and context-dependent (AhR shows antagonistic pleiotropy in aging), but IPA consistently behaves as a protective, anti-inflammatory agonist.

## Gut barrier and immune modulation

- Upregulates tight-junction and barrier proteins ([[Occludin]], Claudin-1, ZO-1, Mucin 2/4) via AhR; strengthens **B3galt5**-mediated mucus defence via PXR.
- Decreases FITC-dextran gut permeability in mice; increases goblet-cell number and mucosal thickness.
- Suppresses proinflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-12, IL-13, MCP-1); promotes Treg/ILC3 balance and Th1/Th17 apoptosis (relevant to IBD).
- Negative correlation of serum IPA with hsCRP in type 2 diabetes.

## IPA–SIRT3–NAD axis in HFpEF

> [!important]
> Source: Wang et al., *Circulation Research* 2024;134:371–389 (doi:10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.123.322381; PMC10923103)
> In the "two-hit" HFpEF mouse model (high-fat diet + NOS inhibitor), IPA was markedly reduced in both plasma and heart tissue. **Dietary IPA supplementation** attenuated diastolic dysfunction, metabolic remodelling, oxidative stress, inflammation, gut dysbiosis, and intestinal barrier damage. Mechanistically, IPA:
> 1. **Bound AhR** → nuclear translocation → restored transcription of **[[SIRT3]]** (Sirt3 knockdown abolished IPA's benefit in vivo);
> 2. **Suppressed [[NNMT]]** (secondarily to inflammation suppression) → preserved [[Nicotinamide|NAM]] → enhanced the **NAD salvage pathway** (NAM → NMN → [[NAD+]]) → restored cardiac NAD⁺/NADH;
> 3. Together raised SIRT3 activity (NAD⁺-dependent) and improved myocardial bioenergetics.
> IPA, NAM, and combined IPA+NAM produced ~equivalent protection, indicating a **common NAD-centric pathway**. IPA levels were significantly reduced in HFpEF patients in **two independent human cohorts** (Cleveland Clinic case/control; Alfred Hospital HFpEF study), and higher IPA quartiles associated with lower odds of HFpEF after multivariable adjustment.

Key conceptual points:

- This defines a **microbiota–mitochondria (gut–heart) axis**: microbial metabolite → nuclear receptor → sirtuin expression + NAD⁺ availability → mitochondrial deacetylation/energy transduction → diastolic function.
- IPA acts **at two levels** in the heart: direct (AhR→SIRT3 transcription) and indirect (anti-inflammatory → NNMT suppression → NAD salvage). NNMT suppression was further validated by NNMT antisense oligonucleotides, which independently improved diastolic function.
- Consistent with prior work showing NMN/NAD⁺ replenishment improves HFpEF diastolic function (Hill laboratory "two-hit" model), IPA offers a **microbiome-routed alternative to NAD⁺ precursor supplementation**.

## Other cardiometabolic effects

- **Cardiomyocyte mitochondria** (Gesper et al., RWTH Aachen): acute IPA **increased** maximal mitochondrial respiration ~21% in HL-1 cardiomyocytes and dose-dependently improved contractility in isolated perfused mouse hearts (+27% at 1 µM to +94% at 100 µM); chronic high exposure could impair respiration (−19%) — biphasic, dose/time-dependent effects with possible fatty-acid-oxidation involvement.
- **Doxorubicin cardiomyopathy** (Li et al., 2023): IPA bound and inhibited **HDAC6**, blunting the HDAC6/NOX2 axis → less ROS, apoptosis, and inflammation in AC16 cardiomyocytes.
- **Diabetic kidney disease** (2024): serum IPA reduced in DKD patients (correlating with HbA1c, UACR, eGFR); supplementation protected glomerular endothelial-cell mitochondria by **preventing ubiquitin-proteasomal degradation of SIRT1** → PGC-1α deacetylation/nuclear translocation → mitochondrial biogenesis and antioxidant gene expression.
- **Type 2 diabetes**: higher plasma IPA associated with lower T2D risk; IPA-producing bacteria reduced in T2D patients; improves glucose homeostasis and insulin sensitivity in mice.

## Neuroprotection (gut–brain axis)

- Potent scavenger of hydroxyl radicals; blocks Aβ-induced lipid peroxidation and neuronal death at 1 µM.
- Improves cognition and reduces Aβ pathology in AD mice (via AhR → NF-κB/NLRP3 suppression); low serum IPA predicts acute stroke and poor prognosis; plasma IPA decreased in Huntington's disease.
- Promotes **nerve regeneration** (Serger et al., *Nature* 2022) and supports adult neurogenesis via AhR (tryptophan-metabolizing microbes, PNAS 2021).
- Inhibits RAGE–JAK2–STAT3 neuroinflammation; activates AMPK/SIRT1 in oxidative-stress neurons; PXR-mediated BBB protection.
- Orally administered IPA raises central **[[Kynurenic Acid|kynurenic acid]]** levels, adding a neuroprotective kynurenine-shunt effect.
- A pilot clinical trial exists (iPROACT-Pilot, NCT06674018); larger trials in AD/PD/stroke have been proposed.

## Clinical and translational relevance

- HFpEF: microbiome-composition modification or **dietary IPA supplementation** as therapy (preclinical + human associative evidence).
- Suggested as add-on therapy to delay AD/PD onset in high-risk populations and limit ischemic-stroke damage.
- Bone: promotes osteoblast differentiation via **Tfam** upregulation and inhibits osteoclastogenesis (NF-κB), protecting bone density (2025 review).
- Caveats: biphasic dose effects on mitochondria, small human HFpEF cohorts, and context-dependent AhR pleiotropy all warrant trial-scale validation.

## Connections

- [[SIRT3]] — IPA restores cardiac SIRT3 expression via AhR binding; SIRT3 is the necessary downstream effector for IPA's protection against HFpEF diastolic dysfunction
- [[NAD+]] — IPA suppresses NNMT, preserving nicotinamide and fuelling the NAD salvage pathway, raising NAD⁺ available to SIRT3
- [[NNMT]] — transcriptionally suppressed by IPA; NNMT overexpression depletes NAD⁺ and promotes steatosis/fibrosis
- [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor]] — principal receptor mediating IPA's transcriptional effects, including Sirt3 induction, NF-κB/NLRP3 suppression, and barrier gene expression
- [[Pregnane X Receptor]] — second IPA receptor; mediates barrier integrity (B3galt5), vasodilation, BBB protection, and anti-inflammatory gene programs
- [[Clostridium sporogenes]] — primary bacterial producer of IPA from dietary tryptophan
- [[Tryptophan]] — dietary precursor; microbiota convert ~4–6% of it to indoles, IPA chief among the circulating ones
- [[Nicotinamide]] — substrate spared by IPA-driven NNMT suppression; NAM co-administration mimics IPA's cardiac benefit
- [[Heart Failure]] — HFpEF is the disease context where IPA deficiency is demonstrated and IPA supplementation is protective
- [[Kynurenic Acid]] — oral IPA raises central KYNA, a neuroprotective kynurenine-pathway metabolite
- [[SIRT1]] — in diabetic kidney disease, IPA prevents ubiquitin-mediated SIRT1 degradation → PGC-1α activation and mitochondrial biogenesis
- [[Mitochondria]] — IPA modulates cardiomyocyte respiration (biphasic) and protects mitochondrial function across heart, kidney, and bone
- [[SIRT3-SIRT4 Ratio]] context: IPA boosts the mitochondrial-sirtuin arm of redox balance via SIRT3
