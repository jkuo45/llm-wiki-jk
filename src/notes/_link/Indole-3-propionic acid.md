# Indole-3-propionic acid

**Indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)** is a tryptophan-derived metabolite produced by commensal gut bacteria (e.g., *Clostridium sporogenes*), absorbed into the circulation where it acts as a potent radical-scavenging antioxidant and aryl hydrocarbon receptor ligand. IPA levels decline with age and are modulated by diet and microbiome composition, positioning it as a representative of the microbiota–host metabolic axis that affects systemic health. It has been investigated for neuroprotective, metabolic, and cardiovascular activities.

## IPA and SIRT3 in HFpEF

Pharmacological potentiation of [[SIRT3]] via [[NAD+|NAD+]]-dependent pathways and gut-derived metabolites such as indole-3-propionic acid has been shown to enhance myocardial bioenergetics and improve diastolic function, especially in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF)[221]-[223]. The proposed axis is a microbiota–mitochondria connection: microbial IPA supports mitochondrial NAD+ availability and/or SIRT3 activity, improving cardiac energy transduction and relaxation in the metabolically stressed failing heart.

## Clinical relevance

HFpEF is a highly prevalent aging-related syndrome with few effective therapies. Microbiota-derived SIRT3 modulators such as IPA represent a translational avenue — either as dietary/microbiome interventions or as supplements — for restoring myocardial mitochondrial function in older patients with diastolic dysfunction.
