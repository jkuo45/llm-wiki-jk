# Tryptophan

**Tryptophan** (Trp) is an essential amino acid in mammals, meaning it must be obtained from the diet. It serves as a precursor for several critical biomolecules including [[Serotonin]], melatonin, and [[NAD+]].

## Metabolic Fate

Approximately 95% of dietary tryptophan (outside of protein synthesis) is catabolized through the [[Kynurenine Pathway]] (KP), which ultimately produces [[NAD+]]. The remaining ~5% enters the serotonin biosynthesis pathway via tryptophan hydroxylase.

### Kynurenine Pathway Route
- Rate-limiting step: [[IDO1]] or [[TDO]] converts Trp to [[N-Formylkynurenine]]
- Downstream products: [[Kynurenine]], [[Kynurenic Acid]], [[3-Hydroxykynurenine]], [[Quinolinic Acid]], and ultimately [[NAD+]]

### Serotonin Route
- Tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH) converts Trp to 5-hydroxy-tryptophan (5-HTP)
- Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AAAD) converts 5-HTP to [[Serotonin]]
- Serotonin is further metabolized to melatonin

## Physiological Roles

- **Protein synthesis:** One of 20 standard amino acids
- **Neurotransmitter precursor:** Rate-limiting precursor for [[Serotonin]] and melatonin
- **NAD+ biosynthesis:** Primary substrate for de novo [[NAD+]] synthesis via the [[Kynurenine Pathway]]
- **Immune modulation:** Regulates [[T cell|T-cell]] function through [[IDO1]]-mediated depletion and [[Kynurenine]]-[[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor|AhR]] signaling

## Nutritional Sources

Rich dietary sources include turkey, chicken, eggs, fish, dairy products, oats, bananas, dried dates, and various seeds and nuts.

## Clinical Relevance

Tryptophan depletion is a mechanism of immune suppression in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]] (via [[IDO1]]). Disrupted Trp metabolism is implicated in [[Depression]], [[Neurodegeneration]], and [[Inflammation]].
