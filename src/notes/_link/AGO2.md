# AGO2

**AGO2** (Argonaute 2; EIF2C2) is the core catalytic component of the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). It loads [[MicroRNA|microRNAs]] and small interfering RNAs, uses their guide strands to recognize complementary mRNAs, and executes silencing through mRNA cleavage (via its PIWI-domain slicer activity), translational repression, and deadenylation. Beyond RNA silencing, AGO2 participates in transcriptional regulation, RNA splicing, and stress-responsive RNA granule biology, making it a central hub of post-transcriptional gene control in essentially all cell types.

## AGO2/cytochrome b axis in diabetic cardiomyopathy

In [[Diabetic Cardiomyopathy|diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM)]], [[SIRT3]] activation improves mitochondrial respiratory capacity and suppresses oxidative stress, primarily through modulation of the AGO2/cytochrome b (CYTB) signaling pathway[237]. This regulatory mechanism uncouples excessive glucose levels from [[Electron Transport Chain|ETC]] dysfunction; SIRT3 downregulation leads to breakdown of this axis, impairing ETC activity and accelerating DCM progression[244],[245]. The proposed model links a microRNA-pathway effector (AGO2) to mitochondrial electron transport (cytochrome b is an mtDNA-encoded [[Electron Transport Chain|complex III]] subunit), exemplifying SIRT3's control over mitochondrial-nuclear/RNA-level crosstalk in metabolic cardiomyopathy.

## Clinical relevance

The AGO2/CYTB axis identifies RNA-silencing machinery as an intermediary of SIRT3's cardiometabolic protection, suggesting that restoration of SIRT3 in diabetic hearts re-engages this axis to normalize mitochondrial respiration.
