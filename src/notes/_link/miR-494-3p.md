# miR-494-3p

**miR-494-3p** is a microRNA (3p arm of the miR-494 stem-loop) that regulates gene expression post-transcriptionally by binding seed sequences in target mRNAs, typically promoting mRNA degradation or translational repression. miR-494 is induced by stressors in multiple tissues and has been implicated in both tumor progression and tissue injury, with target repertoires spanning cell-cycle, apoptosis, and redox genes.

## Targeting SIRT3 in senescence and COPD

Oxidative stress-induced upregulation of microRNA-494-3p leads to decreased [[SIRT3]] levels, establishing a feedback loop that further exacerbates senescence[48],[49]. In the context of COPD, miR-494-3p directly targets SIRT3 in small airway epithelial cells under oxidative stress, leading to downregulation of SIRT3 levels, increased expression of [[p27]], and accelerated cellular senescence — contributing to COPD pathophysiology[49]. Because SIRT3 normally restrains ROS and senescence, miR-494-3p-mediated SIRT3 silencing creates a self-amplifying oxidative-senescent loop in the aging lung.

## Clinical relevance

miR-494-3p is a candidate biomarker and therapeutic target in COPD and age-related diseases; anti-miR strategies aimed at preserving SIRT3 expression represent a translational direction complementary to pharmacological SIRT3 activation.
