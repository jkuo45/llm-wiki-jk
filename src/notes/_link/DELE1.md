# DELE1

**Overview:** DELE1 (death ligand signal enhancer 1) is a mitochondrial protein that functions as a key relay in the mitochondrial-to-cytosolic stress-signaling axis. It couples the detection of mitochondrial dysfunction at the inner membrane to activation of the cytosolic [[Integrated Stress Response]] (ISR), enabling cells to adapt translation and gene expression to organelle stress.

**Mechanism of action:** Under basal conditions, full-length DELE1 is imported into mitochondria. When mitochondrial homeostasis is disrupted—through loss of membrane potential, protein import failure, accumulation of misfolded proteins, or heme depletion—the inner-membrane protease [[OMA1]] is activated and cleaves DELE1. This proteolytic processing generates a short C-terminal fragment (S-DELE1) that is released into the cytosol. There, S-DELE1 binds and activates the heme-regulated eIF2α kinase [[HRI]] (EIF2AK1). Activated HRI phosphorylates the translation initiation factor [[eIF2α]], which globally attenuates cap-dependent protein synthesis while selectively promoting translation of stress-adaptive mRNAs, most notably [[ATF4]].

**The OMA1–DELE1–HRI axis:** Together, OMA1, DELE1, and HRI constitute a defined signaling module that transmits [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] to the ISR. This pathway allows the cell to slow protein production (reducing the folding burden on stressed mitochondria) and to launch transcriptional programs for metabolic remodeling, redox defense, and mitochondrial quality control. Depending on the intensity and duration of stress, the outcome ranges from adaptive recovery to induction of [[Apoptosis]].

**Physiological and disease relevance:** By governing how cells respond to mitochondrial stress, the DELE1 pathway is implicated in neurodegeneration, cardiomyopathy, metabolic disease, and other conditions marked by mitochondrial impairment. It is of growing interest as a therapeutic node for tuning the strength of the integrated stress response.
