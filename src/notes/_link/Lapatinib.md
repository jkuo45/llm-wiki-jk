# Lapatinib

**Lapatinib** (Tykerb; development code GW572016) is an orally bioavailable,
reversible, ATP-competitive [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor|tyrosine kinase inhibitor]]
selective for the intracellular kinase domains of **EGFR/HER1** and **HER2**. It is
a member of the 4-anilinoquinazoline class, structurally related to
[[Gefitinib]] and [[Erlotinib]].

## Mechanism of action

> [!info] Dual EGFR/HER2 inhibition
> Lapatinib binds the ATP-binding pocket of the EGFR and HER2 intracellular
> tyrosine kinase domains, occupying the same site as ATP and blocking
> phosphorylation. Because HER2 homodimers and EGFR/HER2 heterodimers transduce
> signalling through both receptors, dual blockade is intended to shut down signalling
> that either inhibitor alone would leave partly open.

Downstream output is the canonical [[Receptor Tyrosine Kinase|RTK]] arm set —
[[RAS]]/[[RAF]]/[[MEK]]/[[ERK]] (MAPK) and [[PI3K]]/[[AKT]]/[[mTOR]] — which lapatinib
suppresses by disabling the receptor-level input. This attenuates proliferation,
survival and, notably for HER2-driven tumours, [[EMT|epithelial–mesenchymal
transition]] and motility.

Kinase selectivity is imperfect. Off-targets reported at clinically achievable
concentrations include [[Insulin Receptor|IR]] and [[Insulin Receptor Substrate-1|IRS-1]]
(which plausibly contributes to lapatinib-associated hyperglycaemia) and [[EGFR]]/
[[ErbB]] family members beyond HER1/HER2. Notably, lapatinib retains activity against
some [[EGFR]] mutants that confer resistance to first-generation EGFR TKIs in
[[Lung Cancer|non-small cell lung cancer]] — the mechanistic reason for the
`EMLA` combination arm — although lapatinib itself is not approved for that indication.

## Pharmacokinetics

Oral absorption is incomplete and food-dependent; bioavailability is roughly 30%
without food and near 80% with a high-fat meal, so the drug is recommended with food.
The parent compound is extensively metabolised, predominantly by [[Cytochrome P450|CYP3A4/5]],
to several O-demethylated and N-dealkylated metabolites, all of which are
pharmacologically active. Lapatinib strongly inhibits and is a substrate of CYP3A4,
which is the basis of its many CYP3A4-mediated interaction warnings (azole antifungals,
clarithromycin, grapefruit juice, strong CYP3A4 inducers such as carbamazepine and
rifampicin). Elimination is predominantly faecal, with a terminal half-life of about
24 hours at steady state — long enough for once- or twice-daily dosing.

## Clinical use

Approved indications have narrowed as HER2-targeted regimens matured:

- **HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer**, in combination with
  [[Capecitabine]]; in combination with [[Letrozole]] for postmenopausal patients with
  hormone-receptor-positive disease; and in combination with [[Trastuzumab]] in
  heavily pretreated patients.
- The first-line trastuzumab-based antibody combinations displaced lapatinib from
  first-line use; the 78-week PFS benefit of the [[Pertuzumab]]/trastuzumab/
  docetaxel regimen, and later [[Trastuzumab Deruxtecan]] in the metastatic setting,
  are why lapatinib is now largely a later-line agent.
- It remains a reference agent for the *mechanism* of HER2 blockade and a comparator
  arm in resistance studies, and is used in [[Colon Cancer]] off-label in some
  HER2-amplified settings.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat — cardiotoxicity and diarrhoea
> HER2 blockade impairs cardiomyocyte survival signalling (HER2 and
> neuregulin-1/ErbB4 signalling is required for cardiomyocyte homeostasis and repair
> after injury), so HER2-targeted agents can reduce left ventricular ejection
> fraction. Lapatinib causes a *decline* in LVEF in a substantial minority of
> patients, and unlike trastuzumab its effect is often **not reversible on
> discontinuation** — a distinction that matters when sequencing. Cardiac function
> should be monitored by echocardiography at baseline and periodically.
>
> Grade 3–4 diarrhoea and skin rash are the most common dose-limiting toxicities;
> rash is mechanistically plausible given EGFR blockade and is managed
> supportively or with dose reduction/interruption. Lapatinib's off-target IR
> inhibition produces a low but real rate of hyperglycaemia.
>
> A further practical point: lapatinib penetrates the central nervous system poorly,
> unlike the small-molecule HER2 inhibitors dosed at high exposure, so brain
> metastases are a weak point in its coverage.

## Resistance mechanisms

Secondary resistance is well characterised:

- **[[PI3K]] pathway activation** — activating [[PIK3CA]] mutations and PTEN loss
  bypass receptor-level blockade; the most common route.
- **[[HER2]] reactivation** — extracellular-domain truncation, kinase-domain
  mutations, and re-amplification.
- **Axon guidance cues** — expression of [[Semaphorin 3c|SEMA3C]] and [[CRMP1]] via a
  MET-dependent programme drives lapatinib resistance in [[HER2]]-amplified gastric
  cancer, and MET amplification itself confers resistance to HER2 blockade more
  broadly.
- **Efflux and target-site changes** — ABC transporter upregulation.

## Connections

- [[HER2]] — Lapatinib's dominant clinical target. HER2 is amplified or
  overexpressed in roughly 15–20% of breast cancers and a smaller fraction of
  gastric and colorectal cancers, and receptor-level blockade is the shared
  therapeutic logic across lapatinib, trastuzumab and pertuzumab.
- [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors]] — Lapatinib is the canonical example of a reversible
  4-anilinoquinazoline ATP-competitive inhibitor, contrasting with covalent
  third-generation EGFR inhibitors such as osimertinib that bind the covalent
  C797 position.
- [[MET]] — MET amplification is a documented bypass-resistance mechanism for
  HER2 blockade including lapatinib, and the SEMA3C/CRMP1 axis links the two
  receptor systems directly.
- [[Cardiotoxicity]] — HER2-family blockade impairs the neuregulin-1 survival
  signalling cardiomyocytes depend on, producing LVEF decline; lapatinib's
  effect is often non-reversible, unlike trastuzumab's.
- [[Capecitabine]] — lapatinib is approved in combination with capecitabine, and
  the combination was the pivotal late-line regimen that established the drug.
- [[Insulin Sensitivity]] — lapatinib's off-target inhibition of the insulin
  receptor and IRS-1 is a mechanistic explanation for the hyperglycaemia reported
  during therapy.
- [[EMT]] — HER2 signalling via the MAPK/PI3K arms is a driver of
  epithelial–mesenchymal transition, so lapatinib suppresses this phenotypic
  switch as a downstream consequence of receptor blockade.
