# Gastrointestinal Tract

**Overview:** The [[Gastrointestinal Tract]] (GIT) is the organ system that ingests and mechanically/chemically digests food, absorbs nutrients and water, and eliminates waste. It spans the mouth, esophagus, stomach, small and large intestine, and anus.

**Structure and organization:** Coordinated peristalsis and segmentation move contents, while the mucosa—with its epithelial barrier, glands, and villi—carries out secretion and absorption. The tract is richly supplied by the enteric nervous system and hosts the largest aggregate of immune tissue in the body.

**Physiological function:** The GIT performs enzymatic digestion, active and passive [[Absorption|absorption]] of macronutrients and micronutrients, and water/electrolyte balance. It communicates bidirectionally with the brain via the [[Gut-Brain Axis]] and cultivates the [[Gut Microbiome]], which ferments fiber into [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] that nourish colonocytes and modulate [[Inflammation]].

**Pathological relevance:** Barrier failure, dysbiosis, and chronic low-grade [[Inflammation]] in the gut contribute to metabolic disease, [[Aging]]-related frailty, and neurodegenerative risk. Age-related changes include slowed motility, reduced absorptive capacity, and altered microbial composition. Supportive strategies include dietary fiber, [[Fasting]], and [[Exercise]].
