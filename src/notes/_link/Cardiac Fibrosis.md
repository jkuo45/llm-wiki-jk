# Cardiac Fibrosis

**Cardiac fibrosis** is a pathological process characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (collagen) in the myocardium, driven by cardiac fibroblast activation and differentiation into myofibroblasts. It is a key structural hallmark of adverse cardiac remodeling in heart failure, myocardial infarction, and hypertensive heart disease. **[[TGF-β]]** (transforming growth factor-β) is a central profibrotic cytokine that drives myofibroblast differentiation via Smad signaling.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Cardiac fibrosis results from chronic injury signals — pressure overload, ischemia, inflammation, and neurohumoral activation ([[Angiotensin II]]) — that stimulate TGF-β/Smad signaling, fibroblast proliferation, and excessive collagen deposition. Sustained fibrosis impairs myocardial compliance and electrical conduction, progressing to heart failure and arrhythmia.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

- **[[SIRT1]]** and **[[SIRT3]]**: [[Resveratrol]]-mediated activation attenuates fibrosis via inhibition of the TGF-β/Smad3 pathway.
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Systemic knockout induces cardiac fibrosis via TGF-β/Smad3 activation, whereas it prevents Ang II-mediated fibrosis through AMPK–ACE2 signaling.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Overexpression is protective, promoting AMPK activation via LKB1 deacetylation.
- **[[SIRT5]]**: Knockout increases fibrosis compared with age-matched wild-type mice.
- **[[SIRT4]]**: Contributes to fibrosis — global knockout confers resistance to Ang II infusion.
- **[[SIRT7]]**: Inconsistent — enhanced expression/phosphorylation promotes fibrosis via Smad2/ERK, yet SIRT7 knockout is also reported to result in fibrosis.

## Therapeutic Implications

Sirtuin activators (e.g., resveratrol) represent candidate antifibrotic interventions. The predominantly protective SIRT1/SIRT3/SIRT6/SIRT2 axis and the pro-fibrotic contributions of SIRT4 make the NAD⁺–sirtuin network a promising target for limiting cardiac fibrosis and maladaptive remodeling.
