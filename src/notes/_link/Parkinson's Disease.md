# Parkinson's Disease

**Parkinson's disease (PD)** is a progressive [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative disorder]] characterized by the loss of [[Dopamine]]-producing neurons in the [[Substantia Nigra|substantia nigra pars compacta]], leading to motor symptoms: resting [[Tremor]], [[Rigidity]], [[Bradykinesia]], and postural instability. Non-motor features include [[Cognitive Decline|cognitive decline]], [[Depression]], and autonomic dysfunction. A growing body of evidence links PD to [[Senescence]] of neurons, glia, and peripheral cells, and to a distinguishable circulating SASP signature.

## Pathophysiology

- α-[[Synuclein]] aggregation ([[Lewy Bodies]])
- [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]
- Impaired [[Energy Metabolism|cellular energy metabolism]]
- Neuroinflammation, with [[TLR-4]] signaling implicated in neuronal loss
- Defective [[Autophagic Flux]] contributes to the buildup of toxic aggregates and neuronal death
- Impaired [[Mitophagy]] (selective [[Autophagy]] of mitochondria)

## Symptoms

The cardinal motor symptoms of PD include:
- **Tremor:** Often a "pill-rolling" tremor at rest.
- **Rigidity:** Stiffness of the limbs and trunk.
- **Bradykinesia:** Slowness of movement.
- **Postural Instability:** Impaired balance and coordination.

Non-motor symptoms can also occur, including cognitive impairment, depression, and sleep disturbances.

## Pathology

The primary pathological feature of PD is the selective degeneration of neuromelanin-containing neurons. The disease is closely linked to [[Aging]], which is the main risk factor. Progressive intracellular accumulation of [[Neuromelanin]] above a [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]] is hypothesized to trigger neuronal dysfunction and neurodegeneration. Like other neurodegenerative diseases, its pathogenesis may involve [[Oxidative Stress]] and the accumulation of [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]], which can interfere with protein function and promote neuronal damage.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

PD pathogenesis involves progressive dopaminergic neuron loss and α-synuclein misfolding. Senescent astrocytes and microglia mount a pro-inflammatory SASP that includes [[IL-6]] and [[IL-1α]], exacerbating [[Neuroinflammation]] and promoting [[Alpha-synuclein]] spreading. Persistent DNA damage and mitochondrial dysfunction in aging glia further fuel the senescence program, creating a feed-forward loop between proteinopathy and inflammation.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The SASP profile detectable in urinary extracellular vesicles distinguishes older individuals with and without PD, supporting circulating senescence signatures as a non-invasive diagnostic readout (SASP review, PMID 20365073). Senescent cell accumulation in the aging brain and the broader [[Inflammaging]] milieu are hypothesized to accelerate dopaminergic neuron death, placing PD within the spectrum of senescence-associated neurodegeneration rather than a purely cell-autonomous proteinopathy.

## Role in Cellular Reprogramming and Pluripotency

[[Parkinson's Disease]] has been a primary target for [[Induced Pluripotent Stem Cells]] (iPSC) technology since its inception. It serves as a flagship model for both [[Disease Modeling]] and [[Regenerative Medicine]]. iPSCs derived from patients with [[Parkinson's Disease]] have been used to create in vitro models that recapitulate hallmark features of the disease, allowing researchers to study the molecular mechanisms of neuronal degeneration.

In terms of cell replacement therapy, iPSC-derived dopaminergic progenitors have shown great promise. Preclinical studies in primate models demonstrated that these cells could survive, integrate, and restore dopamine production, leading to improved motor function. This has paved the way for first-in-human clinical trials, where autologous and allogeneic iPSC-derived grafts are being evaluated for safety and efficacy. However, challenges remain, such as the potential for uncontrolled growth and the development of dyskinesias in some transplanted subjects.

## Autophagy Modulation

- Inducing autophagy via dietary interventions such as [[Intermittent Fasting]] or [[Ketogenic Diet]] is investigated for its neuroprotective potential in PD.
- [[TFEB]]-mediated autophagy has been shown to rescue dopamine neurons from α-synuclein toxicity.

## Creatine and PD

[[Creatine in Health and Disease]] reviews creatine's role in PD:

- **Resistance training + creatine**: Hass et al. reported that [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine]] during resistance training in PD patients improved muscle strength and the chair sit-to-rise test.
- **Neuroprotection**: The review notes that [[TLR-4]] downregulation by creatine may alter PD pathology and inhibit [[Neuronal Death|neuronal death]].
- **Large trial**: The Bender et al. multicenter study (creatine 9.5 g/day, up to 5 years; n=1687 across PD, HD, ALS) found no significant improvement in PD outcomes.

## COMT and Treatment

In the management of [[Parkinson's Disease]], [[COMT Inhibitors]] are used to increase the effectiveness of [[Levodopa]]. By slowing the degradation of dopamine, these inhibitors help maintain higher levels of the neurotransmitter in the brain.

## Management

Levodopa/carbidopa, dopamine agonists, MAO-B inhibitors, and deep brain stimulation; exercise and [[Creatine Supplementation]] as supportive adjuncts. Beyond standard dopaminergic replacement, senolytic and senomorphic approaches are proposed to blunt PD-associated neuroinflammation. Inflammaging and SASP biomarkers may aid early detection and patient stratification, and senescence-modifying strategies are an emerging therapeutic direction for neurodegenerative disease.

## TFEB

[[TFEB]]-mediated autophagy has been shown to rescue dopamine neurons from α-synuclein toxicity.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

Parkinson's disease is the most common movement disorder of older adults. Sirtuins modulate α-synuclein toxicity, mitochondrial function, and autophagic degradation:

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Ameliorates LC3 deacetylation-mediated autophagic degradation of α-synuclein and improves motor deficits in PD mice.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Pharmacological SIRT3 elevation counteracts α-synuclein-induced mitochondrial dysfunction (reduced oligomers, normalized bioenergetics); age-dependent mitochondrial oxidative stress in the [[Substantia Nigra|substantia nigra]] is associated with decreased SIRT3 protective function.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Mediates exacerbation of α-synuclein toxicity; NAD⁺ metabolism is altered in sporadic PD cells → SIRT2 activation → decreased acetylated α-tubulin; SIRT2 deletion is protective. A rs2015 SNP may influence SIRT2 expression via miR8061 binding → PD risk (Chinese Han case-control; epidemiological data conflicting).
