# Fibroblast

[[Fibroblast|Fibroblasts]] are mesenchymal, spindle-shaped cells responsible for synthesizing and remodeling the [[ECM|extracellular matrix (ECM)]] and [[Collagen]], producing the structural framework (stroma) for animal tissues. They are the most abundant connective-tissue cells and are indispensable for tissue architecture, repair, and homeostasis. Quiescent fibroblasts remain metabolically active, continuously maintaining and remodeling the ECM. In [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis|idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)]], fibroblasts — particularly those adjacent to fibroblast foci — become a central effector of fibrotic remodeling, and their acquisition of a [[Senescence|senescent]], apoptosis-resistant, profibrotic phenotype is a defining feature of disease.

## Origin and Heterogeneity

Fibroblasts arise from embryonic mesoderm and, in many organs, from resident mesenchymal progenitor pools. They are highly heterogeneous and tissue-specific; for example, [[HFF1]] (human foreskin fibroblast) is a widely used cell-culture model. Organ-specific fibroblast subsets express distinctive marker repertoires and are imprinted by local microenvironmental cues. During injury, resident fibroblasts activate into myofibroblasts — contractile, α-smooth-muscle-actin-positive cells that close wounds but, when persistently activated, drive [[Fibrosis]].

## Functions

The principal role of fibroblasts is synthesis and turnover of ECM components (collagens, fibronectin, proteoglycans) and the [[Basal Lamina]] support structures. They secrete matrix metalloproteinases and their inhibitors to remodel the matrix, and release cytokines and growth factors (TGF-β, PDGF, FGF) that instruct neighboring cells. Through [[Integrin]]-mediated contacts and [[FAK Signaling]], fibroblasts sense matrix stiffness and adjust deposition accordingly.

## Physiological Role

In development and adult life, fibroblasts provide mechanical support, maintain stem-cell niches that enforce [[Quiescence]], and orchestrate [[Tissue Repair]]. Their contractile activity is essential for wound closure, and their provisional matrix scaffolds re-epithelialization.

## Senescent Fibroblasts in IPF

IPF-derived [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]] exhibit a senescent phenotype characterized by resistance to [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]], sustained metabolic activity, and increased ECM synthesis. They actively maintain a profibrotic microenvironment by amplifying fibrogenic signaling and responding to the [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]] of neighboring senescent [[Alveolar Type II Cells|alveolar type II cells]].

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - cellular_senescence_ipf_diseases-14-00201|Cellular Senescence in IPF (Baurzhan et al., 2026)]]
> Fibroblasts derived from IPF patients exhibit a senescent phenotype (apoptosis resistance, sustained metabolic activity, increased ECM synthesis) and amplify key fibrogenic signaling pathways. Senescent epithelial SASP components — TGF-β and CTGF — stimulate fibroblast proliferation and myofibroblast differentiation.

## Profibrotic Reprogramming

- [[TGFβ|TGF-β]]/[[SMAD2|SMAD2/3]] signaling drives fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation and ECM deposition.
- [[SASP|SASP]] factors ([[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], [[TGFβ|TGF-β]], [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|MMPs]], [[Connective Tissue Growth Factor|CTGF]]) create a self-perpetuating loop with senescent epithelium.
- [[PTEN]] loss → [[PI3K]]/[[Akt|AKT]] signaling supports senescent fibroblast survival and apoptosis resistance.

## Pathological Relevance

Dysregulated fibroblast activation underlies organ fibrosis (liver, lung, kidney, heart, skin), where excessive ECM replaces parenchyma and elevates tissue stiffness, perpetuating a pro-fibrotic loop. Fibroblasts also influence [[Cancer]] by building the tumor stroma, secreting factors that promote invasion and [[Epithelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]], and suppressing or evading immunity. Fibroblast-targeted therapies (e.g., TGF-β or LOX inhibition) aim to limit pathological scarring; [[Senolytics|senolytic]] clearance of senescent fibroblasts reduces fibrosis in models.
