# System Xc-

**System Xc-** is an amino acid antiporter embedded in the plasma membrane that exchanges intracellular [[Glutamate]] for extracellular cystine at a 1:1 ratio. It is a core negative regulator of [[Ferroptosis]]: by importing cystine—the oxidized dimer of cysteine—it supplies the cysteine required for de novo [[Glutathione]] (GSH) biosynthesis, which in turn fuels the [[GPX4]]-dependent detoxification of [[Lipid Peroxidation]]. Inhibition of system Xc- depletes GSH, inactivates [[GPX4]], and permits iron-dependent lipid peroxide accumulation, ultimately triggering ferroptotic cell death.

## Structure & Subunits

System Xc- is a heterodimer composed of two subunits: the light chain [[SLC7A11]] (also called xCT), which confers substrate specificity and catalyzes the transport reaction, and the heavy chain [[SLC3A2]] (also called 4F2hc or CD98), a type II membrane glycoprotein that traffics and stabilizes the complex at the cell surface. SLC3A2 alone cannot transport cystine but is indispensable for proper assembly and plasma-membrane localization of the functional antiporter. The two subunits are linked by a disulfide bond.

> [!info] Functional definition
> System Xc- does not hydrolyze ATP. It is an obligate antiporter: one cystine in, one glutamate out. Because it exports glutamate, its activity also modulates extracellular glutamate tone and, indirectly, excitatory neurotransmission—relevant in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]] contexts.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

Cystine taken up by system Xc- is rapidly reduced to two cysteine molecules in the cytosol. Cysteine, together with glutamate and glycine, is condensed by glutamate-cysteine ligase and glutathione synthetase into [[Glutathione]]. GSH is the obligate reducing cofactor for [[GPX4]], the phospholipid hydroperoxide glutathione peroxidase that reduces lethal lipid peroxides (L-OOH) to harmless alcohols (L-OH). When system Xc- is blocked—either pharmacologically by [[Erastin]] or genetically by loss of [[SLC7A11]]—cystine import falls, GSH drops, GPX4 activity collapses, and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]-driven lipid peroxidation propagates unchecked, executing ferroptosis.

> [!tip] The p53 connection
> The tumor suppressor [[p53]] transcriptionally downregulates [[SLC7A11]], suppressing cystine uptake. This is one of the mechanisms by which p53 promotes ferroptosis as a tumor-suppression activity (Jiang et al., *Nature* 2015, PMID: 25799957). Conversely, in some wild-type p53 backgrounds, p53 can *suppress* ferroptosis, illustrating context-dependence.

## Physiological Function

System Xc- is widely distributed in mammalian tissues, particularly in cells exposed to oxidative stress—macrophages, neurons, and cancer cells with high metabolic demand. It is a frontline antioxidant defense, maintaining the intracellular cysteine/GSH pool. Under basal conditions it supports redox homeostasis; under cystine starvation it becomes the rate-limiting vulnerability that ferroptosis inducers exploit.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Because many cancer cells—especially those with mesenchymal, [[RAS]]-mutant, or drug-tolerant phenotypes—are addicted to cystine import, system Xc- is a major therapeutic vulnerability. [[Erastin]] and sulfasalazine are prototype system Xc- inhibitors; the MUC1-C/xCT complex is a druggable node in [[Breast Cancer]] (triple-negative) and other tumors. Conversely, up-regulation of system Xc- (and [[SLC7A11]]) confers ferroptosis resistance in [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] and contributes to therapy resistance. In neurons, system Xc- dysfunction links redox imbalance to neurodegeneration.

> [!important] Therapeutic duality
> Inhibiting system Xc- triggers ferroptosis in tumors but can also exacerbate oxidative damage in normal tissues (e.g., kidney, neurons). Selective delivery (nanoparticles, tissue-specific promoters) is an active research direction.
