# IL-1alpha

**IL-1alpha** (Interleukin-1 alpha, *IL1A*) is a dual-function IL-1 family cytokine: an N-terminal nuclear transcriptional regulator and a C-terminal receptor-binding alarmin. Unlike [[Interleukin 1β]], its precursor is constitutively bioactive — no inflammasome cleavage is required — so it spills out fully potent the moment a necrotic or pyroptotic membrane ruptures and signals via IL-1R1/MyD88/[[NF-κB]] on neighboring cells.

In the comparison-table context, IL-1alpha is co-released with IL-1β, [[HMGB1]], and mtDNA through [[Gasdermin D]] pores during [[Pyroptosis]], and leaks passively during [[Necrosis]]. Calpain-processed forms add a regulated-release route in some myeloid settings.

## Connections

- [[Pyroptosis]] — co-released with IL-1β through Gasdermin D pores as a lytic alarm signal.
- [[Necrosis]] — passive alarmin leak on accidental rupture; sterile-inflammation driver alongside HMGB1.
- [[Interleukin 1β]] — sister cytokine requiring inflammasome maturation; the two jointly define IL-1R1 signaling load.
- [[Gasdermin D]] — pore executor licensing IL-1alpha egress during pyroptosis.
- [[NLRP3]] — inflammasome whose priming/output sets the joint IL-1alpha/IL-1β inflammatory tone.
