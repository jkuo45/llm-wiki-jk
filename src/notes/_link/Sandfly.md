# Sandfly

**Sandfly** is not a taxon but a vernacular label applied to any small, biting, blood-sucking fly encountered in sandy habitats — and the label spans several unrelated groups. The group that matters in medicine is the **subfamily Phlebotominae** within the family **Psychodidae** (genus *Phlebotomus* in the Old World, *Lutzomyia* in the Americas). In North America, "sandfly" more often means horse flies of the family **Tabanidae** ("greenheads") or biting midges of the family **Ceratopogonidae** ("no-see-ums"); the New Zealand "sandfly" is a black fly of the genus *Austrosimulium*.

> [!info] Biting biology
> In every group, only females feed on blood. The protein in the blood meal is needed for egg production, which makes these flies **anautogenous** — each blood meal is required for a reproductive cycle, and thus each is a discrete exposure event. Phlebotomine sandflies are small (2–4 mm), nocturnal or crepuscular, and leap or flutter rather than buzzing like mosquitoes, which is why they are poorly targeted by auditory mosquito traps.

## Vector roles

| Agent | Group | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Leishmania* spp. | Protozoa | [[Leishmaniasis]] vectored by *Phlebotomus* (Africa, Asia, Europe) and *Lutzomyia* (Americas) |
| Sandfly fever viruses | Virus | [[Phlebovirus]] transmitted by phlebotomine sandflies; two recognized antigenic groups, Toscana and Karimabad |
| Chandipura virus | Virus | Rhabdovirus closely related to rabies, reported from sandflies in West Africa, with a 2010 Indian outbreak and endemic transmission in Gujarat from 2024 |
| *Bartonella bacilliformis* | Bacteria | Carrion's disease (Oroya fever), restricted to Andean Peru, Ecuador, and formerly southern Colombia |

Leishmaniasis is the dominant disease burden. The parasite is transmitted in the **amastigote** form inside a mammalian macrophage, and the clinical syndrome depends on the *Leishmania* species: *L. donovani* and the *L. infantum* complex cause visceral disease with spiking fevers, hepatosplenomegaly, and pancytopenia; *L. major* and *L. tropica* cause cutaneous disease; *L. braziliensis* causes mucocutaneous disease.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> "Sandfly fever" is ambiguous in older literature: it may refer to phlebovirus infection or be used loosely for any fever following sandfly bites, including early [[Leishmaniasis]]. The term is being retired in favor of explicit virus or parasite naming.

## Clinical features and prevention

Bites produce small, intensely pruritic welts, characteristically in clusters, that peak over several days rather than appearing immediately. This delayed, clustered presentation is the main clinical discriminator from mosquito bites. Secondary bacterial infection and, with phlebotomine bites in sensitized individuals, a sterile allergic reaction are both common. Prevention relies on repellents: high-concentration **DEET** and picaridin are proven effective, and lemon eucalyptus extract is comparable to DEET in field trials. In the laboratory, artificial infection and xenosonization studies show that *Leishmania* development in the fly is arrested if the blood meal contains the pro-inflammatory cytokine interferon-γ, and proceeds if it does not — the basis for a proposed cytokinic therapy.

## Connections

- [[Leishmaniasis]] — the principal disease burden of phlebotomine sandflies and the reason the Phlebotominae group is the medically relevant sense of "sandfly."
- [[Leishmania]] — the protozoan transmitted in amastigote form; the fly is the biological host in which promastigotes develop and are transmitted, and the point at which host immune signals (notably interferon-γ) determine whether infection takes hold.
- [[Autophagy]] — a mechanistic point of overlap rather than a classic pairing: *Leishmania* amastigotes survive and replicate inside macrophage phagolysosomes, so macrophage autophagy is a determinant of whether the infection is contained or progresses. This link is mechanistically real but is a candidate connection for future notes, not an established one in the vault.
- [[Apoptosis]] — also adjacent: macrophage apoptosis and the cell-death repertoire of infected macrophages govern the outcome of sandfly-transmitted infection, and the same machinery governs parasite-induced host cell death in the fly midgut.
