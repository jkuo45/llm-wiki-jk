# Formyl Peptide Receptor

**Formyl peptide receptors (FPRs)** are G-protein-coupled receptors expressed on innate immune cells that recognize N-formyl peptides. Originally characterized for their role in sensing bacterial products, FPRs also detect mitochondrial [[N-Formyl Peptides|N-formyl peptides]] released from damaged mitochondria, linking mitochondrial injury to innate immune recruitment and sterile inflammation.

## Mechanism

FPR engagement triggers chemotaxis, degranulation, and activation of neutrophils, macrophages, and other myeloid cells. In the context of mitochondrial dysfunction, FPR signalling amplifies inflammatory responses by facilitating [[NLRP3]] activation and redox-sensitive transcriptional programmes, creating a feed-forward loop in which inflammation further damages mitochondria.

## Role in Inflammaging

FPRs form part of an interconnected mitochondrial-DAMP sensing network with [[TLR9]], the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING pathway]], and [[NLRP3]]. In ageing, persistent mitochondrial dysfunction favours repeated DAMP release, while defective clearance of damaged cells and organelles sustains immune-cell exposure, contributing to chronic sterile inflammation and [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]].
