# SMAD4

SMAD4 — originally cloned as **DPC4** ("deleted in pancreatic carcinoma,
locus 4") and also called MADH4 — is the single **Co-SMAD** of the
[[SMAD]] family and the obligate nuclear partner of every R-SMAD. It sits at
chromosome 18q21, in a cluster that also includes [[SMAD2]] and DCC, and it
is one of the most frequently inactivated tumor suppressors in
[[Pancreatic Cancer]].

## Structure

SMAD4 is 552 residues with the canonical SMAD layout:

- **MH1 (codons ~1–142)** — DNA-binding domain. Its β-hairpin binds the SBE
  (5′-CAGAC); X-ray structures of SMAD4/SBE, SMAD3/SBE and SMAD1/SBE complexes
  show four helices and three antiparallel β-hairpins, one of which is
  DNA-contacting. Only one of 140 residues varies across vertebrates. Also
  carries a nuclear localization signal. MH1-domain missense mutations
  destabilize the protein, which is then degraded.
- **Linker** (~143–318) — poorly conserved, serine/proline-rich. Contains the
  **SMAD4 activation domain (SAD)**, a 48-residue segment mediating
  interactions with transcriptional activators and repressors, and a nuclear
  export signal. Only ~6% of tumor missense mutations fall here.
- **MH2 (codons ~319–552)** — oligomerization and transcriptional activation.
  A **mutation cluster region (MCR, codons 330–370)** in the loop-helix
  region is the hotspot for tumor missense mutations, accounting for over half
  of MH2 missense hits; the interface residues there (L332, L350, G351,
  F353, N361, L365) mediate intermolecular Smad-Smad contact. Unlike R-SMADs,
  SMAD4 has no C-terminal SSXS motif and is never phosphorylated by type I
  receptors; instead its MH2 basic pocket reads the phosphorylated R-SMAD
  tail. The last 38 C-terminal residues are essential — deletion of them
  (a naturally occurring nonsense mutation at codon 515) abolishes homo- and
  heterooligomerization and destabilizes the protein.

> [!info] MH2-domain folding is dynamic
> Solution NMR and SAXS studies of full-length SMAD4 show a mostly monomeric
> protein in which MH1 and MH2 are flexibly linked, sampling closed and
> extended conformations. The compact intramolecular MH1–MH2 arrangement seen
> in tumor-mutant SMAD4 is a mutant-stabilized state, not the wild-type
> equilibrium.

## Mechanism

SMAD4 is the passive half of the canonical pathway: it does not respond to
ligand, but once phosphorylated R-SMADs are present it stabilizes them,
allows trimer formation, escorts them into the nucleus, and recruits
coactivators/repressors to the complex. The predominant functional unit is a
heterotrimer of two R-SMADs and one SMAD4, though SMAD4 homotrimers,
dimers, R-SMAD homotrimers, and heterotrimers with mixed R-SMAD pairs also
occur in cells and target distinct genes. SMAD4 also partners with BMP-branch
R-SMADs (1/5/8), so its loss disables both branches of the superfamily.

Beyond TGF-β superfamily signaling, SMAD4 has reported roles in
Wnt/β-catenin ([[Wnt-beta-catenin]]) target transcription and, more recently,
in DNA-damage responses: radiation induces SMAD4 nuclear translocation independent of TGF-β,
where its MH1 domain binds the DNA-binding domain of [[PARP]] and reduces
PARP1 recruitment to damaged DNA.

## Physiological roles

Smad4-null mice die during gastrulation with failure of definitive endoderm
and mesoderm formation. In adults, SMAD4 is required for TGF-β-dependent
growth arrest and cytostasis (induction of p21 and p15), and its loss in
keratinocytes causes spontaneous squamous cell carcinoma, establishing SMAD4
as a dominant tumor suppressor in skin.

## Disease and clinical relevance

- **Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.** SMAD4 is inactivated in ~55% of
  cases — ~35% by homozygous deletion, ~20% by mutation plus loss of
  heterozygosity. Because the gene status is hard to assay on mixed surgical
  specimens, SMAD4 immunohistochemistry is a validated surrogate (91%
  sensitive, 94% specific for gene inactivation). SMAD4 loss predicts poorer
  survival and resistance to chemoradiotherapy; mechanistically, SMAD4
  deficiency raises ROS and autophagy, and its failure to restrain PARP1
  reduces DNA repair, so combining the PARP inhibitor olaparib with
  radiotherapy selectively shrinks SMAD4-deficient tumors in preclinical
  models.
- **Colorectal cancer.** SMAD4 loss in ~15% of cases; in *Apc* mutant mice,
  Smad4 loss increases polyp size, produces signet-ring cells, and causes
  >50% invasive adenocarcinoma.
- **Other tumors.** Loss also occurs at lower frequency in gastric, biliary,
  ovarian, breast and lung cancer.
- **Germline loss.** Heterozygous germline SMAD4 mutations cause **juvenile
  polyposis syndrome** with gastrointestinal hamartomatous polyps and elevated
  colorectal cancer risk.

> [!warning] Distinction worth keeping
> SMAD4 is not simply "more important than SMAD2/3." It is the only SMAD with
> a clear, high-frequency two-hit tumor-suppressor role; SMAD2 and SMAD3
> mutations are rare and their functional consequences weaker.

## Connections

- [[SMAD2]] and [[SMAD3]] — SMAD2/SMAD3 are the R-SMADs whose phosphorylation SMAD4 is required to translate into gene expression; loss of SMAD4 disables both TGF-β and BMP branches.
- [[SMAD]] — the family note: SMAD4 is the single Co-SMAD, structurally an R-SMAD but functionally different.
- [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway]] — SMAD4 is the obligatory nuclear effector of this pathway and of the parallel BMP branch.
- [[Pancreatic Cancer]] — the tumor type in which SMAD4 inactivation is most frequent and diagnostically exploited.
- [[Fibrosis]] — SMAD4-dependent TGF-β output drives myofibroblast activation and matrix deposition; the SIRT4/SMAD4 axis restrains hepatic stellate cell activation.
- [[Wnt-beta-catenin]] — SMAD4 has reported non-Smad roles in β-catenin target transcription, alongside its tumor-suppressor function.
- [[PARP]] — SMAD4's MH1 domain binds PARP1 after radiation-induced nuclear translocation, limiting DNA repair.
- [[SIRT4]] — SIRT4 acts through a SIRT4/SMAD4 axis to inhibit fibrogenesis and hepatic stellate cell activation.
