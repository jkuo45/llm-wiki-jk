### Overview

p15INK4b is a cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor of the INK4 family, the protein product of the [[CDKN2B]] tumor-suppressor locus. It binds and inhibits [[CDK4]] and CDK6, preventing formation of the cyclin D–CDK complex and the phosphorylation of the [[Retinoblastoma Protein]] (Rb). By keeping Rb hypophosphorylated, p15INK4b enforces a durable G1 cell-cycle block that is central to senescence and growth suppression.

### Mechanism / Structure

p15INK4b belongs to the INK4 (INhibitor of Kinase 4) family of small ankyrin-repeat proteins, structurally and functionally parallel to [[p16INK4a]]. Its mechanism is direct:

- It occupies the ATP-binding cleft of CDK4/6, blocking cyclin D association and catalytic activity.
- Loss of CDK4/6 activity leaves Rb unphosphorylated, so Rb remains bound to and represses [[E2F]] target genes required for S-phase entry.
- The result is a canonical INK4–Rb axis-mediated G1 arrest.

> [!info] Redundant CDK inhibition
> p15INK4b provides redundant CDK inhibition alongside p16INK4a. Both INK4 proteins can independently lock cells out of the cycle, making the CDKN2A/B locus a robust gatekeeper of cell-cycle exit.

### Role in Senescence

Induction of [[CDKN2B]] is a key event in paracrine and TGFβ-driven senescence. p15INK4b acts cooperatively with [[p21 CIP1]]: p15 blocks CDK4/6 while p21 inhibits CDK2, producing a robust, redundant blockade that durably locks receiving cells out of the cell cycle. This cooperativity strengthens the arrest against mitogenic escape and is a recurring theme in senescence enforcement.

### Clinical & Research Significance

As a product of a co-regulated tumor-suppressor locus, p15INK4b is a core effector of growth inhibition and senescence. Its frequent co-regulation with [[CDKN2B]] (and the neighboring CDKN2A/p16INK4a locus) makes it a useful marker of senescence-associated cell-cycle arrest and a candidate biomarker of the senescent state. Therapeutically, the INK4–Rb axis is a focal point for understanding how senescence is enforced and how its bypass can permit malignant progression.
