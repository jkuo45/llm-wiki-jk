# ACSS2

**ACSS2** (acetyl-CoA synthetase 2) is a nuclear-cytosolic enzyme that ligates [[Acetate]] to coenzyme A to form [[Acetyl-CoA]]. Alongside the [[ACLY]]-dependent pathway, ACSS2 provides a mitochondrial-independent route of acetyl-CoA production that can support [[Histone Acetylation]] and gene expression.

## Overview

ACSS2 is one of three mammalian acetyl-CoA synthetase isoforms. The short-chain acyl-CoA synthetase family member ACSS2 is localized to the cytosol and nucleus, where its product (acetyl-CoA) serves as a substrate for histone acetylation. Because acetyl-CoA is not membrane permeable, exogenous acetate must be converted intracellularly by ACSS2 to replenish the nuclear acetyl-CoA pool.

## Role in Cellular Senescence

- In mitochondria-depleted senescent cells, acetate supplementation is converted into acetyl-CoA by ACSS2 (confirmed by isotope tracing), restoring histone acetylation and partially rescuing the mitochondrial-dependent [[SASP]] programme
- Acetate supplementation increases global nuclear acetylation, including [[H3K27ac]], and induces pro-inflammatory genes (*IL6*, *IL8*, *IL1B*, *CCL2*) without affecting cell cycle regulators p16/p21

> [!info] Acetate–ACSS2 Restores SASP After Mitochondrial Loss
> Acetate supplementation is sufficient to partially restore SASP gene expression in senescent cells lacking mitochondria, establishing acetyl-CoA — not the mitochondria themselves — as the key metabolic link to epigenetic control of inflammatory genes.

## Wider Context

ACSS2 is induced by conditions of metabolic stress and acetate availability. Its nuclear pool of acetyl-CoA supports histone acetylation at loci relevant to memory formation, tumor adaptation, and stress responses. In this study, ACSS2-mediated acetate conversion provided a tool to demonstrate that acetyl-CoA availability, rather than intact mitochondria, gates SASP expression.
