# Gelsolin

**Gelsolin** is an actin-binding and actin-severing protein of the [[Actin]] cytoskeleton. In this vault it appears in three distinct roles: a phagocytic and motility effector that cross-links actin for [[Iba1]]-positive microglia; a cytoskeletal substrate dismantled during [[Apoptosis]] alongside other structural proteins; and a component of the Itch–[[p53]]–[[c-FLIP]] degradation module, where its expression confers resistance to [[Cisplatin]] in gynecologic cancers.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Actin remodeling.** Gelsolin caps and severs filamentous actin, then caps the barbed ends, promoting actin monomer turnover and gel-sol conversion of the cortical network. In microglia, Gelsolin binding supports the actin cross-linking and remodeling required for phagocytosis, chemotaxis and process motility.
- **Apoptotic disassembly.** As a structural substrate, Gelsolin is cleaved during executioner-caspase dismantling of the cytoskeleton, which contributes to cell shrinkage and the loss of shape in apoptotic bodies — the same class of event as cleavage of [[Actin]] and [[ROCK]].
- **Chemoresistance module.** Gelsolin is reported as a further component of the Itch–p53–c-FLIP complex, and its expression confers chemoresistance against cisplatin in gynecologic cancers.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - FLIP the Switch Regulation of Apoptosis and Necroptosis by cFLIP|Tsuchiya et al. 2015]]
> Gelsolin (a regulator of actin dynamics) was shown to participate in the regulation of the Itch–p53–c-FLIP complex, conferring chemoresistance against cisplatin in gynecologic cancers. The c-FLIP arm of that module is the one that matters here: Itch-driven polyubiquitylation lowers cFLIP_L, which raises death-receptor sensitivity — so Gelsolin widens the same axis that Itch narrows.

## Physiological Function

Gelsolin couples cytoskeletal dynamics to cell motility and phagocytic competence. It is a core regulator of the actin cortex, and its cleavage products participate in the dismantling of the apoptotic cell.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cisplatin chemoresistance.** Gelsolin expression stabilizes the Itch–p53–c-FLIP axis in gynecologic cancers, reducing the death-receptor sensitivity that cisplatin is meant to exploit.
- **Microglial activation.** Gelsolin-dependent actin remodeling accompanies the ramified-to-amoeboid transition of activated microglia.
- **Apoptotic clearance.** Cytoskeletal dismantling by Gelsolin cleavage contributes to the formation of [[Apoptotic Bodies]].
