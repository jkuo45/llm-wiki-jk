# DACH1

**DACH1** (dachshund homolog 1) encodes a transcriptional regulator related to the *Drosophila* dachshund protein, functioning as a co-repressor of nuclear receptor and forkhead signaling that controls cell fate decisions, apoptosis, and differentiation. The DACH1 locus also produces noncoding transcripts; a long noncoding RNA (lncRNA DACH1) has been characterized in cardiovascular biology, where it influences mitochondrial function and cell survival.

## SIRT3 interaction in heart failure

In the context of heart failure, [[SIRT3]] orchestrates multiple cardioprotective pathways — FOXO3a-dependent activation of [[MnSOD]] and [[Catalase]], inhibition of [[Cyclophilin D]]-dependent mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, and interaction with the long noncoding RNA DACH1 to regulate mitochondrial oxidative damage and cell death[228]. The lncRNA DACH1–SIRT3 interaction thus represents a noncoding-RNA arm of SIRT3's cardioprotective network, coupling nuclear gene regulation to mitochondrial integrity in the failing heart.

## Clinical relevance

lncRNA DACH1 has been proposed as a biomarker and therapeutic target in cardiac injury. Understanding its SIRT3-dependent regulation may inform RNA-based strategies to bolster mitochondrial quality control in heart failure.
