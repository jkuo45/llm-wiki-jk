# 5-Fluorouracil

[[5-Fluorouracil]] (5-FU) is a fluorinated pyrimidine antimetabolite used widely in cancer chemotherapy, particularly for colorectal, breast, head and neck, and gastrointestinal malignancies. It is metabolized intracellularly to active species: 5-fluoro-2'-deoxyuridine-5'-monophosphate (FdUMP), which inhibits [[Thymidylate Synthase|thymidylate synthase]] (TS), and 5-fluorouridine triphosphate (FUTP), which is incorporated into RNA, disrupting processing and function.

## Mechanism of Action

- **TS inhibition:** FdUMP forms a stable ternary complex with TS and 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate, depleting thymidine nucleotides and inducing DNA damage / [[Apoptosis]].
- **RNA incorporation:** FUTP incorporation corrupts RNA synthesis and processing.
- **DNA incorporation:** 5-fluoro-2'-deoxyuridine-5'-triphosphate (FdUTP) misincorporates into DNA.

## Clinical Relevance & Resistance

5-FU is a backbone of regimens such as [[FOLFOX]] and [[FOLFIRI]]. Resistance arises via altered TS expression, impaired activation (DPD deficiency/overactivity), and enhanced [[DNA Repair]] / [[Drug Resistance]].

> [!info] Source: [[Oral Fenbendazole for Cancer Therapy in Humans and Animals]]
> Fenbendazole showed efficacy against 5-FU-resistant colorectal cancer cells and was more effective than [[Albendazole]] against 5-FU-resistant colorectal cells, likely through intervention in [[Glycolysis]] rather than classical TS pathways (5).
