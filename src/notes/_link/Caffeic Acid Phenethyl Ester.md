# Caffeic Acid Phenethyl Ester

**Caffeic acid phenethyl ester (CAPE)** is the ester formed between [[Caffeic Acid]] and
phenethyl alcohol. It occurs in [[Propolis]] (the resinous hive product bees make from
plant exudates) and is reported in other plant matrices. Its name is sometimes confused
with caffeic acid phenethylamide (CAPEA) in the older literature; CAPE is the ester.

## Reported pharmacology

Nearly all CAPE pharmacology is in vitro or in rodent models, and it is worth being
explicit that the clinical significance of these findings is unknown. Reported activities
include:

- **Antimitogenic and antiproliferative** — arrests cell cycle progression in malignant
  cell lines.
- **Anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory** — suppresses acute inflammatory signalling;
  in animal skin models it reduced papilloma formation after TPA-induced tumorigenesis.
- **Nrf2 activation and heme oxygenase-1 induction** — the most consistently reported
  mechanism. CAPE and its synthetic derivative VP961 restore the BACH1/NRF2 balance in
  Down syndrome cells (Pagnotta et al., *Free Radic Biol Med*, 2022), i.e. they shift
  the KEAP1–NRF2–ARE system toward its active arm.
- **Neuroprotection in traumatic brain injury** — reported reduction of oxidative damage.
- **Radiosensitisation** — a 2026 review of natural phenolic compounds in hepatocellular
  carcinoma includes CAPE among agents reported to sensitise tumour cells by perturbing
  Nrf2-mediated antioxidant defence, inhibiting DNA repair, or reversing hypoxia-driven
  metabolic reprogramming.

> [!info] Mechanism as currently understood
> CAPE is a lipophilic phenolic with an electrophilic alpha,beta-unsaturated ester. Two
> modes of action are proposed and both are consistent with the electrophile chemistry:
> (1) covalent modification of reactive cysteine thiols in [[Keap1]], which is the
> principal sensor of electrophiles and normally tethers [[NRF2]] in the cytoplasm —
> modification releases NRF2, which translocates to the nucleus and drives ARE-dependent
> genes including [[HO-1]], [[Glutathione]] synthesis enzymes and NADPH-generating enzymes;
> and (2) direct redox activity as a phenolic radical scavenger. The first mechanism
> predicts that CAPE is a *hormetin* — a low-dose stressor that upregulates defences
> rather than a simple radical scavenger.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> CAPE has no established therapeutic indication in humans. Its lipophilicity gives it
> poor aqueous solubility and variable oral bioavailability, and the concentrations used
> in cell work (typically 10–100 µM) are rarely reached in human plasma after oral
> dosing. Biosynthetic routes such as engineered-*E. coli* fermentation and nanoformulation
> are being explored to fix this, which is itself an admission that the exposure problem
> is unsolved. Propolis supplements vary enormously in CAPE content, so propolis studies
> are not interchangeable with purified CAPE studies.

## Connections

- [[Propolis]] — Propolis is the natural source of CAPE and the reason for its historical use as a folk medicine. Because propolis is a complex mixture, propolis efficacy studies cannot be attributed to CAPE specifically.
- [[NRF2]] — The principal reported mechanism of CAPE action is activation of the KEAP1–NRF2–ARE antioxidant response, inducing HO-1 and glutathione-related genes. CAPE and its derivative VP961 have been shown to restore the BACH1/NRF2 balance in Down syndrome cells.
- [[HO-1]] — Heme oxygenase-1 is the canonical downstream ARE-dependent gene product induced downstream of NRF2 activation by CAPE. It is the most frequently reported marker of CAPE's antioxidant-response activity.
- [[Keap1]] — Keap1 is the electrophile sensor that normally retains NRF2 in the cytoplasm. CAPE's alpha,beta-unsaturated ester is a candidate covalent electrophile for Keap1 cysteines, which is the mechanistic link between its chemical structure and its NRF2 effect.
- [[Polyphenols]] — CAPE is a phenolic polyphenol structurally derived from caffeic acid, and shares with the wider class the combination of antioxidant activity, electrophilic Michael chemistry, and poor bioavailability.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — CAPE's antioxidant and pro-oxidant character is dose- and context-dependent: it scavenges ROS at low concentrations and can generate radicals at high ones, which is the classic biphasic behaviour of phenolics.
- [[Hormesis]] — Because CAPE is an electrophilic stressor that upregulates defences rather than a purely scavenger, its reported benefits fit the hormetic (biphasic dose-response) model better than a simple antioxidant model.
- [[Radiotherapy]] — CAPE is reported as a radiosensitiser in hepatocellular carcinoma models, working by impairing NRF2-mediated antioxidant defence. The effect is described as context-dependent in the source review and is not established clinically.
- [[Antioxidant]] — CAPE is usually classed as an antioxidant on the basis of radical-scavenging assays in vitro, though the more interesting and better-supported framing is as a pro-antioxidant gene activator via NRF2.
