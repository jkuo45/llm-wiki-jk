# Synthetic Lethality

**Synthetic lethality** describes a situation where the simultaneous loss or inhibition of two genes, pathways, or cellular functions results in cell death, whereas loss of either one alone is compatible with survival. This concept has become a cornerstone of precision cancer therapy, as it allows selective killing of cancer cells that harbor specific genetic alterations while sparing normal cells.

## Principles

- **Genetic context**: Cancer cells often acquire mutations that disable one pathway (e.g., [[DNA Repair]]), creating dependence on a backup pathway. Inhibiting the backup pathway selectively kills cancer cells.
- **Therapeutic window**: Normal cells, which retain both pathways, survive the single inhibition — providing a therapeutic advantage.
- **Biomarker-driven**: Effective synthetic lethal strategies require molecular biomarkers to identify patients whose tumors have the relevant genetic alteration.

## Classical Examples

- **[[BRCA1]]/[[BRCA2]] mutations + [[PARP1]] inhibition**: BRCA-mutant cancers are defective in homologous recombination repair and are selectively killed by PARP inhibitors (olaparib, niraparib).
- **TP53 mutations + WEE1/CHK1 inhibition**: p53-deficient cancers rely on the G2/M checkpoint for DNA damage repair.

## MYC-Driven Synthetic Lethality

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]>
> [[MYC]]^high^ cancer cells exhibit metabolic dependencies that create synthetic lethal opportunities. Elevated [[OXPHOS]] and a [[Glutamine|glutamine-fueled]] [[TCA cycle]] render MYC^high^ cells selectively vulnerable to co-inhibition of [[Complex I]] and [[Glutaminolysis]]. Complex I inhibition depletes [[NAD+]], collapsing the TCA cycle, while [[Glutaminase]] inhibition (e.g., [[CB-839]]) blocks anaplerotic input. Neither alone is sufficient to kill, but combined inhibition produces persistent tumor growth suppression in MYC^high^ [[triple-negative breast cancer|TNBC]] PDX models.

Other MYC-associated synthetic lethal targets include cyclin-dependent kinases, anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2 family]] members, [[mTOR]], aurora-B kinase, and Polo-like kinase 1.
