# Argonaute

**Argonaute (Ago)** proteins are the effector subunits of small-RNA silencing pathways. They bind mature [[MicroRNA|miRNA]] or siRNA guides and, together with partner proteins, form the **[[RISC]]** (RNA-induced silencing complex) that represses translation or cleaves target mRNAs.

## Key features

- Contain a PIWI domain with endonucleolytic ("slicer") activity; mammalian **Argonaute2 (Ago2)** is the catalytically active slicer.
- Loaded with miRNA duplexes produced by [[Dicer]]; the guide strand directs sequence-specific targeting.
- In a HeLa reporter assay, knockdown of Ago2 (like knockdown of [[DGCR8]] or [[Drosha]]) derepresses an miR-21-targeted EGFP transcript, confirming that Microprocessor output requires Ago2 for in vivo silencing.

## Biological relevance

Argonaute-loaded miRNAs post-transcriptionally control hundreds of mRNAs, including regulators of [[Senescence|senescence]], inflammation, and differentiation. Reduced miRNA biogenesis (Microprocessor or [[Dicer]] loss) starves Argonaute of guides, derepressing programs that promote [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]].
