# TGFβ (Transforming Growth Factor-beta)

**TGFβ (transforming growth factor-beta)** is a multifunctional cytokine that acts as an early [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] factor involved in tissue remodelling, [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]], [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|EMT]], fibrosis, and immune suppression.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

TGFβ is secreted as a latent complex and activated in the extracellular space by integrins (αvβ6, αvβ8), proteases (MMPs), or thrombospondin-1. Active TGFβ binds the TGFBR2/TGFBR1 (ALK5) receptor complex, which phosphorylates receptor-regulated SMADs [[SMAD2]] and [[SMAD3]]; these complex with [[SMAD4]] and translocate to the nucleus to regulate transcription. Non-SMAD branches engage [[MAPK]], [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], and Rho GTPases. In senescence, TGFβ is an early-induced SASP component whose expression is later superseded by the NF-κB–driven arm; the [[Notch]] pathway helps orchestrate the switch from early (TGFβ-rich) to late (IL-6/IL-8-rich) SASP (reviewed in SASP Review 2024).

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- TGFβ is an early SASP factor that induces [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]] in neighboring cells.
- It promotes [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|EMT]] and fibroblast activation, driving fibrosis and wound/scar biology.
- It creates immune-suppressive conditions (Treg induction, T-cell inhibition) that can shield senescent cells from clearance.
- In aged mice, senescent-cell TGFβ reduces mesenchymal progenitor cells and delays fracture healing, linking senescence to [[Osteoporosis]]/impaired repair.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance
- TGFβ is a major therapeutic target in fibrosis and cancer (ALK5/SMAD inhibitors, integrin αvβ6 blockers).
- TGFβ modulation is a [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] strategy to limit harmful SASP while sparing beneficial senescence.
- Its dual tumor-suppressor/tumor-promoter role makes context-specific targeting essential.
