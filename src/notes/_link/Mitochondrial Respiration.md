# Mitochondrial Respiration

**Mitochondrial respiration** is the oxygen-consuming process by which the [[Mitochondria]] convert nutritional reducing equivalents into ATP. Reducing equivalents (NADH, FADH2) generated by the TCA cycle donate electrons to the [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain]] (Complexes I–IV); electron flow drives proton pumping that builds the [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] across the inner membrane, and the return of protons through [[ATP Synthase]] powers [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] and ATP production. This coupled process supplies the majority of cellular ATP.

## Control & Regulation

Respiration is tightly controlled by ADP availability (respiratory control): active ATP demand accelerates electron flux, whereas low ADP slows it. Key regulators include [[PGC-1α]] (mitochondrial biogenesis), [[AMPK]] (energy sensing), and [[mTORC1]] (anabolic control). A small fraction of electrons "leak" at Complex I and III, producing [[Reactive Oxygen Species|mtROS]]; modest leak underpins [[Mitohormesis]], an adaptive program that strengthens respiratory capacity.

## Pharmacological Modulation

[[Methylene blue]] is an electron cycler that can accept electrons from NADH and donate them downstream, effectively bypassing impaired Complex I/III. This restores mitochondrial respiration and oxygen consumption while reducing superoxide, a mechanism relevant in [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and certain neurodegenerative states. Uncouplers and inhibitors (e.g., oligomycin, rotenone) are classical tools that dissect coupling versus ATP synthesis.

## Relevance

Age-related decline in respiratory capacity contributes to fatigue, metabolic disease, and cellular senescence. Preserving efficient respiration is a core goal of [[Longevity]] and metabolic-health interventions.
