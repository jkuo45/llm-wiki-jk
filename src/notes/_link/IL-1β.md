# IL-1β

**Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β)** is a potent pro-inflammatory IL-1-family cytokine and a key component and upstream regulator of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]. Its production is often upregulated during chronic [[Inflammation]] and is closely linked with other pro-inflammatory markers like [[TNFα]] and the activation of the [[NFKB|NF-κB]] pathway.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

IL-1β is transcribed as an inactive 31-kDa precursor (pro-IL-1β) and requires two signals: a priming signal (e.g., [[NFKB|NF-κB]]) induces pro-IL-1β and NLRP3, and a danger signal activates the [[NLRP3]] inflammasome, which recruits ASC and [[Caspase-1]] to cleave pro-IL-1β into the mature 17-kDa secreted form. Mature IL-1β binds IL-1R1/IL-1RAcP to activate MyD88, driving [[NFKB|NF-κB]] and [[MAPK]] and inducing downstream SASP factors ([[IL-6]], [[IL-8]]) together with [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]]. Like IL-1α, IL-1β feeds forward to sustain the inflammatory secretome of [[Senescent Cells]].

Upon binding to the [[IL-1 Receptor|IL-1R1]], IL-1β activates [[MyD88]]-dependent signaling, leading to [[NFKB|NF-κB]] and [[p38 MAPK]] activation. NF-κB drives transcription of pro-inflammatory genes including *IL1B* itself, [[TNFα]], and [[COX-2]], while also upregulating antioxidant genes such as [[MnSOD]]. This dual action creates a context-dependent feedback loop: acute IL-1β signaling induces protective antioxidant responses, whereas chronic IL-1β exposure overwhelms these defenses, leading to sustained [[Oxidative Stress]] and tissue damage.

## Redox Regulation of IL-1β Production and Signaling

### NLRP3 Inflammasome Activation by ROS

Elevated [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], particularly mitochondrial ROS, promote NLRP3 oligomerization and ASC speck formation. The redox-sensitive protein [[TXNIP]] dissociates from [[Thioredoxin]] under oxidative conditions and directly binds NLRP3, serving as a critical molecular bridge between [[Oxidative Stress]] and IL-1β secretion.

### Mitochondrial DAMPs and Lysosomal Rupture

[[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] releases [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) and [[Cardiolipin]] into the cytosol, both of which act as damage-associated molecular patterns ([[DAMP]]s) that engage the NLRP3 inflammasome. Additionally, lysosomal rupture — caused by phagocytosis of [[Silica]], [[Uric Acid Crystals]], or [[Amyloid Beta]] aggregates — releases [[Cathepsin B]] and other lysosomal proteases into the cytosol, providing a second signal for NLRP3 activation. Lysosomal membrane permeabilization is potentiated by [[Lipid Peroxidation]] of lysosomal membranes.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

IL-1β is produced by [[Senescent Cells]] and acts as a master regulator of SASP:
- It induces expression of other SASP factors such as [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]].
- It activates [[NFKB|NF-κB]] and [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]] signaling that maintains the senescent phenotype.
- It promotes [[Paracrine Senescence]] in neighboring cells and recruits innate immune cells.

## Sirtuin Regulation of IL-1β

[[SIRT1]] deacetylation of [[NFKB|NF-κB]] downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-1β. [[SIRT7]] kidney-specific knockout reduces IL-1β and other inflammatory factors.

## IL-1β in Neuroinflammation and Neurodegeneration

In the central nervous system, IL-1β is produced primarily by [[Microglia]] and [[Astrocytes]] in response to [[Amyloid Beta]] plaques, [[Alpha-Synuclein]] aggregates, and [[ATP]] released from damaged neurons. Elevated IL-1β in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]] drives synaptic dysfunction, [[Glutamate]] excitotoxicity, and [[Neurodegeneration]]. Antagonizing IL-1β signaling in animal models of neurodegeneration reduces both oxidative damage and cognitive decline.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- Elevated IL-1β is a driver of [[Inflammaging]] and a therapeutic target in gout, rheumatoid arthritis, and atherosclerosis.
- NLRP3 inflammasome activation in senescent cells makes IL-1β a [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] target.
- Chronic IL-1β signaling contributes to age-related metabolic and cardiovascular disease.
- The pathogenic role of IL-1β is best demonstrated by the efficacy of [[Anakinra]] (recombinant IL-1 receptor antagonist), [[Canakinumab]] (anti-IL-1β monoclonal antibody), and [[Rilonacept]] (IL-1 trap).
- The landmark [[CANTOS Trial]] (Canakinumab Anti-inflammatory Thrombosis Outcomes Study) showed that IL-1β inhibition reduces [[Cardiovascular Disease]] events independent of lipid lowering — the first clinical proof that targeting [[Inflammation]] can prevent cardiovascular events. This underscores the central role of IL-1β as a nexus linking [[Oxidative Stress]], [[Autoinflammation]], and [[Atherosclerosis]].
