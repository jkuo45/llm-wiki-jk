# MIP-2

**MIP-2** (macrophage inflammatory protein 2; CXCL2) is a CXC-family chemokine that signals through CXCR2 to recruit neutrophils (polymorphonuclear leukocytes) into inflamed tissues. In mice, MIP-2 is the functional counterpart of human IL-8 (CXCL8) and is produced by macrophages, endothelial cells, and epithelial cells in response to [[NF-κB]]-activating stimuli such as LPS, TNF, and IL-1. Together with its related chemokine CXCL1, MIP-2 is a primary driver of the early neutrophil influx that characterizes acute inflammation and tissue injury.

## SIRT3 and MIP-2

The SIRT3 review reports that [[SIRT3]] activation consistently correlates with reduced expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as [[TNFα|TNF-α]], [[IL-6]], and MIP-2, as well as diminished polymorphonuclear leukocyte infiltration in various preclinical models[141],[142]. Because NF-κB directly transactivates the MIP-2 gene, SIRT3-mediated suppression of [[NF-κB]] signaling (via [[IκBα]] stabilization and nuclear translocation inhibition) provides the mechanistic link between mitochondrial deacetylase activity and chemokine output. Reduced MIP-2 is therefore a downstream readout of the anti-inflammatory program engaged by SIRT3 activation.

## Clinical relevance

Chemokine-driven neutrophil recruitment fuels acute kidney injury, ischemia-reperfusion injury, sepsis, and chronic inflammatory disease. Lowering MIP-2/CXCL2 levels is one component of the leukocyte-infiltration suppression observed upon SIRT3 overexpression, supporting the therapeutic framework of SIRT3 activation for aging-related inflammatory disorders.
