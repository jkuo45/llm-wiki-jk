# Sphingosine-1-phosphate

**Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P)** is a phosphorylated derivative of [[Sphingosine|sphingosine]], produced by sphingosine kinases. It is a potent bioactive signaling lipid that binds a family of G-protein-coupled receptors (S1P receptors) and broadly promotes **pro-survival, pro-proliferative, and anti-[[Apoptosis|apoptotic]]** responses—functionaly opposing [[Ceramide|ceramide]].

## Role in Senescence & Ferroptosis

Over-expression of [[Acid ceramidase]] in [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] raises [[Sphingosine|sphingosine]] and S1P levels, which supports senescent-cell survival (anti-apoptotic). This same ACase activity, however, inadvertently creates a **pro-[[Ferroptosis|ferroptotic]] lipid profile** by releasing free fatty acids from [[Ceramide|ceramide]] catabolism that feed membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipid]] [[PUFA|PUFA]] enrichment.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Acid_ceramidase_modulates_the_lipid_profile_and_ex|Acid ceramidase modulates the lipid profile… (Soriano-Castell et al., 2026)]]
> ACase breaks [[Ceramide|ceramide]] into [[Sphingosine|sphingosine]] + free fatty acid; sphingosine is phosphorylated to S1P. The sphingosine/S1P arm is classically pro-survival, yet the ACase-driven lipid remodeling simultaneously sensitizes senescent cells to [[Ferroptosis]]—a context-dependent dual role relevant to [[Melanoma]] and [[Prostate Cancer]] where ACase is over-expressed.

## Connections

- [[Sphingosine]] — Immediate precursor, phosphorylated to S1P.
- [[Ceramide]] — The rheostat partner; high ceramide = pro-death, high S1P = pro-survival.
- [[Acid ceramidase]] — Produces sphingosine (→ S1P) from ceramide; its over-expression raises S1P.
- [[Sphingomyelin]] — Part of the same sphingolipid network.
- [[Senescent Cells]] — S1P supports senescent-cell survival while ACase activity increases ferroptotic vulnerability.
- [[Ferroptosis]] — S1P-producing ACase activity indirectly sensitizes cells via lipid remodeling.
- [[Apoptosis]] — S1P is anti-apoptotic.
- [[Melanoma]] / [[Prostate Cancer]] — Cancers in which the ACase→S1P axis is pathologically implicated.
