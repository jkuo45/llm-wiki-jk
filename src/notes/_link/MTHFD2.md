# MTHFD2

**MTHFD2** (methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase 2, NAD⁺-dependent, mitochondrial) is a bifunctional enzyme of the **mitochondrial one-carbon metabolism** network. It catalyzes the NAD⁺-dependent oxidation of **5,10-methylene-THF to 5,10-methenyl-THF** and its cyclohydrolase conversion to **10-formyl-THF**, producing **NADH** that is transhydrogenated to **NADPH** in the mitochondrial matrix. By generating mitochondrial NADPH, MTHFD2 links one-carbon (folate) metabolism to both nucleotide biosynthesis and cellular **redox defense**.

## Structure & Mechanism

- **Mitochondrial localization:** MTHFD2 is a mitochondrial matrix enzyme; its cytosolic counterpart is MTHFD1.
- **Bifunctional activity:** combines NAD⁺-dependent methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase and methenyltetrahydrofolate cyclohydrolase activities.
- **One-carbon entry point:** MTHFD2 acts immediately downstream of [[SHMT2]] (which converts [[Serine]] to glycine + 5,10-methylene-THF) in the mitochondrial folate cycle — the dominant source of one-carbon units and mitochondrial NADPH in proliferating/cancer cells.

## Role in Cancer Metabolism & Redox

MTHFD2 is markedly upregulated in many cancers and is a validated target, because its two outputs serve tumor needs:

- **NADPH generation:** MTHFD2 is a major mitochondrial source of **NADPH** — the reducing currency for [[Glutathione]]/[[Thioredoxin]] regeneration that counters mitochondrial [[Reactive Oxygen Species]].
- **Nucleotide synthesis:** 10-formyl-THF is a one-carbon donor for purine/tetrahydrofolate-dependent nucleotide synthesis, supporting proliferation.
- **Metabolic plasticity:** because it generates NADPH while feeding folate/one-carbon flux, MTHFD2 sits at a junction where carbon metabolism and redox defense converge — a contributor to the glycolysis↔OXPHOS adaptability of tumors (see [[Metabolic Plasticity]]).

## SIRT3 Regulation & the Cisplatin Connection

> [!info] Source: Wan et al., *Cell Death Dis* 2020;11:649 (doi:10.1038/s41419-020-02825-y)
> **[[SIRT3]] deacetylates and activates MTHFD2**, boosting mitochondrial NADPH and sustaining cellular redox balance. In colorectal cancer, **[[Cisplatin]] inhibits SIRT3-mediated deacetylation of MTHFD2**, disturbing cellular redox balance and contributing to the drug's cytotoxic effect. This places MTHFD2 as a downstream effector through which SIRT3's redox-shaping activity modulates chemosensitivity: high SIRT3 activity keeps MTHFD2 active (redox-protective, chemoresistance), whereas SIRT3 inhibition (e.g., by cisplatin) deactivates MTHFD2, depleting NADPH and sensitizing cells to oxidative/chemotherapeutic stress.

MTHFD2 is thus an integral part of the SIRT3 ferroptosis-defense and redox network — alongside [[IDH2]], [[SOD2]], and [[SLC25A22]] — that links mitochondrial metabolic flux to antioxidant capacity and therapy response.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Therapeutic target:** MTHFD2 inhibition is being explored as an anti-metabolite/anti-cancer strategy (analogous to classical antifolates) that also impairs redox defense.
- **Chemosensitization:** disrupting the SIRT3-MTHFD2 redox axis depletes NADPH and can sensitize tumor cells to oxidative and DNA-damaging therapies.
- **Biomarker:** high MTHFD2 expression correlates with aggressive tumor behavior and poor prognosis; its activity reflects both proliferation and redox-buffering capacity.
