# Apoptotic Bodies

**Apoptotic Bodies** are 1–5 µm membrane-sealed vesicles shed by blebbing apoptotic cells, packaging condensed chromatin fragments, organelles, and cytosol. Externalized phosphatidylserine ([[Annexin V]]-positive) marks them as "eat-me" signals for macrophage efferocytosis — the silent-clearance step that makes [[Apoptosis]] non-inflammatory.

When efferocytosis fails (notably in vitro without phagocytes), apoptotic bodies lose membrane integrity and progress to [[Secondary Necrosis]], converting quiet death into DAMP-releasing lysis. Their presence without swelling distinguishes apoptosis morphologically from necrotic rupture.
