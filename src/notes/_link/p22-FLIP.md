# p22-FLIP

**p22-FLIP** is the 22 kDa N-terminal fragment produced when [[Caspase-8]] cleaves [[c-FLIP]]
(FLIP_L or FLIP_S) at Asp198 (Asp196 numbering in some isoforms; human-specific) in cytosolic,
[[FADD]]-independent heterodimers. Containing only DED1–DED2, it binds [[NEMO]] (IKKγ) directly and
is among the most potent known activators of the [[IKK complex]] → NF-κB axis — the survival-signaling
counterpart to its sibling fragment [[p43-FLIP]].

## Structure & Generation

- **Cleavage:** cytosolic procaspase-8/FLIP heterodimers cut at Asp198, releasing DED1–DED2 (p22)
  from the (pseudo-)caspase remnant; unlike Asp376 processing, this occurs off the [[DISC]].
- **Binding surface:** the tandem DEDs dock [[NEMO]]'s coiled-coil region — the same interface used
  by viral [[vFLIP]]s, explaining why KSHV vFLIP and p22-FLIP are functionally interchangeable NF-κB
  agonists.
- **Human-specific:** Asp196-dependent p22 generation occurs in human but not mouse FLIP_L, a
  species caveat for knockout-model interpretation.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **IKK super-activation.** p22–[[NEMO]] binding drives [[IKK complex]] activity and canonical NF-κB
  transcription without any death-receptor input — survival signaling generated as a by-product of
  rheostat firing.
- **Rheostat bookkeeping.** Every p22 molecule records one cytosolic heterodimer cycle; p22
  accumulation therefore reports cumulative [[Caspase-8-c-FLIP Rheostat]] throughput while
  simultaneously raising the apoptotic threshold via NF-κB-induced *CFLAR*/*BCL2L1* — a positive
  feedback stabilizing the survival state.

## Physiological Function

In activated T cells, TCR-driven [[Caspase-8]] generates p22-FLIP to license NF-κB-dependent
proliferation; in barrier cells it converts sublethal death-receptor flux into protective gene
expression.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- Tumors with chronic low-level [[DISC]] flux accumulate p22 and run constitutive NF-κB —
  chemoresistance with an inflammatory signature; disrupting the p22–[[NEMO]] interface is a
  proposed tumor-selective strategy.
- Viral mimicry: KSHV [[vFLIP]] occupies the same [[NEMO]] site, hard-wiring infected cells to
  NF-κB survival.
