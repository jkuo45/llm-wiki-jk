# METTL3

**METTL3** (methyltransferase-like 3) is the catalytic subunit of the METTL3–METTL14 heterodimer, the principal mammalian "writer" of N6-methyladenosine ([[N6-methyladenosine|m6A]]) methylation on nuclear RNAs. Beyond its canonical role in RNA modification, METTL3 negatively regulates autophagy flux by decreasing [[TFEB]] expression via m6A modification and, in an enzymatic-activity-independent manner, promotes enhancer–promoter contacts that drive expression of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].

## Overview

METTL3 is a ~100 kDa S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)-dependent methyltransferase belonging to the seven-β-strand methyltransferase fold family. It forms a stable heterodimer with [[METTL14]], which acts as a scaffolding/structural subunit that stimulates METTL3 catalytic activity and helps target the complex to its RNA substrates. The complex is frequently found in larger assemblies (WTAP, VIRMA, ZC3H13, RBM15) that direct m6A deposition near stop codons and 3′ untranslated regions of thousands of mRNAs and noncoding RNAs.

## Structure & Function

The METTL3 catalytic core contains a characteristic DPPW motif and a guanosine triphosphate-binding pocket that positions the target adenine for methyl transfer. METTL14 binds RNA backbone nucleotides and orients the substrate relative to the METTL3 active site. METTL3 installs [[m6A Modification]] on mRNA, regulating splicing, nuclear export, translation, and decay; m6A deposition is read by YTH-domain "reader" proteins.

## Autophagy Regulation

METTL3-mediated m6A methylation of autophagy gene transcripts (e.g., [[ULK1]], [[Beclin1]], [[LC3B]]) modulates their expression. Dysregulated m6A in autophagy genes is implicated in cancer and metabolic disease. In addition, highly expressed METTL3 negatively regulates autophagy flux by decreasing the expression of [[TFEB]] in hypoxia/reoxygenation-treated cardiomyocytes, identifying two m6A consensus sequences (5′-RRACU-3′) in target transcripts.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The review highlights a moonlighting, catalytic-independent function of the METTL3–METTL14 complex in senescence. Upon senescence entry, the complex undergoes genome-wide redistribution and is enriched at enhancers of SASP genes (Iwasaki et al., 2019; PMID 31729328; ref. 48 in source). Rather than methylating RNA, the complex facilitates chromatin looping that juxtaposes SASP promoters with distal enhancers, increasing SASP transcription in oncogene-induced senescence ([[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]]). This effect is independent of METTL3 methyltransferase activity, distinguishing it from the complex's RNA-modifying role. The phenomenon places METTL3 within the 3D-genome rewiring axis that includes [[Condensin]] binding at B-to-A compartment-switching regions and boundary proteins such as [[HMGB2]].

## Mechanistic Details

- Enhancer remodeling at the TAD scale is a hallmark of senescence; SASP enhancers become hyper-connected to their promoters, correlating with elevated SASP transcription.
- METTL3–METTL14 occupancy at these enhancers is required for efficient EP looping, suggesting the complex acts as a structural platform or co-activator rather than an RNA writer in this context.
- The m6A pathway intersects with other epigenetic readers, such as [[BRD4]] (recognizing [[H3K27ac]]) and the pioneer factor [[AP-1]], both of which converge on senescence-activated enhancers.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Because METTL3–METTL14 and other epigenetic modulators can suppress SASP without rescinding the senescence growth arrest, they are candidates for [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] repurposing. Small-molecule METTL3 inhibitors (e.g., STM2457) are in preclinical oncology studies; their ability to blunt SASP-associated inflammation via 3D-genome rewiring remains an open translational question.
