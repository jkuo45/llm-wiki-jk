# Vascular Endothelial Injury

**Vascular endothelial injury** denotes damage to the endothelial monolayer that lines every
blood and lymphatic vessel — the single most exposed tissue in the body, since it interfaces
directly with the entire blood volume. Unlike [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] (a functional
deficit that can be reversible), injury implies structural damage: loss of the luminal
glycocalyx, disruption of intercellular junctions, cytoskeletal collapse, and in severe
cases cell detachment and death.

## What the endothelium does, and therefore what injury costs

The intact endothelium is not a passive lining. It:

- Maintains a non-thrombogenic surface, expressing thrombomodulin and CD39.
- Controls vascular tone via [[Nitric Oxide]] and hydrogen sulfide release.
- Sets permeability through adherens junctions anchored by VE-cadherin.
- Transmits mechanical signals: shear stress is sensed and transduced through the
  glycocalyx and the PECAM-1/VEGFR2 complex, driving KLF2/4-mediated atheroprotective gene
  expression.
- Filters plasma through its glycocalyx, which excludes albumin and maintains colloid
  osmotic pressure.

Injury therefore simultaneously releases a pro-coagulant surface, removes vasodilator tone,
and lets plasma protein and fluid into the interstitium.

## Glycocalyx: the first casualty

The glycocalyx is a meshwork of proteoglycans ([[LAMP1]]-adjacent syndecans, CD44) bearing
glycosaminoglycan chains (heparan sulfate, chondroitin sulfate, hyaluronan) covering the
luminal surface. It is the first structure damaged and usually precedes overt endothelial
damage.

Sheddases responsible include [[Matrix Metalloproteinase|matrix metalloproteinases]] (MMP-2,
MMP-9), ADAM15, heparanase, hyaluronidase, and neutrophil elastase. In
ischemia-reperfusion, reactive oxygen species directly strip heparan sulfate side chains
without affecting synthesis; oxidative stress also raises MMP activity and lowers TIMP-1
and TIMP-3, compounding the loss. Shear stress and atrial natriuretic peptide contribute.

> [!warning] Clinical significance of glycocalyx shedding
> Circulating syndecan-1 and heparan sulfate are the clinical readouts. Elevated syndecan-1
> predicts mortality in trauma, correlates with mortality in septic shock, and predicts
> acute kidney injury in decompensated heart failure. Soluble thrombomodulin, released only
> on direct cell disruption, predicted death better than syndecan-1 in sepsis. After
> ischemic stroke, plasma heparan sulfate, keratan and chondroitin sulfate, CD44,
> syndecan-2 and syndecan-3 all rise within a week.

Shedding also removes docking sites for complement regulators and anticoagulant plasma
proteins, and the released heparan sulfate fragments themselves activate leukocytes and
platelets — a self-amplifying loop.

## Causes and consequences

| Insult | Typical setting | Characteristic damage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ischemia-reperfusion | Stroke, MI, transplant | Glycocalyx thinning within 5 min of reperfusion; permeability rise; ROS burst on reperfusion |
| Mechanical | Balloon angioplasty, stenting, surgery, shear | Focal denudation; loss of barrier and antithrombotic surface |
| Oxidative | Hyperglycemia, sepsis, smoking | Direct GAG stripping, MMP upregulation |
| Inflammatory | TNF-α, IL-1β, LPS, COVID-19 | Adhesion molecule induction, junction loosening, EndMT |
| Metabolic | Hyperglycaemia, oxidised LDL | Junction protein and glycocalyx degradation |

Downstream consequences include increased permeability and oedema; leukocyte adhesion and
transmigration; platelet adhesion and microthrombus formation; and, chronically,
[[EndMT|endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition]] with loss of endothelial identity.

## Transcriptional protection: the SIRT6–GATA5 axis

[[SIRT6]] protects the endothelium from injury by deacetylating histone H3 within the
*Nkx3.2* locus, silencing this repressor and thereby **increasing [[GATA5]] transcription**.
GATA5 then drives a programme that suppresses [[EndMT]], lowers oxidative stress, and
preserves barrier integrity. SIRT6 loss — which occurs with age, since SIRT6 is
[[NAD+]]-dependent — reduces GATA5, worsens endothelial injury, and accelerates
[[Atherosclerosis]]. This is one of the more concrete molecular handles on
age-associated vascular injury.

## Connections

- [[GATA5]] — the endothelial transcription factor whose loss-of-regulation is a principal
  route to endothelial injury; [[SIRT6]] maintains it.
- [[SIRT6]] — the chromatin regulator upstream of GATA5; its age-related decline is a
  candidate driver of vascular ageing.
- [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] — the functional counterpart of structural injury; the two
  coexist and reinforce each other in most vascular disease.
- [[Matrix Metalloproteinase]] — the main sheddase class degrading the glycocalyx and the
  junctions; balance with TIMP-1/[[TIMP3]] sets the threshold.
- [[TIMP3]] — the ECM-anchored broad-spectrum MMP and ADAM inhibitor whose loss removes the
  brake on glycocalyx shedding and cytokine release.
- [[Nitric Oxide]] — endothelial-derived vasodilator and antiplatelet signal lost on injury,
  and itself protective of the glycocalyx when supplied during reperfusion.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — the direct chemical agent of glycocalyx stripping and a
  major amplifier of ischemic and septic injury.
- [[Ischemia]] — the upstream insult in reperfusion injury, the canonical and most damaging
  form of endothelial injury.
- [[EndMT]] — the chronic fate of repeatedly injured endothelium, converting it toward a
  mesenchymal phenotype.
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — the chronic disease whose initiation and progression depend on
  repeated endothelial injury at hemodynamically stressed sites.
