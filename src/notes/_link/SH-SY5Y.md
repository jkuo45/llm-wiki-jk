## Overview

**SH-SY5Y** is a human neuroblastoma cell line originally subcloned from the SK-N-SH parental line. Upon exposure to retinoic acid, phorbol esters, or brain-derived neurotrophic factor, SH-SY5Y cells adopt a neuronal phenotype with neurite outgrowth and acquire biochemical features of sympathetic [[Dopaminergic Neurons]].

## Origin and Genotype

Derived from a metastatic bone marrow neuroblastoma, SH-SY5Y retains catecholaminergic markers (tyrosine hydroxylase, dopa decarboxylase) and synthesizes and stores dopamine. It expresses neuromelanin-precursor machinery and is prone to oxidative stress due to dopamine metabolism.

## Common Research Uses

SH-SY5Y is a principal *in vitro* model for [[Parkinson's Disease]], toxin-based neurodegeneration (MPP+, 6-OHDA, rotenone), and neuroprotective compound screening. It is used to study [[Apoptosis]], mitochondrial dysfunction, and α-synuclein aggregation in [[Neurodegeneration]].

## Relevance to Neuroscience and Aging

The line enables mechanistic dissection of dopaminergic neuron loss, oxidative damage, and [[Inflammation]]-driven injury relevant to aging brains. Its amenability to high-throughput screening makes it valuable for repurposing neuroprotective agents, though it lacks the full circuitry and glial interactions of intact tissue.
