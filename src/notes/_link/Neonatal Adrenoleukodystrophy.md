# Neonatal Adrenoleukodystrophy

Neonatal Adrenoleukodystrophy (NALD) is an intermediate form of Zellweger Spectrum Disorders (ZSD), a group of autosomal recessive peroxisomal biogenesis disorders. NALD results from mutations in PEX genes (most commonly [[PEX1]]). It is distinct from [[X-linked Adrenoleukodystrophy]] (X-ALD). NALD presents in the neonatal/infantile period with progressive neurological deterioration, adrenal insufficiency, and dysmorphic features.

## Genetics & Etiology

NALD is caused by autosomal recessive mutations in PEX genes essential for peroxisome biogenesis: *PEX1* (~60% of ZSD), *PEX2*, *PEX5*, *PEX6*, *PEX10*, *PEX12*, *PEX13*, *PEX14*, and *PEX26*. The most common allele is *PEX1*-G843D (c.2528G>A, p.Gly843Asp), a hypomorphic allele that retains partial peroxisome function, accounting for the milder phenotype compared to [[Zellweger Syndrome]]. Peroxisome biogenesis failure leads to deficiency of all peroxisomal metabolic functions: β-oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids (VLCFA), α-oxidation of phytanic acid, plasmalogen synthesis, and bile acid synthesis. The accumulation of toxic VLCFA and phytanic acid, combined with plasmalogen deficiency, drives the progressive neurodegeneration and multisystem pathology.

## Clinical Presentation

Onset occurs in the neonatal or early infantile period (first months of life). NALD represents an intermediate severity within the Zellweger Spectrum — less severe than [[Zellweger Syndrome]] (lethal in infancy) but more severe than [[Infantile Refsum Disease]].

**Dysmorphic features** (milder than Zellweger Syndrome): high forehead, epicanthal folds, flat nasal bridge, hypotonia.

**Neurological**: Progressive white matter demyelination leading to psychomotor regression, hypotonia evolving to spasticity, seizures, sensorineural hearing loss, and visual impairment (retinopathy, cataracts, optic atrophy).

**Endocrine**: Adrenal insufficiency (may be subclinical; requires monitoring).

**Hepatic**: Hepatomegaly, elevated liver enzymes, coagulopathy, cholestasis.

**Other**: Growth failure, feeding difficulties, and developmental delay.

## Diagnosis

**Biochemical markers**:
- Elevated VLCFA in plasma (C26:0, C26:0/C22:0 ratio, C24:0/C22:0 ratio)
- Decreased plasmalogens in erythrocytes
- Elevated phytanic acid (accumulates with age/dietary exposure)
- Abnormal bile acid intermediates (DHCA, THCA)

**Enzymatic studies**: Impaired peroxisomal β-oxidation and plasmalogen synthesis in cultured fibroblasts.

**Genetic testing**: Sequencing of PEX genes confirms biallelic pathogenic variants.

**Neuroimaging**: Brain MRI reveals white matter abnormalities — demyelination, periventricular leukodystrophy, and delayed myelination.

**Differential diagnosis**:
- [[Zellweger Syndrome]] — more severe, earlier lethality
- [[X-linked Adrenoleukodystrophy]] — later onset, X-linked inheritance, distinct pathophysiology
- [[Infantile Refsum Disease]] — milder form of ZSD
- Other leukodystrophies (e.g., [[Metachromatic Leukodystrophy]], [[Krabbe Disease]])

## Treatment & Prognosis

No curative treatment exists. Management is supportive and multidisciplinary.

**Dietary**: VLCFA-restricted diet (avoid foods high in C26:0), phytanic-acid restricted diet (avoid dairy fat, ruminant meats). Supplementation with DHA/plasmalogen precursors may be considered.

**Bile acid therapy**: Cholic acid and ursodeoxycholic acid to improve liver function and reduce accumulation of toxic bile acid intermediates.

**Symptomatic**:
- Anti-epileptic drugs for seizure control
- Adrenal hormone replacement ([[Hydrocortisone]]) for adrenal insufficiency
- Physical, occupational, and speech therapy for developmental support
- Hearing aids or cochlear implants for hearing loss
- Vision support and cataract management

**Prognosis**: Survival into childhood or early adolescence. Progressive neurodegeneration leads to severe disability. Death typically results from respiratory failure, infection, or complications of neurodegeneration.
