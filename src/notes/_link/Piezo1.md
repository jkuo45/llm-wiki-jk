# Piezo1

**Piezo1** is a mechanosensitive nonselective cation channel that converts membrane tension into cation (mostly Ca2+) influx. In erythrocytes it is a route of stimulus-independent-of-ligand Ca2+ entry that can trigger [[Eryptosis]] when cells are mechanically stressed or when particles accumulate outside the cell surface.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Mechanical stress induces erythrocyte Ca2+ influx through Piezo1, and pristine (non-internalized) SiO2 and LaVO4:Eu3+ nanoparticles trigger Ca2+-dependent eryptosis that is attributed to their direct influence on Piezo1 channels. Nanoparticles and high-molecular-weight polysaccharides can therefore induce eryptosis without being taken up.

## Mechanism and relevance

- Mechanosensitive channel: membrane stretch/tension opens the pore; erythrocytes express Piezo1 and use it as a Ca2+ entry route alongside TRP channels, glutamate receptors and Cav2.1.
- Any Piezo1-mediated Ca2+ rise feeds the same downstream machinery as other eryptosis triggers: [[Gardos Channel|Gardos channel]] K+ loss (shrinkage), [[Scramblase]] activation (PS exposure), [[Calpain]] (blebbing).
- In nucleated cells Piezo1 also regulates endothelial and immune tone, so it is a pharmacological target with a wider safety liability than an erythrocyte-restricted intervention.
