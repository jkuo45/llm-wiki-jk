# SNAP29

**SNAP29** (synaptosome-associated protein 29 kDa; also called GS32) is a **Q-SNARE** protein of the [[SNAP25]] family encoded on human chromosome 22q11.21. It is a near-ubiquitous, soluble ~29 kDa protein that acts as the v-SNARE half of a trans-SNARE complex, pairing with a single-helical Qa-SNARE of the syntaxin family to drive membrane fusion.

> [!info] Mechanism
> SNAP29 is a **promiscuous syntaxin binder** — it engages syntaxin 3, syntaxin 5, syntaxin 6, STX17, and others — which is unusual for a family whose other members (SNAP25, SNAP23) are comparatively syntaxin-selective. This broad binding is what allows one SNAP29 molecule to serve in several distinct trafficking steps. Its N-terminal three-helical SNARE domain supplies the "Q" (glutamine) layer of the four-helix trans-SNARE bundle, while its long coiled-coil middle domain mediates SNAP complex oligomerization. Unlike SNAP25, SNAP29 is not anchored to the plasma membrane: it is largely membrane-bound on internal structures with a significant free cytosolic pool.

## Roles in membrane trafficking

- **Autophagosome maturation.** SNAP29 is a core component of the autophagosomal SNARE machinery, docking at the autophagosome membrane and partnering with STX17 to permit fusion with endosomes and lysosomes. Autophagosome-lysosome fusion is the rate-limiting step of [[Autophagic Flux]], so SNAP29 sits on the critical path of [[Autophagy]].
- **Golgi and ER-Golgi traffic.** SNAP29 operates at the Golgi in complexes with syntaxin 6 and syntaxin 5, contributing to intra-Golgi and ER-to-Golgi vesicle fusion.
- **Synaptic vesicle priming.** Despite the "synaptosome-associated" name, SNAP29 modulates neurotransmitter release by inhibiting SNARE disassembly; SNAP-29 knockdown and overexpression both alter synaptic transmission in cultured hippocampal neurons.
- **Cilia and other compartments.** GO annotations place SNAP29 at ciliary membranes, azurophil granule membranes, and neutrophil degranulation contexts.

## Regulation and pathology

**Enteroviruses target SNAP29.** Enterovirus and coxsackievirus 3C proteases cleave SNAP29, disrupting the SNARE complex; this blocks autophagic flux, and the resulting accumulation of unreduced autophagosomes is exploited to enhance viral replication and cell exit. The same two 2018 reports independently identified SNAP29 as a host target hijacked to suppress antiviral autophagy.

**CEDNIK syndrome.** A homozygous loss-of-function mutation in SNAP29 causes CEDNIK syndrome (cerebral dysgenesis, neuropathy, ichthyosis, and palmoplantar keratoderma), a neurocutaneous disorder described by Sprecher et al. (2005, PMID 15968592). The phenotype makes sense from SNAP29's biology: disrupted membrane trafficking impairs both keratinocyte differentiation (ichthyosis, palmoplantar keratoderma) and neuronal development.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> SNAP29 is a broadly used trafficking factor, so loss-of-function phenotypes are pleiotropic and hard to attribute to a single pathway. Claims that "SNAP29 is required for autophagy" should be read as "required for autophagosome-lysosome fusion," since early autophagosome formation is unaffected.
