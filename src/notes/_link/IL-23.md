# IL-23

**Interleukin-23** is a heterodimeric cytokine of the IL-12 family. It was identified in 2000 by Oppmann and colleagues as the pairing of a newly discovered, IL-23-exclusive **p19** subunit (*IL23A*) with **p40** (*IL12B*), the subunit shared with [[IL-12]] — which pairs p40 with a unique p35 subunit instead. IL-23 is a pro-inflammatory cytokine that acts as an upstream regulator of the type-17 immune axis.

## Receptor and Signalling

IL-23 signals through a heterodimeric receptor composed of **IL-23R** (the specific chain) and **IL-12Rβ1** (shared with the IL-12 receptor). The receptor is associated with JAK2 (on IL-23R) and Tyk2 (on IL-12Rβ1). Ligand binding drives phosphorylation of IL-23R tyrosines, which dock [[STAT3]]; phospho-STAT3 dimerises, enters the nucleus and induces IL-17A, IL-17F, [[IL-22]], GM-CSF, [[Interferon]]-γ and TNF-α transcription, and upregulates RORγt and IL-23R itself — a positive feedback loop that stabilises the pathogenic state.

> [!important] Maintenance, not initiation
> IL-23 cannot initiate Th17 differentiation: IL-23R is absent on naïve T cells. Differentiation is driven by IL-6, IL-1β and TGF-β via RORγt; IL-23 then takes over to expand and maintain the population. Human IL-23R variants (R381Q, L310P) confer protection against psoriasis, one of the earliest genetic links to this axis.

## Producers and Responding Cells

IL-23 is produced mainly by tissue-resident myeloid cells — dendritic cells, [[Macrophages]] and Langerhans cells — in response to Toll-like receptor signalling, TNF-α, IFN-γ and fungal products sensed through C-type lectin receptors. Receptors are expressed on Th17 cells, memory T cells, γδ T cells, NK T cells, innate lymphoid cells and keratinocytes themselves. IL-23 also promotes expansion of tissue-resident memory T cells and suppresses regulatory T cell differentiation, which is why it is linked to the chronicity of immune-mediated inflammatory disease rather than only its onset.

## Clinical Relevance

- **[[Psoriasis]]** — p19 and p40 mRNA are elevated in lesional but not non-lesional skin, while p35 is not; IL-23 levels fall with clinical improvement. Intradermal IL-23 injection reproduces psoriasiform histology in mice.
- **[[Crohn's Disease]] and [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]]** — IL-23p19 is elevated in intestinal mucosa and IL-23-deficient mice are resistant to colitis in several models.
- **Targeted therapy** — three selective anti-p19 monoclonal antibodies are approved for plaque psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis, with superior head-to-head efficacy against adalimumab, secukinumab and the p40-directed ustekinumab. Because p19 is IL-23-exclusive, IL-12-mediated Th1 immunity against mycobacteria is spared; no specific opportunistic-infection signal has emerged.
- **[[Anti-TNF Therapy]]**, cyclosporine and phototherapy all reduce IL-23 levels, consistent with its position upstream in the same circuit.

> [!warning] Shared subunit complicates attribution
> Anti-p40 drugs block IL-12 and IL-23 simultaneously, so efficacy cannot be assigned to IL-23 alone without p19-specific comparators. IL-12 also appears protective in skin, and mutations in IL12B raise prostate cancer risk — an argument for p19-selective targeting.

## Connections
- [[IL-12]] — Shares the p40 subunit with IL-23 while diverging in the p35/p19 partner, so p40-directed therapies cannot distinguish the two; IL-12 drives Th1 while IL-23 sustains Th17.
- [[Th17]] — IL-23 is the survival and expansion signal for Th17 cells, acting after IL-6/IL-1β/TGF-β have initiated their differentiation.
- [[Psoriasis]] — The IL-23/IL-17 axis is the dominant pathogenic circuit in psoriasis, and IL-23p19 inhibition is an established first-line biologic strategy.
- [[Dendritic Cells]] — Dermal dendritic cells are the principal physiological source of IL-23 in psoriatic skin and in response to innate pathogen signals.
- [[STAT3]] — STAT3 is the principal downstream transcription factor of IL-23R signalling and also reinforces IL-23R and RORγt expression, creating the self-amplifying loop.
- [[Rheumatoid synovial fluid]] — IL-23 is detectable in rheumatoid synovial fluid, consistent with its role in maintaining Th17-driven synovitis.
