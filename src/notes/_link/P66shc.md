P66shc (also written p66^Shc^) is the 66 kDa isoform of the ShcA family of adaptor proteins, distinguished by an N-terminal CH2 collagen-homology region absent from the p46^Shc^ and p52^Shc^ isoforms. Unlike its shorter siblings, which primarily relay receptor tyrosine kinase signals to RAS–MAPK, p66Shc is a pro-oxidant protein that links stress signaling to mitochondrial [[Reactive Oxygen Species|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]] generation and [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]].

## Structure and oxidative function

The CH2 domain enables p66Shc to translocate to [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] under oxidative stress, where it interacts with cytochrome c to foster H2O2 production. By stimulating mitochondrial ROS and simultaneously down-regulating antioxidant enzyme synthesis, p66Shc amplifies cellular [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]]. It also inhibits the [[FOXO3a|FOXO3a]] transcription factor (Miyazawa and Tsuji), blunting a key stress-resistance pathway.

## Role in ageing and longevity

p66Shc is a bona fide longevity regulator. **p66Shc knockout mice** live ~30% longer than wild-type controls and show enhanced resistance to [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] and age-related pathologies, including endothelial dysfunction (Francia et al.; Berry et al.; Vikram et al.; Ma et al., reviewed in Kong et al.). Ageing-initiated, p66Shc-mediated endothelial dysfunction is observed in wild-type animals but absent in knockouts, underscoring its role in vascular ageing.

## Regulation by SIRT1

[[SIRT1|SIRT1]] negatively regulates p66Shc: it binds the p66Shc gene promoter and deacetylates [[Histone H3|histone H3]], reducing transcription and thereby lowering both p66Shc abundance and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] intensity (Chen et al.; Zhou et al.). This makes p66Shc a downstream effector through which [[SIRT1|SIRT1]] and [[Caloric Restriction|calorie restriction]] (which raises sirtuin activity) suppress oxidative damage. The SIRT1→p66shc axis is a plausible contributor to the lifespan extension seen in p66Shc-null mice and an attractive node for CR-mimetic interventions.
