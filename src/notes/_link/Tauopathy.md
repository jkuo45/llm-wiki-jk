# Tauopathy

## Overview

[[Tauopathy]] refers to a class of [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative diseases]] defined by the pathological aggregation of the microtubule-associated protein [[Tau]] into insoluble fibrils and neurofibrillary tangles. The spectrum includes [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]] and [[Frontotemporal Dementia|frontotemporal dementia]] (FTD), among others. Beyond its structural role in axonal stability, accumulating evidence positions pathogenic tau as an active instigator of [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegeneration]] through microglial innate immune activation. The progressive spread of pathological tau is a central driver of cognitive decline, with microglia acting as facilitators of tau seeding and propagation.

## Microglial Activation and cGAS-STING

A distinctive feature of tauopathy is the ability of pathogenic tau to directly activate microglial [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] signaling. The [[Tau|tau]]–[[PQBP1]] complex engages cGAS through a direct protein–protein interaction that bypasses the requirement for DNA — an important exception to the otherwise DNA-dependent activation seen across proteinopathies. Depletion of [[mtDNA]] attenuates the tau-induced [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] response, indicating that mitochondrial DNA also contributes. Critically, cGAS drives a *separate* IFN-I–enriched microglial subpopulation that correlates with synapse loss and cognitive impairment, distinct from the canonical [[Disease-Associated Microglia|DAM]] program. Pharmacological inhibition of cGAS suppresses tau seeding and propagation while preserving core microglial functions.

## NF-κB and Tau Propagation

Pathogenic tau also activates microglial [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]] signaling, inducing a transcriptional program that alters tau handling and promotes release of seed-competent tau species. Genetic inhibition of microglial NF-κB traps tau intracellularly and markedly reduces extracellular tau seeding and in vivo propagation, establishing NF-κB as a key regulator of microglial tau export. The elevation of NF-κB likely stems from tau-induced cGAS-STING activation, creating a sustained IFN-I state that reinforces maladaptive inflammation and amplifies tau spread. Notably, microglial cGAS deletion reverses downregulation of neuronal [[MEF2C]], a resilience transcription factor, linking innate immune signaling to loss of cognitive resilience.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Pharmacological cGAS inhibition phenocopies the protective effects of the [[APOE3 Christchurch|APOE3 Christchurch]] (R136S) resilience allele in tauopathy models, preserving synapses and inducing transcriptional signatures mirroring the mutation. These findings validate suppression of cGAS-STING signaling as a strategy to recapitulate genetic protection against tau toxicity.
