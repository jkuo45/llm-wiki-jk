# Ghrelin

**Ghrelin** is a 28-amino acid peptide hormone primarily produced by the stomach. It is best known as the "hunger hormone" for its role in regulating food intake via hypothalamic feeding centers. However, ghrelin also has significant immunomodulatory and thymopoietic properties.

## Structure and Receptor

- **Molecular weight:** ~3.3 kDa (28 amino acids)
- **Receptor:** Growth hormone secretagogue receptor (GHS-R1a)
- **Expression:** Stomach (primary), hypothalamus, immune cells
- **Unique feature:** Octanoylation on Ser3 required for biological activity

## Metabolic Functions

- Major regulator of food intake via hypothalamic effects
- Stimulates [[Human Growth Hormone|growth hormone]] release from the pituitary
- Regulates energy balance and body weight
- Increases during fasting, decreases after feeding

## Immunomodulatory Properties

### Anti-inflammatory effects
- Reduces age-associated [[Inflammation|inflammatory responses]] in mice
- Suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine production
- Levels decrease with age, correlating with [[Thymic Involution|thymic involution]] and elevated inflammation

### Immune cell interactions
- Binds to various immune cell subsets via ghrelin-specific receptors
- Localizes with lipid rafts of activated [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]] (sites of T cell signaling)
- Potential target for T cell stimulation

## Thymic Rejuvenation

### Pre-clinical findings (Dixit et al., 2007; Taub et al., 2010)
- Administration to aged mice increased early thymic progenitors in the [[Thymus|thymus]]
- Increased number of recent thymic emigrants exported to the periphery (measured by [[sjTREC]])
- Improved thymic architecture with increased cortical and medullary [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|epithelial cells]]
- Ghrelin and [[Human Growth Hormone|GH]] levels decrease in aged thymus, correlating with functional involution

> [!tip] Dual mechanism
> Ghrelin promotes thymic rejuvenation through both direct effects on thymic progenitors and indirect anti-inflammatory actions that create a permissive microenvironment for [[Thymopoiesis|thymopoiesis]].

## Clinical Status

As of 2013, no open clinical trials were registered for ghrelin and thymic rejuvenation. The pre-clinical evidence supports further investigation.
