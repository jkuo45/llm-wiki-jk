# ASC

**ASC** (apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a CARD; gene *PYCARD*) is the essential adaptor of most inflammasomes. It bridges sensor proteins (e.g., [[AIM2]], NLRP3) to the effector protease [[Caspase-1]], nucleating supramolecular **ASC specks** that drive inflammatory caspase activation.

## Structure

- Contains an **N-terminal PYRIN domain (PYD)** that binds sensor PYD domains and a **C-terminal CARD** that recruits and oligomerizes [[Caspase-1]].
- Multiple PYD binding sites allow ASC self-association into fibrillar foci (the "speck").

## Function in inflammasome assembly

> [!info] ASC speck formation is the activation readout
> Upon AIM2 (or NLRP3) activation, ASC oligomerizes into a single large per-cell focus — the ASC speck — which concentrates and activates [[Caspase-1]], cleaving pro-[[Interleukin 1β|IL-1β]] and pro-[[IL-18]] into their mature secreted forms and driving [[Pyroptosis]].

In the DROSHA–AIM2 IPF study, transduction of [[MicroRNA|miRNA]] increased ASC speck formation in bone marrow-derived macrophages, required for [[AIM2]] inflammasome activation and DROSHA-dependent lung inflammation.

## Pathological relevance

ASC specks are central to innate immune inflammation in [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis|IPF]], [[Neuroinflammation|neuroinflammation]], and metabolic inflammation. Targeting ASC speck formation is a strategy to curb [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]]-linked disease.
