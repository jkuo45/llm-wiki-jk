# NASH

**Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)** is the inflammatory, potentially progressive form of [[Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease|non-alcoholic fatty liver disease]] (NAFLD), characterized by hepatic steatosis with hepatocyte injury, inflammation, and variable fibrosis. It can progress to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. Its development involves genetic, environmental, and lifestyle risk factors.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

NASH arises from lipid metabolic dysregulation, oxidative stress, inflammation, and fibrosis on top of simple steatosis. Mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired lipid homeostasis via lipogenic regulators such as ChREBP and SREBP-1c contribute to disease progression. Fibrosis, oxidative stress, and related gene expression are significantly elevated in hepatocyte-specific sirtuin-knockout models.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Protective — deacetylates ChREBP and SREBP-1c to maintain hepatic lipid homeostasis; antagonizes liver fibrosis by blocking hepatic stellate cell activation; harmful in alcoholic hepatitis (myeloid SIRT1/SIRT7 prevent p-FoxO3, causing apoptosis defects).
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Hepatocyte-specific knockout elevates fibrosis and oxidative stress in NASH mice; deacetylates ChREBP/SREBP-1c.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Prevents NAFLD by deacetylating hepatocyte nuclear factor 4α; deleterious in hepatic fibrosis via the SIRT2/ERK/c-Myc axis.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Improves mitochondrial function via deacetylation of mitochondrial trifunctional proteins and LCAD.
- **[[SIRT4]]**: Upregulation inhibits HFD-induced lipid accumulation/inflammation/fibrogenesis via the SIRT4/Smad4 axis.
- **[[SIRT5]]**: Systemic knockout impairs medium-chain fatty acid oxidation → periportal macrovascular steatosis.
- **[[SIRT7]]**: High expression in NAFLD is harmful.

## Therapeutic Implications

SIRT1/SIRT6 activation and NAD⁺ restoration are candidate strategies to limit steatohepatitis, oxidative stress, and fibrosis in NASH, complementing lifestyle interventions.
