# Indirect Calorimetry

**Indirect Calorimetry** estimates [[Energy Expenditure|energy expenditure]] from respiratory gas exchange — the rate of O₂ consumption (and CO₂ production) — converted to energy via the respiratory quotient (RQ). It is the standard laboratory method for measuring [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]] and the energy cost of exercise, and is the principal alternative to the [[Doubly-Labelled Water|doubly-labelled water]] method for expenditure assessment.

## Measurement Conditions for RMR

Standard RMR measurement requires:

- **Post-absorptive state** — overnight fast to remove the [[Thermic Effect of Food|thermic effect of food]].
- **Rest and supine posture** at thermoneutral temperatures.
- **Awake but rested** subject, generally measured in the morning; some protocols use minimal sleeping metabolic rate instead.

## Variants

- **Open-circuit respirometry** — subjects breathe room air; expired gases are analysed for O₂ and CO₂.
- **Respiratory chambers / whole-room calorimetry** — subjects reside in a sealed room measuring cumulative gas exchange over hours to days.
- **Portable metabolic analysers** — wearable devices for field measurement during exercise.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review describes RMR as measured by indirect calorimetry under standardised post-absorptive, thermoneutral conditions, with a typical measurement precision of ~3 % (≈200 kJ/d), and combines respirometry with DLW to separate flight costs from post-flight metabolism in bats.
