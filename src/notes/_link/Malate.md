# Malate

**Malate** (L-malate) is a four-carbon dicarboxylic acid intermediate of the [[Citric Acid Cycle|citric acid cycle]]. It is formed from [[Oxaloacetate]] by [[Malate Dehydrogenase|malate dehydrogenase]] (consuming NADH, producing [[NAD+]]) and is re-oxidized to oxaloacetate (producing NADH) within the cycle and the [[Malate-Aspartate Shuttle|malate-aspartate shuttle]].

## Metabolic Role

- **TCA node**: Malate → oxaloacetate is the final, NAD+-regenerating step of the citric acid cycle, closing the loop with acetyl-CoA entry.
- **Redox shuttle carrier**: In the malate-aspartate shuttle, cytosolic malate transports glycolytic reducing equivalents into mitochondria, linking [[Glycolysis]] to [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|OXPHOS]].

## Relevance to Senescence

- The malate/oxaloacetate couple sits at the center of the cytosolic [[NAD+]]/[[NADH]] balance that governs senescence: reduced [[MDH1]] activity lowers NAD+, and blocking the malate-aspartate shuttle (with [[Aminooxyacetate]]) induces senescence.
- Thus malate's interconversion with oxaloacetate is mechanistically tied to whether cells maintain a youthful, non-senescent redox state.
