# NFS1

**NFS1** is a mitochondrial cysteine desulfurase that supplies sulfur for iron-sulfur (Fe-S) cluster biogenesis. In highly differentiated [[Lung cancer|lung adenocarcinomas]], NFS1 is abundantly expressed to maintain Fe-S cluster homeostasis. While NFS1 inhibition alone does not induce ferroptosis, it sensitizes cells to ferroptotic death when ROS are elevated—linking Fe-S biogenesis to iron-dependent cell death.

## Structure & Biochemistry

NFS1 is a pyridoxal-phosphate-dependent enzyme in the mitochondrial matrix that converts cysteine to alanine and elemental sulfur, feeding the Fe-S cluster assembly machinery (ISCU/ISCA). It is essential for maturation of Fe-S proteins across respiration and iron metabolism.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review notes that in differentiated lung adenocarcinomas, NFS1 is highly expressed and maintains Fe-S cluster levels. Suppression of NFS1 alone does not induce ferroptosis, but when cells already produce large amounts of ROS, the iron starvation caused by NFS1 inhibition promotes ferroptosis (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Lung cancer"). Thus NFS1 sits at the intersection of iron metabolism and oxidative stress: its loss reshapes iron handling such that existing ROS tip cells into [[Lipid Peroxidation]]-driven death.

> [!info] Context-dependent ferroptosis
> NFS1 inhibition is not sufficient but is *sensitizing*—a classic ferroptosis-modifier profile where the death requires a second oxidative hit (ROS), echoing how [[p53]] or iron status tune ferroptotic thresholds.

## Clinical Relevance

NFS1 is a potential combinatorial target: pairing NFS1 inhibition with ROS inducers or [[GPX4]] inhibitors could selectively kill lung adenocarcinoma. Its Fe-S role also connects to broader iron-metabolism regulators ([[Iron]], [[STEAP3]], [[DMT1]]).
