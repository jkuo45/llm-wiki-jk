# MOTS-c

MOTS-c is a **16-amino-acid micropeptide** encoded by a short open reading frame within the mitochondrial 12S ribosomal RNA gene (MT-RNR1). It is a member of the **mitochondrial-derived peptides (MDPs)** — along with [[Humanin]] and the small humanin-like peptides SHLP1–6.

> [!important] MOTS-c is a micropeptide, not a classical gene product
> Its gene is not a separate locus. MOTS-c is translated from an internal short ORF inside MT-RNR1, a gene whose primary product is 12S rRNA. That means MOTS-c has no intron/exon structure, no dedicated promoter of its own, no cytosolic mRNA, and no way to be knocked out without knocking out mitochondrial rRNA — which is the single biggest obstacle to its genetic study. Essentially all causal work on MOTS-c is pharmacological (synthetic peptide administration) or uses antisense or RNA-interference approaches, or is done in the mouse *Crif1* model described below. Interpretation of loss-of-function phenotypes is correspondingly indirect.

## Mechanism

The full downstream pathway is not settled, but the core reported chain is:

> [!info] Mechanism
> MOTS-c **activates [[AMPK]]**, which drives [[Akt]] phosphorylation and downstream [[mTORC1]] signalling, and independently promotes [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] via [[PGC-1α]] and [[NRF1]]. It also **localises to the nucleus** during energetic stress, where it associates with chromatin and alters the transcription of stress-response genes. Its best-characterised systemic effects are improved insulin sensitivity, reduced adiposity, and improved glucose tolerance.

Several features of the experimental literature should be kept in mind:

- **Circulating levels are exercise-responsive.** MOTS-c rises acutely in plasma with exercise, and levels decline with age — one of the observations that motivated the "exercise-mimetic" framing of the field.
- **The peptide is cell-permeable in its native form**, which is unusual and is why systemic administration has been feasible at all.
- **MOTS-c is one of the few cases where a hard genetic finding supports the pharmacology.** Deleting one allele of *Crif1* in POMC neurons produces a mild mitochondrial stress that resists diet-induced obesity via a cell-non-autonomous signal identified as MOTS-c. That is a genetic loss-of-function of the pathway, not of MOTS-c itself, but it is the strongest causal evidence that the peptide is a real effector rather than a correlate.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitohormesis - 2023_NOV]]
> The mitohormesis review lists MOTS-c among the established mammalian mitokines, noting that it is a 16-amino-acid peptide encoded by the mitochondrial 12S rRNA gene, that it appears exercise-induced and increases insulin sensitivity while promoting visceral fat accumulation, that circulating levels rise acutely with exercise, and that the peptide has been observed to translocate to the nucleus during energetic stress. It also lists MOTS-c alongside humanin, SHLP1–6, [[FGF21]] and [[GDF15]] as factors giving mitochondrial stress cell-non-autonomous reach.

## Mitohormesis relevance

> [!warning] The framing is contested
> MOTS-c sits squarely in the [[Mitohormesis]] literature, where a mild mitochondrial stress is proposed to trigger a protective adaptive response. Several findings in that same review point the other way for other MDPs — overexpression of the mitochondrial inner-membrane protease LONP1 in muscle confers near-total resistance to diet-induced obesity rather than promoting it — so the general principle that mitochondrial stress is uniformly beneficial is not established, and the sign of the effect depends on which stress is applied and to which tissue. Additionally, a large body of work has re-examined whether some MDPs including humanin have nuclear-encoding NUMT-pseudogene loci that could generate "mitochondrial" peptides in the cytosol, which complicates the cell-autonomy of the whole category.

## Disease and therapeutic relevance

- **Metabolic disease.** MOTS-c administration improves insulin sensitivity and glucose tolerance and reduces weight gain in high-fat-diet mouse models, and MOTS-c therapy has been reported to have additive effects with exercise training on weight and insulin sensitivity. Effects on hepatic steatosis and NAFLD have been reported and are actively investigated.
- **Skeletal muscle and physical decline.** Exercise-induced MOTS-c regulates age-dependent physical decline and muscle homeostasis, with reported preservation of muscle mass and of the mitochondrial response to exercise.
- **Bone.** MOTS-c has been reported to promote osteoblast differentiation and to protect against ovariectomy-induced bone loss, an under-explored arm of the MDP field.
- **Cardiometabolic and other models.** Reported benefits in myocardial ischaemia–reperfusion and in pressure-overload cardiac remodelling exist in the MDP literature, and MOTS-c is one of the molecules most frequently nominated as an "exercise mimetic" candidate.
- **Translation status.** MOTS-c has no approved indication and no completed clinical efficacy trial. Peptide stability, delivery, and dose-response in humans are unresolved, and the field has been damaged by at least one high-profile fabrication-and-retraction episode involving the wider exerkine/mitochondrial peptide literature. Treat translational claims as early.

## Connections

- [[Humanin]] — Sibling mitochondrial-derived peptide, 24 residues and encoded by a short ORF in the 16S rRNA; the two are usually discussed together and are both anti-apoptotic and cytoprotective.
- [[Mitochondrial DNA]] — MOTS-c's genome is not a separate gene but an internal ORF in MT-RNR1 within mtDNA, which determines both its biology and the impossibility of clean knockout studies.
- [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] — A principal reported output, via AMPK activation and the PGC-1α/NRF1 transcriptional programme.
- [[AMPK]] — The most consistently reported direct target; AMPK activation is also the shared mechanism with metformin and with fasting, which is the basis of the "exercise mimetic" claim.
- [[PGC-1α]] — Downstream transcriptional coactivator through which MOTS-c acts to raise mitochondrial capacity.
- [[NRF1]] — Downstream transcription factor driving nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes; the same factor named in the SIRT3 and mitohormesis documents in this vault.
- [[Akt]] — Reported downstream of AMPK activation; the insulin-sensitising effects of MOTS-c are substantially mediated through this arm.
- [[Mitohormesis]] — The theoretical framework MOTS-c is used to illustrate: a mild mitochondrial stress producing a protective adaptive response, with the caveat noted above.
- [[Exercise]] — The stimulus that raises circulating MOTS-c; the exercise-mimetic framing is only meaningful relative to this.
- [[Insulin Resistance]] — The metabolic phenotype MOTS-c is reported to improve in high-fat-diet models, and the phenotype most tightly associated with declining MOTS-c levels in ageing.
- [[Skeletal Muscle]] — A major target tissue, with reported protection against age-dependent physical decline and maintenance of the mitochondrial response to exercise.
- [[Aging]] — MOTS-c levels decline with age, which is the observation that places it in the senescence and geroscience literature alongside [[Cellular Senescence]]-derived mitokines.
- [[FGF21]] — A well-characterised bona fide myokine with a similar profile: mitochondrial stress-induced, liver-secreted, and a claimed exercise-mimetic — useful for triangulating how much of the exerkine claim is mechanistically plausible.
- [[GDF15]] — Another stress-induced mitokine in the same family of cell-non-autonomous signals.
