# Fondaparinux

Fondaparinux is a fully synthetic, single-molecule anticoagulant and the first
drug of its kind: it is the pentasaccharide sequence of [[Heparin]] that binds
[[Antithrombin III]], chemically synthesised to exact specifications. Its
molecular weight is 1,728 Da and, because it is a single defined chemical
species, its batch-to-batch consistency is essentially perfect - a real
advantage over heparin, which is a heterogeneous animal-derived mixture.

## Mechanism of action

Fondaparinux binds a conformational epitope on antithrombin that is far along
the molecule from the reactive site, and induces the conformational change that
exposes the reactive arginine. This accelerates antithrombin's inactivation of
[[Factor Xa]] by roughly 300-fold.

> [!info] The pentasaccharide is the whole of its pharmacology
> Fondaparinux is too short - five saccharide units - to bridge antithrombin to
> [[Thrombin]], which requires at least 18 units. So it is a **pure**
> anti-factor Xa agent with no direct anti-IIa activity whatsoever. This is not
> a limitation to be managed; it is the design, and it explains both its
> efficacy and its very low rate of heparin-associated thrombocytopenia.

The catalysis is catalytic rather than stoichiometric: fondaparinux dissociates
from antithrombin after the enzyme is inactivated, so one molecule can turn
over many. The antithrombin interaction is reversible; the antithrombin-Xa
reaction is not. Fondaparinux also has no effect on [[Fibrinogen]] and does not
meaningfully affect thrombin generation assays or platelet activation.

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Administration**: subcutaneous only. Intravenous use gives unreliable
  kinetics.
- **Bioavailability**: 100% after subcutaneous injection.
- **Peak**: ~2 hours.
- **Half-life**: ~17-21 hours - roughly four times that of
  [[Enoxaparin]] and much longer than unfractionated heparin. This is what
  makes once-daily dosing possible and what makes the drug unforgiving of
  overdose.
- **Elimination**: entirely renal, unchanged in urine.
- **Not dialysable** and not for use in severe renal impairment.
- **Variability**: essentially none of the pharmacokinetic kind - peak and trough
  concentrations are highly predictable across patients of similar weight and
  renal function.

> [!warning] Clinical caveats
> - **Contraindicated at creatinine clearance below 30 mL/min**, where renal
>   clearance falls by more than 50%. This is a substantially harder
>   restriction than for the low-molecular-weight heparins, and it is the
>   single most common reason fondaparinux is unusable.
> - **Prolonged in low body weight (<50 kg) and in the very elderly (>75
>   years)**, where clearance is reduced by roughly 25-30%. Prophylactic
>   dosing is contraindicated below 50 kg.
> - **Long half-life and no specific antidote**: serious bleeding requires
>   protamine (ineffective) or, more usefully, fresh frozen plasma for its
>   antithrombin content, with a very long wait for drug clearance. This is
>   the trade for the clean pharmacology.
> - **HIT risk is very low but not zero**: because the pentasaccharide does not
>   bind platelet factor 4, cross-reactivity with HIT antibodies is negligible
>   and it is the standard choice in acute or past HIT. Isolated cases of
>   HIT-like thrombocytopenia are reported in post-marketing data.
> - **No routine monitoring** is required. Anti-Xa monitoring, using a
>   fondaparinux-calibrated assay, is reserved for severe obesity, pregnancy,
>   renal impairment, or bleeding concerns.
> - **Pregnancy**: generally avoided; it is not the drug of choice in
>   obstetric anticoagulation, and it has been associated with fetal
>   abnormalities in animal studies.

## Indications

Prophylaxis of venous thromboembolism after hip or knee surgery, abdominal
surgery, and in acutely ill medical patients; treatment of acute deep vein
thrombosis and pulmonary embolism; treatment of acute coronary syndromes
without ST elevation; and use in patients with heparin-induced
thrombocytopenia.

> [!info] Where the head-to-head trials disagree
> Fondaparinux beat [[Enoxaparin]] for VTE prevention in hip/knee arthroplasty
> trials (OASIS-5 setting for [[Stroke]]) but produced a statistically
> significant excess of major bleeding. In the ATLAS trial across a broad
> range of acute coronary syndromes it was non-inferior to enoxaparin with
> lower rates of major bleeding but higher rates of minor bleeding and
> catheter thromboses. The pattern is consistent: fondaparinux is
> antithrombotic with a different balance, not a cleaner drug, and the choice
> is largely about which risk is preferable in a given patient.

## Connections

- [[Heparin]] — the natural molecule whose antithrombin-binding
  pentasaccharide fondaparinux reproduces; chain length is what determines the
  drug's Xa-selectivity and its freedom from platelet factor 4 binding.
- [[Enoxaparin]] — the LMWH it is most often substituted for, and the
  comparator in the pivotal trials; the difference is longer half-life, wider
  renal restriction, no reversal agent, and far less HIT risk.
- [[Antithrombin III]] — the obligatory cofactor; the entire drug is an
  antithrombin conformational stabiliser.
- [[Thrombin]] — the enzyme fondaparinux is unable to inhibit, by virtue of
  being five saccharide units long.
- [[Anticoagulant]] — the parent class; fondaparinux is an indirect, not direct,
  factor Xa inhibitor.
- [[Fibrinogen]] — unaffected by fondaparinux, which is why it does not raise
  bleeding through fibrinogen consumption.
- [[Platelet]] — the source of platelet factor 4, which fondaparinux does not
  bind; the structural basis of its near-absence of HIT risk.
- [[Warfarin]] — the other option for long-term anticoagulation after an
  acute-phase parenteral agent; the switch protocol from fondaparinux to
  warfarin is a standard transition because of the long half-life.
- [[Protamine]] — the reversal agent that does not work on fondaparinux.
- [[Pulmonary Embolism]] and [[Stroke]] — two of the three approved
  treatment indications, alongside deep vein thrombosis.
- [[Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia]] — the clinical situation in which
  fondaparinux is the default choice.
- [[Myocardial infarction]] — non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome is the
  third major indication.
