# Protease

A **protease** (preferred nomenclature: **peptidase**) is an enzyme that catalyses the hydrolysis of peptide bonds in a protein or polypeptide substrate. Proteases are class 3 hydrolases, subclass EC 3.4 (peptide hydrolases), and they are among the most numerous and most functionally diverse enzyme groups in biology. The vault already names many members individually — [[Proteasome]], [[Cathepsins]], [[Caspases]] — this note is the class-level container they belong to.

## Classification

Peptidases divide first by cleavage position:

- **Endopeptidases** (endoproteinases, EC 3.4.21–99) cut internal peptide bonds — e.g. [[Thrombin]], the chymotrypsins, [[Matrix Metalloproteinases]].
- **Exopeptidases** (exoproteinases) cut terminal bonds: **aminopeptidases** at the N-terminus, **carboxypeptidases** at the C-terminus.

The second division is by **catalytic type**, defined by the nucleophile at the active site — the scheme used by the MEROPS peptidase database and by the IUBMB enzyme nomenclature:

| Type | Nucleophile / mechanism | Representative vault examples |
|---|---|---|
| Serine (S) | Serine–His–Asp charge relay | [[Thrombin]], [[Plasmin]], [[Tissue Plasminogen Activator]], [[Factor XIIIa]], [[Neutrophil Elastase]] |
| Cysteine (C) | Cys-His dyad, thiolate nucleophile | [[Cathepsin B]], [[Cathepsin K]], [[Calpain]], [[Caspase-3]] and the caspase family (MEROPS clan CD) |
| Aspartic (A) | Asp–Asp dyad activating water | pepsin, HIV-1 protease |
| Metallo (M) | Metal-activated water (usually Zn²⁺) | [[Matrix Metalloproteinases]] (M10), ACE, [[Cathepsin L1]], astacin |
| Threonine (T) | Thr nucleophile | [[Proteasome]] catalytic subunits (T1) |
| Glutamic (G), Asparagine (N) | Bacterial lyases | not represented in mammals |

## Physiological Functions

> [!info] Proteolysis is not merely catabolism
> Limited, site-specific cleavage is a regulatory signal: zymogen activation, signal-peptide processing, pro-protein maturation (e.g. [[Hepatocyte Growth Factor]] activation by an HGF activator protease in the [[MET]] pathway), and complement activation by the serine protease cascade [[Complement System|complement]].

- **Protein turnover and quality control** — cytosolic and lysosomal proteases recycle [[Proteins]]; their insufficiency drives [[Proteostasis]] failure, [[Proteotoxicity]] and [[Proteinopathy|proteinopathies]].
- **Digestion** — gastric pepsin, pancreatic trypsin and the intestinal peptidases.
- **Coagulation and fibrinolysis** — [[Thrombin]]→[[Fibrinogen]]→[[Factor XIIIa]] cross-linked fibrin, counterbalanced by [[Plasminogen]]/[[Plasmin]].
- **Inflammation and immunity** — [[Neutrophils]] release [[Neutrophil Elastase]] and [[Myeloperoxidase]]; the protease-activated receptor (PAR) family converts protease cleavage into G-protein signalling.
- **Regulated cell death** — [[Caspases]] are cysteine proteases whose activation executes [[Apoptosis]]; granzymes are granule serine proteases that execute cytotoxic lymphocyte killing; [[Gasdermin D]] is itself a cysteine-protease substrate whose cleavage triggers [[Pyroptosis]]; [[Caspase-1]] matures IL-1β within the [[NLRP3 Inflammasome|inflammasome]].
- **Microbial and viral proteases** — [[HIV-1]] protease (aspartic) cleaves Gag and Gag-Pol polyproteins during virion maturation and is the target of protease inhibitors; [[Helminth|Helminth]] and bacterial proteases are virulence factors and therapeutic targets.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Protease inhibition is one of the most successful drug strategies in medicine: ACE inhibitors, direct thrombin inhibitors, HIV protease inhibitors, HCV protease inhibitors, [[DPP4]] inhibitors and neutrophil elastase inhibitors all trace to this class. Proteases also act as drug *targets of opportunity* in oncology ([[Matrix Metalloproteinases]] in [[Angiogenesis]] and [[Metastasis]]) and are the main proteolytic component of [[Autophagy]]-independent lysosomal degradation.

## Connections

- [[Proteasome]] — The threonine-peptidase machine of the ubiquitin-proteasome system; the dominant cytosolic protease, distinct from lysosomal cathepsins.
- [[Cathepsins]] — The main lysosomal cysteine- and aspartic-protease family, executing bulk protein turnover and also acting extracellularly in tumour invasion.
- [[Caspases]] — Cysteine peptidases (clan CD) that are simultaneously initiator and executioner proteases of apoptosis.
- [[Thrombin]] — The archetypal serine endopeptidase of coagulation; illustrates protease biology that is simultaneously protective and pathological.
- [[HIV-1]] — Its aspartic protease is the essential maturase of the virion and the anchor of protease-inhibitor therapy.
- [[MET]] — Illustrates regulated proteolytic activation: the receptor tyrosine kinase requires protease processing of its ligand to signal.
- [[Proteostasis]] — Depends on balanced protease capacity; excess proteolysis produces proteinopathies, insufficient proteolysis produces aggregates.
