# Creatine Transporters (CRTR)

**Creatine transporters (CRTR)** are members of the [[Solute Carrier 6 (SLC6) Family|solute carrier 6 (SLC6) family]] of sodium- and chloride-dependent symporters that mediate cellular uptake of [[Creatine]] from the extracellular space. The principal isoform is **SLC6A8** (also called CT1), expressed in many tissues including [[Brain]], [[Muscle]], [[Heart]], and at the [[Blood-Brain Barrier|blood-brain barrier]] and blood-cerebrospinal fluid barrier.

## Function

CRTR imports creatine into cells against a concentration gradient, coupling transport to Na⁺/Cl⁻ gradients. Within cells, imported creatine feeds the [[Creatine Phosphate Shuttle]] via [[Creatine Kinase (CK)]], supporting [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|PCr]] buffering and [[Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)|ATP]] homeostasis. The human genome encodes 19 SLC6 family genes; non-synonymous mutations can produce misfolded transporters causing disease.

## Clinical Significance

Mutations in [[Solute Carrier 6 (SLC6) Family|SLC6A8]] are responsible for **creatine transporter deficiency**, a form of [[Creatine Synthesis Deficiency|cerebral creatine deficiency syndrome]] accounting for ~2% of intellectual disability in boys. Reduced uptake lowers [[Brain]] creatine and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|PCr]], producing developmental delay, [[Epilepsy]], [[Autism]]-like features, and [[Speech Development|speech]] deficits. Because the defect is in the transporter rather than synthesis, oral [[Creatine Supplementation]] is less effective than in AGAT/GAMT deficiencies.

In oncology, the [[Creatine Transport SLC6A8 Gene|SLC6A8 gene]] is markedly upregulated in tumor-infiltrating immune cells, and creatine uptake is required for effective [[CD8 T Cell|CD8 T cell]] antitumor immunity (Di Biase et al., 2019), linking transporter function to [[Cancer]] immunotherapy.
