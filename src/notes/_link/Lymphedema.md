# Lymphedema

**Lymphedema** is the pathological accumulation of protein-rich interstitial
fluid in a tissue when **lymphatic drainage cannot meet the demands of fluid
transport**. Unlike oedema from cardiac, renal or hepatic failure, lymphedematous
fluid is protein-rich, accumulates in the interstitium rather than shifting to
the plasma compartment, and does not respond to diuretics. The chronic form
progresses through fibrosis to the classical picture of
[[Elephantiasis|elephantiasis]].

## Classification

**Primary (congenital)** — a developmental defect of the lymphatic system:
- *Milroy disease* — an autosomal dominant [[VEGFR3]]/[[FOXC2]]-related
  lymphoedema presenting at birth or in childhood.
- *Meige disease* — later-onset, usually pubertal-onset, primary lymphedema.
- *Hennekam lymphangiectasia-lymphedema syndrome* — associated with
  [[PIK3CA]]-related overgrowth spectrum disorders, linking lymphedema to the
  mTOR pathway. Progressive lymphedema is a recognised feature of
  [[Proteus Syndrome]] and [[CLOVES]].

**Secondary (acquired)** — lymphatic vessels destroyed, obstructed or
overloaded. This is far more common:
- **Filarial** — [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] from [[Brugia malayi]],
  *Wuchereria bancrofti* and *B. timori*; the most common cause worldwide and
  the only irreversible form of filarial disease (the worms themselves can be
  killed, the lymphatic damage cannot).
- **Cancer** — nodal dissection, axillary irradiation and tumour infiltration.
  Breast cancer is the leading cause in high-income countries, with reported
  rates of 5–20% after breast cancer treatment.
- **[[Podoconiosis]]** — non-filarial podoconiotic lymphedema from long-term
  barefoot exposure to irritant volcanic soil; morphologically and
  pathologically near-identical to podoconiosis and still often misclassified.
- Chronic trauma, cellulitis, venous hypertension, and parasitic infection.

## Pathophysiology

> [!info] The cycle of stasis, infection and fibrosis
> The initial oedema is a *functional* obstruction. The consequences then
> become self-sustaining:
>
> 1. **Protein-rich interstitial fluid** accumulates. Stagnant lymph is a
>    poor clearance mechanism and a permissive culture medium.
> 2. **Chronic inflammation.** Retained fluid, loaded with antigen and
>    debris, activates macrophages and fibroblasts and recruits
>    [[Toll-like Receptor]] signalling.
> 3. **Fibrosis.** TGF-β-driven fibroblast activation deposits collagen;
>    chronic macrophage-derived TNF and IL-6 amplify it. The interstitium
>    becomes fibrotic and the subcutis thickens — this is the transition from
>    reversible oedema to irreversible elephantiasis.
> 4. **Recurrent cellulitis.** Each episode of acute dermatocellulitis
>    damages lymphatic endothelium further and adds fibrosis, and each
>    episode is more likely and more severe. This is the dominant driver of
>    disease progression after the initial insult.
> 5. **Loss of lymphatic endothelial junctional integrity**, with
>    progressive lymphatic vessel rarefaction.

Lymphatic endothelium is not a passive conduit. It expresses [[VEGFR3]] and
relies on VEGF-C gradient–driven sprouting for developmental and pathological
vessel growth; its failure is the initiating event in most primary and many
secondary cases. Feedback from chronic inflammation to lymphatic endothelial
cell dysfunction is now recognised as central rather than incidental.

## Clinical presentation

Progressive swelling of an extremity (or genitalia, in filarial hydrocele)
predominantly in a **distal, non-pitting** distribution, with peau d'orange
(pebbly skin from dermal lymphoedema) and hyperkeratosis in advanced disease.
Other distinguishing features: [[Pitting]] is absent until late, the limb is
warm and the subcutaneous tissue is thickened, and chronic [[Verrucous]] fungal
or bacterial superinfection is common. Genital and breast lymphedema occur.
Onset after cancer treatment is typically months to years, and the risk is
cumulative with axillary surgery plus radiotherapy.

Filariasis adds a characteristic acute attack, **acute filarial lymphangitis
(AFL)**, with fever, painful tender lymphangitis and generalised lymphadenopathy
occurring in response to dying adult worms — a dead parasite gives an acute
inflammatory episode.

> [!warning] Clinical caveats
> - **Compression therapy is the mainstay but is frequently under-used.** Multi-layer
>   bandaging or compression garments, combined with exercise, skin care and
>   manual lymph drainage, remain standard of care.
> - **Diuretics do not work** for lymphedema. They remain appropriate only
>   for a coexisting systemic cause of oedema.
> - **Massage/manual drainage is contraindicated** in active infection, active
>   [[Cancer|cancer]], acute cellulitis, or uninvestigated limb swelling —
>   a swelling limb must be assessed for tumour before any limb work begins.
> - **Filarial lymphedema cannot be cured by eliminating the infection.** The
>   WHO therefore runs a morbidity-management programme alongside mass drug
>   administration, targeted at limb hygiene, acute-episode care and
>   lymphoedema management. This distinction is central to current
>   [[Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis]] policy.
> - Diagnosis is clinical. Imaging (lymphoscintigraphy, near-infrared
>   fluorescence) is used in equivocal cases; **podoconiosis and filarial
>   lymphedema are clinically indistinguishable** without
>   epidemiological context, which is a source of systematic
>   misclassification in endemic areas.

## Documents

- [[Brugia malayi]] — one of three filarial nematodes causing lymphatic
  obstruction and hence secondary lymphedema; the agent-level
  contribution to the pathophysiology above.
- [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] — supplies the disease, vector, diagnosis
  (circulating filarial antigen tests, ultrasound for adult worms) and the
  MDA/loss-of-elimination-programme context.

## Connections

- [[Lymphatic Filariasis]] — The single most important cause of secondary
  lymphedema worldwide and the clearest case of an irreversible disease
  caused by a curable infection.
- [[Brugia malayi]] — Experimental infection with *B. malayi* in mice is
  the standard model of filarial lymphedema and is used to test whether
  lymphatic damage is inflammation-driven.
- [[Fibrosis]] — The progressive fibrotic component is the reason lymphedema
  is not simply oedema, and it responds to no current therapy.
- [[Toll-like Receptor]] — Lymphatic endothelial cells express TLRs; the
  role of TLR signalling in lymphatic remodelling and in the
  inflammation-driven fibrosis cycle is an active area of work.
- [[Inflammation]] — Recurrent acute dermatocellulitis is the key
  progression mechanism, which is why infection prevention is central to
  management.
- [[VEGF-C]] — The principal lymphangiogenic growth factor; its gradient
  governs developmental lymphatic vessel growth, and its restoration is
  the target of experimental lymphedema therapies.
- [[Lymphatic Vessel Development]] — the biological substrate the pathology
  acts on; developmental failure of lymphatic vasculature is the primary
  (congenital) form of the disease.
- [[Podoconiosis]] — A second, non-infectious, non-filarial cause of the
  same morphological endpoint; the comparison is instructive because the
  two are clinically identical and only context distinguishes them.
- [[Global Programme to Eliminate Lymphatic Filariasis]] — The elimination
  strategy shifted to morbidity management precisely because the damage
  outlives the infection.
- [[Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia]] and lymphoma — a contrasting cause of
  lymphatic obstruction via nodal infiltration, and a reminder that
  lymphedema is a sign with a differential rather than a diagnosis.
- [[Obesity]] — Obesity independently causes and worsens lymphedema through
  increased lymphatic load, and is an emerging area of research
  (leptin/[[Leptin Signaling]] acts directly on lymphatic endothelial cells).
