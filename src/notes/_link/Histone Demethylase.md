## Overview

A [[Histone Demethylase]] is an enzyme that catalyzes removal of methyl groups from histone tails, reversing the methyl marks deposited by histone methyltransferases. Demethylases dynamically tune chromatin accessibility and transcriptional programs, forming a core component of the epigenetic machinery.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

Two major families exist:

- **Lysine-specific demethylase 1 (LSD1/KDM1A)** — a flavin-dependent monoamine oxidase that removes H3K4me1/2 and H3K9me1/2.
- **Jumonji C (JmjC) domain-containing demethylases (KDM2–KDM8 families)** — use Fe(II) and α-ketoglutarate to oxidatively demethylate H3K4, H3K9, H3K27, H3K36, and H4K20 marks.

These enzymes depend on metabolic cofactors (α-ketoglutarate, [[NAD+]], [[FAD]]) linking epigenetic state to cellular metabolism. Demethylation can activate or repress transcription depending on the residue and genomic context.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

Dysregulated histone demethylases drive cancer, developmental disorders, and aging-associated transcriptional drift. They are emerging drug targets (e.g., LSD1 inhibitors). In the wiki, histone demethylases connect metabolic state (via TCA/[[NAD+]]) to gene expression programs relevant to senescence and sirtuin-mediated epigenetic regulation.
