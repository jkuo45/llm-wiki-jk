# Lipid Rafts

**Lipid rafts** are small (10–200 nm), relatively ordered microdomains of the
[[Plasma Membrane]] enriched in [[Cholesterol]] and sphingolipids, and correspondingly
depleted in unsaturated phospholipids. They are not fixed structures but dynamic,
reversible assemblies — fluid yet more tightly packed than the surrounding bilayer —
that function as organising platforms for signalling.

## Physical Basis

- **Cholesterol as a spacer** — The saturated hydrocarbon chains of sphingolipids pack
  more tightly than unsaturated phospholipid chains. Cholesterol intercalates between
  them and acts as a dynamic "glue" that stabilises the ordered phase, partitioning
  preferentially into it.
- **Operational definition** — Rafts are defined by detergent resistance and by
  cholesterol dependence rather than by microscopy, since they are below the resolution
  limit of conventional imaging. Biochemical isolation typically uses
  non-ionic detergents at low temperature, which is why the term attracted criticism for
  being an artefact of the method.
- **Regulated size and lifetime** — Rafts assemble and dissolve on a seconds-to-minutes
  timescale and can be triggered by ligand binding, making them a plausible mechanism
  for *reversible* signal amplification: a small change in partitioning can switch a
  signalling cascade on or off.

> [!warning] Caveat on terminology
> "Lipid raft" is a functional shorthand, not a single molecular species, and raft
  composition varies by cell type and membrane leaflet. The relationship between
> detergent-resistant fractions and physiological rafts remains partly unresolved, and
> some proposed raft functions have not replicated under gentle, artefact-free methods
  such as single-molecule tracking.

## Caveolae and Raft Subtypes

The best-studied raft subtype is the **caveola**, a flask-shaped invagination of the
plasma membrane rich in the scaffolding protein caveolin-1 and the lipid GM1. Caveolae
have three proposed functions: receptor-independent endocytosis, mechanical buffering of
membrane tension during osmotic stress, and a regulated signalling compartment. They
concentrate signalling kinases including Src-family kinases and are implicated in
mechanosensation and mechanoprotection. Related but morphologically distinct raft
populations are marked by flotillins.

## Functional Roles

- **Receptor organisation** — Growth factor receptors (EGFR, PDGFR) and GPCRs partition
  into distinct membrane domains, and cross-talk between receptors is facilitated when
  they share a raft. This can explain why two receptors' signalling can be coupled even
  without a shared second messenger pool.
- **Redox signalling** — Raft-resident NADPH oxidases (Nox) generate
  [[Superoxide]] locally; raft cholesterol content modulates Nox activity and
  [[Rac GTPase]] localisation, so cholesterol depletion suppresses and cholesterol
  loading enhances the respiratory burst.
- **Amyloid precursor protein processing** — In the Alzheimer's disease context,
  rafts compartmentalise the amyloidogenic secretase machinery: BACE1 and γ-secretase are
  enriched in rafts, promoting amyloidogenic [[APP]] processing, whereas α-secretase
  ([[ADAM10]]) activity favours the non-amyloidogenic sAPPα route. Cholesterol depletion
  (e.g. by statins) redirects APP away from rafts and reduces Aβ production in model
  systems.
- **Endocytosis** — Rafts cooperate with [[Clathrin]]-mediated and caveolin-mediated
  internalisation, and define which surface receptors are removed after ligand binding.
- **Infection** — Several pathogens and toxins hijack rafts: cholesterol-rich lipid
  domains concentrate receptors used as entry points for [[SARS-CoV-2]] and other
  enveloped viruses, and raft-disrupting agents can inhibit infection in vitro.
- **Sphingolipid metabolism** — Raft turnover depends on the balance of
  [[Sphingomyelin]] synthesis and its hydrolysis by [[Acid Sphingomyelinase]]; the
  resulting [[Ceramide]] and [[Sphingosine-1-phosphate]] species act as signalling
  lipids in their own right.

## Connections
- [[Cholesterol]] — Cholesterol is the essential structural component of rafts and the
  variable that sets raft size and order; its depletion dissociates most raft-resident
  proteins and disables raft signalling.
- [[Plasma Membrane]] — Rafts are defined by composition relative to the bulk plasma
  membrane, whose fluidity they locally override.
- [[Cell Membranes]] — The fluid mosaic model of membrane organisation is refined by
  raft microdomains, which supply the lateral heterogeneity the model lacks.
- [[APP]] — APP's amyloidogenic versus non-amyloidogenic cleavage fate is set in part by
  whether the secretases sit in raft or non-raft membrane.
- [[Clathrin]] — Raft and clathrin pathways are the two major endocytic routes and
  partition which receptor population is internalised after activation.
- [[NADPH Oxidase]] — Raft-resident Nox enzymes are the local source of the superoxide
  that couples membrane organisation to redox signalling.
- [[Sphingomyelin]] — Sphingomyelin, and its hydrolysis products ceramide and
  sphingosine-1-phosphate, define raft membership and regulate raft turnover.
- [[G Protein-Coupled Receptor]] — GPCR activation redistributes both the receptor and
  its effectors between raft and non-raft membrane, which shapes signal amplitude.
