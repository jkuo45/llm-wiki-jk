# Carotenoids

**Carotenoids** are lipid-soluble C40 isoprenoid pigments (tetraterpenes) built from eight isoprene units, produced by plants, algae, bacteria, and fungi — and by the human cell only from diet. They split into two solubility classes: **carotenes** (pure hydrocarbons: β-carotene, α-carotene, γ-carotene, lycopene, phytoene, phytofluene) and **xanthophylls** (carotenes bearing hydroxyl groups: lutein, zeaxanthin, β-cryptoxanthin, astaxanthin, canthaxanthin, crocetin). Over 600 carotenoids are described; only **three — α-carotene, β-carotene, and β-cryptoxanthin — carry provitamin A activity**, because only they can be cleaved asymmetrically to retinal.

## Chemical Properties and Classification

| Class | Members | Provitamin A | Solubility / location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| β-Carotene | symmetric C40, 11 conjugated double bonds | **Yes** | Hydrophobic; lipid phase, all-trans dominant |
| α-Carotene | one fewer conjugated bond, β-ring | **Yes** | Hydrophobic |
| β-Cryptoxanthin | ring bearing OH | **Yes** | Amphipathic (xanthophyll) |
| Lycopene | acyclic, fully conjugated, 11 db | No | Hydrophobic; most abundant carotenoid in human plasma |
| Lutein / zeaxanthin | two OH groups | No | Amphipathic; lens/macular pigment, oriented at membrane interfaces |
| Astaxanthin | keto + OH; esterified in *Haematococcus* | No | Amphipathic; "superoxide scavenger" claim unsupported |
| Phytoene / phytofluene | colorless, hydrogenated precursors | No | Hydrophobic; upstream of the pathway, low absorbance |

Conjugated polyene chains are the functional core: they absorb visible light (hence pigment), delocalize radical electrons, and — for the 9+ conjugated-double-bond members — quench triplet-state sensitizers and [[Singlet Oxygen]] by physical energy transfer. The hydrophobic anchor lets them partition into lipid droplets, lipoproteins, and [[Cell Membranes| membranes]]; the hydroxyl groups of xanthophylls flip them to sit at the polar interface, which is why lutein/zeaxanthin concentrate in the lens and macula.

## Absorption, Metabolism, and Bioavailability

- **Uptake** requires dietary fat: micelle formation with bile salts, then entry via SR-BI, CD36, and NPC1L1 into enterocytes, packaged into chylomicrons, delivered to the liver, and re-exported in VLDL/LDL. Lycopene is absorbed preferentially via chylomicrons rather than mixed micelles.
- **β-Carotene cleavage** occurs in the intestinal mucosa: **central (BCO1)** cleavage gives two retinal molecules, reduced to retinol and esterified into retinyl palmitate; **eccentric (BCO2)** cleavage yields apocarotenoids (bc; β-apo-8'-lycopene) that reach the circulation and act on nuclear receptors as retinoid-like ligands. Absorbed β-carotene is *less* efficiently converted than preformed retinol — which is exactly why food-form β-carotene has no upper limit while retinol does.
- **Conversions**: 12 μg dietary β-carotene = 1 μg RAE; 24 μg α-carotene or β-cryptoxanthin = 1 μg RAE (IOM/FAO 12:1 and 24:1 rules); 6:1 is used for purified β-carotene *in oil* (the basis of the old "RE" units).
- **Bioavailability determinants**: matrix (raw > cooked for β-carotene in carrots; cooked > raw for lycopene in tomato), co-ingested fat, fiber and plant sterols (reduce), carotenoid isomerization during processing, and smoking (lowers plasma carotenoids ~10–20% via oxidative turnover and lower lipoprotein levels).
- **Excretion** is minor; unabsorbed carotenoids reach the colon and are partly converted by the gut microbiome.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Provitamin A functions** — vision (11-*cis*-retinal in the visual cycle; see [[Retinal Pigment Epithelium]] and [[Macular Degeneration]]), epithelial and immune integrity, and retinoic-acid-dependent gene transcription through RAR/RXR. Deficiency is a leading cause of preventable childhood blindness.
- **Lipid-phase antioxidant function** — carotenoids are the second radical-trapping tier beside tocopherols in membranes: they intercept peroxyl radicals within bilayers, quench singlet oxygen, and interrupt the propagation step of [[Lipid Peroxidation]]. In the radical-trapping hierarchy for [[Ferroptosis]], tocotrienols are the more potent traps (see [[Tocotrienols]]), while carotenoids contribute non-radical (¹O₂) quenching and provitamin A conversion to retinol/retinal, which are themselves direct lipid-radical traps.
- **Gene regulation** — carotenoid metabolites, and β-carotene/lycopene themselves, activate the antioxidant response element via cAMP/CAR-dependent transcription, inducing phase-II enzymes (glutathione transferases, NADPH:quinone oxidoreductase) — a mechanism distinct from chemical radical scavenging.
- **Vascular signaling** — apoB-containing lipoprotein oxidation is not a validated in vivo target of high-dose carotenoid supplements; the ASCEND and CARET trials of β-carotene reduced neither cardiovascular events nor mortality, and in smokers (ATBC, CARET) β-carotene *increased* lung cancer incidence — the basis of the ban on β-carotene supplements for smokers. Food-form carotenoids carry no such signal.
- **Safety**: β-carotene, lutein, zeaxanthin, and lycopene have no UL. Provitamin A conversion is feedback-regulated, so intake cannot produce hypervitaminosis A. High-dose canthaxanthin is a distinct entity — it deposits in the retina and causes crystalline retinopathy; sustained high-dose lycopene is associated with carotenodermia and, rarely, lycopenaemia (no harm, but it excludes other carotenoids from diagnostic assays).
- **Carotenodermia** — yellow-orange skin from palms/soles/knuckles/nasal folds — is a visible, harmless marker of carotenoid intake and of lipid-phase delivery; it is a marker of *carotenoid* intake, not of vitamin A status.

## Red Palm Oil as a Multi-Carotenoid Source

[[Red Palm Oil]] is the richest common food matrix for carotenoids: ~600–750 ppm total, of which ~41% β-carotene and ~41% α-carotene, plus lycopene, phytoene, phytofluene, neurosporene, and β-zeacarotene. One tablespoon (~13 g) delivers ~8.7 mg total carotenoids (≈400–500 μg RAE), and the oil matrix improves carotenoid absorption versus fat-free vegetables. As a result, red palm oil intake at ≤8 g/day already saturates the serum-retinol response in deficiency-population trials. The same oil supplies tocotrienol-rich [[Vitamin E]] and [[Coenzyme Q10]] — a rare single-food co-delivery of the whole lipophilic defense tier (see [[Queen Palm]] for the palm-family comparison).

> [!warning] Supplementation caution
> Prefer food-form carotenoids. Isolated β-carotene supplements are contraindicated in current and former smokers (ATBC, CARET). The Wiki's [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox]] pattern — food, not pills — is the correct default, and food-level carotenoid intake has never been shown to blunt [[Mitohormesis]] in the way high-dose antioxidant supplements do.

## Connections

- [[Vitamin A]]: Three carotenoids are provitamin A; central/eccentric cleavage by BCO1/BCO2 generates retinal and retinol with feedback control.
- [[Retinoic Acid]]: The transcriptional endpoint of provitamin A conversion; retinoic acid acts through RAR/RXR, whereas retinal is the RAR ligand intermediate and the visual pigment precursor.
- [[Retinal Pigment Epithelium]] / [[Macular Degeneration]]: Lutein/zeaxanthin concentrate in the macula and lens as a blue-light filter; carotenoid intake is the modifiable risk variable.
- [[Lipid Peroxidation]]: Carotenoids are the non-tocopherol radical-trapping tier in membranes, and quench singlet oxygen within the lipid phase.
- [[Ferroptosis]]: Carotenoids feed the retinoid branch of the radical-trapping tier; retinol and retinal derived from them are direct lipid-radical traps with potency exceeding α-tocopherol.
- [[Red Palm Oil]]: The richest common dietary carotenoid matrix (~600–750 ppm), co-delivered with tocotrienols and CoQ10 on the same lipid vehicle.
- [[Vitamin E]] / [[Tocopherols]] / [[Tocotrienols]]: Adjacent members of the lipophilic defense tier; carotenoids provide ¹O₂ quenching and provitamin A, tocopherols/tocotrienols provide chain-breaking LOO• termination.
- [[Singlet Oxygen]]: The principal non-radical species carotenoids quench by physical energy transfer.
- [[Queen Palm]]: Palmel-family analogue; jerivá pulp is the closest compositional comparison for palm-derived lipophilic defense.
- [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox]]: High-dose β-carotene supplements (ATBC, CARET) versus food-form carotenoids — the canonical food-versus-pill contrast for this class.
- [[Mitohormesis]] / [[Exercise Hormesis]]: Food-level carotenoids do not quench the ROS signal that drives adaptation; supplements at pharmacologic doses may.
- [[Arachidonic acid]] / [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids]]: PUFA-rich membranes are the substrate whose peroxidation carotenoids limit, and the xanthophylls orient within.
- [[Cell Membranes]] / [[Glutathione]]: Carotenoid–tocopherol synergy in bilayer defense, with tocopheroxyl-radical recycling by the glutathione network.
