# Acid ceramidase modulates the lipid profile and exacerbates sensitivity to ferroptosis in WI-38 replicative senescent cells

**David Soriano-Castell, Marie Goujon, Nawab John Dar, Antonio Currais & Pamela Maher**
*Cellular Neurobiology Laboratory, The Salk Institute for Biological Studies, La Jolla, CA, USA.*

- **Received:** 21 November 2025 **Revised:** 22 June 2026 **Accepted:** 3 July 2026
- **DOI:** [10.1038/s41419-026-09108-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41419-026-09108-y)
- **Companion press release:** [[_document_ - Could this enzyme help remove "zombie" cells from our tissues?|Salk press release — "Could this enzyme help remove 'zombie' cells from our tissues?"]]

> [!info] Study at a glance
> Using human fetal lung [[Fibroblast|WI-38 fibroblasts]] driven to [[Replicative Senescence|replicative senescence]], the authors show that [[Acid ceramidase]] (ASAH1/ACase) is strongly over-expressed in senescent cells and creates a pro-ferroptotic [[Lipid Metabolism|membrane lipid profile]] (elevated [[PUFA|polyunsaturated fatty acids]] in [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]]) that sensitizes cells to [[Ferroptosis]]. This effect is **independent of the [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione|GSH]] axis and of [[Iron|iron]] regulation**, and is transmitted to neighboring proliferative cells through SASP cytokines [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]].

## Abstract

[[Cellular Senescence|Cellular senescence]], a complex biological process characterized by irreversible [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle arrest]] and the [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]], has emerged as a critical target for therapeutic development for age-related diseases. [[Ferroptosis]], an [[Iron|iron]]-dependent regulated cell death pathway driven by the accumulation of [[Lipid Peroxidation|lipid peroxidation]] in cell membranes, has been implicated in age-related disorders. This study investigated the relationship between [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]] and [[Ferroptosis]]. Using human fetal lung WI-38 [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]] induced to senesce via [[Replicative Senescence|replicative exhaustion]], we report a novel role for [[Acid ceramidase]] (ACase), which breaks down [[Ceramide|ceramides]] into sphingosine and free fatty acids, in regulating the sensitivity of senescent cells to [[RSL3]]-induced [[Lipid Peroxidation|lipid peroxidation]] and [[Ferroptosis]] through the modulation of polyunsaturated fatty acid composition of membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]]. Furthermore, we demonstrate a cell non-autonomous paracrine sensitization of non-senescent cells to [[Ferroptosis]] by senescent cells. Together, these findings unveil ACase as a novel regulator of the [[Ferroptosis]] pathway and open promising therapeutic avenues for targeting [[Senescence|senescence]]-linked disorders and advancing healthy aging strategies.

## Introduction

Although emerging evidence suggests a potential link between [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]] and the regulated cell death pathway of [[Ferroptosis]], the molecular mechanisms underpinning this interplay remain largely unresolved. [[Ferroptosis]] is a non-apoptotic [[Iron|iron]]-dependent cell death pathway, also called **oxytosis**, that can be induced by [[Glutathione|GSH]] depletion or the inhibition of the GSH-dependent peroxidase [[GPX4]]. These processes generate a burst of [[Reactive Oxygen Species|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]], resulting in increased [[Lipid Peroxidation|peroxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs)]] in cell membranes which ultimately contributes to cell death. Over the years, the authors have used the [[Ferroptosis]] pathway as a therapeutic target in a phenotypic cell-based screening platform to identify potential drug candidates for age-related diseases. This led to the discovery of potent anti-ferroptotic compounds, two of which ([[J147]] and CMS121) have completed Phase 1 clinical trials for [[Alzheimer's Disease]].

> [!note] Why senescence is a therapeutic target
> Despite the challenges posed by heterogeneous [[Senescent Cells|senescent cell]] populations and the absence of universal biomarkers, accumulating *in vivo* and *in vitro* evidence underscores the pivotal role of [[Senescence|senescence]] in various age-related disorders. [[Senescent Cells|Senescent cells]] have become enticing targets for interventions aimed at mitigating age-related diseases, though clinical translation requires careful consideration of potential off-target toxicity (especially in geropharmacology).

[[Cellular Senescence|Cellular senescence]] is characterized by irreversible [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle arrest]] triggered by prolonged stressors such as [[DNA Damage]], chemotherapy, or [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], and is accompanied by profound metabolic alterations, particularly in [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid metabolism]]. Another hallmark is the secretion of the [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP)]], whose components—including IL-1, [[IL-6]], and [[IL-8]]—reshape local tissue microenvironments and exert broader paracrine effects, even propagating the senescent phenotype to healthy cells and amplifying tissue dysfunction.

> [!info] The lipid-metabolism hook
> [[Senescent Cells|Senescent cells]] display significant alterations in [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid metabolism]]: accumulation of [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid droplets]], increased lipid uptake, altered fatty acid composition, and reduced membrane fluidity. Several [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid-metabolism]] enzymes are over-expressed in senescent cells, including [[ACSL4]] and [[Acid ceramidase]] (ACase, ASAH1). While [[ACSL4]] has been implicated in [[Ferroptosis]], the role of ACase in this type of cell death had never been explored.

Using the WI-38 [[Replicative Senescence|replicative senescence]] model, the present study shows a previously uncharacterized role for ACase in modulating [[Ferroptosis]] through alterations in cellular [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid composition]]. We demonstrate that ACase upregulation drives increased [[Ferroptosis|sensitivity to ferroptosis]] and that inhibition of ACase expression significantly attenuates [[Ferroptosis]] independently of the [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione|GSH]] axis and [[Iron|iron]] regulation, by decreasing the levels of [[PUFA|PUFAs]] crucial for [[Ferroptosis]] in membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]]. Furthermore, our findings reveal a paracrine cell non-autonomous induction of ACase expression and [[Ferroptosis|ferroptotic sensitization]] in proliferative cells by elements of the [[SASP]].

## Results

### Lipid peroxidation and ferroptosis sensitivity increase as cells become senescent

Several studies have suggested a dysregulation of [[Ferroptosis]] in different types of [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]]. Comparing proliferative WI-38 cells with senescent cells generated by [[Replicative Senescence|replicative exhaustion]], cells became senescent after PDL60, displaying strong β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) staining. Treating senescent and proliferative cells (PDL20, PDL30, PDL40) overnight with [[RSL3]] (a [[GPX4]] inhibitor and well-known [[Ferroptosis]] inducer) showed survival against [[RSL3]] decreased significantly as doublings increased, with the lowest survival after senescence. The effect was prevented by co-treating with [[J147]], a compound that specifically prevents [[Lipid Peroxidation|lipid peroxidation]] and [[Ferroptosis]], confirming senescent cells undergo [[Ferroptosis]].

Since [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]] is a main hallmark of [[Ferroptosis]], its levels were quantified by flow cytometry using C11-BODIPY 581/591. Senescent WI-38 cells displayed significantly higher [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]] than replicative cells, both under control conditions and when treated with [[RSL3]]. There is a clear increase in [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]] and [[Ferroptosis|ferroptosis sensitivity]] with the number of cellular divisions, maximal after [[Replicative Senescence|replicative exhaustion]].

### Acid ceramidase inhibition protects senescent cells against ferroptosis

[[ACSL4]] and [[Acid ceramidase]] (ACase) are over-expressed in [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]]. Knockdown (KD) of either [[ACSL4]] or ACase with specific siRNAs (siACSL4; siACase) strongly protected proliferative and senescent cells against [[RSL3]] compared to control siRNA. While [[ACSL4]]'s role in [[Ferroptosis]] is well established, the involvement of ACase was undocumented—so the study focused on ACase. ACase breaks down [[Ceramide|ceramide]] into [[Sphingosine|sphingosine]] and fatty acids, affecting the abundance of lipid species relevant to [[Ferroptosis]] ([[Sphingomyelin|sphingomyelin]], ceramide-1-phosphate, or fatty acids). Western blot confirmed 5- to 20-fold over-expression of ACase in senescent vs. replicative WI-38 cells. After 72 h of siACase transfection, ACase was undetectable in replicative cells and strongly (but not completely) reduced in senescent cells—consistent with increased stability of ACase in senescent cells.

> [!important] Senescence features persist after ACase knockdown
> [[Acid ceramidase]] KD did **not** reverse the senescent phenotype: siACase senescent cells retained high [[p21]] expression, high β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal) activity, and significantly increased secretion of [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]]—two of the best-known SASP cytokines. This means ACase inhibition selectively targets [[Ferroptosis|ferroptotic vulnerability]] while leaving the senescence arrest and SASP intact.

Chemical inhibition with **ARN14794**, a specific ACase inhibitor, also protected proliferative and senescent WI-38 cells and neuronal-derived murine **HT22** cells (a well-studied [[Ferroptosis]] model) against [[RSL3]]. ACase KD reduced [[RSL3]]-induced [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]] in both proliferative and senescent cells, correlating with increased resistance to [[Ferroptosis]].

### The protection by ACase inhibition is independent of both GPX4/GSH axis and iron

To explore the anti-ferroptotic mechanism, the authors measured [[Glutathione|GSH]]. Senescent cells had higher basal [[Glutathione|GSH]] than proliferative cells. ACase silencing induced a significant increase in total [[Glutathione|GSH]] in both. To test whether this increase mediated protection, cells were treated with **buthionine sulfoximine (BSO)**, which inhibits [[Glutathione Synthetase|γ-glutamylcysteine synthetase]] and depletes [[Glutathione|GSH]]. BSO depleted [[Glutathione|GSH]] in all conditions and caused significant death in siCTRL cells—yet **ACase KD conferred strong resistance even after [[RSL3]] treatment**. Likewise, [[GPX4]] protein was increased in senescent cells, but ACase KD *reduced* [[GPX4]] expression while still protecting—suggesting protection is independent of the [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione|GSH]] axis and that GSH/GPX4 changes are consequences of attenuated ferroptotic pressure.

> [!warning] Iron is not the mediator either
> The labile [[Iron|iron]] pool (LIP, reactive Fe²⁺) is a classic [[Ferroptosis]] driver via the [[Fenton Reaction]]. ACase KD increased [[Ferritin|FTH1]] (ferritin heavy chain 1), a buffer of Fe²⁺. Surprisingly, LIP levels were *increased* in senescent cells after ACase KD despite the anti-ferroptotic effect. Thus, ACase inhibition protects **independently of both the [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione|GSH]] axis and [[Iron|Fe²⁺]] regulation**—acting upstream of these classic ferroptosis elements.

### ACase upregulation favors a pro-ferroptotic lipid profile, reverted after ACase inhibition

The execution of [[Ferroptosis]] depends on membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]] (PLs) containing [[PUFA|PUFAs]] (PL-PUFAs), the substrates of [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]]. Comprehensive lipidomic profiling (PCs, PEs, PSs, PGs, PIs, [[Ceramide|ceramides]], [[Sphingomyelin|sphingomyelins]]) showed major differences between proliferative and senescent cells, and after ACase KD.

[[Ceramide|Ceramide]] synthesis consumes free saturated (SFA) and monounsaturated (MUFA) fatty acids, diverting them from PL-PUFA production. Conversely, **[[Acid ceramidase]] breakdown of [[Ceramide|ceramide]] releases free fatty acids that feed PUFA and PL-PUFA synthesis.** Despite over-expressing ACase, senescent cells presented higher [[Ceramide|ceramide]] amounts (siACase raised them further). [[Sphingomyelin|SM]] species were also higher in senescent cells; siACase did not substantially change SM.

- **sn-2 PUFA-containing PLs** (the [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]]-susceptible substrates) showed a general increasing trend in senescent cells, with significant increases in all [[Arachidonic acid|arachidonic acid (AA)]]-containing PLs, plus PC-LA, PC-DGLA, PC-DHA, PE-DHA, PE-DGLA, PS-DGLA, PG-DHA and PG-AdA.
- **ACase KD senescent cells** displayed a general *decrease* across the most abundant PL-PUFA species (statistically significant for PC-AA, PG-DHA, PG-DPA; strong trends for PC-DGLA, PE-DGLA, PI-AA, PG-AdA).

> [!info] ACSL4 is not the mechanism
> [[ACSL4]] drives PUFA incorporation into PL-PUFAs during [[Ferroptosis]]. However, siACase did **not** alter [[ACSL4]] protein expression, so the ACase-KD reduction in PL-PUFAs occurs independently of [[ACSL4]]. The proposed model: ACase over-expression increases [[Ceramide|ceramide]] lipolysis → more free SFA/MUFA → (via the Lands cycle) exchange into sn-2 PUFA-PLs → pro-ferroptotic membrane remodeling.

Taken together, ACase upregulation after [[Replicative Senescence|replicative senescence]] favors a pro-ferroptotic [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid profile]], and ACase inhibition protects by decreasing the [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]] substrate pool in membranes.

### Paracrine SASP signaling induces ACase expression and ferroptosis sensitivity in neighboring cells

[[Senescent Cells|Senescent cells]] affect healthy neighbors via the paracrine [[SASP]]. Proliferative cells were incubated (without direct contact) with inserts containing senescent or proliferative cells. Co-culture with senescent cells induced a significant time-dependent increase in ACase expression in proliferative cells, while [[ACSL4]] was unaffected. A similar effect was observed after incubating proliferative cells with recombinant [[IL-6]]/[[IL-8]] (2:1 ratio)—two representative SASP cytokines used synergistically.

> [!important] Bystander ferroptotic sensitization
> Proliferative cells co-cultured with [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]], or incubated with [[IL-6]]/[[IL-8]], showed a small but significant decrease in viability after [[RSL3]] and significantly elevated [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]]. Thus, SASP cytokines induce senescent-like features—including elevated ACase and exacerbated [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]]—that sensitize neighboring cells to [[Ferroptosis]]. This provides a mechanistic basis for how a few [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] can spread both senescence *and* ferroptotic vulnerability through tissue.

## Discussion

Although the high-PDL WI-38 model represents an advanced stage of [[Replicative Senescence|replicative senescence]] that may occur only in a subset of cells *in vivo*, it consistently recapitulates key SASP and metabolic reprogramming shared with other senescence models, providing a homogeneous, mechanistically defined context to interrogate [[Ferroptosis]]-related metabolic alterations. Mixed reports exist on [[Ferroptosis]] in senescent cells (some show *resistance* via disrupted ferritin/[[Iron|iron]] regulation); however, the present results clearly indicate that replicative senescent WI-38 fibroblasts have elevated [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]] and higher [[Ferroptosis|sensitivity to ferroptosis]] than proliferative cells. The authors predict senescence would be pro-ferroptotic in other contexts where similar ACase upregulation and [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid profile]] changes occur.

> [!note] Why GPX4 rises but isn't the driver
> Senescent cells showed elevated [[GPX4]] and [[Glutathione|GSH]]—likely a compensatory response to a chronically increased [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]] substrate burden. ACase KD reduced both [[GPX4]] and [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]], consistent with an anti-ferroptotic state where less [[PUFA|PUFA]] substrate lowers demand for GSH-dependent antioxidant activity.

The Kennedy pathway and the Lands cycle remodel membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]]: free SFA/MUFAs (from *de novo* lipogenesis, intake, or [[Ceramide|ceramide]] lipolysis) are activated by [[ACSL4]] acyl-CoA synthetases and exchanged into sn-2 PUFA-PLs by PLA2/[[ACSL4]]. ACase over-expression amplifies free SFA/MUFA release from [[Ceramide|ceramide]] lipolysis, and the resulting excess can promote PUFA-PL accumulation through Lands-cycle exchange. PC and PG showed the most significant changes; PG is enriched in mitochondria (inner membrane → [[Cardiolipin]]), and PUFA-CL peroxidation is a marker of mitochondrial [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]], suggesting a possible mitochondrial dimension of senescence-linked [[Ferroptosis]].

[[ACSL4]] is a critical regulator of PL-PUFA composition and is up-regulated during senescence. High [[ACSL4]] expression raises the PL-PUFA content and ferroptosis sensitivity; [[ACSL4]]-KD protects senescent cells against [[RSL3]] in this model. Although ACase inhibition lowered PL-PUFA and [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]], [[ACSL4]] protein remained high after ACase KD—suggesting reduced SFA/MUFA-PL supply (not [[ACSL4]]) drives the PUFA-PL decrease. The increase in [[Ceramide|ceramide]] after ACase KD could further limit free SFA/MUFA for PUFA re-acylation.

> [!warning] A dual, context-dependent role for ACase
> ACase has also been shown to *promote survival* of [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] by controlling [[Ceramide|ceramide]] levels and raising [[Sphingosine|sphingosine]]/S1P (pro-survival, anti-[[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]). This metabolic shift may cause [[Ferroptosis|ferroptotic susceptibility]] as an unintended secondary effect. Conversely, ACase activity contributes to tumorigenesis in [[Melanoma]] and radiotherapy resistance in [[Prostate Cancer]], and ACase is over-expressed in various malignancies—where elevated [[Iron|iron]], oxidative stress, and altered [[Lipid Metabolism|lipid metabolism]] already confer ferroptotic vulnerability. The interplay among survival, senescence, and [[Cancer]] is therefore complex and warrants further study.

> [!tip] Therapeutic implication
> Because eliminating [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] may reduce tissue-wide ferroptotic-related stress of aging, and because ACase inhibitors already exist (e.g., ARN14794, developed for other indications), ACase represents a tractable **[[Senolytic|senolytic]]/[[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] target** for attenuating the deleterious effects of senescent cells during aging—a "two birds with one stone" strategy that eliminates senescent cells while keeping neighboring cells healthy.

## Materials and methods (summary)

- **Cells & senescence:** Human WI-38 embryonic lung [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]] (Coriell) and HT22 mouse hippocampal neurons; senescence induced by [[Replicative Senescence|replicative exhaustion]] (senescence at PDL55–60); characterized by β-galactosidase (SA-β-gal), [[p21]], and SASP cytokines [[IL-6]]/[[IL-8]].
- **Ferroptosis assay:** [[RSL3]] (200 nM) ± compounds; viability by MTT.
- **LPO & labile iron:** C11-BODIPY 581/591 ([[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]]) and FerroFarRed (labile Fe²⁺/LIP) by flow cytometry.
- **GSH:** recycling assay; BSO used to deplete [[Glutathione|GSH]].
- **Western blot:** ASAH1 (ACase), [[ACSL4]], [[Ferritin|FTH1]], [[GPX4]], [[p21]].
- **siRNA:** siACase (ASAH1) and siACSL4 vs. control.
- **Lipidomics:** LIPID MAPS core (UCSD); PCs, PEs, PSs, PGs, PIs, [[Ceramide|ceramides]], [[Sphingomyelin|sphingomyelins]].
- **Cytokines/co-culture:** recombinant [[IL-6]]/[[IL-8]] (2:1) or trans-well inserts with senescent cells.
