# Metformin
**[[Metformin]]** is the world's most widely prescribed medication for type 2 [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]]. Beyond its role in controlling blood sugar, metformin has gained significant attention in [[Geroscience]] as a potential "geroprotector"—a drug that can slow the fundamental processes of aging.
Metformin is a first-line medication for type 2 diabetes that also acts as a caloric restriction mimetic and [[Autophagy Inducer]] by activating [[AMPK]].
## 1. Mechanisms of Action
Metformin's effects are systemic and pleiotropic:
*   **Activation of [[AMPK]]**: Metformin activates the AMPK pathway, which mimics the effects of [[Caloric Restriction]] by promoting energy efficiency and cellular repair.
*   **Inhibition of [[mTOR]]**: By activating AMPK, metformin indirectly inhibits the mTOR pathway, leading to increased [[Autophagy]] and reduced cellular growth.
*   **Reduction of Inflammation**: Metformin has been shown to reduce the production of pro-inflammatory factors, including those associated with the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].
## 2. Epigenetic Effects
Research indicates that metformin can influence the [[Epigenome]]:
*   **Epigenetic Clock**: Studies have shown that metformin treatment can slow the rate of the [[Epigenetic Clock]] (e.g., [[DNAmGrimAge]]), indicating a slower rate of biological aging.
*   **[[DNA Methylation]]**: Metformin may help maintain youthful DNA methylation patterns by influencing the activity of enzymes like [[DNMT1]].
## 3. The TAME Trial
The potential anti-aging effects of metformin are currently being tested in the "Targeting Aging with Metformin" (TAME) trial, the first clinical trial designed to see if a drug can delay the onset of multiple age-related diseases in humans.
## Mechanism of Action
Metformin's primary molecular target is [[Mitochondrial Complex I]] (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase), where it acts as a mild, reversible inhibitor. This inhibition produces a transient decrease in mitochondrial ATP production, increasing the cellular [[AMP]]/[[ATP]] ratio, which activates [[AMP-Activated Protein Kinase]] (AMPK). AMPK activation orchestrates a metabolic shift from anabolic (lipogenesis, gluconeogenesis) to catabolic (fatty acid oxidation, glucose uptake) pathways. The Complex I inhibition is concentration-dependent: at therapeutic portal vein concentrations (100–500 µM), inhibition is approximately 10–30%, producing hormetic — not cytotoxic — metabolic stress. Metformin also inhibits mitochondrial [[Glycerol-3-Phosphate Dehydrogenase]] (mGPDH), altering the cytosolic redox state and suppressing gluconeogenesis from lactate and glycerol. Additional targets include [[Fructose-1,6-Bisphosphatase]], [[Glucagon Signaling]] via AMPK-independent modulation of [[Adenylate Cyclase]], and [[Lysosome]]-dependent [[Rag GTPases]] pathways that converge on [[mTORC1]] suppression.
## Effects on AGE Formation
Metformin reduces circulating [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] through multiple convergent mechanisms. Directly, metformin traps [[Methylglyoxal]] (MG) and other reactive dicarbonyl species through its biguanidine moiety, forming triazepinone adducts that are excreted in urine. In clinical studies, metformin treatment reduces plasma [[Methylglyoxal]] levels by 30–50% in patients with type 2 diabetes, correlating with reduced [[Carboxymethyllysine]] ([[CML]]) and MG-derived [[Hydroimidazolone]] (MG-H1) levels. Indirectly, metformin's AMPK-mediated suppression of gluconeogenesis reduces glycolytic flux, the primary source of [[Methylglyoxal]] production. By lowering blood glucose, metformin also decreases substrate availability for the [[Maillard Reaction]]. Furthermore, metformin upregulates [[Glyoxalase I]] expression through [[NRF2]]/[[ARE]] activation, enhancing endogenous dicarbonyl detoxification capacity. In diabetic animal models, metformin reduces renal and vascular AGE accumulation and attenuates [[RAGE]] expression.
## Anti-Aging Properties and Healthspan Extension
Compelling preclinical evidence suggests metformin extends healthspan across multiple model organisms. In [[C. elegans]], metformin extends lifespan by 20–40% via [[AMPK]]- and [[SKN-1]]/[[NRF2]]-dependent mechanisms. In [[Mus musculus]], metformin delays the onset of age-related pathologies, including [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]], [[Alzheimer's Disease]], and [[Atherosclerosis]], though lifespan extension is inconsistent and more pronounced in female mice and mice on high-fat diets. Mechanistically, metformin mimics aspects of [[Caloric Restriction]] at the transcriptional level, reducing [[mTORC1]] signaling, enhancing [[Autophagy]], attenuating [[Senescence]], and reducing [[Inflammaging]]. Metformin also modulates the gut microbiome, enriching [[Akkermansia muciniphila]] populations that improve metabolic health independently of glucose lowering. The landmark [[Targeting Aging with Metformin]] (TAME) trial — a multi-center, placebo-controlled study — is currently evaluating whether metformin can delay the onset of age-related multimorbidity in non-diabetic older adults.
## Connection to Mitohormesis
Metformin's Complex I inhibition represents a canonical example of [[Mitohormesis]]: mild mitochondrial stress that activates adaptive cytoprotective programs. The transient reduction in [[NADH]] oxidation elevates the mitochondrial [[NADH]]/[[NAD+]] ratio, which is then resolved through [[SIRT1]]-dependent deacetylation of [[PGC-1α]] and [[FOXO]] transcription factors. This adaptive response includes upregulation of [[Superoxide Dismutase 2]] (SOD2), [[Catalase]], and [[Glutathione]] synthesis. The low-grade superoxide burst from Complex I inhibition is essential for metformin's metabolic benefits — antioxidant co-administration (e.g., [[Vitamin C]] or [[Vitamin E]]) attenuates AMPK activation and the glucose-lowering effect in clinical studies. This hormetic principle mirrors the [[_document_ - Mitohormetic Redox-Relay]] strategy, where precisely dosed pro-oxidants (e.g., [[Adrenochrome]] or [[Carbazochrome]]) are used to trigger mitochondrial adaptations. An intriguing synergy exists between metformin and ultralow-dose [[Adrenochrome]]: both act through mitochondrial superoxide signaling to activate overlapping [[NRF2]] and [[AMPK]] pathways, suggesting the possibility of additive or synergistic mitohormetic effects at submaximal doses.
## Clinical Evidence and Potential in Aging
Beyond glycemic control, metformin is associated with reduced all-cause mortality (by 15–30% in observational studies of diabetic patients), decreased cancer incidence (particularly [[Colorectal Cancer|Colorectal]] and [[Breast Cancer]]), and lower risk of [[Cardiovascular Disease]] and [[Cognitive Decline]]. Retrospective analyses suggest metformin-treated diabetic patients may have lower biological age by [[Epigenetic Clock]] measures compared to matched untreated diabetics. Limitations include gastrointestinal intolerance (10–20% of patients), the rare but serious risk of [[Lactic Acidosis]] (primarily in renal impairment), and the absence of definitive evidence for lifespan extension in healthy non-diabetic humans — a gap the TAME trial aims to fill. The potential for combination with other geroprotectors ([[Rapamycin]], [[NAD+]] precursors, [[Urolithin A]], [[Carbazochrome]]) represents an active area of investigation for multimodal aging intervention.
