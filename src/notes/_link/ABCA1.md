# ABCA1

**ABCA1** (ATP-binding cassette subfamily A member 1) is a membrane transporter that promotes the transfer of [[Cholesterol]] and phospholipids to lipid-poor [[Apolipoprotein A1|apolipoprotein A-I (apoA-1)]]. It is primarily responsible for the initiation of [[HDL]] formation, principally in the liver and, to a lesser extent, in the small intestine.

## Mechanism of Action

- ABCA1 mediates [[Cholesterol Efflux]] from peripheral cells (especially [[Macrophages]]) to lipid-poor [[Apolipoprotein A1|apoA-1]]
- This is the first step in [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]] — the process by which excess [[Cholesterol]] is removed from peripheral tissues and transported to the liver
- Over-expression of ABCA1 alters plasma membrane morphology, increases [[Apolipoprotein A1|apoA-1]] binding, and changes [[Cholesterol]] distribution in the external membrane leaflet
- ABCA1 is distinct from [[ABCG1]], which transfers cholesterol to mature [[HDL]] particles rather than lipid-poor apoA-1

## Regulation

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> ABCA1 transcription is strongly induced by [[Liver X Receptor|LXRα/β]] in response to oxysterol accumulation. [[Resveratrol]] induces LXR-α expression in human monocyte-derived macrophages and upregulates ABCA1 protein expression, enhancing apoA-1-mediated cholesterol efflux in a dose-dependent manner.

- [[Liver X Receptor|LXR]] — Master transcriptional activator of ABCA1
- [[Resveratrol]] — Induces ABCA1 protein expression; enhances apoA-1-mediated cholesterol efflux
- Oxidative stress (Fe/ascorbate) impairs ABCA1 expression; [[Resveratrol]] restores it

## Physiological and Clinical Relevance

- By promoting [[Cholesterol Efflux]] from lipid-laden [[Macrophage Foam Cells]], ABCA1 protects against [[Atherosclerosis]]
- ABCA1 dysfunction leads to Tangier disease (severe HDL deficiency)
- ABCA1 is a key therapeutic target for cardiovascular disease and HDL-based strategies
