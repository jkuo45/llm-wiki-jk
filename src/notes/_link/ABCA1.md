# ABCA1

**ABCA1** (ATP-binding cassette subfamily A member 1) is a membrane transporter which in humans is encoded by the *ABCA1* gene. It is a major regulator of cellular [[Cholesterol]] and phospholipid homeostasis, promoting the transfer of [[Cholesterol]] and phospholipids to lipid-poor [[Apolipoprotein A1|apolipoprotein A-I (apoA-I)]]. It is primarily responsible for the initiation of [[HDL]] formation, principally in the liver and, to a lesser extent, in the small intestine.

## Mechanism of Action

- ABCA1 mediates [[Cholesterol Efflux]] from peripheral cells (especially [[Macrophages]]) to lipid-poor [[Apolipoprotein A1|apoA-I]]
- This is the first step in [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]] — the process by which excess [[Cholesterol]] is removed from peripheral tissues and transported to the liver
- Over-expression of ABCA1 alters plasma membrane morphology, increases [[Apolipoprotein A1|apoA-I]] binding, and changes [[Cholesterol]] distribution in the external membrane leaflet
- ABCA1 is distinct from [[ABCG1]], which transfers cholesterol to mature [[HDL]] particles rather than lipid-poor apoA-I
- Its expression is regulated by transcription factors such as [[LXRα]] and [[PPARγ]]

## Regulation

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> ABCA1 transcription is strongly induced by [[Liver X Receptor|LXRα/β]] in response to oxysterol accumulation. [[Resveratrol]] induces LXR-α expression in human monocyte-derived macrophages and upregulates ABCA1 protein expression, enhancing apoA-1-mediated cholesterol efflux in a dose-dependent manner.

- [[Liver X Receptor|LXR]] — Master transcriptional activator of ABCA1
- [[Resveratrol]] — Induces ABCA1 protein expression; enhances apoA-1-mediated cholesterol efflux
- Oxidative stress (Fe/ascorbate) impairs ABCA1 expression; [[Resveratrol]] restores it

## Role in Oxidative Stress

ABCA1 is intimately connected to [[Oxidative Stress]] at multiple levels. The ABCA1 promoter contains [[LXRα]] response elements, and oxidative modifications to LDL (forming **oxidized LDL** or oxLDL) upregulate [[LXRα]] and [[PPARγ]] activity, thereby inducing ABCA1 expression as a protective feedback loop. However, during chronic oxidative stress, this regulatory axis becomes dysregulated.

### Oxidative Modification and Degradation

ABCA1 itself is susceptible to oxidative modification. [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], particularly [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] and [[Peroxynitrite]], can directly oxidize ABCA1 at critical cysteine and methionine residues, leading to:
- **Conformational disruption**: Impairing ATP-binding and hydrolysis necessary for cholesterol translocation
- **Enhanced proteasomal degradation**: Oxidized ABCA1 is recognized by the ubiquitin-proteasome system, reducing its half-life
- **Loss of efflux capacity**: Directly compromising the removal of excess cholesterol from [[Macrophage|macrophages]]

### Connection to Myeloperoxidase and Hypochlorous Acid

[[Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO) derived [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl) selectively chlorinates and inactivates ABCA1 in macrophages. HOCl targets the critical tryptophan and lysine residues in the ATP-binding domains of ABCA1, impairing its ATPase activity. This MPO-mediated ABCA1 dysfunction promotes foam cell formation and accelerates [[Atherosclerosis]] independently of plasma LDL levels.

### ABCA1 and Lipid Peroxidation

ABCA1 facilitates the efflux of oxidized phospholipids and [[Malondialdehyde]] (MDA)-modified lipids to [[Apolipoprotein A1|apoA-I]]. This process is critical for:
- Removing toxic lipid peroxidation products from cells
- Preventing secondary oxidative damage to intracellular [[Mitochondria]] and endoplasmic reticulum
- Reducing [[Inflammation]] by clearing pro-inflammatory lipid species

## Physiological and Clinical Relevance

- By promoting [[Cholesterol Efflux]] from lipid-laden [[Macrophage Foam Cells]], ABCA1 protects against [[Atherosclerosis]]
- ABCA1 dysfunction leads to **Tangier disease** (severe HDL deficiency), characterized by extremely low HDL, accumulation of cholesterol esters in tissues, and increased risk of [[Cardiovascular Disease]]
- ABCA1 is a key therapeutic target for cardiovascular disease and HDL-based strategies
- ABCA1 upregulation by pharmacological LXR agonists is a therapeutic strategy, though limited by hepatic steatotic side effects
- Antioxidants such as [[Vitamin E]] and [[Glutathione]] precursors preserve ABCA1 function by protecting it from oxidative inactivation
