# COL1A1

**COL1A1** encodes the alpha-1 chain of type I collagen, the most abundant fibrillar collagen in mammals and the principal structural protein of the [[Extracellular Matrix|extracellular matrix (ECM)]] in skin, bone, tendon, and myocardium. Type I collagen is synthesized as a heterotrimer of two alpha-1 chains and one alpha-2 chain (COL1A2), and its expression is the hallmark readout of fibroblast activation and fibrotic tissue deposition. COL1A1 transcription is driven by profibrotic programs including [[TGF-beta1|TGF-β1]]/[[SMAD3|Smad3]] signaling and is suppressed by epigenetic and transcriptional brakes such as SIRT3-dependent histone deacetylation.

## Regulation by SIRT3 in cardiac fibrosis

[[SIRT3]] suppresses myocardial fibrosis through inhibition of [[p53]] acetylation and [[Ferroptosis|ferroptosis]], along with deacetylation of pro-fibrotic genes such as COL1A1 and [[TGF-beta1|TGF-β1]], ultimately reducing ECM deposition in pressure-overloaded cardiomyocytes[126]. In heart failure, SIRT3 deficiency therefore shifts the balance toward collagen type I accumulation, increased myocardial stiffness, and progressive ventricular dysfunction — consistent with SIRT3's role as a master anti-fibrotic node operating at the interface of mitochondrial redox and nuclear transcriptional control.

## Clinical relevance

COL1A1-driven myocardial fibrosis is a determinant of diastolic dysfunction, arrhythmic substrate, and heart failure progression. Monitoring COL1A1 suppression (e.g., via circulating collagen biomarkers) may serve as a readout of SIRT3-activating therapies in cardiac fibrosis.
