# CDO1

**CDO1** (cysteine dioxygenase type 1) is a sulfur-metabolism enzyme that oxidizes cysteine to cysteine sulfinate, committing cysteine to the taurine/ sulfate pathways. In [[Gastric cancer]] (GC), CDO1 acts as a positive regulator of [[Ferroptosis]]: by competitively consuming cysteine, it restricts the substrate available for [[Glutathione]] (GSH) synthesis, lowering antioxidant capacity and promoting lipid peroxidation.

## Structure & Biochemistry

CDO1 is a non-heme iron-dependent dioxygenase in the cytosol/mitochondria that oxygenates the thiol of cysteine. Its activity determines how much cysteine is diverted away from GSH biosynthesis toward catabolism.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review (citing Hao et al.) reports that erastin induces ferroptosis in GC cells and that [[CDO1]] plays a key regulatory role: CDO1 competitively absorbs cysteine, restricting GSH synthesis and promoting ferroptosis. Inhibiting CDO1 restores cellular GSH, prevents ROS production, reduces lipid peroxidation, and ultimately inhibits ferroptosis (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Gastric cancer"). Thus CDO1 is a ferroptosis *promoter* by limiting the cysteine pool that feeds GPX4 defense.

> [!info] Cysteine competition as a ferroptosis switch
> CDO1 and system Xc- compete for cysteine: CDO1 diverts it to catabolism, while system Xc- import supports GSH. High CDO1 lowers the GSH set-point and sensitizes GC to erastin.

## Clinical Relevance

CDO1 expression could serve as a biomarker of ferroptosis sensitivity in GC, and modulating CDO1 activity may tune responses to erastin or [[Cisplatin]]. It exemplifies sulfur-transfer/gas-metabolism cross-talk with ferroptosis regulation.
