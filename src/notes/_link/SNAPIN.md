# SNAPIN

SNAPIN is a protein that drives retrograde transport of [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] from synapses to the cell body (soma), coordinating with [[Parkin]]-mediated [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] to maintain synaptic mitochondrial integrity.

## Function in Neuronal Mitophagy

Synaptic mitochondrial integrity is maintained through the coordination of Parkin-mediated mitophagy and SNAPIN-driven retrograde transport. In [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]] models, an imbalance occurs where increased mitophagy initiation is coupled with defective retrograde transport, leading to the accumulation of partially degraded [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] within synapses. Enhancing SNAPIN-mediated transport facilitates the removal of these damaged organelles from the synapse to the soma, effectively alleviating mitophagy stress and rescuing synaptic deficits.

## Implications for Neurodegeneration

SNAPIN function is critical for maintaining mitochondrial quality in distal neuronal processes. The integration of [[PINK1]]-[[DRP1|Drp1]] mediated fission, [[BOK]]-facilitated Parkin recruitment, and SNAPIN-dependent retrograde transport constitutes a multi-layered quality control system ensuring synapses are populated only by healthy, functional mitochondria.
