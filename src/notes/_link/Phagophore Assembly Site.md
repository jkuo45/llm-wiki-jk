# Phagophore Assembly Site

## Overview

The [[Phagophore Assembly Site]] (PAS) is the discrete intracellular location where [[Autophagy]] is initiated. It is the platform on which the phagophore—the crescent-shaped, double-membraned isolation membrane—is nucleated, elongated, and ultimately sealed into the mature [[Autophagosome]]. In yeast the PAS is a single, defined structure; in mammals multiple PASs form in response to stress.

## Structure & Composition

The PAS is organized around the [[ULK1]] initiation complex (ULK1–ATG13–ATG101–FIP200), which recruits the autophagy-specific [[Vps34]] complex containing [[Beclin1]] and [[Atg14L]]. Locally produced [[PtdIns3P]] recruits WIPI/Atg18 family proteins that template downstream ATG conjugation systems. Membranes are supplied from multiple sources, including the endoplasmic reticulum, [[Mitochondria]], and [[Golgi apparatus]]; ATG9-positive vesicles traffic to the PAS to deliver lipids.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Nutrient depletion inactivates [[mTOR]] and activates the AMPK–ULK1 axis, licensing PAS formation. ULK1 phosphorylates downstream targets to assemble the Vps34 complex and generate PtdIns3P, which in turn recruits the ATG12–ATG5–ATG16L1 complex and the [[LC3]] (Atg8) lipidation cascade via the E2 enzyme [[Atg7]] and E3 [[Atg12|ATG12–ATG5]]. Elongation proceeds as LC3-II decorates the phagophore outer membrane, capturing cargo through [[LIR Motif]]-bearing receptors such as [[p62/SQSTM1]]. Closure yields the autophagosome destined for the [[Lysosome]].

## Physiological Function

The PAS concentrates the autophagy machinery to ensure rapid, spatially controlled initiation of autophagic capture, enabling efficient clearance of damaged organelles and protein aggregates under starvation or stress.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Defective PAS organization impairs autophagic flux and contributes to neurodegeneration and [[Cancer]]. Pharmacologic induction of PAS formation is a strategy to harness therapeutic autophagy.
