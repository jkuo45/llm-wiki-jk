# Insulin Receptor

The **insulin receptor** (INSR, CD294) is a single-pass transmembrane
**receptor tyrosine kinase** encoded by the *INSR* gene. It is the obligatory
entry point for insulin's metabolic effects and the highest-affinity receptor
for [[IGF1]] and [[IGF-II]].

## Structure

The receptor is synthesised as a ~320 kDa single-chain precursor that is
cleaved by furin at the Arg810–Lys811 site into a **disulfide-linked α and β
heterodimer**. The α chain is entirely extracellular and forms the ligand
binding surface; the β chain spans the membrane and carries the intracellular
tyrosine kinase.

The ectodomain is organised into leucine-rich repeat domains L1 and L2,
flanking a cysteine-rich region, followed by three fibronectin type III
domains. In the ligand-free state the dimer adopts an **inverted-V**; insulin
binding converts it to a **T-shaped** conformation, which is transmitted
through the transmembrane helices to juxtapose and activate the two cytoplasmic
kinase domains.

Alternative splicing of *INSR* produces two isoforms that differ by the inclusion
or exclusion of **exon 11**, which adds 12 amino acids at the C-terminus of the
α chain (the α<sub>CT</sub> segment):

- **IR-A** (exon 11 excluded) — predominant in fetal and cancer tissues.
- **IR-B** (exon 11 included) — predominant in metabolic tissues, notably
  liver, muscle, and adipose.

The α<sub>CT</sub> segment is required for normal insulin binding; its absence
in IR-A means IR-A binds IGF-II and insulin less avidly, which is one proposed
mechanism by which tumours gain a growth advantage.

> [!info] Binding is negatively cooperative
> Each monomer has two insulin binding sites — "site 1" on the L1 + α<sub>CT</sub>
> surface, and "site 2" in a loop at the FnIII-1/FnIII-2 junction. Occupancy of
> site 1 *inhibits* further binding at site 2, so binding curves deviate from
> simple linearity in a way Scatchard analysis makes explicit.

## Signalling

Autophosphorylation on tyrosines 1158/1162/1163 within the activation loop
creates a binding surface for signalling adapters:

- **[[IRS1]]** and IRS2 dock via their PTB domains; the receptor then
  phosphorylates IRS tyrosines in NPXY motifs.
- **[[SH2-B]]**, APS, and—in muscle and fat—**c-CBL** provide additional
  scaffolding.
- Recruited PI3K generates PIP3, engaging [[PDK1]] and [[Akt]]; the canonical
  output is GLUT4 translocation (via [[Akt]] → AS160/TBC1D4) in muscle and
  adipose, [[GLUT2]]-mediated glucose uptake in liver, and GSK3 inhibition.
- The **Shrapen-binding domain** couples the receptor to the
  [[Grb2]]-[[SOS]]-[[ERK]] arm of the [[MAPK Signaling]] pathway, which is
  mitogenic rather than metabolic.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The distinction between "insulin-sensitive" and "insulin-resistant" is not a
> property of the receptor alone. In [[Type 2 Diabetes]] the receptor is
  usually present and often *hyper*-insulinised; the lesion is in the signal
  attenuation machinery — [[PTP1B]], SOCS, and inflammatory serine
  phosphorylation of IRS1. This is why PTP1B inhibitors were developed as
  insulin sensitisers rather than receptor agonists.

## Pathology

- **Type A insulin resistance** and **Rabson-Mendenhall syndrome** — caused by
  *INSR* mutations; classically accompanied by acanthosis nigricans,
  hyperandrogenism, and pineal hyperplasia.
- **Donohue syndrome (leprechaunism)** — the severe homozygous neonatal form.
- **Acanthosis nigricans with hyperinsulinism** in children, and MODY-like
  phenotypes, are associated with biallelic or heterozygous *INSR* variants.
- **Cancer** — IR-A overexpression is reported in many epithelial tumours, and
  receptor tyrosine kinases are frequent therapeutic targets, though INSR itself
  is rarely drugged directly.
- **Neurodegeneration** — impaired brain insulin signalling is a well-supported
  feature of [[Alzheimer's Disease]]; the relationship is associative and
  remains mechanistically contested (see [[Beta-amyloid]] in the Documents
  section).

## Connections

- [[Insulin]] — The cognate ligand. Insulin binding to the α chain triggers
  conformational change, kinase activation, and IRS phosphorylation; without
  this receptor insulin has no metabolic effect. The stub's first inbound
  document link is retained.

- [[PTP1B]] — PTP1B dephosphorylates the receptor (Tyr1162) and IRS1,
  terminating signalling; it is the dominant negative regulator and the reason
  the pathway is a bounded rather than self-amplifying response. PTP1B knockouts
  are insulin-sensitive and resist diet-induced obesity. The stub's second
  inbound document link is retained.

- [[IRS1]] — The primary signal transducer. IRS1 phosphorylation creates the
  docking sites for PI3K that generate PIP3; IRS1 serine phosphorylation
  instead recruits inhibitory proteins and is a hallmark of inflammatory
  insulin resistance.

- [[IGF1]] — The IR and IGF1R form hybrid receptors with each other and with
  insulin-like growth factor receptor hybrids, creating a continuum of
  signalling specificity. IGF1R itself is a direct upstream activator of IRS1.

- [[Beta-amyloid]] — Impaired cerebral insulin signalling is a recurring
  finding in [[Alzheimer's Disease]], and brain insulin resistance is proposed
  to interact with amyloid and tau pathology. Evidence is associative; a
  causal direction has not been established, and [[ApoE4]] genotype modifies
  the relationship. The stub's inbound document link is retained.

- [[Insulin Resistance]] — The clinical state in which insulin signalling is
  blunted despite normal or elevated receptor abundance. The IR is where the
  defect is expressed, but the lesion usually lies downstream.

- [[Akt]] — The central effector kinase. AKT activation is the event that
  drives GLUT4 trafficking, protein synthesis, and the anti-apoptotic
  programme; it is also a major node in [[mTOR]] signalling.

- [[c-CBL]] — Ubiquitin ligase that is phosphorylated by the IR in muscle and
  adipose and downregulates receptor surface abundance, providing a second,
  receptor-level brake alongside PTP1B.
