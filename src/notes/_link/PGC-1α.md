# PGC-1α

**PGC-1α** (encoded by *PPARGC1A*) is a transcriptional coactivator that serves as the master regulator of [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]], [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|oxidative metabolism]], and [[Oxidative Stress|antioxidant]] defense. It links external stimuli (exercise, cold, fasting) to the activation of mitochondrial and metabolic gene programs.

## Structure & Domains

- **N-terminal activation domain** — interacts with nuclear receptors ([[PPARγ]], [[ERRalpha]]) and [[CREB]]; recruits [[P300]]/[[CBP]] and the Mediator complex
- **Central regulatory region** — harbors phosphorylation sites for [[AMPK]] and [[p38 MAPK]], as well as acetylation sites targeted by [[SIRT1]] and [[GCN5]]
- **C-terminal domain** — contains an RNA recognition motif (RRM) and serine/arginine-rich (RS) domain that couples transcription to pre-mRNA splicing

## Mechanism of Action

PGC-1α lacks intrinsic DNA-binding activity and exerts its coactivator functions through physical interaction with diverse transcription factors:

- **SIRT1 deacetylation**: [[SIRT1]] deacetylates PGC-1α at multiple lysine residues (e.g., K778) in an [[NAD+]]-dependent manner, enhancing its transcriptional activity toward mitochondrial genes (PMID 15592568, 17086196). This couples the cellular NAD+/NADH ratio to oxidative capacity.
- **AMPK phosphorylation**: [[AMPK]] phosphorylates PGC-1α at Thr177 and Ser538, promoting its deacetylation by SIRT1 and increasing its stability. This reciprocal regulation establishes a feed-forward loop linking mitochondrial biogenesis to metabolic state.
- **mTORC1 regulation**: PGC-1α is controlled by [[mTORC1]] through its physical interaction with the transcription factor [[YY1]].
- **Transcriptional program**: Co-activates [[NRF1]], [[NRF2]], [[PPARα]], [[PPARγ]], [[ERRalpha]], and [[YY1]], controlling expression of over 1,000 nuclear-encoded mitochondrial genes.

> [!tip] NAD+ dependence
> Because SIRT1 requires [[NAD+]] as a cosubstrate, PGC-1α activity reports on the cellular redox/energy state — a key reason boosting NAD+ or sirtuin activators enhances mitochondrial fitness.

## Physiological Functions

PGC-1α regulates a broad metabolic programme including:
- **Mitochondrial biogenesis** — increasing mitochondrial number via [[NRF1]]/[[NRF2]] → [[TFAM]] pathway
- **Oxidative phosphorylation gene expression** — promotes mitochondrial ATP production
- **Fatty acid oxidation** — particularly in skeletal muscle
- **Gluconeogenesis** — hepatic glucose production during fasting/CR
- **Adaptive thermogenesis** — brown adipose tissue activation via [[UCP1]]
- **Antioxidant defense** — induces [[SOD2]], [[Catalase]], [[Glutathione Peroxidase]]

## Neuroprotection

PGC-1α is a critical neuroprotective factor:
- **PGC-1α null mice** are significantly more sensitive to neuronal loss by reactive oxygen species-generating compounds: [[MPTP]] (disrupts substantia nigra) and kainic acid (disrupts hippocampus)
- Increasing PGC-1α levels substantially protects neurons from oxidative-stress-induced death
- SIRT1's regulation of PGC-1α is proposed as a primary mechanism of SIRT1-mediated neuroprotection

## Role in Mitohormesis

PGC-1α is a key effector in the [[Mitohormesis]] paradigm: mild mitochondrial dysfunction activates PGC-1α via [[AMPK]] and [[CaMK]], which in turn induces antioxidant programs ([[Manganese superoxide dismutase|SOD2]], [[Catalase]], [[UCP2]]) to mitigate ROS produced during increased metabolic activity.

## Regulation

PGC-1α is regulated by multiple inputs:
- **Phosphorylation:** [[AMPK]], [[p38 MAPK]], [[Akt]]
- **Deacetylation:** [[SIRT1]] (NAD+-dependent), opposing [[GCN5]] acetylation
- **Methylation:** [[PRMT1]]
- **Transcriptional:** [[CREB]], [[MEF2]], [[FOXO]]

## Role in Aging

PGC-1α expression declines with age in multiple tissues, contributing to mitochondrial dysfunction, reduced oxidative capacity, and metabolic inflexibility. Upregulation by exercise, [[Caloric Restriction]], or pharmacological agents ([[Resveratrol]] via SIRT1) improves metabolic health and extends healthspan in model organisms.

## Therapeutic Activation

PGC-1α can be activated by:
- Exercise mimetics ([[AICAR]], [[GW501516]])
- NAD+ precursors ([[Nicotinamide Riboside]], [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide]])
- Natural compounds ([[Resveratrol]], [[Urolithin A]])
- [[Metformin]] (via AMPK activation)
