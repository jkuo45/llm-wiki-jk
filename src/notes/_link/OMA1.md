# OMA1

**OMA1** is a conserved zinc-dependent metalloprotease anchored in the [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] inner membrane. Although present constitutively, its activity is normally restrained and is sharply upregulated in response to [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] — particularly loss of [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential]] (ΔΨm), perturbed protein import, or accumulation of unfolded proteins.

## Substrates & Functions

OMA1's best-characterized substrate is [[OPA1]], the dynamin-like GTPase that maintains cristae integrity and fusion. Stress-activated OMA1 cleaves the long (L-) isoform of OPA1 into short (S-) fragments, disabling fusion and promoting mitochondrial fragmentation, cristae remodeling, and eventual [[Mitophagy]]. A second key substrate is [[DELE1]]: OMA1 releases DELE1's cytosolic tail, which then engages the kinase HRI to trigger the [[Integrated Stress Response]] (ATF4/CHOP axis), linking mitochondrial distress to global translational reprogramming.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Through OPA1 processing, OMA1 sits at the intersection of mitochondrial dynamics, quality control, and cell-death priming. Excessive or prolonged OMA1 activation can collapse cristae, sensitize cells to [[Apoptosis]], and cooperate with the [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] mitophagy machinery. OMA1 thus helps execute [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPRmt]]-associated remodeling and is implicated in neurodegeneration and metabolic disease where mitochondrial turnover is perturbed.
