# STEAP3

**STEAP3** (six-transmembrane epithelial antigen of prostate 3), also known as **SIKE**, is an endosomal ferrireductase that reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ within the recycling endosome. This reduction is required before [[DMT1]] can transport iron into the cytosol, making STEAP3 an essential step in the transferrin-to-labile-iron-pool delivery chain that fuels [[Fenton Reaction]]-dependent [[Ferroptosis]].

## Structure & Biochemistry

STEAP3 is a six-transmembrane protein of the STEAP (six-transmembrane epithelial antigen of the prostate) family, possessing a cytosolic oxidoreductase domain with FAD/NADH-dependent ferrireductase activity. It localizes to the endosomal membrane, where it uses electrons from NADPH (via cytochrome b5 reductase) to reduce ferric to ferrous iron.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

The transferrin cycle brings Fe³⁺ into the endosome via [[Transferrin receptor 1]]. STEAP3 then reduces Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺; [[DMT1]] exports the Fe²⁺ into the cytosol, enlarging the labile iron pool. This iron drives the [[Fenton Reaction]], generating hydroxyl radicals that peroxidate PUFA-phospholipids; when [[GPX4]]/[[FSP1]] defenses fail, ferroptosis occurs (review "Role of iron metabolism in ferroptosis"). STEAP3 is thus an upstream iron-activation node of ferroptosis.

> [!info] Why reduction matters
> DMT1 transports Fe²⁺, not Fe³⁺. STEAP3's reductase activity is the gatekeeper converting transferrin-bound Fe³⁺ into the DMT1-permeable, Fenton-active form—directly coupling iron metabolism to ferroptotic execution.

## Physiological Function

STEAP3 is the major ferrireductase in erythroid and recycling iron pathways; it is highly expressed in prostate and hematopoietic tissues and supports red-cell iron utilization.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

STEAP3 deficiency causes iron-refractory anemia. In ferroptosis contexts, STEAP3-mediated iron reduction sensitizes cells; its inhibition could protect against iron-dependent injury (e.g., [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]], neuronal iron toxicity), while its promotion could sensitize iron-loaded tumors.
