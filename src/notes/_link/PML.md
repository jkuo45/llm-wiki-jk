# PML

**PML** (Promyelocytic Leukemia protein, also known as **TRIM19**) is a tripartite motif-containing protein and tumor-suppressor scaffold best known as the organizer of **PML nuclear bodies (PML-NBs or ND10)**, discrete nuclear foci 0.2–1.0 µm in diameter. PML-NBs are dynamic subnuclear macromolecular structures involved in a wide array of vital cellular processes, including [[DNA Damage|DNA damage]] response, [[Apoptosis]], and [[Senescence]]. They concentrate PML together with many partner proteins ([[p53]], [[DAXX]], [[RB1]], [[BRD4]], and others). PML is a tumor suppressor whose locus is disrupted in acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL) by the t(15;17) translocation generating the PML–RARA fusion oncoprotein, the target of arsenic trioxide and retinoic acid therapy.

## Structure and PML Nuclear Bodies

The PML gene encodes a RING-finger protein containing an N-terminal RING finger, two B-boxes, and a coiled-coil (the TRIpartite Motif, TRIM/RBCC domain), followed by a serine/proline-rich region. Through the coiled-coil domain, PML oligomerizes in a SUMOylation-dependent manner to seed nuclear bodies that recruit dozens of partner proteins — including [[p53]], [[SIRT1]], [[p33ING1b]], [[DAXX]], and [[SP100]] — and SUMOylated chromatin regulators. PML is itself SUMOylated, and this modification is essential for nuclear body maturation and for the sequestration and post-translational modification of client proteins within the bodies. PML-NBs are dynamic, multiprotein hubs involved in DNA repair, apoptosis, antiviral responses, and chromatin regulation; their number and size increase during senescence and in response to stress.

## Role in Senescence and SAHF

PML nuclear bodies are essential platforms for assembly of [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]]: the histone chaperone [[HIRA]] must translocate to PML bodies for SAHF to form, as described in the npj Aging review of the senescent epigenetic landscape (Dasgupta et al., 2024; https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-024-00172-2). Preventing HIRA translocation to PML-NBs inhibits SAHF assembly (Zhang et al., 2005, PMID 15680342), and disrupting PML bodies themselves likewise blocks SAHF (Ye et al., 2007, PMID 17339329). PML therefore sits genetically upstream of the HIRA/ASF1a/UBN1 heterochromatin program. PML also cooperates with [[HIRA]] and p62/[[SQSTM1]] to regulate inflammation during senescence (Dasgupta et al., 2024, Mol. Cell 84:3271–3287).

## The PML–SIRT1–p53 Axis

PML-NBs recruit specific proteins to regulate cellular aging and tumor suppression:

1. **Recruitment of p53 and SIRT1**: Upon oncogenic stress or upregulation of the specific PML isoform **PML-IV**, both **p53** and **SIRT1** are recruited into PML nuclear bodies.
2. **Regulation of Senescence**: Within these subnuclear bodies, **SIRT1** physically associates with PML and deacetylates **p53**. This deacetylation suppresses p53-mediated transactivation and pro-apoptotic signaling.
3. **Protection from Senescence**: By deacetylating p53, SIRT1 rescues primary mouse embryonic fibroblasts from PML-mediated premature cellular senescence and growth arrest, allowing cell survival under stress.

This axis illustrates a context-dependent role of [[SIRT1]]: within PML-NBs it restrains p53 to permit cell survival despite oncogenic stress, yet PML itself is a senescence and tumor-suppressor driver. The balance determines whether cells undergo arrest/senescence or continue cycling — a decision central to [[Aging]] and cancer.

## Mechanistic Details

PML-NBs concentrate the machinery (HIRA complex, histone-modifying enzymes) needed to deposit repressive chromatin at senescent loci. By acting as a phase-separated nuclear compartment, PML bodies concentrate factors that write [[H3K9me3]] and [[H3K27me3]] and promote chromosome-wide condensation into SAHF. The p53 and Rb tumor-suppressor pathways, both stabilized/enhanced at PML bodies, reinforce the senescence arrest.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

PML-NBs are stress-responsive hubs. In response to DNA damage, interferon, or hypoxia they expand and recruit repair and signaling factors. Loss of PML accelerates tumorigenesis and impairs senescence, while PML-IV overexpression is sufficient to induce senescence in a p53-dependent manner. The PML–SIRT1–p53 interplay is thus a molecular rheostat linking chromatin stress to longevity and tumor suppression, intersecting the broader [[Sirtuins]] network and [[NAD+]]-dependent regulation.

## Clinical and Therapeutic Relevance

PML is a classic tumor suppressor whose loss characterizes acute promyelocytic leukemia (targeted by arsenic trioxide). In aging, its role in SAHF/heterochromatin positions PML within the epigenetic control of [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] and senescence. Modulating PML-body function could inform [[Senomorphic|senomorphic]] strategies, albeit with care given its tumor-suppressive role (see [[KDM4]], [[BRD4]]).
