# Galangin

Galangin is a **flavonol**: 3,5,7-trihydroxy-2-phenyl-4H-chromen-4-one,
C15H10O5, molecular weight 270.24, CAS 548-83-4. It is the compound that
defines the class of propolis flavonoids in which it is the second most abundant
constituent after [[Chrysin]], and it is the flagship constituent of galangal
(*Alpinia officinarum* and *Alpinia galanga*) rhizome, a Zingiberaceae
spice used across Southeast Asian traditional medicine.

> [!info] Distinguishing galangin from its relatives
> Galangin is a *flavonol* with a 3-hydroxyl. [[Chrysin]] (5,7-dihydroxyflavone)
> lacks the 3-OH and is a *flavone*; [[Kaempferol]] and [[Quercetin]] are
> flavonols with B-ring or 3',4'-dihydroxy substitution respectively. Galangin is
> therefore the unsubstituted flavonol in this series, which is why its
> antioxidant and receptor pharmacology differ subtly from quercetin's even
> though they are routinely lumped together in the supplement literature.

## Occurrence and provenance

Galangin occurs in high concentration in the rhizomes of *Alpinia
officinarum* and *A. galanga* (lesser and greater galangal), in *Helichrysum
aureonitens*, and in propolis. In propolis it is one of the principal markers of
botanical origin: pinocembrin content marks poplar-type propolis, and the
**sum of chrysin plus galangin**, together with
[[Caffeic Acid Phenethyl Ester]], is a useful discriminator of geographic
provenance. South African propolis in particular is characterised by
pinocembrin, galangin, and chrysin.

Because propolis is collected by bees from plant resin, its galangin content is
a direct readout of local flora and season, which is the underlying reason it
works as a provenance marker.

## Pharmacology

**Aryl hydrocarbon receptor antagonism** is the most distinctive reported
mechanism. Galangin acts as an AhR antagonist, and since AhR is now understood
as an intracellular sensor integrating dietary, environmental, and endogenous
signals - not only a dioxin receptor - this places galangin at a genuine
signalling node rather than merely at a generic antioxidant sink. AhR
antagonism has been reported to induce apoptosis in cancer cells.

**Anti-inflammatory action** is reported through suppression of NF-kB
signalling in mice, with reduced adhesion molecule expression, and through
inhibition of phospholipase A2, which limits release of
[[Arachidonic acid|arachidonic acid]] and therefore of both
[[Cyclooxygenase|cyclooxygenase]] and [[Lipoxygenase|lipoxygenase]] products.
The [[COX-2]] and leukotriene arms are the ones most relevant to the
inflammation axis the vault cares about, and the inhibition of lipoxygenase
products is a plausible route to effects on membrane lipid peroxidation.

**Antioxidant and photoprotective activity**: galangin scavenges free radicals
and, in human keratinocytes, protects against UVB-induced oxidative damage to
DNA, lipids, and proteins, restoring mitochondrial polarisation and reducing
apoptotic protein expression. Note the caveat that this is largely chemical
scavenging capacity, and chemical antioxidant capacity in a cell assay
overestimates what the compound does at bioavailable concentrations in vivo.

**Insulin sensitivity**: in insulin-resistant HepG2 cells, galangin (with
[[Pinocembrin]], but not with chrysin or pinobanksin) increased glucose
consumption and glycogen content, raised hexokinase and pyruvate kinase
activity, and altered phosphorylation of the insulin receptor, Akt, GSK3beta, and
IRS. This is a cell-line finding reported in 2018 and has not been
independently reproduced in animal or human work.

**Autophagy and senescence**: galangin is described in supplier literature as
an autophagy-inducing flavonoid, and appears in senomorphic and
senescence-related compound lists. The evidence base for this is weaker than for
its AhR and NF-kB pharmacology, and it should be treated as preliminary.

**Antimicrobial**: in vitro antibacterial and antiviral activity, with
documented synergy between galangin, chrysin, and pinocembrin from South
African propolis - synergy that reduced cytotoxicity at equivalent
antimicrobial effect.

> [!warning] Clinical caveats
> - Almost all galangin pharmacology is cell-culture or rodent data. There are
>   no human interventional trials of galangin as a single agent, and no
>   approved indication.
> - **Bioavailability is the limiting problem.** Like most flavonols, galangin
>   is extensively glucuronidated and sulfated in the gut wall and liver; the
>   parent compound reaches the circulation in low concentrations as
>   conjugates. Doses in cell-culture experiments are typically in the
>   10-80 micromolar range, which is well above achievable free concentrations
>   from oral intake. This gap is the single biggest reason flavonol
>   preclinical results disappoint in humans.
> - **The AhR antagonism is a double-edged finding**: AhR is required for normal
>   immune function and mucosal barrier integrity, so chronic AhR blockade is
>   not obviously beneficial. Effects will be context-dependent.
> - Safety data are limited; interactions with CYP and UGT metabolising
>   enzymes, and with drugs with narrow therapeutic windows, are not
>   characterised.

## Connections

- [[Propolis]] — the source in which galangin is both abundant and
  mechanistically discussed; propolis is a mixture, so propolis-level effects
  cannot be attributed to galangin alone.
- [[Flavonoid]] and [[Flavonol]] — its class and subclass; the structural
  relationship to [[Chrysin]], [[Kaempferol]], and [[Quercetin]] determines
  which of their pharmacology transfers and which does not.
- [[Chrysin]] — the flavone congener and the other principal propolis
  flavonol marker; the two are used together for provenance analysis.
- [[Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor]] — galangin's most distinctive reported target
  and the reason it is not merely a generic antioxidant.
- [[Arachidonic acid]] and [[Cyclooxygenase]] — the phospholipase A2
  inhibition route that limits pro-inflammatory eicosanoid synthesis.
- [[Lipoxygenase]] — the second arm of arachidonic acid metabolism affected;
  relevant to the vault's lipid-peroxidation chemistry.
- [[NRF2]] and [[Keap1]] — the antioxidant-response axis that flavonols
  generally engage; galangin's ROS-scavenging is the direct effect, NRF2
  activation the indirect one.
- [[Inflammation]] — the aggregate endpoint of the NF-kB, phospholipase A2,
  and eicosanoid data.
- [[Insulin Resistance]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes]] — the context of the HepG2
  insulin-sensitisation data.
- [[Hepatocyte]] and [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] — the cell in which the
  insulin data were generated, and the tumour type in which galangin has been
  tested for endoplasmic-reticulum-stress-mediated apoptosis.
- [[Autophagy]] and [[Senescence]] — the preliminary, less well-supported axis.
- [[Bioavailability]] — the pharmacokinetic constraint that limits nearly every
  translational claim about this and related flavonols.
- [[Quercetin]] and [[Resveratrol]] — the comparison class for evaluating
  whether "flavonoid antioxidant" claims are worth writing down.
