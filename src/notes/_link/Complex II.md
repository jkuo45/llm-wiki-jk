# Complex II

Complex II, or succinate dehydrogenase (SDH), is the only enzyme that participates in both the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle and the [[Electron Transport Chain]]. Embedded in the inner [[Mitochondria]]l membrane, it oxidizes succinate to fumarate, transferring the extracted electrons through FAD to its iron–sulfur clusters and then to ubiquinone (coenzyme Q), reducing it to ubiquinol. Unlike [[Complex I]], [[Complex III]], and [[Complex IV]], Complex II does not pump protons and therefore contributes no directly coupled proton-motive force.

Structurally, SDH is a tetramer (SDHA–SDHD) with a hydrophilic catalytic domain facing the matrix and membrane-anchored subunits binding the heme b and ubiquinone site. Because it lacks proton pumping, Complex II cannot support [[Reverse Electron Transport]] on its own; rather, its product ubiquinol is the fuel that drives RET when the membrane is highly polarized. Mutations in SDH subunits are associated with paragangliomas, pheochromocytomas, and gastrointestinal stromal tumors, where accumulated succinate acts as an oncometabolite inhibiting [[Nrf2]]-regulating and demethylase enzymes.

> [!info] Succinate accumulation (e.g., ischemia) makes Complex II a key upstream driver of RET-derived mitochondrial [[ROS]].

Complex II thus links carbon metabolism, redox signaling, and [[Oxidative Stress]], and is a focal point for understanding metabolic–mitochondrial crosstalk in health and disease.
