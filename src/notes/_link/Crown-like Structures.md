# Crown-like Structures

## Overview

**Crown-like structures (CLS)** are characteristic histological formations observed in [[Adipose Tissue]] during [[Obesity]]. They consist of [[Macrophages]] that encircle dead or dying adipocytes, forming a "crown" appearance. CLS are a hallmark of adipose tissue [[Inflammation]] and are associated with metabolic dysfunction.

## Structure & Formation

### Morphology
- **Appearance:** Ring of macrophages surrounding a single adipocyte
- **Location:** Primarily in visceral and subcutaneous white adipose tissue
- **Composition:** Macrophages (predominantly M1 phenotype), occasional multinucleated giant cells

### Formation Process
1. **Adipocyte hypertrophy:** Enlarged adipocytes experience cellular stress
2. **Adipocyte death:** Hypertrophic adipocytes undergo necrosis or apoptosis
3. **Macrophage recruitment:** Chemokines attract monocytes/macrophages to dying adipocytes
4. **Phagocytosis:** Macrophages surround and attempt to clear lipid debris
5. **CLS formation:** Persistent macrophage accumulation forms crown-like structures

## Role in Adipose Tissue Inflammation

### Macrophage Polarization
- **M1 macrophages:** Pro-inflammatory phenotype predominates in CLS
- **Cytokine secretion:** TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and MCP-1 are produced
- **NF-κB activation:** Inflammatory signaling drives insulin resistance

### Inflammatory Cascade
- **Cytokine release:** Pro-inflammatory cytokines impair [[Insulin Signaling|insulin signaling]]
- **Chemokine production:** Recruits additional immune cells
- **Fibrotic response:** Contributes to [[ECM|extracellular matrix]] remodeling and fibrosis

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - The Sirtuin Network Linking NAD+ Metabolism, Mitochondrial Function, and Metabolic Homoeostasis]]
> Macrophages form characteristic crown‐like structures around dead or dying adipocytes and secrete pro‐inflammatory cytokines, including TNF‐α, IL‐1β, and IL‐6, which directly impair insulin signalling through activation of NF‐κB, JNK, and IKKβ pathways.

## Association with Metabolic Disease

### Obesity
- CLS frequency correlates with adiposity and BMI
- More abundant in visceral than subcutaneous adipose tissue
- Increased with adipocyte hypertrophy and adipose tissue expansion

### Insulin Resistance
- CLS density correlates with [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin resistance]]
- Pro-inflammatory cytokines from CLS impair insulin signaling
- Associated with systemic metabolic dysfunction

### Type 2 Diabetes
- CLS are more prevalent in diabetic vs. non-diabetic individuals
- Contribute to local and systemic inflammation
- Associated with worse glycemic control

## Role of Sirtuins

Sirtuins modulate CLS formation and adipose tissue inflammation:

- **[[SIRT1]]:** Suppresses macrophage infiltration; promotes anti-inflammatory M2 polarization; inhibits [[NF-κB]]
- **[[SIRT6]]:** Reduces inflammatory signaling; promotes M2 macrophage phenotype
- **[[SIRT3]]:** Loss activates [[NLRP3]] inflammasome; enhances adipose tissue inflammation
- **[[SIRT4]] and [[SIRT7]]:** May promote adipose tissue dysfunction and inflammation

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - The Sirtuin Network Linking NAD+ Metabolism, Mitochondrial Function, and Metabolic Homoeostasis]]
> SIRT1 exerts strong anti‐inflammatory effects by inhibiting NF‐κB signalling, reducing macrophage infiltration, and promoting polarisation toward an anti‐inflammatory M2 phenotype. SIRT1 and SIRT6 counteract chronic overnutrition-induced macrophage infiltration by suppressing inflammatory signalling pathways and promoting anti‐inflammatory macrophage polarisation.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Biomarker:** CLS density in adipose tissue biopsies indicates inflammatory status
- **Therapeutic target:** Reducing CLS formation may improve metabolic health
- **Interventions:**
  - Weight loss reduces CLS frequency
  - [[Caloric Restriction]] may attenuate CLS formation
  - Anti-inflammatory therapies may reduce CLS-associated inflammation
