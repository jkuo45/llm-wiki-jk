# Kaposi sarcoma

**Kaposi sarcoma** (KS) is an HHV-8-driven vascular tumour of endothelial and
spindle-cell origin. First described by Moritz Kaposi in 1872 as
"multiple idiopathic hemorrhagic sarcoma" in elderly Viennese men, it became
an AIDS-defining illness in the 1980s, and the causative virus was identified
in 1994.

> [!info] KS is a disease of failed immunity, not of excess immunity
> KS is the clearest cancer in which **loss** of cell-mediated surveillance
> causes the tumour. KSHV infection is near-universal in the populations at
> risk, but clinical KS requires both KSHV and immune suppression. Restoring
> immunity — HAART in HIV — frequently causes lesions to regress, sometimes
> without any anticancer drug. This makes KS a rare and instructive
> counterexample to the usual inflammation-promotes-cancer narrative, and it
> is why the vault's [[Inflammaging]] framing needs the qualifier: chronic
> inflammation promotes cancer, but the immune system's *failure* can promote a
> specific virus-driven cancer.

## Etiology: HHV-8 / KSHV

**Kaposi sarcoma-associated herpesvirus (KSHV, HHV-8)** is present in
essentially 100% of KS lesions across all epidemiological forms. KSHV is a
gammaherpesvirus with latent infection of B cells, endothelial cells, and
spindle cells. Latent genes expressed in the tumour include:

- **LANA** — the principal viral oncogene; maintains latent episome replication,
  binds p53 and Rb, and drives nuclear localisation of host transcription
  factors.
- **Kaposi's sarcoma-associated virus (K)6 / vFLIP** — constitutive NF-κB
  activation, which sustains the pro-survival programme and confers resistance
  to apoptosis and to TNF.
- **vGPCR** — a constitutively active G-protein-coupled receptor that drives
  [[Angiogenesis]] via JAK-STAT signalling; the target of mTOR inhibitors
  ([[Rapamycin]]) in KS.
- **vIL-6** and **vMIP** — viral cytokine analogues that recruit and polarise
  infiltrating cells.

KSHV also encodes a large set of **microRNAs and circular RNAs** that
downregulate host innate-immune sensors and NF-κB signalling, which is how the
virus achieves immune evasion despite a vigorous host response.

> [!warning] Uncertain territory
> Reports that KSHV infection raises non-Hodgkin lymphoma and, in some
> cohorts, [[Colon Cancer]] risk are contested. KS is the well-established
> KSHV-associated malignancy; the broader oncogenic role of KSHV is a live
> research question, not clinical fact.

## Clinical forms

| Form | Population | Behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Classic** | Older men of Mediterranean, Eastern European, and Middle Eastern ancestry | Slow, indolent, usually lower limbs |
| **Endemic (African)** | Sub-Saharan Africa; more aggressive lymphadenopathic form in children | Variable, aggressive in children |
| **Iatrogenic** | Solid-organ transplant recipients on immunosuppressants | Skin-predominant; may regress on immunosuppression reduction |
| **Epidemic (AIDS-related)** | [[HIV-1]]-positive patients, particularly with low CD4 count | Multifocal, aggressive, high mortality untreated |

## Presentation

- **Cutaneous** — painless, non-blanching red-purple-brown-black macules,
  patches, plaques, or nodules; classically on the lower limbs, but face,
  genitalia, and oral mucosa are common. Oedema or lymphoedema accompanies
  lesions when lymphatic drainage is obstructed.
- **Oral** — involved in roughly 30% of cases and the presenting site in
  about 15% of AIDS-related KS; hard palate first, then gingiva. Easily
  traumatised by chewing, so bleeding and secondary infection are common and
  eating may be impaired.
- **Gastrointestinal** — common in transplant- and AIDS-associated disease, and
  can occur without skin involvement. May cause bleeding, weight loss, pain,
  or obstruction.
- **Pulmonary** — the worst prognostic site; presents with dyspnoea, cough,
  haemoptysis, or chest pain. Diagnosis usually needs bronchoscopy with biopsy.

> [!info] Staging and diagnosis
> Diagnosis is by **biopsy**; extent is determined by clinical examination plus
> imaging. For AIDS-associated KS, CD4 count is the single best prognostic
  variable — survival falls sharply below 200 cells/µL.

## Management

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The cornerstone of treatment in HIV-associated KS is **antiretroviral
> therapy**, not cytotoxic chemotherapy. Immune reconstitution alone resolves
> lesions in a substantial fraction of patients. Chemotherapy (liposomal
> anthracyclines such as [[Doxorubicin]] or [[Mitoxantrone]]) is added for
> rapidly progressive or extensive disease. In the transplant setting,
> reduction or withdrawal of immunosuppression is the first move. Local
> therapy — surgical excision, intralesional chemotherapy, or
  [[Radiotherapy]] — suffices for limited cutaneous disease. No KS therapy
  reliably cures the underlying KSHV infection.

## Connections

- [[HIV-1]] — AIDS-associated KS is the epidemic form, and KSHV infection
  precedes it by years in HIV-positive cohorts. The mechanism is HIV-mediated
  destruction of the T-cell surveillance that normally contains KSHV; HAART
  is therefore both prevention and first-line treatment. The stub's sole
  inbound document link is retained in the Documents section.

- [[AIDS]] — KS was one of the original AIDS-defining illnesses and is
  classified as such. Its incidence dropped sharply after HAART, one of the
  clearest population-level demonstrations that immune restoration prevents
  cancer.

- [[Opportunistic infection]] — KS is grouped with opportunistic infections
  because it behaves like one: latent virus reactivated by immune suppression,
  clinically silent until immunity falls, and responsive to immune
  restoration.

- [[Angiogenesis]] — KS is a vascular tumour with spindle-cell morphology, and
  KSHV's vGPCR constitutively activates signalling that drives new vessel
  formation. This is the mechanistic basis for antiangiogenic and mTOR-directed
  therapy in KS.

- [[VEGF]] — Elevated in KS lesions and in KS-associated oedema, and the target
  of bevacizumab and of anti-angiogenic strategies tried in refractory disease.

- [[Rapamycin]] — Its mTOR inhibition directly targets the vGPCR-driven
  JAK-STAT-PI3K axis in KSHV-infected cells; sirolimus has been used with some
  success in refractory or transplant-associated KS. This links the vault's
  mTOR notes to a real clinical application.

- [[Lymphedema]] — Limb swelling in KS is lymphatic obstruction by tumour
  infiltrate, not inflammatory oedema alone. It is a common cause of
  disfigurement and functional loss in classic and endemic KS.

- [[Endothelial Cells]] — The cell of origin of the spindle component; KSHV
  infects and transforms endothelium, which is why KS is classified among
  vascular tumours.

- [[Checkpoint Inhibitor]] — Immune checkpoint blockade has been reported to
  produce responses in KS, which is mechanistically coherent given that the
  tumour arises from failed immune surveillance. The evidence base is
  case-series level, not trial-level.

- [[Inflammaging]] — KS is a useful counterexample: chronic inflammation drives
  most age-related cancer, but here the age-related decline in immune
  surveillance is the permissive condition. Any use of the inflammaging
  framework for KS should acknowledge the inversion.

- [[Metastasis]] — Untreated AIDS-associated KS disseminates widely to
  gastrointestinal and pulmonary sites; the disease is more notable for
  multifocal local spread than for haematogenous metastasis.
