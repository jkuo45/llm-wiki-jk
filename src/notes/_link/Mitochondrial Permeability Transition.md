# Mitochondrial Permeability Transition

## Overview

The mitochondrial permeability transition (MPT) is a sudden increase in inner and outer mitochondrial membrane permeability, with matrix swelling, cristae remodeling, [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] generation, [[NAD+]] depletion, ATP collapse, and necrotic cell death.

## Regulators and Inhibitors

The pore (see [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore]]) is sensitized by calcium overload and oxidative stress and regulated by [[Cyclophilin D]]. It is blocked by the CypD ligands [[Cyclosporine A]] and [[Sanglifehrin A]].

> [!warning]
> Whether MPT is an obligate downstream step of [[Necroptosis]] is controversial: [[RIPK3]]–[[MLKL]] signaling can kill without MPT, and MPT inhibitors often fail where [[Necrostatin-1]] or [[Necrosulfonamide]] succeed. The two necrosis programs overlap but are genetically separable.
