# Skin Longevity

**Skin longevity** is the preservation of skin structure, barrier function, and regenerative capacity during chronological aging and extrinsic stress. The concept frames skin aging as a set of modifiable biological hallmarks rather than a purely cosmetic outcome: mitochondrial dysfunction, disabled macroautophagy, chronic inflammation ([[Inflammaging]]), [[Cellular Senescence]], and degradation of the [[Extracellular Matrix|extracellular matrix (ECM)]].

## Biological Hallmarks in Skin

- **Mitochondrial dysfunction.** Most of the ATP supporting skin cell health derives from [[Mitochondrial Respiration|mitochondrial respiration]]. Age-related decline in mitochondrial quality and quantity reduces the energy available for [[Keratinocyte|epidermal differentiation]] and [[Collagen|collagen]] production.
- **Impaired [[Mitophagy]].** Declining selective clearance of depolarized mitochondria permits damaged organelles to persist, raising [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] and engaging inflammatory signaling.
- **[[Inflammaging]] and [[Cellular Senescence]].** Senescent [[Fibroblast|fibroblasts]] and keratinocytes accumulate and secrete a [[SASP]] that degrades ECM and propagates senescence to neighboring cells. [[Senomorphic]] interventions suppress this secretome without eliminating the cells.
- **ECM degradation.** Collagen production declines approximately 1% per year, rising to about 2% per year after menopause; UV exposure accelerates collagen breakdown through [[Matrix Metalloproteinases|matrix metalloproteinases]].

## Intrinsic and Extrinsic Aging

**Intrinsic aging** comprises the natural, genetic, and biological processes driving temporal decline in skin health. **Extrinsic aging** is imposed by UV exposure, pollution, and lifestyle factors. The two converge on the same hallmarks — mitochondrial decline, inflammation, senescence, and ECM loss — which is why dual-action interventions are sought.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Topical application of urolithin A activates mitophagy and enhances mitochondrial function to promote skin longevity|Topical urolithin A skin study]]
> Topical [[Urolithin A]] acts on multiple skin-longevity hallmarks at once: it increases epidermal TOMM20 and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]], raises PINK1/PARK2 and phospho-ubiquitin ([[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] mitophagy), reduces [[IL-6]] and [[CXCL1]], lowers [[p16]], increases [[Collagen|collagen III]], [[Hyaluronic Acid]], [[Elastin]] and collagen VII, and upregulates collagen-fibril-organization gene sets in human skin biopsies.

## Intervention Context

Conventional "anti-aging" skincare (retinoids, alpha-hydroxy acids, vitamin C, niacinamide, peptides, [[Hyaluronic Acid]]) is largely sign-corrective, addressing downstream manifestations rather than underlying drivers, and some actives provoke irritation that limits long-term use. Mitophagy-inducing postbiotics such as [[Urolithin A]] are investigated for acting on the underlying hallmarks; [[Creatine]] has been studied for [[Topical Application|topical]] energy buffering and collagen synthesis, though the trial evidence is formulation-confounded.

## Connections

- [[Mitophagy]] — Declining mitochondrial clearance is a core skin-aging hallmark; topical urolithin A raises PINK1/PARK2 and phospho-ubiquitin in skin models.
- [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] — Increased epidermal TOMM20 accompanies mitophagy activation.
- [[Inflammaging]] — Chronic low-grade skin inflammation is reduced by topical urolithin A (IL-6, chemokine gene sets down).
- [[Cellular Senescence]] — p16 and CXCL1 decline with senomorphic, not senolytic, activity.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — Collagen III/VII, elastin, and hyaluronic acid are the structural outputs preserved.
- [[UV-induced photoaging]] — Extrinsic arm of skin aging; topical urolithin A attenuated UVB erythema and PM10+UVA oxidative damage.
- [[Urolithin A]] — Postbiotic intervention acting on multiple hallmarks after topical delivery.
- [[Creatine]] — Energy-buffering intervention with separate, formulation-confounded topical evidence.
