# Lactylation

**Lactylation** is a recently discovered post-translational modification in which lactyl groups derived from the glycolytic end-product lactate are covalently attached to lysine residues (Kla). It was first identified on histones, where it functions as an epigenetic mark linking cellular lactate levels to transcription — expanding the classical view of lactate from waste product to signaling metabolite. Enzymes that deposit and erase lactylation are still being characterized, but the modification is established as a mediator of macrophage polarization, tumor metabolism, and tissue fibrosis, with writer/eraser crosstalk that includes sirtuin family deacylases.

## Lactylation in intervertebral disc degeneration

Emerging studies suggest that [[Lactate|lactate]] can downregulate [[SIRT3]], which in turn increases [[ACSL4]] lactylation, thereby promoting [[Ferroptosis|ferroptosis]] and accelerating the decline of [[Nucleus Pulposus|nucleus pulposus cell]] functionality[331]. In degenerated discs, the metabolic environment is hypoxic and lactate-rich; the proposed cascade is: lactate → SIRT3 downregulation → elevated ACSL4 lactylation → ACSL4-dependent lipid peroxidation → ferroptotic cell death of nucleus pulposus cells. Lactylation thereby connects disc metabolic dysregulation to SIRT3 loss and cell death in [[Intervertebral Disc Degeneration|IDD]].

## Clinical relevance

Because lactylation couples metabolic state to protein function, targeting the lactate–SIRT3–ACSL4 axis (e.g., by restoring SIRT3) may suppress ferroptotic disc degeneration. The modification exemplifies the emerging "acylome" layer of metabolic-epigenetic signaling in aging tissues.
