# Pentamidine

**Pentamidine** is a synthetic aromatic diamidine (the 1,5-bis(4-amidinophenoxy)pentane scaffold, the same aromatic diamidine backbone as diminazene aceturate and berenil) supplied as the **isethionate salt** for injection and as an aerosol for inhalation. It has been in continuous use since the 1960s, and its persistence is a genuine historical oddity: it is toxic enough that it is never first-line, yet it retains activity where better drugs have failed.

## Mechanism of Action

Pentamidine is a **multi-target** agent, and no single mechanism accounts for its breadth:

> [!info] Mechanism
> The most consistently supported actions are: (a) **collapse of the parasite's mitochondrial membrane potential**, with the diamine accumulating in the mitochondrion and disrupting kinetoplast and energy metabolism; (b) **interference with kinetoplast DNA topology** — inhibition of topoisomerase I/II and direct binding to kinetoplast DNA, which is why kinetoplast morphology is so characteristically altered; and (c) direct binding to, and aggregation of, DNA and RNA generally, which underlies its antiviral activity. The cationic amphiphilic character of the molecule is what drives all of this: the two amidine groups make it strongly basic, so it concentrates in acidic and DNA-rich compartments.

**Activity against *Pneumocystis* is limited to the non-replicating (trophozoite/cyst) form** — the drug is not effective against proliferating organisms. This is a genuine limitation on its prophylactic value, since it cannot sterilise a host.

## Clinical Uses

- **Treatment of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PJP)** — intravenous pentamidine is the alternative to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. It is a second-line agent precisely because the toxicity burden is higher than for TMP-SMX.
- **Aerosolised pentamidine for prophylaxis** — for patients who cannot tolerate TMP-SMX. The CDC explicitly warns that aerosolised pentamidine is **not** effective as adjunctive therapy for active PJP and must not be used in that way.
- **Visceral leishmaniasis** — historically the drug of choice; now largely displaced by liposomal amphotericin B, miltefosine, and paromomycin. Pentamidine retains a niche for patients unable to take those.
- **African trypanosomiasis** — a drug of last resort; it requires very high doses with correspondingly severe toxicity.
- **Anti-cancer interest** — preclinical work on pentamidine as a direct **NAD+/NADH-binding** molecule that promotes [[ADP-ribosylation|poly(ADP)-ribose]] polymer formation has led to its use as a tool compound for PARP-1 inhibition studies, and it has been trialled as an adjunct in [[Acute Myeloid Leukemia]]. This is investigational and has no established clinical role.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Pentamidine's toxicity is frequent, not rare, and the pattern is unusual enough that clinicians look for it specifically:
> - **Pancreatic β-cell injury** — the signature adverse effect. It causes both **hypoglycaemia** (early, from insulin release by injured β-cells) and **delayed, often irreversible type 2-like diabetes** (later, from β-cell destruction). Unlike streptozotocin, the effect is cumulative, can occur at prophylactic aerosol doses, and is not heralded by the usual serum indicators. Every course of pentamidine leaves behind a patient who may need lifelong hypoglycaemic therapy.
> - **Nephrotoxicity** and **pancreatitis** with raised amylase; **QT prolongation** and torsades; **hypotension**; **neutropenia**.
> - **Sterile abscesses and pain at the injection site** are common with the intramuscular formulation, which is why IV administration is preferred.
> - **Azotemia plus pentamidine accumulation is a feedback loop**: the drug clears renally, so renal impairment raises exposure, which worsens renal injury.

## Connections

- [[Leishmaniasis]] — pentamidine is the drug whose historical use defined the treatment of visceral leishmaniasis, and it remains a listed option where newer agents are unavailable. The mechanism relevant here is kinetoplast DNA targeting, since *Leishmania* is one of the kinetoplastid parasites.
- [[Leishmania]] — as a kinetoplastid, *Leishmania* is killed by the drug's interference with kinetoplast DNA topology and mitochondrial function; the damaged kinetoplast is visible microscopically in treated amastigotes.
- [[Paromomycin]] — the two drugs are alternative parenteral agents for visceral leishmaniasis, and combining them shortens the intensive treatment phase. Their mechanisms are unrelated: paromomycin is a mistranslation-inducing aminoglycoside, pentamidine a DNA/mitochondrial disruptor, which is why they are complementary rather than redundant.
- [[ADP-ribosylation]] — as a small-molecule NAD+-dependent PARP-1 inhibitor, pentamidine is a widely used biochemical probe, and it is the compound from which the PARP inhibitor pharmacophore was partly derived. Note that this is a distinct mechanism from its antimicrobial one.
- [[Acute Myeloid Leukemia]] — pentamidine's NAD+/PARP-1 activity has been exploited in AML as a differentiation- or apoptosis-inducing adjunct, and it is the parent compound of the related compound C-1305. Entirely investigational.
- [[Cell Membranes]] — as a permanently cationic diamidine, pentamidine concentrates in acidic and membrane-rich compartments. This physicochemical fact, not a target-specific binding event, is the root of both its antimicrobial activity and its host-cell toxicity.
- [[Amphotericin B]] — liposomal amphotericin B is the current first-line agent for visceral leishmaniasis and displaced pentamidine; the comparison is useful because amphotericin is itself a membrane-disrupting drug with a different toxicity profile (nephrotoxicity, infusion reactions).
