# Citric Acid Cycle

The **Citric Acid Cycle** (also called the tricarboxylic acid or TCA cycle, or Krebs cycle) is a series of enzyme-catalyzed reactions in the mitochondrial matrix that oxidize acetyl-CoA derived from carbohydrates, fatty acids, and amino acids. It generates the reduced electron carriers [[NADH]] and [[FADH2]] that fuel [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|OXPHOS]], and regenerates the oxidized cofactor [[NAD+]].

## Mechanism & Metabolic Role

- **NAD+/NADH production**: The cycle generates NADH at several steps (isocitrate dehydrogenase, α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, malate dehydrogenase) and FADH2 via succinate dehydrogenase. These reducing equivalents are the principal source of matrix NAD+ regeneration during respiration.
- **Link to senescence metabolism**: Mitochondria regulate cellular [[NAD+|NAD+]] levels by producing NADH during the citric acid cycle and oxidizing it back to NAD+ during OXPHOS. In senescent cells, reduced ETC efficiency and lowered NAD+ levels perturb this balance, decreasing the NAD+/NADH ratio.
- **Coupling to the malate–oxaloacetate node**: Within the cycle, [[Malate Dehydrogenase]] catalyzes the reversible conversion of [[Oxaloacetate]] to [[Malate]], interconverting NAD+ and NADH; this node also feeds the [[Malate-Aspartate Shuttle]] for cytosolic–mitochondrial redox coupling.

## Relevance to Senescence

- The drop in the NAD+/NADH ratio during senescence — driven partly by reduced cycle/ETC flux and by NAD+ consumption in repair pathways — reinforces the senescent phenotype (reduced glycolysis/ATP, elevated AMP/ATP activating [[AMPK]], cell-cycle arrest).
- Restoring the NAD+/NADH ratio (e.g., via [[NAMPT]] upregulation in OIS, or by [[Methylene blue|methylene blue]] redox cycling) modulates senescence and the [[SASP]], underscoring the cycle's upstream role in metabolic control of the senescence program.

## Connections

- [[NAD+]] — Regenerated during OXPHOS; its senescence-associated depletion is tied to cycle/ETC flux
- [[NADH]] — Reducing equivalent produced by the cycle
- [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] — Consumes cycle-derived NADH/FADH2 to regenerate NAD+
- [[Malate Dehydrogenase]] — Catalyzes the malate↔oxaloacetate step within the cycle
- [[Oxaloacetate]] — Cycle intermediate converted to malate
- [[Malate]] — Cycle intermediate produced from oxaloacetate
- [[Malate-Aspartate Shuttle]] — Links cycle intermediates to cytosolic redox
- [[AMPK]] — Downstream energy sensor activated by cycle-driven NAD+/ATP changes
