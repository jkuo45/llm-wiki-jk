# MET

**MET** is the human gene encoding **c-Met** (also c-MET, HGFR, MNNGHOS), the
receptor tyrosine kinase for [[Hepatocyte Growth Factor|HGF]]. MET was
originally cloned as a transforming oncogene from a carcinogen-treated
osteosarcoma cell line (MNNG-HOS), which is where the name comes from. It is a
**proto-oncogene**, and the HGF/MET axis is one of the most consistently
activated receptor tyrosine kinase pathways in human cancer.

## Protein structure

c-Met is a 1390 aa, single-pass type I transmembrane receptor:

- **Semaphorin (Sema) domain** (N-terminal, extracellular) — binds
  **SEMA3C** and other semaphorins, and **binds the integrin-binding domain
  of [[Stromelysin|MMP1]]/[[ADAM]] family proteases through
  **[[RON|MSPRY2]]-adjacent sites**, which regulates receptor
  shedding and activation.
- **PSI (plexin, semaphorin, integrin) domain** — required for receptor
  dimerisation.
- **IPT (immunoglobulin, plexin, transcription) repeats** — three
  extracellular Ig-like domains.
- **Juxtamembrane region** with a **CBL-binding** tyrosine (Tyr985-adjacent
  CBL site, CBL-β) that is the down-regulatory node.
- **Tyrosine kinase domain** — the catalytic domain, with the activation
  loop and the "DFG-in/out" switch.

A distinct **sheddase**, ADAM10, releases the extracellular domain; this
cleavage is required for full, efficient HGF-driven signalling, and
protease-independent activation by non-HGF ligands has been described.

## Activation

> [!info] HGF is secreted as an inactive single chain and activated proteolytically
> HGF is made as **pro-HGF (HGF/MNK1)**, a single-chain inactive
> precursor that requires processing at the **Arg** residue by
> **HGF-activator (HGFAC)**, a serine protease, and related proteases
> (matriptase, hepsin, plasma kallikrein), each in the extracellular
> matrix. Processed HGF is a heterodimer of an **α and β chain** held by a
> **disulfide bond**, and the β chain engages the Sema domain. HGF binding
> **dimerises the receptor**, activating the kinase.

Key pharmacological point: because HGF is the dominant and effectively
ligand, **HGF is secreted in an inactive form and requires
extracellular proteolysis to be presented to MET**. This "proteolytic
paradox" is why anti-HGF antibodies and anti-MET antibodies are not
interchangeable strategies, and why both are used in combination trials.

**Dimerisation → autophosphorylation of the C-terminal tyrosine (Tyr1230)
on each monomer**, which trans-autophosphorylates the activation loop
(Tyr1233, Tyr1234, Tyr1235). The C-terminal tail provides docking
sites for the SH2-domain proteins that build the signalling complex.

## Downstream signalling

- **GAB1 → [[RAS]]/[[RAF]]/[[MEK]]/[[ERK]] ([[MAPK]])** — proliferative
  and, through ERK, [[Cell Cycle]]-regulating output.
- **GAB1/[[PI3K]] → [[AKT]] → [[mTOR]]** — survival,
  growth, [[EMT|epithelial–mesenchymal transition]] and resistance to
  apoptosis.
- **GAB1/PLCγ → [[Calcium Ions|calcium]]/[[Protein Kinase C|PKC]]** —
  [[Cytoskeleton]] remodelling and motility.
- **STAT3** — direct and via [[JAK]]; links MET to transcriptional
  programmes including [[IL-6]]/[[gp130]]-axis crosstalk.
- **[[SRC]]** — cooperative with MET on cytoskeletal signalling.
- **CRMP2/CRMP1/CRMP4 and a [[TANKYRASE]]-linked [[HSP90β]] scaffold** —
  one documented mechanism of
  lapatinib resistance runs through a HGF/MET → CRMP2 → [[CRMP1]] →
  **SEMA3C** axis, which restores motility and confers resistance
  to HER2 blockade.
- **CBL** — binds the juxtamembrane phosphotyrosines, recruits
  [[CBL-B]]/CBL-β, which ubiquitylates MET for internalisation and
  degradation. Loss of CBL function is a common route to
  constitutive MET activation.

## Normal physiology

MET is expressed in epithelial cells and is required for **embryonic
development** — Met knockout mice die in utero with placental and liver
defects. It drives:

- **Liver regeneration** — the "HGF is the main driver of liver
  regeneration after partial hepatectomy" claim is well supported, and
  is mediated substantially by MET on hepatocytes.
- **Wound healing and tissue repair**, via [[Angiogenesis]] and
  [[Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition]] at the wound edge.
- **Cell motility and morphogenesis** — the "scatter factor" activity
  of HGF, the original observation.
- **Stem cell and progenitor biology** — MET is a marker and regulator
  of liver, lung and pancreatic progenitors.

## Cancer biology

> [!info] Four distinct activation mechanisms, with different natural histories
> MET oncogenesis is not one thing:
>
> 1. **MET amplification** — high-level focal amplification (e.g.
>    *MET* 7q31, co-amplified with [[ERBB2|HER2]] on 7q21 in gastric
>    cancer) drives receptor hyperactivation and, in lung cancer,
>    acquired resistance to EGFR TKIs. The *MET* copy-number gain is
>    a specific biomarker for resistance to [[EGFR]] inhibitors.
> 2. **MET exon 14 skipping** — a distinct class of MET kinase
>    domain mutations (loss of exon 14, which contains the CBL-binding
>    site, so degradation is impaired) produce a specific, drug-sensitive
>    tumour type across lung cancers — the basis of approved
>    MET exon 14 inhibitors (capmatinib, tepotinib, savolitinib).
> 3. **HGF autocrine/paracrine loops** — tumour or stromal HGF
>    overexpression activating MET on tumour cells; more relevant in
>    gastric and oesophageal cancers, and a candidate explanation for
>    primary resistance to MET inhibitors.
> 4. **Receptor engagement by other ligands** — [[RON]] activation
>    by [[MSPRY2]]/[[HGF]] and by [[CD44]], [[HGFR]] heterodimerisation,
>    and integrin-mediated [[CD44]] clustering.

**Tumour-type associations.** MET is amplified in gastric cancer
(frequently with [[HER2]]), in glioblastoma, in hepatocellular carcinoma,
and in a subset of [[Colorectal Cancer|colorectal]], oesophageal and
[[Breast Cancer|breast]] cancers; it is also recurrently activated in
[[NSCLC|non-small cell lung cancer]] as a resistance mechanism.
**c-Met is also overexpressed in glioblastoma and drives invasion via
the [[HSP90β]] chaperone axis.** [[Gastric Cancer]] is the tumour type
where MET is most consistently a co-driver alongside HER2, and where
MET amplification is the most established mechanism of resistance to
HER2-directed therapy — this is the vault's clearest [[MET]] ↔ [[HER2]]
link.

## Therapeutics

- **Type Ib MET inhibitors** (capmatinib, tepotinib, savolitinib) — approved
  in **MET exon 14 skipping** non-small cell lung cancer.
- **Type IIa inhibitors** (crizotinib, crizotinib-like) — approved in
  [[ALCL]] and in PDGFRA- or ROS1-rearranged tumours, with MET activity
  as a secondary rationale.
- **Type I / ATP-competitive selective inhibitors** (savolitinib,
  tepotinib) and dual **MET/VEGFR-2** inhibitors (capmatinib,
  [[Foretinib]], [[Tivantinib]], [[Glesatinib]]).
- **Oncolytic adenoviruses** engineered with **MET fibre capsid
  modifications** to target MET-expressing tumours — a distinct,
  biologically elegant approach.
- **HGF/MET bispecific antibodies** (onartuzumab, savolitinib plus
  immunotherapy) and **anti-HGF antibodies** (fitusiran, AMG 337) —
  the anti-HGF-vs-anti-MET distinction noted above.

> [!warning] Clinical caveats
> - **Primary resistance is common and mechanistically instructive.**
>   In [[Gastric Cancer]], most MET-amplified tumours *do not* respond to
>   MET inhibitors; the leading explanations are co-existing
> [[KRAS]]/[[EGFR]]/[[ERBB2]] driving mutations, activation of
> parallel bypass pathways, and the failure of pharmacokinetics and
> receptor degradation dynamics to shut down signalling durably.
>   Notably, type Ib MET inhibitors often cause only **transient**
>   tumour responses followed by outgrowth — the MET axis has a
>   remarkable capacity for adaptive reactivation.
> - **On-target toxicity**: MET inhibition causes **peripheral oedema,
>   hypoalbuminaemia, nausea, fatigue, and hepatotoxicity**; the oedema
>   is mechanistically expected (MET drives lymphatic and endothelial
>   permeability) and is dose-limiting in some trials.
> - **Resistance** to MET inhibitors develops by MET amplification,
>   MET kinase-domain mutations (e.g. G1197, L1198, Y1230), and
>   activation of [[STAT3]]/[[ERK]] bypass routes.
> - The [[HSP90β]] chaperone association is real in models but its
>   therapeutic tractability in patients is not established.

## Connections

- [[c-Met]] — the same entity; this note is the gene-level entry and
  [[c-Met]] the protein-level one.
- [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] — MET is recurrently activated in HCC,
  and HGF is a key driver of hepatocyte proliferation and
  regeneration; the MET link is the clearest physiological-carcinoma
  overlap in the vault.
- [[Gastric Cancer]] — MET amplification is the best-characterised
  MET-driven tumour, co-amplified with [[HER2]] on chromosome 7 and
  the dominant resistance mechanism to HER2 blockade.
- [[Glioblastoma]] — MET overexpression drives invasion in GBM
  through the HSP90-dependent axis; also relevant to the
  HGF-autocrine microenvironment.
- [[HSP90β]] — MET's chaperone association in the GBM invasion
  programme; a direct link between receptor tyrosine kinase signalling
  and the heat-shock response.
- [[HER2]] — Co-amplified with MET on 7q; MET amplification is the
  principal bypass-resistance route after HER2-directed therapy,
  which is the vault's strongest MET–oncology relation.
- [[EMT]] — MET → MAPK/PI3K → EMT is one of the most-replicated
  signalling chains linking a receptor tyrosine kinase to invasion.
- [[PI3K]] and [[AKT]] and [[mTOR]] and [[MAPK]] and [[MEK]] — the
  principal downstream arms, through GAB1.
- [[RAS]] and [[RAF]] and [[ERK]] — the MAPK arm, and the
  convergence point with other RTKs such as EGFR, which is why
  MET activation is a canonical EGFR-inhibitor resistance mechanism.
- [[c-Met]] — HGF/Met signalling in [[Liver Regeneration]] and
  [[Wound Healing]] is the physiological function that the oncogenic
  axis co-opts.
- [[Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition]] and [[Invasion]] — the
  phenotypic output that makes MET a motility receptor rather than
  merely a mitogen.
- [[Angiogenesis]] — HGF/MET is pro-angiogenic and interacts with the
  VEGF axis; the rationale for dual MET/VEGFR inhibitors.
- [[Stem Cells]] — MET marks and regulates progenitor populations in
  liver, lung and pancreas, linking the tumour-initiating cell
  concept to the developmental programme.
- [[Protease]] — the HGF-activator requirement for ligand maturation
  is the reason extracellular proteolysis is a pharmacological
  target in this pathway.
- [[Lymphedema]] — indirect but mechanistically real: MET-driven
  endothelial and lymphatic permeability is the same biology that
  underlies MET-inhibitor-induced peripheral oedema and
  HGF-driven lymphangiogenesis.
