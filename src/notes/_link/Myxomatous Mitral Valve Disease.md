# Myxomatous Mitral Valve Disease

## Overview

**Myxomatous mitral valve disease** (MMVD) is the most common age-dependent valve disease in dogs and a relevant model for human degenerative mitral valve pathology. Senescent activated valve interstitial cells (aVICs) in MMVD show **impaired autophagic flux** and immature autophagosomes. Restoring autophagy via mTOR inhibition reverses the senescent phenotype.

## Key Mechanism (Tang et al., 2025)

> [!important] p62-Selective Autophagy Degrades p16/p21
> In canine MMVD cells, SQSTM1/p62-mediated selective autophagy **directly degrades p16 and p21** (independent of the ubiquitin-proteasome system). This is a direct molecular bridge from mTOR inhibition → autophagy → CDKI clearance → less SASP.

- Senescent aVICs show impaired autophagic flux and immature autophagosomes
- mTOR-dependent autophagy induction ([[Rapamycin]], torin-1) attenuates senescence, lowers CDKN2A/p16 and CDKN1A/p21, reduces SASP
- ATG7/ATG3 overexpression restores flux and reverses senescent phenotype; ATG deficiency induces it
- TGF-β → PI3K/AKT/mTOR drives myofibroblast transition and senescence in canine MMVD VICs (Tang et al., 2023, Cell Prolif, PMID 36869852)

## Translational Relevance

- MMVD is the leading cause of heart failure in dogs, particularly small-breed dogs
- Autophagy restoration is **anti-senescent** in canine valve cells — not merely cytoprotective
- Prospective MMVD stage B1/B2 cohorts exist for studying autophagy/SASP biomarkers
- Circulating or valve p16/p21 protein burden may inversely correlate with p62-mediated selective autophagy capacity
