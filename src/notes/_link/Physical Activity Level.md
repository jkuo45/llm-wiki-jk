# Physical Activity Level

**Physical Activity Level (PAL)** is the dimensionless ratio of total daily [[Energy Expenditure|energy expenditure]] (DEE) to [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]] (RMR), i.e., DEE:RMR. Ignoring the minor contribution of the thermic effect of food, PAL expresses the multiple of resting metabolism at which an individual operates — effectively the energy expended on activity on top of rest.

## Reference Values

- **Contemporary Western adults**: average PAL ≈ 1·66 (_n_ 574 [[Doubly-Labelled Water|DLW]] measurements; Black _et al_. 1996). At this level ~60 % of daily energy is spent at rest and ~40 % on activity.
- **Free-living wild mammals**: PAL ranges 1·4–7·5, mean ≈ 2·6 (Speakman, 2000), but this includes thermoregulatory costs in addition to activity, so the term is used cautiously for animals; only ~32 % of their budget is spent at rest.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The low PAL of modern humans is highlighted as the central feature of the physical-activity side of the [[Obesity|obesity]] epidemic: a 70 kg human expends ~11·7 MJ/d versus an allometric prediction of ~18·7 MJ/d, and male Gambian farm workers achieve ~16·2 MJ/d at lower body weight.

## Applications

- Setting physical-activity guidelines: reaching PALs comparable to primitive societies requires ~90 min of vigorous activity daily; wild-animal levels require ~2·5 h daily.
- Interpreting exercise prescriptions: three 30-min vigorous sessions/week raise weekly expenditure by only ~5·3 % (4·35 MJ vs. 81·9 MJ/week), so PAL-raising benefits require behavioural and metabolic amplification.
