# Endothelial Dysfunction
**Endothelial dysfunction** is a systemic pathological state of the vascular endothelium characterized by reduced bioavailability of [[Nitric Oxide]], impaired vasodilation, increased [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] adhesion molecule expression, and a pro-thrombotic phenotype. It is an early hallmark of [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Cardiovascular Disease]].
## Mechanisms
- Reduced [[eNOS]] activity and [[Nitric Oxide]] production
- Increased [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] from [[NADPH Oxidase]] and [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] ROS
- [[Inflammation|Inflammatory]] cytokine activation ([[TNFα]], [[Interleukin 1β]])
- Impaired [[Insulin Signaling]]
## Clinical Assessment
Measured by flow-mediated dilation (FMD), [[Pulse Wave Velocity]], and circulating biomarkers ([[VCAM-1]], [[ICAM-1]], [[E-selectin]]).
## eNOS Uncoupling: The Central Molecular Mechanism
The primary driver of endothelial dysfunction is reduced bioavailable [[Nitric Oxide]] (NO), which occurs through multiple convergent mechanisms. The endothelial isoform of [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] (eNOS, NOS3) normally dimerizes to catalyze the NADPH-dependent oxidation of [[L-arginine]] to citrulline and NO, requiring the cofactors [[Tetrahydrobiopterin]] (BH4), FAD, and FMN. Under conditions of elevated [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], BH4 is oxidized to dihydrobiopterin (BH2) by [[Peroxynitrite]], a process termed "BH4 deficiency." In the absence of sufficient BH4, eNOS undergoes "uncoupling" — the enzyme's electron transfer becomes uncoupled from L-arginine oxidation, and the oxygenase domain instead reduces molecular oxygen to [[Superoxide Radicals]] rather than producing NO. This converts eNOS from a protective NO-generating enzyme into a pro-oxidant source of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], creating a feed-forward loop: superoxide from uncoupled eNOS rapidly reacts with any remaining NO (diffusion-limited rate constant of ~6.7 × 10^9 M^−1·s^−1) to form [[Peroxynitrite]], which further oxidizes BH4 and perpetuates uncoupling.
## Endothelial ROS Sources Beyond eNOS
While uncoupled eNOS is a major source, endothelial dysfunction involves a network of interconnected [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]-producing systems. [[NADPH Oxidase]] (particularly the NOX1 and NOX4 isoforms in endothelial cells) generates superoxide in response to [[Angiotensin II]], [[TNF-alpha]], and [[shear stress|mechanical shear stress]]. [[Xanthine Oxidase]], which is highly expressed in the [[Cardiovascular Disease|vascular endothelium]] and circulates bound to endothelial glycosaminoglycans, produces both [[Superoxide Radicals]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] during purine catabolism. [[Mitochondria|Mitochondrial]] electron transport chain complexes I and III also contribute significantly; in the dysfunctional endothelium, elevated [[mitochondrial membrane potential]] favors superoxide production at complex III (the Q-cycle), particularly when [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] levels are low and cytochrome c oxidase (Complex IV) is not NO-inhibited. The interplay between these sources is non-additive — [[Xanthine Oxidase|XO]]-derived superoxide activates ROS-sensitive signaling pathways that upregulate [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] expression, while [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]]-derived [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] can stabilize HIF-1α and induce [[Hypoxia|hypoxic]] gene programs that remodel endothelial function.
## Asymmetric Dimethylarginine (ADMA) and Endogenous NOS Inhibition
The endogenous NOS inhibitor [[ADMA|asymmetric dimethylarginine]] (ADMA) accumulates in [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] states because ROS directly inhibit the enzyme [[DDAH|dimethylarginine dimethylaminohydrolase]] (DDAH), which degrades ADMA. DDAH contains a critical cysteine residue (Cys-249) in its active site that is susceptible to [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidation]] and [[S-Nitrosylation|S-nitrosylation]], rendering the enzyme inactive. Elevated ADMA competitively inhibits eNOS, promoting uncoupling and exacerbating endothelial dysfunction. Plasma ADMA levels are a robust independent predictor of [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular events]] and all-cause mortality in patients with [[Atherosclerosis|coronary artery disease]], [[Diabetes Mellitus|type 2 diabetes]], and [[Renal Failure|chronic kidney disease]].
## Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Markers
Endothelial dysfunction is most commonly quantified non-invasively by [[Flow-Mediated Dilation]] (FMD) of the brachial artery, a technique where reactive hyperemia-induced shear stress triggers NO-mediated vasodilation that is measured by ultrasound. Reduced FMD (<5–7% dilation) is an independent predictor of future [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular events]]. Circulating biomarkers include elevated levels of [[von Willebrand Factor]] (vWF), [[sICAM-1|soluble ICAM-1]], [[sVCAM-1|soluble VCAM-1]] (both products of [[NF-κB]] activation), [[E-Selectin]], and [[ADMA|ADMA]]. In the microcirculation, [[reactive hyperemia index]] (RHI) measured by peripheral arterial tonometry (PAT) provides an alternative assessment of small-vessel endothelial function.
## SASP Milieu and Cellular Senescence as Drivers

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Senolytic Treatment With Fisetin Reverses Age‐Related Endothelial Dysfunction Partially Mediated by SASP Factor CXCL12|Mahoney et al. 2025]]
> The circulating [[SASP]] milieu was established as a causal driver of age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] through [[Senescence]] induction. [[Fisetin]] (a [[Senolytic|senolytic]]) improved endothelial function by eliminating senescent [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]] and reducing circulating [[CXCL12]].

Key mechanisms:
- **Cellular Senescence**: [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] are the most senescence-susceptible vascular cell type; aging increases senescent EC burden 10.7-fold
- **Circulating SASP milieu**: Plasma from old mice directly impaired [[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation|EDD]] (−17%), induced EC senescence (6.1-fold SA-β-Gal), reduced [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] (−24%), and increased mitochondrial superoxide (3.5-fold)
- **CXCL12** is the key SASP factor mediating these effects: CXCL12 add-back recapitulated dysfunction; CXCL12 inhibition with [[LIT-927]] rescued function
- **[[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EndoMT)**: The SASP and CXCL12 drive EC transdifferentiation (reduced [[CDH5]], [[PECAM1]]; induced [[TGFB1]], [[ACTA2]]), a novel mechanism of age-related endothelial dysfunction
- **[[Fisetin]] rescue**: Fisetin plasma restored EDD (+14%), reduced senescence (−86% SA-β-Gal), restored NO (+20%), and reduced mitochondrial ROS (−58%)

## Therapeutic Interventions
Restoring endothelial function requires simultaneously enhancing NO bioavailability and reducing ROS production. [[Melatonin|Statins]] (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) improve eNOS expression and activity while reducing [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] expression, independent of lipid-lowering (pleiotropic effects). [[Angiotensin II|Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors]] reduce [[Angiotensin II]]-mediated [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] activation. [[Tetrahydrobiopterin|BH4]] supplementation (sapropterin) has shown mixed results but supports eNOS recoupling. Lifestyle interventions including [[Exercise|aerobic exercise]], [[Caloric Restriction]], and [[Mediterranean Diet]] (rich in [[Flavonoids]] and [[Polyphenols]]) improve FMD through combined [[NRF2|Nrf2]] activation, [[Antioxidants|antioxidant]] enzyme induction, and reduced [[NADPH Oxidase|NOX]] expression. [[Metformin]] improves endothelial function in [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetic]] patients via [[AMPK]]-mediated eNOS phosphorylation at Ser-1177 and reduced [[Mitochondrial ROS|mitochondrial ROS]]. [[Senolytic|Senolytic therapies]] (e.g., [[Fisetin]]) represent a novel therapeutic strategy for age-related endothelial dysfunction by eliminating senescent [[Endothelial Cells|ECs]] and normalizing the circulating [[SASP]] milieu — specifically [[CXCL12]].
