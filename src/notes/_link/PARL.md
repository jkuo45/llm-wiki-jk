# PARL

## Overview

PARL (presenilin-associated rhomboid-like protein) is a mitochondrion-specific rhomboid intramembrane serine protease residing in the [[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane]]. It plays a critical role in mitochondrial quality control by continuously cleaving [[PINK1]] during its import into healthy mitochondria, thereby preventing PINK1 accumulation on the outer mitochondrial membrane.

## Mechanism of Action

PARL's primary function in mitophagy regulation involves the PINK1-Parkin pathway:

1. **Healthy mitochondria** — PINK1 is imported across the [[Outer Mitochondrial Membrane]] via the [[Translocase of the Outer Mitochondrial Membrane]] and processed by PARL in the inner membrane. The cleaved PINK1 is then degraded, preventing its accumulation.
2. **Damaged mitochondria** — When mitochondrial membrane potential collapses, PINK1 import is halted. Without PARL-mediated cleavage, PINK1 stabilizes on the OMM where it phosphorylates ubiquitin and [[Parkin]], initiating the [[Mitophagy]] cascade.

PARL acts together with the [[Mitochondrial Processing Peptidase]] to process PINK1's C-terminal transmembrane domain during routine import.

## Physiological Function

By gating PINK1-dependent mitophagy, PARL serves as a molecular sensor that distinguishes healthy from damaged mitochondria. This positions PARL as a critical determinant of the import-and-degrade versus stabilize-and-signal decision.

## Interactions with Other Proteases

PARL functions alongside other mitochondrial quality control proteases including [[OMA1]] and [[YME1L]], which regulate mitochondrial protein processing and degradation through complementary mechanisms.
