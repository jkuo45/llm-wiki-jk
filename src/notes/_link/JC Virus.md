# JC Virus

**JC virus** (JCV, human polyomavirus 2) is a small, non-enveloped,
circular double-stranded DNA virus of the *Polyomaviridae* family. It was
named in 1971 from the initials of **John Cunningham**, the patient in whose
brain ZuRhein, Chou, Silverman, and Rubinstein observed the virions that cause
[[PML]].

## Infection and latency

Infection is near-universal: **70–90%** of adults are seropositive, acquired
in childhood or adolescence. The portal of entry is thought to be the oropharynx
or the [[Intestine]], after which the virus establishes latency in:

- **Renal tubular epithelium**, where it replicates and is shed continuously in
  urine — the basis of its name as a urinary virus.
- **B cells and lymphoid tissue**, a reservoir relevant to haematological
  malignancy.
- Reported latent sites include chorionic villi and semen.

> [!info] How latent infection is maintained
> Persistence is maintained by host transcription factors binding the viral
> non-coding control region — chiefly the cellular transcription factor
> **Spi-B** (an EBF-family factor) plus NF-κB and c-Jun. PML-associated
> strains carry characteristic mutations/deletions in this control region, and
> the archetypal vs mutant regulatory sequence distinction is used
> diagnostically to separate JCV found in PML from background JCV.

## Pathogenesis

Reactivation is the disease mechanism. JCV crosses the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]]
and infects CNS cells when cell-mediated immunity falls below threshold. The
principal target is the **oligodendrocyte**, whose destruction causes
demyelination and progressive white-matter loss; [[Astrocytes]] are
co-infected, becoming enlarged, hyperchromatic, and bizarre — the
"giant cell" astrocytes of PML histology.

> [!warning] Why reactivation happens where it happens
> Reactivation is triggered by the loss of cell-mediated immune surveillance,
> not by viral strain-specific virulence. The same latent population causes
> disease in every setting where immunity drops: AIDS, natalizumab-treated
> multiple sclerosis, haematological malignancy, and organ transplantation.
> This means PML is a *sentinel* immune complication — its occurrence in any
> patient is evidence of impaired T-cell control.

## PML

**Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy** presents as subacute
progressive focal neurological deficits — hemiparesis, visual field defects,
cerebellar ataxia, cognitive change — with characteristic multifocal,
asymmetric white-matter lesions on MRI. It is usually fatal within months
without immune reconstitution. There is no specific antiviral; management is
reversal of immunosuppression where possible, with
[[Natalizumab (Tysabri)]] discontinuation as the paradigm case.

## Other JCV syndromes

PML is not the whole picture, and the newer syndromes are the more
mechanistically informative ones:

- **JCV granule cell neuronopathy (GCN)** — lytic infection of cerebellar
  granule cell neurons with sparing of Purkinje cells, causing severe
  cerebellar atrophy. Associated with a mutation in the **VP1** capsid gene.
- **JCV cortical pyramidal neuronopathy (CPN)** — infection of cortical
  pyramidal neurons and astrocytes causing encephalopathy. No VP1 mutation;
  instead a 143 bp deletion in the **agnogene** (yielding a 10-residue truncated
  peptide) is implicated, suggesting a distinct molecular determinant of
  neuronal tropism. Subcellular localisation of virions in nuclei, cytoplasm,
  and axons suggests **axonal transport** contributes to spread.
- **JCV aseptic meningitis (JCVM)** — JCV recovered as the sole CSF pathogen in
  a subset of aseptic meningitis cases, with archetype-like regulatory regions
  and no coding mutations.

The existence of three distinct CNS syndromes from one virus with different
genetic determinants is the key mechanistic lesson: **tropism is set by
specific viral coding determinants (VP1, agnogene), not by the host cell
alone.**

## Diagnosis

- **CSF JCV PCR** — the practical clinical test; sensitivity is roughly
  70–80%, and a negative result does not exclude PML.
- **JCV-specific IgG in serum** — indicates past exposure, not disease.
- **MRI** — multifocal non-enhancing (or faintly enhancing) white-matter
  lesions, classically parieto-occipital in the corticospinal tracts.
- **Brain biopsy** — historically the diagnostic gold standard; largely
  displaced by PCR plus imaging.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> JCV is also a factor in [[Colon Cancer]] research, where JCV sequences have
  been reported in malignant colonic tumours. This association remains
  controversial and has not reached the evidentiary status of the PML link;
  it is noted here as an open question rather than an established fact.

## Connections

- [[PML]] — The defining disease of JCV, produced by lytic infection of
  oligodendrocytes in the immunosuppressed brain. Every PML case in a
  pharmacologically immunosuppressed patient is, mechanistically, a JC virus
  case.

- [[Natalizumab (Tysabri)]] — Anti-α4-integrin monoclonal antibody used in
  [[Multiple Sclerosis]]. It blocks lymphocyte trafficking across the
  blood-brain barrier, which is simultaneously the mechanism that stops MS
  lesions forming and the mechanism that allows JC virus to reach and destroy
  the CNS. PML is its signature risk, and it is the clearest illustration that
  an immune-trafficking drug and an antiviral-reativation drug are two sides of
  the same mechanism. The stub's inbound document link is retained in the
  Documents section.

- [[Oligodendrocytes]] — The main lytic target in PML. Killing them strips
  [[Myelin]] from the CNS, which is why PML is a white-matter disease rather
  than a grey-matter one.

- [[Myelin]] — Myelin loss is the structural readout of PML; MRI white-matter
  lesions and clinical progression track myelin destruction directly.

- [[Astrocytes]] — Secondarily infected and transformed into the giant bizarre
  astrocytes that are the histological signature of PML. The astrocyte change
  is partly viral cytopathic effect and partly a bizarre reactive response.

- [[VLA-4]] — The α4β1 integrin targeted by natalizumab. The same molecule is
  the point of pharmacological intervention in the disease context, which is
  why the JCV/PML literature is inseparably tied to integrin biology.

- [[Integrin]] — Natalizumab blocks α4 integrin, not VLA-4 alone; the integrin
  family is the pharmacological lever and the immunological explanation for
  JCV reactivation risk.

- [[Multiple Sclerosis]] — The disease for which natalizumab is standard
  therapy and the patient population in which PML surveillance is routine.
  PML risk scales with treatment duration, prior immunosuppressant exposure,
  and positive JCV serum antibody status.

- [[Neuroinflammation]] — PML is a demyelinating infectious process rather
  than a primary inflammatory one, but it is usually classified within
  neuroinflammatory disease for clinical and trial purposes, which is one
  reason the boundary is worth stating explicitly.
