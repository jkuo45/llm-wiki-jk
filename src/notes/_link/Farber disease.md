# Farber disease

**Farber disease** (FD; OMIM #228000) is a rare autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorder caused by loss-of-function mutations in [[ASAH1]], resulting in <5% residual [[Acid ceramidase]] activity.

## Clinical Features

- **Classic triad:** Painful joint deformities, subcutaneous lipomatous nodules, vocal hoarseness
- **Neurological involvement:** Delayed mental development, seizures, muscle weakness
- **Prognosis:** Often fatal by 2–3 years of age
- **Pathology:** Ceramide accumulation in lysosomes of macrophages, joints, skin, and CNS

## Genetics

- Biallelic mutations in [[ASAH1]] with <200 reported cases (Yu et al., 2018)
- Most mutations destabilize the protein fold rather than directly impairing catalysis
- Genotype-phenotype correlation: severity inversely correlates with residual ACase activity

## Relationship to SMA-PME

[[Spinal muscular atrophy with progressive myoclonic epilepsy|SMA-PME]] is an allelic disorder caused by hypomorphic ASAH1 mutations with higher residual activity (~5–30%), presenting with motor neuron disease and epilepsy without the joint/skin findings of Farber disease.

## Connections

- [[ASAH1]] — The gene mutated in Farber disease
- [[Acid ceramidase]] — The enzyme deficient in Farber disease; <5% residual activity
- [[Ceramide]] — Accumulates in lysosomes due to ACase deficiency
- [[Spinal muscular atrophy with progressive myoclonic epilepsy|SMA-PME]] — Allelic disorder with milder mutations in the same gene
