# Sulfiredoxin

Sulfiredoxin (SRXN1) is a small ATP-dependent repair enzyme that reverses the over-oxidation of typical 2-Cys peroxiredoxins, most notably [[Peroxiredoxin 3]] in [[Mitochondria]]. Peroxiredoxins are unusually efficient peroxide scavengers but are themselves inactivated when their catalytic cysteine is oxidized beyond sulfenic acid to sulfinic acid (Cys-SO₂H) under strong oxidative stress. Sulfiredoxin uses ATP and thioredoxin to catalyze sulfinic acid reduction back to the sulfenic/sulfhydryl state, thereby recycling the peroxiredoxin and restoring peroxide-detoxifying capacity.

This repair cycle is critical because it allows the peroxiredoxin system to recover after oxidative bursts rather than being permanently disabled. By sustaining [[Peroxiredoxin 3]] function, sulfiredoxin preserves mitochondrial [[ROS]] buffering and protects against cumulative [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Apoptosis]]-inducing damage.

> [!tip] Sulfiredoxin expression is itself induced by oxidative stress through [[Nrf2]], forming a feed-forward repair loop that reinforces antioxidant defense.

Sulfiredoxin is conserved from yeast to humans and has been implicated in cytoprotection during ischemia–reperfusion, in cancer cell survival under oxidative pressure, and in aging, where declining repair of oxidized proteins contributes to redox collapse. It thus represents a key node linking reversible cysteine oxidation to mitochondrial resilience and longevity.
