# Transferrin

**Transferrin (TF)** is the principal iron-binding glycoprotein of the blood and extracellular fluid. It binds two Fe³⁺ ions and delivers them to cells via receptor-mediated endocytosis through the [[Transferrin receptor 1]] (TFR1) complex. Because ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of cell death, the availability of transferrin-bound iron directly governs the labile iron pool that drives [[Fenton Reaction]]-mediated [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and [[Ferroptosis]].

## Structure & Biochemistry

Transferrin is a ~80 kDa bilobed glycoprotein, each lobe (N- and C-terminal) with a high-affinity Fe³⁺-binding site requiring bicarbonate as a synergist. Its iron-loaded form (holo-transferrin) docks to TFR1 at the cell surface. Iron release occurs in the acidic endosome, after which apotransferrin and TFR1 recycle to the plasma membrane.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

Extracellular Fe³⁺ (oxidized from Fe²⁺ by [[Ceruloplasmin]]) binds transferrin, forming the TF–Fe³⁺ complex, which is internalized via TFR1. In endosomes Fe³⁺ is reduced to Fe²⁺ by [[STEAP3]] and transported into the cytosol by [[DMT1]], entering the labile iron pool (LIP). This redox-active iron feeds the [[Fenton Reaction]], generating hydroxyl radicals that peroxidate PUFA-phospholipids; when antioxidant defenses ([[GPX4]], [[FSP1]]) are overwhelmed, ferroptosis ensues (review "Role of iron metabolism in ferroptosis").

> [!info] Iron as the ferroptosis engine
> Transferrin-dependent iron delivery is upstream of the lethal chemistry: more LIP iron → more Fenton radicals → more lipid peroxide propagation. Iron chelation (e.g., [[Deferoxamine]]) or TFR1 silencing blocks erastin-induced ferroptosis.

## Physiological Function

Transferrin maintains iron solubility and controls the distribution of iron to erythroid precursors, hepatocytes, and dividing cells; it also exerts antimicrobial "nutritional immunity" by sequestering free iron.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Dysregulated transferrin/TFR1 iron delivery underlies ferroptosis in cancer (e.g., [[Ovarian Cancer]] with elevated TFR1/iron retention) and neurodegeneration. Therapies modulating transferrin iron saturation or TFR1 are being explored to tune ferroptotic vulnerability.
