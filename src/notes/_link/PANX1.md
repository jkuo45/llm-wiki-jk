# PANX1

## Overview

[[PANX1]] (pannexin-1) is a plasma-membrane channel that forms large-pore conduits permeable to ions, ATP, and other small molecules. Best known for its role in ATP release and inflammasome-associated [[Pyroptosis|pyroptotic]] cell death, PANX1 also functions as a gate for releasing the cGAS-STING second messenger [[cGAMP]] into the extracellular milieu.

## PANX1 in Intercellular cGAMP Release

Unlike [[Gap Junction|gap junctions]], which require direct cell–cell contact, PANX1 enables contact-independent dissemination of cGAS-STING activation. Upon cGAS-STING stimulation, PANX1 channels open to export [[cGAMP]], allowing the second messenger to diffuse to distant cells and engage their [[STING]]–[[IRF3]] pathway, thereby propagating NF-κB–associated inflammatory signals beyond the initial activation site. Pharmacological or genetic inhibition of PANX1 limits this spread, underscoring its role as a non-cell-autonomous amplifier of [[Neuroinflammation]].

## Broader Significance

By converting intracellular cGAMP into an extracellular alarm signal, PANX1 bridges localized DNA sensing to systemic innate responses. Its inhibition is being explored as a means to curtail pathological cGAS-STING dissemination in neurodegeneration without broadly suppressing the pathway in every cell.
