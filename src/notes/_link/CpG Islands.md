# CpG Islands

**CpG islands** are genomic regions (typically promoter-associated) with a high frequency of CG dinucleotides and a high observed/expected CpG ratio. In mammalian genomes they are normally unmethylated at active promoters. Critically for innate immunity, **mitochondrial DNA ([[mtDNA]]) also contains unmethylated CpG islands** that resemble bacterial DNA.

## Mechanism & Immune Recognition

- **TLR9 sensing**: Endosomal [[TLR9]] (toll-like receptor 9) recognizes unmethylated CpG motifs. Because mtDNA retains these motifs, released mtDNA is recognized as a pathogen-associated molecular pattern, initiating a signaling cascade that activates [[p38 MAPK]] and [[NF-κB]], driving inflammation.
- **cGAS-STING parallel**: Cytosolic mtDNA is also detected by [[cGAS]], producing [[cGAMP]] and activating [[STING]] to induce [[Type I Interferon|type I interferons]] and the [[SASP]].

## Role in Senescence

- In senescent cells, low-grade [[Apoptosis|apoptotic]] stress causes minority mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization ([[miMOMP]]), releasing mtDNA into the cytosol. The unmethylated CpG islands in this mtDNA trigger TLR9/cGAS-STING signaling, becoming a key **DAMP** that drives the pro-inflammatory SASP.
- Circulating mtDNA (with CpG motifs) rises with age and correlates with inflammatory cytokines, linking CpG-driven innate activation to inflammaging.
