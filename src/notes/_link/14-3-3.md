# 14-3-3

14-3-3 proteins are a family of highly conserved acidic regulatory molecules found across all eukaryotic lineages, first identified in 1967 based on their migration position (14th fraction on gel No. 3-3) during bovine brain extract fractionation. In humans, seven genes encode distinct isoforms — YWHAB (β), YWHAG (γ), YWHAE (ε), YWHAZ (ζ), YWHAH (η), YWHAQ (θ/τ), and SFN (σ) — each sharing >70% amino acid identity. These proteins function as phosphoserine/phosphothreonine-binding adapters that recognize the consensus motifs RXXpS/TXP or modes 1 and 2 binding motifs on >200 known partner proteins. By forming constitutive homo- and heterodimers, 14-3-3 proteins create a flexible scaffold that sequesters, translocates, or allosterically modulates client proteins, making them central hubs in signal transduction networks governing [[Apoptosis]], [[Cell Cycle]], [[MAPK signaling]], [[Insulin signaling]], [[Autophagy]], and the [[DNA damage response]]. Dysregulation of 14-3-3 activity is implicated in multiple pathologies, including [[Cancer]], [[Alzheimer's Disease]], and [[Cardiovascular Disease]].

## Structure & Domains

Each 14-3-3 monomer is a ~30 kDa protein composed of nine antiparallel α-helices (α1–α9), with helices α3, α5, α7, and α9 forming a deep, conserved amphipathic groove that constitutes the phosphopeptide-binding site. The groove is lined with conserved basic and polar residues (notably Lys49, Arg56, Arg127, and Asn173 in the ζ isoform) that coordinate the phosphate group via electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonds. The isoforms differ primarily in the loops connecting these helices, allowing for subtle selectivity in binding partner recognition despite high structural conservation.

14-3-3 proteins form tight homodimers and heterodimers with dissociation constants in the low nanomolar range. The dimer interface is formed by helix α1 and α2 of each monomer, creating a large, continuous binding surface across the dimer. This dimeric arrangement is functionally critical: monomeric 14-3-3 is essentially inactive because the two binding grooves on the dimer are required to simultaneously engage phosphorylated motifs on either a single client protein (bivalent binding) or two distinct clients (bridging). Post-translational modifications, including [[Phosphorylation]], acetylation, and oxidation, regulate 14-3-3 activity. For example, phosphorylation of YWHAZ at Ser58 disrupts dimerization, while [[SUMOylation]] of YWHAZ promotes its cytoplasmic retention.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

14-3-3 proteins exert their regulatory effects through three principal mechanisms. **Scaffolding** brings two interacting proteins into proximity, as when 14-3-3 bridges [[Raf-1]] with [[MEK]], enhancing MAPK pathway activation. **Sequestration** removes a protein from its site of action — the classic example being 14-3-3 binding to phosphorylated [[Cdc25]] phosphatases in the cytoplasm, preventing premature cell cycle entry. **Allosteric modulation** induces conformational changes in the bound partner, altering its enzymatic activity or protein-protein interaction surface, as seen when 14-3-3 binding to [[BAD]] masks its BH3 domain, preventing [[BAX]] activation and inhibiting [[Apoptosis]].

In the MAPK cascade, 14-3-3 binds phosphorylated [[Raf-1]] at [[Ser259]], maintaining Raf-1 in an autoinhibited conformation until growth factor signaling displaces 14-3-3 and permits membrane recruitment. In [[Autophagy]], 14-3-3 competes with [[TFEB]] for cytoplasmic sequestration; under nutrient-rich conditions, [[mTORC1]] phosphorylates TFEB, enabling 14-3-3 binding and cytoplasmic retention, thereby suppressing lysosomal biogenesis and autophagy-related gene transcription. Under starvation, TFEB dephosphorylation releases it from 14-3-3, allowing nuclear translocation and transcriptional activation of autophagy genes. In the [[Insulin signaling]] pathway, 14-3-3 binds phosphorylated [[IRS-1]], modulating downstream [[PI3K]]/[[AKT]] signaling and influencing [[Glucose metabolism]]. In the [[DNA damage response]], 14-3-3 interacts with phosphorylated [[Chk1]] and [[p53]], coordinating cell cycle arrest with repair. 14-3-3θ (YWHAQ) can also interact with [[BCL2]] family members, linking mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization to survival signaling.

## Physiological Function

In the nervous system, 14-3-3 proteins are among the most abundant soluble proteins in the [[Brain]], representing approximately 1% of total soluble brain protein. They regulate [[Synaptic plasticity]] by modulating the trafficking and activity of neurotransmitter receptors, including [[NMDA receptor]] and [[AMPA receptor]] subunits. 14-3-3η (YWHAH) modulates [[Dopamine]] [[Receptor]] trafficking in [[Dopaminergic neurons]], and is implicated in [[Dopamine]] signaling in the [[Prefrontal Cortex]]. In the cardiovascular system, 14-3-3ζ regulates [[Cardiac hypertrophy]] signaling through [[Raf-1]] and [[MEK]]/[[ERK1_2|ERK1/2]] pathway modulation. In hepatocytes, 14-3-3 proteins regulate [[Insulin]] sensitivity through interactions with [[IRS-1]] and downstream [[PI3K]]/[[AKT]] nodes, influencing whole-body [[Glucose metabolism]] and [[Lipid metabolism]].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

**Cancer:** The epithelial-specific isoform 14-3-3σ (SFN) is a well-characterized [[Tumor suppressor]] downstream of [[p53]]. 14-3-3σ binds and sequesters [[Cdc25C]] in the cytoplasm, enforcing G2/M cell cycle arrest following [[DNA damage]]. Epigenetic silencing of 14-3-3σ promoter via [[DNA methylation]] is frequently observed in [[Breast cancer]], [[Lung cancer]], and other epithelial malignancies, abolishing this checkpoint and promoting genomic instability. Conversely, overexpression of other isoforms (14-3-3ζ, 14-3-3ε) correlates with poor prognosis in multiple cancers, where they enhance [[Raf-1]] signaling, inhibit [[Apoptosis]], and promote [[Chemotherapy resistance]].

**Neurodegenerative Diseases:** In [[Alzheimer's Disease]], 14-3-3 proteins interact with [[Hyperphosphorylated Tau]], a hallmark pathological feature. 14-3-3ζ binds to phosphorylated Tau at the same motifs recognized by [[GSK3beta]] and [[CDK5]], potentially stabilizing pathological Tau conformations and promoting [[Neurofibrillary tangles]] formation. Elevated 14-3-3 levels in cerebrospinal fluid serve as a biomarker for neuronal damage and are associated with rapid cognitive decline. In [[Parkinson's Disease]], 14-3-3η has been found to interact with [[Alpha-synuclein]], modulating its aggregation propensity.

**Cardiovascular Disease:** Dysregulation of 14-3-3ζ in cardiomyocytes contributes to pathological [[Cardiac hypertrophy]] and [[Heart failure]] through persistent [[Raf-1]]/[[MEK]]/[[ERK1_2|ERK1/2]] activation. 14-3-3σ loss in vascular smooth muscle cells promotes [[Atherosclerosis]] by enhancing [[Apoptosis]] resistance and proliferative signaling.

**Metabolic Disorders:** Reduced 14-3-3ζ expression in [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] liver tissue impairs [[Insulin]] signaling, contributing to hepatic [[Glucose metabolism]] dysregulation and [[Insulin resistance]].

## SIRT1–14-3-3ζ–Caspase-2 metabolic axis

> [!important] Metabolic gating of caspase-2 by SIRT1
> [[SIRT1]] deacetylates **14-3-3ζ** (lysine 49), maintaining its phosphorylation-dependent binding to [[Caspase-2]] and shielding caspase-2 Ser135 (phosphorylated by [[CaMKII]]) from dephosphorylation. When pentose-phosphate-pathway flux falls, 14-3-3ζ becomes acetylated and **dissociates**, allowing caspase-2 activation and death (Andersen et al., *Mol Cell* 2011 — [[_document_ - A Biotin Switch-Based Proteomics Approach Identifies 14-3-3zeta as a Target of Sirt1 in the Metabolic Regulation of Caspase-2]]).

- **High PPP flux / G6P** → SIRT1 activity ↑ → 14-3-3ζ deacetylated → caspase-2 suppressed (survival).
- **Low PPP flux** → 14-3-3ζ acetylated → released from caspase-2 → caspase-2 activated (death).
- In breast tumor cell lines (cytoplasmic SIRT1), SIRT1 inhibition rapidly dissociates caspase-2 from 14-3-3ζ and markedly enhances **paclitaxel** sensitivity.
- This makes the 14-3-3ζ–caspase-2 pair a central node of the [[Sirtuin-Caspase Crosstalk]].
