# Annexin A1

**Annexin A1 (ANXA1)** is a calcium- and phospholipid-binding protein of the annexin family, historically identified as lipocortin 1, a glucocorticoid-induced mediator of anti-inflammatory action. ANXA1 is expressed by neutrophils, monocytes, and epithelial cells, and acts through formyl peptide receptors (FPR1/FPR2) to limit leukocyte extravasation, promote resolution of inflammation, and drive efferocytosis. Its N-terminal peptides, including the tripeptide mimetic **Ac2-26** (derived from the first 26 N-terminal residues), recapitulate many of its anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving activities.

## SIRT3 upregulation by Ac2-26

The annexin A1-derived tripeptide Ac2-26 was reported to transcriptionally upregulate [[SIRT3]], concurrently reducing oxidative damage while promoting [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] and [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]][390]. In models of ischemic kidney injury, Ac2-26 treatment increases SIRT3 levels and augments mitochondrial function to limit injury (Suliman et al., Front Physiol 2021; doi: 10.3389/fphys.2021.683098)[390], linking pro-resolving inflammation biology to mitochondrial quality control.

## Clinical relevance

ANXA1-based therapies (Ac2-26, full-length protein, or FPR agonists) are under investigation for ischemia-reperfusion injury, sepsis, and inflammatory disease. The SIRT3-dependent mitochondrial component of Ac2-26's action broadens its therapeutic rationale beyond immune modulation to direct cytoprotection.
