# Antioxidant

**Antioxidants** are substances that inhibit or delay [[Oxidative Stress]] by neutralizing or scavenging highly reactive molecules, most notably **[[Reactive Oxygen Species]]** (ROS) and free radicals. Oxidation is a fundamental chemical process involving the loss of electrons from a molecule; while necessary for many biological functions, excessive oxidation — driven by the overproduction of ROS such as the [[Hydroxyl Radicals|hydroxyl radical]], superoxide, and hydrogen peroxide — can lead to significant cellular damage manifesting as lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage. In biological systems, antioxidants are therefore essential for maintaining [[Redox Balance]], protecting cells, tissues, and organs from oxidative processes, and preserving [[HDL]] and [[LDL]] function in contexts such as [[Atherosclerosis]].

Antioxidants work by donating electrons to reactive species, thereby neutralizing them without becoming highly reactive themselves. They are broadly categorized into **endogenous antioxidants**, which are synthesized by the body, and **exogenous antioxidants**, which must be obtained through diet or supplementation.

## Types

- **Endogenous**: [[Glutathione]], [[Superoxide Dismutase]], [[Catalase]], [[PON1]]
- **Dietary**: [[Vitamin E]], [[Vitamin C]], polyphenols, flavonoids
- **Pharmacological**: [[Resveratrol]], BHT, synthetic compounds

### Endogenous Antioxidant Systems

The body employs a multi-layered, sophisticated network of enzymatic and non-enzymatic antioxidants to manage ROS levels:

- **Enzymatic Antioxidants**:
  - **[[Superoxide Dismutase]] (SOD)**: Catalyzes the dismutation of the superoxide radical into oxygen and hydrogen peroxide, serving as the first line of defense against superoxide.
  - **[[Catalase]]**: An enzyme primarily located in peroxisomes that rapidly converts hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen.
  - **Glutathione Peroxidase (GPx)**: Uses reduced glutathione to reduce hydrogen peroxide and organic hydroperoxides to water and alcohols.
- **Non-Enzymatic Antioxidants**:
  - **[[Glutathione]] (GSH)**: A tripeptide that serves as a major intracellular antioxidant. It can directly scavenge ROS or act as a substrate for enzymes like GPx. Its redox state (GSH/GSSG ratio) is a critical indicator of cellular oxidative stress.
  - Other molecules like uric acid and certain vitamins also maintain the redox environment.

### Exogenous Antioxidants

These are substances acquired from external sources that significantly augment the body's endogenous defenses:

- **Vitamins**:
  - **Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid)**: A highly effective water-soluble antioxidant that scavenges various ROS and regenerates other antioxidants like vitamin E.
  - **Vitamin E (Tocopherol)**: A crucial lipid-soluble antioxidant that protects cell membranes from lipid peroxidation by scavenging radicals within the lipid bilayer.
- **Dietary Polyphenols**: Many plant-derived compounds, such as flavonoids, exhibit potent antioxidant properties and modulate cellular redox signaling.

## Mechanisms of Action

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Resveratrol and vitamin E exhibit equivalent DPPH free radical scavenging activity at identical concentrations. Both act rapidly — over 50% of activity observed in the first 30 minutes. Resveratrol protects LDL and HDL against copper- and radical-induced oxidation.

- **Free radical scavenging**: Neutralizing reactive species (measured by [[DPPH]] assay)
- **Chain-breaking**: Preventing propagation of lipid peroxidation
- **Metal chelation**: Binding transition metal ions (e.g., resveratrol chelates copper)
- **Enzymatic**: [[PON1]] hydrolyzes lipid peroxides on HDL

## Role in Atherosclerosis

- Oxidation of [[LDL]] produces [[Oxidized LDL]], which drives [[Foam Cells]] formation
- Oxidation of [[HDL]] impairs its [[Cholesterol Efflux]] capacity
- Antioxidants protect both LDL and HDL from oxidative modification
- [[Resveratrol]] preserves vitamin E content in LDL during oxidative stress

## Clinical Relevance

The balance between pro-oxidants and antioxidants is central to several pathological states:

- **Aging and Longevity**: The "Free Radical Theory of Aging" posits that the accumulation of oxidative damage over time is a primary driver of the aging process. Maintaining robust antioxidant defenses is a key area of longevity research.
- **Neurodegenerative Diseases**: The brain is particularly susceptible to oxidative stress due to its high oxygen consumption and lipid content. Deficiencies in antioxidant capacity are strongly linked to the pathogenesis of diseases like [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and Parkinson's disease.
- **Cancer**: The role of antioxidants in cancer is complex — they can protect healthy cells from DNA damage, but may also protect cancer cells from oxidative-stress-induced apoptosis that immunity or chemotherapy attempts to trigger.
- **Cardiovascular Disease**: Oxidative stress plays a critical role in atherosclerosis, where the oxidation of LDL cholesterol is a key step in plaque formation.
