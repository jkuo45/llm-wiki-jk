# Antioxidant

**Antioxidants** are substances that inhibit or delay [[Oxidative Stress]] by neutralizing reactive oxygen species and free radicals. In the context of [[Atherosclerosis]], antioxidants protect lipoproteins from [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and preserve HDL function.

## Types

- **Endogenous**: Glutathione, superoxide dismutase, catalase, [[PON1]]
- **Dietary**: [[Vitamin E]], [[Vitamin C]], polyphenols, flavonoids
- **Pharmacological**: [[Resveratrol]], BHT, synthetic compounds

## Mechanisms

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Resveratrol and vitamin E exhibit equivalent DPPH free radical scavenging activity at identical concentrations. Both act rapidly — over 50% of activity observed in the first 30 minutes. Resveratrol protects LDL and HDL against copper- and radical-induced oxidation.

- **Free radical scavenging**: Neutralizing reactive species (measured by [[DPPH]] assay)
- **Chain-breaking**: Preventing propagation of lipid peroxidation
- **Metal chelation**: Binding transition metal ions (e.g., resveratrol chelates copper)
- **Enzymatic**: [[PON1]] hydrolyzes lipid peroxides on HDL

## Role in Atherosclerosis

- Oxidation of [[LDL]] produces [[Oxidized LDL]], which drives [[Foam Cells]] formation
- Oxidation of [[HDL]] impairs its [[Cholesterol Efflux]] capacity
- Antioxidants protect both LDL and HDL from oxidative modification
- [[Resveratrol]] preserves vitamin E content in LDL during oxidative stress
