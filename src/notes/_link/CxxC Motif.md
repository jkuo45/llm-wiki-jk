# CxxC Motif

The **CxxC motif** (**Cys-X-X-Cys**) is a short redox-active sequence — two cysteine residues separated by two variable amino acids — that forms the catalytic core of the thioredoxin-fold superfamily of thiol oxidoreductases, including [[Thioredoxin]]/[[Thioredoxin-1]], [[Glutaredoxin]], protein disulfide isomerases, and the bacterial oxidoreductase DsbA. It catalyzes **dithiol-disulfide exchange**: the N-terminal cysteine acts as a nucleophile that attacks substrate disulfide bonds, the second cysteine resolves the transient mixed disulfide, and the oxidized (disulfide) motif is regenerated by the cell's reducing systems. The CxxC redox state is therefore a direct readout of cellular redox status.

> [!important]
> In [[Thioredoxin-1]] the motif is **Cys32–Cys35** (Cys-Gly-Pro-Cys, CGPC): nucleophilic [[Cysteine|Cys32]] attacks the substrate, resolving [[Cysteine|Cys35]] completes the cycle, and reduced Trx1 is restored by [[Thioredoxin reductase]] + NADPH. The same chemistry is why [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]], [[Peroxiredoxin|peroxiredoxins]], and methionine sulfoxide reductases depend on the thioredoxin system.

## Sequence Context & Variants

- **Consensus**: Cys-Xaa-Xaa-Cys; the two variable residues tune the motif's redox potential and substrate selectivity.
- **Thioredoxins**: CGPC (e.g., Trx1), moderate potential (≈ −270 mV), handles protein disulfide reduction.
- **Glutaredoxins**: CPYC, GSH-coupled disulfide reduction and deglutathionylation.
- **Protein disulfide isomerase (PDI)**: CGHC in its redox-active domains — a more oxidizing motif that forms/isomerizes disulfides during protein folding in the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]].
- **Bacterial DsbA**: CPHC, the most oxidizing family member.
- **Important distinction**: not every CxxC sequence is redox-active. **CxxC-type zinc-binding modules** (e.g., the CFP1/MLL1 CxxC DNA-binding domains that recognize unmethylated CpG) coordinate zinc and function in chromatin/DNA recognition rather than thiol-disulfide exchange.

## Catalytic Cycle

1. **Nucleophilic attack**: the N-terminal cysteine, with a depressed pKa (~6.3 in Trx vs ~8.3 for free [[Cysteine]]), exists as a thiolate at physiological pH and attacks the substrate disulfide, forming a transient mixed disulfide and releasing one reduced substrate thiol.
2. **Resolution**: the resolving cysteine attacks the mixed disulfide, releasing the fully reduced substrate and leaving the two motif cysteines as an intramolecular disulfide (oxidized enzyme).
3. **Regeneration**: the oxidized motif is recycled — thioredoxins by [[Thioredoxin reductase]] (NADPH), glutaredoxins by reduced [[Glutathione]] (via [[Glutathione Reductase]]), and PDI by the ER oxidative machinery. Net effect: electrons flow from NADPH or GSH through the CxxC to protein disulfide substrates.

## Physiological Significance

- **Canonical clients**: [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]] (dNTP supply for DNA replication/repair), [[Peroxiredoxin|peroxiredoxins]] (peroxide clearance), methionine sulfoxide reductases, and redox-sensitive transcription factors such as [[NF-κB]] and [[ASK1]]-regulating interactions.
- **Redox signaling**: the CxxC oxidation state switches client proteins between reduced (active) and oxidized (inactive/chaperone-like) states, coupling oxidant tone to cell fate decisions (proliferation vs [[Apoptosis]]/senescence).
- **Pathology**: oxidative over-oxidation of the nucleophile (e.g., sulfinylation) disables the antioxidant enzyme, drives protein oxidation, and contributes to [[Oxidative Stress]]-linked pathology in heart failure, neurodegeneration, and cancer.
