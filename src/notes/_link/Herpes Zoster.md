# Herpes Zoster

**Herpes zoster** (commonly called **shingles**) is a painful, blistering rash that develops when latent **[[Varicella Zoster Virus]]** — the virus that causes chickenpox — reactivates in a sensory ganglion and travels down the nerve to the skin. Anyone who has had chickenpox carries the latent virus and can develop herpes zoster.

## Epidemiology and risk

- Risk rises significantly after age 50 and is higher in immunocompromised individuals.
- Vaccination (the [[Shingles Vaccine|shingles vaccine]], typically recommended for older adults) reduces the likelihood of herpes zoster and lowers the risk of [[Postherpetic Neuralgia|postherpetic neuralgia]] (persistent nerve pain after the rash clears).

> [!info] Inflammaging link
> Recurrent viral reactivation is a source of chronic antigenic and inflammatory stress. A large U.S. cohort study found [[Shingles Vaccine|shingles vaccination]] associated with lower chronic [[Inflammation|inflammation]] and slower [[Biological Aging|biological aging]] / [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]], suggesting vaccination may dampen background inflammation by preventing reactivation.
