# UGA codon

**Overview:** In the standard genetic code the **[[UGA codon]]** is a **stop (termination) signal**. It is uniquely repurposed in **selenoprotein** synthesis, where — in the presence of a 3′UTR **SECIS element** — the ribosome reinterprets UGA as encoding **[[Selenocysteine]]** (the 21st amino acid) instead of terminating translation. This process is called **UGA recoding** or stop-codon reassignment.

## Recoding machinery

- The decision between termination and Sec insertion depends on the **SECIS element** (a 3′UTR stem-loop), bound by **[[SECISBP2]]** (SBP2), which recruits the Sec-specific elongation factor **eEFSec** and tRNA[Ser]Sec.
- Efficiency is modulated post-transcriptionally: the **Um34** modification of tRNA[Ser]Sec, installed by **[[FTSJ1]]**, is required for high-fidelity recoding of a subset of selenoproteins.
- Loss of Um34 causes **ribosomal stalling** at UGA and reduced Sec insertion (Nease et al., 2024).

> [!info]
> Source: [[Selenocysteine tRNA methylation promotes oxidative stress resistance in melanoma metastasis]]
> Um34 regulates UGA recoding efficiency in a subset of selenoproteins involved in oxidative stress; without it, ribosomes stall at the recoded UGA codon.

## Biological significance

- UGA recoding is the sole route to [[Selenocysteine]] incorporation, underlying the entire [[Selenoprotein]] family (GPx, TrxR, deiodinases).
- Because recoding competes with termination, selenoprotein expression is exquisitely sensitive to the cell's translational and redox state, as well as to [[Selenium]] availability.
