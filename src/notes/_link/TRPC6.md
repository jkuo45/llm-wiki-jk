# TRPC6

**TRPC6** is a Ca2+-permeable transient receptor potential channel of the TRPC (TRP-classic) family. In human [[Erythrocytes]] it is a route of Ca2+ entry, and Ca2+ overload through such channels is the initiating event of [[Eryptosis]].

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> TRPC channels are Ca2+-permeable and were long the leading suspects for the eryptosis Ca2+ entry route. TRPC3 mediates Ca2+ influx in erythrocytic progenitors, TRPC6 is expressed in those progenitors and performs Ca2+ entry in human erythrocytes, while mouse erythrocytes rely on TRPC4/5 instead — a species caveat for any TRPC-based target. Whether these channels actually deliver the Ca2+ that drives eryptosis remains unresolved, alongside [[Piezo1]], [[NMDA receptor|glutamate receptors]], and Cav2.1.

## Mechanism and relevance

- Nonselective cation channel activated by DAG/PLC signaling and mechanical/oxidative stimuli; in RBCs the channels rest mostly closed and open on ROS, hyperosmolarity, or Cl− removal with gluconate replacement — all eryptosis-relevant stimuli.
- Ca2+ entry through TRPC (or Piezo1) feeds the same downstream executors: [[Gardos Channel]] K+ loss (shrinkage), [[Scramblase]] activation ([[Phosphatidylserine]] exposure), [[Calpain]] (blebbing).
- Elsewhere in the body TRPC6 is a druggable target in podocyte and vascular disease, which matters if systemic TRPC6 blockade is ever proposed to control eryptosis.
