# Dapansutrile

**Dapansutrile** (OLT1177) is an oral, selective small-molecule inhibitor of the [[NLRP3]] inflammasome. It is being developed for inflammatory conditions in which NLRP3-driven [[IL-1β]] and pyroptotic signalling contribute to tissue injury.

## Mechanism

Dapansutrile binds the NLRP3 NACHT domain and inhibits NLRP3 ATPase activity, preventing the conformational changes and oligomerization required for inflammasome assembly. By acting on the activation (second-signal) step rather than on priming, it blocks [[Caspase-1]] activation, maturation of [[IL-1β]] and [[IL-18]], and [[Gasdermin D|gasdermin D]] cleavage that would otherwise drive [[Pyroptosis]].

## Clinical Evidence

An open-label, dose-adaptive phase 2a trial of dapansutrile in acute [[Gout|gout flares]] provided preliminary clinical evidence of target engagement and tolerability. The uncontrolled design and short treatment period limit conclusions about efficacy and long-term safety, and responses in acute inflammatory disease cannot establish benefit against chronic [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]].

## Therapeutic Context

Dapansutrile represents the class of selective NLRP3 inhibitors (succeeding [[MCC950]]) and is relevant to conditions in which mitochondrial dysfunction supplies the second signal — [[Mitochondrial ROS|ROS]], [[Cardiolipin|cardiolipin]], and [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] failure — making NLRP3 inhibition a candidate strategy for age-related sterile inflammation.
