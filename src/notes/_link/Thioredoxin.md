# Thioredoxin

**Thioredoxin (Trx)** is an evolutionarily conserved family of small redox-active proteins that serve as key antioxidants and cell-signaling regulators. All family members share a characteristic active-site motif (Cys-Gly-Pro-Cys, "CxxC") through which they reduce oxidized cysteine residues — including disulfide bonds and other thiol modifications — on target proteins via thiol-disulfide exchange, becoming oxidized themselves in the process. Trx proteins are recycled to the active reduced state by [[Thioredoxin reductase]] in an NADPH-consuming cycle, and together with the [[Glutathione]] system constitute the bulk of cellular antioxidant capacity.

## Isoforms & Family Members

Mammals express two principal thioredoxin isoforms with distinct subcellular compartments:

- **[[Thioredoxin-1]] (Trx1, TXN)** — the cytosolic (and, on stress, nuclear/secreted) isoform. Regulates [[ASK1]], [[NF-κB]], [[HIF-1α]], and [[APE1]]/Ref-1; regenerates [[Peroxiredoxin|peroxiredoxins]] and [[Methionine sulfoxide reductases]] in the cytosol; is among the few antioxidants whose systemic overexpression extends mouse lifespan and protects the heart.
- **[[Thioredoxin-2]] (Trx2, TXN2)** — the mitochondrial isoform. Maintains mitochondrial thiol-redox balance, supports respiration, regenerates [[Peroxiredoxin 3]], and inhibits the mitochondria-located pool of [[ASK1]].

Related, structurally or functionally connected family members in the vault include:

- [[Thioredoxin reductase]] (TXNRD1/2) — the NADPH-dependent recycling enzymes for both isoforms.
- [[TXNIP]] — thioredoxin-interacting protein, a redox-sensitive negative regulator that sequesters/reduces Trx1 and links oxidative stress to [[NLRP3]] inflammasome activation.
- [[TXNDC5]] — a thioredoxin-domain chaperone involved in inflammatory signaling.
- [[Peroxiredoxin]] and [[Peroxiredoxin 3]] — the primary redox clients of Trx1 and Trx2, respectively.

## Mechanism & Redox Cycle

The catalytic cycle operates through the conserved CxxC motif ([[Cysteine|Cys32]]/[[Cysteine|Cys35]] in Trx1):

1. The N-terminal active-site cysteine ([[Cysteine|Cys32]]) attacks a substrate disulfide, forming a transient mixed disulfide.
2. The resolving cysteine ([[Cysteine|Cys35]]) resolves the mixed disulfide, releasing the reduced substrate and leaving Trx oxidized as an intramolecular disulfide.
3. [[Thioredoxin reductase]] + NADPH regenerates reduced Trx.

Because activity depends on the reduced state of the active site, oxidative and nitrative stress — particularly **nitration** of Trx tyrosines — can irreversibly inactivate the enzyme, a modification implicated in aging and ischemia–reperfusion injury.

## Physiological Roles

Beyond generic antioxidant defense, Trx proteins act as:

- **Redox signaling hubs**: Trx1 and Trx2 gate the activity of stress kinases ([[ASK1]]) and transcription factors ([[NF-κB]], [[HIF-1α]], [[APE1]]/Ref-1, AP-1), converting ROS levels into cellular decisions about survival, inflammation, and apoptosis.
- **Electron donors for repair enzymes**: Ribonucleotide reductase, [[Methionine sulfoxide reductases]], and peroxiredoxins all receive electrons from Trx.
- **Secreted/cytokine-like mediators**: Trx1 is secreted and can modulate redox signaling in the extracellular space, and serves as a serum biomarker in cardiac and inflammatory disease.
- **Cardioprotective and metabolic regulators**: Trx1 maintains [[mTOR]] (Cys1483) in its active reduced state and thereby supports cardiac mitochondrial gene expression and function.

## Longevity & Stress Resistance

Thioredoxin-1 is among the **few antioxidant enzymes whose systemic overexpression prolongs mouse lifespan** — chiefly by improving oxidative-stress resistance and reducing oxidative damage during the earlier portion of life ([[Thioredoxin-1#Longevity & Stress Resistance|see Trx1 note]]). In the sirtuin literature this places Trx1 alongside mitochondrial catalase as a canonical "longevity factor" whose stimulation is proposed as a strategy to increase cardiac resistance to pathologic insults ([[SIRT1]], heart failure context).

## Clinical & Pathological Relevance

- **Heart failure / ischemia–reperfusion**: Trx1 levels fall in advanced heart failure; overexpression is cardioprotective in young animals but is lost in middle age via nitrative inactivation.
- **Cancer**: Tumors up-regulate the Trx system for redox fitness and apoptosis resistance — the basis for investigating Trx/TXNRD inhibitors (e.g., [[Auranofin]]) as anticancer drugs.
- **Aging**: Age-dependent nitrative inactivation of Trx contributes to declining stress resistance.
