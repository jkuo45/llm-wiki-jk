# Prothrombin

**Prothrombin** is a 621-residue, single-chain, vitamin K-dependent plasma
glycoprotein (gene *F2*) that circulates as an inactive zymogen. It is the substrate of
the last step of the coagulation cascade and the source of [[Thrombin]], the enzyme
that converts soluble [[Fibrinogen]] to insoluble fibrin and that also activates
platelet PAR1 and PAR4 receptors. It is a normal plasma protein, not a protease: it
has no catalytic activity until it is cleaved.

## Domain architecture

Prothrombin is built from four functional modules, and the architecture is the reason
it can only be activated on a membrane:

- **Fragment 1 (residues 1–36)** — the **Gla domain**, containing seven
  γ-carboxyglutamate residues generated by vitamin K-dependent carboxylation. These
  chelate Ca²⁺ and bind phosphatidylserine, which is what anchors prothrombin to the
  activated platelet or endothelial surface. Without the Gla domain the molecule
  cannot participate in the reaction it exists for.
- **Fragment 2 (residues 37–432)** — the kringle-containing activation peptide.
- **The A2 domain** — the central body, which binds factor Va and acts as the
  docking platform for factor Xa's protease domain.
- **The B domain** — contributes shape and stability; it is lost on activation.

## Activation by the prothrombinase complex

Prothrombin is activated by **factor Xa with factor Va as cofactor, in the presence of
Ca²⁺ and anionic phospholipid** — the prothrombinase complex. Factor Va is not
optional: it accelerates the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin by roughly
300,000-fold, and factor Xa alone cleaves small peptidyl substrates readily but
prothrombin very poorly. The membrane and the cofactor act together; neither is
sufficient on its own.

Activation requires two cleavages, and their order determines the product:

- **Arg320 first** gives **meizothrombin** — an intermediate that is *already
  catalytically active* because the new N-terminus forms a salt bridge and inserts
  into an N-terminal binding cleft, reorganising the activation domains. Cleavage at
  Arg271 then releases mature two-chain [[Thrombin]] and fragment 1.2.
- **Arg271 first** gives **prethrombin 2**, an inactive intermediate, which then
  requires a second, much slower cleavage at Arg320.

The meizothrombin path is the physiologically preferred one: the prothrombinase
complex and synthetic membranes favour it, and it matters functionally because it
provides catalytic activity before full thrombin exists — an amplification step.

Cryo-EM work in 2022 resolved the prothrombinase complex on lipid nanodiscs at 4.1 Å,
showing the Gla domains of factor Xa and prothrombin aligned on the membrane plane
with the C1 and C2 domains of factor Va, the protease domains curving out of that
plane to contact the A2 domain. The A2 domain is what orients Arg320 toward the
factor Xa active site, which is the structural explanation for meizothrombin
formation.

## Kinetics of clot formation

Thrombin generation is not linear. It has a **lag phase** (the prothrombinase complex
must assemble), a **transition phase** (enough factor V is activated for explosive
prothrombin conversion), and a **propagation phase** (thrombin accelerates its own
production by activating factor V, factor VIII and the platelet surface). This
thresholds-to-explosion shape is what the laboratory thrombin generation assay
measures, and it is why a small procoagulant push can produce a very large thrombin
response.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Too little thrombin generation is the root cause of the haemophilias; too much is
> thrombosis. Prothrombin is also the point at which the fibrinolytic system opposes
> coagulation — [[Plasminogen]] activation is limited by clot-bound thrombin-activatable
> fibrinolysis inhibitor, and the ratio of clot-bound to free plasminogen generation is
> what determines whether a thrombus lyses or propagates.

## Diagnostic and therapeutic relevance

- **Prothrombin time (PT/INR)** measures the extrinsic and common pathway. Because
  warfarin reduces factors II, VII, IX and X in a ratio determined by their
  half-lives, the INR is a composite of the residual activity of four proteins — of
  which prothrombin is the longest-lived.
- **Prothrombin complex concentrate** and, in some settings, four-factor PCC are used
> for urgent reversal of warfarin.
- **Dabigatran** and heparin-class agents act on this step or upstream of it; the
  prothrombinase complex is a target of ongoing inhibitor development.
- Disseminated intravascular coagulation and severe liver disease deplete prothrombin
  alongside other factors, prolonging the PT.

## Documents

- [[Warfarin]] — inhibits VKORC1, blocking the vitamin K cycle that γ-carboxylates
  prothrombin's Gla domain; prothrombin's ~72-hour half-life is why the PT stays
  prolonged for days after warfarin is stopped.
- [[Serum]] — prothrombin is a plasma protein, so a serum sample is unusable for its
  assay; coagulation studies are performed on plasma, which is why plasma rather than
  serum is the correct specimen for PT, [[aPTT]] and factor assays.

## Connections

- [[Thrombin]] — the direct product of prothrombin activation; the entire point of
  the prothrombinase reaction, and the two are the enzyme-substrate pair of the
  common pathway.
- [[Anticoagulant]] — every class acts at or upstream of prothrombin generation:
  heparin-class agents via antithrombin, vitamin K antagonists by depleting the
  precursor, dabigatran by inhibiting the product, factor Xa inhibitors at the
  conversion step itself.
- [[Fibrinogen]] — the substrate of the thrombin that prothrombin becomes;
  prothrombin activation and fibrin formation are the two consecutive terminal steps of
  coagulation.
- [[Warfarin]] — blocks the γ-carboxylation of prothrombin that its membrane binding
  depends on; the delayed onset and offset of warfarin are the half-lives of factor
  II.
- [[Protein C]] — activated protein C inactivates factor Va, which is the cofactor
  of the prothrombinase complex; the natural anticoagulant system targets prothrombin
  generation by disarming its engine rather than by degrading prothrombin.
- [[Platelet aggregation]] — the phosphatidylserine surface on which the prothrombinase
  complex assembles is provided by activated platelets, which is the direct molecular
  coupling of primary and secondary haemostasis.
- [[Serum]] — determines that prothrombin, a plasma protein, is measured on plasma
  samples and that its absence from serum is a property of specimen preparation
  rather than of the protein.
- [[Calcium Signaling]] — the Ca²⁺ dependence of the Gla-domain-mediated membrane
  binding and of the whole prothrombinase reaction makes the cascade intrinsically
  calcium-regulated.
- [[Thromboembolism]] — prothrombin is the point of amplification at which
  anticoagulation earns its keep, and the procoagulant balance of prothrombin
  generation versus thrombin clearance is what defines the disease.
