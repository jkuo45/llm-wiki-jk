# CD36

**CD36 (Cluster of differentiation 36)**, also known as platelet glycoprotein 4 or fatty acid translocase, is a class B scavenger receptor expressed on macrophages, platelets, adipocytes and endothelial cells. It functions as a lipid sensor and is essential for activation of [[NF-κB]] and the downstream [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] in response to oxidized-lipid and related senescence-inducing cues.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

CD36 is a transmembrane glycoprotein that binds oxidized LDL (oxLDL), long-chain fatty acids, thrombospondin-1 and advanced glycation end-products, acting as a co-receptor that nucleates signaling complexes. Ligand engagement recruits Src family kinases and the [[TLR]]/MyD88–[[TRAF6]] axis, leading to [[IKK]]-mediated phosphorylation and degradation of IκBα and nuclear translocation of [[NF-κB]] (p65/[[p65]]). This NF-κB activation is a master switch for SASP transcription, inducing [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], [[IL-1α]] and MMPs. CD36 also feeds [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] activation and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] production, reinforcing the senescent phenotype. Its fatty-acid-translocase activity further couples lipid uptake to metabolic rewiring of senescent cells.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

CD36 is required for NF-κB activation in senescent cells and thereby gates the pro-inflammatory SASP. It acts as a sensor for extracellular lipids (e.g., oxLDL, [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]]) that promote senescence, converting these external signals into sustained SASP output. By linking lipid stress to NF-κB–driven inflammation, CD36 positions the SASP within the broader biology of [[Inflammaging]] and metabolic disease (SASP Review 2024).

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

CD36 mediates foam-cell formation in [[Atherosclerosis]] and contributes to insulin resistance, [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]] and obesity-related [[Inflammation]]. Its role in SASP activation makes it a candidate target for senomorphic modulation of lipid-driven senescence. CD36-blocking antibodies and small molecules are under investigation for cardiovascular and metabolic indications.
