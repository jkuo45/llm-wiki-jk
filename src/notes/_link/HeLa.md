## Overview

**HeLa** is an immortal human cell line established in 1951 from a cervical adenocarcinoma taken from Henrietta Lacks. It was the first continuously cultured human cell line and remains among the most extensively used models in biology.

## Origin and Genotype

HeLa cells are epithelial, hypotriploid, and carry genomic alterations including integrated human papillomavirus 18 (HPV-18) E6 and E7 oncoproteins that inactivate p53 and Rb tumor suppressors, driving uncontrolled proliferation. Sublines (e.g., HeLa Kyoto, HeLa S3) differ in karyotype and growth.

## Common Research Uses

HeLa cells underpin discoveries in [[Cancer]] biology, virology (polio vaccine development), cell cycle, and cytoskeleton dynamics. They are used for transfection, protein localization, and apoptosis assays, though their transformed genotype limits physiological fidelity.

## Relevance to Cancer, Aging, and Biomedicine

As a model of HPV-driven carcinogenesis, HeLa informs [[Cancer]] mechanisms and therapeutic screening. It is also employed in senescence and [[Apoptosis]] studies and as a comparator for primary cells such as [[MEF]]. Ethical provenance (consent and equity) is a defining consideration in its use.
