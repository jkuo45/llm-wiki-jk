# RRM2

**RRM2** (ribonucleotide reductase M2) encodes the small (β) subunit of human [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]] (RNR). Each RRM2 monomer (~45 kDa) contributes a di-iron center and a stable tyrosyl radical that supply the free-radical chemistry powering RNR catalysis from a distance of 30–40 Å. Because the tyrosyl radical is chemically vulnerable, RRM2 is the primary site of action of the clinically used RNR inhibitors [[Hydroxyurea]] and [[Triapine]]. RRM2 is transcriptionally gated to [[S Phase]] by [[E2F]], making it the cell-cycle-controlled switch for de novo dNTP synthesis in proliferating cells. (Note: "RRM" also abbreviates "RNA Recognition Motif"; in this vault RRM1/RRM2/RRM2B refer to the ribonucleotide-reductase subunits.)

## Structure

- **~45 kDa monomer**, forming the β₂ (R2) homodimer that assembles with the [[RRM1]] α₂ dimer into the active α₂β₂ holoenzyme.
- **Radical cofactor**: the binuclear iron center (Fe³⁺/Fe³⁺) and Tyr-derived tyrosyl radical (Tyr122-equivalent in *E. coli*; analogous residue in human RRM2) are generated by O₂-dependent self-assembly.
- **Radical transfer pathway**: a conserved proton-coupled electron-transfer (PCET) chain relays the unpaired electron ~32–40 Å to the RRM1 active site to form the catalytic thiyl radical.

## Cell-Cycle Regulation

- RRM2 **expression is S-phase-restricted**: transcription driven by [[E2F]], and the protein is degraded when cells exit S phase / enter quiescence. This gating makes RNR activity a direct index of proliferation.
- Together with constitutive [[RRM1]], the RRM2/RRM1 dimer delivers the dNTP burst needed for [[DNA Replication]]; [[RRM2B]] (p53R2) substitutes for RRM2 after DNA damage / in non-cycling cells.

## Drug Targeting

- **[[Hydroxyurea]]**: a radical scavenger that reduces/quenches the tyrosyl radical in RRM2, inactivating RNR — the basis of its use in myeloproliferative neoplasms and as an experimental inducer of [[DNA Replication Stress]] and [[Senescence]].
- **[[Triapine]]**: an iron chelator that binds the RRM2 iron center, stripping the radical cofactor and collapsing dNTP synthesis.
- Targeting RRM2 over its p53R2 counterpart is a strategy to spare repair-capable non-cycling cells.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer**: RRM2 is overexpressed in many tumor types and correlates with poor prognosis and chemoresistance; combined with RRM1, it enables the high dNTP turnover tumors need for sustained proliferation and repair.
- **Therapeutic window**: because normal quiescent tissues have low RRM2, blocking it preferentially hurts proliferating (e.g., leukemic, tumor) cells.
- **Genome stability**: deregulated RRM2 prolongs S-phase dNTP supply, promoting imbalance, [[DNA Replication Stress]], and mutagenesis.
