# Protein C

**Protein C** is a 461-residue, vitamin K-dependent plasma glycoprotein (gene *PROC*)
that circulates as an inactive zymogen. It is the central component of the body's own
anticoagulant system — the physiological inverse of the coagulation cascade, and the
brake that is exercised whenever thrombin begins to be generated on an intact
vascular surface.

## Mechanism of action

Activation and action are both surface-dependent, and that is the point:

1. **Activation.** When thrombin binds [[Thrombomodulin]] on an intact endothelial
   cell, the complex becomes a completely different enzyme. Thrombin bound to
   thrombomodulin loses essentially all fibrinogen-cleaving and cell-activating
   activity and gains protein C activation as a substrate reaction — a ~100,000-fold
   switch in substrate specificity. Thrombin–thrombomodulin also activates TAFI, and
   inhibits thrombin clearance some 20-fold faster than free thrombin.
2. **Presentation.** The endothelial protein C receptor (EPCR) binds protein C and
   presents it to the thrombin–thrombomodulin complex, augmenting activation roughly
   20-fold. Thrombomodulin localises the whole assembly, so activation happens on
   endothelium rather than in bulk plasma.
3. **Action.** Activated protein C (APC) dissociates from EPCR and, with [[Protein S]] as cofactor, inactivates factors **Va** and **VIIIa** by limited proteolysis.
   Because Va is the cofactor for the prothrombinase complex, its inactivation is
   what actually terminates thrombin burst.

> [!info] Mechanism
> This is a negative-feedback loop: thrombin generation activates protein C, and
> protein C then switches off the pathway that made the thrombin. Where coagulation
> is an amplification cascade with a lag, a transition phase and an explosive
> propagation phase, the protein C pathway is what makes the system self-limiting
> rather than running away.

## Beyond anticoagulation

APC bound to EPCR also signals through PAR1 and PAR3, with anti-inflammatory,
anti-apoptotic and endothelial-barrier-stabilising effects, and reduces nuclear factor
kappa B-driven cytokine production. This cytoprotective arm is mechanistically
distinct from the cofactor-dependent anticoagulant arm, which requires protein S.
APC reduced mortality in murine endotoxaemia and sepsis models, and showed
neuroprotection in murine ischaemic stroke — the basis for the clinical trials of
activated protein C, thrombomodulin and protein C in severe sepsis.

In sepsis, protein C is consumed, protein S is inactivated, and the activation
complex is degraded by oxidation, cytokine-driven downregulation and proteolytic
release. The result is an acquired protein C pathway defect, which is the accepted
mechanistic explanation for the microvascular thrombosis and purpura fulminans of
meningococcal sepsis.

## Deficiency

Heterozygous *PROC* variants confer a mild thrombophilia with roughly a 3- to 8-fold
increased venous thrombosis risk — one of the commonest inherited thrombophilias.
Homozygous or compound-heterozygous loss of function gives **neonatal purpura
fulminans**, a rapidly fatal microvascular thrombotic disease in the first days of
life, and is the rationale for prophylactic protein C concentrate or protein C
replacement in affected infants. Acquired deficiency occurs in severe sepsis,
disseminated intravascular coagulation, liver disease and vitamin K deficiency.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Because protein C has a short half-life (roughly 6–8 hours) compared with
> [[Prothrombin]] and the other vitamin K-dependent factors, it depletes first when
> the vitamin K pathway is blocked. Starting [[Warfarin]] in a warfarin-naive patient
> with acute thrombosis therefore produces a transient paradoxical procoagulant state
> for the first few days — the specific reason heparin bridging is used in
> high-velocity situations, and the mechanism of warfarin-induced skin necrosis.
> The same property makes protein C activity a mechanistically informative monitor of
> anticoagulant status alongside the [[INR]].

## Pharmacology

Protein C concentrate (human plasma-derived) and, in some regions, recombinant
protein C, are used in severe congenital protein C deficiency and in protein C
deficiency secondary to severe sepsis, though the latter indication is not
universally accepted. Its role as a sepsis drug is historically instructive: the
ACTIVESS trial reported an improved 28-day survival signal in the subgroup with
coagulation dysfunction, while the larger Ximela trial did not confirm a survival
benefit overall — a good example of the difference between a biologic rationale and a
demonstrated outcome.

## Documents

- [[Warfarin]] — warfarin blocks the vitamin K cycle that γ-carboxylates protein C
  along with factors II, VII, IX and X; protein C's short half-life is the reason
  warfarin's onset of action is delayed and its initiation can be procoagulant.

## Connections

- [[Thrombin]] — the dual role is the defining feature: thrombin is the procoagulant
  effector protease, and bound to thrombomodulin it becomes the enzyme that switches
  protein C on. The same molecule that makes clot is the one that limits clot.
- [[Antithrombin III]] — the other half of the endogenous anticoagulant system, acting
  by inhibiting the coagulation serine proteases directly rather than by proteolysing
  their cofactors. The two are mechanistically distinct and both are vitamin
  K-independent and vitamin K-dependent respectively.
- [[Protein S]] — the obligatory non-enzymatic cofactor for APC's inactivation of Va
  and VIIIa; protein S deficiency is an inherited thrombophilia in its own right, and
  C1q-binding protein (C4BP) acts as its inhibitor and carrier.
- [[Vitamin K]] — protein C is a vitamin K-dependent protein: its N-terminal Gla
  domain requires γ-carboxylation for calcium-mediated membrane binding, which is
  also how its deficiency enters into the warfarin story.
- [[Warfarin]] — inhibits VKORC1 and so blocks the recycling of vitamin K that
  γ-carboxylates protein C and factors II, VII, IX and X; the differential
  half-lives produce the paradoxical early prothrombotic state.
- [[Anticoagulant]] — protein C is the endogenous template for the vitamin K
  antagonist class: the same target, used pharmacologically, with a slower onset and
  offset than a direct oral agent.
- [[Endothelial Cells]] — endothelium is the required platform for the whole pathway,
  via thrombomodulin and EPCR; the pathway is intrinsically about intact endothelium
  versus injured endothelium.
- [[Sepsis]] — acquired protein C deficiency through consumption plus inactivation of
  the activation complex is the mechanistic basis of sepsis-associated coagulopathy
  and of the clinical trials of protein C and thrombomodulin in this setting.
- [[Fibrinogen]] — the substrate of the thrombin that protein C's pathway exists to
  limit; protein C acts one step further upstream, on the cofactors that generate that
  thrombin.
