# Malaria

**Malaria** is a vector-borne zoonosis caused by *Plasmodium* species; *P. falciparum* causes most severe disease including cerebral malaria and malaria-induced anemia (responsible for ~1/3 of malaria deaths). Because the parasite matures inside [[Erythrocytes]], red-cell death biology — [[Eryptosis]] in particular — is a host-defense lever.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Infected erythrocytes generate ~2× more free radicals (H2O2, OH−); oxidative stress opens nonselective cation channels (Na+, K+, Ca2+), the Ca2+ rise favors both parasite growth and eryptosis, and the eryptotic cell is engulfed with the parasite inside. Because eryptosis clears rather than lyses, it removes infected cells without a DAMP burst.

## Eryptosis as defense

- Eryptotic, PS-exposing infected cells are recognized and phagocytosed by macrophages — clearing host cell and intraerythrocytic pathogen together, the proposed basis for using eryptosis stimulation therapeutically.
- Eryptosis-lowering traits provide partial protection against severe malaria: [[Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase|G6PD]] deficiency, sickle cell anemia, β-thalassemia trait, and deficiency of GLUT1 or [[Anion Exchanger 1|AE1]].
- Pro-eryptotic compounds slow parasitemia in mice/humans: lead, paclitaxel, cyclosporine, prostaglandin E2, curcumin, amphotericin B, dimethyl fumarate, chlorpromazine — and β-cryptoxanthin has been proposed as an anti-malarial eryptosis inducer.
- Rac1 GTPase facilitates parasite invasion, offering a second pro-eryptosis-adjacent drug angle.
