# FUNDC1

FUNDC1 (FUN14 Domain-Containing Protein 1) is an outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM) protein that functions as a receptor for ubiquitin-independent [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], particularly under hypoxic stress. It is a key mediator of receptor-mediated mitophagy and contributes to the maintenance of [[MERCS|Mitochondria-Associated Endoplasmic Reticulum Membranes (MAMs)]].

## Mechanism

FUNDC1-mediated mitophagy is regulated by phosphorylation. Under normal conditions, [[Src|Src kinase]] phosphorylates FUNDC1, keeping it inactive. During [[Hypoxia|hypoxia]], the mitochondrial kinase [[CK2]] is inhibited, leading to dephosphorylation of FUNDC1 at the Ser13 site. This dephosphorylation activates FUNDC1, enabling it to recruit [[LC3]] via its [[LIR Motif|LIR motif]] and initiate mitophagy.

## Role in MAMs

FUNDC1 is localized to [[MERCS|MAMs]] and interacts with the [[IP3R2]] receptor on the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|endoplasmic reticulum]]. The presence of FUNDC1 helps maintain the structural stability of MAMs. When FUNDC1 is absent, the MAM structure is disrupted, leading to impaired [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|mitochondrial fission]] and abnormal [[Calcium|calcium signaling]]. Given that MAMs are often dysfunctional in [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]], FUNDC1's fine-tuning of MAMs provides a mechanism for understanding calcium homeostasis imbalance in [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]].

## Role in Neuroprotection

Activating FUNDC1-mediated autophagy can clear damaged [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]], significantly reducing neuronal [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] and infarct volume following [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury|ischemia/hypoxia-reperfusion injury]]. Conversely, knockout of FUNDC1 significantly worsens neural damage.
