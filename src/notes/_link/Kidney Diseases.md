# Kidney Diseases

**Kidney disease** covers a spectrum of acute and chronic disorders of renal
structure or function. Two umbrella terms are used:

- **Acute kidney injury (AKI)** — a reduction in kidney function over hours to
  days, staged by rise in serum [[Creatinine]] and fall in urine output.
- **[[Chronic Kidney Disease|CKD]]** — an abnormality of kidney structure or
  function **present for more than three months**, with implications for health.
  Staged G1–G5 by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and A1–A3 by
  albuminuria.

> [!info] Why the kidney is uniquely vulnerable
> The [[Kidney]] is the most vascularised organ per unit mass and receives
> roughly 20–25% of cardiac output against ~0.5% of body mass. It is exposed
> to a high filtered solute load, is a principal site of xenobiotic metabolism,
  and is a terminally differentiated organ with essentially no regenerative
> capacity — nephron loss is permanent and compensated only by hypertrophy of
> surviving nephrons. There is no stem-cell replacement mechanism. Every one of
> these facts is a reason kidney disease is cumulative rather than reversible.

## Epidemiology

CKD affects **8–16% of the adult population** — approximately 753 million
people globally (2016 estimate). It caused about 1.2 million deaths in 2015,
up from 409,000 in 1990. Leading causes of attributable death are
[[Hypertension]] (~550,000), [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]] (~418,000), and
glomerulonephritis (~238,000).

> [!warning] The dominant cause of death in CKD is not kidney failure
> Cardiovascular disease kills CKD patients at a far higher rate than kidney
> failure itself. A large fraction of that risk is attributable to the
> uremic milieu — guanidino compounds, indoxyl sulfate, advanced glycation
> end products, and oxidative stress accelerating [[Atherosclerosis]] — rather
> than to hyperkalaemia or fluid overload. This reframes CKD as a systemic
> metabolic and inflammatory disorder with renal involvement, not a purely
> urological one.

## Pathophysiology

- **Hyperfiltration and glomerular hypertension** — the initial lesion in
  diabetic and hypertensive nephropathy. Elevated intraglomerular pressure
  drives albuminuria, the earliest detectable sign.
- **Proteinuria and albuminuria** — the strongest single predictor of CKD
  progression and of cardiovascular risk. Loss of the charge-selective
  glomerular filtration barrier allows albumin through; the filtered albumin
  overloads proximal tubular cells, inducing oxidative stress and further
  injury.
- **Tubulointerstitial fibrosis** — the common endpoint of essentially all CKD.
  TGF-β1 signalling through SMAD3 drives myofibroblast activation and
  extracellular matrix deposition, progressively replacing parenchyma with
  scar.
- **Renin–angiotensin activation** — reduced perfusion stimulates renin release
  and [[Angiotensin II]] production, which is both a cause and a consequence
  of CKD. This is the rationale for the first-line use of [[ACE Inhibitors]]
  and ARBs, which slow progression and reduce proteinuria.
- **Uraemia** — accumulation of nitrogenous waste when GFR falls below ~15
  mL/min/1.73 m², producing lethargy, pruritus, pericarditis, and encephalopathy.
- **Electrolyte and acid–base disturbance** — [[Potassium]] retention
  (hyperkalaemia, with fatal arrhythmia risk) typically below GFR 20–25;
  hyperphosphatemia with hypocalcaemia; metabolic acidosis. Hyperphosphatemia
  also promotes vascular calcification, and rising FGF-23 may contribute to
  left ventricular hypertrophy.
- **Endocrine failure** — reduced [[Erythropoietin]] production causes
  [[Anemia]] of chronic disease; reduced 1,25-dihydroxy[[Vitamin D]] with
  phosphate retention causes renal osteodystrophy and contributes to
  [[Osteoporosis]]; falling [[Klotho]] is among the earliest measurable
  changes in CKD and is used as a biomarker of renal ageing.

> [!info] TGF-β and the Smad7 brake
> TGF-β signalling is normally held in check by **[[Smad7]]**, an inhibitory
> Smad that competes for the type I TGF-β receptor and recruits SMURF2 for
  receptor ubiquitination. Loss of Smad7 function removes this brake, so
> TGF-β-driven fibrosis is, mechanistically, a brake failure as much as a
  stimulus excess. Smad7 appears in this vault through kidney-disease notes, and
  the anti-fibrotic strategy of enhancing Smad7 signalling is a live
  therapeutic concept. The stub's inbound document link is retained in the
  Documents section.

## Clinical presentation

Early CKD is asymptomatic and detected only by screening: raised creatinine,
reduced eGFR, or [[Albuminuria]]. Later disease presents with leg oedema,
fatigue, anorexia, nausea, pruritus, and confusion. Diagnosis relies on eGFR
plus urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio; [[Cystatin C]] is a useful
alternative creatinine assay where muscle mass makes creatinine unreliable.
Ultrasound or biopsy establishes the cause.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Avoid NSAIDs in CKD — they inhibit prostaglandin synthesis, afferent
> arteriolar constriction, and precipitate AKI. Contrast agents and aminoglycosides
> require dose adjustment. Many herbs and over-the-counter products are
> nephrotoxic, and patients should be asked specifically about them rather than
> about "medications".

## Progression and management

Staging is by eGFR (G1 ≥90 to G5 <15 mL/min/1.73 m²) and albuminuria (A1
<30, A2 30–300, A3 >300 mg/g). Management is: blood-pressure and
albuminuria control with ACEI/ARB; glycaemic control; SGLT2 inhibitors and
finerenone in diabetic CKD; loop diuretics for oedema; correction of anaemia
and mineral bone disorder; plant-forward dietary pattern; and at stage 5,
[[End Stage Renal Disease]] requiring [[Renal Dialysis]], peritoneal dialysis, or
transplantation.

Dietary plant-forward patterns are associated with improved intermediate
outcomes; the caveat is that patients who need specific nutritional
restrictions, or who have protein-energy malnutrition, need dietitian referral
rather than blanket advice.

## Connections

- [[Smad7]] — The inhibitory Smad that brakes TGF-β-driven tubulointerstitial
  fibrosis. Loss of its function is a mechanistic route from TGF-β stimulus to
  renal scarring, and restoring it is an experimental anti-fibrotic strategy.
  The stub's inbound document link is retained.

- [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway]] — TGF-β1 via SMAD3 is the dominant profibrotic
  axis in the kidney, and the pathway most targeted to slow CKD progression.

- [[Kidney Fibrosis]] — Tubulointerstitial scarring is the shared anatomical
  endpoint of diabetic, hypertensive, immune-mediated, and obstructive kidney
  disease. Treating fibrosis rather than each cause is the field's main
  conceptual bet.

- [[Chronic Kidney Disease]] — The chronic arm of this umbrella term, defined by
  a >3-month abnormality in structure or function; the note for the staging and
  management detail.

- [[Acute Kidney Injury]] — The acute arm. AKI episodes accelerate long-term CKD
  and are associated with subsequent CKD risk even after apparent recovery,
  which is why AKI is treated as a predictor rather than an isolated event.

- [[Diabetic nephropathy]] — Diabetes is the second leading cause of
  CKD-associated death and the commonest cause of end-stage renal disease in
  most countries, acting through hyperfiltration, albuminuria, and
  AGE-driven oxidative stress.

- [[Albuminuria]] and [[Proteinuria]] — Earliest measurable markers and
  strongest independent predictors of progression; the basis for renal
  screening in diabetes and hypertension.

- [[Angiotensin II]] and [[ACE Inhibitors]] — The renin–angiotensin system is
  both a driver of glomerular hypertension and the target of first-line
  therapy; ACEI/ARB treatment is the clearest demonstration that modulating a
  hormonal loop slows kidney disease.

- [[NLRP3]] and [[Inflammaging]] — Inflammasome activation links uraemic toxins,
  oxidative stress, and tubular injury to IL-1β/IL-18 release, providing a
  mechanistic bridge between kidney ageing and the systemic inflammation
  described elsewhere in this vault.

- [[Klotho]] — A kidney-secreted anti-ageing hormone whose decline is among
  the earliest signs of renal ageing and a biomarker of CKD stage.

- [[Erythropoietin]] and [[Anemia]] — Loss of erythropoietin synthesis is the
  clearest endocrine consequence of renal failure and one of the most
  treatable.

- [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Cardiovascular Disease]] — The dominant cause of
  mortality in CKD, driven substantially by the uraemic milieu rather than by
  renal failure itself.

- [[Methylation]] and [[Homocysteine]] — Elevated homocysteine accompanies CKD
  and contributes to endothelial injury; the relationship is associative and
  the causal weight of homocysteine in renal disease progression is still
  debated.

- [[Ferroptosis]] — An active research axis in AKI and CKD, where iron-dependent
  lipid peroxidation contributes to tubular and podocyte death; therapeutic
  evidence remains preclinical.

- [[Nephron]] — The functional unit whose non-renewable loss is the defining
  structural constraint on recovery in kidney disease.
