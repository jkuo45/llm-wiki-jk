# TLR7

**Toll-like receptor 7 (TLR7)** is an endosomal pattern-recognition receptor of the [[Toll-like Receptor]] family that detects single-stranded RNA (ssRNA) within acidified endolysosomal compartments. Unlike [[TLR3]] (dsRNA) and [[TLR9]] (CpG DNA), TLR7 responds to the genomic RNA and RNA fragments released during viral replication and to synthetic ssRNA analogues such as imiquimod and resiquimod.

## Overview

- **Ligand:** ssRNA, specifically regions of uridine-rich or guanosine-rich sequence, and small synthetic molecules (imiquimod, resiquimod, loxoribine).
- **Location:** exclusively endosomal; trafficked from the plasma membrane by UNC93B1 and IRF7-dependent pathways. Cells lacking UNC93B1 (including human TLR7-deficient patients) cannot respond to TLR7 agonists.
- **Expressed in:** [[Plasmacytoid Dendritic Cells]] (the professional type I interferon factory), [[B Cells]], monocytes, and — at lower level — most other nucleated cells.

## Structure & Domains

TLR7 belongs to the type I TLR group. It has a large leucine-rich repeat extracellular ectodomain that binds ligand and a single-pass transmembrane helix followed by a cytoplasmic Toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain. The TIR domain recruits the adaptor [[MyD88]] through its own TIR–TIR interaction; unlike TLR4, TLR7 does not use the TRIF adaptor, so it cannot directly trigger the RIPK3 necrosome arm.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

1. Ligand binding in the endosome induces receptor dimerization and conformational change.
2. MyD88 is recruited, then IRAK4 and TRAF6 are activated, driving the canonical [[NF-κB]] branch (IκBα degradation, RelA/p65 and c-Rel nuclear translocation) and the [[MAPK]] arms.
3. In parallel, [[IRF7]] — the master regulator of type I interferon — is phosphorylated and translocates to the nucleus. IRF7 is uniquely co-expressed at high level in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, which explains their massive [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes]] output.
4. Endosomal TLR7 signalling is also linked to [[Apoptosis]] and to [[Autophagy]] clearance of the signalosome; chronic activation drives [[Cellular Senescence]] and senescence-associated secretory phenotypes.

## Physiological Function

TLR7 is the sentinel that couples nucleic-acid sensing to antiviral defence. Type I interferon it induces induces an antiviral state in neighbouring cells, upregulates MHC class I presentation and activates NK cells. In B cells, TLR7 provides a B-cell-intrinsic "adjuvant" signal that drives proliferation, antibody secretion and, together with [[Antigen Presentation]] by dendritic cells, shapes germinal-centre responses. TLR7 also participates in sensing of self-RNA released during tissue injury, linking it to [[Inflammation]] and [[Inflammaging]].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Autoimmunity:** chronic TLR7 activation with self-RNA drives [[Interferon-gamma|IFN-γ]]-dominated type I interferon signatures in systemic lupus erythematosus, Sjögren syndrome and interferonopathies; TLR7 gain-of-function mutations cause lupus-like disease. Mice lacking TLR7 are resistant to autoimmunity, whereas [[TLR9]] loss often worsens it — the two receptors oppose each other on self-DNA versus self-RNA.
- **Cancer:** TLR7 agonists are trialed as intratumoral or systemic immunotherapies; TLR7 is also overexpressed in some myeloid leukaemias.
- **Antivirals:** TLR7 agonism is a proposed strategy for HIV latency reversal in [[HIV]].
- **Drug:** imiquimod (topical, for genital warts and basal cell carcinoma) activates TLR7 on dendritic cells and keratinocytes, driving local type I interferon and TNF-alpha production. Direct TLR7 antagonists are under development for SLE.
