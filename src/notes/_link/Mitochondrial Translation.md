# Mitochondrial Translation

**Mitochondrial translation** is the organelle-autonomous process by which the protein-coding genes of [[Mitochondrial DNA|mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) are expressed. Human mtDNA encodes 13 [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|oxidative phosphorylation]] (OXPHOS) subunits, 22 tRNAs, and 2 rRNAs; the 13 mRNAs are translated within the [[Mitochondrial Matrix]] by dedicated mitochondrial ribosomes (mitoribosomes, 55S in mammals) into the core subunits of complexes I, III, IV, and V. Because these subunits are required for assembly of the respiratory chain, mitochondrial translation is a rate-limiting step for bioenergetic function and a key node in mitochondrial quality control and aging.

## Mechanism and Components

- **mtDNA expression**: mtDNA is transcribed into polycistronic transcripts that are processed into individual mRNAs; translation is coupled to the organelle's own tRNA pool and rRNAs encoded in the mitochondrial genome.
- **Mitoribosomes**: Mammalian mitoribosomes (55S; 39S large + 28S small subunits) are protein-rich relative to bacterial ribosomes, with reduced rRNAs and many mitochondrial-specific ribosomal proteins (MRPs).
- **tRNA modification**: Functional mitochondrial translation requires modified tRNAs; **[[Taurine]]** conjugates to the wobble uridine of mitochondrial tRNAs (e.g., taurine-modified tRNAs for [[Leucine]] and [[Lysine]] codons), stabilizing codon–anticodon interactions. Taurine deficiency thus impairs efficient synthesis of respiratory-chain subunits.
- **Initiation and elongation**: Mitochondrial translation uses mtIF2/3, mtEF-Tu/mtEF-Ts, mtEF-G1, and mtEF4; termination release factors (mtRF1a) and the recycling factor (RRF) complete the cycle.
- **Quality control**: Misfolded or unassembled proteins trigger the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPR^mt^]] via [[ATF4]]/ATF5/CHOP transcription factors, inducing chaperones (HSP60) and proteases (LONP1).

## Role in Mitochondrial Function and Aging

- Because 13 OXPHOS subunits come from mtDNA, defective mitochondrial translation (e.g., from mtDNA mutations, tRNA defects, or taurine loss) directly impairs respiration and [[Mitochondrial Function|mitochondrial function]].
- Age-related decline in translation fidelity and efficiency — alongside rising [[Heteroplasmy]] — contributes to bioenergetic decline in [[Stem Cells|stem cells]] and post-mitotic tissues (Bautista & López-Cortés, 2026).
- Restoring translation capacity, for example via [[NAD+]] repletion, supports [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] and UPR^mt^-mediated resilience.

## Pathology and Clinical Relevance

- Pathogenic mtDNA mutations affecting tRNAs (e.g., the m.3243A>G [[MELAS]] mutation in the tRNA^Leu^ gene) cause severe mitochondrial translation defects and disease.
- Mitochondrial translation inhibitors (e.g., chloramphenicol, doxycycline at high dose) are used experimentally to probe organelle-autonomous function.
