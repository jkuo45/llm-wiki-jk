# Selenocysteine tRNA methylation promotes oxidative stress resistance in melanoma metastasis

> [!info]
> Source: Nease LA, Church KP, Delclaux I, et al. **Selenocysteine tRNA methylation promotes oxidative stress resistance in melanoma metastasis.** *Nature Cancer* 5, 1868–1884 (2024). doi:10.1038/s43018-024-00844-8

## Overview

[[Selenocysteine]] (Sec) is the 21st amino acid, incorporated co-translationally at in-frame [[UGA codon|UGA stop codons]] via a dedicated recoding machinery. Selenocysteine-containing proteins are central pillars of [[Redox Homeostasis]] and the cellular [[Antioxidant]] response. The authors discovered that [[Selenocysteine]] translation depends on **two tRNA[Ser]Sec isodecoders** that differ by a single 2′-*O*-ribose methylation at position 34, called **Um34**, and that **[[FTSJ1]] is the Um34 methyltransferase** responsible for this modification.

> [!important]
> Loss of Um34 causes **ribosomal stalling** and **decreased UGA recoding** of selenocysteine, making cells more sensitive to [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress]] and markedly reducing their ability to form metastatic colonies.

## Key findings (by figure)

- **Fig. 1 — Stress induces the pathway.** Selenoprotein translation and tRNA[Ser]Sec Um34 levels increase in response to [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]].
- **Fig. 2 — FTSJ1 is the Um34 writer.** [[FTSJ1]] is identified and validated as the methyltransferase that installs Um34 on tRNA[Ser]Sec.
- **Fig. 3 — Subset-specific recoding.** Um34 regulates UGA recoding efficiency in a **subset of selenoproteins** involved in oxidative stress (not all selenoproteins equally), pointing to a regulatory layer beyond bulk selenoproteome expression.
- **Fig. 4 — Antioxidant protection.** The Um34 modification protects cancer cells from [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress]] by enabling efficient synthesis of antioxidant selenoproteins.
- **Fig. 5 — Micrometastases are stressed.** Micrometastatic cells experience high levels of stress and upregulate [[Selenocysteine]] biosynthesis, consistent with prior work (Piskounova et al., *Nature* 2015) showing oxidative stress limits distant metastasis.
- **Fig. 6 — Metastasis requirement in vivo.** tRNA[Ser]Sec Um34 modification is **necessary and sufficient** for metastasis in xenograft models of [[Melanoma]].

## Mechanistic significance

The work reveals a **post-transcriptional, tRNA-epitranscriptomic control point** for the selenoprotein machinery:

1. FTSJ1-mediated Um34 methylation of tRNA[Ser]Sec is a prerequisite for high-fidelity insertion of [[Selenocysteine]] at recoded UGA codons.
2. Efficient recoding selectively supports translation of selenoproteins that buffer [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — notably the [[Glutathione Peroxidase 4|GPX4]] and [[Thioredoxin reductase]] families, which are essential for controlling lipid peroxidation and preventing [[Ferroptosis]].
3. Metastatic cells co-opt this axis: by maintaining Um34-modified tRNA[Ser]Sec they sustain an antioxidant shield that permits survival under the high [[Oxidative Stress]] of the metastatic niche.

> [!warning]
> The study positions [[FTSJ1]] / tRNA[Ser]Sec Um34 as a **potential therapeutic target specific to metastatic disease** — inhibiting the modification collapses antioxidant selenoprotein translation and suppresses metastatic colonization without (in the models shown) broadly blocking housekeeping selenoproteins.

## Contextual links to the selenoprotein field

- [[Selenocysteine]] insertion requires the SECIS element, the SECIS-binding protein SECISBP2 (SBP2; suggested new entity note), and the dedicated elongation factor eEFSec; Um34 acts downstream as a fine-tuning modification of the tRNA itself.
- Ribosome stalling during selenoprotein translation was independently shown to expose a [[Ferroptosis]] vulnerability (Li et al., *Nat. Chem. Biol.* 2022), reinforcing the connection between Sec recoding fidelity and cell-death fate.
- [[Selenium]] availability itself regulates UGA recoding (Howard et al., *JBC* 2013), indicating nutrient, tRNA-modification, and stress inputs converge on the same translational step.
