# BMI1

BMI1 (B-cell-specific Moloney murine leukemia virus integration site 1, gene *BMI1*) is a core subunit of Polycomb Repressive Complex 1 (PRC1) that maintains gene silencing through [[Ubiquitin]]-mediated histone H2A monoubiquitylation. It is a well-established senescence suppressor: by repressing the [[CDKN2A|INK4a/ARF (p16/p14ARF)]] locus, BMI1 preserves proliferative capacity, and its loss triggers premature [[Senescence]]. In the SASP context, BMI1 also negatively regulates the chemokine [[CCL2]] through an [[IL-1α]]-mediated mechanism in senescent mesenchymal stromal cells, tuning the inflammatory output of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Polycomb silencing**: As part of PRC1, BMI1 catalyzes H2A ubiquitylation at target loci, compacting chromatin and repressing transcription; it cooperates with PRC2 (e.g., [[EZH2]]) for stable silencing.
- **INK4a/ARF repression**: BMI1 directly represses the *CDKN2A* locus encoding [[p16 INK4a|p16]] and p14ARF, blunting the [[p53]]/Rb tumor-suppressor and senescence arms (Jacobs et al., 1999; PMID 10586044).
- **Stemness**: BMI1 sustains self-renewal of normal and cancer stem cells, linking epigenetic memory to proliferation.
- **SASP modulation**: In senescent mesenchymal stromal cells, BMI1 restrains [[CCL2]] expression downstream of [[IL-1α]] signaling, shaping the chemokine composition of the SASP.

## Role in Senescence

- BMI1 loss de-represses INK4a/ARF, imposing irreversible arrest—making BMI1 a key anti-senescence gatekeeper.
- It modulates SASP composition (notably CCL2/monocyte recruitment) rather than the arrest itself.
- BMI1 therefore couples epigenetic state to both entry into senescence and the quality of the senescent secretome.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- BMI1 is an oncogene and cancer-stem-cell marker; BMI1 inhibitors are explored in oncology.
- Modulating BMI1 could dampen SASP-driven inflammation in [[Aging]] and fibrosis without eliminating the senescent cell.
- BMI1 status influences therapy response and senescence-associated tissue remodeling.
