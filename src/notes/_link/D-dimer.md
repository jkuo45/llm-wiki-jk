# D-dimer

**D-dimer** is the terminal degradation product of *cross-linked* fibrin. It is a ~180 kDa molecule consisting of two D-domain remnants of [[Fibrinogen]], one from each of the two fibrin monomers, held together by the isopeptide bonds that [[Thrombin]] activated [[Factor XIII]] created between the γ-chains.

> [!info] Mechanism
> Coagulation builds a clot; fibrinolysis takes it apart. The half-life of a clot is minutes to hours, so in health, fibrin is laid down and removed continuously and invisibly. When that balance is disturbed — a thrombus forms, or systemic coagulopathy activates both cascades — the fibrinolytic enzyme [[Plasmin]], generated from [[Plasminogen]] (notably by [[Tissue Plasminogen Activator]]), cleaves the cross-linked mesh into measurable fragments. D-dimer is the *specific* readout for that sequence, because it can only form from cross-linked fibrin, never from free circulating fibrinogen.

## D-dimer versus fibrinogen degradation products

The distinction is diagnostically essential:

- **Fibrinogen degradation products (FDPs)** arise from breakdown of *unclotted* fibrinogen as well as fibrin. They are elevated in essentially any state with high turnover, including malignancy without thrombosis, and they have a substantial anticoagulant effect when present in high concentration, which is why FDPs are associated with bleeding in [[Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation]].
- **D-dimer** requires the factor XIII cross-link, so it appears only where thrombin has actually been active on a fibrin surface. Patients with [[Liver|advanced liver disease]] typically have high FDPs with undetectable D-dimer.

> [!warning] D-dimer is cleared, not produced, at a measurable rate
> D-dimer has a plasma half-life of roughly 8 hours, cleared by renal excretion and the reticuloendothelial system. This is far longer than the 10–15 minutes of the thrombin–antithrombin complex or prothrombin fragments. The consequence is clinical: a D-dimer drawn 24 hours after the onset of symptoms may be normalising even though the thrombus persists, and a normal D-dimer in a patient whose symptoms started several days ago is substantially less reassuring. Serial measurement is used to guide anticoagulant duration after a confirmed VTE.

## Clinical use

The dominant role of D-dimer is **exclusion**, not diagnosis. Modern assays are validated at ≥95% sensitivity and ≥97% negative predictive value for the specific decision they are used in: ruling out [[Pulmonary Embolism]] and deep vein thrombosis in a patient with a *low or non-high clinical pretest probability* (e.g. YEARS criteria or the revised Geneva score). Used this way, it avoids imaging in a substantial fraction of suspected cases.

Other established applications:

- **Guiding anticoagulant duration.** A persistently elevated D-dimer after 3 months of anticoagulant therapy identifies patients at high recurrence risk, who may benefit from extended anticoagulation.
- **Diagnosis of [[Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation]]**, in combination with PT, aPTT, fibrinogen and the thrombocytopenia score.
- **Prognostication in [[COVID-19]]** and other inflammatory states, where an elevated D-dimer at presentation identifies a subgroup with markedly higher mortality and microthrombi. This is the *worst* use of the test — low specificity, high cross-sectional prevalence of elevation — and should never be read as a diagnosis of thrombosis.

## Interpretation

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> A high D-dimer is not a diagnosis. Its positive predictive value for VTE is low (commonly under 20%), because so many conditions raise it. Guidelines therefore require the result to be interpreted with a pretest probability score, not in isolation.

**Assay standardisation is a known unsolved problem.** All clinical D-dimer assays detect the same epitope, and patients carry fibrin and FDP species of many sizes, so results are not interchangeable between platforms. The D-dimer:FDP ratio varies between individuals, and the FDA has reviewed 510(k) clearances describing nominally "the same" analyte. A value from one assay cannot be directly compared with a value from another, and reported units (ng/mL FEU, mg/L FEU, DDU) are not equivalent.

**Common causes of an elevated D-dimer beyond thrombosis:**

| Category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Non-thrombotic inflammation | [[Sepsis]], [[Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome]], [[Cytokine Storm]], post-operative state, trauma, burns |
| Malignancy | Any cancer; pancreatic, ovarian and lung are the highest |
| Pregnancy and age | Both raise levels physiologically; a rising age-adjusted cutoff has been proposed, and this is why D-dimer is hard to interpret in pregnancy |
| Other physiological states | Strenuous exercise, immobilisation, recent surgery |
| Tissue injury | [[Myocardial infarction]], rhabdomyolysis, [[Ischemia]] |
| Chronic vascular disease | [[Atherosclerosis]], vasculitis, [[Sepsis]]-related vasculopathy |

**Renal and hepatic caveats:** renal impairment reduces D-dimer clearance, raising levels independent of coagulation; advanced liver disease does the opposite.

## Documents

- [[Fibrinogen]] — the precursor of fibrin and the source of non-cross-linked degradation products; understanding the FDP/D-dimer distinction is the point of this note.
- [[Plasmin]] — the protease that generates D-dimer by degrading the fibrin mesh.
- [[Plasminogen]] — the zymogen activated to [[Plasmin]]; the substrate supply that couples fibrinolysis to fibrinolytic activity.
- [[Tissue Plasminogen Activator]] — the principal physiological activator of [[Plasminogen]], released from endothelial cells; its recombinant form is also the drug rt-PA and the link between fibrinolysis and therapeutic reperfusion.

## Connections

- [[Thrombin]] — generates the fibrin that D-dimer is made from, and its activity is what the [[INR]] measures on the warfarin side of the same physiological loop. Every step from prothrombin to D-dimer is a readout of thrombin generation.
- [[Factor XIII]] — creates the γ–γ cross-link that defines D-dimer. A factor XIII deficiency causes bleeding with normal PT, aPTT, and platelet count, and by definition cannot produce D-dimer.
- [[Fibrinogen]] — the soluble precursor; its consumption and its fragment profile (FDP versus D-dimer) distinguish consumption from fibrinolysis.
- [[Pulmonary Embolism]] — the principal clinical target of the test. The decision rule is a joint one — pretest probability plus D-dimer — and a D-dimer is not interpretable without the first component.
- [[Heparin]] and [[Fondaparinux]] and [[Enoxaparin]] — the anticoagulants used to treat the thrombi D-dimer helps exclude. D-dimer is the tool that lets clinicians stop that treatment safely; a normalising D-dimer underpins duration decisions.
- [[Cytokine Storm]] — inflammation-driven coagulopathy raises D-dimer without any single thrombus being the driver, which is the principal source of false positives in the modern era.
- [[Platelet]] — consumptive thrombocytopenia and thrombin generation travel together in DIC; D-dimer plus platelet count is the standard pairing.
