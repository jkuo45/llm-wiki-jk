# Lysosomal Membrane Permeabilization

## Overview

[[Lysosomal Membrane Permeabilization]] (LMP) is the loss of integrity of the [[Lysosome]]'s limiting membrane, leading to the controlled or catastrophic release of intraluminal [[Acid Hydrolases]] (notably [[Cathepsins]]) into the cytosol. LMP is a distinct cell-death trigger that bridges autophagy, apoptosis, and necrosis depending on its extent.

## Structure & Determinants

Lysosomes maintain an acidic lumen (~pH 4.5–5.0) and a cholesterol- and [[LAMP]]-enriched membrane that resists rupture. LMP can be subtle (permeabilization allowing selective cathepsin leakage) or catastrophic (full membrane rupture). Susceptibility is increased by lysosomal accumulation of [[Lipofuscin]], redox-active compounds, and [[Oxidative Stress]], which oxidizes lysosomal membranes.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Destabilizing cues—such as [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], sphingosine, and lysosomotropic agents—permeabilize the membrane. Mild LMP releases cathepsins that cleave [[Bid]] and other apical caspases, engaging [[Apoptosis]]. Extensive LMP floods the cytosol with hydrolases, causing necrosis-like death. LMP also interfaces with [[Autophagy]]: impaired autophagic clearance of damaged lysosomes (failed [[Mitophagy]]/lysophagy) permits LMP, whereas adaptive autophagy can remove compromised lysosomes via lysophagy. The released cathepsins can activate inflammasomes, linking LMP to inflammation.

## Physiological Function

LMP acts as a quality-control threshold: transient, selective permeabilization can initiate repair or controlled death of damaged cells, while complete rupture eliminates irreparably harmed cells. It thus participates in development, immunity, and tissue remodeling.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Excessive LMP underlies several pathologies, including neurodegeneration, [[Cardiomyocyte Toxicity|cardiomyopathy]], and [[Cancer]] cell death induced by lysosomotropic chemotherapeutics. Pharmacologic induction of LMP is exploited to kill therapy-resistant tumor cells.
