# Rilonacept

**Rilonacept** (brand name **Arcalyst**) is a soluble decoy receptor fusion protein — an "IL-1 trap" — that binds and neutralizes both [[IL-1α]] and [[IL-1β]], preventing them from engaging the cell-surface [[IL-1 Receptor|IL-1R1]]. It is used in cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes and recurrent pericarditis.

## Mechanism of Action

Rilonacept is a dimeric fusion protein comprising the extracellular domains of the [[IL-1 Receptor|IL-1R1]] (ligand-binding portion) and [[IL-1RAcP]] (accessory protein), fused to the Fc region of human IgG1. This design creates a soluble decoy that captures both IL-1α and IL-1β with high affinity in the extracellular space, acting as a molecular "sponge" for IL-1 family ligands.

Unlike [[Anakinra]] (which binds only the receptor on cells), rilonacept intercepts the ligand before it reaches the receptor. Unlike [[Canakinumab]] (which is selective for IL-1β), rilonacept neutralizes both IL-1α and IL-1β.

## Pharmacokinetics

- **Route**: Subcutaneous injection
- **Half-life**: ~8.6 days
- **Dosing**: 160 mg loading dose, then 80 mg weekly
- **Metabolism**: Proteolytic degradation

## Clinical Indications

- **Cryopyrin-Associated Periodic Syndromes (CAPS)** — including familial cold autoinflammatory syndrome and Muckle-Wells syndrome
- **Recurrent Pericarditis** — approved 2021 for reduction of recurrent pericarditis episodes and associated symptoms

## Role in Inflammaging

By sequestering both IL-1α and IL-1β, rilonacept reduces the overall IL-1 inflammatory signaling burden. This is relevant to [[Inflammaging]] because both IL-1α and IL-1β are major drivers of chronic age-related inflammation and [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] from [[Senescent Cells]].

## Adverse Effects

- Injection site reactions (erythema, pain, swelling)
- Upper respiratory tract infections
- Increased cholesterol and triglycerides
- Neutropenia (rare)
- Serious infections
