# Axon Degeneration

**Axon degeneration** is the pathological loss of axonal structure and function, a hallmark of traumatic, metabolic, and neurodegenerative injury. [[Wallerian Degeneration]] is its most studied acute form.

## Mechanism

The dominant execution pathway is [[SARM1]]-dependent: axonal injury → [[NMNAT2]] loss → NMN/[[NAD+]] ratio rise → SARM1 activation → catastrophic NAD⁺ depletion → energy failure → fragmentation. Non-SARM1 pathways (e.g., [[Apoptosis]], [[Autophagy]]-mediated pruning) also contribute in developmental and disease contexts.

## Disease Context

- Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy ([[Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy]])
- Traumatic brain and spinal cord injury ([[Traumatic Brain Injury]])
- Neurodegenerative diseases: [[ALS]], [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Glaucoma]], Parkinson's disease

## Therapeutic

SARM1 inhibitors ([[Disulfiram]], [[DSRM-3716]], [[GSK-428]], [[NB-4746]]) block the execution step and are in clinical development.

## Connections

- [[SARM1]] — central executioner enzyme
- [[Wallerian Degeneration]] — acute injury sub-form
- [[NAD+]] — depleted substrate in the degenerative cascade
- [[NMNAT2]] — protective synthase; its loss triggers degeneration
