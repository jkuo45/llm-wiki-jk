# Selenomethionine

**Selenomethionine** (SeMet) is methionine with the sulfur replaced by selenium. Because selenium and sulfur are chemically similar chalcogens, the substitution is tolerated by protein synthesis machinery and by folded protein structure. It is the predominant form of selenium in Brazil nuts, cereal grains, soybeans and grassland legumes, and it is the form used in most commercial selenium supplements and in selenium-enriched yeast.

## Absorption, incorporation and retention

Orally ingested SeMet is absorbed essentially completely and joins the methionine pool, where it is used by three competing fates:

1. **Non-specific protein incorporation.** SeMet is charged onto tRNA^Met and inserted at methionine positions in any protein being synthesised — this is what makes it useful for protein crystallography and what causes chronic selenosis at excess intake.
2. **Trans-selenation to selenocysteine.** Through the selenophosphate-forming pathway, SeMet is converted to selenophosphate, which is ligated onto tRNA^Ser to form selenocysteine for insertion at UGA codons in [[Selenoprotein|selenoproteins]]. This is the only "intended" route.
3. **Catabolism to methylated metabolites** — selenocysteine is degraded by selenocysteine lyase to selenide, which is either methylated to trimethylselenonium and excreted, or resynthesised into selenophosphate.

Because SeMet sits in the large, constantly-turning-over methionine pool, it is **preferentially retained** over inorganic selenium forms and its body pool turns over slowly (the whole-body SeMet pool has a half-life on the order of a year or more in humans). This retention is the pharmacological rationale for its use in status assessment.

> [!info] Structural tolerance
> Some organisms tolerate very extensive SeMet substitution of methionine — the reason selenomethionine-labelled expression systems work at all. This is also why SeMet is described as a nearly "invisible" substitution: bulk protein function is usually preserved. What it is *not* invisible for is the trans-selenation route, because uncontrolled selenocysteine incorporation disrupts thiol chemistry.

## Biological roles

SeMet per se has no catalytic function. Its value is as a **reservoir**: it raises the body selenium pool, and the selenoproteins translated using that selenium are what actually do the biochemistry. Those are principally:

- **Glutathione peroxidases** (GPx1–4, GPx6) — reduce H₂O₂ and lipid hydroperoxides; GPx4 is the direct gatekeeper of [[Ferroptosis]] by reducing phospholipid hydroperoxides in membrane phospholipids.
- **Thioredoxin reductases** (TXNRD1–3) — regenerate reduced [[Thioredoxin]], feeding the peroxiredoxin/thioredoxin system and the reductive arm of the DNA damage response.
- **Iodothyronine deiodinases** (DIO1–3) — peripheral thyroid hormone conversion.
- Selenoprotein K, S, W, H and the deiodinases in other roles.

Because SeMet and methionine are handled in parallel, selenium status couples to methionine status and to the transsulfuration axis: methionine restriction itself raises the transsulfuration flux (via SAM depletion) that supplies cysteine for [[Glutathione]] synthesis, and selenium is needed to express glutathione peroxidases that consume the resulting peroxide.

## Toxicology

Chronic excess produces **selenosis**: garlic breath, metallic taste, hair loss, brittle or deformed nails, peripheral neuropathy, and in livestock the distinctive **alkali disease** (emaciation, alopecia, hoof deformation and joint erosion) that gave the condition its name. Acute high-dose exposure produces nausea, vomiting, and in severe cases pulmonary oedema.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> More selenium is not better. The U.S. Tolerable Upper Intake Level for adults is 400 µg/day of total selenium, and both the SELECT trial and the Cochrane review of 10 randomised trials (27,232 participants) found **no reduction in overall cancer incidence** (RR 1.01, 95% CI 0.93–1.10) and a slight increase in type 2 diabetes risk; in SELECT, 200 µg/day L-SeMet did not reduce prostate cancer risk (HR 1.09, 99% CI 0.93–1.27) and raised alopecia and dermatitis. The observational literature shows an inverse association between selenium status and cancer risk with **no dose-response relationship**, which is the classic signature of confounding rather than causation. SeMet is best thought of as a nutrient for correcting deficiency (and it is a genuinely effective treatment for Keshan disease), not as a cancer-prevention or longevity agent.

## Non-nutritional uses

Selenomethionine is the standard heavy-atom derivative for X-ray crystallography. Replacing the sulfur of surface methionines with selenium introduces a selenium edge for single- or multi-wavelength anomalous diffraction (SAD/MAD), solving the phase problem without needing isomorphous non-heavy-atom derivatives. It is a benign example of the same principle the vault's [[GlyNAC]] note exploits in the opposite direction — SeMet is used to *add* a handle to a protein, GlyNAC to *suppress* one.
