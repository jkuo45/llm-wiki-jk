# Silybin

**Silybin** (silibinin) is the principal bioactive flavonolignan of silymarin, the standardized extract of milk thistle (*Silybum marianum*) seeds. It is a mixture of diastereomers (A and B) characterized by a flavonolignan scaffold (taxifolin linked to coniferyl alcohol). Silybin has established hepatoprotective activity and is being investigated for anti-inflammatory, anti-fibrotic, and anti-cancer effects, with favorable safety and bioavailability profiles in humans.

## SIRT3-mediated nephroprotection

The Asteraceae-derived flavonoid silybin attenuates [[Cisplatin|cisplatin]]-induced nephrotoxicity by preserving renal tubular integrity and mitochondrial bioenergetics, likely via [[SIRT3]]-mediated mechanisms[372]. Cisplatin nephrotoxicity is driven by proximal tubular mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative injury; SIRT3-dependent preservation of mitochondrial function provides a mechanistic rationale for silybin's tubular protection, placing it among natural compounds that engage the mitochondrial deacetylase axis in kidney injury.

## Applications

- Research use: experimental model of cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury.
- Clinical context: candidate adjunct to chemotherapy regimens to limit nephrotoxicity; established use in liver disease.
