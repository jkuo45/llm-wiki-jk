# Taurine

**Taurine** (2-aminoethanesulfonic acid) is a sulfur-containing β-amino acid that is among the most abundant free amino acids in many tissues, particularly [[Skeletal Muscle]], heart, brain, and retina. It is conditionally essential: synthesized endogenously but requiring dietary intake under certain conditions. Although not incorporated into proteins, taurine serves multiple physiological roles — bile acid conjugation, osmoregulation, [[Calcium Signaling|calcium handling]], antioxidant defense, and support of [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] translation — and its tissue levels decline with age, linking it to aging biology and longevity research.

## Biosynthesis and Metabolism

- Synthesized from [[Cysteine]] via the transsulfuration pathway: cysteine dioxygenase (CDO) oxidizes cysteine to cysteine sulfinate, which is decarboxylated to **hypotaurine** and then oxidized to **taurine** ([[Cysteine]] note; [[Vitamin B6]]-dependent decarboxylation step).
- Obtained from diet (meat, fish, dairy) and, in carnivores such as the cat, dietary taurine is essential because endogenous synthesis is limited.
- Taurine is not metabolized for energy; it is excreted unchanged or conjugated, and its intracellular concentration is regulated by uptake through the taurine transporter (TauT).

## Physiological Functions

### Bile Acid Conjugation
Taurine conjugates bile acids (forming taurocholate and related tauro-conjugates), increasing their solubility and promoting lipid digestion and absorption.

### Osmoregulation and Calcium Handling
- Taurine acts as an organic osmolyte, protecting cells against osmotic stress.
- It modulates [[Calcium Signaling|calcium]] flux across membranes, stabilizing [[Cardiomyocyte|cardiomyocyte]] function and skeletal muscle contractility.

### Antioxidant Defense
- Taurine directly scavenges [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl), forming the more stable **taurine chloramine** — a relatively long-lived mediator that modulates [[Macrophages|macrophage]] function and limits oxidative tissue damage during [[Inflammation]].
- It also buffers hypochlorous acid generated by [[Myeloperoxidase]] in the [[Respiratory Burst]], protecting proteins, lipids, and DNA.

### Mitochondrial Translation
> [!info] Mitochondrial Mechanism
> Taurine is incorporated into the wobble position of certain mitochondrial tRNAs as **5-taurinomethyluridine (τm5U)**. This modification is required for accurate mitochondrial translation of codon families, and its absence underlies the tRNA-modification defects in [[MELAS]] (m.3243A>G).

- The taurine-modified wobble base ensures correct decoding during mitochondrial protein synthesis, supporting [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] and [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]]-encoded complex assembly.
- Deficiency of the τm5U modification in MELAS impairs mitochondrial translation, coupling taurine biochemistry to a major mitochondrial disease.

## Taurine and Aging

- **Age-related decline**: Circulating taurine concentrations decline with age across species, from mice and monkeys to humans.
- **Longevity effects**: In mice, chronic taurine supplementation extended healthspan and lifespan, improving muscle, bone, immune, and metabolic function (Singh et al., 2023, *Science*; PMID 37561875).
- **Mitochondrial fitness**: Taurine is mechanistically tied to mitochondrial resilience through its role in mitochondrial translation via tRNA modification; restoring taurine supports mitochondrial quality in aged stem cell contexts (Bautista & López-Cortés, 2026).
- Consistent with this, taurine status connects dietary sulfur-amino-acid metabolism (via [[Cysteine]] and [[N-Acetylcysteine]]-based regimens such as [[GlyNAC]]) to mitochondrial and systemic aging phenotypes.

## Clinical Relevance

- Taurine deficiency (e.g., in taurine-depleted diets, in cats) causes retinal degeneration, cardiomyopathy, and immune dysfunction; supplementation is therapeutic in these settings.
- Taurine supplementation is being studied for [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], heart failure, exercise performance, and sarcopenia, with generally favorable safety profiles.
