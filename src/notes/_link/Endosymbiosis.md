### Overview
Endosymbiosis is the leading theory explaining the origin of mitochondria: an ancestral eukaryotic cell engulfed an aerobic alphaproteobacterium, which became a permanent, inherited endosymbiont. This event gave rise to the mitochondrion.

### Evidence
Support for endosymbiosis includes the presence of [[Mitochondrial DNA]] as a circular genome, the double-membrane architecture of mitochondria ([[Outer Mitochondrial Membrane]] and [[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane]]), and the bacterial-style ribosomes and replication of mitochondria. The [[Mitochondrial Matrix]] retains metabolic pathways of prokaryotic origin such as the [[TCA Cycle]].

### Relevance to Dynamics
The conserved fusion/fission GTPases reflect this heritage: yeast [[Mgm1]] and Drosophila [[Fzo]] are ancient ancestors of mammalian [[OPA1]] and the mitofusins [[MFN1]]/[[MFN2]].
