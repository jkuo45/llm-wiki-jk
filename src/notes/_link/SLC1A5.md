# SLC1A5

**SLC1A5**, also called **ASCT2** (alanine-serine-cysteine transporter 2), is a plasma-membrane neutral amino acid transporter best known for importing glutamine. In [[Melanoma]], SLC1A5 is the direct target of [[miR-137]], which negatively regulates [[Ferroptosis]]; loss of miR-137 (and consequent SLC1A5 modulation) promotes ferroptotic death. SLC1A5 supports glutamine-dependent antioxidant metabolism that feeds [[Glutathione]] (GSH) synthesis and thus the [[GPX4]] defense.

## Structure & Biochemistry

SLC1A5 is a Na+-dependent exchanger of neutral amino acids (glutamine, alanine, serine, cysteine) belonging to the solute carrier family 1. It is frequently overexpressed in cancers to meet glutamine demand (glutamine addiction).

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

Glutamine imported by SLC1A5 fuels the TCA cycle and provides carbon/nitrogen for nucleotide and GSH synthesis. By sustaining GSH, SLC1A5 helps maintain [[GPX4]] activity and suppresses [[Lipid Peroxidation]]. The review places SLC1A5 downstream of miR-137 in melanoma ferroptosis regulation: miR-137 directly acts on SLC1A5, and its knockdown promotes ferroptosis (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Melanoma"). The glutamine–GSH axis is therefore a post-transcriptionally tuned ferroptosis vulnerability.

> [!info] Glutamine as a ferroptosis determinant
> Glutamine depletion triggers ferroptosis in several models (including ccRCC). SLC1A5 is the import gateway—making it a node where amino-acid transport meets iron-dependent cell death.

## Clinical Relevance

SLC1A5/ASCT2 is a cancer metabolism target; inhibiting it may both starve tumors and sensitize them to ferroptosis. Its regulation by miR-137 adds a miRNA layer exploitable in melanoma therapy.
