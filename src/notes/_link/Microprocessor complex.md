# Microprocessor complex

The **Microprocessor complex** is the nuclear ribonucleoprotein machine that initiates canonical [[MicroRNA|microRNA]] biogenesis. It is composed of the [[Ribonuclease III|RNase III]] enzyme **[[Drosha]]** and the double-stranded [[RNA-Binding Proteins|RNA-binding protein]] **[[DGCR8]]**, which together cleave primary miRNA transcripts (pri-miRNAs) into ~60–70 nt precursor hairpins (pre-miRNAs).

## Composition and Architecture

- One **[[Drosha]]** molecule associates with two **[[DGCR8]]** molecules (a heme-bound dimer) to form the functional complex.
- [[Drosha]] provides the catalytic RNase III cleavage center (two RNase III domains, RIIIDa/RIIIDb).
- [[DGCR8]] provides substrate recognition via two tandem [[Double-stranded RNA-binding domain|dsRNA-binding domains (dsRBDs)]] and a heme cofactor (Rhed domain); its WW motif binds the proline-rich N-terminus of [[Drosha]].

## Mechanism

> [!info] Cleavage geometry
> [[DGCR8]] binds the apical single-stranded/double-stranded RNA junction of the pri-miRNA hairpin and acts as a molecular ruler, positioning [[Drosha]]'s catalytic center ~11 bp from the basal junction. Drosha makes a staggered cut yielding a 2-nt 3′ overhang.

1. [[RNA Polymerase II|RNA Pol II]] transcribes a pri-miRNA hairpin.
2. **[[DGCR8]]** recognizes and anchors the substrate; heme binding licenses activity.
3. **[[Drosha]]** cleaves the stem, releasing **pre-miRNA**.
4. Pre-miRNA is exported by **[[Exportin-5]]** and matured cytoplasmically by **[[Dicer]]** and **[[Argonaute]]** in the [[RISC]].

## Biological and Pathological Significance

The Microprocessor is the rate-limiting gatekeeper of the entire [[MicroRNA]] program. Because miRNAs post-transcriptionally repress hundreds of targets — including senescence and inflammatory regulators — Microprocessor fidelity couples nuclear RNA processing to [[Epigenetic Alterations|epigenetic]] and inflammatory outcomes.

> [!warning] Link to inflammaging (adrenochrome trace)
> Electrophilic quinones such as [[Adrenochrome]] may covalently modify the cysteine-rich [[DGCR8]]/[[Drosha]] Microprocessor, potentially altering pri-miRNA processing rates. Microprocessor dysfunction globally remodels the miRNA-based epigenetic network, unleashing pro-inflammatory and [[Senescence|senescence]]-associated gene programs that feed [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]] (see [[Adrenochrome → Inflammaging Trace]]). Conversely, elevated [[Drosha]] in disease macrophages can drive [[AIM2]] inflammasome activation and lung inflammation in [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis|IPF]].
