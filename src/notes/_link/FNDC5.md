# FNDC5

**FNDC5** (fibronectin type III domain-containing protein 5) is a type I transmembrane protein that serves as the precursor for the myokine [[Irisin]]. First identified in 2002 as a peroxisomal protein linked to myoblast differentiation (Mouse PeP/Frcp1), FNDC5 was virtually ignored until 2012, when Böström et al. demonstrated that its extracellular domain is proteolytically cleaved during exercise and secreted as irisin, driving browning of white adipose tissue and thermogenesis (Böström et al. 2012, *Nature* 481:463-468). Over 2,000 subsequent studies have explored the FNDC5–irisin axis in metabolism, bone, brain, and cardiovascular biology.

## Gene and Expression

- **Human gene:** *FNDC5* (chromosome 1p35.2)
- **Mouse gene:** *Fndc5* (chromosome 1)
- **Tissue expression:** skeletal muscle (highest), heart, brain, adipose tissue, liver, kidney
- **Regulation:** transcriptionally activated by [[PGC-1α]] (via [[ERRalpha]] and PPARγ coactivation), induced by exercise, cold exposure, [[AMPK]] activation, and [[p38 MAPK]] signaling. Myostatin knockout increases FNDC5 via AMPK–PGC-1α–FNDC5 pathway.

> [!info] Tissue expression pattern
> Although FNDC5 was initially characterized as a muscle-specific gene, subsequent studies confirmed its expression in the heart, brain (particularly hippocampus), and adipose tissue. In the brain, hippocampal FNDC5/irisin drives local [[BDNF]] expression. FNDC5 is also expressed in adipose tissue, making irisin both a myokine and an adipokine.

## Protein Structure

FNDC5 is a 209-amino acid type I transmembrane protein comprising:

| Domain | Residues (mouse) | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Signal peptide | 1–28 | ER translocation; co-translationally cleaved |
| FNIII domain (irisin) | 29–140 | The secreted domain; fibronectin type III fold |
| Linker | 141–169 | Flexible tether between ectodomain and TM |
| Transmembrane segment | 170–188 | Membrane anchorage |
| Cytoplasmic tail | 189–209 | Intracellular signaling (poorly characterized) |

The FNIII domain adopts a β-sandwich fold with two antiparallel β-sheets (ABE and CDE faces). The irisin dimer interface is thought to involve strand A of the β-sandwich. Two N-linked glycosylation sites in the ectodomain increase the apparent molecular weight from ~13 kDa (unglycosylated) to ~20 kDa (glycosylated) on SDS-PAGE, with broader smears (18–25 kDa) reflecting heterogeneous glycan structures.

## Proteolytic Processing

The mechanism by which the FNDC5 ectodomain is cleaved to release irisin remains incompletely characterized:

- **Proposed sheddase:** Cathepsin B has been suggested, but the identity of the protease is not definitively established
- **Cleavage site:** the linker region between the FNIII domain and the transmembrane segment (between aa 140 and 141) is the presumed cut site
- **Regulation of cleavage:** exercise and PGC-1α activation increase both FNDC5 transcription and irisin secretion; the rate-limiting step may differ between tissues
- **Co-secretion with eHsp90α:** exercise-induced co-secretion of extracellular heat shock protein 90α (eHsp90α) is required for irisin's optimal binding to its receptor integrin αVβ5 (Liu et al. 2023)

## The Start Codon Controversy

The human *FNDC5* gene carries an ATA (isoleucine) at the annotated translation start codon, rather than the canonical ATG (methionine) found in all other mammalian species examined. This raised the possibility that human FNDC5 might not be efficiently translated:

- **Raschke et al. (2013):** showed that ATA-initiated translation in HEK293 cells produced only ~1% of the protein yield compared to ATG-initiated mouse FNDC5, suggesting human FNDC5 is essentially a pseudogene
- **Albrecht et al. (2015):** concluded that human FNDC5 does not produce full-length irisin and that commercial ELISA data are artifacts of antibody cross-reactivity
- **Jedrychowski et al. (2015):** used targeted mass spectrometry (AQUA peptides) to detect irisin peptides in human plasma (~3.6 ng/mL sedentary, ~4.3 ng/mL exercised), arguing that translation from the ATA start does occur
- **Kurgan et al. (2024):** resolved the controversy by identifying a conserved upstream ATG (cATG) supported by ribosome profiling. Translation initiates primarily at this cATG in both humans and mice, with the N-terminal extension co-translationally cleaved by the signal peptidase. This produces a mature FNDC5 protein of the expected size.

> [!tip] Current understanding
> The cATG discovery effectively resolves the "human pseudogene" concern. FNDC5/irisin is a genuine human protein. However, quantitative differences in translational efficiency between species may exist and warrant further study.

## Biological Functions (via Irisin)

FNDC5's primary characterized function is as the precursor for [[Irisin]], which mediates:
- Browning of white adipose tissue via [[UCP1]] induction
- Improved glucose uptake and insulin sensitivity
- Bone formation (osteoblast promotion; complex effects on osteoclasts)
- [[BDNF]] upregulation in the hippocampus and neuroprotection
- Endothelial function via NO production
- SIRT3-dependent antioxidant and osteoprotective effects in diabetic bone disease

FNDC5 may also have intrinsic signaling functions independent of irisin cleavage, but these remain poorly characterized.

## Genetic Models

### FNDC5 Knockout Mice
Global *Fndc5* knockout mice (Xiong et al. 2019) are viable, with normal muscle development and basal metabolism. However, they show:
- Attenuated exercise-induced browning of inguinal white adipose tissue
- Reduced metabolic benefits of exercise on a high-fat diet
- No gross developmental abnormalities, suggesting FNDC5/irisin is dispensable for basal homeostasis but essential for exercise-induced metabolic adaptation

### FNDC5 Transgenic Mice
Muscle-specific overexpression of *Fndc5* (using the Mck promoter) results in:
- Chronically elevated circulating irisin
- Reduced bone mass at multiple ages, with enhanced in vitro osteoclastogenesis (Giro et al. 2020)
- Improved metabolic parameters on high-fat diets

## Therapeutic Relevance

- **Exercise mimetic target:** pharmacological activation of FNDC5 could recapitulate exercise benefits in individuals unable to exercise
- **NAD+ precursors:** [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide]] and [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] upregulate FNDC5 expression, potentially mediating metabolic benefits of NAD+ boosting
- **GLP-1 agonists:** exenatide increases circulating irisin in T2DM patients
- **Metformin:** upregulates FNDC5/irisin in skeletal muscle via AMPK activation
- **Bone disease:** exogenous irisin prevents bone loss in osteoporosis models
- **Neurodegeneration:** FNDC5-derived irisin enhances BDNF and Aβ clearance in Alzheimer's models
