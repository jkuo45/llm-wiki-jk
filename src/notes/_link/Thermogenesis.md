# Thermogenesis

**Thermogenesis** is the production of heat by an organism. It is a component of total [[Energy Expenditure|energy expenditure]] and comprises several physiologically distinct fractions:

- **Basal/resting thermogenesis** — heat from resting metabolic activity of organs and muscle, the dominant fraction of [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]].
- **Thermic effect of food** (diet-induced thermogenesis) — heat generated during digestion and postprandial metabolism; scales with food intake and protein content.
- **Shivering thermogenesis** — rapid heat production by skeletal muscle contraction during cold exposure.
- **Non-shivering thermogenesis** — heat generated largely by [[Brown Adipose Tissue|brown adipose tissue]] through [[UCP1]]-mediated [[Mitochondrial Uncoupling|mitochondrial uncoupling]], and by futile substrate cycling.

## Determinants

- **[[Mitochondrial Uncoupling]]** — proton leak and uncoupling proteins ([[UCP1]], [[UCP2]], [[UCP3]]) dissipate the proton gradient as heat, uncoupling oxygen consumption from [[ATP]] synthesis (Rolfe & Brand, 1996).
- **Thyroid status** — [[Triiodothyronine]] up-regulates thermogenic processes; thyroid hormone resistance alters metabolic rate (Freake & Oppenheimer, 1995; Brown _et al_. 2000).
- **Physical activity** — exercise generates heat acutely, and training can raise resting thermogenesis via lean-mass accretion and [[Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption|EPOC]].

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review treats thermogenesis in the context of the daily energy budget: the heat increment of feeding is closely tied to food intake (and influenced by dietary protein), while residual RMR variance correlates with thermogenic determinants such as mitochondrial proton leak, uncoupling-protein polymorphisms, and thyroid hormone levels.

## Clinical Relevance

Adaptive thermogenesis defends body weight during caloric restriction and opposes weight-loss maintenance, contributing to the [[Obesity|obesity]] epidemic's persistence. Pharmacological approaches historically targeted thermogenesis (e.g., β3-adrenergic and uncoupling strategies) for weight management, with limited success due to side effects.
