# KDM5C

[[KDM5C]] (Lysine Demethylase 5C, also called JARID1C or SMCX) is an X-linked histone demethylase that catalyzes the removal of trimethyl and dimethyl marks from histone H3 lysine 4 (H3K4me3/H3K4me2). KDM5C regulates gene expression by modulating the balance between active and repressive chromatin states. It is one of six X-linked genes identified as **EXITS** (Escape from X-Inactivation Tumor Suppressors), with loss-of-function mutations enriched in male tumors (Dunford et al., *Nature Genetics* 2017).

## Structure & Domains

KDM5C (~170 kDa) contains:

- **JmjC catalytic domain**: Fe(II)- and α-ketoglutarate-dependent oxygenase responsible for demethylating H3K4me3 → H3K4me2 → H3K4me1.
- **JmjN domain**: N-terminal domain required for catalytic activity.
- **ARID/BRIGHT DNA-binding domain**: Binds AT-rich DNA sequences, targeting KDM5C to specific genomic loci.
- **PHD1 and PHD3 zinc fingers**: Plant homeodomain fingers that recognize methylated histone tails; PHD3 specifically reads H3K4me3, creating an auto-inhibitory feedback loop.
- **C5HC2 zinc finger**: Unique to the KDM5 family; role in protein interactions.

## Function

KDM5C removes the activating trimethyl mark from H3K4, thereby repressing transcription at target genes:

- **Developmental gene regulation**: KDM5C demethylates H3K4me3 at developmental promoters, fine-tuning the transition between pluripotency and lineage commitment.
- **X-chromosome inactivation**: KDM5C is involved in maintaining the inactive X chromosome through H3K4 demethylation.
- **Neuronal gene expression**: KDM5C regulates neuronal differentiation genes; loss-of-function mutations cause X-linked intellectual disability (XLMR/IDD).
- **DNA damage response**: KDM5C is recruited to DNA double-strand breaks where it modulates local H3K4 methylation to facilitate repair.

## Role in Cancer

KDM5C acts as a tumor suppressor through its role in epigenetic regulation:

- Loss-of-function mutations in KDM5C have been identified in renal cell carcinoma, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, melanoma, and other tumor types.
- KDM5C loss leads to aberrant H3K4me3 accumulation at promoters of oncogenes and developmental genes, de-repressing proliferative programs.
- KDM5C interacts with the [[p53]] pathway: KDM5C demethylates H3K4 at p53 target gene promoters, facilitating p53-dependent transcription of cell cycle arrest genes like [[p21 CIP1|p21]].

> [!info]
> KDM5C is the H3K4 demethylase counterpart of [[KDM6A]] (H3K27me3 demethylase). Both are X-linked EXITS genes, and together they regulate the balance between active (H3K4me3) and repressive (H3K27me3) chromatin states — a balance critical for tumor suppression.

### EXITS Framework

KDM5C is one of six X-linked genes (with [[ATRX]], [[CNKSR2]], [[DDX3X]], [[KDM6A]], [[MAGEC3]], see [[EXITS]]) preferentially mutated in male tumors. Across **21 tumor types**, these six X-linked genes harbor loss-of-function mutations significantly more often in males — a sex-biased pattern not seen in any of the **18,053 autosomal genes** examined (Dunford et al., *Nature Genetics* 2017). Biallelic expression in females provides functional redundancy (two hits required); males, with a single X chromosome, are vulnerable to single-hit inactivation — **unless** the Y-homolog backup is also disabled.

#### The Y-Chromosome Homolog Corollary

KDM5C has a Y-chromosome homolog that can partially compensate for its loss in males. This adds a male-specific "second hit" pathway: **mosaic loss of chromosome Y** (common with aging and smoking) removes the Y backup, so male tumors with LOF mutations in EXITS genes with Y homologs (KDM6A, DDX3X, KDM5C) showed a **trend toward chrY loss** (10.2% vs 5.8%, P = 0.077). The Dunford study found **X-X female compensation is stronger than X-Y male compensation** (female 36% chrX loss vs male 8.2% chrY loss among mutated tumors, **P = 0.0022**), indicating the Y homolog is not a fully equivalent tumor suppressor — preserving a net female advantage.

## Sex Differences

> [!tip]
> KDM5C is an X-linked epigenetic regulator with strong sex-biased mutation enrichment in male tumors.

- KDM5C escapes X-inactivation in females, meaning both X chromosomes contribute functional KDM5C protein.
- Males, with only one X, require only one loss-of-function event to eliminate KDM5C activity.
- KDM5C loss leads to hyperactivation of H3K4me3-dependent oncogenic programs, contributing to the male cancer predisposition.
- KDM5C is implicated in **X-linked intellectual disability** (Siderius syndrome), demonstrating the importance of KDM5C dosage for normal development.
- KDM5C regulates genes involved in [[Senescence|senescence]] and [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]] through its control of H3K4 methylation at the [[CDKN2A]] (p16^INK4a^) locus.
