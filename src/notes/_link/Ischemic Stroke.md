# Ischemic Stroke

[[Ischemic Stroke]] is acute brain injury caused by focal arterial occlusion — typically thromboembolic — that deprives downstream tissue of oxygen and glucose. It accounts for roughly 85% of all strokes, the remainder being hemorrhagic.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

- **Occlusion mechanisms:** large-artery atherosclerosis, cardioembolism (e.g. atrial fibrillation), small-vessel (lacunar) disease, and cryptogenic causes.
- **Ischemic core vs. penumbra:** the core suffers rapid necrosis from ATP depletion and loss of ion gradients, while the surrounding penumbra is hypoperfused but potentially salvageable — the target of reperfusion therapy.
- **Injury cascade:** [[Hypoxia]] collapses [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]], triggering [[Excitotoxicity]] (glutamate-driven [[Calcium Ions|calcium]] influx), [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], and a surge of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]]. [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] adds a secondary oxidative burst upon restored blood flow.

## Clinical Presentation & Management

- Sudden focal deficits (hemiparesis, aphasia, visual loss); diagnosis relies on non-contrast CT/MRI to exclude hemorrhage.
- Standard care: IV thrombolysis (tPA) within the therapeutic window and mechanical thrombectomy for large-vessel occlusion.

## Investigational Neuroprotection

> [!info] Methylene blue rationale
> [[Methylene blue]] can act as an alternative electron carrier that bypasses damaged [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain]] complexes, sustaining ATP production and lowering [[Mitochondrial Superoxide]]. This has motivated its study as a neuroprotectant across stroke, [[Traumatic Brain Injury]], and neurodegeneration.
