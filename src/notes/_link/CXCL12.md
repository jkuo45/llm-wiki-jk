# CXCL12 (SDF-1)

CXCL12 (C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 12), also known as SDF-1 (stromal cell-derived factor 1), is a constitutively expressed ELR− C-X-C chemokine that binds [[CXCR4]] (its principal receptor) and [[CXCR7]] (an atypical scavenger receptor). It is a homeostatic chemokine central to stem-cell homing and lymphocyte trafficking, and as a SASP factor it cooperates with matrix metalloproteinases to facilitate cancer metastasis (Coppé et al., 2010, PMID 20723158).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Receptor binding**: CXCL12 is the sole canonical ligand of CXCR4 and also engages CXCR7. CXCR4 is a Gαi-coupled receptor activating PI3K/Akt, [[p38 MAPK]]/ERK, and Rho GTPases that drive actin remodeling and migration.
- **Cell sources**: Broadly produced by stromal, endothelial, and epithelial cells; in senescence it is secreted by senescent cells within the tumor microenvironment and at sites of wound repair.
- **Transcriptional regulation**: Strongly induced by hypoxia via [[HIF-1α]] and by NF-κB; its gradient is shaped by proteolytic cleavage from extracellular matrix by MMPs (e.g., [[MMP-2]], [[MMP-9]], MMP-12), which both release and inactivate the chemokine.
- **Downstream cellular effects**: Directs chemotaxis and homing of CXCR4+ hematopoietic stem cells, lymphocytes, and cancer cells; promotes survival, adhesion, and invasion.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- CXCL12 is a pro-tumorigenic SASP factor that remodels the tumor microenvironment: senescent stromal and cancer cells release CXCL12 that, together with MMPs, establishes chemotactic gradients guiding CXCR4+ cancer cells to metastatic niches.
- Its cooperation with MMPs facilitates extravasation and colonization, linking the SASP to organ-specific metastasis.
- Because CXCL12 is also a physiological homing signal, its SASP-driven overexpression blurs tissue-repair and pro-metastatic functions.

## Role in Vascular Aging and Endothelial Dysfunction

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Senolytic Treatment With Fisetin Reverses Age‐Related Endothelial Dysfunction Partially Mediated by SASP Factor CXCL12|Mahoney et al. 2025]]
> [[CXCL12]] was identified as the most highly upregulated [[SASP]] factor in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells (ECs)]] with aging, and its circulating protein levels were elevated in old mice and normalized by [[Senolytic|senolytic treatment]] with [[Fisetin]]. The study established a causal role for [[CXCL12]] in age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]]:

- **Endothelial function**: CXCL12 add-back to old fisetin plasma recapitulated age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] (−22% peak [[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation|EDD]]); CXCL12 inhibition with [[LIT-927]] rescued function
- **Humoral senescence induction**: Exogenous CXCL12 increased SA-β-Gal, *[[p21 CIP1|Cdkn1a]]* (100%), and *[[p16 INK4a|Cdkn2a]]* (128%) in [[Human Aortic Endothelial Cells|HAECs]]; LIT-927 reduced these markers
- **NO production**: CXCL12 impaired NO production (−13–20%); LIT-927 restored NO (+10%)
- **Mitochondrial ROS**: CXCL12 increased mitochondrial superoxide (2.2-fold in arteries); LIT-927 reduced it (−10%)
- **Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition (EndoMT)**: CXCL12 reduced endothelial markers ([[CDH5]] −45%, [[PECAM1]] −55%) and induced mesenchymal markers ([[TGFB1]] 5.4-fold, [[ACTA2]] 5.4-fold)

[[CXCL12]] signals through two receptors in this context:
- **[[CXCR4]]**: Expression increased in senescent ECs; *Cxcl12-Cxcr4* interactions decreased with aging (defective vascular communication)
- **[[ACKR3]]** (CXCR7): Expression decreased in senescent ECs; *Cxcl12-Ackr3* interactions increased with aging (autocrine and immune signaling)

> [!important] CXCL12 as a SASP Therapeutic Target
> These findings establish circulating [[CXCL12]] as a causal driver of age-related [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] and identify it as a key mechanism by which [[Senolytic|senolytic]] [[Fisetin]] improves vascular function with aging. CXCL12 neutralization ([[LIT-927]]) may represent a [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] strategy for [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular aging]].

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- The CXCL12/CXCR4 axis is a validated therapeutic target (e.g., plerixafor/AMD3100 mobilizes stem cells; CXCR4 antagonists tested in cancer and WHIM syndrome).
- Elevated CXCL12 correlates with metastatic potential and poor prognosis in breast, lung, and other cancers.
- CXCL12 is a causal driver of age-related endothelial dysfunction and vascular aging.
- It is also implicated in HIV entry (CXCR4 co-receptor), cardioprotection, and tissue regeneration.
