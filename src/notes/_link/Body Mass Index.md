# Body Mass Index

**Body Mass Index (BMI)** is the ratio of body weight (kg) to height squared (m²). It is the standard population-level screening index for weight status: < 18·5 underweight, 18·5–24·9 normal, 25·0–29·9 overweight, and ≥ 30·0 [[Obesity|obese]]. BMI does not distinguish fat from [[Fat-Free Mass|lean mass]], so it can misclassify muscular individuals, but it correlates strongly with adiposity at the population level and with cardiometabolic risk.

## Determinants and Correlates

- Sedentary behaviour is a strong cross-sectional correlate: numerous studies link hours of television viewing to higher BMI in children and adults (Tucker & Bagwell, 1991; Vioque _et al_. 2000; Janz _et al_. 2002; Lowry _et al_. 2002).
- Longitudinal data show 1-year changes in physical activity and inactivity correlate with BMI change in 11 887 adolescents (Berkey _et al_. 2003).
- Causal evidence comes from intervention studies: enforced reduction in television viewing reduced BMI in children (Gortmaker _et al_. 1999).

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review uses BMI as the outcome measure in the epidemiological evidence connecting declining physical activity and increasing sedentary screen time to the obesity epidemic.

## Limitations

- Fails to distinguish fat from lean mass and gives no information on fat distribution.
- Ethnic differences in body composition mean fixed cut-offs are imperfect; WHO and regional bodies have proposed ethnic-specific thresholds.
