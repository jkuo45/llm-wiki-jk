# Ifosfamide

**Chemical properties and classification:** [[Ifosfamide]] is a bifunctional nitrogen-mustard [[Chemotherapy|alkylating antineoplastic]] agent structurally related to cyclophosphamide. It is administered as a prodrug requiring hepatic activation.

**Pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics:** Hepatic cytochrome P450 enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP2B6) oxidize ifosfamide to the active ifosfamide mustard, which forms DNA interstrand crosslinks, and to chloroacetaldehyde, a nephro- and neurotoxic byproduct. The inactive metabolite urothelial toxin is detoxified by mesna to prevent hemorrhagic cystitis.

**Applications:** Ifosfamide is used against sarcomas, lymphomas, and certain carcinomas, often in combination regimens.

**Toxicity and antidote:** A well-recognized adverse effect is ifosfamide-induced encephalopathy—confusion, hallucinations, and coma—attributed to chloroacetaldehyde. This neurotoxicity is effectively reversed by [[Methylene blue]] (intravenous 50 mg every 4–6 h until resolution), which restores mitochondrial electron transport inhibited by the toxic metabolite. Prophylactic methylene blue is used in high-risk patients.
