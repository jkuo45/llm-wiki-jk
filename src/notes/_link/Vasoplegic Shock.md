# Vasoplegic Shock

[[Vasoplegic Shock]] (vasoplegia) is a state of refractory [[Hypotension]] driven by excessive [[Vasodilation]], most often encountered after [[Cardiopulmonary Bypass]] (cardiac surgery), and in [[Septic Shock]] or anaphylactic shock. It is characterized by low systemic vascular resistance unresponsive to standard [[Vasopressors]] such as [[Norepinephrine]].

## Methylene Blue as Rescue Therapy

[[Methylene blue]] is used off-label to restore vascular tone when conventional vasopressors fail. Typical adult dosing is an IV bolus of **2 mg/kg over 20–30 min**, sometimes followed by a continuous infusion.

Mechanistically, methylene blue inhibits [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] and [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase]], blocking the NO→[[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate]] vasodilation cascade and attenuating cytokine-mediated vasodilation.

> [!note] Evidence status
> Methylene blue improves blood pressure in refractory vasoplegia, but evidence for routine prophylactic use to reduce mortality is limited; it is not standard of care.
