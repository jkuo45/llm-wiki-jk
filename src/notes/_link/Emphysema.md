# Emphysema

**Etiology and pathophysiology:** Emphysema is a subtype of [[Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease]] (COPD) defined by permanent enlargement of the airspaces distal to the terminal bronchioles, with destruction of their walls and no obvious fibrosis. Cigarette smoking is the dominant risk factor, though occupational dusts, air pollution, and genetic deficiency of [[Alpha-1 Antitrypsin|α1-antitrypsin]] also predispose. The central mechanism is a protease–antiprotease imbalance: cigarette smoke recruits [[Neutrophils]] and [[Macrophages]] that release elastase and other proteases overwhelming local antiproteases, degrading elastin in the alveolar interstitium. Concomitant [[Oxidative Stress]] inactivates antiproteases and amplifies the inflammatory cascade. Loss of elastic recoil and airway collapse during expiration produce airflow limitation and air trapping.

**Clinical presentation and biomarkers:** Patients present with progressive exertional dyspnea, chronic cough, and reduced exercise tolerance. Spirometry shows reduced FEV1/FVC ratio; diffusion capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO) is characteristically low because of lost alveolar-capillary surface. Imaging (CT) reveals panacinar (α1-antitrypsin deficiency) or centriacinar (smoking) emphysema.

**Therapeutic landscape:** Management centers on smoking cessation, bronchodilators (long-acting β2-agonists, anticholinergics), inhaled corticosteroids, supplemental oxygen, and pulmonary rehabilitation. Severe cases may benefit from lung-volume-reduction surgery or transplantation. Age-related decline in repair capacity and persistent low-grade [[Inflammation]] (inflammaging) worsen prognosis, and sirtuin pathways have been implicated in experimental protection against alveolar destruction.
