# Putrescine

**Putrescine** (1,4-diaminobutane, tetramethylenediamine) is the smallest of the
mammalian polyamines and the direct product of ornithine decarboxylase (ODC). It is
the obligatory substrate for spermidine synthase, so essentially all putrescine
consumed in a mammalian cell is channeled into [[Spermidine]] synthesis; the
alternative fates are acetylation and oxidation. Unlike the higher polyamines,
putrescine is not an efficient autophagy inducer — the geroprotective effect
attributed to polyamines is carried mainly by [[Spermidine]] and [[Spermine]].

## Biosynthesis and position in the pathway

Ornithine comes from dietary arginine via intestinal arginase and from the urea cycle.
ODC then removes its carboxyl group in a pyridoxal phosphate-dependent reaction. This
is the rate-limiting step of polyamine synthesis and the most tightly regulated one.
**Putrescine is not produced de novo by any other established mammalian route** — a
mammalian arginine decarboxylase has been proposed but the evidence is contested,
unlike the plant and microbial version.

The aminopropyl group needed for the next step comes from decarboxylated
S-adenosylmethionine (dcSAM) produced by AMD1, and the downstream enzymes are
spermidine synthase (SRM) and spermine synthase (SMS).

> [!info] Mechanism
> Putrescine is not merely passive substrate. It activates AMD1 by enhancing the
> production and catalytic activity of the processed enzyme, and it acts
> post-translationally on ODC itself, increasing processing of the proenzyme and
> shortening ODC half-life. The same molecule that ODC produces therefore feeds
> back to shut down its own synthase — a rate-limiting enzyme that is regulated by
> its own product.

## Regulation of ODC by antizyme

Polyamine homeostasis is governed principally by two proteins, antizyme and
spermidine/spermine N1-acetyltransferase (SSAT), and the vault's [[Polyamine]] note
covers the system; the putrescine-specific point is that the regulation is
polyamine-concentration-dependent and putrescine participates in it:

- **Antizyme** is synthesised by a +1 translational frameshift at a
  polyamine-stimulated stop codon, fusing a short ORF1 to ORF2. Antizyme binds the
  ODC monomer, and the ODC–antizyme complex is a more efficient ubiquitin ligase
  substrate for degradation. High polyamines therefore raise antizyme synthesis,
  inhibit antizyme degradation, and shorten ODC half-life. Spermidine and spermine
  are more effective than putrescine at stimulating antizyme frameshifting.
- **MYC** is a transcriptional activator of ODC in both normal and neoplastic cells,
  which is the mechanism by which polyamine synthesis becomes growth-factor-driven in
  cancer.

## Catabolic fates

Putrescine is not acetylated directly by SSAT in the canonical route. It is instead
generated *downstream* of oxidation: SSAT acetylates spermidine to
N1-acetylspermidine, and acetylpolyamine oxidase (PAOX) then cleaves that to
putrescine, 3-acetylaminopropanal and hydrogen peroxide. Putrescine is thus the
endpoint of the SSAT/PAOX catabolic pathway — which is why polyamine catabolism is
a hydrogen peroxide source, and why catabolism has opposite effects on proliferation
and on oxidative stress.

## Research and pharmacological relevance

- **α-Difluoromethylornithine (DFMO, eflornithine)** irreversibly inhibits ODC and
  blocks putrescine, and therefore all polyamine synthesis, upstream. It is an
  approved treatment for African trypanosomiasis and is used or studied in
  chemoprevention of colorectal cancer.
- **α-Difluoromethylputrescine** is the putrescine analogue that inhibits
  spermidine synthase rather than ODC — a way of blocking the step *downstream* of
  putrescine.
- **Higher polyamine pools raise putrescine.** In mice overexpressing SSAT,
  acetylation-oxidation flux rises, putrescine rises while spermidine and spermine
  fall, antioxidant enzyme activity falls, protein carbonyl content rises tenfold,
  and lifespan falls by about 50% with sustained p53 elevation. This is the clearest
  demonstration that putrescine abundance tracks *catabolic flux* rather than
  polyamine supplementation, and that forced polyamine catabolism is pro-oxidant.
  It also means that elevated tissue putrescine is a marker of polyamine
  catabolism, not of polyamine availability.

> [!warning] Evidence caveat
> The putrescine-specific geroprotection literature is thin. Spermidine has
  extensive lifespan data in yeast, worms, flies and mice; putrescine does not. A
> 1992 study found that putrescine replacement did not restore the polyamine deficit
  or its consequences in regenerating rat liver. The mechanistic claim that putrescine
  is an autophagy inducer on the same footing as spermidine is not well supported.

## Connections

- [[Polyamine]] — putrescine is one of the three mammalian polyamines and the
  metabolic node from which the other two are built; the pathway logic, catabolism and
  cancer framing in that note are the context for everything here.
- [[Spermidine]] — putrescine is the aminopropyl acceptor in spermidine synthase, so
  the entire spermidine pool, and therefore most of the polyamine autophagy and
  geroprotection literature, depends on putrescine supply.
- [[Spermine]] — one aminopropyl transfer downstream of spermidine; the three
  polyamines interconvert via spermine oxidase and the acetyl/oxidation route, which
  returns the catabolic product to putrescine.
- [[Ornithine Decarboxylase]] — the enzyme that makes putrescine and the
  rate-limiting control point of the whole pathway, itself regulated by MYC and by
  polyamine-triggered antizyme frameshifting.
- [[Cancer]] — MYC-driven ODC overexpression is one of the most consistent
  metabolic programmes in tumours, and it is the rationale for ODC inhibition in
  chemoprevention.
- [[Autophagy]] — polyamines as a class induce autophagy and extend lifespan, but the
  evidence base is concentrated on spermidine rather than putrescine.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — the acetyl/oxidation catabolic route generates
  hydrogen peroxide, so increased putrescine is a marker of the oxidative arm of
  polyamine turnover.
