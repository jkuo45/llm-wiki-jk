# Matrine

**Matrine** is a quinolizidine alkaloid isolated from *Sophora flavescens* (kushen), a traditional Chinese medicinal herb. It possesses broad pharmacological activities, including anti-inflammatory, antiviral, anti-arrhythmic, anti-fibrotic, and antitumor effects. Matrine and its derivatives are used clinically in China for hepatitis and cardiac arrhythmias, and are under investigation for renal and cardiovascular protection.

## SIRT3-dependent protection

Matrine engages protective effects against cisplatin-induced [[Acute Kidney Injury|acute kidney injury (AKI)]] by modulating mitochondrial membrane dynamics through the [[SIRT3]]/[[OPA1]] pathway, combining antioxidant and anti-inflammatory mechanisms to preserve renal function[373]. Beyond nephroprotection, matrine confers cardiovascular benefits by enhancing the SIRT3/[[AMPK]] pathway, preserving [[Cardiomyocytes|cardiomyocyte]] viability and limiting cell death in murine models[374]. Both protective programs converge on SIRT3-mediated mitochondrial quality control — mitochondrial fusion/dynamics via OPA1 in the kidney, and energetic stress adaptation via AMPK in the heart.

## Applications

- Research use: experimental models of cisplatin-induced AKI and ischemic/metabolic cardiac injury.
- Clinical context: established use as an anti-arrhythmic/antiviral agent; candidate for cardiorenal protection.
