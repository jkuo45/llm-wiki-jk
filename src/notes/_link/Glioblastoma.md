# Glioblastoma

**Glioblastoma** (GBM) is the most common and most malignant primary brain tumor in adults, classified as a grade IV astrocytoma. Despite aggressive treatment (surgical resection, radiotherapy, temozolomide chemotherapy), prognosis remains poor with median survival under 15 months.

## Acid Ceramidase Connection

> [!info] Source: Doan et al., 2017, *Oncotarget* (PMID: 29348854)
> [[Acid ceramidase|ASAH1]] is an independent poor-prognosis marker in glioblastoma. It is highly expressed in CD133+ glioblastoma stem-like cells (GSCs) and is secreted into the microenvironment. Pharmacological ACase inhibition with [[Carmofur]] kills GSCs with IC50 11–104 µM, compared to temozolomide IC50 >750 µM — a >50-fold therapeutic differential.

## Ivermectin & Repurposed Anthelmintics

[[Ivermectin]] inhibits glioblastoma cell proliferation at micromolar concentrations and reduces glioblastoma stem-like cell (GSC) viability, with demonstrated ability to cross the blood–brain barrier — a key advantage given the poor CNS penetration of standard therapies. The benzimidazole anthelmintic [[Mebendazole]] also shows documented preclinical and early clinical activity against glioblastoma, acting through β-[[Tubulin]] disruption (colchicine-site binding) and inhibition of Hedgehog and Wnt/β-catenin signaling. Together these agents are prominent [[Drug Repositioning]] candidates for GBM, often studied in combination with standard [[Chemotherapy]] (e.g., temozolomide) to deepen responses in this refractory tumor.
