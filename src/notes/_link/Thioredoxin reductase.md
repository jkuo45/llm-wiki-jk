# Thioredoxin reductase

**Thioredoxin reductase (TXNRD)** is a family of flavoenzymes that catalyzes the NADPH-dependent reduction of oxidized [[Thioredoxin]] back to its active, reduced form — the regeneration step that keeps the whole thioredoxin system functional. It is the redox counterpart of [[Glutathione]] reductase: together with [[Glutathione]], thioredoxin and thioredoxin reductase constitute the major thiol-based antioxidant defenses of the cell.

## Mechanism & Special Selenium Dependence

Mammalian TXNRD is unique among reductases in being a **selenoprotein**: a C-terminal selenocysteine (Sec) residue, encoded by a UGA codon that is recoded during translation, is essential for catalytic activity.

- Reduced TXNRD transfers electrons from NADPH, via FAD and a redox-active disulfide, to the selenocysteine-containing C-terminus, which then reduces oxidized thioredoxin.
- The selenium at the active site makes TXNRD exquisitely sensitive to inhibition by electrophiles that react with selenols — including **[[Auranofin]]**, a gold-based anti-rheumatic drug repurposed as an investigational anticancer agent, and other heavy-metal and electrophilic compounds.
- Selenocysteine mutations or selenium deficiency impair TXNRD activity, linking selenium status to antioxidant capacity.

## Isoforms & Localization

- **TXNRD1** — cytosolic (and nuclear) isoform that recycles [[Thioredoxin-1]] (Trx1).
- **TXNRD2** — mitochondrial isoform that recycles [[Thioredoxin-2]] (Trx2) and supports mitochondrial redox balance.

Both isoforms share the same catalytic logic but service their compartment-specific thioredoxin and [[Peroxiredoxin|peroxiredoxin]] pools.

## Physiological Role

- **Regeneration of the thioredoxin system**: By continuously reducing oxidized Trx1/Trx2, TXNRD sustains the reduction of [[Peroxiredoxin|peroxiredoxins]], [[Methionine sulfoxide reductases]], [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]], and redox-sensitive signaling proteins ([[ASK1]], [[NF-κB]], [[HIF-1α]], [[APE1]]).
- **Redox homeostasis**: TXNRD participates in metabolic pathways that maintain cellular redox balance and biosynthetic processes; its expression and activity are tightly regulated across tissues.
- **Cancer biology**: TXNRD is frequently up-regulated in tumors, where the thioredoxin system supports redox fitness and resistance to apoptosis and therapy. TXNRD inhibitors ([[Auranofin]], PX-12, ebselen) are under investigation as anticancer agents.
- **Disease relevance**: Dysregulation of TXNRD activity is implicated in metabolic disorders, oxidative-stress-related pathology, and cancer. Pharmacological modulation is a therapeutic strategy for diseases involving the thioredoxin pathway.

## Connections

- [[Thioredoxin]] — TXNRD recycles the thioredoxin system to its active reduced form; the two are inseparable functional partners.
- [[Thioredoxin-1]] — TXNRD1 regenerates the cytosolic Trx1 isoform.
- [[Thioredoxin-2]] — TXNRD2 regenerates the mitochondrial Trx2 isoform.
- [[Peroxiredoxin]] — Regenerated thioredoxin reduces oxidized peroxiredoxin, completing the H₂O₂-detoxification cycle.
- [[Methionine sulfoxide reductases]] — Depend on the thioredoxin system (via TXNRD) for regeneration.
- [[Glutathione]] — Parallel antioxidant/reductase system.
- [[Oxidative Stress]] — TXNRD activity is central to mounting resistance to oxidative challenge.
- [[Auranofin]] — oral gold(I) drug that irreversibly inhibits TXNRD1/2 via the catalytic selenocysteine; the canonical pharmacological probe of the thioredoxin system and a repurposing candidate in cancer and infectious disease.
- [[ASK1]] — Thioredoxin kept reduced by TXNRD inhibits ASK1; TXNRD inhibition therefore de-represses ASK1-mediated apoptosis (a mechanism used by [[Auranofin]] in cancer).
