# Type I Interferon

## Overview

Type I interferons (IFN-I) are a family of pleiotropic cytokines — principally IFN-α and IFN-β — that constitute the canonical cytokine output of innate immune nucleic-acid sensing. They are secreted downstream of pattern recognition receptor activation and alert neighboring cells to mount an antiviral and inflammatory state. In the central nervous system, IFN-I is a central mediator of [[Neuroinflammation]], and chronic, sustained IFN-I signaling has emerged as a key maladaptive driver of [[Neurodegenerative Disease]].

## Signaling

In the canonical [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis, detection of cytosolic dsDNA activates [[cGAS]] to produce [[cGAMP]], which engages [[STING]] and drives translocation to the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]]-Golgi. Activated STING recruits [[TBK1]], which phosphorylates and activates [[IRF3]] and [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]], licensing transcription of IFN-I and [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISGs]]. Vertebrate STING acquired a C-terminal tail that enabled robust TBK1/IRF3 recruitment, explaining the evolutionary expansion of IFN-I signaling capacity. IFN-I binds the heterodimeric [[IFNAR1]] receptor on target cells, triggering JAK-STAT signaling and a broad ISG transcriptional program.

## Clinical Relevance

Although protective during acute infection, sustained IFN-I signaling is pathogenic in the brain. In [[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]], chronic IFN-I promotes [[Microglia|microglial]] activation, synapse loss, plaque accumulation, and cognitive decline. Microglial cGAS-STING acts as the major upstream regulator of IFN-I responses across neurodegeneration, and [[Neuron|neuronal]] IFNAR1 transmits microglial IFN-I to neurons, linking glial inflammation to neuronal dysfunction. Notably, neuronal IFNAR1 is a key conduit by which microglial IFN-I release drives non-cell-autonomous neuronal injury. The AD risk alleles [[APOE4]] and [[TREM2]] R47H synergistically amplify cGAS-STING/IFN-I signaling, driving a senescence-like microglial state, while resilience alleles such as [[APOE3 Christchurch]] suppress this program. IFN-I signaling also restrains protective neuronal resilience programs such as those orchestrated by [[MEF2C]].

  - [[MEF2C]]: Neuronal resilience transcription factor whose downregulation is associated with sustained IFN-I signaling.
