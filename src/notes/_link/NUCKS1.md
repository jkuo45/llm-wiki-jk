# NUCKS1

**NUCKS1** (nuclear casein kinase and cyclin-dependent kinase substrate 1) is a small (243-amino-acid), highly acidic, intrinsically disordered nuclear phosphoprotein with an HMG-box-like DNA/RNA-binding domain. It is among the most abundantly expressed nuclear proteins in proliferating cells and acts as a pleiotropic chromatin-associated regulator of transcription, DNA repair, and metabolic gene programs. Through SIRT7-dependent deacetylation, NUCKS1 has been linked to chromatin organization and the regulation of [[Cellular Senescence]].

## Overview

- Ubiquitously expressed, evolutionarily conserved among vertebrates, and especially abundant in brain, testis, and proliferating cell populations.
- Named for being a substrate of casein kinase 2 ([[CK2]]) and cyclin-dependent kinases; hyperphosphorylated at multiple Ser/Thr residues.
- Binds both double-stranded DNA (AT-rich) and RNA, placing it at the interface of chromatin structure, transcription, and RNA processing.
- Encoded by NUCKS1 at chromosome 1q32.1, a locus repeatedly associated with type 2 diabetes and Parkinson's disease risk in GWAS.

## Structure & Domains

- **HMG-box-like domain (N-terminus, ~residues 1–79)**: a high-mobility-group fold mediating sequence-tolerant DNA and RNA binding; enables chromatin engagement without sequence specificity.
- **Acidic disordered tail (C-terminus)**: intrinsically disordered, heavily phosphorylated region rich in acidic residues — a platform for interaction with chromatin remodelers, transcription factors, and DNA-repair proteins.
- **Post-translational modifications**: multiple phosphorylation sites targeted by [[CK2]] and [[Cyclin-Dependent Kinase]]s; acetylation sites that are reversed by [[SIRT7]] deacetylase activity; the acetylation/deacetylation balance modulates its chromatin occupancy.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Chromatin & transcription**: NUCKS1 associates with chromatin, including [[Heterochromatin]] marked by [[H3K9me3]], and modulates the expression of proliferation- and metabolism-associated genes; it cooperates with [[Histone]] modifiers and [[Transcription Factor]]s at target promoters.
- **DNA damage response**: NUCKS1 is recruited to sites of [[DNA Damage]] and has been implicated in homologous-recombination repair, contributing to genome stability in proliferating cells (via interaction with repair proteins such as BRCA1).
- **Metabolic regulation**: NUCKS1 regulates expression of metabolic genes, including insulin-signaling and lipid-handling targets (e.g., INSIG2), linking chromatin state to systemic [[Metabolism]]; this underlies its GWAS association with type 2 diabetes.
- **Senescence regulation**: NUCKS1 depletion in cancer cell lines triggers [[Cellular Senescence]] and apoptosis through the [[p53]]/[[p21]] pathway, indicating that NUCKS1 sustains proliferative chromatin programs; the pending sirtuin document links [[SIRT7]]-dependent deacetylation of NUCKS1 to senescence-associated chromatin remodeling and SASP regulation.

## Physiological Function

- **Cell proliferation**: maintains the transcriptional and chromatin state required for continued division; levels fall as cells exit the cell cycle.
- **Genome maintenance**: supports DNA double-strand-break repair, protecting proliferating cells from genomic instability.
- **Metabolic integration**: couples nutrient/insulin signaling to the expression of metabolic genes; the NUCKS1/CDKAL1 locus is among the strongest diabetes-associated regions in human genetics.
- **Nuclear organization**: contributes to the architecture of active chromatin and nuclear bodies (including [[Nucleolus]]-associated regulation), in part via interactions with SIRT7 at rDNA loci.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer**: NUCKS1 is overexpressed in multiple malignancies (breast, hepatocellular carcinoma, gastric, colorectal, lung); high expression correlates with proliferation, metastasis, and poor prognosis. Loss of NUCKS1 inhibits proliferation and induces senescence/apoptosis in cancer models — a potential vulnerability for proliferative tumors.
- **Cellular senescence & aging**: NUCKS1 levels decline or its modification state changes during senescence; the SIRT7–NUCKS1 axis has been implicated in senescence-associated chromatin reorganization and the inflammatory [[SASP]], making it relevant to age-related disease (pending ingestion).
- **Metabolic disease**: the 1q32.1 locus (NUCKS1/CDKAL1) is associated with type 2 diabetes risk and impaired insulin secretion; NUCKS1-mediated metabolic gene regulation may contribute to pancreatic β-cell and insulin-target tissue function.
- **Neurodegeneration**: the same locus is associated with Parkinson's disease risk; NUCKS1 is highly expressed in brain, though the mechanistic link to neurodegeneration remains to be established.
