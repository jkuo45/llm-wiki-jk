# Amyloid Beta

**Overview:** Amyloid beta (Aβ) is a short peptide of 38–43 amino acids released from the type I single-pass transmembrane protein [[APP]] (amyloid precursor protein). It is not made by a dedicated Aβ synthase: it is the residual fragment left after two sequential proteolytic cuts of APP. Aβ is the principal component of the extracellular senile plaques and the vascular amyloid deposits of [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer disease]] (AD), and the soluble oligomeric species are now widely regarded as the more toxic form.

## Biogenesis

APP is a type I membrane protein with a large extracellular N-terminal domain (E1/E2) and an intracellular C-terminal tail (AICD). Three secretase routes process it:

- **α-secretase** (ADAM10, ADAM17/TACE) cuts *inside* the Aβ sequence (Lys16–Leu17), precluding Aβ formation. This "non-amyloidogenic" route releases **sAPPα** and leaves the C83 fragment, which γ-secretase then cuts to yield **p3** and AICD. Activity of the α-secretase route is the dominant determinant of whether a pool of APP is available for Aβ production at all.
- **β-secretase**, better known as **BACE1**, cuts between Met671 and Asp672, releasing **sAPPβ** and leaving the membrane-retained **C99** fragment. BACE1 and γ-secretase both reside largely in endosomes and the trans-Golgi network, so most Aβ is generated in that compartment.
- **γ-secretase** is a four-component intramembrane protease complex (presenilin/PSEN1 or PSEN2 as the catalytic subunit, plus nicastrin, APH-1, and PEN-2). Cutting C99 within the transmembrane domain releases **Aβ** of variable length and the AICD fragment.

Because γ-secretase cleaves without a fixed sequence preference, a mixture of Aβ species is produced — predominantly **Aβ40** with a smaller **Aβ42** fraction.

> [!important] Aβ42 is the more amyloidogenic species
> The extra two C-terminal residues of Aβ42 (Ile41–Ala42) make it markedly more aggregation-prone than Aβ40, and an increased Aβ42:Aβ40 ratio is the biochemical signature shared by familial AD caused by *APP* or *PSEN1* mutations. Aβ aggregation kinetics rather than plaque bulk has become the principal pharmacological development target.

## Structure, aggregation, and clearance

Soluble monomeric Aβ is disordered. Its toxicity arises from self-association into a hierarchy of species:

- **Oligomers (protofibrils)** — soluble assemblies of roughly 5–15 peptides, the species best correlated with synaptic dysfunction in both patient CSF and animal models.
- **Fibrils** — cross-β-sheet amyloid, the structural unit of plaques.
- **Plaques** — deposited cores surrounded by dystrophic neurites and reactive glia.

Clearance is normally balanced against production. Aβ is removed from brain interstitial fluid by enzymatic degradation (neprilysin and insulin-degrading enzyme) and by transport across the blood–brain barrier, chiefly via LRP1, followed by uptake into peripheral sinks. Astrocyte and microglial phagolysosomal degradation contributes substantially. [[ApoE]] is the principal lipid carrier in brain: it binds Aβ, modulates its aggregation, and determines its trafficking and clearance route.

> [!warning] The oligomer-vs-plaque distinction matters for interpretation
> The plaques themselves correlate poorly with cognitive decline, while soluble oligomer burden correlates well. Any claim that "clearing plaques reverses dementia" is not supported; the clinical failures of some anti-amyloid antibodies stem partly from this mismatch.

## Mechanism of neurotoxicity

Soluble Aβ oligomers exert effects at several levels, none of which is fully settled:

- **Synaptic.** Extracellular Aβ oligomers depress glutamatergic transmission, impair [[Long-Term Potentiation|LTP]], and accelerate [[Synaptic plasticity|synapse]] elimination. They also displace cognitive enhancers from synapses.
- **Receptors and signaling.** Aβ oligomers bind [[RAGE]], [[Toll-like Receptor|TLRs]], LRP1, and N-methyl-D-aspartate receptor–containing complexes, activating [[NF-κB]] and aberrant [[Glutamate|glutamatergic]] signaling.
- **Mitochondria.** Aβ is imported into mitochondria via the TOM machinery and localizes to cristae, where it inhibits respiratory complex I and IV, lowers ΔΨm, and raises [[Mitochondrial ROS|mitochondrial ROS]].
- **Oxidation.** Aβ stimulates ROS and lipid peroxidation directly and indirectly, feeding back into impaired proteostasis.
- **Inflammation.** Plaques recruit [[Microglia|microglia]] and [[Astrocytes|astrocytes]]; mitochondrial damage-associated molecular patterns such as cytosolic [[mtDNA]] activate cGAS–[[STING]] signaling, sustaining [[Neuroinflammation]].
- **Proteostasis and tau.** Aβ oligomers also promote [[Phosphorylation|hyperphosphorylation]] of [[Tau]] and accelerate its seeding into [[Neurofibrillary tangles|tangles]], which is why reducing Aβ late in disease yields limited cognitive benefit.

Aβ is also reported to have physiological roles (antibacterial activity in the gut and skin, modulation of [[Vesicle Transport|vesicular]] and synaptic physiology). The weight of evidence supports a pathogenic role in AD and a minor physiological one outside it.

## Therapeutic landscape

Strategies under development include:

- **Anti-amyloid antibodies** (lecanemab, donanemab) that bind fibrillar Aβ and reduce plaque and, modestly, decline on cognitive scales over ~18 months. [[ApoE]] ε4 homozygotes show larger plaque reduction but a higher risk of amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA) with antibodies.
- **BACE1 inhibitors** — failed clinically, largely because an Aβ-lowering ceiling plus earlier cognitive worsening outweighed plaque reduction.
- **γ-secretase modulators** that shift cleavage toward shorter, less aggregatable Aβ species, rather than inhibitors, which worsen cognition by impairing Notch processing.
- **Aβ aggregation inhibitors** (e.g., lanabecestat-class 6E1 antibodies; small-molecule oligomer inhibitors) still in trials.
- **Upstream:** [[Metformin]]-class [[AMPK]] activation, [[NAD+]] and [[SIRT1]] restoration, and [[mTOR]] inhibition, all of which reduce Aβ generation in animal models.

## Connections
- [[APP]] — Aβ is generated by β- and γ-secretase cleavage of APP; APP is also a cell-surface receptor whose signaling is altered by Aβ.
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]] — Aβ accumulation is the defining histopathological lesion, though soluble oligomers rather than plaques are thought to drive neuronal dysfunction.
- [[ApoE]] — principal brain lipid carrier for Aβ; APOE ε4 is the largest genetic risk factor for late-onset AD and impairs Aβ clearance.
- [[BACE1]] — β-secretase that initiates the amyloidogenic cleavage route and is a validated drug target upstream of Aβ.
- [[Tau]] — Aβ oligomers promote tau hyperphosphorylation and seeding, explaining why tau pathology spreads once amyloid is established.
- [[RAGE]] — receptor through which Aβ drives NF-κB activation and oxidative/inflammatory injury in neurons and glia.
- [[Mitochondrial ROS]] — Aβ import into mitochondria and respiratory inhibition generate oxidative stress, linking amyloid to the free-radical theories of aging.
- [[Microglia]] — plaque-associated microglia clear Aβ but also propagate neuroinflammation via DAMPs and inflammasome activation.
- [[SIRT1]] — sirtuin activity modulates Aβ production and clearance; NAD+ decline with age is a proposed upstream driver of amyloid accumulation.
- [[Long-Term Potentiation]] — the synaptic readout most consistently impaired by soluble Aβ oligomers in electrophysiology.
- [[Insulin]] — insulin resistance both raises CNS Aβ and impairs IDE-mediated Aβ degradation.
