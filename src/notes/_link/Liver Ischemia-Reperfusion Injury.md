# Liver Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury

**Liver ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI)** is tissue damage sustained when hepatic blood flow is restored after an ischemic period — a major complication of liver transplantation, hepatic resection, and shock. Reperfusion amplifies injury through oxidative stress, proinflammatory macrophage activation, and hepatocyte apoptosis.

## Sirtuin Axis

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Decreased after IRI; high levels predict superior survival in human liver transplants. SIRT1 suppresses mitochondrial dysfunction via MFN2 and alleviates autophagy depletion/inflammation.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Protective — knockout of Takeda G protein-coupled receptor 5 suppresses SIRT3, driving proinflammatory macrophages and exacerbated injury.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Detrimental — deacetylates MAPK phosphatase-1 to activate MAPK, augmenting inflammatory responses and cell death.

## Clinical Relevance

Minimizing IRI improves graft survival and surgical outcomes. The protective SIRT1/SIRT3 axis and the harmful SIRT2 action define a targetable balance for organ-protective strategies.
