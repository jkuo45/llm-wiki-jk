# Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome

**Ehlers-Danlos syndromes** (EDS) are a group of heritable connective tissue disorders in which the primary defect lies in the structure or processing of [[Collagen]] — most often type I, type III or type V — or, in two subtypes, in enzymes that modify collagen rather than in a collagen gene itself. The unifying clinical signature is a combination of joint hypermobility, skin hyperextensibility, atrophic scarring and tissue fragility. Defects in collagen also affect the vasculature, the eye, the dura and the valves, which is why the same disease can present as trivial hypermobility in one person and catastrophic arterial dissection in another.

> [!warning] EDS is a family of genetically distinct diseases, not one
> The 2017 International Classification defines **13 subtypes** on the basis of gene and molecular defect, replacing the older Villefranche and Roman-numeral systems and abandoning numeric descriptors. Grouping patients by *hypermobility* rather than by *molecular defect* is the single commonest error in EDS practice — it merges conditions with completely different prognoses and different surveillance needs. Hypermobile EDS (hEDS) has **no identified gene** and remains a clinical diagnosis; it should not be assumed to be a collagenopathy.

## Classification

The 2017 framework groups the subtypes by mechanism:

**Group A — defects in collagen primary structure and processing.** *Classical EDS* (cEDS: *COL5A1*, *COL5A2*, occasionally *COL1A1*) is the commonest form, with marked skin hyperextensibility, atrophic scarring and joint hypermobility; *vascular EDS* (vEDS, *COL3A1*) is defined by thin translucent skin, easy bruising, characteristic facies and a high risk of arterial, intestinal and uterine rupture; *arthrochalasia EDS* (*COL1A1*, *COL1A2*) has severe joint hypermobility with reduced mobility; *dermatosparaxis* (*ADAMTS2*) features redundant lax skin and extreme fragility; *kyphoscoliotic EDS* (*PLOD1*, *FKBP14*) adds muscle hypotonia, kyphoscoliosis and microcornea.

**Group B — defects in collagen-modifying enzymes.** *Loeys-Dietz syndrome* (*TGFBR1*, *TGFBR2*, *SMAD3*, *TGFB2/3*) and *arterial tortuosity syndrome* (*SLC2A10*).

**Group C — other connective tissue disorders.** *Myopathic EDS* (*COL12A1*), *classical-like EDS* (*COL1A1* arginine-to-cysteine alleles), and *cardiac-valvular EDS* (*TNXB*).

**Group D — defined by molecular defect.** The most recent subtype, *spheroidene microbodyopathy*.

> [!info] The vascular type is a different clinical problem
> vEDS carries a risk of spontaneous arterial rupture at a mean age in the thirties, and of intestinal and uterine rupture — the latter a leading cause of maternal mortality in the condition. Diagnosis is by *COL3A1* testing, and management centres on blood pressure control, avoidance of high-impact activity, and careful planning of any surgery. [[COL3A1]] — the vault's note for this gene — is the anchor for the single most consequential EDS subtype.

## Molecular Pathology

Collagen's tensile strength depends on a triple helix of three α chains wound together, cross-linked by lysyl oxidase in the extracellular space. EDS results from:

- **Quantitative collagen deficiency** (haploinsufficiency, as in *COL5A1* loss-of-function), producing thin, poorly cross-linked fibrils.
- **Quantitative collagen excess** (*COL1A1* arginine-to-cysteine substitutions in classical-like EDS) — paradoxically *more* type I collagen, but with defective α-chain interaction, so the dermis is fragile rather than strong. This is the clearest demonstration that EDS is a disorder of collagen *architecture*, not collagen amount.
- **Defective processing** — *ADAMTS2* (procollagen N-proteinase) and *PLOD1* (lysyl hydroxylase, which sets hydroxylysine content and hence cross-link density) impair collagen maturation.
- **Altered TGF-β signalling** in Loeys-Dietz syndrome, producing a systemic vasculopathy with a risk profile distinct from vEDS.

> [!important] The fragility is architectural, and collagen content does not predict it
> Both collagen-deficient and collagen-excess forms produce tissue that fails at normal load. This is why clinically meaningful severity correlates with *fibre architecture and cross-linking*, not with measured collagen concentration — and why the biochemical phenotype does not map linearly onto disability.

## Clinical Presentation

- **Joint hypermobility and instability** — assessed with the Beighton score; a degree of generalised hypermobility is common in healthy young people, so a high score alone is not diagnostic.
- **Skin** — hyperextensibility, softness and atrophic ("papery") scarring, easy bruising; in vEDS, thin translucent skin with visible vessels.
- **Tissue fragility** — hernias, pelvic organ prolapse, cervical insufficiency, and in vEDS spontaneous arterial dissection and intestinal perforation.
- **Other systems** — hypermobility spectrum features including chronic pain, fatigue, dysautonomia and POTS; ocular issues including myopia and keratoconus in classical-like EDS; hearing loss in some subtypes.

> [!warning] Handover and anaesthetic risk
> Vascular fragility means that a routine procedure — a dental extraction, an arterial line, a colonoscopy — can cause catastrophic bleeding in vEDS. Vascular access, imaging and surgical planning all need to be flagged in advance, and patient-held records are customary. The same caution applies to pregnancy, which is a principal mortality risk in vEDS.

## Connections

- [[COL3A1]] — type III collagen, whose mutations define vascular EDS, the subtype with the highest acute mortality and the most important to diagnose.
- [[Collagen]] — the protein class whose quantity, architecture or processing is defective in every EDS subtype; the note should be the route from EDS to the collagen biosynthesis pathway.
- [[Connective Tissue]] — EDS is a disorder of the connective tissue matrix as a whole, and the vault's connective tissue note is where the ECM framing belongs.
- [[COL5A2]] — type V collagen, a cause of classical EDS and the most common identified molecular defect in the classically symptomatic forms.
- [[Aging]] — both processes degrade collagen architecture and cross-linking, and the phenotypic overlap between cutis laxa in ageing and connective tissue fragility in EDS is an active area of interest.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — EDS is a disease of ECM assembly and cross-linking, which connects it to the wound healing and fibrosis machinery the vault already documents.
