# Citrate Synthase

**Citrate Synthase (CS)** is a [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] matrix enzyme that catalyses the first committed step of the [[TCA cycle]]: condensation of [[Acetyl-CoA]] with oxaloacetate to form citrate. Its activity is widely used as a biochemical marker of mitochondrial density/content and of tissue oxidative capacity.

## Role in Exercise Adaptation

- Prolonged exercise training increases the activity of aerobic enzymes — including citrate synthase and cytochrome-c oxidase — in tissues closely linked to the exercise, such as [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] and heart (Moraska _et al_. 2000; Houle-Leroy _et al_. 2003).
- Elevated CS activity indicates a higher metabolic rate per unit tissue mass, supporting the argument that training raises [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]] beyond what lean-mass accretion alone predicts.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review cites training-induced induction of citrate synthase and cytochrome-c oxidase as evidence that the metabolic intensity of lean tissues increases following prolonged exercise, contributing to the RMR increase seen in exercising animals despite declining body and fat mass.

## Clinical Relevance

CS activity is a standard readout in muscle-biopsy studies of training adaptations and in mitochondrial-disease diagnostics, where reduced activity reflects impaired mitochondrial biogenesis or content.
