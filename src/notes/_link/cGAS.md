# cGAS

## Overview
[[cGAS]] (cyclic GMP-AMP synthase, MB21D1) is a cytosolic DNA sensor of the innate immune system. Upon binding double-stranded DNA aberrantly present in the cytosol—from viral infection, [[mtDNA]] leakage, or [[DNA Damage]]—cGAS synthesizes the second messenger cGAMP, activating the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] via [[STING]].

## Structure & Activation
cGAS contains a nucleotidyltransferase domain that, upon DNA engagement, undergoes liquid-phase condensation to efficiently produce 2′3′-cGAMP. This cyclic dinucleotide binds and oligomerizes STING on the endoplasmic reticulum.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling
cGAMP–STING recruitment activates [[TBK1]] and [[IRF3]], driving type I interferon and [[Inflammation]]. It also engages [[NF-κB]] for pro-inflammatory cytokines. The pathway is a frontline antiviral defense but, when chronically triggered by self-DNA, promotes [[Senescence]] and the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].

## Redox, Senescence & Longevity
Mitochondrial DNA released during [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] or [[Minority MOMP]] can activate cGAS-STING, fueling [[Inflammaging]] and [[Aging]]. Suppression of chronic cGAS signaling is emerging as a senotherapeutic strategy. Balanced pathway tone intersects with [[Immunosenescence]] and redox control, positioning cGAS as a longevity-relevant innate immune checkpoint.

## Noncanonical & Nuclear Functions

Beyond cytosolic DNA sensing, cGAS carries out DNA-independent and nuclear roles enabled by its N-terminal [[Intrinsically Disordered Region|IDR]], which drives [[Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation|LLPS]].

- **Nuclear tethering:** In the nucleus cGAS is tightly bound to [[Nucleosome|nucleosomes]] (histone H2A–H2B acidic patch), a configuration that prevents autoreactive activation; [[Barrier-to-Autointegration Factor 1|BAF]] further outcompetes cGAS for nuclear DNA.
- **Genome stability:** Nuclear cGAS interacts with [[PARP1]] (disrupting the PARP1–Timeless complex) to suppress [[Homologous Recombination]], and slows replication fork progression. Notably, cGAS from the long-lived [[Naked Mole Rat]] shows prolonged chromatin retention and *enhances* [[DNA Repair]].
- **Interactome:** cGAS scaffolds [[RNA-Binding Proteins]] ([[G3BP1]], [[PQBP1]]), the [[SWI-SNF Complex]] chromatin-remodeler, and the [[Spliceosome]], implicating it in transcription and RNA processing.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - JCI -Expanding roles of cGAS-STING signaling in neuroinflammation|JCI cGAS-STING in Neuroinflammation]]
> The [[Tau|tau]]–[[PQBP1]] complex activates cGAS through a **direct protein-protein interaction that bypasses the requirement for DNA**, a key DNA-independent activation route in [[Tauopathy]].

## Role in Neuroinflammation & Neurodegeneration

Microglia are the dominant source of cGAS in the diseased brain. Tau, [[Amyloid Beta|Aβ]], [[Alpha-synuclein|α-synuclein]], [[TDP-43]], and mutant [[Huntingtin|huntingtin]] converge on [[mtDNA]] leakage (or, for tau, PQBP1) to activate microglial cGAS, driving chronic [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling and [[Neuroinflammation]].

> [!important] Therapeutic uncoupling
> Genetic ablation or pharmacological inhibition of cGAS (e.g., RU.521, TDI inhibitors) selectively suppresses the neurotoxic, [[IRF3]]/[[NF-κB|NF-κB]]-driven inflammatory program while **preserving—or augmenting—microglial phagocytosis** (increased [[Amyloid Beta|Aβ]] plaque clearance). In tauopathy, microglial cGAS deletion restores neuronal [[MEF2C]] cognitive-resilience programs, and cGAS inhibition phenocopies the protective [[APOE3 Christchurch]] (R136S) allele.
