# Optineurin

## Overview

Optineurin (gene: OPTN) is a cytoplasmic selective autophagy receptor that participates in [[Mitophagy]] alongside other autophagy receptors including [[NBR1]], [[NDP52]], and [[p62]]. It has gained significant attention due to its genetic links to neurodegenerative diseases including glaucoma and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS).

## Mechanism of Action

Optineurin mediates selective autophagy through conserved functional domains:

- **LIR motif** — Optineurin contains a [[LIR Motif]] that directly binds [[LC3]] on the autophagosomal membrane, enabling cargo tethering
- **Ubiquitin binding** — Contains ubiquitin-binding domains that recognize ubiquitinated substrates
- **Phosphorylation-dependent activation** — Can be phosphorylated by [[TBK1]], enhancing its affinity for ubiquitin chains and LC3

## Role in Disease

Optineurin dysfunction is linked to several pathological conditions:

- **Glaucoma** — Mutations in optineurin are associated with primary open-angle glaucoma, potentially through dysregulated autophagy in retinal ganglion cells
- **ALS** — Optineurin mutations have been identified in familial ALS, suggesting defective selective autophagy contributes to motor neuron degeneration
- **Inflammatory signaling** — Optineurin negatively regulates [[NF-κB]] signaling by interacting with RIP1 and other components

## Physiological Function

Optineurin contributes to cellular homeostasis by:

- Mediating selective removal of damaged mitochondria
- Regulating inflammatory signaling pathways
- Maintaining protein aggregate clearance
