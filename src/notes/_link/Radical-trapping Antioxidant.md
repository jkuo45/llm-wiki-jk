# Radical-trapping Antioxidant

A **radical-trapping antioxidant (RTA)** is an antioxidant that **halts free-radical chain reactions** by donating a hydrogen atom to a chain-propagating radical — most relevantly a **lipid peroxyl radical (LOO•)** — producing a comparatively stable radical that does not abstract further hydrogen atoms from neighboring polyunsaturated fatty acids. This **chain-breaking** action is the chemical basis of ferroptosis suppression.

## Examples

- **Ubiquinol (CoQ10H₂)** — regenerated at the plasma membrane by **FSP1** using NAD(P)H; the central GPX4-independent RTA.
- **Vitamin K hydroquinone (VKH₂)** — regenerated by FSP1 in the non-canonical vitamin K cycle.
- **α-Tocopherol (Vitamin E)** — the classic lipophilic RTA of lipoproteins and membranes; regenerated by CoQ10 and vitamin C.
- **Ferrostatin-1 / Liproxstatin-1** — synthetic RTAs that rescue cells from ferroptosis and are used as experimental inhibitors.

## Ferroptosis relevance

RTAs are the **preventive** complement to **GPX4**, which *repairs* existing phospholipid hydroperoxides. The FSP1–CoQ10–NAD(P)H axis works precisely by maintaining an RTA (ubiquinol) in the membrane; loss of the RTA — e.g., via mevalonate-pathway depletion of ubiquinone — lets peroxidation chains propagate to lethal levels (see [[Ferroptosis]], [[FSP1]], [[Lipid Peroxidation]]).

> [!info] RTA vs repair
> RTAs (ubiquinol, vitamin E, ferrostatin-1) **prevent** chain initiation/propagation; GPX4 **repairs** downstream hydroperoxides using GSH. Both are required for full protection, which is why the two systems are synergistic.
