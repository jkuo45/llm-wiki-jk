IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1) is a peptide hormone structurally related to insulin, produced mainly by the liver under growth-hormone control. It is a principal mediator of somatic growth and a core node of the conserved insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS) pathway, one of the most robust regulators of longevity across species.

## Role in ageing and longevity

Reduced IIS/IGF-1 signaling extends lifespan in worms, flies, and mice and is associated with altered longevity in humans, positioning IGF-1 as a key ageing hormone. Conversely, high IGF-1 signaling promotes growth and metabolism at the cost of accelerated ageing phenotypes.

## Modulation by sirtuins

[[SIRT6|SIRT6]] intersects the IGF-1 axis: SIRT6 knockout mice show low circulating IGF-1 and glucose, whereas SIRT6 overexpression reduces IGF-1 pathway activity (lower IGF-1, raised IGF-binding protein, altered phosphorylation of pathway components), improving glucose tolerance and reducing fat accumulation—changes that contribute to male lifespan extension (Kanfi et al.). Thus SIRT6 acts as a negative modulator of IGF-1 signaling, linking chromatin/NAD+-dependent sirtuin activity to the somatotropic longevity axis.

## Clinical and pathological relevance

IGF-1 influences muscle, bone, neurodevelopment, and cancer risk (high IGF-1 is a recognized oncogenic/mitogenic signal). Its decline with age contributes to sarcopenia and frailty, whereas dysregulated IGF-1 signaling is implicated in metabolic and age-related disease.
