# GAPDH

**Overview:** [[GAPDH]] (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase) catalyzes the sixth, NAD+-dependent oxidation–phosphorylation step of [[Glycolysis]], converting glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate while reducing [[NAD+]] to NADH.

**Structure and regulation:** The tetrameric enzyme uses a catalytic cysteine thiol whose reactivity makes it a redox sensor. Under [[Oxidative Stress]], GAPDH is inhibited by S-nitrosylation, S-glutathionylation, and oxidation, throttling glycolysis and signaling metabolic crisis.

**Moonlighting functions:** Beyond metabolism, GAPDH acts as a [[Transcription]] cofactor, binds to vesicles, and participates in [[DNA Repair|DNA repair]] and vesicular trafficking. Critically, oxidatively modified GAPDH translocates to the nucleus, where it binds and activates the apoptosis-inducing factor (AIF), triggering caspase-independent [[Apoptosis]].

**Physiological and pathological relevance:** GAPDH integrates energy metabolism with stress surveillance; its nuclear accumulation is implicated in neurodegeneration (e.g., [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]]) and ischemia–reperfusion injury. Because it is constitutively expressed, GAPDH is widely used as a loading control in molecular assays, though its regulation under stress complicates that role.
