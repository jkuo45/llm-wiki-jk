# ALOX15

**ALOX15** (arachidonate 15-lipoxygenase), also termed **15-LOX**, is a member of the [[Lipoxygenase]] family that oxygenates polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) such as arachidonic acid and adrenic acid esterified to phospholipids. It is a positive regulator of [[Ferroptosis]], implicated in the [[p53]]–[[SAT1]]-driven lipid-peroxidation axis and in direct peroxidation of PUFA-phosphatidylethanolamines that drives membrane rupture.

## Structure & Biochemistry

ALOX15 is a non-heme iron-containing dioxygenase that inserts molecular oxygen at the ω-6 position of PUFAs. Like other lipoxygenases it requires ferrous iron at its catalytic center, linking its activity to cellular [[Iron]] availability. It acts on both free and phospholipid-esterified PUFAs, particularly those presented on [[Phosphatidylethanolamine]] (PE) species enriched by [[ACSL4]] and [[LPCAT3]].

## Mechanism of Action & Pathway

ALOX15 generates phospholipid hydroperoxides (PE-OOH) from PUFA-PE. When antioxidant defenses—principally [[GPX4]] and [[FSP1]]–[[Coenzyme Q10|CoQ10]]—are overwhelmed, these hydroperoxides propagate radical chain reactions via the [[Fenton Reaction]], executing ferroptosis. In the p53 pathway, ALOX15 is induced downstream of [[SAT1]] and catalyzes the terminal oxidation step that commits the cell to ferroptotic death (PMID: 25799957).

> [!info] ALOX15 vs. other lipoxygenases
> While [[ALOX5]] (5-LOX) is implicated in hemorrhage-related neuronal ferroptosis (via arachidonate-dependent activity), ALOX15 is the isoform most consistently tied to the canonical p53/SAT1 ferroptotic program and to PUFA-PE peroxidation.

## Physiological Function

15-LOX participates in eicosanoid metabolism, membrane remodeling, and clearance of damaged lipids. In erythroid cells it helps degrade mitochondria during maturation. Its peroxidating activity is normally balanced by GPX4.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

ALOX15 overactivity, when antioxidant capacity is limited, tips cells toward ferroptosis—relevant in [[p53]]-proficient tumors, neuronal injury ([[Stroke]], [[Traumatic Brain Injury]]), and [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]]. Conversely, 15-LOX inhibition can blunt ferroptosis, highlighting it as a candidate modulator (though systemic lipoxygenase inhibitors carry pleiotropic effects).
