# Hydrogen Sulfide

Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) is a colourless, foul-smelling, water- and lipid-soluble gas produced endogenously in most mammalian tissues. Alongside [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] and [[Carbon monoxide|CO]] it is a **gasotransmitter** — a small, membrane-permeable signalling molecule made enzymatically and acting on protein targets by covalent chemistry rather than by classical receptor binding.

> [!info] Biosynthesis
> H₂S is generated from the **transsulfuration pathway** by three principal enzymes:
> - **Cystathionine β-synthase (CBS)** — the first and rate-limiting enzyme of transsulfuration; the main source in brain and a major source in endothelium. The allosteric activator [[Cysteine]] and SAM tune its activity.
> - **Cystathionine γ-lyase (CSE)** — the dominant source in liver, kidney and vascular smooth muscle. Under [[Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress|ER stress]], CSE switches substrate from cystathionine to cysteine and thereby increases H₂S output; it also translocates to the mitochondrion in a TOM20-dependent manner, which helps maintain ATP production.
> - **3-Mercaptopyruvate sulfurtransferase (3-MST)** — reported in both cytosol and mitochondria, and therefore a candidate regulator of mitochondrial protein persulfide levels.
>
> Non-enzymatic sources also contribute: persulfide (R–S–SH) and thiosulfate buffers release H₂S non-enzymatically, and this is where the concept of **sulfane sulfur** — the pool of sulfide carried on protein persulfides and polysulfides — becomes the relevant species rather than free H₂S.

> [!info] Mechanism: S-sulfhydration
> The dominant molecular action of H₂S is **S-sulfhydration** of cysteine thiols: it converts R–SH to the **hydropersulfide R–S–SH**, adding a sulfur atom to the cysteine rather than oxidising it to a disulfide. This is reversible, chemically distinct from irreversible oxidative modification, and is catalysed on protein targets by the persulfide-producing chemistry of the source enzymes themselves. Because a thiol-thiol disulfide switch is a redox switch, sulfhydration behaves as a targeted redox modification that can be localised to specific proteins rather than imposed globally.
>
> A notable consequence: since CBS and CSE are persulfide-producing enzymes, enzymes that combine persulfide production with protein binding (notably CBS itself, and the cysteine desulfurase NFS1) can **trans-sulfurate their own protein partners**, creating a self-catalysed signalling loop that does not require free H₂S at all.

> [!info] Mitochondrial actions
> H₂S regulates mitochondrial bioenergetics in a biphasic, concentration-dependent way:
> - At **low concentrations**, H₂S sulfhydrates complex V (F₁F₀-ATPase) and increases its activity, supporting ATP generation. In swine heart mitochondria, H₂S affects the **Ca²⁺-activated** F₁F₀-ATPase but not the Mg²⁺-activated form.
> - At **higher concentrations**, H₂S becomes toxic, induces oxidative stress, and weakens ATPase activity — the same molecule is protective or harmful depending on dose.
>
> This biphasic behaviour is the recurring pattern across the gasotransmitter literature and is why H₂S donors in the therapeutic literature are calibrated to the low, signalling range.

## Physiology and disease

Reported (sometimes controversially) roles include: relaxation of vascular smooth muscle via K⁺ channel opening and lowering of intracellular [[Calcium]]; inhibition of platelet aggregation; antioxidant action by scavenging [[Superoxide]] and by maintaining [[Glutathione]] in the reduced form; and cytoprotection against [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury|ischemia–reperfusion injury]] and apoptosis.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The translational record is mixed and the reasons are instructive:
> - **H₂S donors** (sodium thiosulfate, sodium hydrosulfide, GYY1017, slow-releasing sulfide donors) have been tested in cardiovascular, inflammatory and lung disease with generally positive but small trial signals. **Sodium thiosulfate** is the furthest advanced, in calciphylaxis.
> - **H₂S biology is not merely H₂S.** A substantial body of work now argues that the physiologically relevant species is **sulfane sulfur** on protein persulfides, not free gas. If correct, then "H₂S donors" and "sulfane sulfur donors" are not interchangeable, and a large part of the pharmacology may be measuring the wrong variable.
> - Redox modification sites are not specific. Sulfhydration of many proteins is essentially a thiol redox switch, and attributing a phenotype to H₂S rather than to generic thiol oxidation requires persistence of effect in the absence of the enzyme and absence of the gas.
> - In cancer, CBS and CSE are frequently overexpressed and both pro- and anti-tumour roles are described, with the direction depending on tumour type. No H₂S-directed agent is an established anticancer therapy.

- [[Nitric Oxide]] and [[Carbon monoxide]] — the other two gasotransmitters. H₂S is the most recent and the most chemically reactive of the three, and all three converge on protein thiols, so their interactions are synergistic rather than independent.
- [[Cysteine]] and the cysteine/cystine pair — cysteine is both the substrate and the CBS allosteric regulator, and the cysteine/cystine ratio is itself a redox signal that sets the thiol baseline against which sulfhydration acts.
- [[Glutathione]] and [[Thioredoxin]] — H₂S sustains the reduced glutathione pool, which is why it appears alongside both in redox-network notes; the two systems are functionally coupled through the same millimolar-thiol pool.
- [[Superoxide]] and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] — H₂S's antioxidant action is largely superoxide-scavenging and thiol-redox, which is also why it and the mitochondrial ROS literature are entangled.
- [[Mitochondria]] and [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] — complex V sulfhydration is the best-defined H₂S target and the clearest example of gasotransmitter control of bioenergetics.
- [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] — a recurring therapeutic indication for H₂S donors, since IRI is exactly the situation where thiols are oxidised en masse and where a targeted reversible thiol reductant should help.
- [[Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress]] — the stress-responsive switch of CSE substrate, which is a direct link between proteotoxic stress signalling and gasotransmitter output.
- [[Inflammation]] — H₂S is generally anti-inflammatory, acting on [[NF-κB]] and on leukocyte adhesion; it is grouped with other endogenous anti-inflammatory mediators in several reviews.
- [[Aging]] and [[NAD+]] — CBS activity is reported to fall with age, and the vault's [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]] and redox notes both touch sulfane-sulfur metabolism, though the H₂S–ageing evidence base is thinner than the reader might assume.
