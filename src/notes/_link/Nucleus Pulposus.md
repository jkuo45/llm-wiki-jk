# Nucleus Pulposus

## Overview

The nucleus pulposus (NP) is the hydrated, gelatinous central component of the [[Intervertebral Disc Degeneration|intervertebral disc]], surrounded by the annulus fibrosus. It consists of notochord-derived nucleus pulposus cells (NPCs) embedded in a matrix rich in proteoglycans (aggrecan) and type II collagen. The NP provides load-bearing compressibility and flexibility to the spinal column; age-related loss of NPC viability and matrix homeostasis drives disc degeneration, chronic back pain, and disability.

## Pathophysiology in Disc Degeneration

- [[Cellular Senescence|Cellular senescence]], [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]], and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] are key mechanisms in intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD).
- [[SIRT3]] expression is significantly reduced in degenerated discs; SIRT3 knockdown exacerbates NPC deterioration under oxidative stress.
- [[Lactate|lactate]] downregulates SIRT3, increasing [[ACSL4|ACSL4]] lactylation and promoting [[Ferroptosis|ferroptosis]] in NPCs, accelerating functional decline.
- SIRT3 safeguards NPCs against [[Advanced Glycation End Products|advanced glycation end product (AGE)]]-induced [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] and supports mitochondrial homeostasis via the [[AMPK]]/[[PGC-1α]] pathway.
- SIRT3 overexpression alleviates IDD severity *in vivo*; hydrogel microspheres co-delivering SIRT3 and [[SOD2]] maintain NPC viability.

## Clinical Relevance

- NPC senescence and mitochondrial redox failure are emerging therapeutic targets for IDD; the AMPK–[[SIRT3]]–PGC-1α axis is central to mitochondrial function and antioxidant capacity in the disc.
