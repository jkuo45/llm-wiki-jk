# Cell Adhesion

Cell adhesion is the process by which a cell establishes and maintains specific, transient or stable physical contact with a neighbouring cell or with the [[Extracellular Matrix|extracellular matrix]], mediated by dedicated surface molecules called cell adhesion molecules (CAMs). It is distinct from nonspecific cohesion, from protein–protein docking inside a cell, and from the general membrane–cytoskeleton coupling of the [[Actin Cytoskeleton]].

> [!info] The basis of tissue architecture
> Every multicellular organism is built by cells that must know who their neighbours are. Adhesion provides positional identity, mechanical coupling and force transmission — it is how an epithelium holds together, how a leukocyte leaves the bloodstream at a site of infection, and how a fibroblast contracts a healing wound. Loss of adhesion is not a neutral event: epithelial cells detach, anoikis-resistant and become metastatic.

## The Four Major CAM Families

CAMs are grouped by structural motif, and the classification tracks the biochemistry of the interaction:

| Family | Interaction | Dependence | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Cadherin|Cadherins]] | Homophilic, Ca<sup>2+</sup>-dependent | Calcium; rigid, strand-swapped extracellular domains | E-cadherin in epithelia |
| [[Integrin|Integrins]] | Heterophilic, divalent-cation-dependent | Mg<sup>2+</sup>/Ca<sup>2+</sup>; bidirectional outside-in and inside-out signalling | α5β1 fibronectin receptor; αVβ3 in angiogenesis |
| Selectins | Heterophilic, Ca<sup>2+</sup>-dependent, lectin-like | Calcium; recognise specific sialylated glycans | [[E-Selectin]] and P-selectin in leukocyte rolling |
| Immunoglobulin superfamily (IgSF) | Homophilic or heterophilic | Calcium-independent; cell–cell adhesion | [[APP]], VCAM-1, ICAM-1, [[CD47]] |

Additional groups include syndecans and other proteoglycans (hyaluronan receptors), dystroglycan, and the epithelial-specific claudins and occludins of [[Adherens Junction|tight junctions]] and [[Adherens Junction|adherens junctions]].

## The Adhesion Cascade

Adhesion of a leukocyte is the paradigm of multistep, affinity-matured capture:

1. **Rolling** — selectins engage low-affinity, high-on-rate interactions with carbohydrate ligands, producing the intermittent sticking that samples the endothelial surface for chemokines.
2. **Activation** — chemokine binding to the leukocyte integrin triggers inside-out signalling, converting the integrin from a bent, low-affinity state to a straight, high-affinity one.
3. **Firm adhesion** — activated integrins bind ICAM-1 or VCAM-1 strongly enough to arrest the cell.
4. **Transmigration (diapedesis)** — the cell squeezes between endothelial cells, assisted by PECAM-1 (CD31).

> [!warning] Selectins are also an antiviral vulnerability
> Because rolling depends on selectin–glycan recognition, several viruses have evolved lectin-binding proteins that hijack the same interaction. SARS-CoV-2 spike engages [[E-Selectin]] and P-selectin via its receptor-binding domain, which has been proposed as a route to leukocyte recruitment and to the vascular prothrombotic state of severe [[COVID-19]].

## Cadherin Adhesion and the Epithelial State

Cadherins couple cell–cell contact directly to the cytoskeleton: classical cadherins bind to [[Actin Cytoskeleton|actin]] through catenin complexes, which is what allows an adherens junction to transmit force. The [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition|epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition]] is fundamentally an adhesion programme: E-cadherin is transcriptionally repressed by SNAIL, TWIST and ZEB factors, β-catenin is released from the junction and relocates to the nucleus, and the cell loses its neighbours. This is the single most consequential adhesion event in [[Cancer]].

A second, related axis is podocyte foot-process adhesion in the kidney, where the negatively charged glycocalyx of [[PODXL]] mediates anti-adhesion and its loss produces proteinuric kidney disease.

## Integrin Adhesion as an Active Process

Integrins are the paradigm of bidirectional signalling: outside-in signals flow from matrix binding to the cytoskeleton and nucleus, while inside-out signals from chemokines, G-protein-coupled receptors and cytoskeletal tension change integrin affinity and avidity. This is why integrins are unusually good therapeutic targets — [[Bevacizumab]]-class anti-angiogenesis therapy, RGD-targeted agents and integrin antagonists all exploit the same adhesion machinery.

## Therapeutic Relevance

CAM biology is a dense source of drug targets: anti-integrin biologics in inflammatory bowel disease and multiple sclerosis; VLA-4 blockade with natalizumab; [[E-Selectin]] antagonists under development for leukaemia stem-cell mobilisation; CAR-T designs that engineer adhesion to improve persistence; and tumour drugs aimed at [[Cadherin]]–β-catenin signalling or at [[Integrin]] αVβ3 in angiogenesis. Loss of adhesion is also central to metastatic cascade biology, since detachment from the basement membrane is a precondition for invasion.

## Connections

- [[Integrin]] — the divalent-cation-dependent, bidirectional-signalling family that mediates adhesion to the extracellular matrix and drives invasion and angiogenesis.
- [[Cadherin]] — the calcium-dependent homophilic family that holds epithelia together and whose loss defines the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition.
- [[E-Selectin]] — the vault's selectin entry point, central to leukocyte rolling and hijacked by viral lectin domains.
- [[Adherens Junction]] — the junctional structure in which cadherin–catenin complexes are anchored to actin, making it the structural expression of cell–cell adhesion.
- [[Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition]] — the transcriptional programme that dismantles cadherin adhesion and is the gateway to invasion.
- [[Basal Lamina]] — the matrix layer whose [[Laminin]] network provides the adhesion substrate for epithelial and endothelial cells.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — the adhesive substrate for integrins and the source of mechanical cues.
- [[Cell Membranes]] — the membrane domain in which all CAMs are embedded and displayed.
- [[Actin Cytoskeleton]] — the effector to which adhesion is mechanically coupled; adhesion without cytoskeletal coupling cannot generate force.
- [[Inflammation]] — the process whose leukocyte recruitment is executed by the selectin–integrin cascade.
- [[Fibrosis]] — during wound healing, myofibroblast contraction is driven by integrin adhesion to the matrix.
- [[APP]] — an immunoglobulin-superfamily adhesion protein, a member of the fourth CAM family.
- [[Cancer]] — the disease context in which adhesion loss, angiogenesis and invasion are mechanistically inseparable.
- [[COVID-19]] — the clinical setting in which adhesion molecules and their glycans are actively co-opted by viral entry and inflammatory cell recruitment.
- [[CD47]] — an IgSF receptor whose "don't eat me" signal is read by macrophages, a distinct function in the same family.
