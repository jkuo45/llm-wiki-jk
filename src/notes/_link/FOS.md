# FOS

**FOS** (c-Fos) is an immediate-early response gene encoding a basic leucine zipper (bZIP) transcription factor. FOS does not homodimerize efficiently; it forms the activator protein-1 ([[AP-1]]) complex by heterodimerizing with [[JUN|JUN]] family members, and these dimers bind TRE (TPA-responsive) elements to regulate genes controlling proliferation, differentiation, inflammation, and matrix remodeling. FOS is transiently and potently induced by mitogens, cytokines, mechanical stress, and [[ROS|reactive oxygen species]], making it a rapid sensor of the cellular environment whose chronic activation sustains pathological programs such as fibrosis and oncogenesis.

## FOS/AP-1 in cardiac fibrosis

In [[Cardiomyocytes|cardiomyocytes]], FOS contributes to fibrogenic transcriptional programming as part of the FOS/[[AP-1]] signaling pathway. The SIRT3 review highlights that [[SIRT3]] attenuates fibrosis by inhibiting both the FOS/AP-1 and [[STAT3]]/[[NFATc2]] signaling pathways in cardiomyocytes, thereby restraining pathological cardiac remodeling[126]. Mechanistically, SIRT3-mediated deacetylation of [[GSK3β|GSK-3β]] at Lys15 prevents [[SMAD3|Smad3]] transcriptional complex formation, and suppression of AP-1 components such as FOS complements this anti-fibrotic program.

## Clinical relevance

Chronic AP-1 activation is a hallmark of hypertensive and post-infarct cardiac remodeling, pulmonary fibrosis, and inflammation-driven cancers. FOS/AP-1 suppression represents one of the transcriptional arms through which SIRT3 activation exerts anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory effects across organs.
