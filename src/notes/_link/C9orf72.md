# C9orf72

## Overview

*C9orf72* is the most common genetic cause of familial [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]] (ALS) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD). A hexanucleotide (GGGGCC) repeat expansion in the first intron of *C9orf72* leads to loss of function, repeat-associated non-AUG translation of dipeptide-repeat proteins, and formation of nuclear RNA foci. Emerging evidence links *C9orf72* pathology to the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]], positioning innate immune activation as a contributor to C9orf72-ALS/FTD [[Neuroinflammation]].

## Mechanism of cGAS-STING Activation

Loss of *C9orf72* function disrupts nucleocytoplasmic transport and impairs the autophagic/endolysosomal clearance of cytoplasmic contents. These defects precipitate [[DNA Damage]] and accumulation of cytosolic nucleic acids that engage [[cGAS]], driving [[STING]]-dependent [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling. As with [[NPC1]] deficiency, C9orf72 loss of function supports a cGAS-independent or stress-driven mode of STING overactivation rooted in nucleocytoplasmic and vesicular trafficking disturbances rather than classical DNA leakage alone.

## Relevance to Neurodegeneration

The C9orf72-cGAS-STING axis illustrates how a single genetic risk factor can converge on the same innate immune pathway engaged by proteinopathies such as [[TDP-43]], [[Tau]], and [[Amyloid Beta]]. Given that STING inhibition reduces TDP-43–mediated motor neuron death, targeting the downstream [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] represents a plausible therapeutic strategy for C9orf72-associated disease, complementing approaches aimed at the repeat expansion itself.
