# Nociceptor

## Overview

[[Nociceptor|Nociceptors]] are specialized primary sensory neurons that detect noxious stimuli and transmit pain signals. Their cell bodies reside largely in the [[Dorsal Root Ganglion|dorsal root ganglion]] (and trigeminal ganglion), with peripheral and central axon projections. Beyond pain transduction, nociceptors are now recognized as active participants in innate immune signaling through the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis.

## cGAS-STING in Pain and Regeneration

Neuronal [[STING]] signaling influences both nociceptor excitability and axonal regeneration. Loss of STING leads to [[Nociceptor|nociceptor]] hyperexcitability through dysregulated [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] signaling, whereas activation of cGAS-STING in nociceptors contributes to chronic neuropathic pain following nerve injury or chemotherapy. Microglial cGAS-STING activity in the surrounding milieu further drives allodynia, linking central glial activation to peripheral sensory dysfunction.

## Peripheral Neuropathy Implications

Because cGAS-STING engagement in nociceptors and adjacent glia promotes persistent pain states, the pathway represents a candidate target for neuropathic pain therapeutics. Balancing pathway suppression to relieve pain while preserving its regenerative and homeostatic neuronal functions remains a key challenge.
