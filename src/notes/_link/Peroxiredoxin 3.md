# Peroxiredoxin 3

Peroxiredoxin 3 (PRDX3) is a mitochondrial matrix-localized member of the typical 2-Cys peroxiredoxin family, specialized in detoxifying hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and peroxynitrite produced as by-products of [[Mitochondria]]l respiration. Like other peroxiredoxins, PRDX3 contains an active-site peroxidatic cysteine that is oxidized to sulfenic acid by its peroxide substrate; this then forms a disulfide with the resolving cysteine of a second subunit and is reduced back by thioredoxin-2 (TRX2), which is in turn regenerated by [[Mitochondria]]l thioredoxin reductase using NADPH.

PRDX3 is the principal H₂O₂ scavenger in the mitochondrial matrix and is essential for buffering the low-level [[ROS]] generated at the [[Electron Transport Chain]]. By keeping matrix peroxide concentrations low, it protects mitochondrial proteins, [DNA], and [lipids] from oxidative damage and helps regulate redox signaling thresholds involved in [[Apoptosis]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] adaptation.

> [!info] PRDX3 is reversibly inactivated by hyperoxidation of its peroxidatic cysteine to sulfinic acid during oxidative bursts—this oxidation is repaired by [[Sulfiredoxin]].

PRDX3 expression is induced by [[Nrf2]] and is frequently upregulated in cancer and stressed tissues as part of an antioxidant program. Its selective mitochondrial localization makes it a central node linking redox homeostasis to mitochondrial quality-control and longevity pathways.
