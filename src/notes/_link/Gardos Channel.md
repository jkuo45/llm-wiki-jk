# Gardos Channel

The **Gardos channel** (KCa3.1, encoded by *KCNN4*, also called IK1) is the Ca2+-activated K+ channel of the erythrocyte membrane. It is the effector that converts a rise in cytosolic Ca2+ into the cell shrinkage that defines [[Eryptosis]]: K+ efflux hyperpolarizes the membrane, driving Cl− exit through chloride channels, and water follows osmotically.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Ca2+ overload opens Gardos channels and the resulting K+ loss is the proximate cause of eryptotic shrinkage; charybdotoxin and clotrimazole avert shrinkage. The same loss of K+ underlies the "Gardos effect" — the rapid volume decrease of activated erythrocytes.

## Mechanism

- Intermediate-conductance Ca2+-activated K+ channel; opens on cytosolic Ca2+ elevation, closes on membrane hyperpolarization.
- Sequence in eryptosis: Ca2+ influx through cation channels → Gardos opening → K+ loss → hyperpolarization → Cl− exit → osmotic water loss → shrinkage (measurable as reduced forward scatter, FSC).
- Same Ca2+ rise concurrently inhibits flippase/activates [[Scramblase]] (PS exposure) and activates [[Calpain]] (blebbing) — hence "Ca2+ is the master regulator of eryptosis."

## Pharmacology and relevance

- Blockers: charybdotoxin, clotrimazole (standard proof-of-mechanism tools in eryptosis assays).
- Because erythrocytes cannot upregulate proteins, ion-transport tuning is the main available regulatory lever in eryptosis — the review argues ion channels and fluxes, not gene expression, are the practical drug targets.
- Aldosterone/sickle-cell literature ties Gardos activity to sickling and to erythrocyte dehydration in storage lesions.
