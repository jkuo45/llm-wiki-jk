# SAHA

**SAHA** (suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid; INN **vorinostat**) is a small-molecule **histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitor** and, in 2006, the first HDAC inhibitor approved by the FDA (for cutaneous T-cell lymphoma).

## Chemical Properties & Mechanism of Action

- A **hydroxamic-acid** class inhibitor whose zinc-chelating hydroxamate group inserts into the catalytic pocket of Zn²⁺-dependent HDACs.
- **Pan-HDAC inhibition:** blocks class I and class II [[HDAC inhibitors|HDACs]], preventing removal of acetyl marks and thereby increasing [[Histone Acetylation]] (e.g. H3K9ac, H4K5ac).
- **Consequences:** an open, transcriptionally permissive chromatin state that derepresses tumor-suppressor and differentiation genes, induces cell-cycle arrest ([[p21]]), and triggers [[Apoptosis]] in transformed cells.

## Applications

> [!info] Epigenetic therapy and aging
> Beyond oncology (cutaneous T-cell lymphoma), SAHA is a key research tool for probing the [[Epigenetics|epigenome]]. Because HDAC activity shapes [[Epigenetic Alterations]] during aging and [[Senescence]], HDAC inhibitors are studied for their ability to remodel chromatin, and are contrasted with [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]]-based NAD⁺-dependent deacetylation.

> [!warning] Toxicity
> Common adverse effects include fatigue, gastrointestinal upset, [[Thrombocytopenia]], and QT prolongation.
