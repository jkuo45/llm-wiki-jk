# Calcium Channel Blockers

**Calcium channel blockers** are cardiovascular drugs that reduce calcium entry
through voltage-gated channels, principally L-type (CaV1.2), by binding an
intracellular allosteric site on the α1 subunit. They are divided into two
chemically and clinically distinct classes whose shared mechanism is the only
thing they have in common.

> [!important] The class division that matters clinically
> **Dihydropyridines (DHPs)** act almost exclusively on vascular smooth muscle,
> producing vasodilation with minimal effect on cardiac conduction, and carry
> the "-dipine" suffix. **Non-dihydropyridines (non-DHPs)** — the
> phenylalkylamine verapamil and the benzothiazepine diltiazem — act on
> the sinoatrial and atrioventricular nodes and on myocardial contractility as
> well as on vessels. That is why DHPs treat blood pressure and non-DHPs treat
> rate and rhythm.

## Mechanism & Pharmacology

CCBs bind allosterically within the inner pore of the channel's α1 subunit; the
pharmacophore that permits binding is itself a dihydropyridine ring, which is
why both classes occupy the same pocket with distinct affinity and geometry.
Channel block reduces Ca²⁺ influx, and because Ca²⁺ is the obligate second
messenger for smooth muscle contraction, vascular tone falls.

Both subclasses are metabolized largely by [[CYP3A4]], and the non-DHPs are
additionally **CYP3A4 inhibitors** themselves.

> [!warning] The interaction consequences are asymmetric
> Because non-DHPs inhibit the enzyme that clears them and many other drugs,
> verapamil and diltiazem raise plasma levels of co-administered substrates —
  notably statins, whose myopathy risk rises
> sharply with verapamil. Grapefruit juice inhibits intestinal CYP3A4 and does
> the same to several "-dipines". DHPs are substrates but not meaningful
> inhibitors, so their interaction profile is markedly cleaner.

## Clinical Uses

- **Hypertension.** First-line, and the class of choice in elderly patients and
  in isolated systolic hypertension, where the DHPs are preferred because they
  do not slow cardiac rate.
- **Angina.** Verapamil and diltiazem relieve effort angina both by reducing
  contractility and heart rate; the DHP nifedipine was historically
  associated with reflex tachycardia, largely mitigated by slow-release and
  amlodipine-like formulations.
- **Rate and rhythm control.** Verapamil and diltiazem are Class IV
  antiarrhythmics for supraventricular tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, and
  atrial flutter, where their nodal action is the therapeutic mechanism — they
  are grouped alongside the other Vaughan Williams classes as
  [[Arrhythmias]] therapy.
- **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.** Diltiazem and verapamil improve diastolic
  filling time and are used where beta-blockers are not tolerated.

## Adverse Effects

Vascular-selective DHPs characteristically produce vasodilator-type side
effects: **ankle oedema** (from increased capillary hydrostatic pressure rather
than sodium retention), flushing, headache, and reflex tachycardia. Gum
hyperplasia and reflux aggravation are class effects.

Non-DHPs produce **rate-related** toxicity: bradycardia, AV block, and
negative inotropy, plus constipation for verapamil specifically (over 20%
frequency). Combining a non-DHP with a beta-blocker risks bradycardia and AV
block and is usually avoided.

> [!warning] Contraindications common to non-DHPs
> High-grade sinoatrial or AV block, symptomatic heart failure with reduced
> ejection fraction, and pre-existing bradycardia. Verapamil is additionally
> contraindicated in hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, where its
> inotropic action worsens the outflow obstruction.

## Connections
- [[Amiodarone]] — a Class III antiarrhythmic with distinct mechanism but
  overlapping clinical territory; both are rate-controlling agents in atrial
  fibrillation and both are heavily metabolized by [[CYP3A4]], which is why
  co-administration of many CCBs with amiodarone raises levels of both drugs.
- [[Cytochrome P450]] — [[CYP3A4]] metabolizes essentially all CCBs, making the
  class one of the most common substrate sets for CYP3A4 inhibition-driven
  interactions in clinical pharmacology. It is also the mechanism behind the
  statin-interaction warning.
- Statins — verapamil in particular raises
  simvastatin and atorvastatin exposure and myopathy risk; the interaction is
  driven by CYP3A4 inhibition, not by a direct pharmacological effect of the
  statin.
- [[Arrhythmias]] — CCBs enter the Vaughan Williams classification as Class IV,
  working by blocking L-type calcium current in nodal tissue. They occupy a
  different class from sodium, potassium, and beta-adrenergic blockers but can
  be combined with them, which is where the interaction burden comes from.
- [[Hypertension]] — the largest single indication, and the reason the DHP
  subclass was designed to be vascular-selective: at vasodilator doses it
  lowers blood pressure without the rate slowing that would be intolerable
  chronically.
- [[Potassium]] — there is a functional counterweight worth noting: calcium
  channel blockade tends to *raise* serum potassium, so CCBs are among the
  drug classes used when potassium must be increased, and they do not carry
  the hypokalaemia risk of thiazide-type diuretics.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — vascular oxidative stress blunts
  calcium-channel-dependent relaxation and drives endothelial dysfunction,
  which is the mechanistic reason endothelial tone — not just contractility — is
  a legitimate CCB target in early vascular disease.
