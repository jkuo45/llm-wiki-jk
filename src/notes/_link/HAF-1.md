# HAF-1

**Overview:** [[HAF-1]] (HIV-1 accessory factor homolog) is an ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporter localized to the inner membrane of [[Mitochondria]] in *Caenorhabditis elegans*. It is the central effector that transmits mitochondrial stress to the nucleus.

**Mechanism of action:** In response to mitochondrial proteotoxic stress, HAF-1 translocates to the inner membrane and exports peptides—generated by mitochondrial proteases—from the matrix into the cytosol. These peptides serve as retrograde signals that activate the transcription factor [[ATFS-1]], driving the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPRmt), a transcriptional program that restores mitochondrial proteostasis and promotes stress resistance and longevity.

**Evolutionary conservation:** The mammalian ortholog [[Mdl1]] (also called ABCB10/ABCB7 family members) performs analogous peptide export, indicating conserved principles of mitochondrial-to-nuclear communication.

**Physiological and pathological relevance:** HAF-1-mediated signaling links mitochondrial dysfunction to systemic [[Aging]] and stress resistance, and exemplifies how mitochondrial peptide export coordinates proteostasis, metabolism, and lifespan across tissues.
