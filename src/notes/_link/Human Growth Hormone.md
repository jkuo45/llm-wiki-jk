# Human Growth Hormone

[[Human Growth Hormone]] (HGH, [[Growth Hormone|GH]], somatotropin) is a pituitary-derived peptide hormone that stimulates IGF-1 production and somatic growth. Although widely marketed for rejuvenation and longevity, exogenous HGH is regarded as a *pro-aging* intervention because it indirectly activates [[mTOR]] and accelerates aging phenotypes ([Blagosklonny, 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6814615/)).

## Mechanism and Aging Relevance

Growth hormone signaling activates the [[mTOR]] pathway downstream of IGF-1/[[AKT]], promoting anabolic growth. Chronically elevated GH/IGF-1 signaling is associated with increased cancer risk and shortened lifespan, contrasting with the growth-suppressive, life-extending effects of [[Rapamycin]]-mediated mTOR inhibition. The popularity of HGH for anti-aging rests largely on a single publication later criticized for misinterpreting acute effects.

> [!warning] Pro-aging, not anti-aging
> Unlike rapamycin, HGH accelerates aging via mTOR activation; it is cited as a cautionary "empty promise" alongside [[Antioxidants]] in the longevity literature.

## Thymic Rejuvenation

Despite its pro-aging properties in longevity contexts, GH has been investigated for thymic rejuvenation in settings of immune deficiency:

- GH impacts the [[Immune System|immune system]] via stimulatory effects on [[IGF1|IGF-1]]
- Hematopoietic cells can produce GH that indirectly promotes immune reconstitution via IGF-1 production
- IGF-1 provides anti-apoptotic signals and promotes immune cell survival
- IGF-1 acts directly on [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|thymic stromal cells]] to stimulate [[IL-7]] production
- Intrathymic IL-7 promotes and regulates [[T Lymphocyte|T cell]] survival and function

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Thymic Rejuvenation and Aging]]
> Multiple studies demonstrate that exogenous GH administration allows for regeneration of aged or damaged [[Thymus|thymus]]. Following syngeneic [[Bone Marrow|bone marrow]] transplantation, GH administration enhances [[Hematopoiesis|hematopoiesis]] and increases spleen and bone marrow hematopoietic progenitors (Tian et al., 1998; Murphy & Longo, 2000).

### Clinical trials
- **NIA/NIH study** (NCT00663611) — Investigating subcutaneous pulsatile GH (Somatropin) on age-induced immune senescence in adults (25–50 years)
- **HIV/AIDS trials** — Three closed studies testing GH for thymus regrowth and naïve T cell repertoire recapitulation in HIV patients (NCT0071240, NCT00287677, NCT00119769)

### Paradox
GH presents a paradox: it is pro-aging via [[mTOR]] activation, yet it promotes thymic rejuvenation and immune reconstitution. This suggests context-dependent effects where short-term immune restoration may be beneficial despite long-term aging concerns.
