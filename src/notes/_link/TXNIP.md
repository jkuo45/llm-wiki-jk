# TXNIP

**TXNIP (Thioredoxin-interacting protein)** is a redox-sensitive negative regulator of the [[Thioredoxin]] system. It links [[Oxidative Stress]] to [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] activation, IL-1β maturation, and metabolic dysfunction, thereby coupling cellular redox state to innate immunity and glucose homeostasis. Its repression preserves pancreatic β-cell function and insulin secretion.

## Structure and Mechanism

TXNIP is an α-arrestin family protein that, under basal conditions, binds reduced [[Thioredoxin]] (TRX) at its active-site cysteines ([[Cysteine|Cys32]], [[Cysteine|Cys35]]), inhibiting TRX's reductive/antioxidant activity. When oxidants accumulate, TRX is oxidized and releases TXNIP. Free TXNIP translocates to the [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]], directly binding NLRP3 and promoting ASC speck formation, caspase-1 activation, and maturation of [[IL-1β|IL-1β]] and IL-18. By suppressing TRX, TXNIP also elevates intracellular ROS (loss of thioredoxin antioxidant buffering), reinforcing the oxidative signal — a positive feedback loop between redox stress and inflammation.

## Metabolic Regulation

TXNIP is transcriptionally induced by glucose through [[ChREBP]] and [[MondoA]] (MondoA–MLX) carbohydrate-response elements, making it a key sensor of nutrient excess. High glucose and [[Diabetes Mellitus|hyperglycemia]] raise TXNIP in pancreatic β-cells, where it promotes IL-1β production, ER stress, and β-cell apoptosis — a mechanism linking metabolic stress to islet inflammation (metaflammation). AMPK phosphorylation suppresses TXNIP, so [[Metformin]] and exercise lower TXNIP expression.

## Regulation by Sirtuins

In pancreatic β-cells SIRT6 preserves glucose-stimulated insulin secretion and survival by repressing TXNIP expression via histone deacetylation (H3K9/H3K56), linking chromatin state to secretory capacity (Szczęsny-Małysiak et al., 2026).

## Physiological and Pathological Roles

TXNIP restrains thioredoxin-dependent tumor survival, and its upregulation can suppress growth of some cancers; conversely, TXNIP deficiency promotes metabolic dysfunction. TXNIP is implicated in: [[Diabetes Mellitus]] (type 2 β-cell failure), [[Cardiovascular Disease]] (endothelial NLRP3 activation, foam-cell formation), [[Neurodegeneration]] (microglial inflammasome activation), and [[kidney disease]] (diabetic nephropathy, tubular inflammation). TXNIP-knockout mice are protected from diet-induced obesity, insulin resistance, and hepatic steatosis, highlighting its role in metabolic syndrome.

## Therapeutic Targeting

Because TXNIP bridges redox and inflammation, it is a candidate drug target. [[Verapamil]] (a phenylalkylamine calcium-channel blocker) inhibits TXNIP transcription and preserves β-cell function in early type 1 diabetes trials. NRF2 activators and AMPK agonists indirectly suppress TXNIP, while strategies to maintain reduced thioredoxin may block inflammasome priming. Small-molecule TXNIP inhibitors are in preclinical development.
