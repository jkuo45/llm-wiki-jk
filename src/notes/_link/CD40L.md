# CD40L

**Overview:** CD40 ligand — CD40L, CD154, *TNFSF5* — is a type II transmembrane glycoprotein of the [[TNF Signaling|TNF superfamily]], expressed on activated CD4+ T cells (and a subset of CD8+ T cells, [[Platelets|platelets]], [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]], and mast cells). It is the sole ligand for CD40, a receptor found almost exclusively on professional antigen-presenting cells. The CD40/CD40L axis is the paradigmatic **T-cell help** signal.

## Structure and domains

- **Extracellular C-terminal TNF homology domain** (~residues 34–261), with the characteristic fold of the TNF family: a β-sheet-rich "jelly roll" that trimerizes into a bell-shaped homotrimer via a membrane-proximal trimerization stalk. The receptor-binding surface is the trimer apex.
- **Membrane-proximal stalk** (~residues 26–34) mediates trimerization and surface localization.
- **Cytoplasmic tail** — only ~30 residues, but functionally important: it carries motifs for association with **Lyn** (via a proline-rich/SH3-binding region) and with the actin cytoskeleton through Ezrin, localizing CD40L to the immunological synapse and microvilli. Truncated soluble isoforms arise from alternative mRNA splicing (soluble CD40L, sCD40L) and are biologically active.

> [!info] A non-membrane-restricted cytokine
> Unlike most TNF-family ligands, CD40L is expressed predominantly on *activated* T cells rather than constitutively, and a substantial fraction is released as a soluble trimer. Soluble CD40L is measurable in plasma and has been investigated as a biomarker of autoimmune activity.

## Mechanism

CD40 is a member of the TNF receptor family lacking a death domain; its signalling recruits the adaptor **TRAF2/TRAF3/TRAF5/6** family.

- **On B cells:** CD40 signalling is delivered *alongside* BCR signalling and is required for T-dependent, thymus-dependent antibody responses. It drives B-cell proliferation, germinal centre formation, activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) expression, somatic hypermutation, class-switch recombination, and differentiation into long-lived plasma and memory cells. CD40-deficient B cells cannot form germinal centres or switch isotype.
- **On dendritic cells:** CD40 licensing increases co-stimulatory molecule (CD80/CD86) and cytokine output, making dendritic cells potent T-cell primers — the dendritic cell and B cell licences are jointly required for optimal T-cell priming.
- **On [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]] and other APCs:** promotes inflammatory and pro-survival gene expression.

Downstream, the outcome depends on cell type: B cells proliferate and differentiate, and myeloid cells produce pro-inflammatory cytokines via [[NF-κB]] and [[JAK-STAT Signaling|STAT]] pathways.

## Clinical relevance

| Setting | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| X-linked hyper-IgM syndrome (*CD40LG* loss-of-function) | Blocked class switching and germinal centres; patients present with recurrent pyogenic infection and *Pneumocystis*; serum IgM normal or high, IgG/IgA/IgE low; neutropenia common |
| Autosomal recessive CD40 deficiency | Similar immunophenotype |
| Solid tumours | CD40 agonism on dendritic cells and B cells is a potent immunotherapeutic — in combination with checkpoint blockade it can convert immunologically "cold" tumours |
| [[Atherosclerosis]] | CD40–CD40L signalling on endothelial cells, [[Macrophages|macrophages]] and [[T Cell|T cells]] drives plaque initiation and progression; blockade reduces lesions in mice |
| Autoimmunity | Elevated soluble CD40L is reported in [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus|lupus]], [[Multiple Sclerosis|multiple sclerosis]], and [[Rheumatoid Arthritis|rheumatoid arthritis]]; CD40–CD40L antagonism is in trials |
| Hematopoietic stem cell transplant | Host CD40L on activated T cells drives graft-versus-host disease; blockade reduces GVHD but also impairs engraftment |
| Vaccination | CD40 agonism is a potent vaccine adjuvant, and CD40 deficiency causes absent germinal centres after immunization |

> [!warning] Antagonism is a double-edged sword
> CD40 blockade simultaneously suppresses protective immunity. The therapeutic window in autoimmunity and in transplantation is narrow, and CD40 agonists and antagonists are both in active clinical development — the same receptor axis in opposite directions.

## Connections
- CD40 — the receptor; the CD40/CD40L pair is the defining T-cell-help axis, and CD40 is expressed on B cells, dendritic cells and myeloid cells.
- [[TNF Signaling]] — CD40L is a TNF-superfamily ligand, so its receptor signalling converges on the TRAF adaptor cascade.
- [[B Cell]] — B cells require CD40L for germinal centre formation, somatic hypermutation, and class-switch recombination.
- [[T Cell]] — activated CD4+ T cells are the main source of CD40L; the ligand is itself induced by T-cell receptor signalling.
- [[NF-κB]] — the dominant downstream transcriptional effector of CD40 signalling in both B cells and myeloid cells.
- [[Senescence]] — CD40L is listed as a cell-surface SASP regulator in senescent cells, connecting T-cell help to the senescent inflammatory programme.
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — CD40L on platelets, T cells, and macrophages is a central driver of atherogenesis.
- [[Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors]] — CD40 agonists are studied as combinations with checkpoint blockade to prime tumour immunity.
