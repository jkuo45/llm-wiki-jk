# Topical Application

**Topical application** is the administration of an active compound directly to the skin or mucosa, producing local bioavailability at the application site with minimal systemic exposure. In dermatology and skin-longevity research it is the delivery route for actives whose target cells — [[Keratinocyte|keratinocytes]], [[Fibroblast|dermal fibroblasts]], and the dermal [[Extracellular Matrix]] — reside in the tissue itself.

## Determinants of Efficacy

- **Penetration.** The compound must cross the stratum corneum to reach viable epidermis and dermis. Hydrophilic zwitterions such as [[Creatine]] may use passive diffusion or specific transporters ([[SLC6A8]]) and are often formulated with liposomes or penetration enhancers; lipophilic polyphenols such as [[Urolithin A]] rely on vehicle-dependent partitioning.
- **Formulation.** Concentration and vehicle determine whether a biologically active tissue level is reached. In topical urolithin A trials, 1% was effective while 0.5% was null for both wrinkle depth and UVB erythema.
- **Local tolerability.** Irritation and sensitization limit long-term use. The urolithin A program used a human repeat-insult patch test (HRIPT, n = 112 enrolled, 109 completed) with no adverse reactions; topical creatine studies likewise reported no irritation at standard concentrations.

> [!warning]
> Topical studies often do not quantify drug levels in skin tissue by LC-MS/MS. The urolithin A trials infer biologically active concentrations from downstream biomarker and clinical changes rather than direct dermal pharmacokinetics; formal skin-tissue quantification remains an identified gap.

## Evidence in Skin Longevity

- [[Urolithin A]] 1% cream: reduced periorbital wrinkle depth after 8 weeks (p = 0.0437 vs placebo) and UVB-induced erythema by 13% at 1.6 MED (p = 0.02), with transcriptomic and mitophagy-marker support.
- [[Creatine]] 0.2% with glycerol/guarana: reduced jowl sagging and crow's-feet wrinkles at 6 weeks; folic-acid/creatine combinations improved firmness and elasticity — effects not attributable to creatine alone.
