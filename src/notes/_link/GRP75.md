# GRP75

**GRP75** (glucose-regulated protein 75; mortalin; HSPA9) is a member of the Hsp70 family of molecular chaperones that localizes predominantly to the mitochondrial matrix, where it assists protein import, folding, and stress responses. Beyond its chaperone function, GRP75 forms a physical and functional bridge at [[Mitochondria-Associated Membranes|mitochondria-associated ER membranes (MAMs)]]: it links the ER-resident inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor ([[IP3R2|IP3R]]) to the mitochondrial outer membrane channel [[VDAC1]], thereby gating calcium transfer from the ER into mitochondria. Through this complex, GRP75 couples ER calcium release to mitochondrial calcium uptake, shaping ATP production, apoptosis sensitivity, and oxidative signaling.

## GRP75 in diabetic neurotoxicity

Chronic [[Hyperglycemia|hyperglycemia]] suppresses [[SIRT3]] expression in the [[Hippocampus|hippocampus]], contributing to cognitive deficits through mitochondrial Ca2+ overload and neuronal [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]][246]. SIRT3 overexpression mitigates neuronal injury by inhibiting the VDAC1/GRP75/IP3R complex, thereby reducing MAM formation and protecting neurons from metabolic stress-induced apoptosis[247]. GRP75 is therefore a structural effector of the pathological ER-mitochondria calcium coupling that SIRT3 opposes in diabetic encephalopathy.

## Clinical relevance

The VDAC1–GRP75–IP3R complex is an emerging pharmacological target across neurodegeneration, metabolic disease, and ischemia-reperfusion injury. In the diabetes setting, SIRT3-dependent modulation of this MAM complex represents a mitochondrial-nuclear-ER crosstalk node linking deacetylase activity to calcium homeostasis and neuronal survival.
