# JAK

**Janus kinase (JAK)** is a family of intracellular, receptor-associated tyrosine kinases (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2) that transduce signals from type I/II cytokine receptors — which lack intrinsic catalytic activity — to the nucleus. They are named for the two near-identical kinase domains in each protein: a catalytically active C-terminal kinase domain and a pseudokinase domain that organizes activation.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

Cytokine binding induces dimerization of cognate receptor chains, bringing their bound JAKs into apposition. Trans-phosphorylation of activation-loop tyrosines (e.g., JAK2 Tyr1007/1008) switches the kinases on, after which they phosphorylate specific receptor tyrosine residues that serve as docking sites for Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription (STAT) proteins. STATs are recruited via their SH2 domains, are themselves tyrosine-phosphorylated (e.g., STAT1 Tyr701, STAT3 Tyr705, STAT6 Tyr641), dimerize, and translocate to the nucleus to drive transcription of inflammatory and growth genes. The division of labor is receptor-specific: JAK1 pairs with JAK2 or TYK2 for interleukins, JAK3 is restricted to the common γ-chain (IL-2, IL-4, IL-15), and JAK2 mediates erythropoietin, growth hormone, and leptin signaling. Negative regulation is provided by SOCS proteins and phosphatases (SHP-1, PTPN6).

## Role in Senescence / SASP

The JAK/STAT axis is a central amplifier of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]. Senescent cells secrete [[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], and other cytokines that engage neighboring cells in an autocrine/paracrine loop: IL-6 binding to gp130 activates JAK1/JAK2 → [[STAT3]], sustaining a self-reinforcing inflammatory transcriptional program (Coppé et al., 2008, PMID 18694530). Genetic or pharmacological blockade of JAK/STAT collapses SASP expression, indicating the pathway is not merely correlative but necessary for SASP maintenance. Because JAK/STAT signaling also promotes proliferation in some contexts, its inhibition is exploited as a **senomorphic** strategy — dampening the deleterious secretome without killing cells.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

JAK inhibitors (jakinibs) are approved for myeloproliferative neoplasms, rheumatoid arthritis, and graft-versus-host disease. [[Ruxolitinib]] (JAK1/2 inhibitor) and related agents are under active investigation as senomorphics to suppress chronic SASP-driven [[Inflammation|inflammation]] and [[Inflammaging]], and have been shown to reduce SASP factor secretion in senescence models. JAK/STAT hyperactivity is also implicated in cytokine-release syndromes and several malignancies, making the pathway a broadly actionable node linking senescence, immunity, and aging.
