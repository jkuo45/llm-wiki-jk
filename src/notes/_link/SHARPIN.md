# SHARPIN

**SHARPIN** (SHANK-associated RH domain interactor; also annotated as *Sipl1*) is a ubiquitin-binding adaptor and one of the three components of the **linear ubiquitin chain assembly complex (LUBAC)** together with HOIL-1 and HOIP. In the necroptosis literature it is best known as a [[RIPK1]]-regulator: its loss licenses TNFR1-dependent inflammatory disease, and it links LUBAC-dependent linear ubiquitination to the survival/death decision at TNFR1 complex I.

## Structure & Function

- SHARPIN participates in LUBAC, the only E3 ligase complex that generates **linear (M1-linked) polyubiquitin** chains. Linear ubiquitination of [[RIPK1]] (and NEMO, see [[NEMO]]) is required for full [[NF-κB]] pro-survival signaling downstream of [[TNFR1]].
- Its loss therefore destabilizes the ubiquitination code that keeps RIPK1 in its pro-survival mode, shifting the balance toward death signaling.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Chronic proliferative dermatitis.** Spontaneous mutations in the mouse *Sharpin* gene produce a multiorgan inflammatory phenotype dominated by chronic proliferative dermatitis, immune-system dysregulation, and multiorgan inflammation (Seymour et al., *Genes Immun* 2007).
- **TNFR1-dependence.** The dermatitis is reversed by combined [[TNFR1]] deficiency, placing SHARPIN/LUBAC upstream of TNF-receptor signaling.
- The 2014 Linkermann & Green review notes that it remains unclear how [[RIPK3]]-dependent [[Necroptosis]] participates in the SHARPIN-deficient inflammatory reaction.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]]
> SHARPIN deficiency is cited as a skin/intestinal example of RIPK1-regulator loss causing TNFR1-dependent inflammatory disease — mechanistically linked to RIPK1 ubiquitination status, with the degree of necroptosis involvement still open as of 2014.

## Connections
