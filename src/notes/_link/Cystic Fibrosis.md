# Cystic Fibrosis

**Cystic fibrosis (CF)** is an autosomal recessive multisystem disease caused by loss-of-function mutations in the *CFTR* gene on chromosome 7q31.2, encoding an epithelial chloride and bicarbonate channel. Failure of salt and water secretion — with unopposed sodium and water absorption — produces dehydrated, viscous secretions in essentially every exocrine organ.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

- **Genetics:** >2,000 pathogenic variants; the most common by far is **F508del** (a three-nucleotide deletion producing a misfolded protein degraded in the ER), present on at least one allele in ~85–90% of patients. Compound heterozygotes span classic (two severe) through residual-function and benign variants, giving a wide phenotypic spectrum.
- **Channel failure:** F508del protein fails to reach the plasma membrane. Class II (F508del), class I (g nonsense, nonsense-mediated decay), class III (gating, e.g. G551D), class IV (conductance, e.g. R117H) and class VI (processing) mechanisms are the main categories.
- **Airway:** thick mucus impairs mucociliary clearance, so infection is established early with *Staphylococcus aureus* and then chronic *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. Neutrophil-dominated inflammation, protease release and chronic bacterial biofilm drive bronchiectasis and progressive decline in lung function.
- **Pancreas:** ductal obstruction causes exocrine pancreatic insufficiency in ~85% of patients, with steatorrhoea, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency and failure to thrive; CF-related diabetes mellitus follows loss of islet mass and is more common with better pulmonary survival.
- **Other organs:** obstructive azoospermia (male infertility), biliary cirrhosis and focal biliary cirrhosis, nephrolithiasis, and bone disease (osteopenia from malabsorption and chronic inflammation).
- **Inflammation phenotype:** airway neutrophils show excess [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] release, exaggerated [[Inflammation]] and accelerated senescence; CFTR-deficient airway epithelium also shows impaired autophagy resolution and increased [[Cellular Senescence]] markers — a link to the ageing literature.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

- **Pulmonary:** chronic productive cough, recurrent chest infections, bronchiectasis, haemoptysis, digital clubbing; progressive airflow obstruction on spirometry (FEV1 decline is the key prognostic marker).
- **Gastrointestinal:** meconium ileus in neonates, pancreatic insufficiency with malabsorption, CF-related diabetes.
- **Diagnosis:** newborn screening by immunoreactive trypsinogen; confirmation by sweat chloride test (>60 mmol/L) and *CFTR* genotyping; a sweat chloride of 30–59 mmol/L is intermediate and requires extended testing.
- **Biomarkers:** fecal elastase-1 for pancreatic function, faecal calprotectin and sputum neutrophil elastase for inflammation, blood eosinophils for CFTR modulator response; sweat rate and lung clearance index (mucociliary clearance) for therapy monitoring.

## Therapeutic Landscape

- **Airway clearance and antibiotics:** airway clearance techniques, inhaled and systemic antibiotics (including inhaled tobramycin and aztreonam against *Pseudomonas*), and dornase alfa (DNAse) to degrade neutrophil DNA in sputum.
- **CFTR modulators:** **correctors** (lumacaftor, tezacaftor, elexacaftor) rescue folding and trafficking of F508del; **potentiators** (ivacaftor) increase the open probability of channels that reach the surface. The triple combination elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor has transformed outcomes, improving lung function, sweat chloride, nutritional status and survival, and reducing exacerbations in patients aged 12 and older (and from 6 years in the two-pill tezacaftor/ivacaftor regimen). Modulators are genotype-specific; the rarer class III variants (e.g. G551D, ivacaftor; ivacaftor/tezacaftor) and residual-function alleles are also targeted.
- **Nutritional and metabolic support:** pancreatic enzyme replacement, fat-soluble vitamin supplementation, high-calorie diet, and insulin for CF-related diabetes.
- **Transplant:** lung transplantation for end-stage disease; CFTR gene therapy remains investigational.
- **Anti-inflammatory and novel approaches:** trials of CFTR amplifiers, once-daily modulators, and anti-inflammatory agents are ongoing; modulator response and lung function remain the practical endpoints.
