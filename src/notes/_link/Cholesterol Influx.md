# Cholesterol Influx

**Cholesterol influx** (or cholesterol uptake) is the movement of [[Cholesterol]] molecules from the extracellular environment into cells, primarily [[Macrophages]]. It is the opposing process to [[Cholesterol Efflux]] and contributes to cellular cholesterol accumulation when not balanced by efflux.

## Mechanism

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Cholesterol influx is defined as the movement of cholesterol molecules from the extracellular environment to cells. This process, mainly observed in macrophages, plays a regulatory role in cellular cholesterol homeostasis. Resveratrol significantly reduces cholesterol influx into J774 macrophages in a dose-dependent manner (r² = 0.89, p = 0.015).

- Cholesterol influx occurs primarily through receptor-mediated uptake of lipoproteins
- In macrophages, excess cholesterol influx leads to [[Foam Cells]] formation
- The balance between cholesterol influx and [[Cholesterol Efflux]] determines cellular cholesterol homeostasis

## Regulation

- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Reduces cholesterol influx into macrophages in a dose-dependent manner
- **Scavenger receptors**: Mediate uptake of oxidized LDL into macrophages
- **LPL and SR-AII**: Lipid uptake genes repressed by resveratrol via LXR-α

## Clinical Relevance

- Excessive cholesterol influx without corresponding efflux leads to foam cell formation
- Reducing cholesterol influx is a therapeutic strategy for [[Atherosclerosis]]
