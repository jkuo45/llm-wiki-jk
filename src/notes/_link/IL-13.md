# IL-13

**Interleukin-13 (IL-13)** is a pleiotropic cytokine produced primarily by activated [[Th2]] cells, mast cells, and innate lymphoid cells (ILC2s). It shares significant structural and functional homology with [[IL-4]], as both cytokines use the **IL-4Rα** receptor subunit and activate **STAT6** signaling. IL-13 is a central mediator of allergic inflammation and type 2 immune responses.

## Structure & Receptor

IL-13 is a four-helix bundle cytokine that signals through a receptor complex containing **IL-4Rα** (shared with [[IL-4]]) and **IL-13Rα1**. A decoy receptor, **IL-13Rα2**, binds IL-13 with high affinity but does not transduce signaling, instead acting as a sink to limit IL-13 activity. IL-13 can also signal through the Type I receptor (IL-4Rα/IL-13Rα1) and the Type II receptor (IL-4Rα/IL-13Rα1).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

IL-13 binds the IL-4Rα/IL-13Rα1 complex, activating **JAK1** and **TYK2**, which phosphorylate **STAT6**. Activated STAT6 dimerizes and translocates to the nucleus, driving transcription of genes involved in mucus production, tissue remodeling, and alternatively activated macrophage polarization. IL-13 also activates **PI3K**, **MAPK**, and **JNK** pathways.

### Key Downstream Effects

- Induces **goblet cell hyperplasia** and mucus hypersecretion in airways (via *MUC5AC* upregulation).
- Promotes **alternative macrophage activation** (M2 polarization) through [[STAT6]] and [[PPARγ]].
- Stimulates **IgE class switching** in B cells (partially through [[IL-4]] synergy).
- Enhances **smooth muscle contractility** and airway hyperresponsiveness.
- Drives **tissue fibrosis** via TGF-β and direct pro-fibrotic signaling.

## Biological Functions

### Allergic Airway Inflammation
IL-13 is a master regulator of the asthmatic phenotype, directly inducing the cardinal features of asthma: airway hyperresponsiveness, mucus overproduction, eosinophilic inflammation, and subepithelial fibrosis.

### Anti-Helminth Immunity
IL-13 promotes expulsion of parasitic worms by stimulating mucus production, smooth muscle contraction, and goblet cell metaplasia in the gut.

### Tissue Remodeling
IL-13 activates fibroblasts and promotes collagen deposition, contributing to both pathological fibrosis (pulmonary fibrosis, liver fibrosis) and wound healing.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **Asthma**: IL-13 is overexpressed in asthmatic airways and drives key disease features. **Dupilumab** (anti-IL-4Rα) blocks both IL-4 and IL-13 signaling and is approved for moderate-to-severe asthma.
- **Atopic Dermatitis**: IL-13 is the predominant cytokine in atopic dermatitis skin lesions; dupilumab is also approved for this indication.
- **Allergic Rhinitis**: IL-13 contributes to nasal mucus production and eosinophilic inflammation.
- **Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis**: IL-13 promotes fibroblast activation and collagen synthesis in the lung.
- **Eosinophilic Esophagitis**: IL-13-driven epithelial remodeling is a key pathological feature.
