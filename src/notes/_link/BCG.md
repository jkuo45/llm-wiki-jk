# BCG

## Overview

BCG is a live attenuated vaccine strain of *Mycobacterium bovis* developed between
1908 and 1921 by Albert Calmette and Camille Guérin, and used intradermally
as a vaccine against tuberculosis. It is also — and uniquely among vaccines —
the only **cancer** immunotherapy with a hundred-year clinical record: instilled
directly into the bladder, it is the standard first-line adjuvant treatment for
high-risk non-muscle-invasive [[Bladder Cancer]].

Its mechanism of action was never fully defined, which is itself the central
point of the modern literature.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The NOME and CISTOR trials showed that BCG is **not** superior to the much
> cheaper cytotoxic intravesical agent mitomycin C for the primary endpoint of
> progression-free survival, and many patients cannot tolerate BCG. Its durable
> advantage is in the subset of high-risk patients who recur after BCG, where
> responders have a markedly better prognosis. Benefit is also real but modest
> on absolute recurrence risk.

## Mechanism of action

The bacteriological origin explains the pharmacology. Myco- bacterial cell wall
ligands — lipoproteins, lipoarabinomannan, and CpG-rich DNA — engage
[[Toll-like Receptor|TLRs]] (notably TLR2, TLR4 and TLR9), [[NOD2]], and
cytosolic sensors such as [[STING]]. Instilled organisms are taken up by
urothelial cells and by recruited [[Macrophage|macrophages]] and
[[Dendritic cells|dendritic cells]], and provoke a local
[[Inflammation|innate inflammatory]] response that matures those dendritic
cells.

> [!info] Mechanism
> The best-supported downstream model is a **local type 1 T-cell response**.
> BCG-infected or BCG-stimulated cells secrete [[IL-12]] and
> [[Interferon-gamma|IFN-γ]], which license [[T Helper Cell|CD4+ Th1]]
> differentiation; recruited lymphocytes produce [[TNF-alpha|TNF]],
> IL-2 and IFN-γ, and form microscopic [[Granuloma|granulomas]] along the
> urothelium. This recruits and activates additional macrophages and
> [[T-Lymphocyte|cytotoxic T cells]] that kill neighbouring tumour cells
> nonspecifically. The early hypothesis that BCG surface antigens
> **cross-react** with putative bladder-tumour antigens is largely abandoned —
> the effect is better explained as a nonspecific, locally-recruited Th1
> environment, though BCG-specific and tumour-specific T cells are both
> detectable.
>
> Urothelial cells themselves also respond: mycobacterial ligation of TLRs
> triggers [[Nitric Oxide]] and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] release, and
> cytokine release, and BCG can induce [[Pyroptosis]] and [[Apoptosis]] in
> tumour cells directly.

The sustained immunological memory of intravesical BCG — why some patients who
respond once stay disease-free for a decade — remains unexplained, and is the
subject of ongoing [[Trained Immunity]] and [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] research on
bladder-resident macrophage and urothelial cell populations.

## Clinical use in bladder cancer

**Administration.** Following transurethral resection of bladder tumour
(TURBT), 45–81 mg of lyophilised BCG in 50 mL saline is instilled through a
catheter for 60–120 minutes — the window of time in which urothelial cells are
most permeable. The regimen is an *induction* phase (typically 6 weekly
instillations) followed, in most current practice, by *maintenance*
instillations to a total of 1–3 years.

**Efficacy.**

- Compared with TURBT alone, BCG reduces the risk of recurrence substantially —
  the classic meta-analytic estimate is a 32% relative risk reduction when BCG
  is compared with mitomycin C within maintenance trials (28% increase in
  recurrence risk for BCG versus MMC in the trial set that did not include
  maintenance).
- BCG maintenance lowers recurrence risk by roughly 21% and improves
  recurrence-free survival by roughly 33% versus induction alone, with
  network meta-analysis finding no clear difference between maintenance
  durations beyond 1 year.
- Dose is not trivial: one randomised study reported a 27.1% recurrence rate
  with reduced-frequency maintenance versus 12% at standard frequency.

**BCG-unresponsive disease.** Guidelines define a patient as BCG-unresponsive
after an initial complete response followed by subsequent high-grade
recurrence, CIS, or any BCG failure. This matters because it gates access to
the next line of therapy: [[Pembrolizumab]] was approved by the FDA in 2020 on
the strength of KEYNOTE-057 cohort A, in which the complete response rate in 96
patients with BCG-unresponsive high-risk NMIBC with carcinoma in situ was 41%
(95% CI 31–51) with a median response duration of 16.2 months (PMID 32155034).

**Adverse effects.** Nearly all patients develop irritative urinary symptoms and
microscopic haematuria. Systemic BCG infection — bacteraemia, granulomatous
prostatitis, and rare disseminated BCG infection with pulmonary or bone marrow
involvement — occurs at a low but real rate and requires antituberculous
therapy. BCG instillation is contraindicated in pregnancy and in patients with
significant immunosuppression.

## Other clinical uses

- **Tuberculosis prophylaxis.** The standard neonatal vaccine in high-TB-burden
  countries, and one of the few vaccines with a documented, if modest,
  reduction in all-cause infant mortality in trials in the 1950s–60s.
- **Congenital and inherited susceptibility.** Disseminated BCG infection in
  otherwise healthy children after vaccination is the classic presentation of
  [[Mendelian Susceptibility to Mycobacterial Disease]], due to defects in the
  [[Interferon-gamma|IFN-γ]] receptor, IL-12/IL-23 axis, or [[NADPH Oxidase]]
  (as in [[Chronic Granulomatous Disease]]).
- **Intralesional melanoma.** Decades of use in the 1970s–90s, largely
  superseded by modern checkpoint blockade.
- **Bladder-cancer risk and mitigation.** Natives of BCG-vaccinated countries
  have a lower incidence of bladder cancer, consistent with the vaccine
  reducing chronic mycobacterial exposure.

> [!warning] Evidence quality
> A large body of epidemiological literature claims that BCG vaccination
> reduces all-cause adult mortality, dementia risk, and COVID-19 morbidity. The
> better-designed analyses — including a 2021 population study testing whether
> revaccination affects all-cause mortality — found **no such effect**. The
> countries-with-vs-without comparisons that underpin most of these claims are
> confounded by geography, health infrastructure, and reporting differences.
> Treat BCG as a tuberculosis vaccine with a genuine bladder-cancer indication,
> not as a general anti-ageing or anti-viral intervention.

## Connections

- [[Bladder Cancer]] — BCG's principal and unique therapeutic indication;
  it is the reference first-line adjuvant for high-risk non-muscle-invasive
  disease, and its failure defines the "BCG-unresponsive" category that gates
  access to [[Pembrolizumab]].
- [[PAMP]] — The PAMP note correctly identifies BCG as a TLR2/4 agonist whose
  structural features are deliberately exploited as an immunotherapeutic
  payload, in the same class of logic as TLR7 agonism with [[Imiquimod]].
- [[Toll-like Receptor]] — TLR2, TLR4 and TLR9 recognition of mycobacterial
  lipoproteins and CpG motifs is the upstream origin of essentially all the
  downstream antitumor effector mechanisms.
- [[Innate Immunity]] — BCG is administered as a live organism precisely to
  hyper-activate innate sensing; the adaptive antitumor response is downstream
  of that innate burst.
- [[Macrophage]] — Recruited and activated macrophages are both the effector
  population for ROS/[[Nitric Oxide]] killing and the source of the
  IL-12 that licenses the Th1 response.
- [[IL-12]] — The key licensing cytokine for IFN-γ production and Th1
  polarization; also the locus of the most common Mendelian BCG susceptibility
  lesions.
- [[NADPH Oxidase]] — Its assembly in phagocytes underlies both BCG-mediated
  ROS cytotoxicity in tumours and the susceptibility to mycobacterial infection
  in [[Chronic Granulomatous Disease]].
- [[Tumor Microenvironment]] — Instilled BCG converts the bladder mucosa from a
  tolerogenic site into a chronically inflamed, Th1-polarised niche, which is
  the closest thing bladder cancer has to a therapeutically useful "hot" TME.
- [[Immune Evasion]] — BCG failure teaches the specific evasion biology that
  the pembrolizumab salvage approach is designed to overcome.
- [[Pyroptosis]] — Mycobacterial sentinel sensing and urothelial cell death
  responses contribute to the local inflammatory environment, though the
  quantitative contribution to tumour control is unquantified.
- [[Chronic Granulomatous Disease]] — The Mendelian deficiency of NADPH
  oxidase that makes disseminated BCG infection a presenting feature, making
  BCG vaccination a contraindication in these patients.
- [[Trained Immunity]] — The durability of response to a single course of
  intravesical BCG is the strongest clinical example of long-lived
  innate-antigen-independent immune memory in solid tumour therapy, and its
  mechanism is still open.
- [[Cisplatin]] — Complementary rather than overlapping: cisplatin is the
  systemic backbone of muscle-invasive disease, BCG is the intravesical
  adjuvant of non-muscle-invasive disease.
