# DPP4

**DPP4** (dipeptidyl-peptidase 4), also known as **CD26**, is a cell-surface serine protease that cleaves N-terminal dipeptides from peptides (including incretin hormones GLP-1/GIP). In [[Colorectal Cancer]] (CRC), DPP4 is a positive regulator of [[Ferroptosis]] through a mechanism in which [[p53]] loss facilitates plasma-membrane-associated DPP4-dependent lipid peroxidation.

## Structure & Biochemistry

DPP4 is a type II transmembrane glycoprotein (and shed soluble form) with a catalytic extracellular domain. It is a validated drug target (gliptins) in diabetes, cleaving incretins to regulate glucose.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review (Xie et al.) reports that p53 *inhibits* erastin-induced ferroptosis in CRC cells by blocking the activity of DPP4—distinct from p53's ferroptosis-promoting role in other cancers. p53 loss inhibits nuclear accumulation of DPP4, facilitating plasma-membrane-associated DPP4-dependent [[Lipid Peroxidation]] that leads to ferroptosis (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Colorectal cancer"). Thus in CRC, DPP4 at the membrane is a ferroptosis-promoting enzyme, restrained by p53.

> [!info] Context-dependent p53 in CRC
> Unlike in many tumors (where p53 promotes ferroptosis via SLC7A11 repression or SAT1/ALOX15), in CRC p53 *suppresses* ferroptosis by restraining DPP4—a striking tissue-specific reversal of p53's ferroptotic role.

## Clinical Relevance

DPP4 is a druggable enzyme; its ferroptosis-promoting role in CRC suggests that DPP4 modulation could tune therapy responses. Combining DPP4-targeted approaches with [[Cisplatin]] (which induces ferroptosis/apoptosis in CRC per the review) is a plausible strategy.
