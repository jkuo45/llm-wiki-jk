# Thromboxane A2

**Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂)** is the prostanoid produced from [[Prostaglandin H₂]] by thromboxane synthase, predominantly in platelets. It is a potent vasoconstrictor and the principal endogenous promoter of platelet aggregation.

## Formation and Chemistry

Thromboxane synthase (TBXAS1), a cytochrome P450-type enzyme, rearranges the endoperoxide bridge of [[Prostaglandin H₂]] to form the oxetane ring characteristic of the thromboxanes. TXA₂ is extremely unstable — its half-life is about 30 seconds at physiological pH, hydrolyzing to the stable but inactive metabolite **thromboxane B₂ (TXB₂)**, which is what is measured in clinical and research samples.

TXA₂ synthesis is concentrated in platelets, where thromboxane synthase is coupled to COX-1. Because platelets are enucleate, they cannot resynthesize COX once it is inhibited — a property exploited pharmacologically.

## Mechanism of Action

TXA₂ acts on the **TP receptor** (a Gq-coupled receptor) on platelets and vascular smooth muscle. In platelets this raises cytosolic calcium, driving shape change, granule release, and aggregation. In smooth muscle it causes contraction. The net effect is a locally pro-thrombotic, vasoconstricted surface at sites of vascular injury.

The physiological counterweight is [[Prostacyclin]] (PGI₂), produced from the same [[Prostaglandin H₂]] pool in endothelium. The **TXA₂/PGI₂ ratio** governs thrombotic tendency; elevation of this ratio is prothrombotic.

> [!info] Aspirin's therapeutic selectivity
> Low-dose aspirin irreversibly acetylates Ser530 of COX-1, blocking [[Prostaglandin H₂]] formation in platelets for the platelet's ~10-day lifespan. Endothelial [[Prostacyclin]] is largely preserved because endothelium is nucleate and can resynthesize COX. This is the mechanistic basis of low-dose aspirin prophylaxis against myocardial infarction and stroke. Non-selective NSAIDs and selective COX-2 inhibitors tip the balance toward thrombosis — the source of the cardiovascular risk signal for COX-2 inhibitors.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Haemostasis**: TXA₂ amplifies platelet recruitment and vasoconstriction at injury sites, limiting blood loss.
- **Atherosclerosis**: plaques overproduce TXA₂; elevated urinary TXB₂ metabolites predict cardiovascular events.
- **Thrombosis**: unopposed TXA₂ drives arterial thrombosis, and its inhibition is protective.
- **Hypertension and vasospasm**: TXA₂ contributes to coronary and cerebral vasospasm; TP receptor antagonists (terutroban) have been trialled as antithrombotics.
- **Renal**: TXA₂ constricts renal vasculature and contributes to reduced glomerular filtration in volume-depleted states.
- **Lipid peroxidation link**: oxidized lipids and [[Oxidized LDL]] upregulate thromboxane synthase and shift the balance toward TXA₂, providing a route by which [[Lipid Peroxidation]] raises thrombotic risk.
