# Aurora Kinase

[[Aurora Kinase|Aurora Kinases]] are a family of conserved serine/threonine kinase isozymes — Aurora-A, Aurora-B, and Aurora-C — that coordinate mitotic spindle assembly, chromosome alignment, segregation, and cytokinesis. Their activity is subject to strict temporal control, and overexpression of Aurora-A/B is a hallmark of many solid tumors.

## Isoforms & Functions

- **Aurora-A (AURKA)**: Localizes to centrosomes and spindle poles; controls centrosome maturation, mitotic entry, and spindle assembly; frequently overexpressed or amplified in cancers.
- **Aurora-B (AURKB)**: Chromosomal passenger complex component; regulates kinetochore-microtubule attachments, the spindle assembly checkpoint, and cytokinesis.
- **Aurora-C (AURKC)**: Testis-restricted under normal conditions; complements Aurora-B in some contexts.

## Role in Sirtuin Biology

Disruption of [[SIRT2]], a [[Tubulin|tubulin deacetylase]] that regulates the cell cycle, increases the levels of mitotic regulators such as Aurora-A and Aurora-B, leading to aneuploidy and mitotic cell death. SIRT2-deficient mice consequently develop gender-specific tumorigenesis (mammary tumors in females, [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma]] in males), linking the sirtuin–Aurora regulatory axis to cancer initiation.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- Aurora-A amplification/overexpression predicts poor prognosis in breast, lung, and pancreatic cancers.
- Selective Aurora kinase inhibitors (e.g., alisertib) are in clinical development; they induce mitotic catastrophe but have been linked to chronic mitotic arrest resistance mechanisms.
