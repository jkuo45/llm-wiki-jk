# Mitochondrial Drivers of Stem Cell Aging and Inflammaging

**Bautista & López-Cortés (2026)** — npj Aging

> [!info] Central Thesis
> [[Mitochondria|Mitochondrial]] dysfunction is not merely a hallmark of [[Aging|aging]] but an upstream driver of [[Stem Cells|stem cell]] exhaustion and [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]]. Mitochondrial damage can be decoded as inflammation through [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] and [[NF-κB]] pathways, while [[NAD+]] depletion represents a metabolic bottleneck that compromises sirtuin-dependent resilience.

## Abstract

This review synthesizes evidence across four interconnected axes:

1. **mtDNA mutations** and clonal mosaicism impair respiration and reshape metabolite availability, reprogramming epigenetic states governing quiescence, lineage commitment, and regenerative output
2. **Erosion of [[Mitochondrial Quality Control|mitochondrial quality control]]** (fission–fusion balance, [[Mitophagy]], [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPR^mt^]]) permits persistence of [[Mitochondrial ROS|ROS-producing organelles]] and lowers containment of mitochondrial danger signals
3. **[[NAD+]] depletion** as a metabolic bottleneck compromising [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]]-dependent resilience and enforcing [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction-Associated Senescence|MiDAS]]
4. **mtDAMP-driven immune sensing**: cytosolic [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] activates [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] and NF-κB pathways, reinforcing senescence-linked cytokine circuits

## Mitochondrial DNA Mutations and Stem Cell Aging

> [!info] mtDNA Mutations as Dual Hallmarks and Drivers
> mtDNA mutational burden can be framed both as a hallmark of aging (clonal mosaicism and rising heteroplasmy in aged tissues) and as a driver of stem cell aging by inducing respiratory insufficiency that feeds into inflammatory signaling and durable metabolic–epigenetic reprogramming.

- Somatic [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] mutations accumulate with age due to high replication frequency, proximity to [[Mitochondrial ROS|ROS]], and relatively inefficient DNA repair compared to nuclear DNA
- **[[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|POLG mutator mice]]** display hematopoietic anomalies, anemia, lymphopenia, and skewed myeloid differentiation — features of stem cell aging — while HSCs retain numbers but lose functional differentiation capacity
- In **intestinal stem cells**, high mtDNA mutation burden reduces LGR5+ cell function and organoid-forming capacity, mediated by ATF5-driven [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPR^mt^]] activation, reversible with [[NMN]] supplementation
- mtDNA mutations reshape stem cell fate by remodeling metabolic–epigenetic coupling: compromised electron transport shifts availability of epigenetically active metabolites (α-ketoglutarate, succinate, fumarate, acetyl-CoA) that gate chromatin-modifying enzymes
- **Mitochondrial age itself** (rather than mtDNA mutation load) can define functionally distinct stem cell subpopulations: selective retention of older mitochondria elevates α-ketoglutarate and promotes TET-dependent epigenetic remodeling

> [!tip] Two Complementary Mechanisms
> (i) mtDNA mutation–driven respiratory insufficiency that reshapes metabolite–epigenome coupling, and (ii) organelle-age–driven mitochondrial heterogeneity that partitions stem cell states and regenerative trajectories.

## Mitochondrial Quality Control in Aging

### Fission–Fusion Balance

- [[Mitochondrial Fusion|Fusion]] ([[MFN1]], [[MFN2]], [[OPA1]]) enables complementation between mitochondria by mixing contents, diluting damaged mtDNA or defective proteins
- [[Mitochondrial Fission|Fission]] ([[DRP1]]) facilitates isolation of dysfunctional mitochondrial fragments for degradation via [[Mitophagy]]
- In aging cells, the balance is disrupted, often favoring fission — associated with increased fragmentation, bioenergetic failure, and apoptosis
- In stem/progenitor compartments, sustained fragmentation maintains stress signaling programs that erode quiescence, bias lineage decisions, and increase vulnerability to inflammatory cues

> [!important] Dynamics Determine Inflammatory Outcomes
> Fusion can buffer the functional impact of heteroplasmic mtDNA mutations through complementation, whereas excessive fission and cristae disorganization can destabilize nucleoids and increase the probability of mtDNA leakage — coupling organelle genome instability to innate immune activation.

### Mitophagy

- The [[PINK1]]–[[Parkin]] pathway orchestrates mitophagy: PINK1 accumulates on depolarized mitochondria, recruits Parkin, and drives ubiquitination of outer membrane proteins
- Aging compromises this pathway at multiple steps: inefficient PINK1 stabilization, defective autophagosome formation, reduced lysosomal clearance
- In stem cell compartments, impaired mitophagy is linked to loss of quiescence, reduced self-renewal, and premature functional exhaustion
- Failed "fission–mitophagy coupling" creates a permissive state where mutant/ROS-producing mitochondria persist long enough to engage mitochondrial danger pathways — mtDNA can translocate to the cytosol and activate [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]]/type I IFN and NF-κB programs

### Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response (UPR^mt^)

- [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPR^mt^]] is triggered by accumulation of misfolded/unassembled proteins due to impaired protein import or mitochondrial translation errors
- Transcription factors [[ATF4]], ATF5, and CHOP mediate UPR^mt^ signaling by inducing genes encoding mitochondrial chaperones (e.g., HSP60), proteases (e.g., LONP1), and antioxidants
- Moderate, properly timed UPR^mt^ activation is linked to improved stress resilience and lifespan extension (hormetic framework)
- Attenuation or dysregulation during aging converts an initially protective response into a chronic stress state
- Restoration of [[NAD+]] levels enhances UPR^mt^ activity; [[PARP]] inhibition can promote mitonuclear communication and UPR^mt^ activation

## NAD+ Metabolism and Mitochondrial Bioenergetic Collapse

> [!info] NAD+ Depletion as a Central Metabolic Axis
> The progressive decline in NAD+ levels during aging has emerged as a central factor in mitochondrial dysfunction, impaired energy production, and stem cell exhaustion.

- [[NAD+]] decline arises from both reduced biosynthetic capacity and increased degradation via [[CD38]], [[PARP1]], and [[SARM1]]
- CD38 becomes hyperactive with age, hydrolyzing NAD+ into ADPR and contributing to calcium imbalance, mitochondrial fragmentation, and oxidative stress
- Reduced NAD+ availability compromises [[SIRT1]] and [[SIRT3]] activity, which regulate [[PGC-1α]], [[FOXO]], and NRF1 transcriptional programs
- A reduced NAD+/NADH ratio triggers [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction-Associated Senescence|MiDAS]] via AMPK–p53 signaling, coupled to a distinct secretory phenotype with attenuated IL-1–dependent inflammatory arm of the [[SASP]]

> [!tip] Stem Cell Vulnerability
> In aged [[Muscle Stem Cell|MuSCs]], neural stem cells, and melanocyte stem cells, NAD+ depletion leads to senescence, loss of quiescence, and diminished regenerative capacity. Zhang et al. demonstrated that restoring NAD+ with [[Nicotinamide Riboside|NR]] reactivated mitochondrial metabolism, upregulated prohibitin proteins, and induced UPR^mt^, delaying stem cell senescence and extending lifespan.

### Amino Acid Modulators of Mitochondrial Resilience

- **[[Taurine]]**: declines with age; linked to improved systemic aging phenotypes upon supplementation; mechanistically tied to mitochondrial fitness through roles in mitochondrial translation via tRNA modification
- **Proline**: can restore mitochondrial function and improve differentiation capacity in senescent/aged progenitor contexts
- **Glutathione synthesis** (glycine and cysteine availability): relieves redox limitation, reducing ROS-driven mitochondrial damage

## Mitochondrial Control of Stem Cell Fate

- Quiescent stem cells typically rely on [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|glycolysis]]; transition to activation involves a metabolic shift toward OXPHOS
- **Asymmetric inheritance**: selective inheritance of aged mitochondria in intestinal stem cells gives rise to ISC^mito-O^ subpopulation with enhanced α-ketoglutarate production, promoting TET-dependent epigenetic remodeling
- Mitochondrial membrane potential (Δψm) can stratify chronologically old [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|HSCs]] into functionally distinct states; _in vivo_ pharmacological potentiation of Δψm in aged HSCs improves engraftment and reverses myeloid-biased output
- Increased mitochondrial mass in aged HSCs is not necessarily a dysfunction marker: a mitochondria-high subset can retain strong self-renewal and regenerative capacity
- **Dnmt3a R878H/+ mutant HSPCs** in clonal hematopoiesis exhibit a mitochondria-driven fitness state with hyperpolarized Δψm — an Achilles' heel exploitable by alkyl-TPP compounds (e.g., [[MitoQ]])

## Oxidative Stress, Inflammation, and the Mitochondrial-Inflammaging Axis

- Mitochondrial dysfunction activates redox-sensitive pathways such as [[NF-κB]], enhancing production of [[IL-6]] and [[TNF-alpha|TNF-α]]
- Reduced [[SIRT3]] activity (due to NAD+ depletion) weakens antioxidant defenses and mitochondrial detoxification
- Cytosolic [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] acts as a [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|damage-associated molecular pattern]], engaging the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING axis]] and inducing type I interferon responses and [[SASP]]
- **Minority MOMP** (sublethal mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization) allows mtDNA release without full apoptotic execution
- Mitochondrial Ca²⁺ dysregulation at [[Mitochondria-Associated Membranes|MERCs]] (IP3R–VDAC1–MCU uptake vs. NCLX efflux) can drive either insufficient matrix Ca²⁺ or Ca²⁺ overload, both amplifying ROS and destabilizing membrane integrity
- Damaged mitochondria accumulate in senescent cells due to defective mitophagy, remaining metabolically active yet dysfunctional, excessively generating ROS that sustains SASP signaling
- Mitochondrial lipid peroxidation byproducts (4-HNE, oxidized cardiolipin) stimulate inflammatory pathways via toll-like receptors and inflammasomes

## Mitochondria-ER Crosstalk

- [[Mitochondria-Associated Membranes|MERCs]] act as regulatory hubs for Ca²⁺ signaling, lipid exchange, mitochondrial dynamics, and stress adaptation
- Impaired ER-to-mitochondria Ca²⁺ transfer through the IP3R–GRP75–VDAC1 axis can produce either insufficient mitochondrial Ca²⁺ (blunting ATP production) or pathological Ca²⁺ overload (sensitizing to permeability transition)
- MERC disruption amplifies proteostatic and redox failure by coupling ER stress to mitochondrial decay, promoting chronic [[Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress|UPR^ER^]] activation
- In stem cell compartments, MERCs support mitochondrial remodeling during fate transitions and asymmetric division; with age, fragmented networks and impaired Ca²⁺ coordination erode tissue regeneration

## Mitochondria-Targeted Anti-Aging Interventions

| Strategy | Examples | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| [[NAD+]] repletion | [[Nicotinamide Riboside\|NR]], [[NMN]] | Restore sirtuin activity, enhance UPR^mt^, improve mitochondrial membrane potential |
| Mitophagy enhancement | [[Urolithin A]] | Selectively remove damaged mitochondria |
| Mitochondrial biogenesis activation | AMPK activators, [[PGC-1α]] pathway | Promote turnover and metabolic homeostasis |
| Mitochondrial transplantation | nMITO (bioengineered) | Directly restore bioenergetics in damaged tissues |
| mtDNA editing | mitoTALENs, mitoZFNs | Selectively eliminate mutant mtDNA |
| Scalable mitochondrial transfer | Droplet-based microfluidic platforms | Tighter control over payload/dosing |

> [!warning] Context Dependence
> Tissue-specific thresholds, dosing/timing, lysosomal competence, and degree of damage burden all determine the effectiveness of mitochondria-targeted interventions.

## Conclusions

Mitochondria have transcended their canonical role as energy generators to become central architects of aging and cellular plasticity. Therapeutically targeting mitochondrial networks — through metabolic repletion, genetic editing, or organelle replacement — holds potential to reshape approaches to aging by enabling regeneration, resilience, and rejuvenation.
