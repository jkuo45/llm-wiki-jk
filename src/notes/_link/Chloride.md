# Chloride

**Chloride** (Cl⁻) is a monovalent halide anion and the most abundant anion of
the extracellular fluid. It is the obligate physiological counter-ion that
balances the sodium gradient established by the
[[Sodium Potassium ATPase]], and it is one of only a handful of ions for which
the body maintains strict homeostatic control via renal handling in the thick
ascending limb and distal convoluted tubule.

## Physiological roles

Chloride is not metabolised; it is a pure ion that cycles between compartments
and is filtered, reabsorbed, and secreted. Its textbook functions are osmotic
and electrical — maintaining plasma volume, transepithelial potential, and
gastric acid secretion — but it also participates in a small number of
genuinely chemical roles in the cell.

> [!info] Prolyl hydroxylase inhibition and HIF-1α
> The 2-oxoglutarate-dependent prolyl hydroxylases (PHDs, i.e. PHD2/EGLN1) that
> hydroxylate [[HIF-1α]] and target it for [[Proteasome|proteasomal]] degradation
> are inhibited by chloride. Chloride binding at a specific site in the
> 2-oxoglutarate-binding pocket of PHD2 (reported by Kato et al., 2007, PMID:
> 18060768) stabilises HIF-1α under normoxia. The physiological interpretation
> of a signal so dependent on an intracellular anion whose concentration is
> itself set by membrane potential remains debated; the enzymology is solid,
> the in vivo signalling weight is not.

> [!info] Substrate for hypochlorous acid production
> Chloride is the two-electron reductant in the
> [[Myeloperoxidase]] catalytic cycle. Myeloperoxidase uses
> [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] to oxidise Cl⁻ to [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl):
> H₂O₂ + Cl⁻ + H⁺ → HOCl + H₂O. This is the respiratory burst's principal
> antimicrobial oxidant and the chemical link between the neutrophil oxidative
> burst and protein chlorination, [[Nitrite|nitrosative]] stress, and
> [[Singlet Oxygen]] chemistry in the [[Phagolysosome]]. See
> [[HOCl-mediated Oxidation]].

## Homeostasis and disease

Plasma chloride sits at 98–107 mM and is the main anion accompanying
[[Sodium]]; within the cell it is low and strictly controlled, and intracellular
chloride functions as a volume regulator by modulating the
[[Oligodynamic]]-like osmotic work of the contractile vacuole in
microbes and by gating [[GABA_A receptor|GABA-A]] and glycine-gated
[[Glutamate-gated Cl- channels|GABA and glycine-gated chloride channels]] in
neurons. Because those inhibitory channels depend on the chloride gradient
established by the neuronal [[KCC2]] chloride-extruder, shifts in intracellular
chloride are a mechanism of disinhibition in pain states and in
[[Excitotoxicity]].

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Serum chloride is read mainly as an acid–base variable, not as a nutritional
> one. Low chloride accompanies vomiting and metabolic alkalosis; high chloride
> accompanies most cases of metabolic acidosis. Chloride-responsive alkalosis
> and the hypochloremic state in ileus or pyloric obstruction are the settings
> where chloride replacement (normal saline, not bicarbonate) is the correct
> intervention. Urinary chloride is used diagnostically to separate
> saline-responsive from saline-resistant metabolic alkalosis.

## Connections

- [[Myeloperoxidase]] — catalyses the two-electron oxidation of chloride to
  hypochlorous acid, the only chemically essential role of chloride in innate
  immunity. Both enzymes and substrate co-localise to the neutrophil
  phagolysosome, so the chemistry is spatially confined.
- [[Sodium Potassium ATPase]] — establishes the sodium gradient that chloride
  passively follows; the two ions are transported as a coupled pair, which is
  why chloride depletion is always a volume-and-electrolyte problem rather
  than an isolated one.
- [[HIF-1α]] — chloride inhibits the 2-oxoglutarate-dependent prolyl
  hydroxylases that degrade HIF-1α, linking chloride tone to
  [[Hypoxia]] signalling. The in vivo magnitude of this effect is uncertain.
- [[GABA_A receptor]] — chloride conductance through GABA-A and glycine
  receptors is inhibitory only because the neuronal chloride gradient is set
  low by KCC2; this is the standard mechanistic explanation for
  disinhibition in central sensitization.
- [[Prostaglandins]] — not a direct link, but acid–base and chloride status
  interact through renal prostaglandin handling; included here only as a
  cross-reference for electrolyte notes that also carry prostaglandin content.
