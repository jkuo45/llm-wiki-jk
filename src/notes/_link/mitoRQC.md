# mitoRQC

**mitoRQC** (mitochondrial ribosome quality control) is a surveillance pathway that monitors [[Mitochondrial ribosome|mitochondrial ribosomes]] (mitoribosomes) for stalled or aberrant translation events and targets the incomplete nascent [[Polypeptide|polypeptides]] for degradation. It is the mitochondrial counterpart of the cytosolic ribosome-associated quality control (RQC) system.

## Mechanism

- **Detection:** ribosome stalling during translation of mitochondrial mRNAs (e.g. on truncated or damaged transcripts) is recognized by RQC factors associated with the large mitoribosomal subunit.
- **Extraction:** [[Vms1]] (yeast) / [[ANKZF1]] (mammals) helps release and process the stalled nascent chain from the ribosome.
- **Tagging & degradation:** the E3 ligase [[Ltn1]] (yeast) / [[LISTERIN]] (mammals) mediates [[Ubiquitin|ubiquitylation]] of the stalled polypeptide, which is then routed to the [[Proteasome]] or to [[Mitochondrial Protease|mitochondrial proteases]] for degradation.
- **Outcome:** clearance of truncated, aggregation-prone translation products that would otherwise poison the organelle.

## Physiological Importance

> [!info] Mitochondrial proteostasis and mitohormesis
> By eliminating faulty nascent chains, mitoRQC protects [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] [[Proteostasis]] and works alongside the [[UPRmt]], [[Mitophagy]], and import-surveillance pathways ([[mitoCPR]], [[mitoTAD]]). This network underlies adaptive [[Mitohormesis]]; its failure contributes to [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], [[Oxidative Stress]], and age-related decline.
