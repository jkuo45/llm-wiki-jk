# Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

**Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)**, commonly known as **lupus**, is a chronic, heterogeneous autoimmune disease characterized by loss of immunological tolerance to self-antigens, production of pathogenic autoantibodies, and immune complex-mediated inflammation affecting multiple organ systems. SLE disproportionately affects women of reproductive age, with a female-to-male ratio of approximately 9:1.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

### Genetic Susceptibility
SLE has a strong genetic component, with concordance rates of 24–69% in monozygotic twins. Key genetic associations include:
- **HLA-DR2 and HLA-DR3** — Major histocompatibility complex class II alleles conferring highest risk.
- **Complement deficiencies** (C1q, C2, C4) — Impair clearance of apoptotic debris and immune complexes.
- **IRF5**, **STAT4**, **TNFAIP3** — Polymorphisms in interferon and [[NFKB|NF-κB]] regulatory pathways.
- **TREX1** — Exonuclease involved in cytoplasmic DNA sensing; loss-of-function mutations drive type I interferon production.

### Immune Dysregulation
The central immunological defect in SLE is failure to clear apoptotic cells and cellular debris, leading to exposure of nuclear self-antigens (dsDNA, histones, ribonucleoproteins). These are captured by dendritic cells and presented to autoreactive T and B cells, initiating a self-amplifying cycle:

- **Type I Interferon (IFN-α) pathway**: Plasmacytoid dendritic cells produce excessive IFN-α in response to immune complexes containing nucleic acids, detected by [[TLR7]]/[[TLR9]] and the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] pathway. IFN-α promotes dendritic cell maturation, B cell activation, and autoantibody production.
- **B cell hyperactivity**: Loss of peripheral tolerance checkpoints allows autoreactive B cells to produce anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith, and anti-phospholipid antibodies.
- **T cell dysfunction**: Aberrant T cell signaling lowers activation thresholds, and follicular helper T cells (Tfh) provide excessive help to autoreactive B cells.
- **Complement consumption**: Immune complex deposition activates complement, consuming C3 and C4 and reducing opsonization capacity.

### Organ Damage Mechanisms
- **Immune complex nephritis**: Anti-dsDNA antibodies form complexes that deposit in glomeruli, activating complement and recruiting inflammatory cells.
- **Vasculitis**: Immune complexes in vessel walls trigger complement-mediated endothelial damage.
- **Serositis**: Pleuritis and pericarditis from immune complex deposition in serosal surfaces.
- **Cerebritis**: Autoantibody-mediated neuronal damage and blood-brain barrier disruption.

## Clinical Presentation

SLE is a multisystem disease with highly variable presentation:
- **Constitutional**: Fatigue, fever, weight loss.
- **Musculoskeletal**: Symmetric polyarthralgia/arthritis (non-erosive).
- **Dermatologic**: Malar ("butterfly") rash, discoid lesions, photosensitivity, oral ulcers.
- **Renal**: Lupus nephritis (class I–VI, ISN/RPS classification) — hematuria, proteinuria, nephrotic syndrome.
- **Hematologic**: Autoimmune hemolytic anemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia.
- **Neurological**: Seizures, psychosis, cognitive dysfunction, stroke.
- **Cardiovascular**: Pericarditis, accelerated atherosclerosis, Libman-Sacks endocarditis.
- **Pulmonary**: Pleuritis, interstitial lung disease, shrinking lung syndrome.

## Biomarkers

- **Antinuclear antibody (ANA)** — Highly sensitive (~98%) but not specific; positive ANA with clinical features is required for diagnosis (2019 EULAR/ACR criteria).
- **Anti-dsDNA** — Specific; titers correlate with disease activity and nephritis.
- **Anti-Smith (Anti-Sm)** — Highly specific but low sensitivity.
- **Anti-phospholipid antibodies** — Associated with thrombosis and pregnancy complications.
- **Low C3/C4** — Indicates complement consumption and active disease.
- **Urine protein/creatinine ratio** — Monitors renal involvement.

## Therapeutic Landscape

| Drug Class | Examples | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Antimalarials | [[Hydroxychloroquine]] | Inhibits TLR7/9 signaling; reduces flare frequency |
| Corticosteroids | Prednisone | Broad immunosuppression |
| Calcineurin inhibitors | Voclosporin | Inhibits T cell activation; approved for lupus nephritis |
| Mycophenolate | Mycophenolate mofetil | Inhibits lymphocyte proliferation; induction/maintenance of lupus nephritis |
| Belimumab | Belimumab (Benlysta) | Anti-BAFF; reduces B cell survival; approved for SLE and lupus nephritis |
| Anifrolumab | Anifrolumab (Saphnelo) | Anti-IFNAR1; blocks type I interferon signaling; approved for SLE |
| Rituximab | Rituximab (off-label) | Anti-CD20; depletes B cells; used in refractory disease |
| Voclosporin | Voclosporin (Lupkynis) | Calcineurin inhibitor; approved for lupus nephritis |
