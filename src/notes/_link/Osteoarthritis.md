# Osteoarthritis

**Osteoarthritis (OA)** is the most common joint disease, a degenerative disorder of articular cartilage characterized by chondrocyte apoptosis, extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation, and subchondral bone remodeling. It is a leading cause of pain and disability, driven by aging, mechanical stress, and low-grade inflammation. Its development involves genetic, environmental, and lifestyle risk factors.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

OA pathogenesis involves imbalance between anabolic and catabolic chondrocyte activity, with matrix metalloproteinase-driven ECM degradation, [[Oxidative Stress]], chondrocyte [[Cellular Senescence|senescence]], and IL-1β/inflammatory signaling. Mechanical overload and age-related mitochondrial dysfunction accelerate degeneration.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Best studied — reduces apoptosis and ECM degradation via Wnt/β-catenin; the circ0001103/miR-375 axis regulates SIRT1 to attenuate IL-1β-induced apoptosis/ECM degradation; reverses homocysteine-induced changes via the SIRT1/PGC-1α/PPAR-γ cascade; inhibits EGFR ubiquitination → improves cartilage oxidative-stress resistance; inhibits chondrocyte senescence via Wnt/β-catenin.
- **[[SIRT2]]**: Protective — inhibits ECM degradation by preventing p65 acetylation.
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Inhibits chondrocyte senescence by negatively regulating [[NF-κB]]-mediated inflammatory responses.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Dual role — inhibits degeneration by maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis (restores SOD2 acetylation-dependent activity), but overexpression promotes OA chondrocyte apoptosis and reduced proliferation → OA progression.

## Therapeutic Implications

Sirtuin activation (especially SIRT1/SIRT6) represents a candidate disease-modifying strategy to limit chondrocyte apoptosis, ECM degradation, and senescence in OA.
