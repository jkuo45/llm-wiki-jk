# Ornithine

**L-ornithine** is a non-proteinogenic amino acid that sits at a metabolic branch point: it is both an intermediate of the [[Urea Cycle]] and the committed precursor of the [[Polyamine]] pathway. It is not incorporated into proteins and has no separate dietary requirement, being synthesized from [[Arginine]].

## Metabolic Fates

Ornithine is formed when [[Arginase]] hydrolyzes arginine to ornithine and urea, in the second step of the urea cycle. From there it branches in two directions.

**Urea cycle.** Ornithine carbamoyltransferase condenses ornithine with carbamoyl phosphate to form citrulline, then returns to the cytosol to restart the cycle. Every ornithine molecule can pass through this loop multiple times, giving the cycle its catalytic character. Defects in this step, as in [[Ornithine transcarbamylase]] deficiency, cause hyperammonemia.

**Polyamine synthesis.** Ornithine decarboxylase converts ornithine to putrescine, the rate-limiting and committed step, which then feeds [[Putrescine]] → [[Spermidine]] → [[Spermine]]. These polyamines are cationic and bind nucleic acids and polyanionic surfaces, and they are actively being pursued as longevity interventions — spermidine, for instance, is a well-known autophagy-stimulating compound in this vault.

> [!info] Ornithine is also a neurotransmitter precursor
> Ornithine itself is not a classical neurotransmitter, but it participates in the glutamate–glutamine–GABA shunt in the brain, linking neuronal amino acid pools to neurotransmitter supply and to ammonia detoxification.

## Ornithine Aminotransferase

The other major fate is transamination. Ornithine aminotransferase (OAT) is a mitochondrial matrix pyridoxal-5′-phosphate-dependent enzyme that reversibly transfers the δ-amino group of ornithine to α-ketoglutarate, yielding glutamate-5-semialdehyde and glutamate. Glutamate-5-semialdehyde cyclizes to Δ1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate, placing ornithine on the proline pathway as well.

OAT handles ornithine catabolism in adults, which is why its deficiency produces hyperornithinemia.

## Clinical Significance

- **Gyrate atrophy.** Biallelic *OAT* mutations cause gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina (MIM 258870), an autosomal recessive disorder with progressive retinal deterioration and blindness, theoretical incidence roughly 1:1,500,000. Hyperornithinemia is the biochemical hallmark. Patients are managed with an arginine-restricted diet to limit ornithine load, or with vitamin B6 as a precursor of the OAT cofactor pyridoxal phosphate (Montioli et al., *Biochim Biophys Acta Proteins Proteom* 2021; PMID 33068755).
- **Hepatic encephalopathy.** Because ornithine is a urea-cycle intermediate, its handling is relevant to ammonia clearance and to the ornithine-phenylacetate and sodium benzoate strategies used to shunt nitrogen away from the cycle.
- **Cancer metabolism.** OAT is recognized as a potential chemotherapeutic target because it is overexpressed in some cancers, and it has been studied as a therapeutic vulnerability in hepatocellular carcinoma.
- **Fibrosis.** Ornithine-derived polyamines and nitric oxide metabolism intersect in hepatic stellate cell activation and liver fibrosis.

## Connections

- [[Arginine]] — the direct precursor; arginase splits arginine into ornithine and urea, making ornithine a urea-cycle intermediate.
- [[Urea Cycle]] — ornithine is regenerated each turn of the cycle and is the substrate of carbamoyl phosphate transfer.
- [[Polyamine]] — ornithine decarboxylase commits ornithine to putrescine and thence to spermidine and spermine.
- [[Ornithine transcarbamylase]] — the urea-cycle enzyme that consumes ornithine; its deficiency is the commonest ornithine-related inborn error.
- [[Ornithine Aminotransferase (OAT) Deficiency]] — the catabolic enzyme for adult ornithine; its loss causes hyperornithinemia and gyrate atrophy.
- [[Gyrate Atrophy]] — the clinical consequence of OAT deficiency, with progressive chorioretinal degeneration.
- [[Arginase]] — the enzyme that generates ornithine from arginine, and a point of competition between the urea cycle and nitric oxide synthesis.
- [[Citrulline]] — the product of carbamoyl phosphate transferase acting on ornithine.
- [[Putrescine]], [[Spermidine]], [[Spermine]] — the downstream polyamine series derived from ornithine.
- [[Autophagy]] — spermidine derived from this pathway stimulates autophagy and is a longevity-relevant intervention.
- [[Liver]] — the organ where the urea cycle operates and where ornithine metabolism is clinically leveraged.
- [[Arachidonic acid]] — unrelated directly, but polyamine and eicosanoid metabolism share the arginine pool.
- [[Nitric Oxide]] — arginine is diverted between NOS-dependent nitric oxide production and ornithine production by arginase.
