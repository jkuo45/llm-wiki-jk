# Guanidinoacetate (GAA)

**Guanidinoacetate (GAA)**, also called glycocyamine, is a naturally occurring guanidino compound and the direct precursor of [[Creatine]]. It is formed in the [[Kidney]] (and other AGAT-expressing tissues) from [[Arginine]] and [[Glycine]] by [[Arginine Glycine Amidinotransferase|arginine:glycine amidinotransferase (AGAT)]], then methylated by [[Guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase|guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase (GAMT)]] using [[S-adenosyl Methionine|S-adenosyl methionine (SAMe)]] to yield [[Creatine]] in the [[Liver]].

## Role in Creatine Biosynthesis

GAA sits at the branch point of the endogenous creatine synthesis pathway:

```
Arginine + Glycine --(AGAT)--> Guanidinoacetate (GAA)
GAA + SAMe --(GAMT)--> Creatine + SAH
```

In [[Creatine Synthesis Deficiency|GAMT deficiency]], GAA accumulates to toxic levels, contributing to neurological damage; in [[Creatine Synthesis Deficiency|AGAT deficiency]], both GAA and creatine are low.

## As a Supplement

GAA has attracted interest as an alternative or adjunct to [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine supplementation]]:

- It is a more potent methyl acceptor and may elevate tissue creatine more efficiently in some contexts.
- In the context of [[Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS)|chronic fatigue syndrome]], GAA supplementation (2.4 g/day for 3 months) was reported to positively affect [[Creatine Metabolism|creatine metabolism]] and work capacity in women with CFS (Ostojic et al.), though without changing general fatigue or musculoskeletal soreness.
- As a precursor, it is sometimes co-administered to support endogenous creatine synthesis.

## Relevance

Discussed in [[Creatine in Health and Disease]] alongside creatine's therapeutic uses and as a creatine precursor relevant to [[Creatine Synthesis Deficiency]] and fatigue syndromes.
