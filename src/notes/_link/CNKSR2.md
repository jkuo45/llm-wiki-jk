# CNKSR2

[[CNKSR2]] (Connector Enhancer of Kinase Suppressor of Ras 2, also called CNK2) is an X-linked gene encoding a synaptic scaffolding protein that regulates the [[RAS]]/[[MAPK]] signaling pathway. CNKSR2 is one of six X-linked genes identified as **EXITS** (Escape from X-Inactivation Tumor Suppressors) — loss-of-function mutations enriched in male tumors due to females' biallelic dosage protection (Dunford et al., *Nature Genetics* 2017).

## Overview

CNKSR2 belongs to the CNK (Connector Enhancer of Kinase Suppressor of Ras) family of scaffolding proteins that modulate the intensity and duration of RAS–MAPK signaling. The CNK family members act as molecular hubs that integrate upstream signals from receptor tyrosine kinases and modulate downstream effectors including RAF, MEK, and ERK.

## Structure & Function

- **PDZ domain**: Mediates protein–protein interactions at the postsynaptic density.
- **CRIC domain**: Conserved region involved in CNK family scaffolding.
- **PH domain**: Lipid-binding domain targeting the protein to the plasma membrane.
- **SAM domain**: Sterile alpha motif involved in oligomerization.

CNKSR2 is predominantly expressed in the brain (synaptic tissue) where it localizes to the postsynaptic density and regulates [[RAS]]/[[MAPK]] signal transduction in neurons. Its scaffolding activity modulates the strength of MAPK cascade activation downstream of synaptic receptor tyrosine kinases.

## Role in Cancer

CNKSR2 is one of six EXITS genes with recurrent loss-of-function mutations enriched in male tumors:

- Loss of CNKSR2 may derepress RAS–MAPK signaling, contributing to the proliferative advantage of cancer cells.
- CNKSR2 loss-of-function mutations have been identified across multiple tumor types, with a consistent male enrichment pattern.
- The mechanism likely involves disruption of negative regulatory scaffolds that normally attenuate RAS–MAPK signaling intensity.

> [!info]
> CNKSR2's role as a RAS pathway regulator makes it relevant to the broader landscape of [[KRAS]]-driven cancers, including [[Lung Cancer]], [[Colorectal Cancer]], and [[Pancreatic Cancer]].

### EXITS Framework

CNKSR2 is one of six X-linked genes (with [[ATRX]], [[DDX3X]], [[KDM5C]], [[KDM6A]], [[MAGEC3]], see [[EXITS]]) that are preferentially loss-of-function mutated in male tumors. Across **21 tumor types**, these six X-linked genes harbor loss-of-function mutations significantly more often in males — a sex-biased pattern not seen in any of the **18,053 autosomal genes** examined (Dunford et al., *Nature Genetics* 2017). In females, biallelic expression provides a functional buffer requiring **two hits** (double mutation or mutation + whole-chrX loss); males, hemizygous for X-linked genes, are vulnerable to **single-hit** inactivation.

## Sex Differences

> [!tip]
> CNKSR2 loss-of-function mutations are enriched in male tumors due to X-linked hemizygosity.

- As an X-linked gene escaping full inactivation in females, CNKSR2 is biallelically expressed in female cells.
- Males carry only one copy; loss-of-function mutation on the single X allele eliminates CNKSR2 tumor suppressor activity entirely.
- This provides a mechanistic explanation for the broader male predominance in cancer incidence at most non-reproductive sites.
