## Overview

An **ITIM** is a cytoplasmic amino-acid consensus motif (~26 aa including the membrane-proximal region, core sequence S/I/V/LxYxxI/V/L) found in the intracellular tails of inhibitory receptors of the immune system. When the receptor's extracellular domain is ligated, Src-family kinases phosphorylate the motif's tyrosine, creating docking sites for the SH2 domains of SHP-1 and SHP-2 phosphatases (and sometimes SHIP); the recruited phosphatases dephosphorylate activating substrates and suppress the cell's effector response.

## Receptors using ITIM logic

In the eat-me/don't-eat-me space ([[Efferocytosis]]), the motif is how several anti-phagocytic signals act:

- [[CD47]] — its "don't eat me" output is delivered when [[SIRPalpha|SIRPα]]'s ITIMs recruit SHP-1/2, blocking actin rearrangement at the phagocytic cup.
- [[Siglec-10]] — ITIM recruitment of SHP phosphatases implements the [[CD24]]- and sialic-acid-dependent anti-phagocytic brake.
- [[PECAM1|CD31]] — cytoplasmic ITIMs mediate the live-cell homophilic "don't-eat-me" signal.
- [[Fc Gamma Receptor|Fc gamma RIIB]] and KIR-family inhibitory receptors — the classical ITIM bearers of adaptive immunity.
- [[PD-1]] — carries an ITIM plus an inhibitory-switch motif (ITSM) that is the stronger SHP-2 recruiter.

Note: [[PD-L1]]-macrophage anti-phagocytosis flows through PD-1's ITIM/ITSM on the macrophage side, so ITIM logic also underlies the checkpoint-modulation route.
