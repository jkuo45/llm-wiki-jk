# Testosterone

## Overview
[[Testosterone]] is the primary anabolic androgenic steroid in males (also present at lower levels in females), produced by Leydig cells under [[Luteinizing hormone]] control. It drives muscle accretion, bone density, and erythrogenesis, and its age-related decline ("andropause") contributes to [[Aging]] phenotypes.

## Synthesis & Metabolism
Synthesized from cholesterol via the gonadal steroidogenic cascade, testosterone can be aromatized to [[Estrogen]] and reduced to dihydrotestosterone. It acts through the androgen receptor to regulate transcription of growth and metabolic genes.

## Mechanism of Action
Androgen-receptor binding promotes protein synthesis, [[Muscle Atrophy]] resistance, and [[Osteoporosis]] prevention. It modulates [[Insulin]] sensitivity and lipid handling, influencing [[Metabolic Syndrome]] and [[Cardiovascular Disease]] risk.

## Redox, Mitochondria & Longevity
Testosterone supports [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] resilience and lowers [[Oxidative Stress]] in muscle and vasculature, partly via [[SIRT1]]-associated pathways. Deficiency associates with sarcopenia, adiposity, and inflammaging. Balanced androgen tone—within a [[Mitohormesis]]-like optimum—favors [[Healthspan]], whereas supraphysiological dosing raises redox and thrombotic risk. Lifestyle, [[Exercise]], and NAD+ support (e.g., [[Nicotinamide Riboside]]) interact with androgen status in longevity.
