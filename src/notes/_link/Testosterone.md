# Testosterone

## Overview
[[Testosterone]] is the primary anabolic androgenic steroid in males (also present at lower levels in females), produced by Leydig cells under [[Luteinizing hormone]] control. It drives muscle accretion, bone density, and erythrogenesis, and its age-related decline ("andropause") contributes to [[Aging]] phenotypes.

## Synthesis & Metabolism
Synthesized from cholesterol via the gonadal steroidogenic cascade, testosterone can be aromatized to [[Estrogen]] and reduced to dihydrotestosterone. It acts through the androgen receptor to regulate transcription of growth and metabolic genes.

## Mechanism of Action
Androgen-receptor binding promotes protein synthesis, [[Muscle Atrophy]] resistance, and [[Osteoporosis]] prevention. It modulates [[Insulin]] sensitivity and lipid handling, influencing [[Metabolic Syndrome]] and [[Cardiovascular Disease]] risk.

## Redox, Mitochondria & Longevity
Testosterone supports [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] resilience and lowers [[Oxidative Stress]] in muscle and vasculature, partly via [[SIRT1]]-associated pathways. Deficiency associates with sarcopenia, adiposity, and inflammaging. Balanced androgen tone—within a [[Mitohormesis]]-like optimum—favors [[Healthspan]], whereas supraphysiological dosing raises redox and thrombotic risk. Lifestyle, [[Exercise]], and NAD+ support (e.g., [[Nicotinamide Riboside]]) interact with androgen status in longevity.

## Sex Differences — Male-Axis Counterpart (Not 1:1 With Estrogen)

[[Testosterone]]→[[Androgen Receptor|AR]] is the closest male counterpart to the estrogen→ER master hub, but distributed rather than centralized: anabolic/immune rather than 9-pathway.

- **AR→mTOR/Akt/FoxO3a:** 100 nM T → p-ERK 5 min, p-Akt 15 min, p-S6K1 60 min 3.5×; PI3K/Akt/mTOR inhibition (not ERK) abolishes hypertrophy; bicalutamide abolishes CSA gain (Basualto-Alarcón 2013). Castration ↓ IGF-1 mRNA, myofibrillar synthesis, p-Akt/p-GSK3β/p-PRAS40/p-FoxO3a and ↑ MuRF1/atrogin-1/REDD1; nandrolone reverses. Low T activates mTOR Akt-independently; high T required for Akt (White 2013).
- **AR→NF-κB/T-cell suppression:** androgen→USP18 inhibits NF-κB in T cells, suppressing antitumor immunity (Zhang Cancer Cell 2023); AR in T cells limits checkpoint blockade (Guan Nature 2022); AR activity inversely correlates with immune infiltration (Hu 2024 bioRxiv). Parallel to E2→NF-κB suppression, opposite oncologic valence.
- **Inflammaging→LOH (reverse direction):** IL-6 + GDF-15 mediate age T-decline (UKB men n=18,347; GDF-15 strongest); ROS → p38→COX2 + NF-κB→COX2 → StAR/P450scc↓ in aged Leydig cells, restored synergistically by SB203580 + Bay 11-7082 (SAMP8/Sci Rep 2019); hypothalamic NF-κB impairs GnRH, M2→M1 macrophage shift impairs testis (Front Endocrinol 2024). Gradual LOH, no cliff.
- **AR/ER lipid mirror:** MBOAT2 is a direct AR target vs MBOAT1 an ER target, sensitizing AR+ prostate / ER+ breast cancers to ferroptosis induction + hormonal blockade (Cell 2023 PMC10330611). Testosterone is the dominant renal-ischemia susceptibility factor (castration protects males; T sensitizes females — Park JBC 2004, pre-ferroptosis era).
- **Telomere/p53 caveats:** T lacks antioxidant telomere protection and may ↑ oxidative damage (Gardner 2014); T ↓p-p53 only in C2C12 oxidative model (Pronsato 2017) — do not generalize.

See [[Androgen Receptor]], [[Prostate Cancer]] (male pole), [[Breast Cancer]] (female mirror), [[Loss of Y Chromosome]] (LOY cliff).
