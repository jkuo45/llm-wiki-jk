# Gut-Brain Axis

**Overview:** The [[Gut-Brain Axis]] is a bidirectional communication network connecting the central nervous system with the [[Gastrointestinal Tract]] and its resident [[Gut Microbiome]]. It integrates neural, hormonal, immunological, and metabolic information.

**Mechanisms of communication:** The vagus nerve provides rapid afferent and efferent signaling between gut and brain. Microbial fermentation yields [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] and other metabolites that act on enteroendocrine cells and the enteric nervous system. The [[Immune System]] relays signals via cytokines, while the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis contributes endocrine crosstalk.

**Physiological and pathological relevance:** This axis influences mood, cognition, appetite, and stress responses. Disruption is linked to [[Inflammation]], [[Aging]], and [[Parkinson's Disease]] (where α-synuclein pathology may originate in the gut) and [[Alzheimer's Disease]]. Supporting a healthy microbiome through diet, [[Fasting]], and [[Exercise]] is being explored to preserve brain health.

**Research context:** The gut–brain axis is a growing target for psychobiotics and dietary interventions aimed at healthy [[Aging]] and neurodegeneration prevention.
