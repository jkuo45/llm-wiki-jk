# Adenosine

**Adenosine** is a naturally occurring endogenous purine nucleoside consisting of adenine plus D-ribose. It was first isolated from heart tissue extracts in 1929, and its hypnotic effects on the cat brain were demonstrated in 1954. Today it is best understood as two related things: a **homeostatic sleep pressure signal** in the brain, and a **local metabolic regulator of blood flow** that matches tissue oxygen delivery to consumption. It was the first molecule identified in the history of the adenosinergic sleep research that eventually explained caffeine's mechanism.

## Metabolism & Receptors

Adenosine is generated intracellularly and extracellularly by several routes, and its concentration is held tightly by opposing synthetic and degradative enzymes:

- **Extracellular production** is the physiologically dominant route: extracellular ATP (released by neurons, glia and elsewhere) is hydrolysed by ectonucleotidases **CD39 (ENTPDase1)** and **CD73** (ecto-5′-nucleotidase, which converts AMP to adenosine) to generate adenosine. Brain 5′-ectonucleotidase activity appears to be the key sleep-promoting enzyme.
- **Intracellular sources** include AMP via cytosolic 5′-nucleotidase and S-adenosylhomocysteine via SAH hydrolase. [[Adenosine Kinase]] recycles adenosine back to AMP and is the dominant intracellular terminator; **adenosine deaminase** degrades it to inosine.
- **Transport** across the membrane is via equilibrative nucleoside transporters, which is why systemic and neural pools equilibrate.

Four receptor subtypes mediate its effects:

| Receptor | Coupling | Principal role |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Gi/o | Inhibitory neuromodulator: reduced neuronal excitability, sedation, analgesia, anticonvulsant effect; inhibits Ca²⁺ channels, activates K⁺ channels |
| **A2A** | Gs | Sleep gating, arousal suppression; abundant in striatum, forms heteromers with D2 receptors |
| **A2B** | Gs, Gq | Least studied; vasodilation, [[Neuroinflammation]] regulation |
| **A3** | Gi/o, Gq | Immune and microglial effects, neuroprotection |

All four activate [[Adenosine Kinase]]-linked intracellular pathways converging on protein kinase A and exchange protein directly activated by cAMP, and on MAPK and PI3K/PKB signalling.

> [!info] Adenosine is a *tonic* signal, not a phasic one
> Basal extracellular adenosine continuously activates receptors; antagonists like [[Caffeine]] relieve that tonic inhibition rather than blocking a transient pulse. This is why the therapeutic effect of antagonism is robust and why the receptor populations that matter can be mapped by loss-of-function experiments.

## Sleep Homeostasis & Caffeine

Adenosine accumulates in the extracellular space during prolonged wakefulness as a consequence of ATP consumption, and is dissipated during sleep — the biochemical basis of the **two-process model** of sleep regulation (Process S, sleep homeostasis, layered on the circadian Process C). Extracellular adenosine rises particularly in the basal forebrain. Stimulation of either A1 or A2A receptors increases non-REM slow-wave activity, and specific agonists increase slow-wave sleep.

The receptor division of labour has been clarified by genetic work:

- **A2A receptors gate sleep.** Caffeine's wake-promoting effect requires A2A: caffeine failed to disrupt sleep in mice lacking A2A function, while remaining effective in mice lacking A1 receptors. Deleting A2A receptors in the **nucleus accumbens shell** specifically blocks caffeine-induced wakefulness; chemogenetic or optogenetic activation of accumbens A2A-receptor neurons projecting to the basal forebrain induces slow-wave sleep.
- **A1 receptors resolve and express sleep need.** Constitutive or CNS-conditional A1 knockout mice show near-normal baseline sleep, but fail to extend sleep or show a normal rebound in slow-wave activity after sleep deprivation, and have impaired working memory. A1 signalling also mediates homeostatic sleep pressure via astrocytic gliotransmission.

> [!warning] The A1/A2A split is not fully settled
> Whether A1 receptors contribute the homeostatic feedback signal and A2A receptors merely provide gating and modulate behavioural sleepiness remains under active investigation. Genetic knockout results are inconsistent across studies and species, so the two-receptor model above is best read as a working framework rather than a settled mechanism.

Human genetics support a role for A2A: the common *ADORA2A* variant rs5751876 modulates sleep EEG in NREM and REM sleep, with C/C homozygotes showing more time awake and more frequent sleep complaints. Double-blind administration of 2 × 200 mg caffeine produced insomnia-type sleep EEG changes specifically in C-allele carriers.

## Cardiovascular & Metabolic Physiology

Adenosine is a potent coronary and vascular **vasodilator**, produced locally when the myocardium's oxygen demand outstrips supply, so it dilates the coronary bed to rebalance supply and demand — the metabolic control hypothesis of coronary blood flow regulation. It also inhibits cardiac pacemaker and contractile function, and is used as a pharmacological agent to terminate supraventricular tachyarrhythmias. Adenosine triphosphate, not adenosine, is the principal neurotransmitter at some synapses and the storage form; the [[Adenosine Triphosphate]] note covers that side of the purine story.

## Neuroprotection & Disease

- **A2A antagonists in neurodegeneration.** Because A2A signalling promotes synaptic loss and [[Neuroinflammation]], A2A blockade is a candidate disease-modifying approach in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]]. Preclinically, caffeine protects against cognitive impairment and neuronal damage in models, reduces hippocampal A2A upregulation, and A2A deletion is protective in a tauopathy model. Human epidemiological and trial data remain equivocal.
- **Striatal A2A–D2 heteromers.** A2A receptors form tetrameric A2A–D2R complexes with Gs, Gi and adenylyl cyclase subtype 5 in striatopallidal neurons; this complex is the mechanistic basis for caffeine's psychostimulant action and is a therapeutic target in Parkinson's akinesia. A1–D1R heteromers in spinal motoneurons mediate spinally generated caffeine-induced locomotion.
- **Cerebral ischaemia:** adenosine accumulates massively during ischaemia, and A1 receptors mediate hypoxic tolerance, though the same signalling contributes to excitotoxic injury — a genuine double edge.
- **Metabolism:** adenosine opposes [[Mitochondria|mitophagy]]-relevant AMPK signalling in ways that are still being mapped; the acid–adenosine axis has become a target in oncology, where CD73 and CD39 are being inhibited to restore antitumour immunity.
- **Effects on [[Aging]] and sleep:** chronic sleep restriction and age-related changes in adenosine clearance both perturb adenosine tone, linking the metabolite to cognitive decline.

> [!info] Therapeutic attempts and their barriers
> Classical A2A agonists produce sleep but have adverse cardiovascular effects and poor blood-brain barrier penetration; the same pharmacology that sedates also dilates the coronary bed and drops blood pressure. The current hope is allosteric positive allosteric modulators of A2A receptors, which enhance signalling at physiological ligand tone without the cardiovascular cost — an approach that induced slow-wave sleep without cardiovascular effects in mice and alleviated mania- and schizophrenia-linked insomnia.

## Connections
- [[Caffeine]] — the archetypal adenosine receptor antagonist and the entire pharmacological entry point to the adenosinergic system; caffeine's wake effect maps specifically onto A2A blockade in the nucleus accumbens shell.
- [[Adenosine Triphosphate]] — adenosine's metabolic parent and extracellular precursor; CD39/CD73 hydrolyse ATP → AMP → adenosine extracellularly, making ATP release the upstream event that sets adenosine tone.
- [[Adenosine Kinase]] — the principal intracellular terminator of adenosine signalling, recycling it back to AMP, and therefore the main determinant of how long a transient adenosine signal lasts.
- [[Neurodegeneration]] — adenosine tone is a modifiable node: A2A antagonism is proposed as disease-modifying therapy in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.
- [[Neuroinflammation]] — A2B and A3 receptor signalling regulates microglial activation, placing adenosine at the interface between sleep homeostasis and innate immune tone in the brain.
- [[Mitochondria]] — the organelle whose ATP consumption generates the adenosine signal, and whose dysfunction alters adenosine handling, tying the metabolite to bioenergetic state.
- [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] — the barrier that prevents most exogenous adenosine agonists from reaching the CNS, and the reason systemic adenosine agonists cause vasodilation without sedation.
- [[Homeostasis]] — adenosine accumulation is the canonical example of a homeostatic "need" variable accumulating with use and discharging during rest, as in Process S of sleep homeostasis.
- [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — ischaemic and mitochondrial stress elevate adenosine alongside ROS, and adenosine signalling modulates the same injury pathways.
