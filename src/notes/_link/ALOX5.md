# ALOX5

**ALOX5** (arachidonate 5-lipoxygenase), or **5-LOX**, is a [[Lipoxygenase]] that oxygenates arachidonic acid to leukotriene precursors and downstream lipid hydroperoxides. In ferroptosis, ALOX5 is implicated in hemorrhagic brain injury: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) inhibits heme-induced neuronal ferroptosis by neutralizing toxic lipids produced via arachidonate-dependent ALOX5 activity.

## Structure & Biochemistry

ALOX5 is a non-heme iron-containing dioxygenase that initiates leukotriene biosynthesis and can peroxidate membrane phospholipids. Like other lipoxygenases it requires Fe²⁺ at its active site, tying its activity to cellular [[Iron]] status and to the [[Fenton Reaction]]-amplified peroxidation cascade.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

The review reports that in hemorrhagic stroke, NAC inhibits heme-induced ferroptosis in brain cells by neutralizing toxic lipids produced by arachidonate-dependent [[ALOX5]] activity; early NAC after intracerebral hemorrhage reduces neuronal mortality (review "Ferroptosis and neurological diseases / Stroke"). ALOX5 thus generates the peroxidized lipids that execute neuronal ferroptosis downstream of heme/iron liberation, complementing the [[ALOX15]] axis in other contexts.

> [!info] ALOX5 in hemorrhage vs ALOX15 in p53 ferroptosis
> ALOX15 drives the canonical p53/SAT1 program, whereas ALOX5 is highlighted in heme/iron-driven neuronal ferroptosis of hemorrhagic stroke—different lipoxygenase, different trigger, same lethal lipid peroxidation.

## Clinical Relevance

ALOX5 inhibitors (e.g., zileuton) are approved for asthma; repurposing them (or NAC) to block ALOX5-dependent neuronal ferroptosis is a candidate strategy for intracerebral hemorrhage and possibly other [[Stroke]] subtypes and [[Traumatic Brain Injury]].
