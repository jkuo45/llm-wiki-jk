# MUL1

MUL1 (Mitochondrial E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 1, also known as [[MAPL]]) is an E3 [[Ubiquitin|ubiquitin]] ligase located on the outer mitochondrial membrane (OMM) that functions as a parallel and compensatory pathway to [[Parkin]]-mediated [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]].

## Function in Mitophagy

Unlike [[Parkin]], MUL1 does not need to be "recruited" — it continuously monitors mitochondrial quality by ubiquitinating [[MFN2|Mitofusin 2 (Mfn2)]]. This ubiquitination serves two purposes: suppressing mitochondrial fusion by targeting Mfn2 for degradation, while simultaneously tagging the [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] with a ubiquitin label that promotes their clearance via [[Autophagy|autophagy]].

## Compensatory Role

Studies in both [[Drosophila melanogaster|Drosophila]] and mammalian cell models have demonstrated that MUL1 expression rescues mitochondrial accumulation as well as muscle and neurodegenerative phenotypes caused by Parkin deficiency. When [[PINK1]] or Parkin function is impaired (as in familial [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]]), MUL1-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of Mfn2 provides an essential backup mechanism to maintain [[Mitochondrial Quality Control|mitochondrial quality control]].

## Connections

- [[Parkin]] — MUL1 provides a parallel, compensatory pathway when Parkin function is lost

- [[MFN2]] — Primary substrate; MUL1 ubiquitinates Mfn2 to suppress fusion and tag mitochondria
- [[Parkinson's Disease]] — MUL1 compensates for Parkin deficiency in PD models
