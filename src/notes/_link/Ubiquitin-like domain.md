# Ubiquitin-like domain

The ubiquitin-like domain (UBL) is the N-terminal domain of [[Parkin]], a multidomain E3 ubiquitin ligase. Parkin's architecture comprises an N-terminal UBL followed by RING0, RING1, the in-between-RING (IBR), and RING2. In the autoinhibited state, the UBL engages RING0, folding the enzyme into a closed, inactive conformation that occludes the catalytic RING2.

Activation of Parkin requires disruption of this UBL–RING0 interaction. Phosphorylation events—such as PINK1-mediated phosphorylation of Parkin's serine/threonine residues and relief of repression by [[Ubiquitin]]-dependent mechanisms—displace the UBL, allowing Parkin to adopt its active conformation and catalyze ubiquitination of mitochondrial substrates during [[Mitophagy]].

The UBL therefore serves as a critical autoinhibitory switch whose regulation determines whether Parkin remains dormant or engages the mitophagy machinery.
