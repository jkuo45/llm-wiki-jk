# ACE2

ACE2 (angiotensin-converting enzyme 2, also called ACEH) is a zinc-dependent carboxypeptidase of the M13 family and a type I membrane protein first described in 2000 as the only known homologue of [[Angiotensin II Type 1 Receptor|ACE]]. It performs a single, well-defined reaction — the removal of the C-terminal residue from angiotensin II to yield angiotensin-(1-7) — and that one reaction underpins most of what follows.

> [!info] The counter-regulatory arm
> The renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system is usually presented as a vasoconstrictor system: renin cleaves angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, ACE converts that to angiotensin II, and angiotensin II acting on [[Angiotensin II Type 1 Receptor|AT1]] causes vasoconstriction, aldosterone release, sodium retention and oxidative stress. ACE2 inverts the output. Converting angiotensin II to angiotensin-(1-7) removes the vasoconstrictor signal and generates one that acts on the Mas receptor to promote vasodilation, natriuresis and anti-inflammatory signalling. ACE2 is therefore a natural brake on the system rather than a component of it.

## Structure and Distribution

ACE2 is a type I membrane glycoprotein with a catalytic domain homologous to ACE, a glycosylated stalk, and a short cytoplasmic tail. Unlike ACE, which is mainly endothelial and pulmonary, ACE2 is expressed on:

- alveolar type II cells and, at lower level, airway epithelium;
- cardiac myocytes, fibroblasts and endothelium;
- kidney proximal tubule and, notably, the taste buds;
- intestinal enterocytes — where its expression is the highest in the body;
- salivary gland, tongue, liver, spleen, pancreas and reproductive tissues.

It is also shed as a soluble ectodomain by [[ADAM17]] and circulates in plasma at low concentration.

> [!warning] Soluble ACE2 as a biomarker, not a therapy
> Because shedding is driven by the protease that cleaves [[ACE2|its own]] receptor and that also releases TNF-α, circulating soluble ACE2 rises with age and with disease states that raise protease activity, which confounds its interpretation as an activity marker.

## SARS-CoV-2 Entry and the RAAS Hypothesis

The spike protein of [[SARS-CoV-2]] binds the extracellular N-terminal domain of ACE2; the C-terminal furin-cleaved S2 subunit is then primed by host proteases such as TMPRSS2 and cathepsin L, and the viral envelope fuses. Two consequences are often discussed:

1. **ACE2 downregulation and shedding.** ACE2 internalisation after engagement, plus shedding of the S1–ACE2 complex, reduces surface ACE2 in lung tissue, which would *favour* ACE over ACE2, raising angiotensin II and dampening angiotensin-(1-7) — predicting an RAAS-imbalance phenotype of vasoconstriction and pro-inflammatory signalling in severe disease. This is the basis of the "RAAS imbalance hypothesis".
2. **The ACE2–S2 hypothesis.** An S2-derived fragment resembling the S1′ receptor-binding motif of ACE2, released by furin cleavage, may act as a decoy and interfere with further spike engagement.

> [!warning] The clinical trial that settled it
> Neither the receptor-blockade arm of the RAAS hypothesis nor the interferon arm of an inhaled decoy trial was supported by randomised evidence. The pragmatic outcome is that [[ACE Inhibitors|ACE inhibitors]] and angiotensin receptor blockers were *not* withdrawn for COVID-19 and continue to be indicated for their original indications; systemic ACE2 blockade is not used.

## Therapeutic Directions

- Soluble ACE2 as a decoy in both prophylaxis and treatment has been trialled without established efficacy.
- Humanised anti-ACE2 antibodies are in development for renal and cardiovascular indications.
- Small-molecule ACE2 inhibitors are used as tools rather than therapeutics, since systemic ACE2 blockade would remove a protective arm of the system.
- Enhancing ACE2 activity indirectly — via [[ACE Inhibitors]] that raise circulating ACE2 through shedding, or via lifestyle interventions — is mechanistically attractive but not established.

## Connections

- [[Angiotensin II]] — the substrate ACE2 inactivates; the ACE2/ACE balance, not either enzyme alone, sets the tone of the renin-angiotensin system.
- [[Angiotensin II Type 1 Receptor]] — the receptor through which the unopposed angiotensin II acts, and the reason ACE2 loss has a pressor rather than a neutral phenotype.
- [[ACE Inhibitors]] — block the counter-regulatory enzyme's counterpart and thereby *raise* ACE2 levels by shedding; the class that made the RAAS axis therapeutically accessible.
- [[ADAM17]] — the sheddase that releases both soluble ACE2 and TNF-α, the mechanistic link between ACE2 handling and the inflammatory phenotype of severe COVID-19.
- [[SARS-CoV-2]] — the virus for which ACE2 is the entry receptor, which converted this enzyme from a cardiovascular regulator into a global research object.
- [[COVID-19]] — the clinical context of the RAAS imbalance hypothesis and the trials that addressed it.

- [[Alveolar Type II Cells]] — the principal respiratory epithelial target of ACE2-mediated infection, and the cell whose loss contributes to the lung injury of severe disease.
- [[Hypertension]] — the clinical readout of ACE/ACE2 imbalance, and the therapeutic domain in which ACE2 modulation has been pursued.
- [[Kidney]] — a major site of ACE2 expression and of ACE2-associated pathophysiology, including in diabetic nephropathy.
- [[Nitric Oxide]] — the vasodilator whose endothelial bioavailability ACE2 supports, and whose loss contributes to the vascular dysfunction of ACE2 deficiency.
- [[Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease]] — among the chronic lung conditions in which ACE2 expression and activity are altered and have been proposed as disease modifiers.
- [[Inflammation]] — the process in which the angiotensin-(1-7)/Mas arm exerts anti-inflammatory effects, opposed by angiotensin II.
