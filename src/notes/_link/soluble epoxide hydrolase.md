# Soluble epoxide hydrolase

**Soluble epoxide hydrolase (sEH)** is a bifunctional cytosolic enzyme encoded by *EPHX2* that hydrolyzes epoxy-fatty acids — particularly epoxyeicosatrienoic acids (EETs) derived from arachidonic acid via cytochrome P450 — into their corresponding diols. Because EETs exert vasodilatory, anti-inflammatory, and organ-protective effects, sEH-mediated degradation is generally pro-pathological, and sEH inhibition is an active therapeutic strategy in cardiovascular, renal, and inflammatory disease. The enzyme also possesses a C-terminal hydrolase domain whose substrates extend to protein and peptide epoxides, allowing it to influence protein fate beyond lipid signaling.

## sEH regulation of SIRT3 turnover

Biochemical analyses delineated a regulatory axis in which sEH modulates [[SIRT3]] turnover via proteolytic degradation, with sEH deficiency preserving SIRT3 bioavailability to fulfill bioenergetic demands and impede renal [[Vascular Calcification|vascular calcification]] progression[315]. In this model, sEH activity shortens SIRT3 protein half-life; genetic or pharmacological loss of sEH stabilizes SIRT3, maintaining mitochondrial function and opposing the calcific transformation of vascular smooth muscle cells that characterizes advanced chronic kidney disease.

## Clinical relevance

sEH inhibitors (e.g., 12-(3-adamantan-1-yl-ureido)-dodecanoic acid, AUDA) are in preclinical and clinical development for hypertension and kidney disease. The sEH–SIRT3 axis suggests that sEH inhibition could double as an indirect SIRT3-stabilizing strategy in vascular calcification, linking lipid-epoxide biology to mitochondrial deacetylase availability.
