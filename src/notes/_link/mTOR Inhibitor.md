# mTOR Inhibitor

An mTOR inhibitor is an agent that suppresses the [[mTOR]] (mechanistic target of rapamycin) serine/threonine kinase, a central nutrient- and growth-sensing hub integrating signals from amino acids, insulin/[[IGF1]], and cellular energy. Inhibition of the mTORC1 complex shifts cells from anabolic growth toward catabolic maintenance.

## Mechanism of Action

mTOR inhibitors fall into two broad classes: (1) allosteric [[Rapalogs]] such as [[Rapamycin]] and [[Everolimus]] that bind FKBP12 to inhibit mTORC1, and (2) ATP-competitive catalytic inhibitors targeting both mTORC1 and mTORC2. Suppressed mTOR relieves inhibition of [[Autophagy]] and [[Lysosome|lysosomal]] biogenesis (via [[TFEB]]), promoting cellular cleanup.

## Physiological & Longevity Effects

By inhibiting mTOR, these agents recapitulate key features of [[Caloric Restriction]] — improved [[Insulin Signaling|insulin sensitivity]], reduced [[Inflammation]], and enhanced proteostasis — without dietary restriction. They are leading [[Geroprotectors]] under study for [[Healthspan]] and [[Longevity]] extension, and are being evaluated in [[Aging|age-related]] disease.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Beyond longevity, mTOR inhibitors are licensed as immunosuppressants and anticancer drugs; their pleiotropic actions demand careful dosing to balance benefit against metabolic and immune side effects.

> [!warning] Dual-Complex Effects
> Catalytic inhibitors also block mTORC2, which can disrupt glucose homeostasis; rapalogs more selectively target mTORC1.
