# Mitochondrial Processing Peptidase

The mitochondrial processing peptidase (MPP) is a matrix-localized metalloprotease that removes N-terminal [[Mitochondrial Targeting Sequence|mitochondrial targeting sequences]] (MTSs) from newly imported nuclear-encoded proteins. It resides in the [[Mitochondrial Matrix]] and acts shortly after substrates traverse the inner membrane, enabling proper folding and function of mature mitochondrial proteins.

In [[PINK1]] biology, MPP cleaves the PINK1 mitochondrial targeting sequence (residues 1–34) upon import. This processing, together with cleavage by [[PARL]], ensures that PINK1 is fully imported and degraded under healthy conditions, preventing spurious activation of mitophagy. Disruption of membrane potential blocks import, sparing PINK1 from MPP and PARL processing and allowing it to accumulate on the [[Mitochondria|outer mitochondrial membrane]] to recruit [[Parkin]].

MPP therefore functions as a gatekeeper of the PINK1 import-and-degrade pathway, central to the decision between mitochondrial turnover and preservation.
