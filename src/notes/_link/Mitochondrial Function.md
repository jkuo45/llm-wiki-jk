# Mitochondrial Function

**Mitochondrial function** refers to the integrated physiological capacity of [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] to perform their core cellular roles, principally [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] (ATP production via the [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain|electron transport chain]]), metabolite and ion homeostasis, [[Mitochondrial ROS|redox balance]], [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|dynamics]], quality control, and stress signaling. Because mitochondria sit at the intersection of bioenergetics, metabolism, and innate immune sensing, "mitochondrial function" is often used as a composite measure encompassing [[Mitochondrial Respiration|respiratory capacity]], [[Mitochondrial Membrane Potential|membrane potential]], [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|biogenesis]], [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], and the integrity of [[Mitochondrial DNA|mitochondrial DNA]].

## Components of Mitochondrial Function

- **Bioenergetics**: ATP synthesis through oxidative phosphorylation couples the [[TCA cycle]], [[Pyruvate Dehydrogenase]], and [[Fatty acid oxidation|fatty-acid oxidation]] to the electron transport chain; respiratory capacity is a primary readout of function.
- **Metabolic hub**: Mitochondria integrate glucose, lipid, and amino-acid metabolism and supply biosynthetic precursors (e.g., citrate for cytosolic [[Acetyl-CoA]] and [[Histone Acetylation]]).
- **Redox and ion homeostasis**: The organelle buffers calcium and produces/consumes [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]; [[Mitochondrial Uncoupling|mild uncoupling]] and uncoupling proteins modulate proton leak and energy efficiency.
- **Quality control**: [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|Fusion–fission]] dynamics, [[Mitophagy]], and the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPR^mt^]] maintain a healthy mitochondrial network.
- **Signaling**: Mitochondria signal to the nucleus and innate immune system, notably through [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] release activating the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING axis]].

## Decline with Aging and Disease

- Mitochondrial function declines with age through accumulation of mtDNA mutations (rising [[Heteroplasmy]]), erosion of quality control, and [[NAD+]] depletion — a metabolic bottleneck that compromises sirtuin ([[SIRT1]], [[SIRT3]]) and AMPK-dependent resilience.
- Loss of function drives [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction-Associated Senescence|MiDAS]], stem cell exhaustion, and [[Inflammaging]], coupling bioenergetic failure to chronic inflammation (Bautista & López-Cortés, 2026).
- Functional decline underlies insulin resistance, sarcopenia, neurodegeneration, and heart failure; measures such as RMR variation are partly attributable to differences in mitochondrial efficiency (Speakman & Selman, 2003).

## Assessing Mitochondrial Function

- [[Indirect Calorimetry]] and measurements of [[Resting Metabolic Rate]] reflect whole-body mitochondrial energy conversion.
- Ex vivo assessments include respirometry (oxygen consumption), membrane potential measurement, ROS production, and citrate synthase activity as a proxy for mitochondrial content.
- In aging research, restoring mitochondrial function — via [[NAD+]] repletion ([[Nicotinamide Riboside|NR]], [[NMN]]), mitophagy enhancement ([[Urolithin A]]), [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] activation, or mtDNA editing ([[mitoTALENs]], [[mitoZFNs]]) — is a major therapeutic strategy.
