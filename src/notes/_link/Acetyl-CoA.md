# Acetyl-CoA

**Overview:** Acetyl-coenzyme A ([[Acetyl-CoA]]) is a central metabolic hub: the two-carbon acetyl unit is carried by coenzyme A and feeds [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|oxidative phosphorylation]], biosynthesis, and epigenetics. It is generated from pyruvate (pyruvate dehydrogenase), fatty-acid β-oxidation, amino-acid catabolism, and acetate (via acetyl-CoA synthetase).

**Role in Energy Metabolism:** Acetyl-CoA enters the citric acid (TCA) cycle in the [[Mitochondria]], where condensation with oxaloacetate forms citrate. Its oxidation drives [[NAD+]] and FADH₂ production and ultimately ATP synthesis. The acetyl-CoA/CoA and NAD+/NADH ratios are key metabolic sensors.

**Role in Epigenetics:** Acetyl-CoA is the obligate acetyl donor for [[Histone Acetylation]], catalyzed by histone acetyltransferases (HATs); the opposing reaction is performed by [[Sirtuins]] (NAD+-dependent deacetylases) and [[Histone Deacetylase|HDACs]]. Thus the availability of acetyl-CoA directly couples nutrient state to chromatin state and gene expression. High acetyl-CoA favors open, transcriptionally active chromatin; sirtuin-mediated deacetylation is energy- and [[Caloric Restriction]]-linked.

**Cross-talk with Autophagy:** Nutrient sufficiency raises acetyl-CoA, which can inhibit [[Autophagy]] via mTOR activation, whereas fasting lowers acetyl-CoA and promotes autophagic flux. This positions acetyl-CoA as a metabolic switch between anabolism and catabolism.

**Longevity Relevance:** The acetyl-CoA pool integrates diet, exercise, and [[Sirtuins]] activity. Interventions that modulate acetyl-CoA availability (e.g., [[Nicotinamide Riboside]], caloric restriction, [[Mitohormesis]]) influence downstream acetylation and stress-resistance pathways relevant to [[Longevity]].
