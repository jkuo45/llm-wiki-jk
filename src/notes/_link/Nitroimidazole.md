# Nitroimidazole

[[Nitroimidazole|Nitroimidazoles]] are a class of nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compounds (e.g., metronidazole, nimorazole, misonidazole) used as antimicrobials and investigated as hypoxic cell [[Radiosensitizer|radiosensitizers]]. They act by replacing oxygen in the chemical reactions that produce DNA damage from ionizing radiation, thereby increasing cytotoxicity in [[Hypoxia|hypoxic]] cells while having no effect on already-aerobic (maximally radiosensitive) cells.

## Relevance to Fenbendazole

> [!info] Source: [[Fenbendazole as a Potential Anticancer Drug]]
> Nitroimidazoles and benzimidazoles are discussed as compounds with selective effects on hypoxic cells; some modified benzimidazoles were effective hypoxic cell radiosensitizers. Fenbendazole's structural resemblance to these compounds motivated the investigation of its potential radiosensitizing properties (Duan et al. 2013).
