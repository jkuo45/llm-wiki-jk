# Intestinal Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury

**Intestinal ischemia-reperfusion injury** is mucosal damage that occurs when blood flow is restored to an ischemic gut segment — relevant to mesenteric ischemia, bowel transplantation, and hemorrhagic shock. Reperfusion drives epithelial reactive-oxygen accumulation, mitochondrial oxidative damage, apoptosis, and loss of barrier integrity, which can propagate systemic inflammation.

## Sirtuin Axis

- **[[SIRT1]]**: Suppresses epithelial ROS accumulation and apoptosis (e.g., via miR-34a-5p modulation); resveratrol protects through the SIRT1–NF-κB–iNOS–NO pathway.
- **[[SIRT3]]**: Alleviates mitochondrial oxidative damage and apoptosis via deacetylation of [[Peroxiredoxin 3]].
- **[[SIRT6]]**: Downregulation by miR-351-5p aggravates injury via oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis.

## Clinical Relevance

Protection of the mucosal barrier during reperfusion reduces bacterial translocation and systemic inflammatory sequelae. Sirtuin activation (especially SIRT1/SIRT3) is a candidate intestinal-protective strategy.
