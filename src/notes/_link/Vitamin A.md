# Vitamin A

**Vitamin A** is the collective name for a family of fat-soluble **retinoids** — the alcohol **retinol**, the aldehyde **retinal** (retinaldehyde), the carboxylic acid **all-*trans* retinoic acid** ([[Retinoic Acid|ATRA]]), and the storage ester **retinyl esters** — plus the plant **[[Carotenoids|provitamin A carotenoids]]** (β-carotene, α-carotene, β-cryptoxanthin) that the body cleaves into retinal. Humans cannot synthesize the polyene ring de novo, so vitamin A status is entirely dietary. Vitamin A is not only a vision and differentiation nutrient: retinol, retinal, and ATRA are all direct suppressors of [[Ferroptosis]] through a combination of lipid-radical trapping and transcriptional induction of the [[GPX4]]/[[FSP1]] defense modules.

## Chemical Properties and Classification

| Metabolite | Form | Key role | Pro-vitamin A activity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Retinol | C20H30O, alcohol | Storage/transport form; non-genomic mitochondrial cofactor | Full (1 RE) |
| Retinal (11-*cis* / all-*trans*) | C20H28O, aldehyde | Chromophore of rhodopsin/opsins (11-*cis*); RAR ligand | Full (1 RE) |
| All-*trans* retinoic acid | C20H28O2, acid | Nuclear RAR/RXR ligand; gene transcription | None (irreversible end of the pathway) |
| Retinyl palmitate/acetate | Esterified | Hepatic storage form in [[Hepatic Stellate Cells]]; circulating form on RBP4 | Full |

The retinoid motif is a C20 trimethyl-cyclohexenyl polyene chain ending in a polar group; as the polar group oxidizes (retinol → retinal → retinoic acid), the molecule becomes progressively less able to be stored and more able to bind nuclear receptors — the pathway is a one-way ratchet toward transcriptional activity, with retinoic acid catabolized by CYP26. β-Carotene (from [[Red Palm Oil]], carrots, leafy greens) converts centrally via BCO1 to two retinal molecules, and eccentrically via BCO2 to apocarotenoids; α-carotene and β-cryptoxanthin yield one retinal each.

## Absorption, Transport, Storage, and Turnover

- **Absorption** requires dietary fat: retinyl esters are hydrolyzed by brush-border lipase/RETH, retinol joins mixed micelles, and is taken up by SR-BI; retinol is re-esterified (LRAT) in the enterocyte, packaged into chylomicrons, and delivered to the liver.
- **Plasma transport**: retinol circulates bound to **retinol-binding protein 4 (RBP4)** in a 1:1 complex with transthyretin, which prevents glomerular filtration; the RBP4–transthyretin complex delivers retinol to RBP receptors (STRA6-family and others).
- **Storage**: ~50–80% of the body's vitamin A sits in hepatic stellate cells as retinyl esters — the reason hepatic stellate cell loss (e.g., in hepatic injury) can precipitate deficiency.
- **Homeostatic regulation**: the liver converts retinol to retinyl esters when supply is ample (via lecithin:retinol acyltransferase, LRAT) and mobilizes them when needed; retinoids do not circulate appreciably in free form, so tissue delivery is tightly buffered.
- **Requirement**: RDA 900 μg RAE/day (men), 700 (women); 12:1 conversion for dietary β-carotene, 24:1 for α-carotene/β-cryptoxanthin. UL 3,000 μg/day of **preformed** vitamin A (adults); **provitamin A carotenoids have no UL** because conversion is feedback-regulated.

## Mechanism of Action and the Four Arms of Vitamin A Signaling

1. **Genomic — RAR/RXR**: retinoic acid heterodimerizes retinoic acid receptors (RARα/β/γ) with retinoid X receptors on retinoic acid response elements, driving differentiation, proliferation arrest, and anti-inflammatory gene programs. Among the induced targets for redox defense: **GPX4, FSP1, GCH1** (BH4 synthesis), **ACSL3, SCD1, PPARα** — the transcriptional anti-ferroptotic module (Studer et al., *Nat Commun* 2024).
2. **Vision**: 11-*cis*-retinal is the chromophore of rhodopsin; its isomerization to all-*trans*-retinal initiates the visual transduction cascade, and its regeneration depends on the RPE/retinoid cycle fed by dietary vitamin A.
3. **Mitochondrial/oxidative metabolism (non-genomic)**: retinol acts as a cofactor for mitochondrial **PKCδ** in the pyruvate dehydrogenase/oxidative-phosphosphorylation axis; retinol deficiency impairs complex I/IV function and increases mitochondrial ROS (see the Wiki's fission task output). Excess (supraphysiological) retinoid is the opposite — pro-oxidant, with mitochondrial swelling, permeability transition, and ROS-driven lipid peroxidation that is blunted by vitamin E analogs.
4. **Direct radical trapping (anti-ferroptotic)**: retinol and retinal, as polyenes adjacent to the lipid-phase vitamin E, directly trap lipid peroxyl radicals. In erastin/RSL3 models, retinol (EC50 0.4–4.8 μM) and atRAL (0.7–1.1 μM) inhibited ferroptosis **more potently than α-tocopherol (7.4–36.1 μM)**; atRA (4.4–11.8 μM) also acted, chiefly via transcription (Jakaria et al. 2023; summarized in *Antioxidants* 2024;13:1571). Vitamin A is thus a first-line *dietary* brake on the same lipid-peroxidation chemistry that defines ferroptosis.

## Physiological Function and Clinical Relevance

- **Vision and epithelial integrity**: night blindness, xerophthalmia, Bitot's spots from deficiency; severe deficiency is a leading cause of preventable childhood blindness and is corrected effectively by red palm oil in deficiency-endemic populations.
- **Immune function**: epithelial and mucosal barrier integrity, lymphocyte development; retinoids are used therapeutically in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma and acne (supraphysiological dosing).
- **Mitochondrial fitness**: retinol deficiency lowers complex I activity and raises ROS; conversely, retinoid-driven fission (ATRA ↑ Drp1) is held in check by membrane-phase vitamin E — the same A/E interaction is at the core of the "too much retinoid is pro-oxidant" caveat.
- **Clinical pharmacology**: isotretinoin/tretinoin (acne, APL), alitretinoin (Kaposi), fenretinide (chemoprevention) — all retinoids at pharmacologic doses, precisely the range where the pro-oxidant and transcriptional arms dominate over the nutritional one.
- **Toxicity (hypervitaminosis A)**: hepatotoxicity, bone pain/fracture, intracranial hypertension, dry skin, and teratogenicity from preformed retinoids — the reason provitamin A carotenoids are the safer nutritional route and the reason the UL is set on preformed vitamin A.
- **Ferroptosis-relevant caveat**: the same lipophilicity that makes vitamin A a membrane radical trap also makes high-dose retinoids pro-oxidant and pro-fission; nutritional (RDA-level, food-form) vitamin A acts as defense, pharmacologic retinoid dosing as stress.
