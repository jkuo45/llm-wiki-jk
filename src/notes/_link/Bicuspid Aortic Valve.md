# Bicuspid Aortic Valve

A **bicuspid aortic valve** is a congenital defect in which two of the three aortic valve leaflets fuse during embryogenesis, leaving a functionally two-leaflet valve instead of the normal tricuspid one. It is the most common congenital cardiac valve lesion, present in roughly 1–1.3% of adults, and it is usually inherited in an autosomal dominant with reduced penetrance.

## Embryology and anatomy

The defect arises from failure of endocardial cushion remodelling during the separation of the primitive aortic root. Fusion pattern matters clinically: right–left coronary leaflet fusion is by far the commonest (~80%), right–noncoronary fusion ~17%, and left–noncoronary ~2%. The right–noncoronary pattern carries the strongest association with later [[Aortic Stenosis|aortic stenosis]] and regurgitation.

> [!info] Genetics
> The strongest causal gene is *NOTCH1*: hypomorphic [[Notch]] variants are found in a substantial fraction of familial and sporadic bicuspid valve cases, and *NOTCH1* haploinsufficiency is established for bicuspid aortic valve with early-onset thoracic [[Aortic Aneurysm|aortic aneurysm]]. [[GATA5]] mutations are also reported in BAV families and in BAV with atrial septal defects; however, the yield of a single gene in an individual patient is low, and BAV is genetically heterogeneous with additional loci still unresolved. This is the basis for the vault link from [[GATA5]].

## Haemodynamics

The valve opens asymmetrically, so outflow is eccentric and helical at >1 m/s throughout the ascending aorta, rather than a short central burst as with a tricuspid valve. The jet impinges on one wall, raising wall shear stress in a fusion-pattern-specific location — right-anterior for right–left fusion, right-posterior for right–noncoronary fusion. This is the mechanical explanation for why the aneurysm in bicuspid aortopathy tends to be asymmetric and focal rather than a uniform annular dilation.

## Clinical course

Most patients are asymptomatic for decades, but three problems accumulate:

- **Calcific aortic stenosis** — earlier onset than in tricuspid valves, often by one to two decades. Once symptomatic, valve replacement is indicated.
- **Aortic regurgitation** — from leaflet prolapse or cusp perforation at the raphe, or from coexisting [[Endocarditis]].
- **Ascending aortic aneurysm and dissection** — the "bicuspid aortopathy" phenotype. The aortic wall is intrinsically abnormal: reduced fibrillin-1 in the extracellular matrix, and an increased [[Matrix Metalloproteinases|MMP]]-to-[[TIMP1|TIMP]] balance (MMP2/TIMP1 in one report, MMP9 involvement in others) that shifts the wall toward matrix degradation. Genetic contributors to aneurysm risk, including [[SMAD3]] and [[TGF-beta1|TGF-β]] pathway variants identified in thoracic aortic aneurysm/dissection cohorts, overlap with the same pathway.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Contemporary management requires imaging the ascending aorta at the time of diagnosis, not only the valve. Current guidelines are moving toward aortic-size and aortic-diameter-index based intervention thresholds rather than valve symptoms alone, and follow-up intervals are set by measured aortic growth rate. Reports disagree on the exact thresholds, so local guidelines should be checked rather than any single number quoted here.

## Connections

- [[GATA5]] — GATA5 is a cardiogenic and endocardial transcription factor required for valve morphogenesis; *GATA5* variants segregate with bicuspid aortic valve in some families. The relationship is suggestive rather than universal, since most BAV is NOTCH-driven.
- [[Cardiac Development]] — BAV is a structural defect of valvulogenesis, the last major phase of heart development, arising from abnormal endocardial cushion remodelling. It is one of the clearest congenital lesions for which human genetics maps onto a specific step in cardiac morphogenesis.
- [[Notch]] — *NOTCH1* is the single best-validated gene for bicuspid aortic valve and for bicuspid aortopathy, making NOTCH signalling the leading candidate pathway for the embryonic leaflet fusion defect.
- [[Aortic Aneurysm]] — BAV is the strongest known heritable risk factor for ascending thoracic aortic aneurysm; the wall phenotype (reduced fibrillin-1, raised MMP/TIMP ratio) is the mechanism, not merely the jet.
- [[Cardiac Fibrosis]] — Bicuspid valves calcify and remodel earlier than tricuspid valves, and leaflet fibrosis is a component of the disease trajectory alongside calcification.
- [[Endocardial Cushion]] — The embryological structure whose abnormal remodelling produces the fused commissure; the direct developmental antecedent of the lesion.
