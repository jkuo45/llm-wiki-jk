# Threonine Dehydrogenase

**Threonine dehydrogenase** (L-threonine 3-dehydrogenase; TDH) is an NAD⁺-dependent enzyme of [[Threonine|threonine]] catabolism that catalyses the first step of a destructive route in which [[Threonine]] is converted to **aminoacetone**, which is then cyclised by aminoacetone synthase to **methylglyoxal**. Methylglyoxal is detoxified by the [[Glyoxalase System|glyoxalase system]]; when that system is saturated, methylglyoxal accumulates and becomes a reactive precursor of [[Advanced Glycation End Products|dicarbonyl and AGE chemistry]].

## Reaction and Properties

The overall reaction is:

**L-threonine + NAD⁺ → aminoacetone + NH₃ + CO₂ + NADH**

- The enzyme is **mitochondrial**, located in the [[Mitochondrial Matrix|matrix]] of liver and kidney; well-characterised preparations come from rodent and ruminant liver (classically goat liver).
- Reported goat-liver enzyme properties: a single subunit of Mr ≈ 89,000 with apparent Km values of ~5.5 mM for L-threonine and ~1 mM for NAD⁺.
- **Product feedback inhibition**: methylglyoxal — the downstream end product — inhibits TDH at a site *other than* the active site (allosteric), providing a self-limiting brake on the pathway.
- Some organisms carry NADP⁺-dependent or D-specific forms of the enzyme, so "threonine dehydrogenase" is a family name covering several stereospecificities.

> [!warning] Human relevance is uncertain
> Whether humans express a functional mitochondrial L-threonine dehydrogenase is genuinely unsettled. Human threonine is predominantly catabolised by the alternative route that yields **glycine and acetyl-CoA** (via 2-amino-3-ketobutyrate, through ketoaminolevulinate synthase or branched-chain aminotransferase), and the TDH → aminoacetone → methylglyoxal route is well documented in mice and in liver enzyme preparations but has not been unambiguously established as a significant human in vivo flux. Treat methylglyoxal yield from threonine in humans as a proposed, not proven, contribution.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Methylglyoxal supply.** Because methylglyoxal is a major endogenous AGE precursor and a glycation toxin, upstream routes that increase its formation (threonine catabolism) are of interest in dicarbonyl stress, [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]] and [[Aging|aging]]. Experimentally, inhibiting this route is a strategy for lowering methylglyoxal generation.
- **Fasting and starvation.** Threonine catabolism is upregulated in fasting and in poorly controlled diabetes, when hepatic energy demand rises and protein turnover increases.
- **Enzyme and pathway annotation.** TDH activity is a useful experimental handle: measuring aminoacetone or methylglyoxal formation from labelled threonine is a standard way to test whether a manipulation shifts amino-acid catabolism into the dicarbonyl pathway.

## Connections

- [[Threonine]] — TDH is the committing enzyme of the destructive threonine catabolic route, distinct from the threonine synthase/GS-GOGAT salvage and the threonine → glycine + acetyl-CoA route.
- [[Methylglyoxal]] — TDH's product aminoacetone is the direct precursor of methylglyoxal; this link is the reason threonine catabolism features in dicarbonyl and AGE biochemistry.
- [[Glyoxalase System]] — Glyoxalase I/II detoxify methylglyoxal to lactate; the balance between TDH supply and glyoxalase capacity sets steady-state methylglyoxal.
- [[One-Carbon Metabolism]] — Threonine catabolism intersects one-carbon and nucleotide biosynthesis through the glycine/pyruvate and deaminated products, so pathway flux is metabolically interconnected rather than linear.
- [[Mitochondrial Matrix]] — The mitochondrial matrix localisation places TDH alongside the β-oxidation and TCA enzymes whose NAD⁺/NADH state sets the oxidative driving force for the reaction.
- [[Glycine]] — Glycine is a one-carbon donor and a product of the competing threonine route, so the balance between the two threonine pathways redistributes glycine availability.
