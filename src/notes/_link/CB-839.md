# CB-839

**CB-839** (telaglenastat) is a potent, selective, orally bioavailable inhibitor of **glutaminase (GLS1)** — the enzyme that converts [[Glutamine]] into glutamate, the entry point for [[Glutaminolysis]]. By blocking glutamine-to-α-ketoglutarate flux, CB-839 cuts off the anaplerotic carbon supply that glutamine-addicted tumors rely on to refill the [[TCA cycle]].

## Mechanism and the MYC-high synthetic-lethality story

- **Monotherapy limits**: in unselected [[triple-negative breast cancer|TNBC]] PDX models and clinical trials, CB-839 alone showed limited antitumor activity — most tumors compensate by rerouting redox and energy metabolism.
- **Synthetic lethality with Complex I inhibition**: [[MYC]]-high cells shift the [[TCA cycle]] to run on glutamine-derived carbon instead of glucose-derived carbon. This makes them exquisitely dependent on Complex I — because mitochondrial Complex I regenerates the [[NAD+]] needed to keep the glutamine-fueled cycle turning. Combining CB-839 with Complex I inhibitors ([[Mubritinib]], [[Metformin]]) collapses the cycle on both ends — no anaplerotic input *and* no NAD⁺ regeneration — producing persistent tumor-growth suppression.
- **Biomarker implication**: MYC^high^ status stratifies patients likely to respond, explaining why unselected trials disappointed.
