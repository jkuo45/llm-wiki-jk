# PD-L1

## Overview

[[PD-L1]] (Programmed Death-Ligand 1, also known as CD274 or B7-H1) is a transmembrane protein of the B7 family that serves as a co-inhibitory ligand for the PD-1 receptor on T cells. PD-L1 expression on tumor cells and antigen-presenting cells is a major mechanism of immune evasion in cancer. PD-L1 engagement with PD-1 delivers inhibitory signals that suppress T-cell activation, proliferation, and cytokine production, leading to T-cell exhaustion and immune tolerance.

## Molecular Mechanism

- Binds [[PD-1]] receptor on activated T cells, B cells, and myeloid cells
- PD-1/PD-L1 engagement recruits SHP-2 phosphatase, dephosphorylating TCR signaling molecules
- Results in T-cell anergy, exhaustion, and apoptosis
- Expressed on tumor cells, antigen-presenting cells, and stromal cells under various conditions

## Regulation of Expression

PD-L1 expression is regulated by multiple pathways:

- **IFN-γ signaling**: Major inducer of PD-L1 via JAK/STAT1/IRF1 axis
- **Oncogenic signaling**: PI3K/AKT, MAPK, and ALK pathways upregulate PD-L1 transcriptionally
- **Epigenetic regulation**: Promoter methylation and histone modifications
- **Post-transcriptional**: miRNA-mediated regulation and mRNA stability
- **Adenosine signaling**: In the senescent [[Tumor Microenvironment]], CD73-generated adenosine upregulates PD-L1 expression on macrophages via adenosine receptor signaling

> [!info] Source: Dong et al. 2024
> In the senescent TME, IL-6 from senescent tumor cells elevates adenosine levels, which promotes CD73 expression in macrophages via JAK/STAT3 signaling. Adenosine then upregulates PD-L1 expression in human macrophages, further suppressing anti-tumor immunity. Targeting CD73 enhances the efficacy of anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy.

## Role in Senescence and SASP

The [[SASP]] influences PD-L1 expression through multiple indirect mechanisms:

- SASP-derived adenosine (via CD73) upregulates PD-L1 on macrophages
- SASP inflammatory cytokines can induce PD-L1 on tumor and immune cells
- SASP-mediated immunosuppression synergizes with PD-L1/PD-1 axis to create an immune-excluded TME

## Role in Cancer

- **Immune Evasion**: Many cancer cells express PD-L1 to inhibit the activity of T cells, allowing the tumor to evade the immune response.
- **Breast Cancer**: PD-L1 expression is high in [[triple-negative breast cancer]] and [[HER2-enriched breast cancer]].
- **Regulation**: [[melittin]] has been shown to significantly reduce PD-L1 expression in tumors.

## Therapeutic Targeting

- **Anti-PD-L1 antibodies**: Atezolizumab, durvalumab, avelumab
- **Anti-PD-1 antibodies**: Pembrolizumab, nivolumab (targeting the receptor)
- **Combination with senolytics**: Clearance of senescent cells may reduce PD-L1-driven immunosuppression
- **Combination with CD73 inhibitors**: Block adenosine-mediated PD-L1 upregulation in the senescent TME

> [!tip] Therapeutic Implication
> Combining senolytic therapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-L1/PD-1 could overcome the immunosuppressive barrier created by senescent cells in the TME, restoring anti-tumor immune surveillance.
