# Fenofibrate

**Fenofibrate** is a member of the fibrate (fibric acid) class of lipid-regulating agents and one of the most widely prescribed drugs in the world. It is a prodrug: oral fenofibrate is rapidly hydrolysed by esterases to **fenofibric acid**, which is the only form detected in plasma. Fenofibric acid has a plasma half-life of roughly 16–23 hours, which is what allows once-daily dosing (FDA label).

## Mechanism of Action

Fenofibric acid activates **nuclear receptor [[PPARα]]** in hepatocytes and adipocytes, and the mechanism is well modelled in *Pparα* knockout mice and human hepatocyte cultures:

- **Increased lipolysis** in adipose tissue and enhanced hepatic uptake of fatty acids.
- **Increased lipoprotein lipase activity** at the capillary endothelium, accelerating clearance of triglyceride-rich particles.
- **Decreased hepatic apolipoprotein C-III** production — apoC-III being the natural inhibitor of lipoprotein lipase — so the two effects act together.
- **Reduced VLDL and chylomicron secretion**, which lowers plasma triglycerides and downstream [[Low-Density Lipoprotein|LDL]]; increased [[Apolipoprotein A1|apoA-I]] and apoA-II raises [[HDL]].

Secondary effects of clinical interest: fenofibrate lowers serum uric acid by increasing urate clearance, and it modestly reduces plasma [[Cholesterol]] and apolipoprotein B.

## Clinical Uses

- **Severe hypertriglyceridemia** (TG ≥ 500 mg/dL) to reduce pancreatitis risk — its clearest FDA indication.
- **Mixed dyslipidemia** as adjunct to diet, with the caveat that its LDL-lowering is modest and inconsistent.
- Historically used for type 2 diabetes dyslipidaemia and for raising HDL.

> [!warning] Lipid lowering is not the same as outcome lowering
> In **ACCORD-Lipid** (type 2 diabetes, mixed dyslipidaemia), fenofibrate lowered triglycerides but **failed to reduce cardiovascular morbidity or mortality** versus simvastatin (Girard *et al.*, *NEJM* 2010, PMID 20185005). It should not be used as an LDL-lowering agent with the expectation of cardiovascular benefit, and combining it with a [[Statins|statin]] brings added cost without added outcome benefit. It also lacks the antiplatelet effect of aspirin and has no place in atherosclerosis primary prevention.

## Toxicology

- **Hepatic**: transaminase elevation; severe hepatotoxicity is uncommon but discontinuation should follow marked persistent rises.
- **Muscle**: myalgia and *CPK* elevation; greater risk in combination with statins, in renal impairment, or with viral hepatitis/alcohol-related liver disease.
- **Gallbladder**: increased cholesterol gallstone risk (cholesterol is excreted into bile).
- **Renal**: contraindicated in severe renal impairment because fenofibric acid accumulates; mild–moderate impairment prolongs its half-life.
- **Haematological**: rare thrombocytopenia/agranulocytosis.
- **Drug interactions**: fibrates potentiate coumarin-type anticoagulants such as [[Warfarin]]; **fibrate–statin** combinations raise myopathy risk; biliary imaging agents can cause false gallstone readings.
- **Reproductive**: contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

## Mechanisms Beyond Lipids

PPARα agonism is of mechanistic interest to the ageing vault beyond lipid lowering: fenofibrate induces peroxisome proliferation, enhances [[Beta-Oxidation]] and mitochondrial fatty acid handling, and has been reported to activate [[AMPK]] and [[Autophagy]] programs with [[Mitophagy]] effects; it also suppresses [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] activation and expression of [[VCAM-1]] and other endothelial adhesion molecules, which is the basis of its experimental anti-inflammatory and anti-atherosclerotic use. These are mechanistic and preclinical observations — they are not established clinical outcomes.

## Connections

- [[PPARα]] — Its defining target: fenofibrate's entire lipid-lowering profile, peroxisome proliferation and metabolic gene programme run through PPARα.
- [[Statins]] — The other major lipid-lowering class; they lower LDL and reduce events, whereas fenofibrate lowers triglycerides and did not reduce events, so the two are complementary at best and risky in combination.
- [[Lipid Metabolism]] — Fenofibrate is a pharmacological lever on triglyceride-rich particle handling: lipoprotein lipase activation and apoC-III suppression.
- [[Atherosclerosis]] — Fenofibrate lowers a lipid causally implicated in atherosclerotic progression, but the outcome trials mean it is not an anti-atherosclerotic drug in the clinical sense.
- [[Hepatic Steatosis]] — PPARα agonism induces peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation, which is why fibrates are explored in fatty liver disease even though they are not approved for it.
- [[Warfarin]] — Fibrate potentiation of vitamin K antagonist anticoagulation is a classic, clinically important interaction.
