# Proteinopathy

## Overview

[[Proteinopathy]] describes a family of [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegenerative diseases]] driven by the misfolding, accumulation, and aggregation of specific proteins. Classic examples include [[Amyloid Beta|Aβ]] and [[Tau|tau]] in [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]], [[Alpha-synuclein|α-synuclein]] in [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]], mutant [[TDP-43]] in [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]] and FTD, mutant [[Huntingtin|huntingtin]] in [[Huntington's Disease|Huntington's disease]], and mutant SOD1. Although the offending proteins differ, a unifying principle has emerged: many proteinopathies ultimately generate cytosolic dsDNA capable of activating [[cGAS]] and thereby engaging the [[Innate Immune System|innate immune]] machinery.

## Cytosolic dsDNA Generation

The most consistent source of activating DNA across proteinopathies is leakage of [[mtDNA]] from [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|damaged or stressed mitochondria]], often via [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore|mPTP]]/[[VDAC1]] opening or [[BAX|Bax]]-mediated [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|outer membrane permeabilization]]. Nuclear [[DNA Damage|DNA damage]] provides an additional, albeit less universal, source of cytosolic dsDNA. Once in the cytosol, this self-DNA engages cGAS, driving [[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]] and [[Cytokines|cytokine]] production through [[IRF3]] and [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]]. Different pathogenic proteins engage distinct mitochondrial release mechanisms that are both cell-type specific and trigger dependent.

## The Tau–PQBP1 Exception

A notable exception to the DNA-dependent rule is the [[Tauopathy|tau]]–[[PQBP1]] complex, which activates cGAS through a direct protein–protein interaction that bypasses the requirement for DNA entirely. This noncanonical mechanism underscores that cGAS can be engaged by proteotoxic stress through multiple routes, broadening the relevance of cGAS-STING signaling across the proteinopathy spectrum.

## Broader Implications

Genetic risk factors such as [[APOE4]] and [[TREM2]] R47H synergistically amplify cGAS-STING/IFN-I signaling, driving senescence-like glial states marked by mitochondrial stress, [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle]] arrest, and a [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]]. Pharmacological inhibition of cGAS or STING, as well as protective variants like [[APOE3 Christchurch]], restore homeostasis, reduce proteinopathy burden, and improve cognitive and motor outcomes across multiple models.
