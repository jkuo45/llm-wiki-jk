## Overview

Platelet aggregation is the process whereby activated platelets bind to one another, primarily through fibrinogen bridging the integrin receptor [[Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa]] (αIIbβ3) on adjacent platelets. It is the terminal common pathway of primary hemostasis and the basis of pathological arterial thrombosis.

## Mechanism

- Platelet activation exposes/activates αIIbβ3, enabling fibrinogen- and von Willebrand factor-mediated cross-linking.
- Secondary mediators (thromboxane A2, ADP, serotonin) amplify recruitment and aggregation.
- The resulting aggregate is stabilized by the coagulation cascade (thrombin, fibrin).

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Physiologically essential for wound sealing; pathologically, excessive aggregation underlies myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, and peripheral vascular disease. Antiplatelet agents (aspirin, P2Y12 inhibitors, GPIIb/IIIa antagonists) target this process. Aggregation is commonly quantified by light-transmission aggregometry.
