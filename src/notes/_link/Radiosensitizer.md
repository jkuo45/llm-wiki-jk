# Radiosensitizer

[[Radiosensitizer|Radiosensitizers]] are agents that enhance the cytotoxic effects of ionizing radiation. Hypoxic cell radiosensitizers (e.g., nimorazole, misonidazole) replace molecular oxygen in the chemical reactions that produce radiation-induced DNA damage, increasing radiation killing in [[Hypoxia|hypoxic]] cells while having no effect on aerobic cells (which are already maximally radiosensitive).

## Classes

- **Nitroimidazoles** and **benzimidazoles** — classic hypoxic cell radiosensitizers.
- **Nitroheterocyclic cytotoxins** — overlap with radiosensitizer mechanisms.
- Oxygen itself — the endogenous chemical radiosensitizer.

## Relevance to Fenbendazole

> [!warning] Source: [[Fenbendazole as a Potential Anticancer Drug]]
> Because fenbendazole's chemical structure resembles known radiosensitizers, it was tested for radiosensitizing activity in EMT6 cells. Fenbendazole did not alter the radiation response of aerobic or hypoxic EMT6 cells, nor did it sensitize EMT6 tumors to radiation in vivo (Duan et al. 2013).
