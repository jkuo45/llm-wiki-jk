# TOP1 (Topoisomerase 1)

TOP1 (Topoisomerase 1) is an enzyme that relieves DNA supercoiling by creating transient single-strand breaks. Its trapped cleavage complexes (TOP1cc) have crucial roles in assisting [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS]] recognition of and binding to cytoplasmic chromatin fragments (CCFs), which is essential for downstream SASP regulation.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

TOP1 catalyzes controlled nicking of one DNA strand, allowing controlled rotation to remove supercoils generated by transcription and replication, then religates the break. Camptothecin-class drugs (topotecan, irinotecan) trap the covalent TOP1–DNA intermediate as TOP1cc. In senescence, nuclear envelope dysfunction and persistent DNA damage release CCFs into the cytosol; TOP1 present on these fragments licenses cGAS binding by exposing appropriate DNA topology and co-localizing with the sensor. This cooperates with [[G3BP1]], which also scaffolds cGAS onto CCFs. cGAS activation yields cGAMP → STING → TBK1/IRF3 and NF-κB, launching the type-I interferon and inflammatory arms of the SASP.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

TOP1cc are essential for cGAS–STING activation in senescence:
- They link DNA damage/topo stress to SASP regulation through the cytosolic DNA axis.
- Pharmacologic TOP1 trapping (e.g., low-dose irinotecan) can exacerbate senescence-associated interferon signaling, whereas TOP1 modulation attenuates it.
- TOP1 thus positions DNA topology and replication stress as upstream triggers of the SASP, complementing the DDR–p53/pRb growth-arrest program.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- TOP1 is a validated cancer target (topotecan, irinotecan, belotecan) for lung, ovarian, and colorectal cancers.
- TOP1-targeted therapy induces senescence; managing the resultant SASP is relevant to treatment toxicity and [[Inflammaging]].
- Modulating TOP1cc–cGAS licensing is a candidate strategy to tune SASP in [[Senescent Cells]].
