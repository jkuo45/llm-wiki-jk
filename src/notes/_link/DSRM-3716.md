# DSRM-3716

**DSRM-3716** is a second-generation **[[SARM1]] inhibitor** developed to overcome the promiscuity of the repurposed covalent inhibitor [[Disulfiram]].

## Mechanism

DSRM-3716 is a non-covalent small molecule that occupies the [[NAD+]]-binding pocket of the [[SARM1]] TIR dimer without covalent modification. It achieves greater potency (low nanomolar IC₅₀) and selectivity than disulfiram, blocking SARM1 NADase activity and protecting axons from Wallerian degeneration.

## Therapeutic Context

- Part of the same chemical-class advance that produced [[GSK-428]] and related quinazoline / pyrazolopyrimidine scaffolds.
- Targeted at chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), traumatic brain injury ([[Traumatic Brain Injury]]), and chronic peripheral neuropathy.
- Belongs to the **adduct-forming uncompetitive inhibitor** class shared conceptually with the [[CD38]] inhibitor 78c (Bratkowski et al. 2022, _Neuron_).
