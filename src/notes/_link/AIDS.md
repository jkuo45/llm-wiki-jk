# AIDS

**AIDS** (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome) is not a separate organism or a distinct
disease but the terminal clinical phase of infection with [[HIV]], chiefly [[HIV-1]]. It is
defined operationally by either a [[CD4]] T-cell count below 200 cells/mm3 (or a
CD4 percentage below 14%) or by the occurrence of one of a list of designated
opportunistic infections and malignancies regardless of CD4 count.

## Relationship to HIV infection

The natural history of untreated [[HIV-1]] infection runs from acute infection, through a
prolonged asymptomatic phase, to progressive immunodeficiency. AIDS is the terminal
stage. The transition is driven principally by progressive depletion of CD4 T cells,
through direct cytolytic infection of CD4 cells, immune-mediated killing of infected
cells, and pyroptosis triggered by abortive infection. Chronic immune activation —
elevated soluble [[Interferon|interferon]]-inducible proteins, gut barrier disruption
with microbial translocation, and persistent lymphoid hyperplasia — is a major additional
driver of CD4 loss and of non-AIDS morbidity.

> [!important] Not every HIV infection becomes AIDS
> With combination [[Antiretroviral Therapy|antiretroviral therapy (ART)]], started early,
> progression to AIDS is rare and life expectancy approaches that of the general
> population. The natural history above describes *untreated* infection.

## Defining clinical entities

The US CDC/NIH surveillance case definition lists conditions that constitute AIDS in a
person with documented HIV. Representative categories:

- **Pneumocystis** jirovecii pneumonia
- [[Esophageal Candidiasis|Esophageal candidiasis]] and other endemic mycoses
  ([[Cryptococcal Meningitis|cryptococcal meningitis]], [[Histoplasmosis]],
  [[Blastomycosis]])
- Recurrent bacterial pneumonia; chronic cryptosporidiosis or isosporiasis
- Disseminated or extrapulmonary [[Kaposi sarcoma]], which is HHV-8-associated and
  diagnoses AIDS in its visceral forms
- AIDS-related [[Lymphoma]] (primary CNS, immunoblastic or Burkitt-like)
- [[Cervical Cancer|Cervical carcinoma]], invasive
- [[Mycobacterium|Mycobacterial]] disease other than tuberculosis; *Mycobacterium avium*
  complex with CD4 below 50
- [[Toxoplasma gondii|Toxoplasmosis]] of brain
- [[Wasting|Wasting syndrome]] due to HIV; recurrent or severe bacterial pneumonia

Notably, several of these entities — candidiasis, cryptococcosis, tuberculosis,
Pneumocystis — are also seen in other profoundly immunocompromised states, so AIDS
designation requires documented HIV infection.

## Disease burden and trajectory

The [[Global Pandemic|global HIV pandemic]] accounts for roughly 630,000–700,000
deaths per year. Without ART, survival after AIDS diagnosis is typically about
1–3 years. With suppressive ART, immune recovery occurs in most patients over 1–3 years,
although a substantial fraction retain immune dysfunction despite a restored CD4 count.

> [!warning] Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome
> Rapid ART initiation in a patient with high pathogen burden can produce IRIS — an
> exaggerated inflammatory response to residual opportunistic infection, most often
> *M. avium*, cryptococcus or TB. This is a management problem of *treated* AIDS, not
> of untreated disease.

## Prevention and treatment

ART suppresses plasma viral load to undetectable levels in the great majority of adherent
patients, which prevents progression, prevents transmission (treatment as prevention),
and eliminates the majority of AIDS-defining events. Prevention of [[Scabies]] is
sometimes flagged in opportunistic-infection protocols because crusted (Norwegian) scabies
is markedly associated with advanced HIV.

## Connections

- [[HIV-1]] — AIDS is the clinical end stage of HIV-1 infection. Untreated, roughly half
  of infected individuals reach AIDS within about a decade, a trajectory that ART
  essentially abolishes.
- [[CD4]] — the CD4 count is the axis on which AIDS is defined and on which immune
  recovery after ART is judged; it is a staging marker, not a cause.
- [[Scabies]] — crusted scabies occurs almost exclusively in severely immunocompromised
  hosts, so scabies in a patient with known HIV is a marker to check CD4 count and
  consider an AIDS-defining state.
- [[Opportunistic infection]] — opportunistic infections are the clinical face of AIDS;
  most are unmasked by the loss of T-cell help rather than being intrinsically
  AIDS-specific.
- [[Inflammaging]] — chronic immune activation persists even in treated HIV and
  contributes to cardiovascular, renal and neurocognitive comorbidity, so a
  residual-AIDS-phenotype exists after virologic suppression.
