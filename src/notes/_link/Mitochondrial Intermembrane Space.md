### Overview
The Mitochondrial Intermembrane Space (IMS) is the narrow region between the [[Outer Mitochondrial Membrane]] and the [[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane]]. It contains a distinct proteome and serves as a critical interface for the machinery that tethers and fuses mitochondria.

### Fusion Machinery Localization
The HR2 (heptad-repeat) domains of the mitofusins [[MFN1]] and [[MFN2]] extend into the IMS, where they engage in trans interactions between adjacent mitochondria. The bulk of short-form OPA1, known as S-OPA1, also faces the IMS.

### Redox-Dependent Oligomerization
Redox-dependent disulfide bond formation between MFN HR2 domains within the IMS drives OMM oligomerization, a key step in mitochondrial fusion. This oxidative regulation links cellular redox state to organelle morphology and [[Mitochondrial Integrity]].

### Functional Significance
By concentrating the tethering and oligomerization domains of the major fusion GTPases, the IMS coordinates the symmetrical merger of both mitochondrial membranes during fusion events.
