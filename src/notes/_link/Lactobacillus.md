# Lactobacillus

**Overview:** [[Lactobacillus]] is a genus of Gram-positive, facultative anaerobic or microaerophilic, rod-shaped, non-spore-forming bacteria renowned for fermenting carbohydrates into [[Lactic Acid]]. Once classified broadly, many species have been reclassified into genera such as *Lacticaseibacillus* and *Ligilactobacillus*, though the name persists clinically.

**Physiological function:** Lactobacilli produce lactic acid, bacteriocins, and hydrogen peroxide, lowering pH to inhibit pathogens and reinforcing mucosal barriers. In the gut they complement [[Bifidobacterium]] and contribute to fermentation, though they are less dominant than in fermented foods or the vagina. They support [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] production and interact with the [[Immune System]] to promote tolerance.

**Relevance to aging and disease:** A healthy Lactobacillus predominance is associated with metabolic and vaginal health; its decline with [[Aging]] or antibiotic use can permit dysbiosis. Probiotic Lactobacillus strains are studied for glycemic control, [[Inflammation]] reduction, and gut barrier protection.
