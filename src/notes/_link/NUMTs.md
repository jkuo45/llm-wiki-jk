# NUMTs

NUMTs (Nuclear insertions of mitochondrial origin) are copies of [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] that have been integrated into the nuclear genome during evolution. They represent "molecular fossils" of the ancient endosymbiotic transfer of genetic material from mitochondria to the nucleus, and are generally considered pseudogenes.

## Characteristics

- **Size**: Most NUMTs are small insertions (<500 bp); only 12.85% are >1500 bp
- **Distribution**: An estimated 755 NUMTs are dispersed throughout the human nuclear genome
- **Chromosome distribution**: Chromosome 2 has the most insertions; chromosome 18 has the least
- **Sequence identity**: Mean percentage identity between NUMTs and mtDNA is 79.2% (range 63.5%–100%)
- **Size-identity correlation**: Percentage identity is inversely correlated with insertion size

## The Humanin Controversy

> [!warning] Ongoing Debate
> The mitochondrial origin of [[Humanin]] is still debated. NUMTs reflecting humanin sequences are found in chromosomes 1–13 with varying degrees of homology, but none are fully identical to the original cDNA clone from the Alzheimer's patient brain. Strong circumstantial data supports a mitochondrial origin, but a NUMT-derived humanin has not been ruled out.

Evidence supporting mitochondrial origin:
- The humanin cDNA clone from AD brain is 100% identical to the mitochondrial 16S rRNA sequence
- Northern blot hybridization shows humanin ORF probe binds to mitochondrial 16S rRNA-sized transcripts
- ρ0 HeLa cells (lacking [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]]) do not show hybridization
- Formylated humanin has enhanced [[FPRL1|FPRL1]] binding, consistent with mitochondrial N-terminal formylation

Evidence suggesting possible NUMT origin:
- NUMT-derived peptides with 90% homology retain some biological activity
- The translational site (mitochondrial vs. cytoplasmic) remains undetermined

## Evolutionary Significance

NUMTs document the ongoing process of genome reduction in mitochondria and gene transfer to the nucleus. They serve as:
- Markers of evolutionary history
- Potential sources of novel gene functions
- Tools for phylogenetic analysis
- Sources of potential confounding in [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] sequencing studies
