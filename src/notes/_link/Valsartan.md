# Valsartan

## Overview

Valsartan is an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) used clinically to treat [[Hypertension]] and [[Heart Failure]]. Beyond its hemodynamic effects, valsartan modulates mitochondrial dynamics by inhibiting [[Parkin]] activity or [[Mitophagy]], contributing to cardiac protection.

## Mechanism of Action

Valsartan acts through multiple mechanisms:

- **Angiotensin II receptor blockade** — Blocks AT1 receptors, reducing vasoconstriction, aldosterone secretion, and sympathetic activation
- **Mitochondrial effects** — Inhibits Parkin activity or mitophagy, which contributes to its cardioprotective properties
- **Anti-hypertrophic effects** — Alleviates [[Left Ventricular Hypertrophy]] by reducing afterload and direct cardiac effects

## Therapeutic Applications

Valsartan is used in several cardiovascular conditions:

- **Hypertension** — First-line antihypertensive therapy
- **Heart failure** — Used in combination with sacubitril (as LCZ696/Entresto) for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction
- **Post-myocardial infarction** — Reduces cardiac remodeling after MI

## Effects on Mitochondrial Dynamics

Valsartan's impact on mitochondrial function includes:

- **Mitophagy modulation** — Inhibits excessive mitophagy that can deplete mitochondrial populations
- **Mitochondrial biogenesis** — Increases mitochondrial biogenesis in experimental hypertension
- **Left ventricular hypertrophy** — Alleviates hypertrophy through both hemodynamic and direct cardiac effects
