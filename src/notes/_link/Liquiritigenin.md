# Liquiritigenin

**Liquiritigenin** is a flavanone (4',7-dihydroxyflavanone) found in licorice (*Glycyrrhiza* species), where it exists as the aglycone of liquiritin. It is a selective agonist of the estrogen receptor beta and possesses anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidative, and hepatoprotective activities. As a small lipophilic molecule, it is well suited to intracellular redox modulation.

## SIRT3 activation via Nrf2

The licorice-derived compound liquiritigenin induces [[NRF2|Nrf2]] translocation, thereby potentiating [[SIRT3]] activity and facilitating [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] while simultaneously suppressing [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] in acute kidney injury models[375]. The proposed cascade is: liquiritigenin → nuclear Nrf2 → transcriptional support of SIRT3/antioxidant programs → mitochondrial biogenesis and cytoprotection. This dual action mitigates oxidative stress injury and cell death, highlighting liquiritigenin's therapeutic value in AKI and related redox-driven pathologies.

## Applications

- Research use: experimental acute kidney injury models.
- Clinical context: candidate adjunct for ischemic and nephrotoxic renal injury; traditional use of licorice in herbal medicine.
