# IGF1

**Insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF1, IGF-1, somatomedin C)** is a 70-amino acid polypeptide hormone structurally similar to [[Insulin|insulin]]. It is the principal downstream mediator of [[Human Growth Hormone|growth hormone (GH)]] signaling and plays critical roles in growth, metabolism, and immune function.

## Structure and Signaling

- **Molecular weight:** ~7.6 kDa (70 amino acids)
- **Receptor:** IGF1R (tyrosine kinase)
- **Signaling:** IRS1/2 → PI3K → [[AKT]] → cell survival and growth; Ras → MAPK → proliferation
- **Binding proteins:** IGFBP1-6 modulate IGF1 bioavailability and half-life

## Biological Functions

### Growth and metabolism
- Mediates GH effects on somatic growth
- Promotes cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival
- Regulates glucose and lipid metabolism
- Anti-apoptotic signaling via Bcl-2 family members

### Immune system effects
- Provides anti-apoptotic signals that promote immune cell survival
- Enhances hematopoiesis and immune reconstitution
- Stimulates [[IL-7]] production by [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|thymic stromal cells]]
- Acts as component of thymic microenvironment cross-talk network

## Role in Thymic Rejuvenation

[[Human Growth Hormone|Growth hormone]] impacts the [[Immune System|immune system]] via its stimulatory effects on IGF1:

- Hematopoietic cells can produce GH that indirectly promotes immune reconstitution via IGF1 production
- IGF1 provides anti-apoptotic signals and promotes immune cell survival
- IGF1 acts directly on thymic stromal cells to stimulate IL-7 production
- Intrathymic IL-7 in turn promotes and regulates [[T Lymphocyte|T cell]] survival and function

> [!warning] Longevity considerations
> Chronically elevated GH/IGF1 signaling is associated with increased cancer risk and shortened lifespan. The [[mTOR]]-activating properties of IGF1 contrast with the life-extending effects of [[Rapamycin]]-mediated mTOR inhibition.
