# ADP

**Adenosine diphosphate ([[ADP]])** is the low-energy adenine nucleotide produced when [[ATP]] loses its terminal phosphate to perform cellular work. The ATP/ADP ratio is the central gauge of cellular energy charge, and a rising ADP (or [[AMP]]) concentration is the immediate signal of an energy deficit that initiates mitohormetic adaptations.

## Recycling to ATP

ADP is rephosphorylated to ATP through two principal routes:

- The **phosphocreatine shuttle**: [[Creatine]] accepts a phosphate from ATP (via creatine kinase) to form phosphocreatine; during high-demand moments phosphocreatine rapidly donates the phosphate back to ADP, recycling ATP within milliseconds. This buffers the ATP/ADP ratio during intense effort and protects [[Heart Rate Variability|HRV]] from plunging.
- **Oxidative phosphorylation**: The [[Electron Transport Chain|electron transport chain]] and [[ATP Synthase|ATP synthase]] use the proton-motive force to phosphorylate ADP to ATP in the [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]].

## Role in Mitohormesis

A transient rise in ADP (and AMP) during exercise or fasting signals a cellular energy deficit, activating [[AMPK]], which in turn stimulates [[PGC-1α]] and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]]. Sustaining cellular energy pools with creatine reduces metabolic stress and supports recovery.
