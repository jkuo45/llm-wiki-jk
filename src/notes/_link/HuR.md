# HuR

## Overview

HuR (**ELAVL1**, Hu antigen R) is the founding member of the
**ELAVL** (embryonic lethal abnormal vision-like) family of RNA-binding
proteins, which also includes ELAVL2/HuB, ELAVL3/HuC, and ELAVL4/HuD. HuD is
the paraneoplastic Hu antigen recognised in small-cell lung carcinoma and
neuroblastoma; HuR is the **ubiquitously expressed** member.

HuR's core function is simple and its target set is enormous: it binds
AU-rich elements (AREs) in 3′ UTRs and **stabilises** the mRNAs it binds,
protecting them from deadenylation and exonucleolytic decay. Because AREs are
present in the 3′ UTRs of a large fraction of human transcripts, HuR acts as a
broad rheostat on mRNA stability and hence on proteome composition.

## Structure and domains

Human HuR is 326 residues (UniProt Q15717) with three RNA-recognition motifs
(RRMs):

- **RRM1** (residues ~20–98) — the primary ARE-binding domain. In vitro it
  binds preferentially to 5′-UUUU[AG]UUU-3′.
- **RRM2** (residues ~106–186) — the "HuD-like" RRM2, which cooperates with
  RRM1 in ARE recognition.
- **RRM3** (residues ~244–322) — the *differentially localised* RRM; it
  contributes to poly-A tail interaction and to several protein partners, and
  is the domain required for binding to some 3′ UTR features other than the
  canonical ARE.
- **Hinge region** between RRM2 and RRM3 (residues ~187–243) — a flexible
  linker whose conformation differs between HuR and HuD and contributes to
  both the conformational change on RNA binding and to shuttling behaviour.
- **No catalytic domain.** HuR has no enzymatic activity; it acts purely as a
  RNP component. It functions as a monomer in solution and as a homodimer
  *in vitro* on RNA — dimerisation occurring before RNA binding.

X-ray structures of RRM1/2 in apo and RNA-bound forms show that RRM1 is the
primary ARE-binding domain and that **RNA binding induces conformational
changes** that allow secondary contacts of the RNA with the interdomain linker
and RRM2, markedly increasing affinity.

## Mechanism of action

> [!info] ARE recognition and shuttling
> HuR binds poly-U elements and AU-rich elements in 3′ UTRs; it binds avidly to
> the AREs of *FOS* (a 27-nt core containing AUUUA, AUUUUA, AUUUUUA motifs)
> and *IL3*. Binding occludes the ARE from the deadenylase machinery,
> slowing poly(A) shortening and thus exonucleolytic decay.

- **Shuttling.** HuR is constitutively nuclear and nucleocytoplasmic.
  Phosphorylation by **MK2 (MAPKAPK2)** and by **PKCδ (PRKCD)** promotes
  translocation from nucleus to cytoplasm, where HuR associates with free and
  cytoskeleton-bound polysomes. HuR also localises to **stress granules** and
  to **P-bodies**.
- **m6A coupling.** HuR preferentially binds mRNAs that are *not*
  N6-methyladenosine-modified, stabilising them and thereby promoting embryonic
  stem cell differentiation. It can also bind m6A-containing mRNAs and
  contribute to *MYC* mRNA stability. In bladder cancer, HuR stabilises
  hypermethylated m5C-modified oncogene transcripts in partnership with the
  m5C reader YBX1, whose cold-shock domain W65 indole ring recognises m5C and
  whose binding recruits HuR. HuR also cooperates with the m6A readers
  IGF2BP1–3 (the IGF2BP interaction being enhanced by a small regulatory
  peptide), with ILF3/NF90, HNRNPL, HNRNPU, PCBP2, PTBP2, STAU1/2, SYNCRIP,
  and YBX1 in large mRNP complexes, and with AGO1/AGO2.
- **Interactors.** Known partners include ANP32A, ZNF385A (in an
  mRNA-dependent complex that controls *TP53* and *CCNB1* mRNA nuclear export),
  DHX9, DDX3X, TARDBP, SDCBP, PLEKHN1 (which recruits HuR to stress granules),
  SHFL, and FXR1. HuR can be arginine-methylated at Arg217 by **CARM1/PRMT4**
  in macrophages after LPS challenge.

## Physiological roles

- **Response to temperature.** Because ARE-bearing transcripts include mRNAs
  for heat-shock proteins, HuR links ambient temperature to proteome
  composition: warmth shifts HuR toward cytosolic stabilisation of ARE mRNAs.
  This is part of the rationale for cold exposure and temperature-based
  longevity speculation.
- **Inflammation and immune signalling.** HuR stabilises mRNAs encoding
  cytokines and adhesion molecules. Small-molecule HuR inhibitors (MS-444,
  dehydromutactin, okicenone) reduce cytokine expression and T-cell
  activation — the first demonstration that HuR is chemically "drugable."
- **Metabolism.** Hepatic HuR modulates glucose metabolism through the
  C/EBPβ/PCK1 axis.
- **Apoptosis and DNA repair.** HuR stabilises mRNAs of anti-apoptotic genes;
  in response to oxidative DNA damage HuR is phosphorylated by Chk2 and
  dissociates from *SIRT1* mRNA, lowering SIRT1 and tipping the balance from
  senescence toward apoptosis.

## Pathology

> [!important] HuR is stabilised in cancer and in ageing
> - **Cancer.** HuR (ELAVL1) overexpression stabilises oncogenic, cytokine,
>   and anti-apoptotic transcripts and is associated with poor prognosis in
>   many tumours; it functionally cooperates with BRAF-driven programmes.
>   Degrading HuR therefore exposes the dependencies of BRAF-driven cancers.
>   HuR also cooperates with TTP/ZFP36 as a post-transcriptional regulatory
>   node, and with YBX1 in m5C-driven urothelial carcinoma.
> - **Senescence and ageing.** HuR is a *suppressor* of senescence. It binds
>   and stabilises the mRNAs of proliferative genes — *c-Fos*, Cyclin A,
>   Cyclin B — and also the *SIRT1* mRNA (via a 3′UTR HuR binding site).
>   [[AMPK]] activation during senescence prevents HuR translocation from
>   nucleus to cytosol, reducing translation of those proliferative genes and
>   assisting cell-cycle arrest. HuR levels fall dramatically during
>   senescence, which is also proposed as a cause of the age-related decline
>   in SIRT1 protein. Because platelets are a major HuR reservoir, HuR has
>   been proposed as a systemic regulator of ageing through platelet
>   infiltration.
> - **Neurodegeneration.** ELAVL proteins including HuR are implicated in
>   mRNA fate in brain ageing and neurodegeneration, and in ALS network
>   analyses.

## Connections

- [[Cellular Senescence]] — HuR stabilises proliferative mRNAs and *SIRT1* mRNA; its cytosolic withdrawal on AMPK activation, and its decline with senescence, are part of the mechanism by which senescence suppresses proliferation.
- [[AMPK]] — activated AMPK prevents HuR nuclear-to-cytosol translocation, which is a direct mechanistic route from energy stress to reduced translation of HuR-stabilised proliferative mRNAs.
- [[SIRT1]] — HuR binds the *SIRT1* 3′UTR and stabilises it; Chk2-mediated phosphorylation dissociates HuR from *SIRT1* mRNA after oxidative DNA damage. Because SIRT1 activity falls with age, HuR decline contributes to the age-related loss of SIRT1 protein.
- [[Cell Cycle]] — HuR stabilises Cyclin A and Cyclin B mRNAs, making it a direct post-transcriptional supporter of cell-cycle progression.
- [[MYC]] — HuR contributes to MYC mRNA stability by binding m6A-containing transcripts, linking HuR to the core proliferation programme.
- [[SASP]] — HuR stabilises cytokine mRNAs, so it indirectly supports the inflammatory secretory programme; where SASP components are ARE-bearing transcripts, HuR contributes to their persistence.
- [[IGF1R]] — not a direct target, but IGF2BP1 is HuR's m6A-reading partner; the IGF2BP axis and the HuR axis converge on stabilising the same oncogenic transcripts.
- [[Heat Shock Proteins]] — ARE-bearing HSP mRNAs are HuR targets, which is the molecular basis for HuR's temperature-sensing role.
- [[DNA Repair]] — Chk2 phosphorylation of HuR in response to oxidative DNA damage is the switch that couples DNA damage to reduced SIRT1 and a shift from senescence to apoptosis.
