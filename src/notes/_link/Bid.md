# Bid

**Bid** (BH3 interacting-domain death agonist) is a pro-apoptotic BH3-only member of the [[Bcl-2 family]] of proteins. It serves as a critical link between the [[Extrinsic Pathway]] (death receptor) and the [[Intrinsic Pathway]] (mitochondrial) of [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]].

## Structure and Activation

Bid is a 22 kDa protein that exists in an inactive cytosolic conformation. Upon cleavage by [[Caspase-8]] (downstream of death receptor engagement), the N-terminal fragment (tBid, truncated Bid) translocates to the mitochondria, where it activates [[Bax]] and [[Bak]] to trigger mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization ([[MOMP]]), release of [[Cytochrome c]], and activation of the intrinsic pathway.

> [!important] Bid as an Apoptosis Amplifier
> tBid (truncated Bid) is a potent activator of [[Bax]], directly triggering Bax conformational change and oligomerization at the mitochondrial outer membrane. This mechanism amplifies apoptotic signals from death receptors by engaging the mitochondrial apoptotic machinery, allowing the extrinsic signal to be amplified through the mitochondrial loop.

## Regulation

Bid activity is regulated by:
- **[[Caspase-8]] cleavage**: The primary activating event, converting full-length Bid to tBid.
- **[[Caspase-2]] and Caspase-10**: Also capable of activating Bid by proteolytic cleavage.
- **[[Humanin]]**: Directly binds and nullifies tBid activity, blocking its ability to activate [[Bax]] and [[Bak]].
- **[[Phosphorylation]]**: JNK-mediated phosphorylation at Ser61 and Ser78 enhances Bid pro-apoptotic activity; conversely, [[CK1]] / [[CK2]] phosphorylation at Thr58, Ser61, Ser64 inhibits its cleavage by Caspase-8, blocking pro-apoptotic function.
- **Deamidation**: Asparagine deamidation at positions 60 and 85 activates Bid independently of caspase cleavage.

## Biological Significance

Bid is essential for:
- Amplification of death receptor-mediated apoptosis
- Developmental tissue remodeling
- Immune system homeostasis (Bid knockout mice show lymphocyte accumulation)
- Chemotherapy-induced tumor cell death
