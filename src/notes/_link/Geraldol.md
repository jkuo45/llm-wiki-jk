# Geraldol

**Geraldol** (3'-O-methylfisetin) is a naturally occurring flavonoid and an O-methylated derivative of [[Fisetin]]. Like its parent, it belongs to the flavonol subclass and retains the core 3-hydroxyflavone scaffold responsible for radical-scavenging and metal-chelating activity.

Geraldol is studied for [[Senescence]]-targeted (senolytic) and antioxidant effects. It counters oxidative stress through direct free-radical quenching and by supporting the [[Glutathione]] system, helping neutralize electrophilic quinones such as [[Aminochrome]] and [[Adrenochrome]] derivatives. Its methylated 3'-hydroxyl improves lipophilicity and cellular uptake relative to fisetin, potentially enhancing access to redox-stressed compartments. By dampening ROS and [[NF-κB]]-driven signaling, geraldol can suppress the SASP and protect against catecholamine-derived oxidative damage. These properties position it as a candidate co-agent in adrenochrome-handling protocols aimed at limiting redox-driven senescence.

> [!tip] Geraldol's value in the adrenochrome context lies in combining fisetin-like senolytic activity with improved membrane permeability from 3'-O-methylation.

## Formation by COMT

> [!info] Source: [[task_output_comt_fisetin_senolytic_09_July_2026|COMT × Fisetin Research Evaluation]]
> Geraldol is generated by [[COMT]]-mediated O-methylation of [[Fisetin]] at the 3′-hydroxyl position. This was directly demonstrated by Poor et al. (2016, Biomed Pharmacother 83:998–1005). The process is concentration-dependent: at low fisetin concentrations, COMT primarily methylates fisetin to geraldol (substrate behavior); at higher concentrations, fisetin occupies the COMT active site without being efficiently methylated (inhibitor behavior). Geraldol formation consumes [[SAMe]] and generates [[SAH]], potentially affecting global methylation capacity with chronic fisetin supplementation.

Geraldol has been detected in plasma at levels exceeding free fisetin, suggesting it may be a major circulating metabolite. However, its pharmacology is **essentially uncharacterized** — no published data exists on its COMT-inhibitory potency, senolytic activity (Bcl-2/Bcl-xL binding), or receptor binding profile. This represents a critical literature gap, as geraldol may contribute significantly to the biological effects attributed to fisetin.
