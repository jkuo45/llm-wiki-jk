# TIR domain

The **TIR domain** (Toll/IL-1 Receptor domain) is the C-terminal catalytic region of [[SARM1]] and the founding structural motif of the TIR-domain-containing protein family.

## Structure and Catalysis

In the autoinhibited state, the TIR domain is sterically blocked by the N-terminal ARM repeats. Upon injury-triggered release, TIR domains dimerize across adjacent SARM1 protomers to form the composite [[NAD+]] glycohydrolase active site. A glutamate residue is essential for catalysis. Structural studies (cryo-EM, X-ray) have resolved the NAD⁺-binding pocket targeted by inhibitors.

## Inhibitor Binding

The TIR dimer NAD⁺-binding pocket is the site of action for SARM1 inhibitors: [[Disulfiram]] covalently modifies a TIR cysteine; [[DSRM-3716]] and [[GSK-428]] occupy the pocket non-covalently. The [[CD38]] inhibitor 78c shares the conserved adduct-forming uncompetitive mechanism (Bratkowski et al. 2022, _Neuron_).

## Additional Signaling

Beyond NADase activity, the TIR domain scaffolds [[TRAF6]] to activate [[JNK]] and [[p38 MAPK]], and negatively regulates [[TLR3]]/[[TLR4]] innate immune signaling.
