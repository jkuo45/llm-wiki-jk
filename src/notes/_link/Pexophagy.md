# Pexophagy

**Pexophagy** is the selective form of [[Autophagy]] that targets [[Peroxisomes]] for degradation in the [[Lysosome]] (or vacuole in yeast). It is the principal mechanism by which cells adjust peroxisome number and eliminate damaged or superfluous peroxisomes, safeguarding organelle quality control and redox balance.

## Mechanism

- **Receptor-mediated recognition:** in mammals, [[NCOA4]]-independent adaptors [[p62]]/SQSTM1 and NBR1 recognize ubiquitinated peroxisomal membrane proteins (e.g. PEX5, PMP70) and bridge them to [[LC3]] on the forming autophagosome.
- **Ubiquitin signal:** stress or import failure triggers mono/poly-ubiquitination of peroxisomal proteins such as [[PEX5]], marking the organelle for turnover.
- **Core machinery:** the isolation membrane engulfs the tagged peroxisome, forming a pexophagosome that fuses with the lysosome for hydrolytic degradation. In yeast, Atg30 (*P. pastoris*) and Atg36 (*S. cerevisiae*) serve as pexophagy receptors.

## Physiological Importance & Dysregulation

> [!info] Redox and metabolic homeostasis
> Peroxisomes handle very-long-chain fatty-acid β-oxidation and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] metabolism, so pexophagy is central to controlling peroxisomal [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and lipid metabolism. Impaired pexophagy allows accumulation of dysfunctional, ROS-generating peroxisomes, contributing to [[Oxidative Stress]], aging, metabolic disease, and neurodegeneration; excessive pexophagy contributes to peroxisome-biogenesis disorders.
