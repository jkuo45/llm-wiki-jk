# Kinase Inhibitor

**Kinase inhibitors** are small-molecule drugs that block the ATP-binding or allosteric sites of protein kinases, interrupting phosphorylation-driven signaling. With ~94 FDA-approved agents (10 newly approved in 2025; ~200 in clinical trials), they are a powerful, rapidly expanding targeted-therapy class [^1].

## Hormesis in Kinase Inhibitors

Because kinases sit inside densely wired signaling networks, their inhibition can produce [[Biphasic Dose-Response Curve|non-monotonic (hormetic) dose responses]] rather than the expected monotonic sigmoidal curve with a clean [[IC50]]. The network-topology basis—an [[Incoherent Bivalent Motif]] centered on the target kinase, often with a [[Saturated Enzymatic Regime]] backward link—explains why inhibitors such as [[Rapamycin]] (mTOR), and potentially many others, show inverted-U efficacy [^1].
