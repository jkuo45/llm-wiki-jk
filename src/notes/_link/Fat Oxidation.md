# Fat Oxidation

**Fat Oxidation** is the breakdown of [[Fatty acid|fatty acids]] to [[Acetyl-CoA]] via β-oxidation in the mitochondrial matrix, with acetyl-CoA entering the [[TCA cycle]] and [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain|electron transport chain]] to generate [[ATP]]. It is the dominant fuel during low-intensity, prolonged exercise and in the post-absorptive state.

## Regulation

- **Intensity dependence**: low-to-moderate exercise intensity favours fat oxidation; high intensity shifts toward carbohydrate oxidation.
- **Post-exercise**: the prolonged phase of [[Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption|EPOC]] (> 1 h) involves a switch from carbohydrate toward fat metabolism (Borsheim _et al_. 1998), sustaining elevated oxygen consumption during recovery.
- **Hormonal**: β-adrenergic stimulation and insulin suppression promote lipolysis and fat oxidation.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review notes that longer-term EPOC (> 1 h) may be driven by a shift from carbohydrate to fat metabolism, which contributes to the prolonged post-exercise elevation of resting metabolism.

## Clinical Relevance

Fat oxidation capacity reflects metabolic fitness and is reduced in obesity, [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|type 2 diabetes]], and metabolic syndrome. Exercise training improves muscle fat-oxidative capacity (in part via mitochondrial biogenesis and aerobic-enzyme induction), supporting weight management and metabolic health.
