# PLA

**Poly(lactic acid) (PLA)** is a biodegradable, aliphatic polyester produced by the ring-opening polymerization of lactide, the cyclic dimer of lactic acid. Derived from renewable feedstocks, it is compostable, biocompatible, and hydrolytically degrades in vivo to lactic acid, which enters normal metabolism — properties that have earned it regulatory approval in devices such as resorbable sutures and implants.

## Use in Drug Delivery

In [[Nanotechnology|nanomedicine]], PLA is a workhorse matrix for [[Nanoparticles]] and microspheres enabling sustained, targeted release of poorly soluble drugs. In the fisetin context, fisetin-loaded PLA [[Nanoparticles|nanoparticles]] prepared by spontaneous emulsification solvent diffusion (≈226.85 nm; 90.35% entrapment efficiency) increased the drug's AUC 2.32-fold and reduced tumor volume to 64.7% in a 4T1 breast-cancer model (Feng et al., 2019), directly improving [[Bioavailability]] and anticancer efficacy.

## Material Variants

Blending or copolymerizing PLA with other monomers tunes degradation kinetics and drug release. The copolymer [[PLGA]] (PLA with glycolide) is the most widely used injectable depot system; related carriers include [[MPEG-PCL]] and [[PVP]]-stabilized formulations, while [[Liposomes]] and [[Solid Lipid Nanoparticles]] offer lipid-based alternatives.

## Broader Relevance

Beyond delivery, PLA is used in tissue-engineering scaffolds, 3D-printed constructs, and food packaging, making it a central material in sustainable and biomedical polymer science.
