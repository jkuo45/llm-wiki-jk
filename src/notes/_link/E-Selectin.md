# E-Selectin

**E-Selectin** is one of three members of the selectin family of calcium-dependent
carbohydrate-binding adhesion molecules (E-, L-, and P-selectin). It is encoded by
the *SELE* gene and is named for endothelium, where it is predominantly expressed.
Where P-selectin is stored in granules and mobilised within minutes, E-selectin
requires new gene transcription, making it the **slow inducer** of the leukocyte
adhesion cascade and the most specific marker of sustained endothelial
inflammation.

> [!info] Where it sits in the adhesion cascade
> Leukocyte recruitment proceeds in an ordered sequence: **rolling** (selectins),
> **firm adhesion** (integrins binding [[ICAM-1]] and VCAM-1), and finally
> **diapedesis** through the endothelium. E-selectin initiates the first, weakest
> step. Because it requires transcription, its sustained presence marks an
> endothelium that has been activated for hours rather than minutes — the reason
> it is used as a biomarker while P-selectin is not.

## Structure & Ligands

E-selectin is a type I transmembrane glycoprotein of roughly 610 residues carrying
an N-terminal C-type lectin domain, an epidermal growth factor (EGF)-like domain,
and a series of complement control protein (CCP) repeats that together form the
scavenger receptor cysteine-rich domain binding the lectin head to its
carbohydrate ligand.

E-selectin does not bind protein backbones. It recognises **sialofucosylated
glycans** — specifically the sialyl Lewis X (sLe^x, CD15s) motif — displayed on
PSGL-1 (P-selectin glycoprotein ligand-1) on leukocytes, and on
glycoprotein ligands such as ESL-1 and CD44 on other cells.

> [!warning] Glycan-dependent binding has a therapeutic consequence
> Because the interaction requires a specific carbohydrate rather than a protein
> epitope, it is far harder to block with a conventional antibody or small
> molecule. Therapeutics in this space have instead relied on glycomimetic small
> molecules and on antibodies against E-selectin itself, and on the upstream
> transcription factor [[NF-κB]] that drives *SELE* expression.

## Regulation & Expression

Basal expression is low. Induction is driven by inflammatory cytokines —
particularly [[TNFα]] and [[IL-1β]], acting through [[NF-κB]] — with
synergistic potentiation between the two. Hypoxia and [[Oxidative Stress]] also
raise surface E-selectin. Expression persists for roughly 24–48 hours after the
stimulus is removed, which is why plasma E-selectin stays elevated well after
clinical symptoms have settled.

Transcriptional control also depends on the cofactor p300/CBP and on the
endothelial transcription factor family that includes KLF2, whose activity is
itself modulated by shear stress.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Endothelial dysfunction.** Elevated soluble E-selectin accompanies reduced
  flow-mediated dilation, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, and subclinical
  heart failure. Like soluble [[ICAM-1]] and VCAM-1, it is an NF-κB-driven
  marker of a dysfunctional endothelium and is used in
  [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] panels alongside vWF and ADMA.
- **Ischemia–reperfusion injury.** Reperfusion after a hypoxic interval
  re-expresses E-selectin on the reperfused endothelium alongside
  P-selectin and ICAM-1, driving the neutrophil influx and protease release
  that damage tissue — see [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]].
- **Atherosclerosis and atherosclerosis regression.** E-selectin is among the
  earliest endothelial markers to appear at human atherosclerotic lesions, and
  soluble levels track plaque burden and, in some cohorts, regression on
  statin therapy.
- **Cancer.** Tumour endothelium over-expresses E-selectin, and E-selectin
  ligands on circulating tumour cells mediate vascular adhesion and transit.
  Selectin blockade reduces experimental metastasis in mouse models; no selectin
  inhibitor has yet been approved for oncology.
- **Cerebrovascular disease and the eye.** E-selectin is implicated in
  leukocyte plugging in cerebral small-vessel disease and in the diabetic
  retinopathy phenotype of the [[Retinal Pigment Epithelium]], where impaired
  clearance and chronic low-grade inflammation sustain a pro-angiogenic state.

## Connections
- [[Endothelial Dysfunction]] — soluble E-selectin is one of the standard
  circulating markers of an activated, NF-κB-driven endothelium, alongside
  soluble ICAM-1, VCAM-1, von Willebrand factor and ADMA. Reduced flow-mediated
  dilation together with raised E-selectin is the classic non-invasive signature
  of preclinical vascular disease.
- [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]] — E-selectin is re-induced on reperfused
  endothelium and drives the delayed neutrophil influx that sustains reperfusion
  damage long after oxidative burst has peaked. Blocking selectin-dependent
  rolling attenuates the secondary inflammatory wave.
- [[ICAM-1]] — selectins act in sequence with integrins: E-selectin mediates
  weak, transient rolling contacts, and integrin–ICAM-1 binding converts these
  into stable firm adhesion before transendothelial migration. Neither molecule
  alone is sufficient for leukocyte extravasation.
- [[NF-κB]] — NF-κB is the master transcriptional activator of *SELE* downstream
  of TNF-α and IL-1β, and is the mechanistic reason E-selectin is used as a
  readout of inflammatory signalling rather than simply of vasoconstriction.
- [[MHC Class Ib]] — E-selectin-bearing endothelium and non-classical MHC
  molecules such as HLA-E sit on the same activated endothelial surface, where
  they together shape both leukocyte recruitment and immune evasion. This overlap
  is why senescent and inflamed endothelium is a recurring theme in immune
  evasion.
