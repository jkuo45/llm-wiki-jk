# Coenzyme A

**Coenzyme A** (CoA-SH) is a 3'-phosphoadenosine diphosphate-linked thiol
that exists almost entirely in its free, reduced form inside the cell. Its
functional group is a terminal cysteine thiol on a pantetheine arm, and the
entire biological role of the molecule follows from that single reactive
sulfhydryl: it makes activated acyl groups soluble, non-toxic, and
water-transferable.

## The thioester handle

The bond CoA forms with an acyl group is a **thioester**, not an ordinary
ester. Thioesters are high-energy because the resonance stabilisation that
spreads the carbonyl over the adjacent oxygen is unavailable when the
heteroatom is sulfur. The thioester bond is therefore roughly one order of
magnitude more hydrolysable than an oxygen ester, which is exactly what makes
CoA a carrier of chemical energy rather than a stable structural group.

> [!info] Reaction families requiring CoA
> - **Carbon entry into metabolism:** pyruvate dehydrogenase makes
>   [[Acetyl-CoA]]; [[Citrate Synthase]] and [[HMG-CoA reductase]] use it.
> - **Biosynthesis:** [[Fatty Acid Synthase]] carries malonyl- and acetyl-
>   intermediates on CoA; sterol and bile-acid synthesis run entirely on
>   acyl-CoA species; the [[Mevalonate pathway]] produces the CoA-linked
>   building blocks.
> - **Catabolism:** β-oxidation feeds [[Acetyl-CoA]] back to the
>   [[Citric Acid Cycle|TCA cycle]]; ketone body synthesis in
>   [[Ketogenesis]] consumes it; amino acid catabolism funnels through
>   succinyl-CoA, acetoacetyl-CoA, and propionyl-CoA.
> - **Detoxification and activation:** conjugation of organic acids and
>   xenobiotics, N-acetylation, and activation of fatty acids for
>   mitochondrial import all depend on it.

Because CoA participates in essentially all of these at once, the size of the
cellular CoA pool is rate-limiting for oxidation and for synthesis
simultaneously, and the CoA/acetyl-CoA ratio is a commonly used readout of
mitochondrial redox state. In the [[GlyNAC]] context, glycine and
[[N-Acetylcysteine]] support synthesis of the precursors — pantothenate from
calcium pantothenate, itself from [[Beta-Alanine]] and pantothenic acid — and
the resulting CoA pool influences the [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] and
[[Autophagy]] programs that GSH-derived reducing power is meant to protect.

## Biosynthesis and recycling

De novo synthesis is five steps: pantothenate kinase phosphorylates
pantothenic acid (vitamin B5); 4'-phosphopantothenoylcysteine synthetase
couples it to cysteine; phosphopantothenoylcysteine decarboxylase and
phosphopantothenoylcysteine synthase complete the pantetheine; and
pantothenate synthetase closes the ring onto adenosine 3',5'-bisphosphate.
The committed, rate-limiting step is pantothenate kinase.

> [!info] The CoA degradation-recycling cycle
> CoA turnover is fast — the CoA pool in rat liver turns over roughly 10% per
> day — and degradation proceeds by stepwise dephosphorylation to
> 4'-phosphopantetheine, then to cysteamine, which is re-entered into
> pantetheine synthesis. This cyclic flux is a target in inflammatory disease:
> the cytokine TNFα downregulates pantothenate kinase 2, limiting CoA
> availability, and pharmacological pantothenate kinase activation restores
> CoA pools and reduces inflammatory readouts in rodent models of
> inflammatory bowel disease. The human evidence is early.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> CoA itself is not an established therapy; the drug in clinical use is
> **pantothenic acid** or its derivatives, and pantothenate supplementation
> has a much weaker effect on tissue CoA than the biochemistry would predict
> because pantothenate kinase operates near saturation. Claims about
> pantothenate treating diabetic neuropathy or dyslipidaemia are not supported
> by adequate trials. The zebrafish and rodent lifespan data on
> pantothenate supplementation are interesting but have not translated.
> Separately, acyl-CoA accumulation in the acyl-CoA pool of
> phosphatidylcholine and diacylglycerol is a genuine driver of
> lipotoxicity and [[Insulin Resistance]], and it is distinct from the free
> CoA pool discussed here — the two are not interchangeable in a metabolic
> reading.

## Connections

- [[Acetyl-CoA]] — the thioester product of pyruvate dehydrogenase and the
  single most abundant acyl-CoA in the cell; it is both the entry point for
  [[Fatty Acid Synthase]] and the TCA cycle and the direct donor of the acetyl
  group used in histone acetylation, so CoA pool size feeds epigenetic
  acetylation capacity.
- [[Citrate Synthase]] — condenses acetyl-CoA with oxaloacetate, the first
  committed step of the TCA cycle; irreversible, and therefore the point at
  which CoA-derived carbon enters the cycle.
- [[HMG-CoA reductase]] — the committed, rate-limiting step of the
  [[Mevalonate pathway]] and the target of [[Statins]], which is why statin
  efficacy tracks the hepatic HMG-CoA/CoA ratio.
- [[GlyNAC]] — the vault's note asserting that glycine and
  N-acetylcysteine raise GSH and, in parallel, support CoA and bile-acid
  precursors. The CoA claim here is biosynthetic (vitamin B5 availability),
  not a direct pharmacological effect of GSH on CoA.
- [[Ketogenesis]] — mitochondrial ketogenesis drains acetyl-CoA away from the
  TCA cycle into acetoacetyl-CoA and acetoacetate, which is the reason
  [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]] impairs ketogenesis while the
  [[Malonyl-CoA]]-to-carnitine shuttle blocks β-oxidation.
- [[Methylmalonyl-CoA]] — cobalamin-dependent mutase converts
  methylmalonyl-CoA to succinyl-CoA, making the CoA pool the substrate of the
  [[Cobalamin|B12]]-dependent step of odd-chain fatty acid and
  branched-chain amino acid catabolism.
- [[Autophagy]] — autophagy requires fatty acid release from
  phosphatidylcholine and cardiolipin, both acyl-CoA-dependent; CoA depletion
  couples nutrient scarcity to autophagic flux.
