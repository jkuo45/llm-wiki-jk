## Chemotherapy-Induced Muscle Wasting

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Chemotherapy NAD+ Loss Pöllänen 2026]]
> [[Chemotherapy]] alone — independent of tumor burden — can disrupt [[NAD+]] metabolism in skeletal muscle, paralleling findings in cancer [[Cachexia|cachexia]]. The NAD+ biosynthetic enzyme [[NRK2|Nrk2]] is an early, responsive marker of muscle mass changes.

### NAD+ Metabolism in Skeletal Muscle

- In skeletal muscle, NAD+ is primarily generated via the salvage pathway through [[NAMPT]]
- [[NRK2|Nrk2]] is a muscle-specific enzyme that provides a stress-responsive supplementary route for NAD+ biosynthesis
- Chemotherapy-induced muscle NAD+ loss is primarily driven by impaired biosynthesis rather than increased consumption (via [[PARP]] enzymes)
- Cisplatin (−14% NAD+) and prolonged FOLFIRI (−18% NAD+) reduce muscle NAD+ levels accompanying atrophy

### Nrk2 as a Biomarker of Muscle Mass

- [[NRK2|Nrk2]] expression dynamically tracks changes in muscle mass across wasting and hypertrophy conditions
- Consistently downregulated in cachexia across murine models and human studies
- Restored by cachexia-targeted interventions (activin receptor ligand blockade, IL-6 deficiency, FoxO inhibition)
- Downregulated by inactivity (bed rest, limb immobilization) and upregulated by resistance training
