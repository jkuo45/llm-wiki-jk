# Non-homologous End Joining

## Overview

Non-homologous End Joining (NHEJ) is the principal [[DNA Repair|DNA double-strand break (DSB)]] repair pathway in mammalian cells, ligating broken DNA ends directly without requiring a homologous template. It operates throughout the cell cycle but dominates in G0/G1 and in post-mitotic, terminally differentiated cells. NHEJ errors (small insertions/deletions) contribute to genome instability when dysregulated, while its restoration preserves genomic integrity and suppresses senescence.

## Key Players

- **Damage sensing:** [[Ku70|Ku70/80]] heterodimer binds DSB ends and recruits the DNA-PK catalytic subunit.
- **End processing:** [[PARP1]], [[XRCC1]], and DNA Ligase IV (with XRCC4) coordinate end trimming and ligation; [[53BP1]] promotes NHEJ by opposing homologous recombination at damaged chromatin.
- **Chromatin context:** NHEJ efficiency is modulated by histone modifications such as [[H3K56ac|H3K56 acetylation/deacetylation]]; [[SIRT3]] facilitates DSB repair by enhancing 53BP1 recruitment and thus NHEJ efficiency, and its knockdown compromises NHEJ fidelity during oncogenic transformation.

## SIRT3 Connection

- SIRT3 promotes NHEJ via 53BP1 recruitment, countering heterochromatin silencing defects that otherwise impair repair.
- SIRT3-mediated H3K56 deacetylation enhances NHEJ fidelity, positioning SIRT3 as a genomic sentinel in aging tissues prone to malignant transformation.
- Loss of SIRT3 impairs the chromatin-silencing machinery, leading to altered transcriptional landscapes and compromised chromatin homeostasis.
