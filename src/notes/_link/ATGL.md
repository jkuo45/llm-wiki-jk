# ATGL

**ATGL** (adipose triglyceride lipase, encoded by *PNPLA2*) is the first and rate-limiting enzyme of catabolic lipolysis, hydrolyzing triacylglycerol in [[Lipid Droplet|lipid droplets]] to diacylglycerol and free fatty acids. Its activity determines the rate of free fatty acid release from adipose tissue and therefore systemic lipid mobilization.

## Function and Regulation

- **Cytosolic Lipolysis**: ATGL localizes to the surface of lipid droplets and hydrolyzes triglycerides into diacylglycerols and free fatty acids, providing substrate for [[Lipophagy]] and fatty acid oxidation.
- **Nutrient Status**: Activated during [[Starvation]] and [[Fasting]] to mobilize energy reserves.
- **Independence from Lipophagy**: In [[C. elegans]], ATGL (encoded by *atgl-1*) acts in parallel to the lipophagy pathway involving [[LIPL-1]] and [[LIPL-3]].

## Regulation by Sirtuins

- [[SIRT1]] promotes lipolysis in white adipocytes partly through FOXO1-dependent induction of ATGL expression, enhancing triglyceride hydrolysis.
- [[SIRT6]] promotes adipose lipolysis and prevents ectopic lipid accumulation through FOXO1-dependent induction of ATGL, protecting against lipotoxic insulin resistance.
- Together with [[HSL]] (hormone-sensitive lipase) and [[Monoacylglycerol Lipase|MGL]] (monoacylglycerol lipase), ATGL governs the stepwise mobilization of stored fat used for energy and thermogenesis.
