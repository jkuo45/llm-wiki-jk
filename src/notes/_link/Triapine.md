# Triapine

**Triapine** (**3-AP**, 3-aminopyridine-2-carboxaldehyde thiosemicarbazone) is an investigational α-(N)-heterocyclic **thiosemicarbazone** iron chelator developed as a ribonucleotide-reductase inhibitor. It remains an Investigational New Drug (IND) — never FDA-approved — and has been evaluated in 35+ clinical trials across solid tumors and leukemias, most frequently as a chemosensitizer or radiosensitizer. Its relevance to this vault: Triapine directly poisons the radical cofactor of [[RRM2]] (and [[RRM2B]]), starving replication and repair of [[dNTP]] supply.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- Small-molecule α-N-heterocyclic thiosemicarbazone (N⁴-substituted; parent 3-AP), NSC 663249.
- Potent **tridentate iron (Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺) and copper chelator**; the metal-complexed form also redox-cycles to generate [[Reactive Oxygen Species]].
- Orally- and IV-investigated; intravenous dosing dominates the clinical schedule.

## Mechanism of Action

- **Ribonucleotide reductase inhibition**: chelates the iron atoms of the binuclear center in the [[RRM2]]/[[RRM2B]] small subunit of [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]], destabilizing and quenching the tyrosyl-radical cofactor. RNR is inactivated → conversion of NDPs to dNDPs ceases → cellular [[dNTP]] pools collapse → replicative [[DNA Replication Stress]] and death of proliferating cells.
- **Radiosensitization/chemosensitization**: by depleting dNTPs and impairing DNA-repair capacity, Triapine synergizes with ionizing radiation and genotoxic agents (cisplatin, gemcitabine), enhancing strand-break accumulation.
- **ROS generation**: iron-complex redox cycling augments oxidative damage, contributing to antitumor activity in already redox-stressed tumor cells.

## Clinical Development

- **Solid tumors**: phase I–II trials in pancreatic adenocarcinoma, non-small-cell lung cancer (with gemcitabine), cervical/vaginal/gynecologic cancers (pelvic radiation ± weekly cisplatin), glioblastoma, and neuroendocrine tumors (with ¹⁷⁷Lu-DOTATATE; a 2025/2026 *Clin Cancer Res* report of a multicenter phase 1 ETCTN trial). A phase 2 trial was initiated as recently as 2024.
- **Hematologic malignancies**: studied in leukemias/adenocarcinoma contexts, often combined with nucleoside analogs.
- **Toxicities**: dose-limiting **myelosuppression** and [[Methemoglobinemia|methemoglobinemia]]; fatigue, GI effects. The narrow therapeutic window has slowed development but continues to motivate combination strategies (e.g., 2026 dose-optimization reports).
