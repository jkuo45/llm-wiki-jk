# Mitochondrial-Derived Peptide

[[Mitochondrial-Derived Peptide]] (MDP) refers to a family of small bioactive peptides encoded within the [[Mitochondrial DNA|mitochondrial genome]] that are transcribed and translated independently of the canonical 13 mitochondrial-encoded proteins. These peptides function as both intracellular survival factors and endocrine signaling molecules, representing a novel layer of mitochondrial communication with the cell and distant tissues.

## Paradigm Shift

> [!important] From End-Function Organelle to Signaling Hub
> The discovery of MDPs fundamentally changed the understanding of [[Mitochondria]] from passive "end-function" organelles that merely produce ATP and regulate [[Apoptosis]] to active signaling centers that communicate metabolic status to the nucleus and to distant tissues. MDPs are unique among [[Retrograde Signaling|retrograde signals]] because they are encoded within the mitochondrial genome itself, unlike other retrograde signals (Ca²⁺, [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], [[Cytochrome c]]) that are secondary products of mitochondrial metabolism.

## Known MDPs

The MDP family includes:

| Peptide | Encoding Locus | Length | Key Functions |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| [[Humanin]] | MT-RNR2 (16S rRNA) | 24 aa | Neuroprotection, anti-apoptosis, metabolic regulation |
| [[MOTS-c]] | MT-RNR1 (12S rRNA) | 16 aa | Glucose metabolism, exercise response, longevity |
| SHLP1-6 | MT-RNR2 (16S rRNA) | Various | Cytokine-like functions, tissue-specific effects |
| [[Humanin-Like Peptides]] | Nuclear pseudogenes | Various | Similar to humanin, derived from NUMTs |

## Molecular Mechanisms

MDPs exert their effects through multiple mechanisms:

### Intracellular Actions
- Direct protein-protein interactions (e.g., [[Humanin]] binding [[Bax]], [[Bid]], [[IGFBP3]])
- Mitochondrial function regulation
- Anti-apoptotic signaling

### Endocrine Actions
- Secretion into circulation
- Receptor-mediated signaling at distant tissues:
  - CNTFR/WSX-1/[[gp130]] complex → [[STAT3]] activation
  - [[FPRL1]] (FPR2) → [[ERK]]1/2 activation
  - [[Insulin-Like Growth Factor 1 Receptor|IGF1R]] → [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] signaling

## Regulation of MDP Expression

MDP expression is regulated by:
- **Mitochondrial stress**: [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]], Complex I inhibition
- **Nutrient sensing**: Upregulated by [[NAD+]]/[[SIRT1]] and [[AMPK]]; suppressed by [[mTORC1]]
- **Aging**: MDP levels decline with age, correlating with metabolic disease
- **Exercise**: Acute exercise induces MDP expression

## Biological Significance

MDPs regulate:
- [[Aging|Longevity]] and lifespan (humanin overexpression extends lifespan 15–20% in mice)
- [[Insulin Sensitivity|Glucose metabolism]] and [[Insulin Sensitivity]]
- Stress resistance and [[Oxidative Stress|redox homeostasis]]
- [[Apoptosis]] and cell survival
- [[Inflammation]] modulation
- Neuroprotection

## Clinical Relevance

MDPs are implicated in:
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]]: Humanin levels inversely correlate with disease severity
- [[Diabetes Mellitus|Type 1 and Type 2 Diabetes]]: Humanin improves glucose tolerance
- [[Atherosclerosis]]: Humanin preserves endothelial function
- [[Aging|Age-related decline]]: MDP levels decrease with age
- [[MELAS]] and [[CPEO]]: MDPs are elevated as compensatory responses
- Cancer: Humanin reduces incidence of age-related tumors in transgenic mice

## Connections

- [[Humanin]]: The first and most studied MDP, discovered in 2001.
- [[MOTS-c]]: Another well-characterized MDP with metabolic regulatory functions.
- [[Mitochondria]]: The source organelle where MDPs are encoded and produced.
- [[Mitochondrial DNA]]: MDPs are encoded within the mitochondrial genome.
- [[Retrograde Signaling]]: MDPs represent a novel class of mitochondria-to-nucleus signals.
- [[Mitokines]]: MDPs are considered mitokines — circulating mitochondrial factors.
- [[Oxidative Stress]]: MDP expression is induced by and protects against oxidative stress.
- [[Aging]]: MDP levels decline with age, linking mitochondrial dysfunction to aging.
- [[AMPK]]: Activates MDP expression under energy stress.
- [[SIRT1]]: NAD+-dependent regulator of MDP expression.
- [[NAD+]]: Essential cofactor for SIRT1-mediated MDP regulation.
