# Estrogen Receptor

The **estrogen receptors** ([[Estrogen Receptor|ERα]] encoded by *ESR1*, and [[Estrogen Receptor|ERβ]] encoded by *ESR2*) are ligand-activated [[Nuclear Receptor|nuclear receptors]] that regulate gene transcription in reproductive and other tissues.

## ERα vs ERβ

ERα ([[Estrogen Receptor|ESR1]]) and ERβ ([[Erβ|ESR2]]) are distinct nuclear receptors with different tissue distributions and often opposing outcomes. ERα dominates reproductive tissues and drives proliferation (luminal [[Breast Cancer]], endometrium); ERβ is the predominant subtype in brain (hippocampus, cortex), vasculature, prostate, lung, colon, and immune cells. ERβ signaling upregulates [[SIRT1]] expression and activity (see [[Erβ]]), linking estrogens to metabolic and neuroprotective programs, and in several settings opposes ERα-driven proliferation (prostate, colon, some breast contexts). This α/β opposition is the rationale for ERβ-selective agonism: estrogen-like benefits (neuroprotection, anti-inflammation, anti-fibrosis) with less proliferative and uterine drive.

## Role in Cancer

ERα is the dominant driver of luminal [[Breast Cancer]]; its expression guides endocrine therapy. Flavonoids such as [[Apigenin]] and [[Genistein]] show biphasic estrogenic/anti-estrogenic activity via ER-binding-dependent and -independent mechanisms, and apigenin exerts anti-estrogenic effects in MCF-7 breast cancer cells. ER status also influences apigenin's growth-inhibitory effects in endometrial cancer. ERβ-selective agents are also investigated in prostate and glioblastoma (preclinical).

## ERβ-Selective Therapeutic Landscape

- **Erteberel (LY500307)**: synthetic non-steroidal selective ERβ agonist, ~14× binding / ~32× functional selectivity over ERα. Reached Ph2 in schizophrenia negative/cognitive symptoms (NCT01874756, terminated — funding ended), perimenopausal depression (NCT03689543, NIMH, completed 2024), and BPH (failed to improve symptoms; high-dose selectivity loss suppresses testosterone in men). Development discontinued as of 2021, with low and variable oral bioavailability cited as a contributor.
- **MF-101**: herbal ERβ-selective preparation, safe and effective on vasomotor symptoms in a Ph2 in postmenopausal women; the herbal composition hindered Ph3 progression.
- **ERB-041**: ERβ agonist that reached Ph2 in inflammatory bowel disease; ERβ/ERB-257-class compounds reached Ph1 in [[Sepsis]].
- **OSU-ERβ-12** (2026): carborane-based ERβ agonist with >100-fold functional selectivity and superior preclinical PK vs erteberel; anti-fibrotic, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cancer efficacy with an improved endocrine-safety profile in males — currently the strongest candidate to revive ERβ-selective pharmacology, including for non-female populations.
- Pipeline overall (2024): thin — ~18 ERβ-targeted molecules including university programs, 1–2 in Ph2, mostly women's-health and CNS indications.

## ERβ in Inflammation & Cell Death

ERβ, not ERα, is the subtype linking estrogen to female-preferential restraint of the [[NLRP3]] [[Inflammasome]]/[[Pyroptosis]] axis: 17β-estradiol suppresses NLRP3, [[ASC]], cleaved caspase-1, and mature IL-1β after ovariectomy across lung and hippocampal ischemia models, reproduced by ERβ agonism but not ERα agonism ([[Pyroptosis]]; Xu et al. 2016, PMID 26928197). ERβ deficiency drives macrophage pyroptosis via enhanced fatty-acid oxidation, acetyl-CoA accumulation, and Stoml2 K221 acetylation, and ERβ expression is reduced in [[Sepsis]] patients and correlates with severity (Zhu et al. 2026, *J Clin Invest*;136(10):e196636). In ischemic [[Stroke]] the female-protective axis is double: estradiol neuroprotection is female-specific and requires intact [[PARP1]] (McCullough 2005, PMID 15689952), while ERβ agonism restrains pyroptotic amplification. This positions ERβ agonism as the female-side, modality-matched partner to male-side PARP inhibition in a sex-stratified cell-death therapeutic design — the "ER for women / parthanatos for men" axis (see `task_output_cell_death_modality_first_therapy_sex_stratified_13_Sep_2026.md` and [[Amelparib]]).
