# Hyperfunction Theory of Aging

The **hyperfunction theory of aging** (Mikhail Blagosklonny) proposes that aging is **not** a dedicated program of destruction but the **quasi-programmed, maladaptive continuation of the developmental growth program** into adulthood, when growth is no longer needed. The master driver is the nutrient/growth-sensing [[mTOR]] pathway. Aging is "twisted growth" (geroconversion) — cells continue growth- and secretion-related programs (e.g., the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]) that were adaptive in development but become pathological later (`src/notes/_link/_document_ - Rapamycin for longevity opinion article.md:113`).

## Core tenets

- **Growth that outlives its purpose is harmful.** Once development is complete, continued [[mTORC1]]-driven anabolism, protein synthesis, and secretion drives age-related disease.
- **Geroconversion:** when the cell cycle is blocked (e.g., by [[Senescence|senescence]]), mTOR-driven signals are re-routed into hypertrophic, hypersecretory "twisted growth" rather than proliferation — the cellular substrate of aging (`...opinion article.md:111`).
- **Rapamycin as proof-of-concept:** because mTOR is the driver, its inhibitor [[Rapamycin]] is an anti-aging drug across model organisms; it extends lifespan by slowing geroconversion, not by treating any single disease (`...opinion article.md:111-113`).
- **Not strictly programmed:** "quasi-programmed" = selected-for developmental program that overshoots, rather than an engineered death program.

## Why this reframes the "benign tumor" question

The theory draws a sharp line that resolves the [[Benign Tumor]] debate:

- **Arrested growth = good.** A benign lesion that is *capped* by [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence]] (e.g., a mole) is protective because the growth program was shut off.
- **Ongoing growth = bad.** Benign overgrowths driven by continuous signaling — [[Hamartoma|hamartomas]] in [[TSC]]/mTORopathies, or any mTOR-hyperactivated proliferation — are disease, even when "benign."

In short, longevity is served by *terminating* growth signaling (rapamycin, [[Caloric Restriction]], [[Metformin]]), not by growth per se. The protective "tumor" is the one that stopped growing.

## Relation to other aging frameworks

- Distinguishes itself from **antagonistic pleiotropy** (senescence beneficial early, harmful late) by locating the harm in *continued growth execution* rather than in trade-offs.
- Overlaps with [[mTORopathies]]: monogenic mTOR hyperactivation (TSC) produces accelerated, rapamycin-responsive pathology — a "fast-forward" of the hyperfunction process.
