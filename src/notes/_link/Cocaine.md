# Cocaine

**Cocaine** is a tropane alkaloid with two mechanistically distinct actions that together account for nearly all of its clinical profile: it is a **non-selective monoamine reuptake inhibitor**, and it is a **use-dependent sodium-channel blocker** (a local anaesthetic, Vaughn-Williams class IC effect). Recreational use, medical use as a topical anaesthetic and vasoconstrictor, and its presence as an adulterant levamisole in crack cocaine are the main contexts.

Cocaine does not act as a dopamine-releaser the way amphetamines do. It is a **reuptake inhibitor**: the transporter-bound drug prevents clearance of transmitter from the synapse, and the accumulated monoamine stimulates postsynaptic receptors for as long as it is present.

## Mechanism

Binding affinities at the three transporters are similar (Ki roughly 0.14 μM for SERT, 0.64 μM for DAT, 1.6 μM for NET), so **[[DAT]] inhibition accounts for the majority of the psychostimulant effect**. This is not inference from affinity alone — behavioural and lethal potency of cocaine analogues correlates with DAT potency, mice lacking DAT do not develop cocaine place preference, and a DAT mutant that cannot bind cocaine also fails to support it. Molecular modelling indicates the cocaine site is adjacent to, but does not overlap, the dopamine site; cocaine blocks dopamine entry into the binding cavity and traps the transporter in an outward-open conformation, so it inhibits both by preventing substrate binding and by reducing kinetic turnover.

> [!important] Two receptors, two toxicities
> The separation of effects is pharmacologically clean and clinically useful: at the 1–3 μM concentrations typical in the emergency department, the monoamine transporters are already maximally inhibited, so raising the concentration adds no further monoamine effect — but it does begin to occupy sodium and potassium channels, which is where cardiotoxicity comes from. Because DAT affinity is roughly ten-fold higher than cardiac ion-channel affinity, direct cardiotoxicity essentially never occurs without CNS effects.

The consequences cascade:

- **Central** — dopaminergic and adrenergic surge producing euphoria, agitation, mydriasis, hyperreflexia, hyperthermia (impaired heat loss through vasoconstriction), and at higher doses generalised tonic-clonic seizures followed by CNS depression.
- **Cardiovascular** — increased heart rate, contractility and blood pressure raise myocardial oxygen demand, while [[Coronary Artery Disease|coronary vasoconstriction]], platelet activation and prothrombotic state reduce supply. The resulting demand/supply mismatch is the mechanism of cocaine-induced myocardial infarction and ischaemia, often occurring within minutes to hours of use.
- **Vascular** — alpha-adrenergic vasoconstriction plus raised endothelin-1, impaired acetylcholine-induced vasorelaxation, inhibition of [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] synthase, and disturbed intracellular calcium handling; the NO synthase inhibition is why endothelial function is degraded.
- **Local anaesthetic** — binding preferentially to inactivated cardiac sodium channels (Ki about 8 μM), increasing as pH falls, producing conduction slowing, QRS and QT prolongation, [[Arrhythmias|arrhythmias]] and negative inotropy.

## Chronic Use

Chronic exposure produces structural myocardial disease — increased left ventricular mass, reduced end-diastolic volume, dilated cardiomyopathy with myocyte hypertrophy and elevated reactive oxygen species, and myocarditis in a substantial minority of autopsy series. [[Oxidative Stress]] and chronic catecholamine excess are the shared upstream mechanisms, structurally similar to the adrenergic cardiomyopathy of pheochromocytoma.

Cocaine also causes endothelial injury, vascular fibrosis, accelerated atherosclerosis and coronary dissection, and is a recognised risk factor for both ischaemic and haemorrhagic stroke, with risk concentrated in the first 24 hours after use.

## Treatment

> [!warning] The beta-blocker controversy
> The "unopposed alpha-stimulation" doctrine — that beta-blockade leaves unopposed alpha vasoconstriction and is therefore contraindicated in cocaine chest pain — drove absolute avoidance of beta-blockers for two decades. It is now largely rejected: systematic review of 1744 patients found only seven adverse events plausibly attributable to the phenomenon, several of which may have been caused by cocaine alone. Benzodiazepines are first-line for cocaine-induced chest pain and hypertension, and sodium bicarbonate is the antidote for wide-complex sodium-channel blockade.

## Connections
- [[VTA]] — The VTA is where cocaine's excess dopamine is released; its phasic firing encodes reward prediction error and is the circuit whose hijacking underlies cocaine reward.
- [[Dopamine Transporter]] — DAT blockade is cocaine's principal mechanism, and DAT occupancy tracks the magnitude of the subjective high in human users.
- [[Dopamine]] — Cocaine's abuse liability and its stimulant action both run through increased synaptic dopamine rather than through direct receptor agonism.
- [[Norepinephrine]] — NET inhibition raises synaptic noradrenaline, producing the sympathetic surge behind tachycardia, hypertension and vasoconstriction.
- [[Serotonin]] — SERT inhibition contributes to cocaine's stimulant profile and to its comparatively mild effect on pulmonary vascular resistance relative to amphetamines.
- [[Coronary artery constriction]] — This vault note already links cocaine as a direct vasoconstrictor; the demand/supply mismatch it describes is cocaine's main ischaemic mechanism.
- [[Addiction]] — Cocaine is the archetypal psychostimulant addiction; DAT-dependent reinforcement is the shared substrate with other drugs of abuse.
- [[Oxidative Stress]] — Chronic cocaine exposure elevates myocardial ROS and shares its adrenergic cardiomyopathy profile with catecholamine overload states.
- [[Nitric Oxide]] — Cocaine inhibits NO synthase and degrades endothelium-dependent vasorelaxation, which is why it is both a vasoconstrictor and a long-term endothelial toxin.
- [[Arrhythmias]] — The class IC sodium-channel effect is the proximate cause of the acute conduction abnormalities and many of the immediate arrhythmias.
