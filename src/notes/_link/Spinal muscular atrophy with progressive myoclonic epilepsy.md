# Spinal muscular atrophy with progressive myoclonic epilepsy

**Spinal muscular atrophy with progressive myoclonic epilepsy** (SMA-PME; OMIM #159950) is a rare autosomal recessive neurodegenerative disorder caused by hypomorphic mutations in [[ASAH1]] retaining ~5–30% residual [[Acid ceramidase]] activity. It is allelic with [[Farber disease]].

## Clinical Features

- **Onset:** Early childhood
- **Motor neuron disease:** Anterior horn degeneration causing muscle weakness, atrophy, hypotonia
- **Epilepsy:** Progressive drug-resistant myoclonic epilepsy
- **Additional features:** Tremor, sensorineural hearing loss, cognitive dysfunction, cerebellar atrophy
- **Progression:** Death from respiratory complications or status epilepticus typically in adolescence

## Genetics

- The c.125C>T (p.Thr42Met) mutation is a recurrent hotspot
- Residual ACase activity (~5–30%) is higher than in Farber disease (<5%), explaining the milder phenotype
- ~44 patients diagnosed to date (Cuinat et al., 2025)

## Biomarkers

- C26-ceramide quantification on dried blood spots (DBS)
- *In cell* ceramide degradation assay in living skin fibroblasts

## Connections

- [[ASAH1]] — The gene mutated in SMA-PME
- [[Acid ceramidase]] — Partially deficient enzyme; loss of function drives pathology
- [[Ceramide]] — Accumulates due to deficient ACase activity
- [[Farber disease]] — Allelic disorder caused by more severe mutations in the same gene
