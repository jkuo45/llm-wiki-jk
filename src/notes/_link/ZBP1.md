# ZBP1

## Overview

ZBP1 (Z-DNA-binding protein 1, also known as DAI) is a cytosolic sensor of Z-nucleic acids — the left-handed [[Z-DNA]] and Z-RNA conformations. By recognizing these noncanonical structures, ZBP1 acts as a danger sensor that triggers programmed cell death, notably PANoptosis, a blended inflammatory death pathway involving pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis. ZBP1 contains Z-alpha domains that confer its structural specificity, distinguishing it from sequence-based DNA sensors such as [[cGAS]].

## PANoptosis and Viral Encephalitis

In the context of infection, ZBP1 is a key mediator of host defense and pathology. During herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1) encephalitis, ZBP1 detects viral Z-nucleic acids and triggers PANoptosis through coordinated engagement of [[STING]] and MAVS signaling, driving [[RIPK3]]-dependent cell death. This places ZBP1 at the intersection of multiple innate immune adaptors, amplifying [[Inflammation]] and tissue injury in the [[Central Nervous System]]. The RIPK3-dependent death axis underscores ZBP1's role as an executioner of inflammatory cell loss rather than merely an interferon inducer.

## Nuclear Functions and Z-Nucleic-Acid Sensing

Beyond the cytosol, ZBP1 also localizes to the [[Nucleus]], where its Z-nucleic-acid sensing may intersect with chromatin stress and transcription. Together with [[cGAS]] and [[AIM2]], ZBP1 forms part of the broader constellation of DNA/structure-sensing proteins that link nucleic-acid conformation to innate immunity. Its ability to recruit both STING- and MAVS-dependent pathways makes ZBP1 a conduit through which noncanonical nucleic-acid structures propagate inflammatory and cell-death signals relevant to [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Neurodegenerative Disease|neurodegeneration]].
