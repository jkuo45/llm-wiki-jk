# α-Ketoglutarate

**α-Ketoglutarate** (α-KG, 2-oxoglutarate) is a pivotal [[TCA cycle]] intermediate that sits at the crossroads of carbon metabolism, amino acid biosynthesis, and epigenetic regulation. It is generated from [[Glutamate]] (via glutamate dehydrogenase or transaminases) and from [[Glutamine]] (via [[Glutaminase]] and glutamate conversion), making it the primary entry point for glutamine-derived carbon into the TCA cycle.

## Metabolic Role

- **Anaplerosis**: α-KG is the key intermediate through which glutamine carbon enters the [[TCA cycle]] via [[Anaplerosis|glutamine anaplerosis]]. This is critical in rapidly proliferating cells that extract TCA intermediates for biosynthesis.
- **Biosynthesis**: Serves as a precursor for synthesis of amino acids (glutamate, glutamine, proline, arginine), fatty acids, and sterols.
- **Nitrogen metabolism**: Acts as a nitrogen acceptor/donor in transamination reactions, linking carbon and nitrogen metabolism.

## Role in Cancer

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]]
> In [[MYC]]^high^ cancer cells, [[Glutamine]]-derived α-ketoglutarate is the primary carbon source for the [[TCA cycle]]. Metabolic ^13^C tracing shows that MYC activation significantly increases α-KG M+5 isotopologue levels from labeled glutamine. Upon [[Complex I]] inhibition, α-KG accumulates (M+5 increases) but cannot be efficiently processed into downstream TCA intermediates, indicating that Complex I inhibition blocks the oxidative reactions of the TCA cycle without preventing glutamine-to-α-KG conversion itself.

## Epigenetic Regulation

α-KG is an obligate co-substrate for several families of dioxygenases:
- **TET enzymes** (DNA demethylation)
- **JmjC-domain histone demethylases**
- **PHD/EGLN prolyl hydroxylases** (HIF-α regulation)

Changes in α-KG levels can therefore influence the [[Epigenome]] and oxygen sensing.
