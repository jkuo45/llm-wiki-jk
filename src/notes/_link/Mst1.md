# Mst1

**Mst1** (mammalian STE20-like kinase 1; STK4) is a serine/threonine kinase and the apical kinase of the Hippo signaling pathway. Together with its paralog Mst2, it phosphorylates and activates LATS1/2 kinases, which in turn phosphorylate the transcriptional coactivators [[YAP|YAP]]/TAZ to control organ size, proliferation, and apoptosis. Beyond Hippo signaling, Mst1 directly promotes mitochondrial fission and pro-apoptotic signaling, and suppresses protective [[Autophagy|autophagy]] — making it a central node integrating cell death and stress-response decisions, particularly in cardiomyocytes during ischemia, hypertrophy, and metabolic stress.

## Mst1 in diabetic cardiomyopathy

In [[Diabetic Cardiomyopathy|diabetic cardiomyopathy (DCM)]], [[Melatonin|melatonin]] counteracts adverse left ventricle remodeling and mitigates cardiac dysfunction. These benefits are attributed to promotion of [[Autophagy|autophagy]], reduction of [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]], and mitigation of mitochondrial dysfunction, facilitated by inhibition of Mst1 phosphorylation and upregulation of [[SIRT3]] expression in DCM models[383]. Because phosphorylated/activated Mst1 drives cardiomyocyte death and suppresses autophagic clearance, melatonin's suppression of Mst1 phosphorylation pairs with SIRT3 upregulation to restore mitochondrial quality control in the diabetic heart.

## Clinical relevance

Mst1 is an emerging therapeutic target in ischemic and metabolic heart disease; strategies that restrain Mst1 activity (including SIRT3-dependent pathways) preserve cardiomyocyte viability and contractile function. The Mst1–SIRT3 interplay illustrates crosstalk between the Hippo kinase cascade and mitochondrial deacetylase biology.
