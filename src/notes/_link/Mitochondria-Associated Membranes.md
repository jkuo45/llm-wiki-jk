# Mitochondria-Associated Membranes

Mitochondria-associated membranes (MAMs) are specialized contact sites between the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] and [[Mitochondria]] that are devoid of membrane fusion but physically tether the two organelles. These junctions facilitate the exchange of [[Calcium]] and lipids and coordinate organelle dynamics and signaling.

According to [[Mitochondrial Dynamics]] and [[MFN2]], MFN2 tethers the ER to mitochondria at MAMs. This tethering not only enables calcium transfer that regulates [[Mitochondrial Fusion]] and fission but also couples ER and mitochondrial homeostasis. Disruption of MAM integrity or MFN2 function perturbs calcium signaling and mitochondrial morphology.

MAMs thus serve as hubs linking ER stress, calcium handling, lipid metabolism, and mitochondrial dynamics to cellular health.
