### Overview
The Mitochondrial Matrix is the aqueous compartment enclosed by the [[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane]]. It contains the soluble enzymes of central carbon metabolism and the organelle's genetic apparatus, and it is the primary site of mitochondrial ATP-generating reactions.

### Metabolic Functions
The matrix is the site of the [[TCA Cycle]] (citric acid cycle/Krebs cycle), where acetyl-CoA is oxidized to produce NADH and FADH2 that feed the electron transport chain. It also houses the [[Mitochondrial DNA]] and the mitochondrial translation machinery.

### Proteolytic Processing
On import into the matrix, proteins destined for the IMM or matrix carry an N-terminal [[Mitochondrial Targeting Sequence]] that is cleaved by the matrix processing protease. For example, [[OPA1]]'s N-terminal targeting sequence is removed by this protease during import, yielding the mature isoforms that regulate fusion.

### Homeostasis
Matrix composition and volume are tightly regulated and contribute to [[Mitochondrial Integrity]]. Perturbations in matrix metabolism and proteostasis contribute to aging and disease.
