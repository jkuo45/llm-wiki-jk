# β-Catenin

[[β-Catenin]] (encoded by *CTNNB1*) is a multifunctional protein that orchestrates the canonical [[Wnt signaling]] pathway as a transcriptional coactivator of TCF/LEF factors and that links cadherins to the actin cytoskeleton at adherens junctions. Deregulated β-Catenin signaling is a hallmark of numerous cancers.

## Function & Pathways

- **Wnt signaling**: In the absence of Wnt ligands, β-Catenin is phosphorylated by a destruction complex (APC, Axin, GSK-3β, CK1) and marked for proteasomal degradation. Wnt binding stabilizes β-Catenin, which translocates to the nucleus and activates TCF/LEF target genes driving proliferation.
- **Cell adhesion**: Bound to E-cadherin at adherens junctions, β-Catenin couples cell-cell adhesion to the actin cytoskeleton.
- **Acetylation regulation**: β-Catenin undergoes reversible acetylation; its transcriptional activity is modulated by deacetylases including sirtuins.

## Role in Sirtuin Biology

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates β-Catenin and thereby suppresses its ability to activate transcription and drive cell proliferation. This is one of the mechanisms by which SIRT1 can act as a tumor suppressor in some contexts (in addition to inhibition of the [[NF-κB|RelA/p65 NF-κB]] subunit). Because SIRT1 also inactivates [[p53]], the net oncogenic or tumor-suppressive outcome is context-dependent.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- *CTNNB1* activating mutations and Wnt pathway deregulation drive colorectal, hepatocellular, and other cancers.
- Nuclear/cytoplasmic β-Catenin accumulation is a poor-prognosis biomarker.
- Therapeutic strategies target β-Catenin coactivator activity, TCF/β-Catenin interaction, or upstream Wnt components.
