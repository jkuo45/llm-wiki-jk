# Antibody

An **antibody** (immunoglobulin, Ig) is the soluble immune receptor of the adaptive immune system. It is a Y-shaped glycoprotein of roughly 150 kDa built from two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, disulfide-linked into a single molecule. Each antibody binds its epitope with high affinity and specificity, and then converts that binding event into a biological action — neutralization, agglutination, complement activation, phagocytosis, or antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity — via its constant (Fc) region. Antibodies are also the workhorse reagent of essentially every technique in molecular and cell biology.

> [!info] The variable/constant split is the whole design
> Antigen specificity resides entirely in the **variable (V) regions** at the amino termini of all four chains, generated by V(D)J recombination and somatic hypermutation. Effector function resides entirely in the **constant (Fc) region**. Separating the two means antigen recognition and biological action can be engineered independently — which is exactly what modern therapeutic antibody engineering exploits.

## Structure

- **Heavy chains** — one of γ (IgG), α (IgA), μ (IgM), δ (IgD), or ε (IgE) defines the isotype. Each heavy chain contributes one variable and three or four constant domains.
- **Light chains** — κ (kappa) or λ (lambda); each contributes one variable and one constant domain. Light chain type is not functionally significant.
- **Fab and Fc.** The antigen-binding fragment (Fab) comprises one complete light chain plus the VH and first CH1 domain. The crystallizable fragment (Fc) comprises the remaining constant domains and carries all effector activity. A papain digest yields two Fab plus one Fc; a pepsin digest yields F(ab′)₂.
- **Valency.** IgG and IgA are bivalent; IgM is pentameric (10 antigen-binding sites) and therefore its primary role is **agglutination** and complement fixation rather than long-lived receptor occupancy; IgA is secreted as a dimer at mucosal surfaces; IgE is monomeric in serum and mast-cell-bound in tissue.
- **Valency is engineered.** Bispecific antibodies and multispecific constructs (e.g., tetravalent formats) are designed to bridge two distinct targets or to increase avidity (PMID: 42757708, 42653742).

## Generation & Affinity Maturation

Antibody diversity is generated in the bone marrow by V(D)J recombination, with junctional diversity at the heavy-chain and light-chain joins. The potential repertoire is astronomically large before any selection. Affinity maturation then occurs in germinal centers: B cells that bind antigen with higher affinity are preferentially expanded, and [[Somatic Hypermutation]] mutates the V-region genes at a high rate; B cells whose mutants bind more strongly survive and differentiate into long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells.

> [!warning] Germinal-center maturation is not optional
> Affinity maturation — the increase in affinity from roughly 10⁵–10⁶ to as high as 10⁹–10¹¹ — happens only in germinal centers. This has direct consequences for vaccine and biologic design: any antigen or scaffold that fails to recruit a germinal center response (e.g., certain highly soluble or repetitive antigens) will not affinity-mature. It also means that affinity is a *selected* property tied to a particular antigen context, and an antibody's affinity for one epitope is not a statement about its affinity for anything else.

## Effector Functions

The Fc region determines what happens after binding, through isotype-specific Fc receptors and complement engagement:

- **Neutralization** — blocks pathogen entry or receptor binding directly, with no downstream effector required.
- **Opsonization and phagocytosis** — IgG-coated targets are recognized by Fcγ receptors on [[Macrophage]]s and neutrophils.
- **Complement activation** — IgM and IgG1/IgG3 fix [[Complement System|C1q]], initiating the classical pathway and generating [[Membrane Attack Complex|C3b/MAC]]-mediated lysis.
- **ADCC** — NK cells and other FcγRIII-expressing cells kill IgG-opsonized targets.
- **Mast-cell sensitization** — IgE bound to FcεRI arms mast cells and basophils for immediate hypersensitivity.
- **Mucosal defense** — secretory IgA in mucosal secretions blocks adherence and neutralizes without recruiting significant inflammation.

> [!important] Isotype engineering is rational, not cosmetic
> Because all effector function sits in the Fc region, an antibody's isotype determines whether it lyses, opsonizes, or silently neutralizes. Therapeutic selection is made deliberately: IgG1 for ADCC and complement fixation, IgG4 or Fc-silenced formats when inflammation must be avoided, IgA for mucosal delivery. Fc-engineering to improve these profiles is an active field (PMID: 42621748).

## Applications in Research & Medicine

**Research reagents.** Antibodies are the primary tool for [[Immunohistochemistry]], [[Western Blot]], [[Immunofluorescence]], [[Flow Cytometry]], [[ELISA]], immunoprecipitation, and affinity purification. Protein A affinity capture is the common first step. Techniques that depend on antibody-tissue interaction — [[CUT&Tag]], in which an antibody is used to tether a transposase to chromatin — inherit the antibody's specificity entirely, so a weak antibody produces weak data rather than obviously wrong data.

> [!warning] Antibody quality limits every experiment that uses one
> Off-target binding, lot-to-lot variability, and cross-reactivity are the most common root causes of irreproducible antibody-based results. Polyclonal sera contain multiple clones with different affinities and epitopes, which is both a strength (recognizes several epitopes) and the source of the variability. Monoclonal antibodies give specificity and reproducibility, but at the cost of sensitivity when the chosen epitope is masked or conformationally altered. In [[CUT&Tag]], antibody validation is not optional: specificity of the data is exactly specificity of the antibody.

**Therapeutics.** Monoclonal antibodies are used across [[Immunotherapy]], [[Targeted Therapy]], [[Chemotherapy]]-adjuvant settings, and [[Inflammation|anti-inflammatory]] therapy. Because they are proteins, they have good specificity and half-life but poor oral bioavailability and are expensive to manufacture, and they can provoke anti-drug antibodies. Bispecific formats can recruit one cell type against another, as in CD3-engaging T-cell engagers.

## Connections

- [[Immune System]] — antibodies are the effector arm of humoral immunity, the branch of the adaptive system that circulates and acts away from the cell surface.
- [[Complement System]] — IgM and IgG1/IgG3 initiate the classical complement pathway via C1q binding to Fc, linking antibody recognition to a protease cascade and membrane attack complex.
- [[Immunotherapy]] — checkpoint blockade and anti-angiogenic antibodies are the main clinical applications of the antigen-specificity-plus-effector design, and their efficacy depends on the Fc region as much as the epitope.
- [[CD47]] — an antibody–antigen pair of major current interest: anti-CD47 antibodies block the "don't eat me" signal, and understanding why the same antibody fails in some trials and works in others requires the effector-function framework in this note.
- [[Autophagy]] — antibodies are the standard detection reagents for autophagy markers such as LC3; this is why LC3 antibody specificity has been a recurring source of contested autophagy literature.
- [[CUT&Tag]] — a technique in which the antibody's specificity becomes the experiment's spatial resolution limit, making antibody validation inseparable from the validity of the chromatin data.
- Protein A — the bacterial surface protein that binds the Fc region and is the standard capture reagent for antibody purification.
- [[Inflammation]] — isotype choice determines whether antibody engagement is pro-inflammatory or anti-inflammatory, which is the design principle behind essentially every therapeutic antibody.
