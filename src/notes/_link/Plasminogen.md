# Plasminogen

**Plasminogen** is the inactive zymogen precursor of [[Plasmin]], the central effector enzyme of the fibrinolytic system. It is a 92 kDa single-chain glycoprotein synthesised in the liver and secreted into plasma at ~2 µM concentration. Plasminogen is converted to active [[Plasmin]] by cleavage at Arg561-Val562 by tissue-type ([[Tissue Plasminogen Activator|tPA]]) and urokinase-type ([[uPA]]) plasminogen activators. Beyond its role as a plasmin precursor, plasminogen serves as the substrate for [[Angiostatin]] generation by MMPs, linking the fibrinolytic system to [[Angiogenesis]] regulation.

## Structure

- **Gene:** *PLG* is located on chromosome 6q26, spanning ~52 kb with 19 exons.
- **Protein:** Plasminogen is a 92 kDa single-chain glycoprotein composed of:
  - **5 kringle domains (K1–5):** Mediate binding to fibrin, α2-antiplasmin, cell-surface receptors, and extracellular matrix components. Kringle domains contain lysine-binding sites essential for substrate recognition.
  - **Serine protease domain:** Catalytic domain; inactive in the zymogen form due to conformational constraints.
- **Conformational states:**
  - **Glu-Plasminogen:** The native circulating form (N-terminal glutamic acid). Compact, looped conformation held together by internal lysine-binding site interactions. Lower activity.
  - **Lys-Plasminogen:** After N-terminal cleavage by plasmin or other proteases. Open, extended conformation with higher activity and faster activation. Generated at clot surfaces.
- **Activation site:** Cleavage at Arg561-Val562 by tPA or uPA converts the single-chain zymogen to the two-chain active plasmin (heavy chain: kringle domains; light chain: catalytic domain).

> [!important] Fibrin-Specific Activation
> Plasminogen activation by tPA is dramatically enhanced (~500-fold) when plasminogen is bound to fibrin. This ensures that plasmin generation is localised to the clot surface rather than occurring systemically, preventing widespread proteolysis.

## Activation Pathways

### tPA-Mediated Activation (Fibrin-Specific)
[[Tissue Plasminogen Activator|tPA]] activates plasminogen efficiently only when both are bound to fibrin. This fibrin-specific mechanism ensures that clot lysis is spatially controlled. The clinical thrombolytic agent alteplase is recombinant tPA.

### uPA-Mediated Activation (Cell-Surface)
[[uPA]] activates plasminogen at the cell surface via the urokinase receptor (uPAR/CD87). This cell-associated activation is independent of fibrin and is important for:
- Cell migration through ECM barriers
- Tissue remodelling
- Tumour invasion

### Other Activators
- **Factor XII (Hageman factor):** Can activate plasminogen, linking the contact activation pathway to fibrinolysis.
- **Kallikrein:** Activates plasminogen during inflammation.
- **MMP-12:** Cleaves plasminogen to generate [[Angiostatin]] (anti-angiogenic fragment) rather than active plasmin.

## Regulation

- **α2-Antiplasmin (α2-AP):** The primary rapid-acting inhibitor. Forms a 1:1 complex with free plasmin (half-life ~0.1 seconds), ensuring that plasmin generated systemically is immediately neutralised.
- **[[PAI-1]]:** Inhibits tPA and uPA, indirectly controlling plasmin generation.
- **PAI-2:** Inhibits uPA less efficiently; expressed in placenta and monocytes.
- **TAFI (Thrombin-Activatable Fibrinolysis Inhibitor):** Removes C-terminal lysine residues from partially degraded fibrin, reducing plasminogen and tPA binding and attenuating fibrinolysis.
- **Fibrin itself:** Serves as both substrate and cofactor that accelerates tPA-mediated plasminogen activation, creating a positive feedback at the clot surface.

## Physiological Roles

### Fibrinolysis
Plasminogen's primary physiological role is to serve as the precursor for plasmin-mediated fibrinolysis—the dissolution of fibrin clots. This is essential for maintaining vascular patency and preventing pathological thrombus persistence.

### Wound Healing
Plasminogen-derived plasmin degrades ECM components (fibronectin, laminin, collagen IV) and activates pro-MMPs, facilitating cell migration during tissue repair.

### Embryonic Development
PLG-knockout mice exhibit growth retardation and die by 3 weeks of age due to fibrin deposition and impaired tissue remodelling, demonstrating that plasminogen is essential for embryonic development.

### Immune Defence
Plasminogen binds to the surface of bacteria and fungi, where its activation generates plasmin that degrades ECM and facilitates pathogen dissemination. This is exploited by group A streptococcus and other pathogens.

## Pathological Roles

### Thrombosis
Reduced plasminogen levels or impaired activation contributes to thrombotic disease (deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, myocardial infarction).

### Cancer
Plasminogen-derived plasmin promotes tumour invasion by degrading ECM barriers, activating pro-MMPs, and releasing matrix-bound growth factors. Elevated uPA and low PAI-1 are poor-prognosis markers in breast cancer.

### Kidney Disease
Plasminogen and plasmin contribute to glomerular basement membrane damage in inflammatory renal diseases.

### Neurological Diseases
Plasmin participates in BBB remodelling following [[Ischemic Stroke|ischaemic stroke]]. tPA (alteplase) is the primary thrombolytic agent, but excessive plasmin generation can cause haemorrhagic transformation.
