# CZ-48

**CZ-48** (sulfo-ara-F-NMN) is a synthetic, cell-permeant analog of [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide|NMN]] that is the only molecule known to have **opposite effects** on the two principal NAD⁺ glycohydrolases: it **activates** [[SARM1]] while **inhibiting** [[CD38]] (Zhao et al. 2019, _iScience_).

## Mechanism

- **SARM1:** CZ-48 acts as an NMN mimetic that binds the SARM1 ARM domain, relieving autoinhibition and driving NADase activation. Activated SARM1 produces cyclic ADP-ribose (cADPR), depletes [[NAD+]] and ATP, and induces non-apoptotic cell death.
- **CD38:** CZ-48 inhibits CD38 ecto-enzymatic NADase activity, reducing extracellular NAD⁺/NMN consumption.

## Significance

CZ-48 is a pharmacological proof-of-concept that the two NADases — despite sharing substrate (NAD⁺) and products (nicotinamide, ADP-ribose, cADPR) — can be pharmacologically separated. This divergence underpins the **NMN-sensitization paradox**: raising NMN/NAD⁺ precursors (to inhibit CD38 and feed sirtuins) could, in principle, sensitize SARM1 — the exact tension addressed by combined CD38 + SARM1 inhibition.
