# HPA Axis

The **HPA axis** is the neuroendocrine cascade that converts a stressor into a
circulating glucocorticoid signal. It is the primary effector of the stress
response and the reason [[Cortisol]] is the canonical stress hormone.

## Organization

The axis is a three-level feed-forward loop with negative feedback at every
level:

- **Hypothalamus.** Neurons of the paraventricular nucleus (PVN) secrete
  corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) and, co-released, arginine
  vasopressin (AVP) into the hypophysial portal plexus at the median eminence.
  Stressor type matters: homeostatic disruption drives PVN neurons through
  direct noradrenergic and peptidergic sensory relays, whereas anticipatory
  responses use oligosynaptic limbic pathways and are largely driven by
  disinhibition of tonic GABAergic input from the amygdala.
- **Anterior pituitary.** CRH acting at CRHR1 (GPCR → adenylate cyclase) is
  the "main line" of the axis — CRH knockout mice lack both basal and
  stress-induced ACTH. AVP acts through AVP1B receptors and protein kinase C,
  complementing CRH but not driving ACTH alone at physiological levels.
- **Adrenal cortex.** ACTH acts at melanocortin 2 receptors (MC2R) in the zona
  fasciculata to drive glucocorticoid synthesis — [[Cortisol]] in primates,
  corticosterone in rodents.

> [!info] Timing
> ACTH rises within minutes of the stressor, but peak corticosterone lags
> ACTH by roughly 30–60 minutes because of adrenal synthesis time. Rapid
> non-genomic GR feedback caps the ACTH response within about 15 minutes;
> the slower genomic phase represses POMC, CRH and AVP transcription.

Secretion is **ultradian** pulsatile (sub-hypothalamic pulse generator) on
top of a **circadian** rhythm, and each pulse amplitude is set primarily by
hypothalamic modulation. Anticipatory activation primes the axis before the
active phase of the day.

## Negative Feedback

Cortisol suppresses its own production at three sites: CRH release in the
hypothalamus, ACTH release in the pituitary, and adrenal glucocorticoid
sensitivity. Feedback acts through glucocorticoid receptors (GR) and
mineralocorticoid receptors (MR) with both rapid non-genomic and slower
genomic components; the genomic component represses POMC in the pituitary and
CRH/AVP in the hypothalamus.

> [!warning] Feedback strength is the individual variable
> Sensitivity to glucocorticoids — set by GR density and accessibility in
> hippocampus and prefrontal cortex — determines how tightly the axis is
> clamped. This is the mechanistic basis for the well-documented individual
> variation in stress responsiveness, and it is modifiable by chronic stress
> exposure itself.

## Dysregulation

Single measurements mislead. Adrenal resistance can mask hypothalamic and
pituitary activation, so a normal corticosterone value does not prove an
unstressed axis.

Chronic stress produces several distinct phenotypes rather than one: chronic
basal hypersecretion, sensitized stress responses, or adrenal exhaustion,
depending on stressor chronicity, intensity, frequency and modality. Models
that include gland-mass dynamics explain why ACTH responses stay blunted for
weeks after cortisol has normalized (post-partum, alcoholism, anorexia).

Reported chronic-stress phenotypes include sustained hypercortisolemia,
flattened diurnal slope, impaired feedback inhibition, and — paradoxically —
glucocorticoid-receptor resistance with downstream hypocortisolism. The net
effect is a shift toward IL-6/TNF-α/IL-17 dominance with reduced IL-10, i.e.
a pro-inflammatory state, which is why HPA dysfunction tracks with
[[Inflammaging]] and [[Inflammation]]-driven disease.

A model with functional gland-mass dynamics is described in
`_document_ - Mitohormesis - 2023_NOV` — the referenced POMC-neuron
literature there concerns hypothalamic circuits, not the axis itself.

## Aging and Measurement

Aging alters both circadian and stress-induced cortisol secretion, and older
adults are more susceptible to the neurological effects of prolonged HPA
dysregulation — hippocampus and prefrontal GR density fall, feedback weakens,
and mitochondrial allostatic load accumulates. Longitudinal work shows daily
stressor exposure predicts elevated allostatic load only in older adults,
placing age as a modifier rather than the driver.

Practical readouts: salivary or urinary free cortisol across the day for
rhythm, hair cortisol for integrated exposure, and [[Heart Rate Variability]]
as a non-invasive proxy for autonomic coupling. Single morning cortisol
values are weak evidence.

## Connections

- [[Cortisol]] — the axis's terminal output and its main feedback signal;
  everything measured about the HPA axis is usually measured through cortisol.
- [[Adrenal Cortex]] — the site of ACTH-dependent glucocorticoid synthesis and
  where adrenal sensitivity can independently blunt or amplify axis output.
- [[Adrenal gland]] — the anatomical target of ACTH; its medulla is driven by
  the parallel splanchnic/PNMT arm rather than by ACTH.
- [[Hippocampus]] and [[Prefrontal Cortex]] — GR-rich regions whose receptor
  loss weakens feedback inhibition, the core loop of chronic-stress
  dysregulation.
- [[Yerkes-Dodson Law]] — the behavioural dose-response curve for arousal that
  the axis underlies; moderate activation is adaptive, sustained or absent
  activation is not.
- [[Inflammaging]] — chronic HPA activation with GR resistance produces the
  pro-inflammatory cytokine balance that drives immunosenescence.
- [[Depression]] — frequently associated with blunted or dysregulated HPA
  output and impaired feedback, in either direction.
- [[Autonomic Nervous System]] — the sympatho-adrenal arm runs in parallel with
  the axis and shares central PVN drive.
