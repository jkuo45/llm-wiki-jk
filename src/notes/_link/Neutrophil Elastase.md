# Neutrophil Elastase

**Neutrophil Elastase** (NE, gene *ELANE*) is a granule serine protease released during neutrophil activation. In [[NETosis]] it translocates to the nucleus, clips histones to aid decondensation (a PAD4-independent route when dominant), and decorates extruded NETs with [[Myeloperoxidase]] and [[cit-H3]] — forming the DNA–histone–protease web that traps pathogens and, pathologically, drives immunothrombosis. NE/MPO inhibition is part of the standard NETosis blockade panel.

## Mechanism in suicidal NETosis

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> In resting neutrophils, NE is sequestered with [[Myeloperoxidase]], azurocidin, CatG, defensin-1, lysozyme, and lactoferrin in an azurophil-granule complex called the azurosome. NADPH-oxidase-derived [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] activate MPO and dissociate MPO/NE from the azurosome, releasing NE into the cytosol by a mechanism requiring intracellular MPO activity (Metzler et al. 2011, 2014). Cytosolic NE degrades F-[[Actin]], immobilizing the neutrophil; nuclear NE cleaves histones H4 and H2B to drive chromatin decondensation. NE also cleaves [[Gasdermin D]] to GSDMD-p30 during [[Suicidal NETosis]], initiating a feedforward loop of granule rupture, further NE release, and plasma-membrane rupture (Sollberger et al. 2018). Murine NE deficiency prevents NET formation during in vivo *Klebsiella pneumoniae* challenge (Papayannopoulos et al. 2010). NE is a direct NET antibacterial effector, cleaving bacterial outer-membrane proteins and virulence factors of enterobacteria (Belaaouaj et al. 1998). NE and MPO are dispensable for [[Noncanonical NETosis]].
