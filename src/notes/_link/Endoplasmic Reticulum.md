# Endoplasmic Reticulum

**Overview:** The [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] (ER) is a continuous network of membranous tubules and cisternae extending from the nuclear envelope throughout the cytoplasm. The rough ER (studded with ribosomes) synthesizes transmembrane and secreted proteins, while the smooth ER specializes in lipid biosynthesis, steroidogenesis, and detoxification.

**Structure and organization:** The ER lumen hosts chaperones such as BiP/GRP78 and protein-disulfide isomerase that catalyze proper folding and disulfide-bond formation. Its membrane forms close physical contacts—[[Mitochondria|mitochondria–ER contacts]] (MAMs)—that coordinate calcium and lipid transfer and redox signaling.

**Physiological function:** Beyond protein folding, the ER is the principal intracellular store of [[Calcium Ions|calcium]], releasing it to drive muscle contraction, secretion, and apoptosis. It synthesizes phospholipids and cholesterol and metabolizes xenobiotics via cytochrome P450 enzymes.

**Pathological relevance:** Accumulation of misfolded proteins triggers [[ER Stress]] and the unfolded protein response (UPR), which attempts restoration but, if unresolved, activates [[Inflammation]] (via IRE1α–TRAF2–NF-κB), [[Oxidative Stress]], and [[Apoptosis]] (CHOP/TRB3, caspase-12). ER stress is implicated in neurodegeneration, [[Aging]], diabetes, and cancer. Selective ER turnover, ER-phagy, is coordinated with [[Autophagy]] through factors such as FAM134B.
