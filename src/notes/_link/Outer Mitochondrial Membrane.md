### Overview
The Outer Mitochondrial Membrane (OMM) is the outermost lipid bilayer of the mitochondrion, separating the organelle from the cytoplasm and defining the boundary through which most fission and fusion machinery is recruited.

### Fusion Machinery
OMM fusion is mediated by the mitofusins [[MFN1]] and [[MFN2]], large GTPases that reside in the OMM and tether adjacent mitochondria through their heptad-repeat (HR2) domains. Redox-dependent disulfide bond formation between MFN HR2 domains in the [[Mitochondrial Intermembrane Space|intermembrane space]] drives OMM oligomerization and membrane merger.

### Fission Machinery
Mitochondrial fission is executed on the OMM by [[DRP1]], a cytosolic dynamin-like GTPase that is recruited to the OMM by a set of adaptor proteins: [[MFF]], [[MID49]], [[MID51]], and [[FIS1]]. These anchors position DRP1 oligomers at constriction sites where GTP hydrolysis drives membrane scission.

### Functional Significance
Balanced OMM fusion and fission maintains mitochondrial distribution, [[Mitochondrial Integrity]], and quality control. Disruption of OMM dynamics underlies axonal neuropathies such as [[Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease Type 2A]], where [[MFN2]] mutations impair transport.
