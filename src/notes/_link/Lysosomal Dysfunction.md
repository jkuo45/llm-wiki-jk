# Lysosomal Dysfunction

**Lysosomal dysfunction** is the loss of lysosomal degradative capacity — through failing acidification (V-ATPase), depleted hydrolases, damaged membranes, or overwhelmed flux — and it is one of the nine hallmarks of aging. Because the lysosome terminates [[Autophagy]], [[Mitophagy]], and endocytosis, its failure poisons every upstream clearance system at once: substrates accumulate, membranes leak, and the cell tips from waste management into inflammatory signaling.

## Mechanisms of failure

- **Acidification loss**: rising lysosomal pH disables cathepsins and other hydrolases; sphingolipid bis(monoacylglycero)phosphate/bisretinoid and lipofuscin burden buffer pH upward with age.
- **Membrane permeabilization**: leakage of cathepsins (e.g., cathepsin B) into the cytosol converts the lysosome into a death-signal source — feeding [[NLRP3]]-dependent pyroptosis and apoptosis.
- **Flux overload**: when [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] generates damaged organelles faster than mitophagy/lysosomes clear them, partially degraded cargo accumulates as lipofuscin — itself phototoxic and further pH-buffering.

## Role in neurodegeneration

In Parkinson's disease, lysosomal dysfunction is upstream and causal: dopaminergic neurons are especially dependent on autophagy for [[Alpha-synuclein]] clearance. Failing lysosomes allow α-synuclein oligomers and [[Lewy Bodies]] to accumulate; dying neurons then release neuromelanin and aggregates as DAMPs, priming microglia ([[Neuroinflammation]]) confined to the pigmented nuclei. Complex I defects ([[MPTP]] model) and lysosomal failure act together to sustain this cycle. Dolichol accumulation inside neuromelanin granules marks the same lysosomal/isoprenoid intersection and tracks dopaminergic aging.

## Regulation and rescue

- **[[TFEB]] / MiT-TFE factors**: the CLEAR-network master regulators expand lysosomal mass and acidification — the primary transcriptional rescue axis.
- **[[mTOR]] inhibition / [[AMPK]] activation**: relieve the repression that keeps autophagic flux low (rapamycin, trehalose, fasting).
- **Autophagy-enhancing compounds**: spermidine, urolithin A — restore flux in arterial aging and mitophagy models.
- **Galectin-3 and ESCRT**: membrane-damage sensors; chronic engagement marks persistently leaky lysosomes.
