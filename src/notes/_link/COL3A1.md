# COL3A1

*COL3A1* (2q32.2) encodes the alpha-1 chain of type III collagen — three identical chains
assembled into a homotrimer. Type III is one of the fibrillar collagens and, unlike type I,
is not a tissue "backbone" so much as a **regulator**: it coats the surface of type I
fibrils and sets their diameter. Its abundance is therefore the switch that determines
how thick and how strong collagen fibres become.

## The gene and the protein

The gene spans ~40 kb over 51 exons and is transcribed bidirectionally in tail-to-tail
orientation with *COL5A2*, the nearest neighbour on the chromosome. The primary
translation product, prepro-alpha-1(III), is 1466 residues: an N-terminal signal peptide
(residues 1–23), an N-terminal propeptide, a ~1000-residue triple-helical domain, and a
C-terminal propeptide.

## Biosynthesis

Type III collagen follows the classic fibrillar collagen route:

- Three pro-alpha-1(III) chains recognise each other at their C-terminal propeptides,
  form disulphide bonds, and align. The C-terminal propeptide trimer is the structure
  that enforces chain selection — all three chains are identical here, but the same
  mechanism lets mixed-collagen molecules (e.g. type V) choose which alpha chains combine.
- Each chain folds into a left-handed polyproline-II-like helix (about 3.3 residues per
  turn); the three wrap into a right-handed superhelix, one turn per ~20 residues.
- Hydroxylation and glycosylation happen co-translationally: ~145 of the 239 prolines
  in the triple-helical domain become 4-hydroxyproline via prolyl-4-hydroxylase, and
  selected lysines are hydroxylated and glycosylated.
- Lysyl oxidase catalyses oxidative deamination of lysine and hydroxylysine to form the
  aldehydes that crosslink.
- C- and N-terminal proproteinases cleave the globular propeptides off, releasing
  **tropocollagen**, which self-assembles in the extracellular space into fibrils and
  fibres.

> [!info] Why glycine matters
> The triple helix is only possible because glycine occupies every third position
> (Gly-X-Y)₃₄₃ across the ~1000-residue domain. Glycine is the only residue small
> enough to sit at the core where the three chains meet, and the periodicity of glycines
> at non-integer spacing is what generates the superhelical twist. This is also why the
> archetypal COL3A1 mutation is a glycine substitution.

## Tissue distribution and function

Type III is a major structural component of the **reticular fibres** of hollow organs —
arteries, uterus, bowel, and the muscularis of the gut — and is found alongside type I
in skin, lung, tendon and vasculature. Three functions matter beyond structure:

1. **Fibril diameter control** — type III co-polymers with type I and restricts fibril
   calibre; the ratio of type I to III determines tissue stiffness.
2. **Platelet aggregation** — type III binds and activates [[Platelet|platelets]] and is
   directly involved in clot formation, hence the bleeding diathesis in deficiency.
3. **Smooth muscle phenotype** — type III expression marks synthetic, dedifferentiated
   smooth muscle and is a marker of the fibrotic, matrix-producing vascular smooth
   muscle phenotype.

## Regulation by TGF-β

*COL3A1* is one of the canonical transcriptional targets of the [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway|TGF-β/SMAD]] axis. Canonical signalling proceeds as follows:

- Ligand [[TGF-beta1|TGF-β1]] binds [[TGF-beta Receptor|TGFBR2]], which phosphorylates and
  activates [[TGFBR1]].
- Smad (Phospho-Smad) receptor-interacting protein 1 is recruited and the receptor complex
  is internalised into an endosome containing [[SMAD2]] and [[SMAD3]].
- p-SMAD2/3 complex with [[SMAD4]] and translocates to the nucleus.
- The complex binds SBE/CAGE motifs in the *COL3A1* promoter, and co-activators including
  p300/CBP, SMAD4 and the E-box-binding factors raise transcription.

*COL3A1* transcription is a downstream readout of pathway activity, which is why it is
used as a marker of TGF-β engagement in fibrotic tissue. [[SMAD3]] occupancy at the
*COL3A1* promoter is a direct molecular readout. Matrix turnover is simultaneously
gated by [[TIMP1]] and the [[Matrix Metalloproteinases|MMP]] family: increasing
TIMP1 relative to active MMP tips the balance toward accumulation.

## Clinical significance

Heterozygous dominant-negative mutations in *COL3A1* cause **vascular Ehlers-Danlos
syndrome (vEDS)**, formerly EDS type IV, the most severe of the Ehlers-Danlos
subtypes. The first mutation was described in 1989 — a glycine-to-serine substitution —
and more than 600 variants have since been reported; roughly two thirds are glycine
substitutions. Hallmarks are thin translucent skin, easy bruising, characteristic
arterialised facies, arterial/intestinal/uterine fragility with spontaneous dissection
or perforation, and early death from rupture of large arteries or hollow organs.

*COL3A1* variants have also been found in patients with isolated arterial aneurysm
without overt EDS, and — more recently — in patients with severe cortical malformation
resembling the phenotype of *ADGRG1* (GPR56) loss of function, since type III collagen is
a ligand for that adhesion GPCR.

> [!warning] Genotype–phenotype caveat
> The *COL3A1* variant class matters more than the variant identity. Haploinsufficiency
> variants behave differently from dominant-negative glycine substitutions, and
> null alleles are associated with a severe, often lethal neonatal phenotype rather than
> classical vEDS. Antifibrinolytics (tranexamic acid) reduce bleeding, but the
> cornerstone of management is blood-pressure control and restricted aortic
> surveillance — beta-blockers and/or ARBs to limit wall stress.

## Connections

- [[TGF-beta1]] — TGF-β1 signalling through TGFBR1/2 and SMAD2/3/4 is the dominant transcriptional regulator of COL3A1. Chronic or repeated TGF-β exposure drives excess type III deposition, which is the shared endpoint of fibrosis across organs.
- [[SMAD3]] — Phosphorylated SMAD3 is the DNA-binding component of the TGF-β transcriptional complex at the COL3A1 promoter. It is also the gene through which the TGF-beta1 and COL3A1 nodes connect in the vault graph.
- [[TIMP1]] — TIMP1 inhibits MMP activity, so the TIMP:MMP ratio determines whether TGF-beta1-driven collagen synthesis results in net matrix accumulation or net remodelling.
- [[Collagen]] — COL3A1 is one of the fibrillar collagen genes; the vault's Collagen note is the parent concept, with type III distinguished by its fibril-diameter-regulating role.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — Type III collagen is a core structural component of the reticular fibre network. Its loss or excess directly remodels the matrix, which is why ECM notes and COL3A1 note are tightly coupled.
- [[Vascular Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome]] — vEDS is caused by dominant-negative glycine substitutions in COL3A1; the resulting thin, fragile vessels dissect and rupture. This is the definitive disease link and the reason the gene matters clinically.
- [[Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome]] — Ehlers-Danlos syndrome is the connective tissue dysplasia family; COL3A1 defines the vascular (type IV) subtype, distinct from the classical (COL5A1/COL5A2) and classical-like forms.
- [[Platelet]] — Type III collagen binds and activates platelets, which is the structural basis for the bleeding tendency in vEDS and for its role in clot formation.
- [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway]] — The pathway whose nuclear output is COL3A1 transcription. COL3A1 expression is used as a downstream readout of pathway activation in fibrotic tissue.
- [[Wound Healing]] — Type III collagen is abundant in early granulation tissue and is progressively replaced by type I during scar maturation. The ratio of the two is the classic histological measure of wound age.
- [[Fibrosis]] — Excessive type III collagen deposition is the histological signature of fibrosis, driven by TGF-beta1 and SMAD3 and opposed by matrix metalloproteinase activity.
