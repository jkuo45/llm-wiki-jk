# SREBP

**SREBP** (sterol regulatory element-binding protein) is the family of ER-anchored basic helix-loop-helix transcription factors that govern lipid synthesis and cholesterol homeostasis. The three members have distinct jobs: **SREBP-1c** drives fatty-acid and triglyceride synthesis (adipose and liver), **SREBP-1a** is broadly expressed and links lipid synthesis to cell growth, and **SREBP-2** drives cholesterol synthesis and LDL-receptor expression.

## Structure & Activation

- SREBPs are synthesised as precursors with an N-terminal transcription factor domain, a basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper, a regulatory domain that binds SCAP, and a C-terminal membrane anchor.
- In the ER, SREBP is retained by SCAP and the anchor; low cholesterol (and low sterols) allow SCAP to escort SREBP-2 to the Golgi, where S1P/S2P/S2P proteases cleave it, releasing the N-terminal fragment into the nucleus.
- [[Insulin]] and high glucose also activate SREBP-1c via [[Akt]]-mediated induction and by promoting its processing; [[Insulin Signaling]] and glucose therefore feed directly into lipogenesis.
- [[AMPK]] phosphorylates SREBP-1c (and inhibits its processing), which is the molecular basis of the anti-lipogenic effect of AMPK activation and of many nutrient-restriction interventions.

## Physiological Function

- SREBP-2 raises HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme of cholesterol synthesis, and the LDL receptor; this axis is the target of [[Statins]].
- SREBP-1c raises fatty-acid synthesis enzymes (ATP-citrate lyase, fatty-acid synthase) and triglyceride synthesis, and it is the transcriptional node that converts excess carbohydrate into lipid.
- Loss of SREBP activity causes lipodystrophy and hepatic steatosis; chronic over-activation drives fatty liver, dyslipidaemia and insulin resistance.
- Nuclear SREBPs also induce apoptosis and cell-cycle arrest programs when proteolysis is uncoupled from degradation — the basis of the "lipotoxic ER stress" axis linking lipid synthesis to [[Cellular Senescence]] and hepatocyte death.

## Clinical Relevance

Statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibition), PCSK9 inhibitors, mTOR and AMPK modulators, and insulin-sensitising agents all act upstream or downstream of the SREBP axis; SREBP-2 activation status predicts statin response, and SREBP-1c is a target in NAFLD and metabolic syndrome.
