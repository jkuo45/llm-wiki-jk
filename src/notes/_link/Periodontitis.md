# Periodontitis

**Periodontitis** is a chronic inflammatory disease affecting the supporting apparatus of the teeth — the gingiva, periodontal ligament, cementum, and alveolar bone. It is initiated by a dysbiotic subgingival biofilm and perpetuated by host immune-inflammatory responses, progressing through gingival pocket formation, loss of connective tissue attachment, and alveolar bone resorption by [[Osteoclast|osteoclasts]]. Diabetes mellitus is a major risk factor and severity modifier: hyperglycemia amplifies inflammation, oxidative stress, and bone resorption, establishing the diabetic periodontitis phenotype. Periodontitis has also been epidemiologically linked to systemic conditions including cardiovascular disease and metabolic syndrome.

## SIRT3 and the irisin axis in diabetic periodontitis

In diabetic periodontitis models, the review describes that [[Irisin|irisin]] exerts antioxidative and osteoprotective effects that are strictly dependent on [[SIRT3]]: lentivirus-mediated SIRT3 silencing abolishes irisin's protection against osteoclastic alveolar bone resorption and [[ROS|ROS]] overproduction[248],[240]. This places SIRT3 at the center of redox homeostasis and bone preservation in the hyperlipidemic, inflamed periodontal microenvironment, and identifies SIRT3 as a candidate target for preventing diabetic periodontal bone loss.

## Therapeutic landscape

Current standard of care is mechanical debridement (scaling and root planning) with adjunctive antimicrobials; host-modulatory agents (e.g., subantimicrobial-dose doxycycline) are used adjunctively. SIRT3-activating strategies represent an emerging host-modulatory direction aimed at mitochondrial redox control in periodontal tissues.
