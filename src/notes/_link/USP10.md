# USP10

**USP10** (Ubiquitin Specific Peptidase 10) is a deubiquitinating enzyme (DUB) that removes ubiquitin chains from target proteins, counteracting [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System|proteasomal degradation]]. It is a key regulator of [[SIRT6]] stability and also modulates [[p53]] and [[c-Myc]] transcriptional activity.

## SIRT6 Stabilization

USP10 binds the C-terminal region of [[SIRT6]] (amino acids 298–355) and deubiquitinates it, protecting SIRT6 from proteasomal degradation[29]. This interaction is disrupted by the centenarian SIRT6 N308K mutation, but the resulting elevation in SIRT6 levels in centenarian SIRT6 (CentSIRT6) cells is independent of USP10 — mediated instead by weakened interaction with [[Vimentin]].

## Connections

  - [[SIRT6]]: USP10 stabilizes SIRT6 by deubiquitination; centenarian variants elevate SIRT6 independently of USP10.
  - [[Vimentin]]: Alternative pathway for SIRT6 stabilization (vimentin-mediated, not USP10).
  - [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]]: UPS is the degradation pathway counteracted by USP10.
  - [[c-Myc]]: USP10 antagonizes c-Myc transcriptional activation through SIRT6 stabilization[29].
  - [[p53]]: USP10 also regulates p53 stability and activity.
