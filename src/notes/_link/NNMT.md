# NNMT

**NNMT** (nicotinamide N-methyltransferase) is a cytosolic S-adenosylmethionine–dependent methyltransferase that converts [[Nicotinamide]] into 1-methylnicotinamide (1-MNA) and S-adenosylhomocysteine. In doing so it withdraws nicotinamide from the [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]] (which requires [[NAMPT]] to recycle nicotinamide into NMN), acting as a sink that limits NAD+ biosynthesis.

## Role in NAD+ Homeostasis and Sirtuin Activity

- NNMT expression and activity strongly influence the intracellular pool of NAD+ precursors; high NNMT flux lowers available nicotinamide for salvage and thus can indirectly restrain [[NAD+]]-dependent enzymes such as the [[Sirtuins]].
- Because all sirtuins require [[NAD+]] as an obligate co-substrate, NNMT is positioned as an upstream modulator of sirtuin network activity through its control of precursor availability.
- Dysregulated NNMT is implicated in metabolic disorders (obesity, metabolic syndrome), multiple cancers, and cardiovascular disease, where it contributes to metabolic reprogramming and altered NAD+ handling.
