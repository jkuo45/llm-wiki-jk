# Titin

**Titin** is the largest protein known, encoded by **TTN**, the largest human gene (~106 exons, up to ~100 kb of transcript). It is a single polypeptide over 1 µm long — up to ~1.3 µm in adult sarcomeres — with a molecular weight in the megadalton range (~3.5 MDa for the full-length primary transcript; cardiac and skeletal isoforms range from ~1 to ~3.5 MDa depending on splice choice). It is the third filament of the sarcomere, running from the Z-disc to the M-band alongside actin (thin filament) and myosin (thick filament).

> [!info] What it physically is
> Titin is a molecular spring. It comprises **244 individually folded immunoglobulin (Ig) and fibronectin-III (FnIII) domains** connected by unstructured, extensible peptide sequences. The folded domains unfold reversibly when the sarcomere is stretched and refold when tension is removed; the unstructured segments act as the entropic spring. This is what gives striated muscle its **passive elasticity** — the resistance to stretch that is most of diastolic filling in the heart and the basis of the afterload-independent component of muscle recoil.

## Isoforms and the elastic region

Alternative splicing of the I-band region produces a nested series of **N2B, N2A and N1 (and N2/N3) titin isoforms**, differing in length of the elastic segment. N2B is the shortest and stiffer, enriched in atria and in fast-twitch muscle; the longer N2BA/N2B-containing splice forms predominate in adult ventricular and skeletal muscle. Because more splice elements means a longer spring, the isoform is a molecular determinant of passive stiffness, and isoform switching is developmentally regulated.

**Titin N2B**, a 116 kDa fragment consisting of the two Ig domains flanking the tandem **tandem-Ig (TIG) segment** plus the N2B sequence, is the canonical molecule for studying elasticity. The tandem-Ig region unfolds under force in a two-step process; binding of **calmodulin** to the N2B PEVK segment reduces passive force generation, and phosphorylation of the PEVK region by protein kinase A (activated by β-adrenergic stimulation) similarly stiffens the molecule — the molecular substrate of the Frank-Starling mechanism.

## Mechanosensing

Titin is not just a passive spring; it is the sarcomere's **stretch sensor** (Linke 2008 review). More than 20 titin ligands bind specific regions:

- **Z-disc:** telethonin, LIM-binding proteins, and signalling adapters.
- **N2B/PEVK:** calmodulin, S100 proteins, and **annexin A5**; the N2B segment is a PKA/PKC/ERK phosphorylation target.
- **M-band:** myomesin, M-protein, and titin-associated kinase signalling scaffolds, notably the M-band kinase signalling complex that includes ERK and p38 MAPK machinery.

Because the anchored titin filament transmits axial force along its whole length, tension in the sarcomere is converted into local signalling at the Z-disc and M-band — the linke lab's "hot spots" model. Anchoring of nuclear and cytoplasmic signalling machinery to titin has been reported, which would make titin a direct conduit from myofibrillar tension to transcription, though the extent of this is contested.

## Disease

Titin is now the most mutated gene in human dilated cardiomyopathy, and its mutations are distributed across the whole molecule. Disease associations:

- **Dilated cardiomyopathy and heart failure.** Mutations in the A-band and elastic I-band regions of TTN are the single largest genetic contributor. Notably, the pathogenic variants are typically truncating, and the *more* severe the truncation the *more* severe the phenotype — consistent with haploinsufficiency rather than dominant-negative poisoning.
- **Titinopathies** as a class, including autosomal recessive congenital myopathies.
- **Vertebral, dental and skeletal developmental defects** from TTN truncation in mice.
- **Long QT / arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy** in some cohorts, and a candidate gene in [[Atrial Fibrillation]] susceptibility.
- Titin expression declines in heart failure and in [[Aging]] and cachexia, and truncated titin fragments are released into the circulation in disease, where they are being explored as biomarkers.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> The functional consequences of a given TTN mutation are not predictable from its position or type alone. Titin's domains fold and unfold cooperatively and its partners are numerous, so genotype–phenotype correlation is weak: identical variants produce variable disease, and the same patient can be asymptomatic or severely affected. Clinical TTN interpretation is still largely genotype-plus-imaging based rather than mechanistically predictive.

## Connections

- [[Cardiomyocytes]] — Titin spans the sarcomere of every striated myocyte and is the largest protein in the cardiac myocyte by mass. It is the substrate on which passive ventricular stiffness and systolic stretch are determined, so it is a direct determinant of diastolic filling in the beating heart.
- [[Sarcomere]] — Titin is one of the three filament systems of the sarcomere (with actin and myosin), anchored at the Z-disc and M-band, and it is the filament that carries the passive mechanical properties. Its Ig/FnIII domain architecture is the structural reason a sarcomere is elastically rather than rigidly extensible.
- [[Heart Failure]] — TTN truncating variants are the commonest genetic cause of dilated cardiomyopathy, and titin expression and structure change as the failing heart remodels, so titin sits at the intersection of the genetic and the acquired disease.
- [[Skeletal Muscle]] — Titin is what makes skeletal muscle elastic, and its isoform and phosphorylation state tune passive stiffness and contraction speed in the same way that myosin heavy-chain isoforms tune kinetics. Titin loss is also a feature of the atrophy programmes in sarcopenia and cachexia.
- [[Sarcopenia]] — Titin is a large, structural, non-regenerating protein, and its loss in disuse and in age-related atrophy is part of why muscle loses both mass and passive elastic function. Titin degradation products also appear in sarcopenia biomarker panels.
- [[Dystrophin]] — Dystrophin sits at the costameric sarcolemma, the mechanical load-bearing interface with the extracellular matrix, titin sits inside the sarcomere. Both are structural proteins whose loss is catastrophic for muscle because the mechanical load path is interrupted rather than because signalling fails.
- [[Calcium]] — Titin elasticity is modulated by calcium binding (calmodulin to the PEVK region), and titin-based stretch in turn modulates calcium release, so titin participates in the mechanical side of excitation-contraction coupling.
- [[Protein Kinase A]] — PKA phosphorylation of titin's PEVK region stiffens the elastic spring, raising the Frank-Starling calcium sensitivity. This is the molecular link between β-adrenergic stimulation and increased stroke volume in the intact heart.
- [[Autophagy]] — Autophagy and the ubiquitin-proteasome system clear damaged and truncated titin in muscle, and impaired clearance of titin aggregates contributes to myofibrillar proteostasis failure in ageing and dystrophy muscle. Titin is one of the specific clients of the muscle proteostasis machinery.
- [[Ubiquitin]] — Titin is subject to proteolytic and ubiquitin-dependent processing, and truncated titin species generated by calpain and by autophagic pathways are a recognised feature of failing muscle.
- [[Atrial Fibrillation]] — TTN variation has been associated with atrial fibrillation susceptibility and with atrial remodelling, an area of active genetic investigation.
Mechanotransduction — Titin is the vault's clearest muscle example of a load-bearing structure doubling as a sensor: force on the sarcomere becomes biochemical signalling at the Z-disc and M-band.
