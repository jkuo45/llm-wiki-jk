# Foam Cells

**Foam cells** are lipid-laden [[Macrophages]] that have accumulated excessive amounts of [[Cholesterol]] and cholesteryl esters, giving them a foamy appearance under microscopy. They are the hallmark of early atherosclerotic lesions and are central to the initiation and progression of [[Atherosclerosis]].

## Formation

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Macrophage cholesterol accumulation and foam cell formation are the hallmarks of early atherogenesis. LDL can be taken up and oxidized by macrophages, resulting in a significant increase in macrophage cholesterol mass. Macrophages can also accumulate cholesterol by increasing biosynthesis and/or decreasing HDL-mediated cholesterol efflux.

Foam cells form when:

1. **LDL uptake**: Macrophages take up [[LDL]] (especially [[Oxidized LDL]]) via scavenger receptors (e.g., SR-A and CD36) that — unlike the classical LDL receptor — are not down-regulated by cellular cholesterol content
2. **Impaired efflux**: [[Cholesterol Efflux]] via [[ABCA1]] and [[ABCG1]] is insufficient to remove excess cholesterol
3. **Cholesterol accumulation**: Free cholesterol and cholesteryl esters accumulate in the cytoplasm, producing the vacuolated "foamy" morphology of fatty streaks

### Glycation as a Driver of LDL Modification

A key driver of LDL modification is **glycation**: under hyperglycemic and pro-oxidative conditions, glucose and dicarbonyls such as [[Methylglyoxal]] form [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]] on ApoB-100, which both oxidize the particle and render it a high-affinity ligand for scavenger receptors. Glycated/oxidized [[LDL]] is therefore more atherogenic than native LDL, tightening the link between metabolic dysfunction and vascular disease.

> [!info] Redox and clearance
> Foam-cell formation is amplified by [[Oxidative Stress]] and impaired [[Autophagy]] (inefficient cholesterol efflux and defective autophagic clearance), linking foam-cell biology to the broader redox and lysosomal networks of aging.

Foam cells eventually undergo necrosis, releasing debris that fuels the necrotic core of advanced plaques, while efferocytosis failure perpetuates inflammation.

## Role in Atherosclerosis

- Foam cells are the predominant cell type in fatty streaks (early atherosclerotic lesions)
- They contribute to plaque inflammation and progression
- They produce pro-inflammatory cytokines via [[NF-κB]] signaling
- [[ABCA1]] and [[ABCG1]] prevent foam cell formation by promoting cholesterol efflux
- Foam-cell necrosis and efferocytosis failure drive necrotic core expansion in advanced plaques

## Prevention

- **[[Cholesterol Efflux]]**: ABCA1 and ABCG1 export cholesterol from macrophages
- **[[HDL]]**: Mediates reverse cholesterol transport
- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Upregulates ABCA1; restores cholesterol efflux under oxidative stress
- **[[Liver X Receptor|LXR]]**: Transcriptionally activates ABCA1 and ABCG1
