## Overview

**B2M** (beta-2 microglobulin, β2-microglobulin) is a small (11.8 kDa) invariant protein that forms the light chain of all [[MHC type I|MHC class I]] molecules, including classical HLA-A, HLA-B, HLA-C and non-classical [[HLA-E]]. It is encoded by the *B2M* gene and is required for proper folding, stability, and surface expression of MHC-I complexes. Free B2M is present in serum and is a clinically measured marker of renal function and, more recently, of [[Senescence|senescence]] burden.

## Structural Role in MHC Class I

B2M associates non-covalently with the heavy chain (α-chain) of MHC class I molecules, providing structural integrity to the peptide-binding groove. Without B2M, MHC-I complexes cannot fold correctly and are retained in the endoplasmic reticulum. Surface expression of MHC-I—and by extension B2M—reports on the overall health of the antigen-presentation machinery to [[Natural Killer Cells]] via inhibitory receptors.

In the context of the [[HLA-E]] / [[NKG2A]] / [[CD94]] immune checkpoint axis, B2M is the light chain that stabilizes HLA-E surface expression. Upregulation of HLA-E (and its B2M subunit) on [[Senescent Cells]] and tumor cells engages NKG2A/CD94 on [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]] and [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]], delivering an inhibitory signal that blunts cytotoxic killing and enables [[Immune Evasion]].

## Senescence Surface Marker and Therapeutic Target

B2M is enriched on the surface of [[Senescent Cells]] and has been exploited as a selective target for antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) in senolytic therapy. Anti-B2M antibodies conjugated to pyrrolobenzodiazepine (PBD) cytotoxic payloads selectively kill senescent human dermal fibroblasts while sparing proliferating cells. This targeting strategy is part of a broader push toward more specific senolytic agents that spare non-senescent tissues.

## Biomarker in Aging

B2M is one of the plasma protein surrogates incorporated into the [[DNAmGrimAge]] epigenetic clock, where DNA methylation patterns are used to estimate B2M levels as a component of the composite mortality predictor. Elevated circulating B2M is associated with [[Inflammaging]], [[Chronic Kidney Disease]], and [[Cardiovascular Disease]].
