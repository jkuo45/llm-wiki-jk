# Crohn's Disease

**Crohn's disease** is a chronic, relapsing form of [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease|IBD]] characterized by transmural, often discontinuous inflammation that can involve any segment of the gastrointestinal tract from mouth to anus. In the cell-death literature it is the clinical disease whose pathology most resembles the spontaneous [[Necroptosis|necroptotic]] intestinal damage produced by loss of the [[FADD]]–[[Caspase-8]]–[[c-FLIP]] complex.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

- **A necroptotic signature in the epithelium.** [[Caspase-8]] or [[FADD]] deletion from intestinal epithelium triggers spontaneous necroptosis with pathologies morphologically similar to inflammatory bowel disease "especially Crohn's disease" (Günther et al., *Nature* 2011; Welz et al., *Nature* 2011). The phenotype is fully prevented on a [[RIPK3]]-deficient background — i.e. it is RIPK3-dependent necroptosis.
- **Immunogenic and barrier consequences.** Necrotic epithelial death releases [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMPs]] and destroys the mucosal barrier, both of which feed mucosal inflammation.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]]
> The 2014 NEJM review uses Crohn's disease as the clinical archetype for spontaneous necroptotic intestinal disease, and stresses that the epithelial pathology of the FADD/caspase-8-conditional mice is "morphologically similar to those seen in inflammatory bowel diseases, especially Crohn's disease."

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

Recurrent abdominal pain, diarrhea, weight loss, fistulae, strictures, and perianal disease; diagnosis combines endoscopy, histology (transmural inflammation, granulomas), and imaging. As with IBD generally, no necroptosis-specific biomarker is clinically established.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Conventional therapy is anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive (aminosalicylates, steroids, immunomodulators, anti-TNF biologics). Preclinical necroptosis evidence points to restoring the FADD–caspase-8–[[c-FLIP]] brake or blocking [[RIPK3]]/[[MLKL]] as an additional strategy to preserve barrier function and limit DAMP-driven inflammation — unproven clinically as of 2014.
