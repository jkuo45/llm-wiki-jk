# Thymopoiesis

**Thymopoiesis** is the multistep process by which bone marrow-derived lymphoid progenitors enter the [[Thymus|thymus]], undergo [[T Cell Receptor|T cell receptor]] gene rearrangement, positive and negative selection, and mature into functional [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]] capable of peripheral immune surveillance.

## Stages of Development

1. **Thymic seeding** — Lymphoid progenitors from [[Bone Marrow|bone marrow]] enter the thymus at the corticomedullary junction
2. **Early cortical development** — Thymocytes are CD4⁻CD8⁻ (double negative, DN) and undergo TCR β-chain rearrangement
3. **Positive selection** — CD4⁺CD8⁺ (double positive, DP) thymocytes in the cortex interact with cortical [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|TECs]]; cells that recognize self-peptide/MHC with adequate affinity survive
4. **Medullary maturation** — Positively selected thymocytes migrate to the medulla
5. **Negative selection** — Thymocytes reactive to self-antigens are eliminated via apoptosis; mediated by medullary TECs expressing tissue-restricted antigens (AIRE-dependent)
6. **Peripheral export** — Mature naïve single-positive (CD4⁺ or CD8⁺) T cells exit the thymus as recent thymic emigrants

## Age-Related Decline

[[Thymic Involution|Thymic involution]] leads to progressive decline in thymopoiesis:

- Decreased thymic cellularity and TCR gene rearrangement capacity
- Reduced output of naïve T cells to the periphery
- Restricted TCR repertoire diversity
- Measurable decline in [[sjTREC]] levels (three-log decline across 80-year human lifespan)

> [!info] Cross-talk requirement
> Thymopoiesis depends on bidirectional signaling between developing thymocytes and the thymic stromal microenvironment, particularly [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|TECs]]. Disruption of this cross-talk with aging contributes to thymic decline.

## Therapeutic Restoration

Strategies to restore thymopoiesis include:

- [[Sex Steroid Ablation]] — Restores early thymic progenitor numbers and thymic architecture
- [[IL-7]] — Promotes survival and differentiation of immature and mature thymocytes
- [[Keratinocyte Growth Factor|KGF]] — Stimulates TEC proliferation and differentiation
- [[IL-22]] — Drives TEC regeneration and thymocyte expansion after injury
- [[Human Growth Hormone|Growth hormone]] — Enhances hematopoiesis and thymic function via [[IGF1|IGF-1]]
- [[Ghrelin]] — Increases early thymic progenitors and recent thymic emigrants
