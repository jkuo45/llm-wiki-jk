# Erythropoiesis

**Erythropoiesis** is the ~120-day marrow production line that turns hematopoietic stem cells into mature [[Erythrocytes]], ending with enucleation and organelle disposal. It is the reason [[Eryptosis]] is shaped the way it is: what is expelled at the end of maturation is what mature red cells can no longer do.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Lineage: HSC → myeloid progenitors → megakaryocyte-erythroid progenitors → BFU-e → CFU-e → proerythroblasts → basophilic/polychromatic/orthochromatic erythroblasts → reticulocytes → mature RBCs. Enucleation is rate-limiting and coordinated by FOXO3, E2F2, miR-30a/miR-191/miR-181a, F-actin/dynein/tropomodulin and p38 MAPK; mitochondria are cleared by mitophagy and lysosomes, peroxisomes, Golgi, ER and ribosomes by autophagy.

## Why the maturation steps shape cell death

- Enucleation removes the genome; organelle clearance removes mitochondria and the ER. Consequently mature RBCs cannot regulate death transcriptionally, translationally or epigenetically — only by adjusting the activity of retained proteins (Fas/FasL, caspase-3/-8, RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL, channels).
- The contrast explains the organelle loss → Ca2+ dominance argument for eryptosis: calcium signalling predates the mitochondrial apoptotic toolkit, so it is what remains when mitochondria do.
- Caspases are non-apoptotic during erythropoiesis (they drive differentiation/maturation), which is why they persist in mature cells without being the primary death executors.
- Erythroid precursors (which still have organelles) can execute full intrinsic/extrinsic [[Apoptosis]], [[Necroptosis]], and [[Ferroptosis]]; only after maturation does the repertoire collapse to eryptosis/erythronecroptosis.

## Clinical coupling

- Steady-state red-cell mass is the balance of production (driven by [[Erythropoietin]]) versus eryptosis and senescence removal; uncompensated eryptosis or suppressed production both produce [[Anemia]].
- [[Erythropoietin]] both stimulates production and (reported) restrains eryptosis; sustained high EPO may raise eryptosis sensitivity.
- In renal disease low EPO plus uremic-toxin-driven eryptosis compound into renal anemia.
