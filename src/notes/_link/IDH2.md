# IDH2

[[IDH2]] (isocitrate dehydrogenase 2, mitochondrial) catalyzes the oxidative decarboxylation of isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate in the mitochondrial matrix, generating [[NADPH]]. This NADPH pool feeds [[Glutathione|glutathione]] regeneration and other antioxidant systems, making IDH2 a key controller of mitochondrial [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] balance.

## SIRT3 regulation

[[SIRT3]] deacetylates and activates [[IDH2]]. During [[Dietary Restriction|dietary restriction (DR)]], hepatic SIRT3 deacetylates both [[SOD2]] and IDH2, reducing ROS levels; IDH2 deacetylation allows regeneration of the antioxidant glutathione ([[_document_ - Sirtuins Guardians of Mammalian Healthspan|Sirtuins: Guardians of Mammalian Healthspan, Giblin et al. 2014]], refs 118, 189, 191–192). In the cancer context, SIRT3-mediated activation of IDH2 (and SOD2) suppresses ROS-driven tumorigenesis and genomic instability.

> [!tip] Redox relay
> SIRT3 → IDH2 → NADPH → glutathione regeneration is a core mitochondrial antioxidant axis, complementing SIRT3 → SOD2.

## Anti-apoptotic role in the sirtuin–caspase axis

Beyond antioxidant defense, SIRT3-driven IDH2 activation is **anti-apoptotic**: by quenching mitochondrial ROS (Ma et al. 2021, cardiomyocytes), IDH2 removes a stimulus for Bax thiol oxidation and mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, delaying [[Cytochrome c]] release and the downstream caspase cascade ([[Sirtuin-Caspase Crosstalk]]). Conversely, SIRT3 loss elevates mitochondrial ROS and sensitizes cells to [[Caspase-3]] activation.
