# APOE3 Christchurch

## Overview

[[APOE3 Christchurch]] is a rare variant of apolipoprotein E3 carrying the R136S substitution, first identified in a cohort from Christchurch, New Zealand. It confers remarkable resilience against [[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]] and dementia even in individuals carrying autosomal-dominant AD mutations and exhibiting high [[Amyloid Beta|amyloid]] pathology. This resilience makes it a uniquely informative natural experiment for dissecting protective versus destructive cascades in [[Neurodegeneration]].

## Neuroprotective Signature

The R136S mutation preserves cognition despite abundant plaques and tangles, suggesting it interrupts the downstream toxic signaling that normally converts protein pathology into neuronal dysfunction. Strikingly, pharmacological [[cGAS]] inhibition phenocopies the protective effects of APOE3 Christchurch in tauopathy models: cGAS inhibitor treatment induces neuronal and microglial transcriptional signatures that closely mirror those of the R136S allele and rescues synapses and network function ([41](#B41)). This provides direct evidence that suppression of [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] signaling is sufficient to recapitulate genetic resilience.

## Mechanistic Contrast with APOE4

APOE3 Christchurch protects by suppressing microglial cGAS/IFN-I signaling while preserving phagocytic capacity — the inverse of the [[APOE4]]/TREM2 R47H axis, which amplifies inflammatory and senescent programs. The variant thus defines a resilience node in which microglia remain functional yet non-inflammatory, offering a template for [[Neuroinflammation]]-modulating therapeutics that uncouple toxicity from clearance.
