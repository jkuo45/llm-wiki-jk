# XBP1

**X-box binding protein 1 (XBP1)** is a transcription factor that plays a crucial role in the [[Unfolded Protein Response|unfolded protein response (UPR)]] and regulates hepatic autophagy by activating [[TFEB]]. Its spliced form, XBP1s, also modulates inflammatory and metabolic gene programs; SIRT6 regulates XBP1 to alleviate endoplasmic reticulum stress in the liver.

## Structure and Function

- XBP1 is a bZIP transcription factor in the unfolded protein response (UPR).
- Its active form (XBP1s) arises from [[IRE1]]-mediated alternative splicing of XBP1 mRNA during [[ER Stress]].
- As a member of the C/EBP family of transcription factors, XBP1 binds to UPRE on its target genes (Zhang et al.).
- XBP1s drives UPR target genes as the principal UPR transcription factor.

## Autophagy Connection

- XBP1s transcriptionally activates autophagy genes including [[Beclin1]] and [[LC3B]].
- XBP1 deficiency impairs autophagy and sensitizes cells to ER stress.
- XBP1 regulates hepatic autophagy by activating [[TFEB]].

## Regulation by SIRT6

SIRT6 alleviates endoplasmic reticulum stress by regulating XBP1, complementing its H3K9 deacetylation at glycolytic promoters to limit inflammatory and metabolic dysfunction in the liver (Szczęsny-Małysiak et al., 2026). SIRT6 thereby connects XBP1 biology to hepatic metabolic homeostasis.
