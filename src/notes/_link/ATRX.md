# ATRX

[[ATRX]] (ATRX chromatin remodeler, also called X-linked helicase 2 / XH2) is an ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler of the SWI/SNF superfamily, encoded on the X chromosome. ATRX forms a complex with [[DAXX]] to deposit the histone variant [[H3.3]] at telomeric and pericentric heterochromatin. It is one of six X-linked genes identified as **EXITS** (Escape from X-Inactivation Tumor Suppressors) — genes that are loss-of-function mutated more frequently in male tumors due to females' biallelic protective dosage (Dunford et al., *Nature Genetics* 2017).

## Structure & Domains

ATRX is a large (~280 kDa) multidomain protein containing:

- **SWI/SNF helicase domain**: ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling activity.
- **ADD (ATRX-DNMT3-DNMT3L) domain**: Binds histone H3 tails specifically when H3 is **unmethylated at Lys9** (H3K4me0), linking ATRX activity to transcriptional silencing at heterochromatic loci.
- **HP1-interacting region**: Mediates association with heterochromatin protein 1 (HP1) at pericentric repeats and telomeres.
- **Nuclear localization signals**: Constitutively nuclear; enriched at DAPI-bright heterochromatic regions.

## Chromatin Remodeling Function

ATRX cooperates with the histone chaperone [[DAXX]] to assemble the histone variant [[H3.3]] into chromatin at:

- **Telomeres** (independent of replication)
- **Pericentric and centromeric heterochromatin**
- **Endogenous retroviruses** and other repetitive elements
- **Imprinted loci** (e.g., H19/IGF2)

The ATRX–DAXX–H3.3 axis maintains heterochromatic silencing of repetitive elements and proper telomere structure. Loss of ATRX leads to telomere dysfunction and activation of the alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT) pathway — a recombination-based telomere maintenance mechanism observed in ~10–15% of cancers.

## Role in Cancer

ATRX is one of the most frequently mutated chromatin remodelers in cancer:

- **Gliomas / Glioblastoma**: ATRX mutations occur in ~30% of adult diffuse gliomas (IDH-mutant, 1p19q non-codeleted); ATRX loss is a diagnostic marker distinguishing astrocytoma from oligodendroglioma.
- **Pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors**: ATRX mutations in ~45%.
- **Neuroblastoma**: ATRX mutations in ~10–20% of high-risk cases.
- **Osteosarcoma, endometrial cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma**: Recurrently mutated.

> [!important]
> ATRX loss → ALT activation: In tumors, ATRX loss-of-function triggers the Alternative Lengthening of Telomeres pathway, allowing cancer cells to maintain telomeres without telomerase. ALT is associated with more aggressive tumor behavior and distinct clinical trajectories.

### EXITS Framework

ATRX is one of six X-linked genes (with [[CNKSR2]], [[DDX3X]], [[KDM5C]], [[KDM6A]], [[MAGEC3]], see [[EXITS]]) that are preferentially loss-of-function mutated in male tumors. Across **21 tumor types**, these six X-linked genes harbor loss-of-function mutations significantly more often in males — a sex-biased pattern not seen in any of the **18,053 autosomal genes** examined (Dunford et al., *Nature Genetics* 2017). In females, biallelic expression (escape from X-inactivation) provides a functional buffer, requiring **two hits** (double mutation or mutation + whole-chrX loss); males, with only one X chromosome, lack this protection and are more vulnerable to **single-hit** inactivation.

> [!important]
> ATRX escape is **context-dependent**: unlike KDM6A/DDX3X/KDM5C (multi-tissue escape genes), ATRX shows strong evidence of escape chiefly in **female brain** (heterogeneous, tissue-specific escape), matching its male-biased mutation enrichment being largely confined to lower-grade gliomas.

## Role in Telomere Biology

ATRX is directly relevant to [[Telomere]] biology:

- ATRX deposits [[H3.3]] at telomeres via [[DAXX]], maintaining telomeric chromatin integrity.
- ATRX loss causes telomere decondensation, replication stress, and DNA damage at telomeric repeat sequences.
- ALT activation upon ATRX loss involves homologous recombination between telomeric sequences — a process that is template-directed rather than telomerase-mediated.

## Sex Differences

> [!tip]
> ATRX is an X-linked tumor suppressor with sex-biased mutation patterns.

- ATRX loss-of-function mutations are **enriched in male tumors** across multiple cancer types, consistent with its X-linked location and the EXITS model.
- Females' biallelic expression of ATRX provides functional redundancy; a single mutation is insufficient to inactivate the pathway.
- Males, hemizygous for ATRX, require only one loss-of-function event to lose ATRX-mediated tumor suppression.
