# Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome

**Overview:** [[Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome]] ([[ARDS]]) is a syndrome of acute, diffuse lung injury causing increased pulmonary vascular permeability, edema, and refractory hypoxemic respiratory failure. It is not a single disease but a clinical pattern triggered by pulmonary or systemic insults.

**Etiology and Pathophysiology:** Common triggers include sepsis, pneumonia, aspiration, trauma, pancreatitis, and severe [[COVID-19]]. Damage to alveolar endothelium and epithelium releases [[Pro-inflammatory Cytokines]] and activates [[Neutrophils]], producing diffuse alveolar damage, hyaline membranes, and surfactant loss. Shunt physiology (perfusion of non-ventilated lung) drives [[Hypoxia]] unresponsive to supplemental oxygen.

**Clinical Presentation:** Patients develop dyspnea, tachypnea, and bilateral infiltrates on imaging within hours to days of the inciting event, with PaO₂/FiO₂ ratios defining severity (Berlin criteria). [[Respiratory Distress]] and low lung compliance are typical.

**Therapeutic Landscape:** Supportive care is paramount—lung-protective mechanical ventilation, prone positioning, and treating the underlying cause. [[Methylene blue]] has been proposed (on the basis of antioxidant/anti-inflammatory effects) for [[COVID-19]] and ARDS, but clinical efficacy is unestablished and it is not FDA-approved for this indication; it remains investigational.

**Prognosis:** Survivors may have persistent fibrosis and cognitive deficits. Mortality remains substantial in severe cases.
