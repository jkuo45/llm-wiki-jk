# Clofarabine

**Clofarabine** (Clolar/Evoltra; 2-chloro-9-(2-deoxy-2-fluoro-β-D-arabinofuranosyl)adenine) is a second-generation **purine nucleoside antimetabolite** and RNR-targeting chemotherapy. It received FDA accelerated approval in **December 2004** for pediatric patients (ages 1–21) with relapsed or refractory **acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL)** after at least two prior regimens — the first new drug approved for pediatric leukemia in more than a decade, and the only oncology drug approved for pediatric use before adult use. It acts as a triple threat on DNA metabolism: inhibiting [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]], poisoning [[DNA Polymerase]]s, and incorporating into DNA to terminate chain elongation.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- Deoxyadenosine analog (2-Cl, 2′-F, arabino-configuration); the 2-fluoro sugar renders it resistant to adenosine deaminase and acid hydrolysis (more stable than fludarabine/cladribine).
- Molecular formula C₁₀H₁₁ClFN₅O₃, MW ≈ 303.7; CAS 123318-82-1.

## Mechanism of Action

1. **Prodrug activation**: intracellular phosphorylation by deoxycytidine kinase to the monophosphate, then by nucleotide kinases to the active **clofarabine triphosphate** (cf-TP).
2. **Ribonucleotide reductase inhibition**: clofarabine **diphosphate** inhibits [[Ribonucleotide Reductase]] (IC₅₀ ≈ 65 nM), depleting [[dNTP]] pools and further starving DNA synthesis — the direct RNR link relevant to this vault.
3. **DNA polymerase inhibition & chain termination**: cf-TP competitively inhibits DNA polymerases α, β, ε and is incorporated into DNA, terminating chain elongation and interfering with [[DNA Repair]].
4. **Mitochondrial apoptosis**: cf-TP disrupts mitochondrial membrane integrity, releasing cytochrome c and AIF → [[Apoptosis]].

## Pharmacokinetics & Indications

- **Approved indication**: pediatric relapsed/refractory ALL (1–21 y) after ≥2 prior regimens; typical dosing 52 mg/m² IV over 2 h × 5 days per cycle.
- Distribution/clearance: ~47% plasma-protein bound; elimination half-life ~5 h; 50–60% renal excretion. Studies extend to adult acute leukemias (AML) but adult AML did not gain FDA approval (2009 complete-response letter).
- **Toxicities**: myelosuppression, febrile neutropenia, hepatotoxicity (including hepatic veno-occlusive disease risk),hepatorenal syndrome, rash/cytokine-release (SIRS-like) reactions, requiring careful monitoring.
