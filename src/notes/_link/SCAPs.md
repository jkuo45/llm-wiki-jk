### Overview

SCAPs (senescence-cell anti-apoptotic pathways) are the ensemble of pro-survival mechanisms that allow [[Senescent Cells]] to persist in tissues despite expressing a damaging [[SASP]] (senescence-associated secretory phenotype) and harboring DNA damage. Rather than dying, senescent cells raise their apoptotic threshold through coordinated upregulation of anti-death signals. This network is what distinguishes a stably arrested senescent cell from a dying or normally cycling cell, and it converts a transient cell-cycle arrest into a durable, long-lived cellular state.

### Mechanism / Structure

SCAPs are not a single molecule but an integrated network built from several redundant arms:

- **BCL-2 family proteins** — anti-apoptotic members such as [[Bcl-2]], BCL-XL, and MCL-1 buffer mitochondrial outer-membrane permeabilization. They restrain [[Minority MOMP]] and block the irreversible commitment to [[Apoptosis]].
- **PI3K/Akt survival signaling** — growth-factor and integrin inputs sustain Akt activation, which phosphorylates and inhibits pro-apoptotic effectors (e.g., BAD, FOXO) and promotes metabolic fitness.
- **Additional survival cues** — senescent cells co-opt stress-response transcription, survival kinase networks, and altered metabolism to further tilt the balance away from death.

> [!info] The death threshold is shifted, not removed
> Senescent cells remain primed for death relative to normal cells — their survival depends on continuously active SCAPs. This is precisely why disabling SCAPs selectively kills them: the latent apoptotic pressure is released and the cells cross the threshold.

### Role in Senescence

SCAPs are engaged and reinforced by the senescent program itself. [[Therapy-Induced Senescence]] and other senescence triggers induce both the arrest and the protective pro-survival response, creating a cell that is simultaneously arrested and resistant to clearance. By buffering death signals, SCAPs permit long-term persistence of cells that would otherwise be eliminated, contributing to aging phenotypes and therapy side effects.

### Clinical & Research Significance

The existence of SCAPs is the mechanistic foundation of [[Senolytic Therapy]]. [[Senolytics]] work by transiently disabling specific SCAP components, pushing the vulnerable senescent cell across the apoptotic threshold while sparing most healthy cells that do not depend on those same survival nodes:

- **Navitoclax** — inhibits BCL-2/BCL-XL, directly attacking the BCL-2 arm of SCAPs.
- **Dasatinib + Quercetin (D+Q)** — combination regimens that disrupt survival signaling across multiple SCAP arms.

The strategy is intermittent, selective clearance rather than continuous suppression, which is intended to minimize toxicity. SCAPs thus represent both the explanation for senescent-cell persistence and the Achilles' heel exploited by senolytic drugs.
