# NADPH

**NADPH** (reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate) is the dominant **reducing equivalent** in biosynthetic and antioxidant metabolism. In ferroptosis defense it plays a dual role: it is the **electron donor for FSP1**, driving reduction of ubiquinone to the radical-trapping ubiquinol, and it **recycles oxidized glutathione (GSSG) back to GSH** via glutathione reductase, sustaining the parallel [[GPX4]] axis. Cellular NADPH availability therefore sets the capacity of *both* ferroptosis-suppression systems.

## Biochemical Role

NADPH provides reducing power for:

- **Lipid biosynthesis** (fatty acid, cholesterol, and isoprenoid synthesis via the [[Mevalonate pathway]]).
- **Antioxidant recycling** — glutathione reductase (GSSG → 2 GSH) and thioredoxin reductase.
- **FSP1-catalyzed ubiquinone reduction** at the plasma membrane.

NADPH is primarily generated by the **[[Pentose Phosphate Pathway]]** (glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase / 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase), malic enzyme, and folate metabolism, and is kept distinct from the NADH pool used mainly for mitochondrial ATP production.

## Role in Ferroptosis Defense

### FSP1 electron donor

In the **FSP1–CoQ10–NAD(P)H pathway**, NADPH (or NADH) directly reduces ubiquinone:

> **Ubiquinone + NAD(P)H + H⁺ → Ubiquinol + NAD(P)⁺** (FSP1)

Without a supply of NAD(P)H, FSP1 cannot regenerate ubiquinol, and the plasma-membrane radical trap collapses, sensitizing cells to [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and [[Ferroptosis]].

### Glutathione axis support

Separately, NADPH feeds **glutathione reductase**, maintaining the GSH pool that [[GPX4]] requires to reduce phospholipid hydroperoxides. Thus NAD(P)H availability is a shared determinant of the two-pillar ferroptosis defense.

> [!info] NADPH as a ferroptosis-sensitivity biomarker
> NAD(P)H levels (e.g., measured by fluorescence lifetime or endogenous NAD(P)H imaging) have been proposed as a biomarker of ferroptosis sensitivity across cancer cell lines, reflecting the reductive capacity available to both the FSP1 and GPX4 axes.

## Physiological & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer ferroptosis sensitivity:** Tumor cells with high NADPH-generating flux can resist ferroptosis via both axes; agents that drain NADPH (e.g., via AMPK/ACC modulation) can sensitize them.
- **Metabolic coupling:** The pentose phosphate pathway and one-carbon metabolism are upstream of NADPH and therefore indirectly gate FSP1/GPX4 function.
- **MVA/CoQ10 convergence:** NAD(P)H reduction of ubiquinone is only effective when ubiquinone is supplied by the [[Mevalonate pathway]]; loss of CoQ10 (statins, squalene-synthase engagement) converges on FSP1 regardless of NADPH abundance.
