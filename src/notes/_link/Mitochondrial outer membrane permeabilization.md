# Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization

**Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP)** is a pivotal event in the intrinsic (mitochondrial) pathway of [[Apoptosis]]. It is regulated by the [[Bcl-2]] family of proteins, where pro-apoptotic members like [[BAX]] and [[BAK]] form pores in the outer mitochondrial membrane. Full, synchronized MOMP commits a cell to death; partial or "minority" MOMP (miMOMP) can instead push a cell into a senescent, pro-inflammatory state.

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Pore assembly**: BH3-only proteins activate BAX and dislodge BAK from anti-apoptotic restraint, allowing BAX/BAK oligomerization into lipidic pores.
- **Full MOMP**: Uniform permeabilization releases the bulk of [[Cytochrome c]] and other intermembrane proteins, nucleating the [[Apoptosome]] and activating [[Caspase-9]]/[[Caspase-3]] for apoptosis.
- **mtDNA release**: High-order macropores additionally allow inner-membrane herniation and cytosolic [[mtDNA]] release.
- **Sublethal MOMP**: When only a minority of mitochondria are permeabilized, released factors fall below the apoptotic threshold, leaving the cell viable but signaling through the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] axis.

## Role in Senescence

- MOMP acts as a switch between [[Apoptosis]] and [[Senescence]]: the outcome depends on the extent and reversibility of pore formation.
- Sublethal MOMP releases [[mtDNA]] and other mitochondrial DAMPs into the cytosol without triggering full apoptosis, activating cGAS–STING and driving the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]].
- Because [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways|SCAPs]] restrain BAX/BAK, many senescent cells live in a state of chronic, low-level MOMP.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- MOMP regulators (BCL-2 family) are major oncology targets; BH3 mimetics exploit the all-or-nothing nature of full MOMP.
- Failure of MOMP underpins the survival of [[Cancer]] cells and their resistance to [[Chemotherapy]] and radiotherapy, since many cytotoxic agents act upstream by triggering mitochondrial apoptosis. Conversely, excessive or mistimed MOMP drives degenerative and ischemic injury.
- BH3 mimetics such as venetoclax (targeting Bcl-2) push cells toward MOMP, and agents that activate Bax/Bak or disable Mcl-1 are in development. Quantifying MOMP (e.g., by cytochrome c release or caspase activation assays) informs prognostic and pharmacodynamic assessment.
- The miMOMP–cGAS–STING link explains SASP-driven [[Inflammaging]] and motivates [[Senolytics]] and cGAS–STING inhibitors for age-related disease.
