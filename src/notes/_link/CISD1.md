# CISD1

**CISD1**, also called **mitoNEET**, is a mitochondrial outer-membrane protein bearing a CDGSH-type iron-sulfur cluster. It is a negative regulator of [[Ferroptosis]] in several cancers (notably [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] and liver cancer cells), where it protects mitochondria from iron-dependent injury by buffering mitochondrial labile iron and limiting [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]]-driven [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

## Structure & Biochemistry

CISD1 is a [2Fe-2S] cluster-containing protein with a single transmembrane anchor at the mitochondrial outer membrane and a cytosolic cluster-binding domain. Its labile cluster can exchange iron, positioning it as a mitochondrial iron sensor/handler.

## Mechanism & Ferroptosis Relevance

By sequestering and regulating mitochondrial iron, CISD1 reduces the labile Fe²⁺ available to drive the [[Fenton Reaction]] within mitochondria, thereby protecting mitochondrial membranes from lipid peroxidation. The review lists CISD1 among negative regulators of ferroptosis in HCC—alongside [[NRF2]], metallothionein-1G, and [[p53]]—that inhibit ferroptosis in liver cancer cells. Loss or inhibition of CISD1 increases mitochondrial iron and lipid ROS, sensitizing cells to ferroptosis (a role mirrored by its paralog [[CISD2]] in [[Head and neck cancer]]).

> [!info] Mitochondrial iron gatekeeper
> CISD1 is the mitochondrial counterpart to cytosolic iron buffers; its protective effect explains why liver cancers with high CISD1 resist ferroptosis and why targeting it sensitizes tumors.

## Clinical Relevance

CISD1 is a ferroptosis-resistance factor and potential therapeutic target in HCC and other CISD1-high tumors. Countering CISD1 could enhance ferroptosis-based therapy; its paralog CISD2 is similarly relevant in HNC.
