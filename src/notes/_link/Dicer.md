# Dicer

**Dicer** is a cytoplasmic [[Ribonuclease III|RNase III]] enzyme that performs the second cleavage step of small-RNA biogenesis. It processes precursor miRNA hairpins (pre-miRNAs, exported from the nucleus by [[Exportin-5]]) and long double-stranded RNA into ~21–23 nt RNA duplexes, which are then loaded onto **[[Argonaute]]** proteins in the [[RISC]].

## Mechanism

- Pre-miRNA (~60–70 nt hairpin) is recognized and cleaved by Dicer's RNase III domains to release a miRNA duplex with characteristic 2-nt 3′ overhangs.
- In *Drosophila*, **Dicer-1** acts in the miRNA pathway and **Dicer-2** in the siRNA pathway; in mammals a single Dicer handles both.
- Loss of Dicer causes pre-miRNA accumulation and mature miRNA loss — but, unlike [[DGCR8]]/[[Drosha]] knockout, Dicer-null cells also lack siRNAs, producing a more severe, miRNA-independent block to differentiation (e.g., in embryonic stem cells).

> [!info] Dicer vs. Microprocessor
> The [[Microprocessor complex|Microprocessor]] ([[Drosha]] + [[DGCR8]]) acts in the nucleus on pri-miRNA; Dicer acts in the cytoplasm on pre-miRNA. DGCR8 knockout is a cleaner tool to study miRNA-specific functions because Dicer also processes siRNAs.

## Pathological relevance

Dicer and downstream miRNAs regulate inflammatory responses and are implicated in [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis|IPF]], cancer, and [[Senescence|senescence]]. Reduced mature miRNA output can derepress pro-inflammatory and senescence gene programs that contribute to [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]].
