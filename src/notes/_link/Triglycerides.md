# Triglycerides

**Triglycerides** (triacylglycerols, TAGs) are esters of three fatty acids with glycerol. They are chemically inert, osmotically inert, and energy-dense, which makes them the dominant storage form of lipid in vertebrates and the dominant fat in the human diet. Despite this abundance they are metabolically inert until lipolytic enzymes — chiefly [[ATGL]] — cleave them to free fatty acids.

## Structure & Classification

A triacylglycerol is a glycerol backbone (sn-1, sn-2, sn-3) esterified to three fatty acid chains that may be saturated, monounsaturated, or polyunsaturated. The sn-2 position is preferentially occupied by polyunsaturated fatty acids in both dietary and endogenous TAG. Chemically simple ("simple") TAGs contain three identical chains; mixed TAGs do not. Isotopic tracing shows most tissue TAG is synthesized locally rather than absorbed intact, whereas dietary TAG is absorbed intact after pancreatic lipase hydrolysis and re-esterification in enterocytes.

## Biosynthesis & Metabolism

TAG synthesis proceeds by the glycerol-3-phosphate pathway: acyl-CoA:glycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase (GPAT) adds the first acyl chain, 1-acylglycerol-3-phosphate acyltransferase (AGPAT) the second, and phosphatidic acid phosphatase (lipin-1) the third, releasing free fatty acids from [[Acetyl-CoA]] and [[Fatty acid|fatty acids]].

Dietary fat is packaged in chylomicrons, enter the circulation via the lymphatics, and are hydrolysed by lipoprotein lipase (LPL) anchored to capillary endothelium; the released fatty acids are taken up by muscle and [[Adipose Tissue]], re-esterified into TAG, and the chylomicron remnant is cleared hepatically. Endogenous TAG is synthesized in the [[Liver]] and secreted as [[Very Low Density Lipoprotein|VLDL]], which is itself lipolysed by LPL to yield remnant particles that return to the liver.

Storage occurs in adipocytes as [[Lipid Droplet]]-bound TAG; the adipose pool is the body's dominant energy reserve (roughly 100–300 kJ/kg of body mass in adults). Mobilisation is via [[Lipolysis]], which is hormonally gated: fasting, exercise, and catecholamines drive it through β-adrenergic signalling, while [[Insulin]] suppresses it by activating phosphodiesterase and lowering cAMP.

## Physiological Function

- Dense energy storage with minimal water and no osmotic penalty.
- Insulation and mechanical padding of viscera and subcutaneous fat.
- Long-chain fatty acids released by lipolysis are oxidised in muscle and heart, and re-esterified in [[Liver]]; excess delivery to [[Liver]] drives [[Non-alcoholic Fatty Liver Disease|hepatic steatosis]].
- Dietary TAG quality (chain length, saturation) influences membrane lipid composition and the profile of lipid mediators derived from arachidonic and omega-3 fatty acids.
- TAG carried in [[LDL]] and remnant particles is a direct driver of [[Atherosclerosis]], independent of cholesterol.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Hypertriglyceridemia**: Fasting TG above ~1.7 mmol/L is common, frequently secondary to [[Insulin Resistance]], [[Type 2 Diabetes]], obesity, or alcohol. TG-rich remnant particles are a causal atherosclerosis risk factor.
- **Acute pancreatitis**: TG above ~11.3 mmol/L (1000 mg/dL) risks hypertriglyceridaemic pancreatitis, in which triglyceride lipase hydrolyses excess luminal TAG, releasing fatty acids and free radicals that injure the pancreas — a direct clinical link between [[Pancreatitis]] and adipose lipid flux.
- **Familial hyperchylomicronemia** (LPL or apolipoprotein C-II deficiency) causes severe chylomicronaemia, eruptive xanthomas, and pancreatitis in childhood.
- **Ageing**: adipose TAG storage capacity declines, so fat is redistributed from subcutaneous depots to visceral and ectopic sites (liver, muscle, bone marrow) — a driver of the metabolic phenotype of ageing and of lipotoxicity.
- **Senescence**: senescent adipocytes accumulate TAG and secrete a pro-inflammatory secretome, and senescent cells themselves shift toward a lipid-laden phenotype; both expand the pool of fatty acids available for re-esterification into [[Ceramide]] and damaging lipid species.
