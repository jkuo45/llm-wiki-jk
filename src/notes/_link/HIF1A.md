# HIF1A

HIF1A (Hypoxia-Inducible Factor 1 Subunit Alpha) is a stress-responsive transcription factor that mediates the cellular response to [[Hypoxia|low oxygen levels]] and mitochondrial stress. It dimerizes with [[ARNT]]/HIF1β to form the active HIF-1 complex, which binds Hypoxia Response Elements (HREs) in target gene promoters. It regulates genes involved in glycolysis, angiogenesis, cell survival, and mitophagy.

## Regulation

Under normoxia, HIF1A is hydroxylated by PHD (prolyl hydroxylase) enzymes on key proline residues (Pro402, Pro564), which targets it for [[VHL]]-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation. Under [[Hypoxia]], PHD activity is suppressed (they require O₂ as substrate), allowing HIF1A to accumulate, translocate to the nucleus, and dimerize with [[ARNT]].

## Dimerization and DNA Binding

Upon stabilization, HIF1A translocates to the nucleus where it dimerizes with [[ARNT]] (HIF1β) via their PAS domains. The HIF-1α/ARNT heterodimer binds to Hypoxia Response Elements (HREs) containing the conserved consensus sequence 5'-RCGTG-3' in the promoters of target genes. Transcriptional coactivators such as [[p300]]/[[CBP]] are recruited to drive transactivation.

## Autophagy and Mitophagy Connection

HIF1A induces [[Autophagy]] under hypoxia through [[BNIP3]] and [[BNIP3L]]/NIX-mediated displacement of [[Beclin1]] from BCL-2. HIF1A also activates [[REDD1]] to inhibit [[mTORC1]].

The HIF-1α/[[ARNT]] dimer directly binds HREs in the [[BNIP3]] and [[BNIP3L]] promoters. Upon stabilization:
1. HIF1α dimerizes with [[ARNT]]/HIF1β
2. The dimer binds HREs in BNIP3/BNIP3L promoters
3. BNIP3/BNIP3L proteins insert into the outer mitochondrial membrane
4. Their [[LIR Motif|LIR motifs]] recruit [[LC3]]/[[GABARAP]]-family proteins on the phagophore
5. The mitochondrion is engulfed and degraded by [[Mitophagy]]

## NAD+ Connection

[[NAD+ Biosynthesis]] and HIF1A share two common network neighbors: [[Autophagy]] and [[Mitohormesis]]. NAD+-dependent [[SIRT1]] modulates autophagy machinery (via [[FOXO3a]], [[TFEB]], [[Atg5]], [[Atg7]], [[LC3]]), while NAD+-dependent [[SIRT3]] regulates mitochondrial redox balance to influence [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] signaling, which in turn stabilizes HIF1A. This places HIF1A at the intersection of metabolic (NAD+) and oxygen-sensing pathways.
