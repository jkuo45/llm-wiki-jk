# Cristae

## Overview

Cristae are the characteristic infoldings of the [[Inner Mitochondrial Membrane]] that dramatically increase its surface area and house the [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] (OXPHOS) complexes. They represent the primary site of ATP synthesis and are essential for efficient cellular energy production.

## Structure and Organization

Cristae architecture is maintained by several key components:

- **MICOS complex** — The mitochondrial contact site and cristae organizing system (MICOS) is essential for cristae formation and maintenance, connecting inner and outer membranes at cristae junctions
- **[[Cardiolipin]]** — This unique mitochondrial phospholipid is vital for cristae assembly and stability, contributing to the curvature and integrity of inner membrane folds
- **[[OPA1]]** — Dynamin-related GTPase that regulates cristae morphology and inner membrane fusion; OPA1 oligomers maintain cristae junction tightness

## Physiological Function

Cristae serve multiple essential functions:

- **Energy production** — House the electron transport chain complexes (I-IV) and ATP synthase (complex V) for [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]]
- **Metabolite processing** — Provide a confined space for TCA cycle intermediates and metabolic reactions
- **Calcium buffering** — Contribute to mitochondrial calcium sequestration
- **Apoptosis regulation** — Cristae remodeling releases [[Cytochrome c]] during programmed cell death

## Pathology

Disruption of cristae integrity is implicated in multiple disease states:

- **[[Heart Failure]]** — Cristae disorganization in cardiomyocytes impairs ATP production and contributes to cardiac dysfunction
- **[[Alzheimer's Disease]]** — Cristae damage in neurons leads to mitochondrial dysfunction and energy deficits
- **Neurodegeneration** — Cristae disruption is a common feature across multiple neurodegenerative conditions
