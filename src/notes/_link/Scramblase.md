# Scramblase

**Scramblases** are membrane proteins that bidirectionally and ATP-independently randomize phospholipids across the bilayer. The calcium-activated scramblase TMEM16F (ANO6) and the phospholipid scramblase (PLSCR) family externalize phosphatidylserine (PS) from the inner to the outer leaflet — the Annexin V–positive "eat-me" signal. In [[Eryptosis]], Ca2+ influx activates scramblases (while inhibiting flippases), driving the PS exposure that marks shrunken erythrocytes for splenic clearance; the same PS logic operates in [[Apoptosis]] via XKR-family scramblases downstream of caspases.

## Mechanism

Resting asymmetry (PS inward) is maintained by flippases; Ca2+ elevation flips the balance — scramblase on, flippase off — collapsing asymmetry within minutes. In erythrocytes this couples to calpain activation and cell shrinkage; in nucleated cells caspase-cleaved XKR8 performs the analogous role.

## Detection and relevance

PS externalization is read out by [[Annexin V]] binding. Scramblase activity sits at the junction of regulated death ([[Eryptosis]], [[Apoptosis]]), coagulation (PS-positive procoagulant surfaces), and clearance biology.
