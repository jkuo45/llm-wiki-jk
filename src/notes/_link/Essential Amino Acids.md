# Essential Amino Acids

**Essential amino acids (EAAs)**, also called indispensable amino acids, are the nine amino acids that cannot be synthesized de novo in humans and must be obtained from the diet: [[Histidine]], [[Isoleucine]], [[Leucine]], [[Lysine]], [[Methionine]], [[Phenylalanine]], [[Threonine]], [[Tryptophan]], and [[Valine]].

## Overview

Non-essential amino acids (NEAAs) can be synthesized endogenously, whereas EAAs must be supplied through dietary protein. Individual EAAs serve as substrates for [[Protein Synthesis]] and also act as metabolic signals. Their circulating concentrations are sensed by dedicated amino-acid-sensing pathways — most notably the [[GCN2]]–[[eIF2α]]–[[ATF4]] axis (activated by EAA depletion) and [[mTORC1]] (activated by [[Leucine]]) — coupling dietary amino-acid supply to cellular metabolism and growth.

## Dietary Restriction of Individual EAAs

Individual EAA restriction recapitulates many hallmarks of [[Protein Restriction]]:

- Restriction of the [[Branched-Chain Amino Acids]] ([[Isoleucine]], [[Valine]], and, to a lesser extent, [[Leucine]]) improves metabolic health and extends lifespan in rodents; isoleucine and valine restriction act partly through amino-acid sensing and metabolic remodeling rather than mTORC1.
- [[Methionine Restriction]] extends lifespan, improves mitochondrial function, and reduces [[Frailty]], partly through [[FGF21]] and methyl-donor effects.
- Leucine restriction acts primarily through reduced [[mTORC1]] signaling.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - The-hallmarks-of-protein-and-amino-acid-restriction|The Hallmarks of Protein and Amino Acid Restriction]]
> A hallmark-based framework attributes much of the protein-restriction response to restriction of specific EAAs — methionine and the branched-chain amino acids are the primary drivers. In humans, low-protein and low-EAA diets reduce body weight and improve metabolic markers, though individual EAA restriction in humans is less studied.
