# Kupffer Cell

**Kupffer cells** are the resident macrophages of the hepatic sinusoids. They are the liver's clearance arm for red cells: aged and PS-damaged erythrocytes are sequestered in the hepatic sinusoid and engulfed into erythrophagolysosomes, and roughly 80% of circulating RBC-derived vesicles are removed within 5 minutes via scavenger receptors.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> In pathological conditions the liver — not the spleen — is the primary site of erythrocyte clearance. Sinusoidal endothelial cells recognize PS-exposed RBCs via stabilin-1 and stabilin-2 and hand them to Kupffer cells. Because eryptotic RBCs arrive with externalized [[Phosphatidylserine]], Kupffer-cell uptake drives PS-driven macrophage reprogramming (antiinflammatory, [[HO-1]]-inducing) rather than a DAMP-driven inflammatory burst.

## Role in red-cell homeostasis

- Clearance of eryptotic and senescent erythrocytes and of RBC microvesicles/exosomes; iron from phagocytosed hemoglobin is recycled by the liver.
- Uptake threshold is set by the balance of PS "eat-me" and [[CD47]]–SIRPα "don't-eat-me" signals, so Kupffer cells clear cells whose asymmetry has flipped rather than healthy erythrocytes.
- Fast RBC-derived vesicle removal implies the liver is also a sink for the DAMP-bearing EVs that eryptosis promotes (EV-associated DAMPs are found in stored red-cell concentrates).
