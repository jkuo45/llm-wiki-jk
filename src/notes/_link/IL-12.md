# IL-12

**Interleukin-12 (IL-12)** is a heterodimeric pro-inflammatory cytokine that plays a pivotal role in bridging innate and adaptive immunity. It is primarily produced by activated [[Macrophages]] and [[Dendritic Cells]] in response to intracellular pathogens. The bioactive form, **IL-12p70**, is composed of two subunits: **p35** (encoded by *IL12A*) and **p40** (encoded by *IL12B*).

## Structure & Receptor

IL-12p70 is a disulfide-linked heterodimer of p35 and p40 subunits. The p40 subunit is shared with **IL-23** (as p40/p19), creating a regulatory relationship between the two cytokines. IL-12 signals through a receptor composed of **IL-12Rβ1** and **IL-12Rβ2**, which are expressed primarily on T cells and [[Natural Killer Cells]].

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

IL-12 binding activates the **JAK2/TYK2** kinase complex, leading to phosphorylation and activation of **STAT3** and **STAT4**. STAT4 activation induces **IFNγ** production in T cells and NK cells, which is the defining effector function of the IL-12 axis. IL-12 also promotes **T-bet** expression, reinforcing [[Th1]] lineage commitment.

### Downstream Effects

- Potent inducer of **IFNγ** production from [[Th1]] cells and [[Natural Killer Cells]].
- Promotes cytotoxic activity of [[CD8+ T Cells]] and NK cells.
- Enhances MHC class II expression and antigen presentation.
- Drives differentiation of naïve [[CD4+ T Cells]] toward the [[Th1]] phenotype.

## Biological Functions

### Cell-Mediated Immunity
IL-12 is essential for host defense against intracellular pathogens including *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *Leishmania*, and *Toxoplasma gondii*. It activates macrophage killing mechanisms via IFNγ-dependent pathways.

### Th1 Polarization
IL-12 is the primary cytokine driving Th1 differentiation, inducing [[T-bet]] expression and IFNγ production while suppressing [[Th2]] and [[Th17]] differentiation.

### NK Cell Activation
IL-12 activates NK cells to produce IFNγ and enhance cytotoxicity, providing an early innate defense before adaptive immunity develops.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **Immunodeficiency**: Mutations in *IL12B* or *IL12RB1* cause Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial diseases (MSMD), characterized by severe infections with weakly virulent mycobacteria.
- **Autoimmune Disease**: Excessive IL-12 signaling is implicated in Crohn's disease, psoriasis, and multiple sclerosis. The anti-IL-12/IL-23 antibody **ustekinumab** (targets p40) is approved for these conditions.
- **Cancer**: IL-12 has anti-tumor properties through IFNγ-mediated immune activation, but systemic administration causes dose-limiting toxicity. Engineered IL-12 variants are in clinical development.
- **Infection**: IL-12 is critical for controlling intracellular infections but can contribute to immunopathology if overproduced.
