# Muscular Dystrophies (MD)

**Muscular dystrophies (MD)** are a heterogeneous group of inherited [[Neuromuscular Diseases|neuromuscular disorders]] defined by progressive [[Muscle Weakness|skeletal muscle weakness]] and degeneration. Major forms include Duchenne (DMD), Becker (BMD), myotonic, facioscapulohumeral, and limb-girdle dystrophies, typically caused by mutations in structural proteins (e.g., [[Dystrophin]], sarcoglycans).

## Pathophysiology

Defective sarcolemmal integrity leads to repeated muscle fiber necrosis, chronic [[Inflammation]], fibro-fatty replacement, and declining force production. The [[Muscle]] is the primary affected tissue, making it a direct target for anabolic/energy-supportive interventions.

## Creatine and MD

As summarized in [[Creatine in Health and Disease]], results of creatine in MD have been more promising than in many [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]] (where neuron loss limits reversibility) because muscle is the primary target. A Cochrane systematic review (Kley et al.) found high-quality RCT evidence that short- and medium-term [[Creatine Supplementation|creatine supplementation]] increases muscle strength in muscular dystrophies and improves functional outcomes.

## Management

Corticosteroids (DMD), supportive care, physical therapy, and emerging genetic therapies (exon skipping, gene therapy).
