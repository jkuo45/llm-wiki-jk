# Chemotherapy-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy

**Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN)** is a dose-limiting adverse effect of antineoplastic agents — particularly taxanes and platinum compounds — characterized by painful, symmetric distal sensory loss.

## Mechanism

Taxanes and platinum agents downregulate [[NMNAT2]] in sensory neurons, raising the NMN/[[NAD+]] ratio and activating [[SARM1]]. SARM1-dependent NAD⁺ depletion drives axonal degeneration ([[Axon Degeneration]], [[Wallerian Degeneration]]). SARM1 knockout protects against CIPN in preclinical models.

## Therapeutic

SARM1 inhibitors ([[Disulfiram]], [[DSRM-3716]], [[GSK-428]], [[NB-4746]]) are in development to prevent CIPN by preserving axonal NAD⁺ during chemotherapy windows.
