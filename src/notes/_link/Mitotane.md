# Mitotane

**Mitotane** (o,p'-DDD; Lysodren) is an adrenolytic agent used in the management of [[Adrenocortical carcinomas]] (ACCs), particularly as adjuvant therapy after resection and for advanced/metastatic disease. It acts as a cytotoxic and mitotane-induced adrenal corticosteroid synthesis inhibitor. The ferroptosis literature frames ACC as a malignancy in which GPX4 is elevated and ferroptosis induction is a promising, potentially less toxic therapeutic strategy than mitotane.

## Pharmacology & Mechanism

Mitotane is a derivative of the pesticide DDT. It selectively accumulates in adrenocortical tissue, where it causes mitochondrial damage, inhibits steroidogenesis (via CYP11A1/CYP11B1 disruption), and promotes adrenocortical cell death through mixed apoptotic/autophagic and metabolic mechanisms. Its use is limited by narrow therapeutic index, gastrointestinal and neurotoxic effects, and required lifelong adrenal replacement.

## Ferroptosis Relevance

The review notes that ACCs show significantly increased expression of [[GPX4]] and sensitivity to ferroptosis, suggesting that inducing ferroptosis may be a promising approach for ACC treatment. Because GPX4 is the master negative regulator of [[Ferroptosis]], ACC's high GPX4 establishes a vulnerability: ferroptosis inducers (e.g., [[RSL3]], [[Erastin]], FINs) may achieve equivalent or better efficacy with less systemic toxicity than mitotane (review "Ferroptosis and tumors / Adrenocortical carcinomas").

> [!important] Therapeutic shift
> The review posits that ferroptosis inducers "may be more effective and less toxic than mitotane" in ACC—positioning ferroptosis targeting as a next-generation ACC strategy to circumvent mitotane's burden.

## Clinical Relevance

Mitotane remains standard for ACC but with considerable toxicity. Ferroptosis-based approaches (GPX4 inhibition, iron loading, system Xc- blockade) represent an investigational alternative justified by ACC's GPX4-high, ferroptosis-sensitive phenotype.
