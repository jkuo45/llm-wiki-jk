# CESD

**CESD** (cholesteryl ester storage disease) is the later-onset, milder phenotypic end
of the [[Lysosomal Acid Lipase|LIPA]] deficiency spectrum, the same autosomal recessive
mutation in *LIPA* that causes the infantile form, [[Wolman Disease]]. Roughly 1 in
40,000–100,000 individuals carry a pathogenic *LIPA* variant, and most heterozygous
carriers are entirely asymptomatic.

## Pathogenesis

Lysosomal acid lipase is a glycoprotein lysosomal hydrolase that hydrolyses cholesteryl
esters and triglycerides delivered to the lysosome by receptor-mediated endocytosis of
[[Low-Density Lipoprotein|LDL]] particles. Its activity is pH-dependent, optimal around
pH 4.5, and it is processed from a 40 kDa propeptide through a 30 kDa intermediate to
the active 25 kDa enzyme. When the enzyme is absent, unhydrolysed cholesteryl esters and
triglycerides accumulate in hepatocytes, Kupffer cells, macrophages, adrenal cortex and
intestinal mucosa. The downstream consequences are hepatic steatosis and fibrosis,
malabsorption from gut-wall lipid accumulation, dyslipidaemia (typically low HDL-C and
LDL-C, with a raised Lp-X and abnormal apoB-containing remnant particles), and adrenal
calcification.

> [!info] One mutation, two diseases
> The distinction between [[Wolman Disease]] and CESD is one of residual enzyme activity,
> not of gene. A null genotype gives Wolman disease: onset in the first months of life,
> vomiting, failure to thrive, malabsorption, adrenal calcification and death usually
> within the first year. Residual activity above roughly a few percent of normal
> produces CESD, which can present in late childhood or only be recognised in adulthood.
> Because the biochemistry is identical, the two terms are best understood as points on
> one continuum.

## Clinical presentation

CESD is frequently undiagnosed, with an average diagnostic delay measured in years.
Presenting features are nonspecific and overlap with common hepatic and metabolic
disease:

- Hepatic steatosis, hepatomegaly, elevated transaminases and GGT, and progressive
  [[Fibrosis]] or cirrhosis with portal hypertension.
- Malabsorption — steatorrhoea, chronic diarrhoea, abdominal pain, poor growth in
  children, and unintentional weight loss in adults.
- Dyslipidaemia with a *low* LDL-C, frequently mistaken for a favourable lipid profile;
  a low LDL-C with steatosis is itself a diagnostic clue.
- Xanthomas (particularly in the palpebral region) and occasionally eruptive xanthomas.
- Adrenal calcification on imaging.
- A history of premature atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease or premature stroke in
  adults, attributed to the abnormal apoB-containing remnant particles.

## Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis rests on a combination of low plasma lysosomal acid lipase activity
(commercially available assay), biallelic *LIPA* variants, and the characteristic lipid
profile — a high Lp-X/LDL-C ratio. Genetic testing is required for confirmation, since
activity alone is affected by sample handling.

> [!info] The glucocorticoid trap
> The same cholesterol substrate that builds up in the liver is diverted into
> adrenal cortical steroidogenesis, producing adrenal insufficiency and the
> characteristic adrenal calcification. Empiric glucocorticoid replacement is
> therefore often started early and permanently, and this is why many adults with CESD
> present with adrenal insufficiency as the dominant feature. It is a consequence of the
> disease, not an independent diagnosis.

**Enzyme replacement therapy with [[Sebelipase Alfa|sebelipase alfa]]** (approved in the
US in 2015, the EU in 2015 and Japan in 2016) is the definitive treatment. It normalises
adrenal function, reduces lipids and improves hepatic outcomes over follow-up. Before
ERT, the alternatives were statins (for the dyslipidaemia), fibrates, cholestyramine,
dietary fat restriction and vitamin D/E supplementation — none of which addressed the
underlying lipid accumulation.

## Connections

- [[Lysosomal Acid Lipase]] — CESD is the disease of deficiency of this single lysosomal hydrolase. Loss of LAL activity blocks cholesteryl ester hydrolysis in the acidic lysosomal lumen, so the entire clinical picture follows from that one block.
- [[Wolman Disease]] — Wolman disease and CESD are the severe and mild ends of one *LIPA* genotype–phenotype continuum, distinguished by residual enzyme activity. The mechanistic relationship is identical; only the onset and severity differ.
- [[LipA]] — The murine LipA gene product performs the same cholesteryl-esterase and triglyceride-esterase function in the mouse lysosome, and LipA-null mice are the standard experimental model. Linking CESD from this note reflects that orthology.
- [[Low-Density Lipoprotein]] — LDL particle uptake delivers the cholesteryl ester substrate that LAL hydrolyses, which is why the biochemical defect is framed in terms of LDL clearance.
- [[Fibrosis]] — Progressive hepatic fibrosis and cirrhosis are the main long-term organ complication in CESD, and the principal indication for transplant referral when ERT is not available.
- [[Liver]] — Hepatic steatosis and its progression are the dominant lesion in CESD; the liver is where macrophage cholesteryl ester accumulation drives the injury.
- [[Lysosome]] — The disease is fundamentally a lysosomal storage disease: the substrate cannot be degraded because the compartment's hydrolase is missing, not because trafficking fails.
- [[Cholesterol]] — Cholesterol handling is the axis of the whole disease, from hepatic cholesteryl ester accumulation to the low-serum-LDL paradox and the adrenal steroidogenesis diversion.
