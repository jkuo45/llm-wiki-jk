# PARG53

**PARG53** is a ~53-kDa splice isoform of [[PARG]] (poly(ADP-ribose) glycohydrolase) that retains catalytic activity. It was described in 2026 (bioRxiv 2026.05.12.724507, Hoch lab) and revises earlier annotations of the short PARG isoforms.

## Overview

- Encoded by a transcript spanning exons 1–8 of the single PARG gene, yielding a ~53-kDa protein.
- Catalytically active PAR glycohydrolase, unlike the previously annotated short PARG55/60 forms, which the new work reports as mis-annotated/dead.
- Its presence explains a long-standing discrepancy: CRISPR knockout strategies targeting PARG exons 2–7 can leave PARG53 intact, so hypomorphic clones (and viable exon-3/7 knockouts) may retain PAR turnover even when bulk PARG protein appears lost.
- Consequence for the field: PARG manipulations must be graded (inhibitor dose–response) and isoform-aware, not simply classified as knockout versus wild-type.

## Role in parthanatos

- [[PARG]] activity is required for parthanatos execution in the 2026 RPE1/HeLa/A549 MNNG model: complete PARG inhibition prevents ATP loss and death, whereas 1–3 µM residual activity still kills. This made PARG endo-activity the proposed generator of the free-[[PAR]] executioner.
- Because PARG53 contributes catalytic activity, cryptic retention of PARG53 confounds knockout-based conclusions and may account for the conflicting PARG literature (see [[PARG]]).
- [[ARH3]] knockout had no effect in the same system, distinguishing the PARG53-dependent arm.
