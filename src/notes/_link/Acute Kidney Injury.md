# Acute Kidney Injury

**Acute kidney injury (AKI)** is a sudden decline in renal function — over hours to days — marked by rising serum creatinine, falling urine output, and impaired filtration. Sepsis, ischemia-reperfusion, and nephrotoxins (e.g., cisplatin) are major triggers, and the tubulointerstitium is the primary site of injury.

## Pathophysiology & Sirtuin Axis

Mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, apoptosis, and inflammation drive tubular injury. Sirtuins form a coordinated defense network:

- **[[SIRT3]]** is the central mitochondrial protector — its deficiency exacerbates sepsis-induced tubular damage and apoptosis (Bax/caspase-3 up, Bcl-2 down) and aggravates fatty acid oxidation dysfunction; activation (e.g., honokiol) increases ATP and reduces ROS/lipid peroxidation, and overexpression attenuates ischemia-reperfusion mitochondrial damage.
- **[[SIRT1]]** protects via JNK/DUSP16 deacetylation, p53-modulator/FoxO3a deacetylation improving tubulointerstitial fibrosis, and the NAD+/SIRT1/GSK-3β/Nrf2 axis.
- **[[SIRT5]]** and **[[SIRT6]]** are protective — SIRT5 balances mitochondrial vs. peroxisomal fatty acid oxidation in proximal tubular cells; SIRT6 inhibits LPS-induced apoptosis and promotes autophagy in HK-2 cells.
- **[[SIRT2]]** is detrimental — knockout ameliorates cisplatin-induced injury while transgenic overexpression aggravates it (MAPK phosphatase-1 deacetylation).
- **[[SIRT7]]** promotes tubular damage — SIRT7-deficient mice are protected against AKI.

## Clinical Relevance

AKI predisposes to chronic kidney disease, and the sirtuin profile (especially mitochondrial SIRT3) is a candidate axis for renoprotective interventions and biomarker development.
