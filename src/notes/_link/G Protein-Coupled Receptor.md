# G Protein-Coupled Receptor

**G protein-coupled receptors** (GPCRs) are integral membrane proteins that transduce
signals from an extracellular ligand to a heterotrimeric [[GTP|GTPase]] inside the cell.
They are the largest receptor superfamily in mammals and the single largest family of
protein drug targets.

## Structure

All GPCRs share a common topology: an extracellular N-terminus, seven transmembrane
α-helices (TM1–TM7) bundled into a barrel within the membrane, three intracellular loops
and three extracellular loops joined by a cytoplasmic C-terminus, plus a distinct
cytoplasmic helix 8. Conserved extracellular cysteines form a stabilising disulfide bond,
and the extracellular loops and N-terminus are frequently glycosylated. The helices form
a ligand-binding cavity whose shape and accessibility differ between classes.

> [!info] Classes
> Mammalian GPCRs divide into four principal classes: **Class A** (rhodopsin-like —
> the largest, containing adrenergic, dopaminergic and serotonergic receptors),
> **Class B** (secretin/glucagon/PACAP, with a large extracellular domain), **Class C**
> (glutamate, GABA-B, calcium-sensing, sweet and umami taste receptors, with a
> Venus-flytrap extracellular domain) and **Class F** (Frizzled/Smoothened). Adiponectin
> receptors ADIPOR1/2 share the 7TM architecture but are inversely oriented and do not
> couple to G proteins.

## Mechanism of Action

- **Heterotrimeric G proteins** — Ligand binding shifts the conformational equilibrium of
  the receptor toward an active state that binds a heterotrimeric G protein (Gα-Gβγ)
  on the cytoplasmic face. Gα then exchanges GDP for [[GTP]] and dissociates from Gβγ;
  both Gα-GTP and free Gβγ act on downstream effectors. Receptor catalysis is fast
  (turnover rates up to ~10³ s⁻¹), which is why GPCRs need continuous agonist exposure to
  sustain signalling.
- **Four Gα axes** — **Gs** stimulates adenylyl cyclase and raises [[cAMP]]; **Gi/o**
  inhibits adenylyl cyclase and lowers cAMP; **Gq** activates [[Phospholipase C]], which
  cleaves PIP2 into IP3 (releasing calcium from the endoplasmic reticulum) and DAG
  (activating PKC); **G12/13** signals to Rho-family GTPases, shaping the cytoskeleton
  and cell adhesion.
- **Arrestin-mediated termination** — GRK-mediated receptor phosphorylation recruits
  β-arrestins, which sterically block further G-protein coupling and recruit internalisation
  machinery. Internalised receptors continue to signal from endosomes, so termination and
  signal propagation are the same process.
- **Biased agonism** — Different ligands at the same receptor can favour G-protein or
  arrestin coupling, so a ligand's effect cannot be predicted from efficacy at a single
  downstream readout. This complicates drug development considerably.

## Pharmacology and Therapeutics

> [!important] The drug target of last and first resort
> GPCRs are the largest family of proteins targeted by approved drugs. Analyses of
> US/EU approvals (Hauser et al., 2017) found 134 GPCRs targeted by approved drugs and
> estimated ~700 approved drugs act on GPCRs, i.e. roughly **35% of all approved drugs**.
> GPCRs themselves account for about 12% of all protein drug targets — the largest single
> share. Targets cluster in aminergic (adrenergic, dopaminergic, serotonergic,
> muscarinic), peptidergic and sensory receptors; ~100 of ~360 non-sensory GPCRs remain
  > "orphan" receptors with no known endogenous agonist, and are therefore attractive but
> hard targets.

- **Druggability** — Class A receptors carry a conserved internal binding pocket and the
  binding site for retinal in rhodopsin; the majority of aminergic GPCR ligands are
  aminergic compounds occupying this pocket. Biased agonism and allosteric modulators
  are major current themes, aimed at separating wanted from on-target side effects.
- **Viral pharmacology** — [[SARS-CoV-2]] and several other coronavirus spike proteins
  engage [[ACKR3]] (CXCR7) and possibly [[CXCR4]] as co-receptors alongside ACE2.

## Connections
- [[Cell Signaling]] — GPCRs are the archetype of the first-messenger/receptor/effector
  architecture; the textbook example of transduction from an external cue to an
  intracellular change.
- [[Serotonin Receptor]] — All 5-HT receptors except 5-HT3 are GPCRs; the family
  illustrates Gαi (5-HT1), Gq (5-HT2) and Gs (5-HT4/6/7) coupling.
- [[Beta-Adrenergic Receptor]] — The archetypal Class A GPCR; β-adrenergic agonists and
  antagonists span asthma, cardiovascular disease and ophthalmology.
- [[Dopamine]] — Dopamine's four receptors (D1–D5) are Class A GPCRs and among the most
  intensively drug-targeted receptor families in psychiatry and neurology.
- [[MC1R]] — A Class A GPCR (though a melanocortin receptor) whose loss-of-function
  variants drive red hair and melanoma susceptibility.
- [[ACKR3]] — An atypical, poorly G-protein-coupled chemokine receptor now implicated as
  a SARS-CoV-2 spike co-receptor.
- [[Plasma Membrane]] — GPCRs are integral components of the plasma membrane, and
  [[Lipid Rafts|lipid raft]] partitioning influences their signalling and trafficking.
- [[Phospholipase C]] — The Gq effector that converts PIP2 into IP3 and DAG, coupling
  GPCR activation to calcium signalling and PKC.
