# IFNAR1

## Overview

[[IFNAR1]] (interferon alpha/beta receptor subunit 1) is a transmembrane receptor subunit that partners with [[IFNAR1|IFNAR2]] to form the heterodimeric receptor for type I interferons ([[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]]), including IFN-α and IFN-β. Within the [[Central Nervous System|CNS]], neuronal IFNAR1 serves as a critical conduit linking microglial IFN-I release to neuronal dysfunction. In the context of cGAS-STING–driven [[Neuroinflammation]], sustained IFN-I production by activated [[Microglia]] engages neuronal IFNAR1, restraining protective transcriptional programs and contributing to [[Neurodegeneration]].

## Mechanism in Neuron-Glia Communication

Activation of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] in microglia drives robust expression of [[Interferon-Stimulated Genes|ISGs]] and secretion of IFN-I. Neuronal [[IFNAR1]] binds this extracellular IFN-I, initiating downstream JAK-STAT signaling that suppresses resilience-associated networks such as the [[MEF2C]] program in [[Tauopathy|tauopathy]] models ([17](#B17), [135](#B135)). Because microglia are the dominant source of cGAS expression in the diseased brain, much of the neuronal injury is non–cell-autonomous, relayed through IFNAR1 rather than through intrinsic neuronal cGAS activity.

## Therapeutic Relevance

Targeting IFNAR1 offers a strategy to uncouple microglial cGAS/IFN-I neurotoxicity from beneficial microglial functions. Selective blockade of neuronal IFNAR1 is being explored to preserve synaptic integrity and cognitive resilience while sparing the phagocytic and surveillance roles of glia. This positions [[IFNAR1]] alongside [[cGAS]] and [[STING]] inhibitors as a context-dependent node for modulating [[Neuroinflammation]] without broadly disabling innate immunity.
