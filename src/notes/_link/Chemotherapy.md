# Chemotherapy

Chemotherapy refers to pharmacological treatment of [[Cancer]] using cytotoxic or cytostatic drugs that target rapidly dividing cells. Major classes include [[Alkylating Agents]], [[Antimetabolites]], [[Topoisomerase Inhibitors]], [[Platinum-based drugs]] ([[Cisplatin]], [[Carboplatin]], [[Oxaliplatin]]), [[Taxanes]], [[Anthracyclines]], and [[Vinca Alkaloids]]. Chemotherapy works primarily by inducing [[DNA Damage]], inhibiting [[Mitosis]], or blocking [[DNA Replication]]. It is associated with significant adverse effects including [[Myelosuppression]], [[Nausea]], [[Alopecia]], [[Cardiotoxicity]], and [[Peripheral Neuropathy]]. Chemotherapy is often combined with [[Radiotherapy]], [[Immunotherapy]], or [[Targeted Therapy]].

## Chemotherapy and NAD+ Metabolism

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Chemotherapy NAD+ Loss Pöllänen 2026]]
> [[Chemotherapy]] alone — independent of tumor burden — can disrupt tissue [[NAD+]] metabolism. Commonly used regimens ([[Cisplatin]], [[FOLFIRI]], [[FOLFOX]]) induce skeletal muscle NAD+ loss and downregulation of the NAD+ biosynthetic enzyme [[NRK2|Nrk2]], recapitulating metabolic alterations previously observed in cancer [[Cachexia|cachexia]].

### Chemotherapy-Induced Muscle Wasting

- Chemotherapy activates pro-atrophic signaling pathways, suppresses anabolic processes, and induces [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] dysfunction and metabolic derangements
- [[Cisplatin]] induces the most severe cachexia phenotype with −14% muscle NAD+ loss and −93% [[NRK2|Nrk2]] downregulation
- [[FOLFIRI]] (prolonged) results in modest muscle loss with −18% NAD+ decline
- [[FOLFOX]] preserves NAD+ levels but downregulates Nrk2 (−92%), suggesting Nrk2 repression may precede NAD+ depletion
- [[Doxorubicin]] causes systemic NAD+ depletion; Nrk2 is the most downregulated transcript after a single dose

### Hepatic Effects

Prolonged chemotherapy exposure induces modest but significant decreases in hepatic NAD+ levels (−15 to −20%), with treatment-specific reorganization of hepatic NAD+ biosynthesis pathways.
