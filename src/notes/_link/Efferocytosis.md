# Efferocytosis

**Efferocytosis** is the recognition and engulfment of apoptotic cells by professional phagocytes — here specifically of PS-exposing [[Erythrocytes]] after [[Eryptosis]]. Because it happens in **minutes** (versus days for senescent cells), efferocytosis is the clearance route that lets eryptosis substitute for lytic [[Hemolysis]].

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Externalized [[Phosphatidylserine]] is the "eat-me" signal; PS is itself immunosuppressive, and PS-mediated uptake of erythrocytes reprogrammes monocyte-derived macrophages toward downregulated proinflammatory cytokines and upregulated antioxidant/antiinflammatory factors including heme oxygenase-1 — observed after experimental intracerebral hemorrhage.

## Mechanism and consequences

- Macrophages recognize PS (directly or via bridging molecules) on apoptotic/eryptotic cells; [[CD47]]–SIRPα provides the opposing "don't-eat-me" signal on healthy cells.
- Clearance sites: splenic and hepatic sinusoid macrophages, [[Kupffer Cell|Kupffer cells]] (eryptotic RBCs are engulfed into erythrophagolysosomes); Kupffer cells also remove ~80% of RBC-derived vesicles within 5 min via scavenger receptors.
- Anti-inflammatory payoff: PS recognition alone is immunosuppressive, so eryptotic clearance actively resolves inflammation instead of amplifying it — the mechanistic opposite of hemolysis-derived DAMPs.
- Efferocytosis also produces extracellular-vesicle shedding whose EV-associated DAMPs appear in stored red-cell concentrates, a potential bridge back to proinflammatory effects.
