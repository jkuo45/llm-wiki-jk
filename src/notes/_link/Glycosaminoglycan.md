# Glycosaminoglycan

Glycosaminoglycans are unbranched linear polysaccharides made of repeating **disaccharide units**, each consisting of a uronic acid (D-glucuronic acid or its L-iduronate epimer) alternating with an N-acetylated (or N-sulfated) hexosamine. The enormous molecular weight and high charge density of a GAG chain are what give the [[Extracellular Matrix|extracellular matrix]] its physical properties: high viscosity, low compressibility, and the ability to resist compressive load while still permitting cell migration through hydrated pores.

> [!info] The five classes
> | Class | Repeating disaccharide | Sulfated | Notes |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Hyaluronan | GlcA β1→3 GlcNAc | No | Made at the plasma membrane, not in the Golgi; no core protein; can reach MW >10⁷ |
> | Chondroitin sulfate / dermatan sulfate | GlcA (or IdoA) β1→3 GalNAc | Yes | DS differs by epimerization of GlcA to IdoA; abundant in cartilage |
> | Heparin / heparan sulfate | IdoA or GlcA α/β1→4 GlcN-SO₃⁻ | Yes | HS is less sulfated; heparin is the mast-cell granule form |
> | Keratan sulfate | Gal β1→4 GlcNAc-6-SO₄ | Yes | No uronic acid; named for cornea, where it was first identified |
>
> With the exception of hyaluronan, GAG chains are covalently linked to serine residues of core proteins via a common trisaccharide linker, forming **proteoglycans**. Some keratan sulfates instead link N-asparaginyl on the core protein.

## Structure & assembly

GAG synthesis occurs in the Golgi. Sulfated chains are built on a primer (a xylose-O-serine trisaccharide) by sequential addition of nucleotide sugars (UDP-GlcNAc, UDP-GlcA, PAPS as sulfate donor) by Golgi glycosyltransferases. Heparan sulfate is unusual in being **not template-driven**: the same enzymes act on the growing chain, and the final sulfation pattern is set stochastically, producing highly heterogeneous domains rather than a single defined sequence. That heterogeneity is functionally load-bearing — it determines which proteins bind HS with what affinity.

> [!info] Mechanism
> Two binding modes dominate GAG biology. **Electrostatic** binding is non-specific: the polyanionic chain concentrates divalent cations (Ca²⁺, Na⁺) and any positively charged protein that happens to diffuse past. **Specific** binding requires a defined sulfation pattern — for heparan sulfate, the FGF2–FGFR1 ternary complex requires a specific 2-O-sulfated iduronic acid–glucosamine disaccharide. The same chain can therefore be permissive for one growth factor and inhibitory for another.

## Physiological function

- **Hyaluronan**: synovial fluid lubricant and joint-space sieve, the main component of the vitreous humour and of the ECM of loose connective tissue; a high-molecular-weight form (HMW-HA) is a potent pro-inflammatory signal through CD44 and TLR2/4 that is degraded by [[Hyaluronidase]]-like activity in inflamed tissue. HA also serves as the backbone to which aggrecan monomers attach in cartilage.
- **Chondroitin sulfate**: the most abundant GAG, and a major component of cartilage aggrecan; also found in bone, heart valves and brain.
- **Heparin**: the anticoagulant GAG, released from mast cell granules, acting chiefly via antithrombin III to accelerate thrombin and factor Xa inactivation. Its sulfation pattern is dense and largely sequence-independent — that is precisely what makes it a promiscuous anticoagulant.
- **Heparan sulfate**: a constitutive component of [[Basal Lamina|basement membranes]] and the cell surface, where it stores growth factors (FGF, VEGF) and shapes their diffusion and signalling.
- **Keratan sulfate**: transparency of the cornea (its regular spacing and hydration control light scattering), and a secondary role in brain and cartilage.

> [!warning] Clinical caveat
> Degradation of GAGs is the shared mechanism of several severe diseases. Defective lysosomal enzymes that cannot degrade GAGs cause the [[Mucopolysaccharidoses|mucopolysaccharidoses]] — accumulation of keratan sulfate and heparan sulfate underlies [[Mucopolysaccharidosis-IIIA]]; accumulation of dermatan and heparan sulfate underlies Hurler (MPS I) and Hunter (MPS II) disease. In osteoarthritis, aggrecan and collagen fragmentation in cartilage is driven by [[Matrix Metalloproteinases|MMPs]] and by the aggrecanases ADAMTS-4/5. In [[Heparin|heparin]]-induced thrombocytopenia, the clinical entity is a drug–protein complex, not a GAG defect — worth keeping distinct.

## Connections

- [[Heparin]] — the best-known clinical GAG, an unusually sulfated heparan sulfate made by mast cells and used as an anticoagulant. It is the direct reason this umbrella note matters pharmacologically: the same sulfation chemistry that makes heparin a drug makes cell-surface heparan sulfate a growth-factor reservoir.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — sulfated GAGs exist in the ECM almost exclusively as proteoglycans, where the core protein and the chain do different jobs: the protein sets the position, the chain sets the charge and hydration.
- [[Basal Lamina]] — basement membranes are built on type IV collagen networks studded with heparan sulfate proteoglycans (perlecan). This is the anatomical reason HS controls growth-factor access to epithelia and endothelium.
- [[FGF]] — the FGF–FGFR signalling complex requires a specific heparan sulfate disaccharide to assemble; removing HS abolishes both the potency and the gradient of FGF signalling.
- [[Mucopolysaccharidosis-IIIA]] — a lysosomal storage disease defined by accumulation of heparan sulfate and keratan sulfate, so it is a direct readout of GAG catabolism.
- [[Matrix Metalloproteinases]] — MMPs and aggrecanases are the main GAG-degrading enzymes in degenerative joint disease, coupling cartilage loss to the senescence and pain pathways the vault studies elsewhere.
- [[Sulfation]] — sulfation is the single chemical variable that switches GAG chains between inert structural polymer and active signalling surface.
