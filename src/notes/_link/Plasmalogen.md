# Plasmalogen

**Plasmalogen** is a class of [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]] distinguished by a *vinyl ether* (alkenyl) bond at the sn-1 position rather than the usual ester bond. This single chemical difference makes plasmalogens unusually resistant to oxidation and makes them a structural and functional arm of the cell's antioxidant defence. They are the dominant glycerophospholipid of [[Myelin|myelin]] and are concentrated in [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] membranes, in heart and brain tissue, and in [[Platelet|platelets]].

## Overview

Two families dominate: the ethanolamine plasmalogens (the major species, including the platelet-activating factor precursor) and the choline plasmalogens. Biosynthesis begins in the endoplasmic reticulum, where the *ether* bond is installed by the glycerophosphate O-acyltransferase/alkyl-dihydroxyacetone phosphate synthase pathway (the "ether lipid" pathway), and continues in peroxisomes, which are essential because the final steps of plasmalogen maturation — the desaturase step that creates the vinyl ether bond — occur there.

> [!info] Why the vinyl ether matters
> Vinyl ether bonds are far more oxidation-sensitive than ester bonds in the *initiation* step but, crucially, the resulting radical and peroxyl species are resonance-stabilised and rapidly rearrange rather than propagating. Net effect: plasmalogens are preferentially consumed during oxidative challenge and act as sacrificial antioxidant buffers within the membrane.

## Mechanism

- **Antioxidant membrane protection.** Plasmalogens quench singlet oxygen and intercept peroxyl radicals, protecting polyunsaturated fatty acids in the same bilayer from [[Lipid Peroxidation]]. Loss of plasmalogen content is a recognised feature of ageing and of neurodegenerative disease.
- **Platelet-activating factor reservoir.** The choline plasmalogen most abundant in platelets and neutrophils is the precursor of platelet-activating factor (PAF), a potent mediator released upon activation; hydrolysis by platelet-activating factor acetylhydrolase yields the corresponding lysophospholipid (lyso-PAF), which itself is bioactive.
- **Membrane fluidity and packing.** The vinyl ether linkage packs efficiently into the bilayer and stabilises membrane domains, contributing to the properties of [[Lipid Rafts]] and to the functional integrity of mitochondrial and myelin membranes.
- **Peroxisomal dependence.** Because of the peroxisomal desaturase step, plasmalogen synthesis requires functional peroxisomes; peroxisomal biogenesis disorders therefore produce plasmalogen deficiency (see [[Zellweger Spectrum Disorders]] and the Zellweger syndrome note).
- **Oxidation markers.** Peroxidation of plasmalogens generates distinctive isoprostane-like products; the free radical marker [[F2-Isoprostanes]] and the [[Malondialdehyde]] pathway reflect this, and plasmalogen depletion is itself a readout of oxidative burden.

## Physiological Role

- Maintenance of myelin integrity and of axonal conduction — the reason the brain is rich in plasmalogens.
- Maintenance of mitochondrial inner-membrane fluidity and of respiratory function, linking plasmalogen content to [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] and to mitochondrial quality control.
- Platelet activation and the PAF inflammatory axis, connecting to [[Thromboxane A₂]] and to platelet aggregation.
- Neuroprotection: plasmalogen depletion is reported in [[Parkinson's Disease]], [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and in ageing brain.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Rhizomelic chondrodysplasia punctata.** Deficiency of ether-phospholipid biosynthesis (most often PEX7, or the GNPAT/AGPS and FAR1 pathways) causes this rare peroxisomal disorder, with characteristic stippled epiphyses, rhizomelia, cataracts and profound plasmalogen deficiency — the clearest demonstration that plasmalogens are essential.
- **Zellweger spectrum and other peroxisomal disorders.** Areflexia, hypotonia and severe neurological impairment reflect combined plasmalogen and plasminogen deficiency.
- **Ageing and neurodegeneration.** Brain plasmalogen content declines with age; reduced plasmalogen is reported in Alzheimer and Parkinson disease, and plasmalogen synthesis is a target of interest in these conditions.
- **Cardiovascular disease and platelet biology.** Altered plasmalogen and PAF metabolism contribute to atherosclerosis and to platelet-driven thrombosis; PAF receptor antagonists have been explored in inflammatory and cardiovascular indications.
- **Inflammation.** PAF is a chemotactic and vasoactive mediator in asthma and sepsis; the plasmalogen pool is the local reservoir from which it is made on demand.
