# Keratinocyte Growth Factor

**Keratinocyte growth factor (KGF, FGF7)** is a member of the fibroblast growth factor family that signals through FGFR2IIIb. It is critical for fetal [[Thymus|thymus]] organogenesis and has been identified as a key factor in thymic rejuvenation.

## Structure and Signaling

- **Gene:** FGF7
- **Receptor:** FGFR2IIIb (epithelial cell-specific)
- **Signaling:** Ras/MAPK and PI3K/AKT pathways → proliferation and differentiation
- **Expression:** Mesenchymal cells; acts on epithelial cells in a paracrine manner

## Thymic Effects

### Fetal development
- Critical for thymus organogenesis via FGFR2IIIb signaling
- Required for initial TEC proliferation and differentiation

### Postnatal and aged thymus
- Stimulates proliferation and differentiation of [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|TECs]]
- Increases thymus size and thymocyte production in aged animals
- Restores defined cortical and medullary compartments
- Increases output of naïve phenotype [[CD4+ T Cells|CD4 T cells]]
- Enhances humoral responses to T-dependent antigens

> [!info] KGF efficacy in aged mice
> Min et al. demonstrated that a single course of KGF treatment in aged mice increased thymic cellularity for two months. Monthly follow-up administration sustained this improvement, with restoration of thymic architecture and enhanced immune function.

## Clinical Development

KGF (as **Palifermin** or **Kepavance**) has advanced to human clinical trials:

- **Cambridge University** (NCT01712945) — Testing KGF to prevent secondary autoimmunity after Campath-1H treatment for [[Multiple Sclerosis|multiple sclerosis]]
- **Indiana University** (NCT00593554) — Phase II study of KGF in haplotype-mismatched [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cell (CD34+)]] transplant
- **Fred Hutchinson/UW** (NCT01233921) — Pharmacodynamics of palifermin in adult stem cell transplant for hematologic cancer; testing whether KGF prevents chronic graft-versus-host disease

## Limitations

- KGF has multiple targets outside the thymus
- Mechanisms of action in the postnatal thymus are still under investigation
- Functional quality of the thymus after KGF-induced rebound requires further study
