# Frailty Index

**Frailty Index (FI)** is a validated measure of the accumulation of age-related health deficits (symptoms, signs, disabilities, diseases) across multiple domains, used to quantify frailty and predict adverse outcomes. In mice, the Whitehead clinical frailty index scores 31 parameters spanning body condition, locomotion, and integumentary signs, and correlates with biological age.

## Role in Aging Research

The FI operationalizes [[Frailty]] as a continuous score, allowing interventional studies to test whether pharmacological or genetic interventions improve healthspan, not just lifespan. It is a standard readout in preclinical aging and senescence research.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP|Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP]]
> Aged mice treated with the SLC25A1 inhibitor [[CTPI2]] showed improved healthspan with reduced age-associated frailty, scored using the Whitehead 31-parameter clinical frailty index — linking SASP suppression to measurable healthspan gains.
