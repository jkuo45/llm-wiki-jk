# Interferon-Stimulated Genes

## Overview

Interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) are the aggregated transcriptional output of type I interferon ([[Type I Interferon|IFN-I]]) signaling. Binding of IFN-I to its receptor ([[IFNAR1]]) activates JAK-STAT pathways that induce hundreds of ISGs, whose protein products establish an antiviral and inflammatory cellular state. In neurodegeneration, ISGs serve as a sensitive molecular readout of cGAS-STING activation and chronic [[Neuroinflammation]].

## Signaling

ISGs are induced downstream of the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] axis: cytosolic dsDNA activates [[cGAS]] to produce [[cGAMP]], engaging [[STING]], which recruits [[TBK1]] and activates [[IRF3]] and [[NF-kappaB|NF-κB]] to drive IFN-I and ISG transcription. Elevated ISG expression is therefore a downstream effector signature of sustained IFN-I signaling across neurodegenerative contexts. ISGs mark pro-inflammatory glial subpopulations and are co-induced with pro-inflammatory cytokines in activated [[Microglia]] and [[Astrocytes]].

## Clinical Relevance

Across diverse neurodegenerative conditions — including [[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]], [[Parkinson's Disease|PD]], [[Frontotemporal Dementia|FTD]], and [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis|ALS]] — elevated ISGs are a shared feature of mouse models, human patient tissues, and disease-stressed glial cells. In AD, ISGs along with inflammatory cytokines correlate with cognitive decline. Pharmacological or genetic inhibition of cGAS or STING markedly reduces IRF3- and NF-κB–driven ISG expression while preserving beneficial microglial functions such as [[Amyloid Beta|Aβ]] clearance. ISG induction also characterizes an IFN-I–enriched microglial subpopulation linked to synapse loss and cognitive impairment in tauopathy, distinct from the canonical [[Disease-Associated Microglia|DAM]] program. ISG elevation thus serves both as a biomarker of maladaptive innate immune activation and as a therapeutic target index.
