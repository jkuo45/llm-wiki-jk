# Beta Blockers

**Beta blockers** are drugs that competitively antagonise [[Beta-Adrenergic Receptor|beta-adrenergic receptors]], blocking the actions of [[Epinephrine|adrenaline]] and [[Norepinephrine|noradrenaline]]. The class was created by Sir James Black and colleagues around propranolol in the 1960s — the first truly rational design of a receptor-blocking drug — and it became one of the most prescribed drug classes in cardiovascular medicine.

> [!info] The class is not one drug
> "Beta blocker" hides more pharmacological variation than any other drug
> class. Selectivity for receptor subtype, intrinsic sympathomimetic activity,
> membrane-stabilising (quinidine-like) activity, and ancillary alpha-blocking or
> antioxidant activity all differ between agents, and those differences change
> which indications and which adverse effects apply.

## Mechanism & Pharmacology

Beta receptors are GPCRs coupled to adenylyl cyclase through Gs
(beta-1 and beta-2) and Gi (beta-3). Antagonism therefore lowers intracellular
cAMP and calcium flux in target cells, producing three main cardiac effects:
decreased heart rate (chronotropy), decreased contractility (inotropy), and
faster conduction deceleration at the atrioventricular node (dromotropy).
Peripheral beta-2 blockade produces bronchoconstriction and vasoconstriction,
while blockade of beta-2-mediated vasodilation in skeletal muscle blunts the
hypoglycaemic response to [[Insulin]] and exercise.

**Subtype selectivity.** The clinically important divide is beta-1 selective
agents (atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol, nebivolol) versus non-selective
agents (propranolol, carvedilol, labetalol, sotalol). Cardioselectivity is
dose-dependent and incomplete, so it is lost at the doses often used for
hypertension. Cardioselective agents are preferred in asthma and COPD; the
non-selective agent carvedilol is the exception that gained outcomes data in
heart failure because its alpha-1 and antioxidant activity is added on top of
beta blockade.

> [!warning] Not all agents are interchangeable
> Immediate-release propranolol and other short-acting agents have no
> mortality benefit in hypertension, and a well-publicised 2005 meta-analysis
> claiming reduced all-cause mortality for atenolol has been substantially
> criticised on methodological grounds. Long-acting formulations and agents
> with trial-proven outcomes — bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate,
> nebivolol, and the mixed alpha-beta blocker doxazosin for some indications —
> are the forms that carry evidence.

## Clinical Uses

- **Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction** — a class effect, with
  mortality benefit; beta blockers are the only chronic therapy that both
  reduces [[Heart Failure]] hospitalisation and mortality.
- **Chronic ischaemic heart disease and post-MI** — reduce heart rate, reduce
  angina, and lower mortality.
- **Arrhythmias** — rate and, for some agents, rhythm control in
  [[Atrial Fibrillation]]; sotalol and esmolol are antiarrhythmics proper, and
  beta blockers are the mainstay in adrenergic triggers.
- **Hypertension** — a first-line option, especially where tachycardia,
  [[Diabetes]], or cardiac disease coexists.
- **Non-cardiac uses** — essential tremor, migraine prophylaxis, anxiety
  performance disorders, portal hypertension, thyrotoxicosis, glaucoma
  (timolol), and PTSD (prazosin is alpha-1, but the class family is the same
  receptor axis).

## Adverse Effects

Class effects include **bronchospasm** (contraindicated in asthma),
bradycardia, AV block, negative inotropy, fatigue, cold extremities, sleep
disturbance including nightmares, and depression-like symptoms. Beta-3
associated effects are less commonly discussed but are real. Nonselective
agents can mask the adrenergic warning signs of hypoglycaemia.

> [!warning] The cardioselective-agent question in longevity practice
> Beta blockers are sometimes marketed as a pro-longevity intervention because
> they reduce sympathetic tone and because some retrospective data associate
> beta-blocker use with lower [[Cancer]] incidence. The randomised evidence
> does not support a longevity claim: propranolol failed to extend lifespan in
> animal models, and human data are observational and confounded by
> indication. This is a real gap between the mechanistic story and the
> outcome data, and it is worth stating rather than glossing.

## Connections
- [[Beta-Adrenergic Receptor]] — the class is defined by competitive antagonism at this receptor, and beta-1 selectivity is what separates the safer agents from the bronchospasm-prone ones.
- [[Sympathetic Nervous System]] — beta blockade is pharmacological interruption of sympathetic outflow, which is why the class lowers rate, contractility, and vascular tone simultaneously.
- [[Calcium Channel Blockers]] — the two dominant rate-controlling antihypertensive classes; combining a non-dihydropyridine CCB with a beta blocker risks bradycardia and AV block and is generally avoided.
- [[Arrhythmias]] — nodal slowing is the therapeutic mechanism in supraventricular rate control, which places beta blockers in the Vaughan Williams scheme alongside amiodarone and the CCBs.
- [[Insulin]] — beta-2 blockade blunts the adrenergic counter-regulatory response to hypoglycaemia, which is why cardioselectivity matters in metabolic disease.
- [[Oxidative Stress]] — carvedilol's antioxidant activity is one reason its outcomes data exceed those of pure beta antagonists, and adrenergic tone is itself a source of reactive oxygen species signalling in the failing heart.
