# Autism

**Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)** is a neurodevelopmental condition defined by two core domains: persistent deficits in **social communication and social interaction**, and **restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activity**. It is a *spectrum* rather than a single disorder — presentation ranges from minimally verbal individuals with intellectual disability to highly articulate individuals with average or above-average cognitive testing scores, and core features can change over time. Autism is lifelong, begins in early childhood, and is diagnosed on behavioral criteria; there is no validated biological diagnostic test.

> [!warning] The single-cause framing is wrong
> Autism is one of the most **etologically heterogeneous** conditions in neurodevelopmental medicine. Its heritability is high — commonly estimated at roughly 60–90% from twin studies — but no single gene, pathway, or immune marker explains more than a small fraction of cases. Autistic individuals also frequently carry identifiable genetic co-conditions including epilepsy, sleep disorders, ADHD, [[Epilepsy]], and gastrointestinal complaints, and clinical presentation varies by intellectual and language level. Any mechanistic account offered here must be framed as applying to subgroups, not to autism as a whole.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

**Genetics.** Hundreds of genes contribute, converging on a smaller number of processes: synaptic transmission and plasticity, transcription and chromatin regulation, and protein synthesis. Many implicated genes regulate [[mTORC1]] signaling — including *PTEN*, *TSC1*, and *TSC2* — such that ASD sits on a continuum with the mTORopathies. Rare de novo variants explain a meaningful minority of cases; common polygenic variants account for much of the remaining liability. Synaptic-function genes are consistently enriched, which is why synaptic plasticity and [[Long-Term Potentiation]] biology recur throughout the mechanistic literature.

**Neuroimmune mechanisms.** Immune dysregulation is increasingly framed as a **modifier** rather than a universal or isolated cause. Candidate routes include maternal immune activation, maternal brain-reactive autoantibodies, altered peripheral immune cell profiles, microglial and astrocytic function, complement-mediated synaptic remodeling, and gut–immune–brain signaling (PMID: 42794832). Given the framing above, complement-mediated excess synaptic pruning during a critical developmental window is a prominent hypothesis — the same period in which LTP and synaptic consolidation normally occur.

**Synaptic and circuit dysfunction.** Findings across postmortem and imaging studies point to reduced synaptic density, altered excitatory/inhibitory balance involving [[GABA]] and [[Glutamate]] signaling, and atypical local connectivity and long-range connectivity. Aberrant mTOR pathway signaling is the best-replicated of the "convergent pathway" accounts, because TSC1/TSC2/PTEN mutations cause a syndromic autism-predominant phenotype.

**Gastrointestinal and metabolic features.** GI complaints are markedly overrepresented. The vault's creatine transporter work ([[_document_ - Creatine in Health and Disease|Creatine in Health and Disease]]) documents reduced brain creatine in [[Creatine Transporter]] and creatine synthesis deficiencies, with [[Autism]] among the associated neurodevelopmental presentations — relevant because low brain creatine is a candidate mechanism linking cerebral energy limitation to synaptic dysfunction, though the creatine-deficient syndromes are distinct conditions from idiopathic ASD.

> [!warning] What is not established
> Immune involvement in ASD is an active, contested research area. Reviews assess immune dysfunction as a possible **modifier** of ASD phenotypes rather than a universal or isolated cause, and direct longitudinal evidence linking specific immune profiles to defined neuropsychological trajectories remains limited — especially in adults (PMID: 42794832). Claims that a specific immune intervention treats autism are not supported by evidence, and intervention claims should be held to that standard regardless of how plausible the mechanism.

## Clinical Presentation

- Early childhood onset of social communication differences, with a well-documented early trajectory: reduced social engagement and atypical eye contact in infancy, absence of babbling, and later joint-attention and language differences.
- Restricted or repetitive behaviors, including stimming, insistence on routine, and circumscribed interests; atypical sensory responsivity is near-universal and its severity tracks with functional outcome.
- Co-occurring conditions are common: intellectual disability in a substantial minority, [[Epilepsy]] in a higher proportion than the general population, sleep disturbance (often linked to melatonin signaling), ADHD, anxiety, and GI symptoms.
- Intellectual ability and adaptive function vary widely and are largely independent of core diagnostic criteria.

> [!info] Terminology
> "Autism spectrum disorder" remains the DSM-5/ICD-11 diagnosis. Identity-first language ("autistic person") is widely preferred by many autistic self-advocates; person-first language ("person with autism") is still used in clinical contexts. Both are in current use, and the preference is contested rather than settled.

## Therapeutic Landscape

There is **no approved pharmacological treatment for the core features of autism**. Available interventions are supportive rather than curative:

- **Behavioral and educational approaches.** Early intensive behavioral intervention and structured educational programs remain the best-supported interventions for adaptive and communication outcomes.
- **Targeted symptom treatment.** Co-occurring epilepsy, ADHD, anxiety, sleep disturbance, and GI symptoms are treated in their own right. Notably, stimulant medication for co-occurring ADHD is generally effective and does not appear to worsen core features.
- **Dietary and metabolic intervention.** Restricted dietary regimens and complementary/alternative approaches are widely used by families, with weak and largely inconsistent evidentiary support.
- **Repurposed agents.** Trials of [[Rapamycin]] and other mTOR-modulating compounds in ASD have been motivated by the TSC/PTEN/mTOR convergence, but results are preliminary and not sufficient to support clinical use.

> [!warning] A methodological caution for interpreting the mTOR-ASD literature
> Much of the supporting preclinical evidence comes from TSC or Fragile X mouse models, which are syndromic conditions with autism-predominant phenotypes — not idiopathic ASD. Temptation to generalize from these models to autism broadly is the single most common overreach in this literature. Fragile X syndrome (no vault note yet) in particular carries an intellectual disability and autism phenotype together, so ASD-specific phenotypic changes in these models are difficult to attribute unambiguously.

## Connections

- [[mTORC1]] — the pathway through which the largest convergent genetic risk signal flows; *TSC1*, *TSC2*, and *PTEN* mutations all produce autism-predominant syndromic phenotypes, making mTOR dysregulation the best-replicated mechanistic convergence in ASD.
- [[PTEN]] — a direct ASD gene whose loss activates mTORC1, providing the strongest single-gene link between the pathway and the condition.
- [[TSC1]] / [[TSC2]] — loss-of-function causes tuberous sclerosis complex, whose autism-predominant phenotype is the clearest evidence for mTOR-driven ASD pathogenesis.
- [[GABA]] — inhibitory signaling alterations are among the most replicated electrophysiological findings in ASD, and excitation–inhibition imbalance is a leading circuit-level account.
- [[Glutamate]] — glutamatergic dysregulation, including NMDA receptor hypofunction, is a recurrent finding and underlies much of the synaptic plasticity literature.
- [[Long-Term Potentiation]] — impaired synaptic plasticity and abnormal LTP are documented in ASD models and syndromic forms; this note supplies the missing target that the vault's plasticity notes point to.
- [[Epilepsy]] — an important co-occurring condition in ASD with a substantially elevated prevalence relative to the general population.
- [[Microglia]] — glial activation and complement-mediated synaptic remodeling are central to the neuroimmune-hypothesis literature on ASD.
- [[Gut Microbiome]] — gut–immune–brain signaling and the overrepresentation of GI symptoms in ASD make the microbiome an active area of investigation.
- [[Rapamycin]] — mTOR inhibition is among the few pharmacological strategies being trialed on the mechanistic rationale that ASD lies downstream of mTOR dysregulation.
