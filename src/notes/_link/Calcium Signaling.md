# Calcium Signaling

Calcium signaling refers to the spatiotemporal regulation of intracellular [[Calcium]] concentration to control diverse cellular processes. A particularly important locus for mitochondrial biology is the [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]]–mitochondria interface, where calcium is transferred from the ER to mitochondria at [[Mitochondria-Associated Membranes]].

According to [[Mitochondrial Dynamics]], calcium transfer at MAMs and calcium-dependent inner-membrane constriction regulate mitochondrial dynamics. ER-initiated mitochondrial fission is mediated by [[INF2]] and actin, and calcium fluxes modulate the constrictive machinery that executes [[Mitochondrial Fission]]. By influencing both fusion and fission equilibria, calcium signaling integrates ER stress, bioenergetic demand, and organelle morphology.

Dysregulated calcium handling at MAMs is implicated in neurodegeneration and metabolic disease, linking calcium signaling to mitochondrial quality control and cell survival.
