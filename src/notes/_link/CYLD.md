# CYLD

**CYLD** (cylindromatosis; 16q12.1) is a USP-family deubiquitinase specific for K63-linked and
linear (M1) ubiquitin chains. Its decisive act in cell-death signaling is deubiquitinating
[[RIPK1]] inside TNFR1 [[TNFR1 complex I|complex I]] — dissolving the survival scaffold and
releasing TRADD–[[RIPK1]] to nucleate [[TNFR1 complex II]]. Without CYLD there is no complex I →
II transition; with excessive CYLD activity, TNF responses default to death.

## Structure & Domains

Three CAP-Gly domains (microtubule/substrate targeting) plus a C-terminal USP catalytic domain
(Cys601) that hydrolyzes K63/M1 linkages on [[RIPK1]], [[TRAF2]], TRAF6 and NEMO, opposing the
ligase activity of [[cIAPs]]/LUBAC at every step.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Complex-I dissolution.** CYLD removes the K63 chains [[cIAPs]] built on [[RIPK1]], collapsing
  the IKK-recruitment platform and freeing TRADD–[[RIPK1]] to bind [[FADD]]–[[Caspase-8]]–[[c-FLIP]]
  as [[TNFR1 complex II]].
- **Necroptosis licensing.** The same deubiquitination exposes [[RIPK1]]'s RHIM/DD for necrosome
  and [[Ripoptosome]] incorporation — CYLD is therefore pro-necroptotic whenever [[Caspase-8]]
  activity is limiting.
- **NF-κB restraint.** By trimming TRAF2/TRAF6/NEMO chains, CYLD terminates IKK signaling; it is one
  of the few DUBs whose loss causes *increased* NF-κB output.

## Physiological Function

CYLD times the TNF response: early complex-I survival/gene-activation first, CYLD-gated death
checkpoint second. *Cyld⁻/⁻* cells are TNF-resistant; CYLD overactivity drives sterile inflammation
via excess complex-II flux.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Tumor suppressor:** germline *CYLD* loss causes familial cylindromatosis (skin adnexal tumors);
  somatic loss/mutation appears in myeloma, liver and colon cancers with hyperactive NF-κB.
- **Inflammation:** impaired CYLD → unrestrained complex I → chronic NF-κB; excessive CYLD →
  unrestrained complex II → epithelial death and inflammatory disease — both directions pathogenic.
