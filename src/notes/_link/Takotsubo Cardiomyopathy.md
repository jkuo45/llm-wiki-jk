# Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy

**Takotsubo cardiomyopathy** (stress cardiomyopathy, broken-heart syndrome) is an acute, reversible left ventricular systolic dysfunction that mimics acute myocardial infarction but occurs in the absence of obstructive coronary artery disease. It is classically triggered by intense emotional or physical stress and is driven by a surge of [[Catecholamines]] — particularly [[Epinephrine]] and [[Norepinephrine]] — that directly injures the [[Myocardium]].

## Etiology & Triggers

Physical triggers include critical illness (sepsis, subarachnoid hemorrhage, [[Ischemia-reperfusion Injury]]), surgery, high-dose exogenous catecholamines (vasopressors for [[Anaphylaxis]], [[Cardiac arrest]], or septic shock), and [[Pheochromocytoma]]. Emotional triggers — acute grief, fear, anger, surprise — activate the [[Sympathetic Nervous System]], releasing norepinephrine from cardiac sympathetic nerves and epinephrine from the [[Adrenal medulla]]. The stereotypical apical ballooning pattern (akinesis of apex with hyperdynamic base) may arise from a base-to-apex gradient of β-adrenergic receptor density: apical [[Cardiomyocyte|cardiomyocytes]] have higher β2-AR density, which at supraphysiologic catecholamine concentrations switches from Gs to Gi signaling, producing negative inotropy (a "stunned" apex).

## Pathophysiology: Adrenochrome and Lipid Peroxidation

The pathophysiologic cascade converges on [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Lipid Peroxidation]]:

1. **Catecholamine autoxidation** — High catecholamine concentrations drive their oxidation to [[Adrenochrome]] (from epinephrine) and related [[Aminochromes]]. This process generates [[Superoxide]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] via one-electron reduction of O₂ by the semiquinone radical intermediate (see [[Adrenochrome Semiquinone Radical]]).
2. **Fenton chemistry** — H₂O₂ in the presence of catalytic [[Iron]] (Fe²⁺) produces the [[Hydroxyl radical]] (•OH) via the [[Fenton Reaction]].
3. **Lipid peroxidation initiation** — •OH abstracts bis-allylic hydrogens from [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids]] in cardiomyocyte [[Cell Membranes]], initiating a chain reaction that generates [[Lipid peroxyl radical|lipid peroxyl radicals]] and [[Lipid hydroperoxide|lipid hydroperoxides]].
4. **Membrane damage** — Peroxidized membranes lose ion selectivity, causing [[Calcium Overload|calcium overload]], impaired [[SERCA2a]] function, and contractile dysfunction.
5. **Amplification** — [[Adrenochrome]] redox-cycles with [[NADPH]] and depletes [[Glutathione]], impairing membrane repair and sustaining [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

This mechanism is documented in `notes/adrenochrome/Peroxidation.md` and `notes/oxidative-stress/Lipid Peroxidation.md` (see the Connection to Catecholamine-Induced Oxidative Damage section). The catalytic nature of adrenochrome redox cycling amplifies damage beyond what stoichiometric catecholamine oxidation would produce.

## Clinical Presentation & Diagnosis

Patients present with chest pain, dyspnea, and ST-segment elevation or deep T-wave inversion on ECG — indistinguishable from ST-elevation [[Myocardial infarction]]. Troponin is elevated (modestly, often discordant with the degree of wall-motion abnormality). Coronary angiography shows normal or non-obstructive coronaries. Ventriculography or echocardiography reveals the characteristic apical ballooning (takotsubo, "octopus trap" in Japanese) with hyperkinetic basal segments. [[Cardiac Magnetic Resonance]] may show myocardial edema without delayed gadolinium enhancement (distinguishing it from infarction or myocarditis).

## Prognosis & Management

Most patients recover left ventricular function within days to weeks with supportive care: beta-blockers (to reduce adrenergic drive), ACE inhibitors/ARBs, and diuretics for congestion. [[Heart Failure]] management is guideline-directed. Avoid catecholaminergic inotropes. Recurrence rates are ~5–10% over 5 years. Rare complications include cardiogenic shock, left ventricular outflow tract obstruction, apical thrombus, and ventricular rupture. In-hospital mortality is ~2–5%, similar to age-matched myocardial infarction.
