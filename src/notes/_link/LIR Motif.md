# LIR Motif

## Overview

The [[LIR Motif]] (LC3-interacting region; also called the ATG8-interacting motif, AIM) is a short linear peptide sequence found in autophagy receptors and numerous cargo and regulatory proteins. It mediates direct binding to ATG8-family proteins—[[LC3]] and GABARAP/GABARAPL proteins—on the surface of the phagophore and mature [[Autophagosome]], thereby tethering cargo into the degrading organelle.

## Structure & Sequence

A canonical LIR consists of an aromatic residue (W/Y/F), a hydrophobic residue (L/I/V), and a negatively charged residue (D/E), arranged as [W/Y/F]–X–X–[L/I/V]–[D/E]. Optimal binding requires an upstream serine or threonine that can be phosphorylated to modulate affinity. Structural studies show the motif inserts into a hydrophobic pocket on the ATG8 surface.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

LIR-containing receptors such as [[p62/SQSTM1]], NBR1, OPTN, and NDP52 recognize ubiquitinated substrates and, through their LIR, dock them onto LC3-II–decorated phagophores. This couples [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System|ubiquitylation]] to selective [[Autophagy]], enabling mitophagy, aggrephagy, and xenophagy. Phosphorylation of the LIR (e.g., by [[ULK1]], [[TBK1]], or [[AMPK]]) dynamically regulates cargo recruitment and autophagic throughput. The motif is also used by autophagy regulators—including components of the [[Vps34]] complex—to localize to autophagic membranes.

## Physiological Function

The LIR motif is the principal molecular adapter that confers selectivity on autophagy, allowing the cell to target specific substrates while sparing healthy constituents. It is essential for organelle quality control and protection against [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Mutated or dysregulated LIR interactions impair selective autophagy and contribute to [[Cancer]], neurodegeneration (e.g., failure to clear α-synuclein), and infection. Engineered LIR peptides are investigated as tools to redirect cargo to autophagosomes.
