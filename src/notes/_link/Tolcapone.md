# Tolcapone

**Tolcapone** is a potent, centrally-acting [[COMT Inhibitors|COMT inhibitor]] of the nitrocatechol class. It is used as an adjunct to [[Levodopa]]/carbidopa therapy in [[Parkinson's Disease]] and is the most potent clinical COMT inhibitor available.

## Mechanism

Tolcapone inhibits COMT with an IC₅₀ of ~0.048 µM — approximately 5× more potent than [[Entacapone]] (~0.23 µM) and ~50–100× more potent than [[Fisetin]] (2.6–5.8 µM). Unlike entacapone, tolcapone crosses the [[Blood-Brain Barrier|blood-brain barrier]] and inhibits both peripheral and central COMT, providing more complete catecholamine metabolism blockade.

## Hepatotoxicity Risk

Tolcapone carries a black box warning for hepatotoxicity. Rare but fatal cases of acute liver failure have been reported, requiring regular liver function monitoring. This risk limits its use compared to entacapone and the newer opicapone.

## Comparison with Fisetin

> [!info] Source: [[task_output_comt_fisetin_senolytic_09_July_2026|COMT × Fisetin Research Evaluation]]
> [[Fisetin]] has a COMT IC₅₀ of 2.6–5.8 µM — approximately 50–100× weaker than tolcapone (~0.048 µM). However, fisetin's mixed-type inhibition, substrate-inhibitor mechanism (generating [[Geraldol]]), and additional senolytic/antioxidant properties create a fundamentally different pharmacological profile. Fisetin at senolytic doses may transiently approach COMT-inhibitory concentrations in specific tissues (brain, GI tract).
