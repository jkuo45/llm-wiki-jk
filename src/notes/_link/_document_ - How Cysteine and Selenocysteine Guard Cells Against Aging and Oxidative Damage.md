# How Cysteine and Selenocysteine Guard Cells Against Aging and Oxidative Damage

> [!info]
> Source: Pace M, Giorgi C, Lombardozzi G, Cimini A, Castelli V, d'Angelo M. **How Cysteine and Selenocysteine Guard Cells Against Aging and Oxidative Damage.** *Biomolecules* 15(8):1115 (2025). doi:10.3390/biom15081115

## Overview

This 2025 narrative review reframes aging not simply as cumulative [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] damage but as a progressive loss of **redox resilience** — the capacity to sense, respond to, and recover from oxidative challenges. Two structurally related but functionally distinct thiol/selenol-containing amino acids anchor this defense:

- **[[Cysteine]]** — the rate-limiting precursor for [[Glutathione]] (GSH), the cell's main antioxidant buffer, and a dynamic redox switch in proteins.
- **[[Selenocysteine]]** — the 21st amino acid, embedded as the catalytic residue of powerful redox enzymes including [[Glutathione Peroxidase]] and [[Thioredoxin reductase]].

## Biochemical distinction: thiol vs selenol

> [!important]
> [[Selenocysteine]]'s selenol group has a far lower pKa (~5.2) than [[Cysteine]]'s thiol (~8.3). At physiological pH the selenol exists predominantly in its reactive deprotonated form, making Sec a superior nucleophile and electrophile. This explains why selenoenzymes such as GPx and TrxR achieve catalytic rates that cysteine-containing analogs cannot match.

## Selenocysteine translational machinery

Incorporation of [[Selenocysteine]] is a major biosynthetic investment:

- **[[UGA codon]]** recoding — the stop codon is reinterpreted as Sec.
- **SECIS element** — a structured RNA motif in the 3′UTR of selenoprotein mRNAs.
- **[[SECISBP2]]** (SBP2) — binds the SECIS element.
- **eEFSec** — the dedicated selenocysteine-specific elongation factor that delivers tRNA[Ser]Sec to the ribosome.

## Cysteine roles

- **Glutathione synthesis:** [[Cysteine]] availability directly governs intracellular [[Glutathione]] (GSH) levels; it is the rate-limiting substrate of GSH biosynthesis.
- **Redox switches:** protein cysteine residues undergo reversible oxidation to sulfenic acid (–SOH), modulating signaling; irreversible over-oxidation to sulfinic/sulfonic acid can inactivate enzymes. [[Peroxiredoxin]] and protein disulfide isomerase rely on these cysteine-based mechanisms.
- **Extracellular redox couple:** the Cys/CySS (cysteine/cystine) couple is a marker of systemic redox state, distinct from and not in equilibrium with the intracellular GSH/GSSG pair.

## Aging, redox decline, and disease

With aging, mitochondrial dysfunction amplifies [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] production while the capacity to regenerate [[Glutathione]] and maintain [[Selenocysteine|selenoprotein]] expression declines. The review connects this redox imbalance to the onset and progression of **neurodegenerative diseases** and systemic functional deterioration.

> [!warning]
> The redox theory of aging distinguishes **harmful oxidative damage** from physiologically beneficial **oxidative eustress** — low-level ROS signaling that supports cellular adaptation. Maintaining the proper balance requires adequate, regulated supply of both [[Cysteine]] and [[Selenocysteine]] throughout life.

## Supplementation evidence

- **N-acetylcysteine ([[N-Acetylcysteine|NAC]])** — neuroprotective effects observed in aged rodent models.
- **[[Selenocysteine]] dietary supplementation** — extends lifespan and increases oxidative-stress resistance in *C. elegans* models.
- **GlyNAC ([[Glycine]] + NAC)** — in older human adults, demonstrated restoration of GSH levels, reduced oxidative-stress markers, improved mitochondrial function, and favorable effects on aging biomarkers in clinical studies.

## Study limitations (per authors)

As a narrative review it performs no systematic meta-analysis and does not quantify effect sizes. Human evidence for GlyNAC and [[Selenocysteine]] supplementation comes from small or short-duration trials, and the translational relevance of *C. elegans* longevity findings to humans remains uncertain.
