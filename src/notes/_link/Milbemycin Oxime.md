# Milbemycin Oxime

**Milbemycin oxime** is a macrocyclic lactone anthelmintic of the **milbemycin** class (the 5-oxime derivative of milbemycin, a fermentation product of *Streptomyces hygroscopicus*). It is the ivermectin-equivalent backbone of the monthly canine and feline heartworm preventives Interceptor and Sentinel, covering heartworm plus the common intestinal worms. Among all the ivermectin-like (macrocyclic lactone) drugs, it has the **widest safety margin at label doses — including in dogs carrying the [[P-gp]] (MDR1/ABCB1) mutation** — which is why it is the standard recommendation when ivermectin is considered risky for a herding-breed dog.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

Milbemycins share the 16-membered macrocyclic lactone scaffold of the [[Avermectin]]s but differ in the absence of the oleandrose disaccharide. Milbemycin oxime is the oxime at position 5, a modification that markedly improves oral absorption — milbemycin itself is poorly absorbed, and the oxime pro-drug is what makes once-monthly oral dosing feasible. Dogs and cats absorb it well from the gut, and tissue levels accumulate between monthly doses.

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

Mechanistically, milbemycin oxime is directly analogous to [[Ivermectin]]: it potentiates invertebrate glutamate-gated chloride channels (and interacts with GABA-gated channels), producing sustained Cl⁻ influx, flaccid paralysis, and death of nematodes and some arthropods. Mammalian channels are structurally distinct and protected by the blood–brain barrier in concert with P-glycoprotein efflux. It is active against larval and adult stages of the species listed below and has a long enough effective half-life in dogs for once-monthly dosing.

## Applications

- **Heartworm prevention** (*Dirofilaria immitis*): monthly, label dose 0.5–0.99 mg/kg (Interceptor / Sentinel in dogs; Interceptor in cats).
- **Intestinal nematodes in dogs**: roundworms (*Toxocara canis*), hookworms (*Ancylostoma caninum*), and whipworms (*Trichuris vulpis*).
- **Combination products**: Interceptor Plus adds [[praziquantel]] for tapeworms; Sentinel adds lufenuron (flea control via sterile eggs). It does **not** kill adult heartworms, and is not effective against fleas, ticks, or ear mites on its own.

## Safety in Dogs and the MDR1 Gene

Milbemycin oxime is a P-glycoprotein substrate, but at label preventive doses it is the safest of the macrocyclic lactones, with label-safety studies demonstrating tolerance even in ivermectin-sensitive Collies — some studies report no adverse effects at multiples (≈5–20×) of the preventive dose. This makes it the **preferred heartworm preventive for dogs homozygous for the *ABCB1-1Δ* (MDR1) deletion** (collies, Australian shepherds, Shelties, and related herding breeds). As with all macrocyclic lactones, accidental massive overdose or concurrent P-glycoprotein inhibition (e.g., spinosad, ketoconazole-type azoles) should still be avoided in affected breeds.

## Connections

- [[Ivermectin]]: Parallel mechanism and heartworm-preventive role; milbemycin oxime's oxime scaffold and milligram dosing give it the class's widest safety margin in MDR1 dogs.
- [[Avermectin]]: Closely related macrocyclic lactone family; milbemycins differ chiefly by lacking the disaccharide and by their *Streptomyces hygroscopicus* origin.
- [[Moxidectin]]: Fellow milbemycin; more potent and far longer-acting, so moxidectin's margin is narrower and less suitable for MDR1-affected dogs than milbemycin oxime's.
- [[Selamectin]]: Topical avermectin-family alternative with excellent MDR1-dog safety; milbemycin oxime adds whipworm coverage and is oral.
- [[Doramectin]]: Livestock-oriented avermectin relative with a poor margin in MDR1 dogs — not a dog-label product.
- [[P-gp]]: P-glycoprotein efflux keeps macrocyclic lactones out of the CNS; milbemycin oxime's margin reflects both efflux and its intrinsically lower potency at mammalian channels.
- [[Fenbendazole]]: Benzimidazole alternative for intestinal worms that is fully P-gp-independent, but with no heartworm activity.
