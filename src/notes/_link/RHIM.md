# RHIM

The **RIP homotypic interaction motif (RHIM)** is a short protein–protein interaction module shared by [[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]], [[ZBP1]], and [[TRIF]] that nucleates amyloid-like signaling complexes — most notably the [[necrosome]] that executes [[Necroptosis]].

## Structure

The RHIM core is a ~18–22 residue segment centered on a highly conserved I/V-Q-I/V-G tetrad flanked by hydrophobic residues. RHIM–RHIM contacts stack into cross-β fibrils, giving the necrosome its amyloid-like stability. Mammalian RHIM carriers: RIPK1, RIPK3, ZBP1/DAI, and TRIF (TICAM1).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **Classical necrosome:** [[TNFR1]] → deubiquitinated [[RIPK1]] exposes its RHIM → RHIM–RHIM recruitment and phosphorylation of [[RIPK3]] → [[MLKL]] recruitment, phosphorylation, oligomerization, and membrane rupture.
- **Non-classical necrosomes (RIPK1-independent):** [[ZBP1]] senses viral z-RNA and [[TRIF]] downstream of [[TLR3]]/[[TLR4]] bind [[RIPK3]] directly via their own RHIMs.
- **Suppression:** active [[Caspase-8]] (often as Caspase-8/c-FLIP heterodimer) cleaves RIPK1, RIPK3, and TRIF, dissolving RHIM scaffolds and flipping signaling back toward [[Apoptosis]].

## Physiological Function

RHIM scaffolding converts transient receptor stimuli (TNF, dsRNA, LPS) into a stable, all-or-none death/inflammation decision, coupling pathogen sensing to lytic cell death and [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|DAMP]] release.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Viral RHIM mimics (e.g. MCMV M45/vIRA) block RHIM–RHIM assembly to evade necroptosis. Mutations disrupting the RHIM tetrad abolish necrosome formation. RHIM amyloids are therapeutic targets for inflammatory tissue injury.
