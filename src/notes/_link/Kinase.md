# Kinase

[[Kinase|Kinases]] are enzymes that catalyze the transfer of the γ-phosphate of ATP to hydroxyl groups of protein, lipid, or sugar substrates, constituting the largest enzyme superfamily in eukaryotes. Protein kinases regulate proliferation, differentiation, metabolism, apoptosis, and the cell cycle, and are major drug targets.

## Classification

- **Protein kinases**: Serine/threonine (e.g., [[Aurora Kinase|Aurora]], PKC, Raf), tyrosine (e.g., receptor tyrosine kinases), and dual-specificity.
- **Lipid/sugar kinases**: PI3K, hexokinase, etc.

## Shared Adenosine-Binding Site with Sirtuins

Sirtuins and protein kinases both contain an adenosine/ATP-binding site. This structural overlap allowed screening of known [[Kinase Inhibitor|kinase inhibitors]] against sirtuins, identifying scaffolds such as bisindolylmaleimides ([[Ro31-8220]]) and indolinones ([[GW5074]]) as [[SIRT1]]/[[SIRT2]] inhibitors active in the low micromolar range.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- Deregulated kinase activity (mutations, amplification, translocations) drives many cancers.
- Kinase inhibitors are among the most successful targeted cancer therapeutics; sirtuin cross-reactivity is a relevant off-target consideration.
