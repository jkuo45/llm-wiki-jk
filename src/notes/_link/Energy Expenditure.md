# Energy Expenditure

**Energy Expenditure** is the total energy an organism consumes metabolically per unit time. In humans the daily energy budget is conventionally divided into three components:

1. **[[Resting Metabolic Rate|Resting metabolic rate (RMR)]]** — the largest component, ~60 % of daily expenditure in sedentary Western adults.
2. **Thermic effect of food** (heat increment of feeding) — energy cost of digestion, absorption, and postprandial metabolism; proportional to food intake and influenced by dietary protein content.
3. **Physical activity energy expenditure** — energy cost of volitional exercise plus [[Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis|non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT)]] (Levine _et al_. 1999, 2000).

## Measurement

- **[[Doubly-Labelled Water|Doubly-labelled water (DLW)]]** — the criterion field method: isotopically labelled water ([²H] and [¹⁸O]) tracks total daily CO₂ production over 1–3 weeks, giving a measure of free-living daily energy expenditure (Schoeller _et al_. 1986; Speakman, 1997).
- **Indirect calorimetry** — measures O₂ consumption and CO₂ production (respiratory quotient) to compute energy expenditure during RMR measurement or exercise.
- **Direct calorimetry** — measures heat loss directly; technically demanding and rarely used.

## Comparative Energetics

DLW data show a DEE:RMR ratio ([[Physical Activity Level|physical activity level]], PAL) of ~1·66 in contemporary Western adults (_n_ 574; Black _et al_. 1996), meaning ~60 % of energy is spent at rest. Free-living wild mammals average ~2·6 × RMR (range 1·4–7·5), expending only ~32 % of their daily budget at rest (Ricklefs _et al_. 1996; Nagy _et al_. 1999; Speakman, 2000). Allometric prediction for a 70 kg human is ~18·7 MJ/d versus an actual Western average of ~11·7 MJ/d; male Gambian farm workers expend ~16·2 MJ/d.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review argues that reductions in physical activity — not reductions in RMR — best explain the low daily energy expenditure of modern Western societies and the [[Obesity|obesity]] epidemic, and quantifies why exercise alone (≈5 % of weekly expenditure) must act through NEAT, appetite, and RMR amplification.

## Exercise Effects

Vigorous exercise (jogging/rowing ≈ 2·9 MJ/h for a 70 kg subject, ≈10 × BMR) performed as three 30-min sessions/week adds only ~4·35 MJ — about 5·3 % of a weekly budget of ~81·9 MJ. Matching the expenditure of primitive societies would require ~90 min of vigorous activity daily, and wild-animal levels ~2·5 h daily. Because real-world exercise reliably drives weight loss, its effects must be amplified via behavioural (NEAT) changes, appetite, and resting metabolism ([[Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption|EPOC]], training-induced RMR increases).
