# DNA Ligase IV

**DNA ligase IV (LIG4)** is an ATP-dependent DNA ligase that catalyzes the final phosphodiester-bond sealing step of [[Non-homologous End Joining|non-homologous end joining (NHEJ)]], the dominant pathway for repairing [[Double-Strand Break|DNA double-strand breaks]] in G1 phase and in post-mitotic cells. Ligase IV functions with its cofactor XRCC4 and the accessory factor XLF (Cernunnos) to ligate the two broken ends after processing by Artemis, polymerases, and the [[Ku70|Ku70/80]] complex. Loss of ligase IV causes profound genomic instability, immunodeficiency, and radiosensitivity (LIG4 syndrome).

## Association with SIRT3-mediated DNA repair

The SIRT3 review describes a transcriptional/epigenetic program in which SIRT3 promotes efficient NHEJ: [[SIRT3]] facilitates [[Double-Strand Break|DNA double-strand break]] repair by enhancing recruitment of [[53BP1]], promoting NHEJ efficiency[95], and facilitates H3K56 deacetylation to enhance NHEJ fidelity[55]. In the figure summarizing SIRT3's DNA repair network, SIRT3 interacts with critical DNA repair proteins such as [[PARP1]], [[Ku70|Ku70/80]], [[XRCC1]], and DNA ligase 4, facilitating the repair of [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] damage. Ligase IV is therefore the terminal enzymatic effector of the NHEJ pathway whose upstream recruitment and fidelity are supported by SIRT3-dependent chromatin modifications.

## Clinical relevance

NHEJ fidelity declines with age and is a determinant of genomic stability in stem cells and post-mitotic tissues. SIRT3-dependent support of ligase IV-mediated repair connects mitochondrial redox/metabolic status to nuclear genome maintenance.
