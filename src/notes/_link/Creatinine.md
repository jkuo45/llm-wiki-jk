**Creatinine** is a cyclic, nitrogenous waste product formed from the non-enzymatic dehydration of creatine and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|phosphocreatine]] in muscle. It is produced at a rate proportional to muscle mass and is freely filtered by the renal glomeruli, making it the most commonly employed endogenous marker of [[Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)|glomerular filtration rate (GFR)]].

## Metabolism & Production

- Creatinine is generated spontaneously (non-enzymatically) from [[Creatine]] and [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)|PCr]] at a relatively stable rate of ~1–2% of the total creatine pool per day.
- Production is directly proportional to total body muscle mass; men typically exhibit higher serum creatinine than women, and older adults with [[Sarcopenia]] show lower levels.
- Dietary creatine intake (from red meat, Fish) transiently elevates serum creatinine, but this does not reflect impaired renal function.

## Clinical Significance

### Renal Function Biomarker

- Serum creatinine is the cornerstone of routine renal function assessment, used to estimate GFR via equations such as Cockcroft–Gault, MDRD, and CKD-EPI.
- **Limitations:** Creatinine is influenced by muscle mass, protein intake, and certain drugs (e.g., [[Cimetidine]], [[Trimethoprim]]) that inhibit tubular secretion, leading to overestimation of renal function in cachectic or elderly patients.

### Cardiovascular Risk

- Even mildly elevated creatinine within the normal range is an independent risk factor for [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular disease]] and [[Mortality]].

### Creatine Supplementation & Creatinine

- Creatine supplementation increases serum creatinine transiently because a fraction of the ingested creatine is converted non-enzymatically to creatinine. This benign elevation does **not** indicate kidney damage in healthy individuals — a point underscored by multiple safety reviews.
- The distinction between benign creatine-induced creatinine elevation and pathological [[Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)|acute kidney injury]] can be made by checking [[Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)|BUN]], [[Cystatin C]], and urine sediment.

- [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)]]: The phosphorylated form of creatine that also contributes to creatinine formation.
- [[Kidney]]: Primary organ of creatinine excretion; serum creatinine concentration is determined by GFR.
- [[Sarcopenia]]: Reduced muscle mass in sarcopenia lowers creatinine production, complicating GFR estimation.
- [[Cardiovascular Disease]]: Elevated creatinine is a marker of renal impairment and an independent cardiovascular risk factor.
