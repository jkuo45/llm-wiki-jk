# Anion Exchanger 1

**Anion exchanger 1** (AE1, erythrocyte band 3, *SLC4A1*) is the most abundant integral protein of the erythrocyte membrane. It exchanges HCO3−/Cl− across the membrane, anchors the cytoskeleton to the membrane (sustaining the biconcave shape), and carries a cytoplasmic N-terminal domain that death pathways cleave.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> AE1 provides membrane–cytoskeleton interactions and shape stability; caspase-3 can cleave its cytoplasmic N-terminal domain and [[Calpain]] degrades the protein, contributing to the blebbing and deformation of [[Eryptosis]]. Aging-related AE1 conformational change creates a senescence-specific antigen; IgG naturally occurring autoantibodies in healthy plasma largely target AE1, so AE1 loss of symmetry marks cells for immune clearance. Enhanced AE1–aquaporin-1 interaction supports osmotic resistance and limits accidental lysis. Long-term aluminum exposure induces AE1 degradation alongside PS exposure and Ca2+ elevation.

## Functions

- **CO2 transport:** completes the bicarbonate/chloride shift with [[Carbonic Anhydrase]] (~23% of CO2 carried this way).
- **Shape:** links spectrin/ankyrin network to membrane; loss of anchoring destabilizes the biconcave disc.
- **Clearance marker:** conformational change with age exposes a neo-antigen; AE1 is also a eryptosis-relevant protease substrate.
