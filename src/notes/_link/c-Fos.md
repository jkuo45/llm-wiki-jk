# c-Fos

**c-Fos** is an immediate-early response gene encoding the Fos protein, a core subunit of the AP-1 (activator protein-1) transcription factor heterodimer (with Jun family members). c-Fos is rapidly induced by mitogenic signaling and promotes cell-cycle progression and proliferation; as a proto-oncogene it is also linked to oncogene-induced transformation.

## Structure & Function

- **AP-1 complex**: Fos–Jun heterodimers bind AP-1 DNA sites to regulate genes controlling proliferation, differentiation, and stress responses.
- **Regulation**: c-Fos expression is tightly controlled at transcription (serum response element) and mRNA stability.

## Role in Senescence

- c-Fos mRNA is bound and stabilized by the RNA-binding protein [[HuR]] (human antigen R). In senescent/metabolically stressed cells, activated [[AMPK]] prevents HuR's nuclear-to-cytosolic translocation; with less cytosolic HuR, translation of c-Fos, [[Cyclin A]], and [[Cyclin B]] mRNA falls, contributing to the proliferative arrest that defines [[Senescence|senescence]].
- Loss of c-Fos/AP-1 activity is consistent with the growth-arrested, anti-proliferative state of senescent cells.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction in cellular senescence a bridge to neurodegenerative disease|Hruby & Higuchi-Sanabria 2025 — Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Senescence Bridges Neurodegeneration]]: Lists c-Fos among HuR-stabilized proliferative mRNAs whose reduced cytosolic translation (via AMPK-mediated HuR retention) aids senescence-associated arrest.
