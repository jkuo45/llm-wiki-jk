### Overview
INF2 (Inverted Formin 2) is an ER-bound formin that functions at [[Endoplasmic Reticulum|ER]]–mitochondria contact sites. It cooperates with Spire1C to regulate actin assembly required for mitochondrial constriction.

### Mechanism
At ER–mitochondria contact sites, INF2 nucleates and organizes actin filaments that drive initial constriction of the mitochondrion. This actin-dependent step precedes the recruitment and oligomerization of [[DRP1]], which then executes membrane scission during [[Mitochondrial Fission|fission]].

### Functional Significance
INF2 links cytoskeletal remodeling at membrane contact sites to organelle division, illustrating the spatial coordination between the ER and mitochondria that underpins [[Mitochondrial Integrity]].
