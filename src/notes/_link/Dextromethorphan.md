# Dextromethorphan

**Overview:** [[Dextromethorphan]] (DXM) is a synthetic morphinan derivative used over-the-counter as a centrally acting antitussive (cough suppressant). It is the dextrorotatory stereoisomer of levorphanol and lacks significant opioid analgesia or addiction liability at therapeutic doses.

**Pharmacodynamics:** DXM suppresses the cough reflex through actions in the medulla. Its primary metabolite, dextrorphan, is an [[N-methyl-D-aspartate|NMDA]] receptor antagonist, giving DXM downstream NMDA-antagonist and dissociative properties at high doses. DXM also inhibits serotonin reuptake and has activity at sigma-1 receptors, contributing serotonergic effects.

**Pharmacokinetics:** DXM is extensively metabolized by [[CYP2D6]] (and to a lesser extent [[CYP3A4]]) to dextrorphan. Poor and ultra-rapid metabolizers show marked inter-individual variability; concomitant CYP2D6 inhibitors raise DXM levels.

**Pathology and Clinical Relevance — Serotonin Syndrome Risk:** The most important safety concern is the interaction with serotonergic drugs. Combined use with a [[Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitor]] (such as [[Methylene blue]], phenelzine, or selegiline) can precipitate [[Serotonin Syndrome]]—a potentially life-threatening condition of hyperthermia, clonus, agitation, and autonomic instability. This combination is contraindicated. DXM is also abused at supratherapeutic doses for its dissociative effects, causing confusion, ataxia, and psychosis, and it carries abuse potential in adolescents.
