# Valine

**Valine** is one of the three [[Branched-Chain Amino Acids]] and an [[Essential Amino Acids|essential amino acid]]. Unlike [[Leucine]] and [[Isoleucine]], valine is fully glucogenic: its metabolism involves conversion to succinyl-CoA, which enters the [[TCA cycle]] to produce ATP. Restricting valine alone recapitulates many of the metabolic benefits of [[Protein Restriction]], while dysregulated valine catabolism contributes to [[Insulin Resistance]].

## Overview

Valine shares the branched-chain amino acid catabolic pathway (transamination followed by branched-chain α-ketoacid dehydrogenase) with leucine and isoleucine, but its carbon skeleton is routed exclusively to succinyl-CoA, making it a glucogenic amino acid that can support hepatic [[Gluconeogenesis]].

## Metabolism and Signaling

Valine is catabolized primarily in skeletal muscle. Its catabolic intermediate, 3-hydroxyisobutyrate (3-HIB), is produced during incomplete valine oxidation and has been shown to promote muscle insulin resistance in diabetic rats. Dysregulated valine catabolism therefore links high valine intake to systemic metabolic dysfunction.

## Valine Restriction

Restricting dietary valine alone (~66% reduction) produces robust metabolic benefits:

- Reduces body weight and improves metabolic health
- Reduces hepatic [[Cellular Senescence]]
- Extends lifespan of male mice (~23% in C57BL/6J mice)
- In female mice, valine restriction is protective in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] progression

> [!warning] Species Differences
> Human plasma analysis found reduced circulating valine is associated with aging and moderate cognitive impairment, but valine supplementation worsens glucose tolerance and induces gut inflammation and hepatic lipid deposition. The role of valine in healthy aging may vary between species.
