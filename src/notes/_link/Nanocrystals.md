# Nanocrystals

**Nanocrystals** are pure, carrier-free drug particles with diameters below 1 µm, dispersed (with a stabilizer) into a nanosuspension. Unlike [[Nanoparticles|nanoparticles]], they contain no matrix material — only active compound. Reducing particle size into the nanorange increases surface area and dissolution velocity (Noyes–Whitney), improving gastrointestinal absorption and [[Bioavailability|bioavailability]] of poorly soluble drugs.

## Fisetin Nanocrystals

- Dzakwan et al. (precipitation): solubility rose to 420 µg/mL vs. 60.57 µg/mL for free fisetin; particle size depended on stabilizer (polysorbate 80 < SLS < HPC < HPMC < Eudragit).
- Ma et al. (bottom-up solvent/antisolvent): 134.9–148.6 nm poloxamer-coated crystals; IC<sub>85</sub> against EA.hy926 endothelial cells decreased 4.8- to 5.6-fold; enhanced [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] and anti-[[Angiogenesis|angiogenic]] effect. Stable 120 days (−80 °C) / 30 days (5 °C).

> [!info] Carrier-free advantage
> Fisetin [[Nanocrystals|nanocrystals]] were the most cytotoxic formulation tested against Lewis lung carcinoma (3LL) and EA.hy926 cells in the reviewed in vitro panel — high drug loading with no inert carrier mass.
