# Colloidal Silver

**Colloidal silver** is a suspension of [[Silver]] nanoparticles, typically
1–100 nm, stabilised by charge or by a surface coating and dispersed in
aqueous solution. It has no approved medicinal use anywhere. It is sold as a
dietary supplement, a topical wound dressing (in the form of
[[Silver sulfadiazine]] or nanocrystalline dressings), and a
water purifier chemical, and the antimicrobial claim is the same in all three
marketing contexts: silver ions released from the particles damage microbial
cell membranes and enzyme systems.

## The antimicrobial claim

The mechanism is real in vitro. Silver cations Ag⁺ bind avidly to thiol
groups on microbial proteins, denaturing enzymes and disrupting membrane
transport; the oligodynamic effect means the potency per unit concentration
is high. Broad-spectrum activity against bacteria, fungi, and viruses in
culture is consistently reproducible.

> [!warning] The in vitro–in vivo gap
> The clinical gap is not subtle. Randomized and systematic-review evidence
> has not shown colloidal silver to treat infection, wound infection, or
> sepsis at doses anyone can tolerate orally, and the US FDA has taken action
> against colloidal silver products for unapproved disease claims, having
> previously determined there was no OTC topical silver product with
> substantial scientific evidence of efficacy. Key failures of the in vitro
> claim in vivo: (1) the free Ag⁺ fraction that actually kills microbes is
> tiny and is immediately scavenged by chloride, sulfide, and protein thiols;
> (2) biofilms and wound beds are diffusion-limited in ways that plate assays
> are not; (3) the same thiol reactivity that kills bacteria also injures host
> cells; (4) dose required in culture is orders of magnitude above the dose
> that chronic human exposure supports.

> [!info] Drug interaction hazard
> Silver binds thiols and other ligands avidly and has documented
> pathological interactions with antibiotics and with thyroxine, disabling
> both. This is a specific and underappreciated reason to avoid the product
> beyond cosmetic risk — an interaction can be clinically consequential in a
> patient who is also on anticoagulant, antibiotic, or thyroid therapy.

## Toxicity: argyria and beyond

Chronic ingestion or inhalation of silver compounds produces [[Argyria]] —
irreversible blue-grey discolouration of sun-exposed skin, conjunctiva, and
other light-exposed mucosa — from deposition of microscopic metallic silver
and silver sulfide particles in the dermis. Exposure can continue for
years before discolouration appears, and stopping exposure does not reverse
it, because the deposited metal cannot be cleared. Some cases of localized
argyria follow from topical silver or silver-containing eye drops in
childhood.

The US EPA reference dose for total silver exposure is 5 μg/(kg·d), and the
US Tolerable Upper Intake Level for silver in food is likewise low. Beyond
argyria, reported concerns include silver deposition in the cornea and lens,
possible kidney function decrement, and interference with the absorption of
thiamin and other ligands.

> [!warning] Historical context matters here
> Silver nitrate was a standard antiseptic and a standard caustic treatment for
> urethral stricture and for neonatal eye prophylaxis until antibiotics
> displaced it, and silver sulfadiazine cream remains a standard burn
> antimicrobial. That institutional history is real and is the strongest
> legitimate use of silver pharmacology. It is also the reason colloidal
> silver as an oral "alternative medicine" product persists — the historic
> use is being read backwards as an endorsement of a modern product that is
  pharmacologically different and dosed far above any historic exposure.

## Connections

- [[Silver]] — the bulk element whose ionic form provides the antimicrobial
  chemistry; colloidal silver is a delivery form for it rather than a
  distinct pharmacological entity.
- [[Argyria]] — the dose- and duration-dependent consequence of silver
  deposition in light-exposed tissue; irreversible, and the main reason the
  risk–benefit arithmetic of oral colloidal silver fails.
- [[Silver ion]] — the species actually responsible for thiol-protein binding
  and antimicrobial activity, released in small amounts from the nanoparticle
  surface.
- [[Silver nanoparticles]] — the particulate form used in antimicrobial
  coatings and dressings, where the same oligodynamic chemistry is applied
  locally and systemically rather than chronically and orally.
- [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] — both silver and peroxide chemistry are used
  together in some wound dressings; the interaction between transition-metal
  catalysis and ROS is the same chemistry that drives the [[Fenton Reaction]]
  and the [[Adrenochrome]] quinone cycling in this vault.
- [[Multidrug Resistance]] — silver resistance in clinical isolates is
  increasingly documented and is mechanistically distinct from antibiotic
  resistance, which is one reason the "silver as an alternative to
  antibiotics" argument has weakened in the infection literature.
