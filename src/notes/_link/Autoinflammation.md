# Autoinflammation

**Autoinflammation** refers to dysregulated activation of the innate immune system — particularly the [[NLRP3]] inflammasome and [[IL-1β]] pathway — without the adaptive immune involvement (autoantibodies, autoreactive T cells) that characterizes autoimmune disease. Autoinflammatory diseases are driven by excessive or uncontrolled production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, especially [[IL-1β]], leading to recurrent episodes of systemic and organ-specific inflammation.

## Mechanism

The core mechanism of autoinflammation involves:

1. **Inflammasome Hyperactivation** — Genetic mutations or acquired triggers cause constitutive or excessive activation of the [[NLRP3]] inflammasome
2. **IL-1β Overproduction** — Uncontrolled [[Caspase-1]] activity leads to excessive cleavage and secretion of mature [[IL-1β]]
3. **Loss of Anti-inflammatory Regulation** — Impaired endogenous [[IL-1Ra]] (IL-1 receptor antagonist) production or IL-1R2 decoy receptor function fails to counterbalance IL-1 signaling
4. **Sterile Inflammation** — Inflammation occurs in the absence of infection, driven by endogenous danger signals (DAMPs) such as [[Uric Acid Crystals]], [[Silica]], oxidized [[Cardiolipin]], and mitochondrial DNA

## Autoinflammatory vs. Autoimmune Disease

| Feature | Autoinflammation | Autoimmunity |
|---------|-----------------|--------------|
| Immune arm | Innate immunity | Adaptive immunity |
| Key mediators | IL-1β, IL-18, inflammasomes | Autoantibodies, autoreactive T cells |
| HLA association | Weak/absent | Strong |
| Examples | CAPS, FMF, gout | RA, SLE, type 1 diabetes |
| Treatment | IL-1 blockade | B cell depletion, T cell modulation |

## Prototypical Autoinflammatory Diseases

### Cryopyrin-Associated Periodic Syndromes (CAPS)

Monogenic autoinflammatory diseases caused by gain-of-function mutations in *NLRP3*:
- **Familial Cold Autoinflammatory Syndrome (FCAS)** — urticaria, fever, and arthralgia triggered by cold exposure
- **Muckle-Wells Syndrome (MWS)** — episodic fever, urticaria, sensorineural deafness, and amyloidosis
- **Neonatal-Onset Multisystem Inflammatory Disease (NOMID)** — severe, continuous inflammation from birth

All CAPS subtypes respond dramatically to IL-1 blockade with [[Anakinra]], [[Canakinumab]], or [[Rilonacept]].

### Familial Mediterranean Fever (FMF)

Autosomal recessive mutations in *MEFV* (pyrin) cause dysregulated [[IL-1β]] activation. Characterized by recurrent episodes of fever, serositis, and erythema. Treated with colchicine and IL-1 inhibitors.

### Gout

[[Uric Acid Crystals]] activate [[NLRP3]] in joints, causing acute inflammatory flares. The prototypical acquired autoinflammatory disease.

### TNF Receptor-Associated Periodic Syndrome (TRAPS)

Mutations in *TNFRSF1A* cause impaired TNF receptor shedding and enhanced IL-1β production.

## Autoinflammation and Aging

Chronic low-grade autoinflammation — driven by persistent NLRP3 activation from endogenous DAMPs (oxidized [[Cardiolipin]], mtDNA, [[Uric Acid Crystals]]) — is a major component of [[Inflammaging]]. The [[CANTOS Trial]] demonstrated that reducing autoinflammatory IL-1β signaling decreases cardiovascular events, directly linking autoinflammation to age-related disease.

## Therapeutic Approaches

- **IL-1 Blockade**: [[Anakinra]] (IL-1Ra), [[Canakinumab]] (anti-IL-1β), [[Rilonacept]] (IL-1 trap)
- **NLRP3 Inhibition**: Emerging small-molecule NLRP3 inhibitors (e.g., MCC950/CRID3)
- **Colchicine**: Inhibits inflammasome assembly; standard for FMF and gout
- **JAK Inhibitors**: Block downstream cytokine signaling
- **Corticosteroids**: Broad immunosuppression; used as bridge therapy
