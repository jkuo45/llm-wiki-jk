# MAML1

**Mastermind-like 1 (MAML1)** is the human orthologue of *Drosophila* Mastermind and the principal transcriptional co-activator of [[Notch]] signalling. It acts as a molecular bridge: its N-terminal domain binds the interface formed by the Notch intracellular domain ([[Notch|N1ICD]]) and the DNA-binding factor CSL/RBPJ, nucleating a ternary complex at Notch target promoters, while its C-terminal activation domain recruits the histone acetyltransferases p300/CBP to switch those promoters on. In the SASP-induction scheme of Birch and Gil, MAML1 appears with N1ICD in the nuclear control layer regulating trafficking-gene expression — the Notch effector arm that restrains the late, highly inflammatory phase of the [[SASP|senescence-associated secretory phenotype]].

## Structure & Domains

MAML1 is a largely intrinsically disordered protein of roughly 1,000 residues, a property that allows it to wrap around the composite surface formed by CSL and the ankyrin repeat domain of N1ICD. Three functional regions are recognised. The N-terminal basic domain (approximately residues 13–74) is necessary and sufficient for high-affinity binding to the CSL–N1ICD interface and for Notch-dependent coactivation. A central region contributes to complex stability. The C-terminal domain carries the transcriptional activation function, recruiting p300/CBP and thereby driving local histone acetylation and chromatin opening. Because the co-activator is delivered by the receptor rather than expressed in response to it, MAML1 abundance is not the rate-limiting step for Notch output; complex assembly is.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Canonical Notch signalling proceeds through ligand-induced proteolytic release of N1ICD, which translocates to the nucleus and binds CSL/RBPJ on target enhancers. CSL alone is a weak activator; recruitment of MAML1 converts the complex into a strong activator by recruiting p300/CBP and associated cofactors. The best-characterised MAML1-dependent targets are the *HES* and *HEY* families of transcriptional repressors, which then propagate Notch output to downstream gene programmes.

MAML1 also has **Notch-independent** coactivator functions. It has been reported to coactivate p53 and thereby support p21 induction, to act as a coactivator for MEF2C, and to cooperate with LEF1/TCF in Wnt-responsive transcription. These non-canonical roles mean that interpreting MAML1 perturbation requires attention to which transcription factor is the relevant partner in a given cell type.

## Physiological Function

MAML1 is required for Notch-dependent cell-fate decisions across development, including T-cell lineage commitment, neurogenesis, and vascular and skeletal patterning. *MAML1* loss-of-function in humans causes a rare form of deafness (with associated craniofacial and skeletal features), consistent with disrupted Notch-dependent patterning. In haematopoiesis and lymphocyte development, MAML1-dependent Notch signalling is essential for T-cell specification.

## Role in Senescence & SASP

Notch acts as a temporal gatekeeper of the SASP, timing the transition from an early, [[TGFβ]]-dominated secretome to a late, highly inflammatory secretome dominated by [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]]; that restraint is mediated by repression of [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]] transcriptional activity, and Notch signalling is also required to establish [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]] in neighbouring cells. As the obligatory co-activator of the Notch–CSL complex, MAML1 is the effector through which N1ICD executes this programme. In the Birch and Gil scheme, N1ICD and MAML1 are grouped in the nuclear control layer under trafficking-gene regulation, alongside [[PTBP1]].

> [!note] Figure grouping is schematic
> The scheme groups N1ICD/MAML1 with PTBP1 under "alternative splicing of trafficking genes". MAML1's established molecular function is transcriptional coactivation through CSL/RBPJ, not splicing; the figure is identifying the nuclear control layer and the Notch effector arm rather than asserting a splicing activity for MAML1.
