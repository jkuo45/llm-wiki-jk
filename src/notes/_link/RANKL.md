# RANKL

RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand; *TNFSF11*) is the sole
cognate ligand of [[RANK]] and the essential cytokine for [[Osteoclast]]
formation, activation and survival in both normal and pathological bone
remodeling. It is also the target of a licensed drug, Denosumab.

## Structure and isoforms

RANKL is a 317-residue type II transmembrane glycoprotein encoded on
chromosome 13q14.11. The N-terminal cytoplasmic tail is short; the
biologically active **C-terminal ectodomain** contains a TNF-homology domain
that assembles into a **homotrimer**, the characteristic architecture of the
TNF superfamily. The trimer docks a receptor molecule into each of three
symmetric crevices.

Three splice isoforms are described:

- **RANKL1** — full length, with transmembrane and stalk regions.
- **RANKL2** — a shorter cytoplasmic domain; still membrane-anchored.
- **RANKL3** — no transmembrane domain; secreted without proteolysis.

In addition, the membrane form is **shed** by metalloproteinases (ADAM10,
MMP-14) to generate soluble RANKL. Both membrane-bound and soluble trimers
are active, and which form dominates depends on context — soluble RANKL is
measurable in circulation and contributes more to remodeling in adult mice,
while membrane RANKL permits juxtacrine, cell-contact signaling that soluble
RANKL cannot.

> [!warning] "RANKL" is used for two different proteins in the literature
> Most writing means TNFSF11. A small number of papers use "RANKL" loosely for
> the RANK receptor itself. Always check the gene symbol.

## Mechanism and regulation

RANKL on osteoblast-lineage cells (osteoblasts, osteocytes) binds RANK on
monocyte/macrophage precursors, trimerizing RANK and recruiting [[TRAF6]] →
[[TAK1]] → [[IKK complex]] → [[NF-κB]], plus [[MAPK]] and [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]].
The master output is NFATc1, which with [[SMAD]]-independent cofactors drives
the osteoclast gene program (TRAP, cathepsin K, calcitonin receptor, *c-Fos*).
Calcium flux through PLCγ2 and ITAM co-receptors (DAP12, FcRγ) is the
obligate co-stimulus.

Regulation is transcriptional and mechanical: estrogen raises
Osteoprotegerin (the decoy) rather than lowering RANKL, so the
RANKL/OPG ratio — not either value alone — sets resorption. PGE2, IL-1, IL-6,
IL-11, PTH, vitamin D and mechanical loading all raise RANKL; [[TGF-beta1]]
biphasically modulates it.

Two other RANKL receptors matter:

- **Osteoprotegerin** (TNFRSF11B) — a secreted decoy that sequesters RANKL,
  so its loss causes osteoporosis and its overexpression osteopetrosis.
- **LGR4** — competes with RANK for RANKL and activates Gαq/GSK3β to suppress
  NFATc1, providing a second brake.

RANKL can also act in "reverse," as a receptor itself, transducing signals
into osteoblast-lineage cells that promote bone formation.

## Physiological roles

RANKL is produced by osteoblasts, osteocytes, bone marrow stromal cells,
activated T and B lymphocytes, Mast Cells and [[Dendritic cells]]. Its
dominant sources shift with age: hypertrophic chondrocytes and osteoblasts
supply RANKL during endochondral ossification, whereas trabecular osteocytes
supply most RANKL in mature bone and in response to mechanical stress. This
osteocyte-centric control is why unloaded bone resorbs.

Beyond bone, RANKL regulates dendritic-cell survival and T-cell proliferation
and, with RANK, is required for lymph node organogenesis and the
development of the lactating mammary gland.

## Clinical relevance

- **[[Osteoporosis]].** The dominant driver of postmenopausal bone loss.
  Denosumab (60 mg SC q6 months) reduces vertebral, hip and
  non-vertebral fracture in the FREEDOM trial and is approved for
  postmenopausal and glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis.
- **Cancer bone disease.** Tumor cells and the bone microenvironment express
  RANK/RANKL, creating the "vicious cycle" of osteolysis, growth-factor
  release, and further tumor growth. Denosumab (120 mg q4 weeks) delays
  skeletal-related events in [[Multiple Myeloma]] and in bone metastases from
  solid tumors such as [[Breast Cancer]] and [[Prostate Cancer]], and is
  approved for giant cell tumor of bone and hypercalcemia of malignancy.
- **Arthritis and periodontal disease.** T-cell–derived RANKL mediates focal
  bone erosion in [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]] and [[Periodontitis]]; denosumab
  is approved in Japan to slow rheumatoid bone erosion.
- **Breast cancer.** RANK expression in tumor tissue correlates with poor
  outcome, and RANKL is being studied as a marker of bone-metastatic risk.

> [!warning] Denosumab pharmacology caveat
> Discontinuation causes rebound bone turnover and rapid bone loss; stopping
> requires a bisphosphonate transition. Suppression of remodeling is also
> associated with osteonecrosis of the jaw and atypical femoral fracture, so
> dental evaluation matters before dosing.

## Connections

- [[RANK]] — the sole signaling receptor; RANKL binding trimerizes RANK and starts the TRAF6 cascade.
- [[Osteoclast]] — the target cell; RANKL controls differentiation, activation and survival.
- Osteoprotegerin — soluble decoy receptor that sequesters RANKL; the RANKL/OPG ratio is the resorption set-point.
- [[TRAF6]] — the obligatory cytoplasmic adaptor downstream of RANK engaged by RANKL.
- Denosumab — a fully human IgG2 neutralizing antibody against RANKL and the only licensed RANKL inhibitor.
- [[NF-κB]] — canonical pathway downstream of RANK that RANKL drives in osteoclast precursors.
