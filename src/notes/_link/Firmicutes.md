# Firmicutes

**Overview:** [[Firmicutes]] is one of the two most abundant bacterial phyla of the human [[Gut Microbiome]], alongside [[Bacteroidetes]]. Members are predominantly Gram-positive, with a thick peptidoglycan wall, and include the classes Bacilli (e.g., streptococci, staphylococci, lactobacilli) and Clostridia (e.g., *Clostridium*, *Faecalibacterium*).

**Physiological function:** Many Firmicutes are adept at harvesting energy from dietary polysaccharides and ferment fibers into [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] such as [[Butyrate]], acetate, and propionate. Butyrate serves as a primary colonocyte fuel, reinforces barrier integrity, and exerts anti-inflammatory and epigenetic (histone deacetylase-inhibitory) effects.

**Relevance to aging and disease:** The Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes (F/B) ratio has been explored as a marker of [[Dysbiosis]], with shifts observed in [[Obesity]], metabolic syndrome, and [[Aging]]. A decline in butyrate-producing Firmicutes (e.g., *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii*) is associated with intestinal inflammation and reduced resilience.

**Research context:** Modulating Firmicutes abundance through diet, [[Fasting]], probiotics, or exercise is a focus of longevity and metabolic research, though causality of the F/B ratio remains debated.
