# Myristoylation

**Myristoylation** is a co-translational or post-translational **covalent attachment of a 14-carbon myristoyl group** (from myristoyl-CoA) to an N-terminal glycine. For **FSP1**, an **N-terminal myristoylation motif** is essential: it anchors the flavoprotein to the **inner leaflet of the plasma membrane**.

## Ferroptosis relevance

Plasma-membrane localization is what makes FSP1 an effective ferroptosis suppressor. Lipid peroxidation is initiated and propagated in the **plasma membrane**, so tethering FSP1 there places its **NAD(P)H-dependent ubiquinone-reductase activity** exactly where the **ubiquinol radical trap** is needed. Non-myristoylated FSP1 fails to protect against ferroptosis.

> [!info] Localization = function
> The myristoylation signal is the reason FSP1 guards the plasma membrane specifically, complementing mitochondrial and cytosolic antioxidant systems. It is a defining feature distinguishing the FSP1 axis from the broadly distributed GPX4 isoforms.

## Physiological & clinical relevance

- Mutation or blockade of the myristoylation signal abolishes FSP1 ferroptosis protection.
- Myristoylation is a general membrane-targeting mechanism shared with many signaling proteins (e.g., Src-family kinases, certain G-proteins).
