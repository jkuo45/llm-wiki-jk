# T Cell Receptor

The **T cell receptor (TCR)** is a heterodimeric surface receptor on [[T Lymphocyte|T cells]] that recognizes peptide fragments presented by major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules. TCR gene rearrangement during [[Thymopoiesis|thymopoiesis]] generates the diverse receptor repertoire essential for adaptive immune function.

## Structure

- **Heterodimer:** Most commonly αβ (alpha-beta) chains; γδ (gamma-delta) in a minority of T cells
- **Associated molecules:** CD3 complex (γε, δε, ζζ) for signal transduction
- **Variable regions:** V(D)J recombination generates unique antigen-binding sites
- **Diversity:** Estimated >10¹⁵ possible TCR specificities

## Gene Rearrangement

### In the thymus
- TCR β-chain rearrangement occurs in CD4⁻CD8⁻ (double negative) thymocytes in the cortex
- TCR α-chain rearrangement occurs in CD4⁺CD8⁺ (double positive) thymocytes
- Rearrangement involves V(D)J recombination, generating [[sjTREC]] as a byproduct
- Successful rearrangement is required for positive selection

### Age-related decline
- [[Thymic Involution|Thymic involution]] reduces TCR gene rearrangement capacity
- Decreased output of diverse TCR repertoires with age
- Restricted peripheral TCR diversity contributes to immune senescence

## Selection in the Thymus

### Positive selection (cortex)
- Thymocytes that recognize self-peptide/MHC with adequate affinity survive
- Mediated by cortical [[Thymic Epithelial Cells|TECs]]
- Determines CD4 vs CD8 lineage commitment

### Negative selection (medulla)
- Thymocytes highly reactive to self-antigens are eliminated
- Mediated by medullary TECs expressing tissue-restricted antigens (AIRE-dependent)
- Establishes central self-tolerance

## Age-Related Changes

- Reduced TCR gene rearrangement capacity with [[Thymic Involution|thymic involution]]
- Restricted peripheral TCR repertoire diversity
- Increased oligoclonal expansion of memory T cells
- Decreased naïve T cell output
