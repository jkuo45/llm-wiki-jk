# SARM1

## Overview

[[SARM1]] (Sterile Alpha and TIR Motif Containing 1) is a NAD+-cleaving enzyme that plays a central role in axon degeneration (Wallerian degeneration). SARM1 activity depletes intracellular [[NAD+]] pools, which can compromise [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]] function and other NAD+-dependent processes.

## Structure & Function

- **Gene:** SARM1, located on chromosome 17q21.31
- **Protein:** ~724 amino acid multi-domain protein
- **Domains:** ARM repeats, SAM domains, and TIR domain (NAD+ cleavage)
- **Activity:** NAD+ glycohydrolase; cleaves NAD+ to generate ADPR, cADPR, and nicotinamide

## Mechanism of Action

### NAD+ Consumption

SARM1 catalyzes the cleavage of [[NAD+]]:
- **NAD+ → ADPR + Nicotinamide + cADPR**
- This reaction depletes cellular NAD+ pools
- Depleted NAD+ impairs [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]] activity and other NAD+-dependent enzymes

### Axon Degeneration

SARM1 activation triggers axon degeneration through:
- Massive NAD+ depletion in axons
- ATP depletion and metabolic collapse
- Calcium influx and calpain activation
- Cytoskeletal breakdown and axon fragmentation

> [!info] NAD+ consuming enzymes
> Beyond sirtuins, other major [[NAD+]]‐consuming enzymes include [[PARP1]], [[PARP2]], [[CD38]], and [[SARM1]]. Activity of these enzymes—particularly [[CD38]], which increases with [[Aging|age]]—can deplete cellular [[NAD+]] pools and compromise sirtuin function.

## Physiological Function

- **Axon integrity:** SARM1 is a key executor of axon degeneration after injury
- **Wallerian degeneration:** Programmed axon degeneration pathway following nerve injury
- **Neuroinflammation:** SARM1 mediates innate immune responses in neurons
- **Development:** Regulated axon pruning during neural development

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Neurodegeneration:** SARM1 activation contributes to axonal loss in neurodegenerative diseases
- **Peripheral neuropathy:** SARM1-mediated axon degeneration in chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy
- **Traumatic brain injury:** SARM1 activation after TBI contributes to secondary axonal damage
- **Therapeutic target:** SARM1 inhibitors are being developed as neuroprotective agents
