## Overview

[[Locked Nucleic Acid]] (LNA) is a synthetic RNA analog in which the ribose ring is "locked" by a methylene bridge connecting the 2'-oxygen and 4'-carbon. This rigidification dramatically increases thermal stability of duplexes with complementary RNA/DNA and confers resistance to nucleases.

## Biological Role / Mechanism

LNA monomers can be incorporated into antisense oligonucleotides, siRNAs, and aptamers. Hybridizing an LNA to a target [[Messenger RNA]] or microRNA:

- Raises melting temperature (~3–8°C per residue), improving target affinity.
- Protects the oligonucleotide from endo- and exonucleases, extending half-life.
- Enables potent and specific silencing or splicing modulation of disease-relevant transcripts.

LNAs are delivered via systemic or local administration and are being evaluated for targeting oncogenes, miRNAs, and inflammatory mediators.

## Physiological / Pathological Relevance

LNA-based drugs (e.g., miravirsen, oligonucleotide therapeutics) demonstrate clinical efficacy with manageable off-target effects. In the wiki, LNA oligonucleotides are proposed as tools to modulate expression of senescence- and [[Inflammation]]-associated transcripts within sirtuin/adrenochrome research strategies, offering a nucleic-acid approach to senomorphic or senolytic intervention.
