# Beta-Adrenergic Receptor

**Beta-Adrenergic Receptors (β-ARs)** are G protein-coupled receptors activated by [[Norepinephrine]] and [[Epinephrine]]. The β1 subtype predominates in the heart, β2 in smooth muscle and skeletal muscle, and β3 in [[Adipose Tissue|adipose tissue]], where it stimulates lipolysis and [[Thermogenesis|thermogenesis]].

## Physiological Roles

- **Cardiovascular**: β1-ARs increase heart rate and contractility.
- **Metabolic**: β-adrenergic stimulation promotes lipolysis, fatty-acid oxidation, and energy expenditure; β3-ARs are a target of adaptive thermogenesis research.
- **Post-exercise metabolism**: the prolonged component of [[Excess Post-Exercise Oxygen Consumption|EPOC]] is β-adrenergically mediated — β-antagonists blunt the long-term elevation of resting metabolism after exercise (Borsheim _et al_. 1998).

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - speakman2003_Resting Metabolic Rate|Speakman & Selman (2003)]]
> The review notes that the long-term (> 1 h) EPOC in dogs is abolished by β-antagonists, implicating the β-adrenergic system in post-exercise metabolic elevation — paralleling human findings. β3-adrenoreceptor variants are also listed among the genetic correlates of residual [[Resting Metabolic Rate|resting metabolic rate]].

## Clinical Relevance

β-blockers (β1-selective or non-selective) are used for hypertension, angina, and heart failure but can reduce exercise tolerance and resting energy expenditure; β3-agonists have been explored (with limited clinical success) for obesity and overactive bladder.
