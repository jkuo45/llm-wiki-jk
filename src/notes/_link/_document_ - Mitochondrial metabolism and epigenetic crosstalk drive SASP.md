# Mitochondrial Metabolism and Epigenetic Crosstalk Drive SASP

**Martini et al. (2026)** — *Nature* (Open Access, CC BY 4.0)

> [!important] Central Thesis
> In senescent cells, mitochondrial metabolism is upregulated along a pyruvate–citrate–acetyl-CoA axis that supplies [[Acetyl-CoA|acetyl-CoA]] for [[Histone Acetylation|histone acetylation]] ([[H3K27ac]]) at [[SASP]] gene loci. Mitochondrial metabolism and [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]]–[[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] signalling are independent but complementary inputs that converge to enable full SASP gene expression. Pharmacological inhibition of [[SLC25A1]], the mitochondrial citrate exporter, dampens inflammation and improves [[Healthspan]] in aged mice without clearing [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] — identifying a mitochondrial metabolic checkpoint that selectively controls the inflammatory output of senescence.

## Abstract

[[Senescent Cells|Senescent cells]] promote tissue dysfunction in part through the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) [^1]. Cytosolic [[Mitochondrial DNA|mitochondrial nucleic acids]] activate [[Innate Immune System|innate immune]] signalling to initiate this inflammatory programme [^2] [^3]. Here we show that mitochondrial metabolism provides a second layer of control that enables execution of the inflammatory programme. In senescent cells, the mitochondrial pyruvate–citrate–[[Acetyl-CoA|acetyl-CoA]] axis is upregulated, increasing the availability of acetyl-CoA to support [[Histone Acetylation|histone acetylation]] at SASP genes. Whereas [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]]-driven signalling activates inflammatory transcription factors, acetyl-CoA availability is required for robust transcription of SASP genes. Accordingly, enhancing acetyl-CoA levels promotes SASP gene expression, whereas inhibition of [[SLC25A1]], the mitochondrial citrate exporter, reduces histone acetylation at SASP loci, limiting activity of this programme. In vivo, inhibition of SLC25A1 reduces chromatin accessibility at SASP loci, dampens inflammation and improves healthspan in aged mice. Together, these findings identify a mitochondrial metabolic checkpoint that enables the epigenetic execution of innate immune signalling, revealing a mechanism that selectively controls the inflammatory output of senescent cells.

## Cellular Senescence and Mitochondrial Metabolism

[[Cellular Senescence]] is a key response to various cellular stresses, characterized by a permanent [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle]] arrest and the secretion of a pro-inflammatory cocktail of factors known as [[SASP]] [^1]. Senescence serves important roles in embryonic development [^4] [^5], tumour suppression [^6] and tissue repair [^7] [^8]. However, persistent SASP activation can lead to chronic [[Inflammation|inflammation]], contributing to tissue dysfunction, ageing and a broad spectrum of age-related diseases, including [[Cancer|cancer]] [^1]. This dual nature makes SASP a focal point for understanding both protective and detrimental effects of senescence.

[[Mitochondria]], which are central to cellular metabolism, undergo substantial changes during senescence [^9] [^10]. These include impaired bioenergetic function, disrupted [[Mitochondrial Dynamics|mitochondrial dynamics]] and metabolic reprogramming, particularly involving the [[TCA cycle|tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle]] [^11]. Increased levels of [[TCA cycle]] intermediates, such as [[Acetyl-CoA|acetyl-CoA]], fumarate and succinate, accumulate in senescent cells [^11].

> [!info] Two Mitochondrial Inputs into the SASP
> Beyond metabolic remodelling, mitochondria in senescent cells generate innate immune signals: in senescence and ageing, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] promotes cytosolic release of [[Mitochondrial DNA|mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)]], which activates [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] signalling and drives inflammatory responses and SASP [^3] [^12] [^13]. How these inflammatory signals integrate with mitochondrial metabolic cues to regulate SASP gene expression was unclear — the central question of this study.

Here we show that mitochondrial metabolism is a key determinant of SASP expression through regulation of histone acetylation at inflammatory gene loci. In senescent cells, pathways that control cytosolic acetyl-CoA production, including mitochondrial [[Citrate|citrate]] export and its conversion by [[ACLY|ATP-citrate lyase]], are upregulated. This metabolic rewiring sustains [[Chromatin|chromatin]] accessibility at SASP regulatory elements. Disrupting this axis suppresses SASP expression despite persistent cytosolic mtDNA signalling. Pharmacological inhibition of this pathway in aged mice selectively suppresses SASP-associated inflammation without reversing cellular senescence, reducing tissue inflammation and improving healthspan.

## Mitochondria Promote Acetylation at SASP Loci

[[Acetyl-CoA]] is the obligate donor for [[Protein Acetylation|protein acetylation]], including [[Histone Acetylation|histone acetylation]] [^14] [^15], a modification associated with chromatin relaxation and increased accessibility to transcriptional machinery. Although acetyl-CoA can be generated through several metabolic pathways, mitochondrial metabolism represents a major hub of acetyl group production.

> [!info] Mitochondria Are Required for H3K27ac at SASP Genes
> Using a previously established model of mitochondrial clearance (human fibroblasts overexpressing [[Parkin]] treated with CCCP to induce [[Mitophagy]]) [^16] [^17], the authors found that mitochondrial depletion abolished respiration and mitochondrial protein content but did not affect senescent cell viability. [[ChIP-seq|ChIP–seq]] analysis of [[Histone H3]] acetylation at lysine 27 ([[H3K27ac]]) revealed that loci gaining acetylation during senescence were markedly reduced following mitochondrial clearance. Pathway analysis showed these regions are enriched for inflammatory and [[Interleukins|interleukin]] signalling pathways — central components of SASP. A 51-gene SASP signature induced during senescence was suppressed upon mitochondrial removal, with representative genes such as *IL6* and *CCL2* showing coordinated decreases in chromatin acetylation and mRNA expression. Analysis of published datasets spanning multiple acetylated histone marks and senescence models (irradiation, etoposide, oncogene-induced, replicative) confirmed consistent enrichment of histone acetylation at SASP gene loci.

To test whether this effect reflects reduced availability of acetyl-CoA — which is not membrane permeable — cells were supplemented with [[Acetate|acetate]], converted intracellularly into acetyl-CoA by [[ACSS2|ACSS2]] [^18]. Acetate isotope tracing confirmed rapid incorporation of exogenous acetate into acetyl-CoA. Acetate supplementation increased global nuclear acetylation, including H3K27ac, and induced expression of pro-inflammatory genes including *[[IL-6|IL6]]*, *[[IL-8|IL8]]*, *[[IL-1β|IL1B]]* and *[[CCL2]]*, without affecting cell cycle regulators p16 ([[CDKN2A]]) and p21 ([[CDKN1A]]). In mitochondria-depleted senescent cells, acetate supplementation increased histone acetylation and partially restored a subset of the mitochondrial-dependent SASP programme (RNA-seq and qPCR).

Together, these findings demonstrate that mitochondria regulate SASP gene expression through acetyl-CoA-dependent histone acetylation, establishing acetyl-CoA as a key metabolic link between mitochondria and epigenetic regulation of inflammatory genes.

## Mitochondrial Citrate–Acetyl-CoA Axis Drives SASP

### Mitochondrial Pyruvate Carrier (MPC)

The [[MPC|mitochondrial pyruvate carrier (MPC)]], linking glycolysis-derived [[Pyruvate|pyruvate]] to the [[TCA cycle]], was examined first. Expression of MPC1 and MPC2 increased across multiple senescence models (irradiation-induced, doxorubicin-induced, replicative).

> [!info] MPC Inhibition Suppresses the SASP Without Affecting Cell Cycle Arrest
> Pharmacological inhibition of MPC with [[UK5099]] reduced TCA cycle intermediates (which are upregulated in senescence) and increased [[Lactate|lactate]] levels, consistent with impaired mitochondrial pyruvate import. RNA-seq and cytokine profiling revealed that MPC inhibition suppressed SASP gene expression and secretion without affecting cell cycle arrest (proliferation-associated gene expression, [[p16]]/[[p21]] levels, [[EdU]] incorporation, DNA damage foci). Similar effects were seen across distinct senescence models, and CRISPR–Cas9-mediated deletion of MPC1 reduced expression of SASP factors such as *IL6* and *IL8*.

### SLC25A1 — the Mitochondrial Citrate Carrier

The mitochondrial [[SLC25A1|citrate carrier SLC25A1]] exports [[Citrate|citrate]] to the cytosol, where it can be converted to acetyl-CoA [^19]. SLC25A1 expression was significantly increased at both mRNA and protein levels in multiple senescence contexts. Genetic ablation of SLC25A1 did not alter expression of CDK inhibitors or cell cycle arrest, but led to a marked reduction in SASP gene expression. Similarly, pharmacological inhibition of SLC25A1 using [[CTPI2]], a third-generation inhibitor with high binding affinity and specificity [^20], resulted in dose-dependent suppression of SASP components at transcript and protein levels — without alterations in cell cycle regulators, [[HMGB1]] localization, DNA damage foci or proliferation markers. RNA-seq and pathway analysis confirmed that CTPI2 downregulated inflammatory and mitochondrial-dependent SASP programmes but had no effect on cell-cycle-associated gene expression, across senescence models and treatment windows (early after induction and in fully established senescent cells).

> [!warning] Selective Suppression of SASP, Not Senescence
> SLC25A1 inhibition reduced global nuclear acetylation (pan-acetylated lysine, H3K27ac, and H3K9ac) and decreased H3K27ac at SASP loci by ChIP–seq. [[Isotope Tracing|13C-glucose metabolic tracing]] showed incorporation of glucose-derived carbon into histone acetylation was abolished upon SLC25A1 inhibition. Notably, CTPI2 did not broadly impair mitochondrial function: [[Oxidative Phosphorylation|OXPHOS]] gene expression, ATP production, coupling efficiency, [[Mitochondrial ROS|mitochondrial ROS]] levels and mitochondrial morphology were largely unchanged. Metabolomics showed reduced citrate, isocitrate and *cis*-aconitate in whole-cell extracts; subcellular fractionation revealed acetyl-CoA levels were selectively reduced in the cytosolic fraction, indicating impaired generation of the nucleocytosolic acetyl-CoA required for histone acetylation.

### ACLY — ATP-Citrate Lyase

The downstream conversion of mitochondria-derived citrate into acetyl-CoA is catalysed by [[ACLY|ATP-citrate lyase (ACLY)]]. Because exported citrate is the principal substrate for cytosolic acetyl-CoA production, the authors tested whether increasing citrate availability is sufficient to drive histone acetylation and inflammatory gene expression: citrate supplementation increased histone acetylation and induced pro-inflammatory gene expression in proliferating cells. *ACLY* expression was increased across multiple senescence models, and its silencing reduced both H3K27ac levels and SASP gene expression in irradiation-induced and replicative senescence.

> [!important] The Mitochondrial Citrate–Acetyl-CoA Axis Is a Central Regulator of SASP
> Multiple nodes within this pathway — from pyruvate import ([[MPC]]), to citrate export ([[SLC25A1]]) and acetyl-CoA generation ([[ACLY]]) — converge to control histone acetylation at SASP loci, thereby selectively modulating inflammatory gene expression without affecting the senescence-associated cell cycle arrest.

## mtDNA and Metabolism Co-regulate SASP

Cytosolic DNA species activate [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] signalling in senescent cells [^21], and mtDNA leakage has emerged as a key mitochondrial input driving inflammatory programmes during senescence and ageing [^3] [^12] [^13]. The authors tested whether mitochondrial regulation of histone acetylation and mtDNA–cGAS–STING signalling are mechanistically linked or distinct inputs.

Cytosolic mtDNA levels were unchanged in senescent MRC5 and IMR90 fibroblasts following SLC25A1 inhibition, indicating that metabolic regulation of histone acetylation restrains SASP independently of mtDNA release. Reciprocally, senescent [[BAX]]- and [[BAK]]-deficient fibroblasts (impaired cytosolic mtDNA release) showed reduced SASP gene expression as previously reported [^11], yet global nuclear acetylation and H3K27ac levels at SASP loci were unchanged. Similarly, pharmacological inhibition of cGAS–STING signalling with the [[STING]] inhibitor [[SN011]] suppressed SASP gene expression without affecting histone acetylation. Thus, mtDNA–cGAS–STING signalling and mitochondrial control of histone acetylation operate as independently regulated pathways.

> [!info] Two Signals Are Required — Neither Alone Is Sufficient
> Despite independence, the pathways functionally cooperate. Acetate supplementation increased H3K27ac independently of STING inhibition, but acetate-induced SASP gene expression was suppressed by SN011 — chromatin accessibility alone is not sufficient to drive SASP transcription without innate immune activation. In senescent cells lacking mitochondria, mtDNA transfection did not alter H3K27ac, whereas acetate increased histone acetylation with no additional effect of mtDNA co-treatment. Transcriptomic analyses showed enrichment of inflammatory and cytokine signalling programmes after either treatment alone, with markedly greater enrichment when combined; qPCR and RNA-seq showed acetate or mtDNA alone partially induced SASP gene expression, while their combination resulted in stronger SASP activation.

Together, these findings demonstrate that mitochondrial metabolic control of histone acetylation and mtDNA–cGAS–STING signalling represent distinct but complementary pathways that converge to enable full SASP gene expression.

## SLC25A1 Inhibition Improves Healthspan in Vivo

As an initial step, the effects of SLC25A1 inhibition were assessed ex vivo using stromal cells isolated from young and aged mouse tissues. Aged stromal cells from heart and kidney exhibited increased expression of [[p16]] and genes encoding SASP factors compared with young controls, which were selectively reduced by [[CTPI2]] without altering p16 expression.

> [!important] CTPI2 Improves Frailty and Muscle Function in Aged Mice
> Aged (19-month-old) C57BL/6 mice were gavaged with CTPI2 (50 mg kg^-1^, three times weekly for 3 months). CTPI2-treated mice exhibited a more lustrous and uniform fur coat, reduced alopecia and less fur greying, accompanied by delayed onset of [[Frailty|frailty]] as measured by a 31-parameter [[Frailty Index|frailty index]] [^22]. Functional assessment revealed enhanced neuromuscular performance: improved coordination and balance (tightrope test), increased strength (hanging and grip strength tests), and preservation of muscle integrity (increased myofibre cross-sectional area, reduced centrally nucleated fibres). CTPI2 treatment did not improve spine and femur bone microarchitecture.

CTPI2 reduced SASP gene expression in skeletal muscle without altering p16 or p21; expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines *Il6* and *Il1b* negatively correlated with muscle strength, and circulating inflammatory cytokines were also reduced, indicating a systemic attenuation of inflammatory burden. In the liver, CTPI2 reduced expression of SASP-associated genes and decreased enrichment of the SenMayo senescence signature, with more pronounced effects in male mice, while p16 and p21 remained unchanged. In the heart, p16 and p21 were similarly unchanged, but CTPI2 specifically reduced cardiomyocyte-associated secretory factors (*[[GDF15|Gdf15]]* and *Edn3*) [^23] in the whole heart, consistent with reduced [[Cardiomyocytes|cadiomyocyte]] hypertrophy, alongside suppression of classical SASP factors in cardiac stromal cells and reduced pro-inflammatory [[CCR2]]^+^ [[Macrophages|macrophage]] markers in female mice — consistent with decreased SASP-driven immune recruitment described in cardiac ageing [^24].

> [!info] SASP Modulation, Not Senescent Cell Clearance
> Together, these findings demonstrate that targeting mitochondrial citrate metabolism suppresses inflammation across tissues and improves tissue function and healthspan during ageing, without detectable changes in p16 or p21 expression — consistent with SASP modulation rather than senolytic clearance of senescent cells.

## SLC25A1 Inhibition Remodels SASP Chromatin In Vivo

Single-nucleus multiome profiling (snRNA-seq + snATAC-seq) of quadriceps muscle from aged mice treated with CTPI2 enabled simultaneous assessment of transcriptional output and chromatin accessibility within the same nucleus.

- Cell-type annotation revealed a predominance of type IIA and IIB myofibres with comparable cellular composition between groups; immune cell abundance was reduced in CTPI2-treated muscles, consistent with diminished inflammatory burden
- Senescent-like nuclei (defined by the validated CoreScence gene signature [^25]) were present at similar abundance and distribution across conditions, consistent with unchanged p16/p21 expression
- Despite unchanged senescent cell abundance, CTPI2 treatment significantly reduced SASP-associated transcription within senescent-like nuclei, accompanied by decreased [[Chromatin|chromatin accessibility]] at SASP loci (e.g., *Ccl2*, *Igfbp4*); similar results were obtained with an independent senescence gene set (GenAge) [^26]

In the liver, iterative indirect immunofluorescence imaging (4i) with antibodies against p21, p16, PCNA and pan-lysine acetylation (with p16 signal validated in p16-knockout mice) showed that the abundance of p16^+^ and/or p21^+^ PCNA^-^ cells remained unchanged across conditions. By contrast, these senescent cells exhibited increased nuclear histone acetylation compared with non-senescent cells, which was reduced following CTPI2 treatment.

> [!info] No Broad Metabolic Reprogramming
> CTPI2-treated aged mice displayed glucose tolerance comparable to controls with modest reductions in fasting and fed glucose. Body weight, body composition, metabolic rate, respiratory exchange ratio, activity, and lipid and fatty acid metabolic pathways were largely unaffected — consistent with in vitro observations showing no effect of CTPI2 on lipid metabolic gene expression in senescent fibroblasts.

In human tissues (muscle, heart, hippocampus, kidney; GTEx [^40]), expression of the mitochondrial citrate transporter gene *SLC25A1* positively correlated with a mitochondrial-dependent SASP gene signature and with canonical senescence markers p16 and p21, whereas chronological age alone showed little or no association — linking mitochondrial citrate metabolism to senescence-associated inflammatory programmes across diverse human tissues.

## Discussion

This study identifies mitochondrial metabolism as a regulator of histone acetylation and inflammatory gene expression during senescence. Using genetic and pharmacological perturbations across multiple nodes of the mitochondrial citrate–acetyl-CoA axis ([[MPC]], [[SLC25A1]], [[ACLY]]), the authors show that mitochondrial metabolism is required to sustain histone acetylation at SASP loci and maintain inflammatory gene expression, positioning mitochondrial metabolism as an upstream determinant of chromatin accessibility in senescent cells — aligning with previous reports linking citrate metabolism to SASP regulation [^27].

- Although acetyl-CoA can be generated from alternative sources (acetate, [[Fatty Acid Oxidation|fatty acid oxidation]], amino acid metabolism), in senescent cells mitochondria constitute a dominant source of acetyl-CoA for chromatin modification, creating a dependence on mitochondrial acetyl-CoA to sustain epigenetic activation of inflammatory gene programmes
- Many SASP-associated genes are linked to [[Super-enhancer|super-enhancer]]-like regions that are particularly sensitive to changes in histone acetylation [^28]; such loci probably require sustained acetyl-CoA availability to maintain high transcriptional output, rendering them especially vulnerable to interventions that limit acetyl-CoA supply
- Mitochondrial control of histone acetylation and mtDNA–cGAS–STING signalling function as independent pathways that converge to enable SASP gene expression: while mitochondrial acetyl-CoA allows chromatin accessibility at SASP loci, mtDNA-dependent innate immune signalling provides the inflammatory activation required to engage these transcriptionally permissive regions. Disruption of either pathway is sufficient to suppress SASP, yet activation of either alone is not sufficient to fully restore it

[[CTPI2]], a selective inhibitor of SLC25A1 [^20], had previously been shown to improve metabolic dysfunction in diet-induced [[Steatohepatitis|steatohepatitis]] [^29], a context associated with [[Obesity|obesity]] and altered systemic metabolism. Here, CTPI2 improves functional outcomes in aged mice while broadly suppressing SASP across tissues, without evidence of large-scale metabolic remodelling or reduced senescent cell abundance. This differs from obesity-associated states, in which SLC25A1 inhibition influences glucose and lipid metabolism [^29]: in aged tissues, citrate export appears less critical for sustaining bulk metabolic flux and instead contributes to acetyl-CoA-dependent chromatin acetylation and inflammatory gene expression.

> [!important] Therapeutic Implications
> These findings position SLC25A1 inhibition as a novel therapeutic target that modulates the inflammatory output of senescent cells through metabolic–epigenetic coupling. More broadly, they suggest that targeting metabolic inputs into chromatin regulation may represent a tractable strategy to mitigate age-associated inflammation and functional decline.

## Key Methods

- **Models**: stress-induced senescence (20 Gy X-ray irradiation, day 10–12), chemotherapy-induced (250 nM [[Doxorubicin|doxorubicin]]), replicative senescence; human embryonic lung MRC5 and IMR90 fibroblasts; Parkin-mediated mitochondrial clearance (CCCP, 12.5 μM)
- **Metabolic analyses**: Seahorse Mito Stress Test; 13C-glucose and 13C-acetate isotope tracing with LC–MS; GC–MS quantification of TCA metabolites and subcellular citrate/acetyl-CoA
- **Genetics**: CRISPR–Cas9 deletion of SLC25A1, MPC1, BAK, BAX; siRNA silencing of ACLY
- **Epigenomics**: H3K27ac and H3 [[ChIP-seq|ChIP–seq]] (nf-core/chipseq pipeline); published datasets GSE106146, GSE56307, GSE74238, GSE103590; new data GSE279410
- **In vivo**: aged C57BL/6 mice (19–22 months), CTPI2 50 mg kg^-1^ gavage 3×/week for 3 months; frailty index (31 items), tightrope/hanging/grip tests, μCT bone imaging, CLAMS indirect calorimetry, plasma cytokine arrays
- **Multiome**: single-nucleus RNA-seq + ATAC-seq (10x Genomics), CellRanger-ARC, Seurat, Signac; CoreScence and GenAge senescence scores; SenoQuant image analysis pipeline
- **Human data**: GTEx (Analysis V11) bulk RNA-seq across muscle, heart, hippocampus, kidney
- **Data availability**: GSE279410, GSE326624 (ChIP–seq); GSE279411, GSE319937 (RNA-seq); GSE324238 (multiome)
