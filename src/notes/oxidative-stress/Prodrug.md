# Prodrug

## Overview

A prodrug is an inactive or less-active compound that is converted to the active drug through metabolic or chemical transformation in vivo. Prodrug strategies are used to improve bioavailability, targeting, stability, and tolerability of parent drugs.

## Example: Adrenochrome Monoaminoguanidine

For [[Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine]] to act as an active AGE inhibitor despite its hydrazine group being locked in a hydrazone bond, it must either retain sufficient nucleophilicity through its remaining guanidine nitrogens, act as a prodrug that undergoes controlled in vivo hydrolysis to slowly release free [[Aminoguanidine]], or work via alternative pathways such as chelating transition metals that catalyze glycoxidation.

> [!info]
| Mechanism | Feasibility |
|---|---|
| Retained nucleophilicity | Possible via remaining guanidine nitrogens |
| Prodrug hydrolysis | Plausible — conjugate is stable yet could release aminoguanidine over time |
| Metal chelation | Alternative pathway |

The prodrug hypothesis is plausible because the conjugate is stable for clinical use yet could hydrolyze to release aminoguanidine over time.

## Open Questions

Whether the bound aminoguanidine retains dicarbonyl-scavenging activity remains unresolved, and this determines whether AMM acts directly or as a controlled-release prodrug.
