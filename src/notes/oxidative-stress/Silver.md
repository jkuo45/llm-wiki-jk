# Silver

## Overview

Silver is a metallic element with the chemical symbol Ag and atomic number 47. It occurs naturally in free form, in alloys with [[Gold]] and other metals, and in minerals such as argentite and chlorargyrite. Silver(I) oxide serves as a convenient silver source for synthesis and electrochemical applications.

## Chemical Behavior

Silver's redox chemistry is central to its biology: metallic silver that is oxidized in the body may be reduced back to the elemental state by [[Ascorbic Acid]] or [[Catecholamines]] and deposited in tissues, driving the pigmentation seen in [[Argyria]]. The [[Silver ion]] (Ag+) inhibits enzymes including glutathione peroxidase and Na+,K+-ATPase, linking silver to oxidative stress and toxicity.

## Applications

Silver is used in jewelry, electronics, batteries, and antimicrobial formulations. Its compounds range from [[Silver compounds|reactive precursors]] to [[Silver salts|soluble salts]] and [[Silver nanoparticles|nanoparticles]] with distinct toxicological profiles.
