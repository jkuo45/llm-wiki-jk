# Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids

**Polyunsaturated fatty acids ([[PUFA|PUFAs]])** are fatty acids containing two or more *cis* double bonds. As essential structural components of [[Cell Membranes]], they are particularly susceptible to [[Lipid Peroxidation]], yet they also serve as precursors for critical signaling lipids. Their oxidation is the biochemical engine of ferroptotic and oxidative cell death.

## Structure and Reactivity

The bis-allylic methylene groups (–CH₂– flanked by two double bonds) in PUFAs are the primary targets of radical attack: hydrogen abstraction leaves a carbon-centered radical that is resonance-stabilized across the pentadienyl system. Reactivity scales with the number of bis-allylic positions — linoleic acid (18:2, n-6) = 1×, arachidonic acid (20:4, n-6) = 6×, docosahexaenoic acid (22:6, n-3) = 8×. Membranes enriched in long-chain n-3 PUFAs are thus both functionally flexible and oxidatively fragile — a trade-off with direct disease implications.

The *cis* geometry introduces a kink of ~30° per double bond, preventing tight packing and keeping the bilayer fluid at physiological temperature. That same disorder exposes the bis-allylic hydrogens to soluble initiators such as [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] and [[Peroxynitrite]], which is why peroxidation is a diffusion-controlled hazard rather than a rare event.

## Major PUFAs

- **Linoleic acid (LA, 18:2, n-6)**: Essential dietary fatty acid; precursor to arachidonic acid via Δ6-desaturase/elongase. Dominant PUFA of LDL and storage lipids.
- **Arachidonic acid (AA, 20:4, n-6)**: Substrate for eicosanoids ([[Prostaglandins]], [[Leukotrienes]], [[Thromboxane A₂|thromboxanes]]) and for non-enzymatic [[F2-Isoprostanes]] formation. Esterified at the *sn-2* position of membrane phospholipids.
- **Adrenic acid (AdA, 22:4, n-6)**: Elongation product of AA; enriched in myelin and adrenal cortex, and a preferred ferroptotic substrate in some contexts.
- **Docosahexaenoic acid (DHA, 22:6, n-3)**: Enriched in brain and retina; the most oxidizable common PUFA. Also the parent of protectins/maresins.
- **Eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5, n-3)**: Anti-inflammatory eicosanoid precursor; parent of E-series [[Resolvins]].
- **Dihomo-γ-linolenic acid (DGLA, 20:3, n-6)**: Minor but regulatory; yields anti-inflammatory 1-series prostaglandins.

## Membrane Localization and Ferroptosis Susceptibility

When esterified at the *sn-2* position of membrane [[Phospholipid|phospholipids]] (PL-PUFAs), PUFAs become the direct substrates for iron-dependent peroxidation — the execution step of [[Ferroptosis]]. Free PUFAs are comparatively inert; it is the phospholipid-esterified pool that matters. Two enzymatic steps set that pool:

1. **Activation**: [[ACSL4]] (acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4) charges AA and AdA to acyl-CoA, and LPCAT3 inserts them into phosphatidylethanolamines. ACSL4 loss is strongly protective against ferroptosis.
2. **Release**: cytosolic phospholipase A2 (cPLA2) and Lands-cycle remodeling enzymes free esterified PUFAs for re-incorporation or oxidation.

Abundance and species of membrane PL-PUFAs are therefore major determinants of cellular ferroptosis sensitivity — more so than total antioxidant capacity in some models.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Acid_ceramidase_modulates_the_lipid_profile_and_ex|Acid ceramidase modulates the lipid profile… (Soriano-Castell et al., 2026)]]
> [[Acid ceramidase]] over-expression in replicatively [[Senescent Cells|senescent]] fibroblasts increased membrane PL-PUFA content (notably all [[Arachidonic acid|arachidonic acid]]-containing PLs), creating a pro-ferroptotic profile. ACase knockdown lowered PL-PUFAs and protected against [[RSL3]]-induced [[Ferroptosis]] independently of [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione|GSH]] and [[Iron|iron]] — because less PUFA substrate means less [[Lipid Peroxidation|LPO]].

## Oxidation Products and Specialized Mediators

PUFA peroxidation generates reactive aldehydes — [[Malondialdehyde]] and [[4-Hydroxynonenal]] — that adduct proteins and [[DNA]], propagating damage beyond the membrane. While PUFA-rich membranes are more oxidation-prone, controlled PUFA oxidation also produces specialized pro-resolving mediators that terminate [[Inflammation]]:

- **n-6 series**: [[Prostaglandin G₂]] and downstream prostaglandins/thromboxanes (enzymatic, via [[Cyclooxygenases]]); [[Hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acids]] and leukotrienes (via [[Lipoxygenase]]); [[F2-Isoprostanes]] (non-enzymatic).
- **n-3 series**: E-series resolvins (from EPA), D-series resolvins, protectins/neuroprotectins and maresins (from DHA) — resolution agonists rather than simple "anti-inflammatory" agents.

The same chemistry is thus double-edged: unregulated radical peroxidation is tissue damage, while tightly channeled enzymatic oxidation is lipid signaling. [[Vitamin E]] and [[Coenzyme Q10]] set the threshold between the two by scavenging chain-carrying peroxyl radicals.

## Physiological and Pathological Significance

- **Ferroptosis**: peroxidation of AA/AdA-containing phosphatidylethanolamines is the lethal event; [[GPX4]] (a [[Glutathione Peroxidase]]) normally reduces these hydroperoxides, and [[Glutathione]] depletion or [[GPX4]] inhibition unleashes them. Implicated in neurodegeneration, ischemia–reperfusion injury, and tumor suppression.
- **Atherosclerosis**: PUFA peroxidation within LDL initiates [[Oxidized LDL]] formation (see [[Cholesterol]]), the trigger for foam-cell uptake.
- **Neural tissue**: DHA enrichment makes neuronal membranes especially vulnerable to peroxidation cascades; elevated MDA/4-HNE mark injury in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]].
- **Diet**: high n-3 intake raises peroxidation potential but yields resolvins; antioxidant defenses ([[Vitamin E]], [[Coenzyme Q10]]) must scale accordingly. The net effect of n-3 supplementation depends on the oxidative context — a [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox|paradox]] seen across supplementation trials.
