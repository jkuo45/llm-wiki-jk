# Silver nanoparticles

## Overview

Silver nanoparticles are nanoscale (typically 1–100 nm) forms of metallic [[Silver]] that are believed to disrupt the [[Mitochondrial Electron Transport Chain]], causing oxidative stress, reduced ATP synthesis, and DNA damage.

## Mechanism of Toxicity

The mitochondrial electron transport chain is the major source of cellular energy, and its disruption collapses oxidative phosphorylation and elevates reactive oxygen production.

> [!warning]
| Effect | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Respiratory chain disruption | Reduced ATP synthesis |
| Elevated ROS | Oxidative stress, DNA damage |

This nanoparticle-specific toxicity is attributed to sustained release of [[Silver ion|silver ions]] and direct interaction with respiratory complexes, distinguishing it from the bulk-metal effects of larger silver particles.

## Applications & Concern

Silver nanoparticles are widely used for antimicrobial activity in consumer products, medical devices, and electronics. Their mitochondrial toxicity raises safety concerns for environmental and occupational exposure, and their ionic release profile connects them to the broader oxidative stress biology of silver.
