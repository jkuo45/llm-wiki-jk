# Hypobromous Acid

## Chemical Properties

Hypobromous acid (HOBr) is a reactive oxygen halogen species produced when [[Eosinophil Peroxidase]] uses bromide and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] to brominate targets. It is the eosinophil analog of neutrophil-produced [[Hypochlorous Acid]] (HOCl) and is similarly reactive toward proteins, particularly at tyrosine, histidine, and thiol residues.

## Production & Physiology

In eosinophilic inflammation such as allergic asthma and helminth infections, activated eosinophils release [[Eosinophil Peroxidase]], which catalyzes HOBr formation. HOBr is the principal brominating oxidant of the phagocyte arsenal.

> [!info]
> HOBr is less studied than HOCl, and bromination biomarkers are less established. Bromotyrosine adducts are elevated in allergic airway disease and serve as specific markers of eosinophil-mediated oxidant damage.

## Pathological Role

HOBr brominates protein residues, generating stable 3-bromotyrosine adducts that are used as biomarkers of eosinophil activation in asthma and other eosinophilic conditions. Uncontrolled HOBr production contributes to tissue damage and oxidative stress in allergic airway disease.
