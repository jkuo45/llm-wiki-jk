# Flavanone

## Chemical Properties & Classification

Flavanones are a subclass of [[Flavonoid|flavonoids]] characterized by a saturated C-ring (2,3-dihydroflavone core) and the absence of vicinal (catechol) hydroxyl groups on the B-ring. This structural distinction separates them from catechol flavonoids such as [[Quercetin]] or [[Luteolin]]. Common flavanones include [[Hesperidin]], hesperetin, and naringenin, which are abundant in citrus peels and juices.

> [!info]
> The absence of the catechol motif is the key to flavanones' COMT neutrality: lacking vicinal hydroxyls, they cannot serve as substrates or inhibitors of [[COMT]], unlike catechol flavonoids.

## Metabolic Fate

The saturated C-ring determines the metabolic fate of flavanones. They undergo extensive phase II conjugation (glucuronidation, sulfation) and are metabolized by the gut microbiota, which ring-opens the C-ring to yield phenylpropionic acid derivatives. These structural features shape their pharmacokinetics and bioactivity.

## Applications

Flavanones contribute to the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity of citrus. Their structural relationship to other flavonoid subclasses is central to understanding structure-activity relationships in dietary polyphenol research.
