# Cyanidin-3-glucoside

## Chemical Properties & Classification

Cyanidin-3-glucoside (C3G) is the most common anthocyanin in nature, composed of the aglycone [[Cyanidin]] conjugated to a glucose moiety at the 3-position. Black elderberries are among the richest dietary sources, providing roughly 350–450 mg per 100 g of fresh fruit, so that a ~300 mg therapeutic dose is achievable from 70–85 g of fruit. As an anthocyanin, C3G belongs to the broader [[Flavonoid]] class and exhibits potent antioxidant activity.

## Pharmacodynamics & Bioactivity

C3G is a powerful natural activator of [[SIRT6]], positioning anthocyanin-rich foods such as elderberry as dietary SIRT6-modulating interventions. SIRT6 activation is linked to genomic stability, glucose metabolism, and longevity pathways, making C3G of interest in aging research. Like other anthocyanins, C3G also scavenges [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and modulates inflammatory signaling.

> [!tip]
> Understanding the dose achievable from food is essential for interpreting dietary studies: elderberry can supply a 300 mg C3G dose from a modest serving, unlike many polyphenols whose effective dose exceeds dietary intake.

## Applications

The traditional use of elderberry for immune support is consistent with the bioactivity of its anthocyanin content. C3G's combination of antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and SIRT6-activating properties makes it a candidate for research on metabolic health and longevity, though human bioavailability remains limited by rapid phase II conjugation.
