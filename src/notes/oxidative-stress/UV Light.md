# UV Light

## Overview

Ultraviolet (UV) light is high-energy electromagnetic radiation that generates [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS), which drive the oxidation of [[Catecholamines]] such as [[Epinephrine]]. Because ROS can steal electrons from the catechol hydroxyls, producing the o-quinone intermediate, light exposure accelerates the pathway that converts adrenaline into [[Adrenochrome]].

> [!info]
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| UV-generated ROS | Oxidation of catechol hydroxyls |
| o-quinone intermediate | Accelerated adrenochrome formation |
| Temperature | Kinetic energy controlling reaction rate |

## Relevance to Drug Stability

This photochemical sensitivity is why pharmaceutical epinephrine is stored in amber vials away from light: preventing light-induced ROS formation slows the oxidation and cyclization reactions that degrade the drug. The same principle applies more broadly to catecholamine biochemistry, where photo-oxidation can convert adrenaline solutions pink.

> [!warning]
> Temperature and light, as factors governing kinetic energy and radical generation, are environmental controls that determine whether epinephrine remains stable or proceeds toward adrenochrome and its toxic downstream chemistry.

## Broader Context

UV-induced oxidative chemistry also underlies photoaging and UV-mediated tissue damage, connecting this entity to the broader oxidative stress and ROS biology of the vault.
