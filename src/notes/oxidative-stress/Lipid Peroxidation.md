# Lipid Peroxidation

**Lipid peroxidation** is the free radical chain reaction in which radicals abstract hydrogen from the polyunsaturated fatty acids of [[Cell Membranes]], producing reactive aldehydes such as [[Malondialdehyde]] and [[4-Hydroxynonenal]]. It is a central execution pathway of [[Oxidative Stress]], damaging membranes, proteins, and DNA, and is a key process in [[Atherosclerosis]].

## Initiation, Propagation, and Termination

Lipid peroxidation proceeds through three classical phases. **Initiation** occurs when a radical—most notably [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] (•OH), [[Peroxynitrite]] (ONOO⁻), or a ferryl complex—abstracts hydrogen from a bis-allylic methylene (–CH₂–) of a PUFA. The resulting carbon-centered radical (L•) is resonance-stabilized by adjacent double bonds. **Propagation** is rapid and self-sustaining: L• reacts with O₂ at near-diffusion-limited rates (k ≈ 10⁸ M⁻¹s⁻¹) to form a lipid peroxyl radical (LOO•), which abstracts H from an adjacent PUFA, generating a lipid hydroperoxide (LOOH) and a new L•. Each initiator can oxidize dozens to hundreds of lipids. LOOH decomposes via the [[Fenton Reaction]] (LOOH + Fe²⁺ → LO• + OH⁻ + Fe³⁺), yielding alkoxyl radicals (LO•) that branch new chains—exponential amplification. **Termination** occurs when two radicals recombine (L• + L• → L–L; 2 LOO• → LOOL + O₂, Russell mechanism). Chain-breaking [[Antioxidants]] such as [[Vitamin E]] (k ≈ 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹) intercept LOO•, donating H to form a stable tocopheroxyl radical; [[Ascorbic Acid]] regenerates α-tocopherol at the membrane–water interface.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Lipid peroxidation is characterized by the formation of conjugated dienes, lipid hydroperoxides, TBARS, and aldehydes. In LDL, oxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids produces conjugated dienes (measured at 234 nm) and alters the apoB protein moiety. In HDL, lipid peroxidation alters phospholipid fluidity, modifies apoprotein structure, and decreases PON1 activity.

## Major Products and Reactivity

Primary products are lipid hydroperoxides, which fragment into secondary aldehydes: [[Malondialdehyde]] (from PUFAs with ≥3 double bonds), 4-Hydroxynonenal (4-HNE, from n-6 PUFAs), acrolein, and [[F2-Isoprostanes]] (non-enzymatic prostaglandin-like compounds from arachidonic acid). 4-HNE and MDA form covalent adducts with protein cysteine/histidine/lysine and DNA (M₁dG lesions), while 4-HNE at low concentrations activates the [[NRF2]]/ARE protective pathway—an example of redox signaling.

- **[[Conjugated Dienes]]**: Early markers; measured at 234 nm
- **Malondialdehyde (MDA)**: End-product; measured by TBARS assay
- **Lipid hydroperoxides**: Primary products
- **Reactive aldehydes**: Cross-link with proteins (e.g., apoB)

## Enzymatic vs Non-Enzymatic Pathways

**Enzymatic peroxidation** is catalyzed by stereoselective oxidoreductases. [[Lipoxygenases]] (LOX) incorporate molecular oxygen at specific positions of arachidonic acid, producing signaling molecules such as [[Leukotrienes]] and [[Hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acids]] (HETEs). [[Cyclooxygenases]] (COX-1 and COX-2) mediate the bis-dioxygenation of arachidonic acid to [[Prostaglandin G₂]], initiating the prostanoid cascade. **Non-enzymatic peroxidation** is unregulated and produces isomeric products ([[Isoprostanes]]) that serve as in vivo biomarkers of oxidative damage.

## Role of Polyunsaturated Fatty Acids

PUFAs, particularly [[Arachidonic acid]] (20:4, n-6) and [[Docosahexaenoic acid]] (22:6, n-3), are highly susceptible to peroxidation due to the presence of multiple bis-allylic methylene groups. The lability increases geometrically with the number of double bonds: DHA, with six double bonds, is ~10-fold more oxidizable than linoleic acid (18:2). Neuronal membranes are enriched in DHA, rendering neural tissue especially vulnerable to [[Lipid Peroxidation]] cascades.

## Physiological and Pathological Consequences

Peroxidation inserts polar groups into the bilayer, increasing permeability, fluidity, and fusion tendency. In [[Mitochondria]], cardiolipin peroxidation (driven by [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] at Complex III) facilitates cytochrome c release and [[Apoptosis]]. In [[Atherosclerosis]], oxLDL formation via lipid peroxidation is an initiating event: oxLDL is cleared unregulated by [[Macrophage]] scavenger receptors (SR-A, [[CD36 Receptor]]), forming foam cells; 4-HNE-modified proteins in plaques activate [[NF-κB]]. In [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]], elevated MDA and 4-HNE in CSF and tissue of [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]], and [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]] indicate neuronal injury. 4-HNE adduction of the astrocytic glutamate transporter GLT-1 (EAAT2) impairs clearance, causing excitotoxicity. Iron-dependent peroxidation also drives [[Ferroptosis]], a regulated necrotic cell death.

### Role in Atherosclerosis

- LDL lipid peroxidation produces [[Oxidized LDL]], which is taken up by [[Macrophages]] to form [[Foam Cells]]
- HDL lipid peroxidation impairs its ability to mediate [[Cholesterol Efflux]]
- Lipid peroxidation markers correlate with [[Atherosclerosis]] progression

## Connection to Catecholamine-Induced Oxidative Damage

Catecholamine autoxidation provides an initiating radical source that can trigger myocardial lipid peroxidation. [[Epinephrine]] oxidation generates [[Superoxide]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]], which in the presence of transition metals yields [[Hydroxyl Radicals|hydroxyl radical]] via Fenton chemistry. The hydroxyl radical rapidly initiates lipid peroxidation in [[Cardiomyocyte]] membranes, disrupting ion homeostasis and contractile function. This mechanism is implicated in [[Catecholamine-induced cardiomyopathy]] and [[Stress cardiomyopathy]] (Takotsubo syndrome). [[Aminochromes]], including [[Adrenochrome]], further amplify damage by redox-cycling with [[NADPH]] and consuming [[Glutathione]], impairing membrane repair.

## Inhibition

- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Inhibits copper- and radical-induced lipid peroxidation in LDL and HDL
- **[[Vitamin E]]**: Endogenous chain-breaking antioxidant in lipoproteins
- **[[PON1]]**: HDL-associated enzyme that hydrolyzes lipid peroxides
- **Glutathione peroxidase**: Reduces lipid hydroperoxides
