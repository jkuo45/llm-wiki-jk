# Fructosamine

## Overview

Fructosamines are the Amadori rearrangement products formed when glucose non-enzymatically binds primary amines of proteins (mainly lysine residues) during early [[Glycation]]. They are the reversible, early-stage intermediates that precede irreversible [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] formation, and serum fructosamine serves as a short-term marker of glycemic control (reflecting the preceding 2–3 weeks).

## Enzymatic Repair

[[Fructosamine-3-kinase]] (FN3K) is the intracellular enzyme that initiates enzymatic protein deglycation by phosphorylating fructosamine residues on glycated proteins to form [[Fructosamine-3-phosphate]]. This phosphorylated compound is highly unstable and spontaneously decomposes, regenerating the original undamaged amino acid and releasing [[3-deoxyglucosone]], which is then neutralized by detoxifying enzymes (e.g., glyoxalase pathway).

> [!info]
> By repairing early glycation intermediates before they progress to irreversible AGEs, FN3K provides the repair arm of enzymatic defense against AGE accumulation, complementary to prevention by dicarbonyl detoxification.

## Pathophysiology & Clinical Relevance

When repair capacity is overwhelmed, fructosamines mature into AGEs, driving diabetic complications and age-related tissue damage. Fructosamine is therefore both a clinical biomarker and a strategic node in the fight against glycation-induced damage.
