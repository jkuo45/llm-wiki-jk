# Keshan disease

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Keshan disease is an endemic cardiomyopathy found in selenium-poor regions, driven by insufficient cardiac [[Glutathione Peroxidase]] activity and consequent oxidant damage to the myocardium. [[Selenium]] is a structural component of the selenocysteine active sites of glutathione peroxidases, which detoxify hydroperoxides such as [[Lipid hydroperoxide]]; selenium deficiency therefore cripples this antioxidant defense.

> [!warning]
> A coxsackievirus infection interacts with the selenium deficiency, so Keshan disease is not purely a selenium-deficiency disorder — it is a nutritionally conditioned viral cardiomyopathy.

Affected hearts show necrosis and fibrosis with reduced antiviral and antioxidant defenses.

## Clinical Presentation

Keshan disease manifests as acute or chronic cardiac failure, cardiomegaly, arrhythmias, and pathological ECG changes in children and women of childbearing age in endemic areas of China. Pathologically, the myocardium shows multifocal necrosis, fibrous replacement, and inflammatory infiltration.

## Therapeutics & Prevention

The disease is prevented by selenium supplementation and dietary improvement, which dramatically reduced incidence after public health interventions. This disease is a landmark example of nutrient deficiency → enzyme failure → oxidative tissue injury.
