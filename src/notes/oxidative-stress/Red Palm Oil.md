# Red Palm Oil

**Red palm oil** is the carotenoid-rich, minimally refined oil pressed from the mesocarp (flesh) of the African oil palm (*Elaeis guineensis*). Unlike fully bleached and deodorized "palm olein," low-temperature molecular distillation preserves ~80% of the carotenoids and vitamin E of the crude oil, making red palm oil one of the richest natural co-sources of provitamin A and tocotrienol-rich [[Vitamin E]] — plus [[Coenzyme Q10]], squalene, and phytosterols — on the same fatty triglyceride vehicle that delivers them to [[Cell Membranes]].

## Chemical Properties and Composition

Approximate composition of commercial red palm oil / red palm olein (Nagendran et al., doi:10.1177/156482650002100213; MPOB/JOPR data):

| Minor constituent | Content | Profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carotenoids | 600–750 ppm (~665 ppm avg) | β-carotene ~41%, α-carotene ~41%, plus lycopene, phytoene, phytofluene, and 8 other carotenoids |
| Vitamin E (tocochromanols) | 717–863 ppm | **~70% tocotrienols** — α-[[Tocotrienols|tocotrienol]] 29%, γ-tocotrienol 41%, δ-tocotrienol 10%; α-[[Alpha-tocopherol]] 19% |
| Coenzyme Q10 (ubiquinones) | 18–25 ppm | FSP1-axis radical-trap substrate |
| Phytosterols | 235–365 ppm | β-sitosterol 59%, campesterol 22%, stigmasterol 17% |
| Squalene | 14–15 ppm | |
| Fatty acids | vehicle | ~40% saturated (palmitic), ~40–47% monounsaturated (oleic), ~10–13% polyunsaturated (linoleic) |

One tablespoon (~13 g) supplies roughly 8.7 mg total carotenoids (≈400–500 µg RAE provitamin A) and ~10 mg vitamin E — within the same order as the vitamin E RDA (15 mg) and a large fraction of the vitamin A RDA (900/700 µg RAE), in food form.

## Pharmacodynamics and Mechanism of Action

Red palm oil's anti-peroxidation value is the **synergy of co-delivered lipophilic defenders**, each hitting a different node of the [[Lipid Peroxidation]] chain:

- **[[Tocotrienols]] (dominant vitamin E)** — chain-breaking LOO• traps that integrate efficiently into bilayers. Head-to-head against pharmacological and genetic ferroptosis models, tocotrienols suppressed [[Ferroptosis]] at ~10× lower concentrations than tocopherols (EC50 0.12 µM α-tocotrienol vs 2.0 µM α-tocopherol in *Gpx4*-deleted cells; Yang et al., *Sci Rep* 2026, doi:10.1038/s41598-025-34673-1).
- **Provitamin-A carotenoids** — quench [[Singlet Oxygen]] and convert with feedback control to retinol; retinol/retinal are themselves radical-trapping antioxidants with anti-ferroptotic potency ≥ α-tocopherol, and all-trans [[Retinoic Acid]] upregulates [[GPX4]], [[FSP1]], GCH1, and ACSL3 (Jakaria et al. 2023, PMID 37236031; Studer et al., *Nat Commun* 2024).
- **CoQ10 and phytosterols** — ubiquinol feeds the GPX4-independent FSP1 ferroptosis brake; sterols stabilize bilayer packing.
- **The lipid vehicle itself** — the oleic/palmitic matrix solubilizes all of the above into mixed micelles, bypassing the absorption problem that limits carotenoid delivery from fat-free vegetables.

## Applications and Evidence

- **Vitamin A deficiency**: meta-analysis of randomized trials shows red palm oil intake raises serum retinol, with the effect saturating at ≤8 g/day — i.e. the carotenoid-conversion ceiling makes overdosing implausible (Zhang et al., PMC5748732; IOM RDA met by ~1 tsp–1 Tbsp/day depending on population).
- **Cooking stability**: tocotrienols and carotenoids survive moderate heat; heavy bleaching/deodorization refining destroys 50–80% of tocopherols and essentially all carotenoids (see the refining warning under [[Vitamin E]]).
- **Heat-cooking caution**: prolonged high-heat frying still degrades carotenes — use low-to-moderate heat or finish-drizzle.
- **Caveat**: RPO is not an ω-3 or meaningful ω-6 source; it is a phytonutrient carrier, not an essential-fatty-acid oil.

> [!tip] Role in the [[Queen Palm]] tier ranking
> Red palm oil sits in Tier 1 of dietary lipophilic-defender sources (with buriti and sea buckthorn), above Queen Palm pulp oil, because its tocotrienol fraction and total carotenoid load both outrank the palm-family competition.
