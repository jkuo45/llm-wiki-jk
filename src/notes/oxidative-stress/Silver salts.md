# Silver salts

## Overview

Silver salts are ionic compounds of [[Silver]] with anions such as halides, nitrate, and sulfate. Insoluble silver salts, in particular, undergo transformation in the body into soluble [[Silver sulfide albuminates]], which circulate bound to protein.

## Systemic Transformation

Insoluble silver salts are transformed in the body into soluble silver sulfide albuminates, which circulate bound to protein. This transformation is central to silver's systemic distribution because the albumin-bound species can travel through the blood to tissues.

> [!info]
> Formation of silver sulfide is chemically significant, paralleling the mineral argentite. The protein-bound silver then interacts with cellular RNA, DNA, and protein amino or carboxyl groups, or is reduced to metallic silver by [[Ascorbic Acid]] or [[Catecholamines]].

## Relevance

Silver salts link exposure to systemic silver biology: they are the gateway to albumin-bound transport, tissue deposition, and ultimately the pigmentation of [[Argyria]].
