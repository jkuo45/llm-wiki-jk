# OGG1

**OGG1 (8-oxoguanine DNA glycosylase 1)** is a bifunctional glycosylase that initiates the [[Base Excision Repair]] pathway by recognizing and excising [[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine|8-oxo-7,8-dihydroguanine]] (8-oxoG), the most abundant oxidative DNA lesion.

## Structure & Function

OGG1 is a member of the helix-hairpin-helix (HhH) DNA glycosylase superfamily. It contains a C-terminal nuclear localization signal and can localize to both the nucleus and mitochondria. OGG1 recognizes 8-oxoG paired with cytosine (8-oxoG:C) with high specificity. Its bifunctional mechanism:
1. **Glycosylase Activity**: Cleaves the N-glycosidic bond, releasing free 8-oxoG and creating an abasic (AP) site
2. **AP Lyase Activity**: Incises the DNA backbone 3′ to the AP site via β-elimination, generating a single-strand break with 3′-α,β-unsaturated aldehyde and 5′-phosphate termini

The resulting single-strand break is processed by [[APE1]] (AP endonuclease 1), which removes the 3′-blocking group, followed by gap-filling by [[DNA Polymerase β]] and ligation by [[LIG3]]/[[XRCC1]].

## Regulation

OGG1 expression is not strongly induced by oxidative stress; regulation occurs primarily at the post-translational level. [[PARP1]] poly(ADP-ribosyl)ates OGG1, stimulating its activity. [[p53]] transactivates OGG1 expression, linking DNA damage response to repair capacity. [[SIRT1]] deacetylates OGG1, enhancing its activity in mitochondria.

## Clinical Relevance

- **[[Cancer Susceptibility]]**: OGG1 polymorphisms (especially Ser326Cys) are associated with increased risk of lung, breast, and prostate cancers. OGG1⁻/⁻ mice accumulate 8-oxoG and show increased tumor incidence.
- **[[Aging]]**: OGG1 activity declines with age, contributing to age-related accumulation of oxidative DNA damage and [[Genomic Instability|genomic instability]].
- **[[Neurodegeneration|Neurodegenerative diseases]]**: Reduced OGG1 activity in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]] brains correlates with elevated 8-oxoG levels in mitochondrial DNA.
- **Mitochondrial Repair**: Mitochondrial OGG1 (mtOGG1) protects mtDNA from oxidative damage; mtOGG1 overexpression extends lifespan in mice by preserving mitochondrial function.
