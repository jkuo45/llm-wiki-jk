# Indoles

## Chemical Properties

Indoles are heterocyclic aromatic compounds consisting of a fused benzene-pyrrole bicyclic ring system. As a chemical class, indoles are central to many bioactive molecules, including tryptophan, serotonin, and plant auxins.

## Adrenochrome Connection

[[Adrenochrome]] is a cyclic amino ketone pigment classified as a member of indoles. It bears the core indoline-5,6-dione scaffold, a fused bicyclic ring system derived from the cyclized catecholamine backbone. This indole classification reflects adrenochrome's origin as an oxidation product of the catecholamine [[Epinephrine]], whose benzene ring and amine side chain fold into a five-membered nitrogen-containing ring during intramolecular cyclization.

> [!info]
> As an indole quinone, adrenochrome sits at the intersection of catecholamine and pigment biochemistry. Its indole structure underlies both its characteristic pink color and its tendency to undergo further reactions, including polymerization into melanins — and its behavior as an electron-deficient quinone that forms adducts with cellular nucleophiles.

## Relevance

The indole scaffold links catecholamine oxidative chemistry (adrenochrome formation) to melanin pigment biochemistry and to redox-active electrophile biology, connecting this class to oxidative stress research.
