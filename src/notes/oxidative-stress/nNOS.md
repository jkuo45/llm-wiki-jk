# nNOS

**nNOS** (neuronal nitric oxide synthase, NOS1, gene *NOS1*, chromosome 12q24.22) is one of three [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] isoforms. It is a calcium/calmodulin-dependent flavo-hemoprotein that oxidizes [[L-arginine]] to [[Nitric Oxide]] and [[Citrulline]], using NADPH, FAD, FMN, heme, and tetrahydrobiopterin.

## Structure

Like the other NOS isoforms, nNOS is a homodimeric enzyme in which each monomer carries an N-terminal oxygenase domain (binding heme, BH₄, and substrate) and a C-terminal reductase domain (binding FAD, FMN, and NADPH), joined by a calmodulin-binding linker region. Calmodulin binding is the switch that lets electrons flow from the reductase domain to the oxygenase domain.

nNOS is distinguished from eNOS and iNOS by a distinctive **PDZ domain** at its extreme N-terminus, which anchors the enzyme to postsynaptic scaffold proteins. This localization is what allows nNOS-derived nitric oxide to be delivered to specific synaptic targets rather than diffusing freely.

## Physiological Function

nNOS is constitutively expressed in central and peripheral neurons, skeletal muscle, and the macula densa of the kidney.

- **Neurotransmission** — nNOS-derived NO acts as a retrograde messenger, signaling from the postsynaptic neuron back to the presynaptic terminal to modulate transmitter release.
- **Synaptic plasticity** — required for long-term potentiation; the NO–soluble guanylate cyclase–cGMP pathway couples neuronal activity to lasting synaptic strengthening.
- **Cerebral blood flow** — nNOS contributes to autoregulation of cerebral vessels, though eNOS is the dominant vascular source.
- **Skeletal muscle** — nNOS in muscle fibers influences fatigue and glucose handling.

> [!info] Regulation
> nNOS activity is calcium/calmodulin-dependent rather than transcriptionally inducible. It is activated by [[NMDA receptor]] stimulation and consequent calcium influx, by phosphorylation at Ser1257 (which lowers the calcium threshold for activation and effectively increases CaM affinity), and by interaction with the PSD-95/DLG4 scaffold via its PDZ domain. By contrast, iNOS is transcriptionally induced and calcium-independent once made.

## Pathological Roles

- **Stroke and excitotoxicity** — excessive nNOS-derived NO during ischemia-reperfusion reacts with superoxide from [[NADPH Oxidase]] to form [[Peroxynitrite]], driving nitration of proteins and lipids and precipitating neuronal death.
- **Neurodegeneration** — nNOS contributes to dopaminergic neuron loss in [[Parkinson's Disease]] and to broader [[Neurodegeneration]]. The neuronal and microglial balance of nNOS-derived NO and superoxide determines whether the outcome is neuroprotection or neurotoxicity.
- **Neuroinflammation** — inducible NOS dominates in activated glia, but excess nNOS-derived NO during inflammation drives [[Nitrative Stress]] and S-nitrosylation of target proteins.
- **Cardiovascular disease** — nNOS-derived nitrite is a signaling reservoir for NO bioactivity, particularly relevant where oxygen tension is low and the oxygenase reaction is substrate-limited.

> [!warning] The dual role
> nNOS is not straightforwardly protective or damaging. Physiological NO signaling supports plasticity and vascular tone; pathological NO production, particularly when paired with superoxide, becomes a nitrating species. Any claim about nNOS must specify which regime it is describing.
