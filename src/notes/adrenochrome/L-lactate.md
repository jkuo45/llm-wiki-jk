# L-lactate

**L-lactate** is the physiological enantiomer of lactate, produced predominantly by [[Glycolysis]] and anaerobic metabolism and long dismissed merely as a "waste" product. It is now recognized as a signaling metabolite, energy substrate (via the Cori cycle and neuronal monocarboxylate transporters), and epigenetic modulator (lactylation of histones). The mirror-image D-lactate is a minor, partially toxin enantiomer generated by gut microbes and certain detoxification routes.

Beyond canonical pathways, L-lactate is a direct product of **dicarbonyl detoxification**. The peroxiredoxin-like protein [[PARK7]] (DJ-1) stereospecifically converts free [[Methylglyoxal]] — a potent glycating agent — into L-lactate in a glutathione-*independent* manner, neutralizing a major AGE precursor without consuming the principal antioxidant buffer. This route complements the [[Glyoxalase System]] (which uses glutathione) and the [[Fructosyl-Amino Acid Oxidases]]/PARK7 family of defenses against glycation.

> [!info] Redox relevance
> L-lactate can also act as a mild ROS scavenger and fuel for oxidative phosphorylation, linking glycolytic flux to the mitochondrial redox economy central to [[Mitohormesis]].

Lactate shuttles between cells (astrocyte–neuron, myocyte–hepatocyte) thus coordinate both energy and redox homeostasis.
