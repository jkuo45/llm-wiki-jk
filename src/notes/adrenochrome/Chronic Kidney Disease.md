# Chronic Kidney Disease

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a type of kidney disease in which there is gradual loss of kidney function over a period of months to years. It is both a cause and a result of elevated [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGE]] levels in the blood. CKD affects ~10% of adults worldwide and is the 14th leading cause of death.

## Sirtuin Involvement (Wu et al., 2022)

- **Kidney fibrosis**: [[SIRT1]] overexpression abolishes TGF-β1-induced apoptosis/fibrosis (CTGF suppression); SRT1720 attenuates UUO-induced fibrosis; [[SIRT2]] knockdown/inhibition attenuates TGF-β1-induced fibroblast activation; [[SIRT3]] knockout mice are susceptible to severe fibrosis (PDH E1α hyperacetylation at K385); proximal tubule-specific [[SIRT6]] knockout aggravates UUO fibrosis (MDL-800 mitigates). Conversely, SIRT1/SIRT2 downregulation may inhibit fibroblast activation (EGFR/PDGFR-β signaling), a therapeutic lever for CKD.
- **Kidney stones**: SIRT3/SIRT1 downregulated; SIRT3 protective via Nrf2/heme oxygenase-1; suppressing SIRT1 promotes calcium oxalate crystal-cell adhesion.
- **Aging-induced kidney injury**: SIRT1-induced HIF-1α deacetylation protects tubulointerstitial damage; podocyte SIRT1 reduction → aggravated glomerulosclerosis/albuminuria; SIRT6-deficient mice exhibit kidney hypertrophy, glomerular enlargement, and proteinuria.
- **Vascular calcification**: Soluble epoxide hydrolase destabilizes [[SIRT3]] → accelerates VSMC calcification; [[SIRT6]] markedly downregulated in CKD/VC patients — suppresses osteogenic transdifferentiation of VSMCs via runt-related transcription factor 2; MSC exosomes inhibit high-phosphate aortic calcification via SIRT6–HMGB1 deacetylation.
