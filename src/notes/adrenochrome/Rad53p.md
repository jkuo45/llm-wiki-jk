# Rad53p

Rad53p is a serine/threonine protein kinase in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* and the functional homolog of human [[CHK2]]. It serves as a central checkpoint kinase in the DNA damage response, coordinating cell cycle arrest, transcriptional induction of repair genes, and replication fork stabilization upon genotoxic stress. Rad53p is phosphorylated and activated by the upstream kinase [[Tel1p]] (the yeast ortholog of human ATM), which itself responds to DNA double-strand breaks and telomere dysfunction.

## Mitochondrial ROS Connection

Beyond its canonical nuclear role, Rad53p operates in a non-canonical signaling pathway linking mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) to longevity through [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] reprogramming. Studies in [[S. cerevisiae]] have demonstrated that mild mitochondrial dysfunction — induced by genetic perturbations such as deletion of *clk-1* or the cytochrome *c* oxidase assembly factor *cco-1* — elevates mitochondrial ROS, which in turn activates the [[Tel1p]]/Rad53p signaling cascade. This pathway drives changes in histone post-translational modifications, specifically a reduction in [[H3K4me3]] and an increase in [[H4K16ac]], leading to altered transcriptional profiles that promote lifespan extension. This phenomenon represents a form of [[Mitohormesis]], whereby low-level mitochondrial stress elicits an adaptive pro-longevity response. Critically, deletion of *RAD53* or *TEL1* abolishes both the epigenetic changes and the lifespan extension, establishing Rad53p as a required mediator of mitohormetic longevity in yeast.

## Mechanism

The signal transduction sequence proceeds as follows: mitochondrial dysfunction (e.g., loss of respiratory complex subunits or cofactors) elevates superoxide and hydrogen peroxide production from the electron transport chain, particularly at complexes III and I. These ROS are sensed by a signaling module involving Tel1p, which phosphorylates and activates Rad53p. Activated Rad53p translocates to the nucleus where it interfaces with chromatin-modifying complexes, leading to genome-wide decreases in H3K4me3 (a mark associated with active transcription) and increases in H4K16ac (a mark linked to chromatin decondensation and transcriptional competence). These epigenetic changes reprogram the expression of longevity-associated genes, including those involved in stress resistance, metabolism, and mitochondrial quality control. The result is a shift toward a pro-longevity transcriptional state that extends replicative and chronological lifespan in yeast.

This Rad53p/Tel1p-dependent mitohormetic pathway is independent of the canonical DNA damage checkpoint function — the same kinase that arrests the cell cycle upon genotoxic stress can, under different activation thresholds and contexts, orchestrate an adaptive survival program. The pathway thus illustrates how a core DNA damage signaling module has been co-opted to serve as a redox-responsive longevity regulator, highlighting the deep evolutionary links between stress signaling, metabolic state, and aging.
