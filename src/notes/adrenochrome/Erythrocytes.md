# Erythrocytes

**Erythrocytes**, or **red blood cells** (RBCs), are the most common type of blood cell and the principal means of delivering oxygen (O₂) to body tissues via blood flow. Mature RBCs are 7–8 µm biconcave discs, filled with hemoglobin, and — uniquely among the body's cells — lack a nucleus and all organelles.

## Structural constraints

Erythrocytes arise by [[Erythropoiesis]]: enucleation (rate-limiting; FOXO3/E2F2, miR-30a/miR-191/miR-181a, F-actin/dynein/tropomodulin, p38 MAPK) and clearance of mitochondria (mitophagy), lysosomes, peroxisomes, Golgi, ER and ribosomes. The payoff is haemoglobin-packed, deformable, metabolically self-sufficient cells — and the price is that mature RBCs cannot synthesize new protein, so all regulation (including cell death) is post-translational.

## Canonical and non-canonical functions

- **Gas transport:** hemoglobin carries O₂ and ~23% of CO₂ (with [[Carbonic Anhydrase]] and [[Anion Exchanger 1|AE1/Band 3]] completing the bicarbonate shift).
- **Vascular tone:** RBCs scavenge eNOS-derived [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] via oxyhemoglobin and can store/release it (as S-nitrosohemoglobin) in hypoxia, tuning NO bioavailability.
- **Hemostasis:** altered viscosity and flow; ATP/ADP export on shear stimulating platelets; PS exposure driving thrombin (up to 40% of thrombin in some conditions); RBC microvesicles activating factor XII; vWF–ABO glycosylation; polyhedrocytes decreasing clot embologenicity.
- **Redox:** GSH-based scavenging of neutrophil/monocyte/endothelial ROS and RNS; cannot upregulate antioxidant enzymes transcriptionally.
- **Immunity:** [[TLR9]] binding cell-free DNA, [[Duffy Antigen Receptor for Chemokines|DARC]] chemokine scavenging, pathogen binding, and bactericidal extracellular hemoglobin ROS.

## Cell death

Only two regulated death modalities remain in mature RBCs: [[Eryptosis]] (Ca2+-driven, PS-exposing, non-lytic) and [[Erythronecroptosis]] (RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL, lytic). Accidental death is [[Hemolysis]]. The erythrocyte-specific repertoire is a direct consequence of organelle loss: caspases, Fas/FasL and the necrosome survive as "heritage" of the erythroid lineage, but mitochondria, nucleus and the receptor set (TNFR1/2, TRAIL receptors) are gone.

## Vulnerability to Oxidation

Erythrocytes are subject to [[Oxidative Stress]] from the autoxidation of [[Hemoglobin]] (iron-mediated Fenton chemistry), the presence of circulating catecholamine metabolites like [[Adrenochrome]], and NADPH oxidase/xanthine oxidoreductase activity — which can lead to [[Hemolysis]], [[Methemoglobinemia]], or ROS-mediated [[Eryptosis]]. Because RBCs cannot upregulate catalase/SOD/glutathione peroxidase, redox damage is essentially irreversible once it occurs, and GSH/PPP flux (via [[Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase|G6PD]]) is the main defence.
