# Rtg1

Rtg1 is a basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factor in the budding yeast [[S. cerevisiae]] and a core component of the retrograde response (RTG) pathway. It forms a heterodimer with the bHLH/leucine-zipper protein [[Rtg3]]; in healthy cells the complex is phosphorylated and sequestered in the cytoplasm by the 14-3-3 proteins Bmh1/2. When mitochondrial function is compromised — by loss of mitochondrial DNA, respiratory-chain inhibition, or nutrient shifts — the kinases that maintain cytoplasmic retention are inactivated, Rtg1–Rtg3 translocates to the nucleus, and the heterodimer drives expression of nuclear genes encoding mitochondrial and peroxisomal proteins, including the citrate–isocitrate shuttle enzymes (CIT2, ACO1, IDH1/2).

The RTG pathway thus reconfigures intermediary metabolism so that the cell can compensate for lost mitochondrial capacity, a yeast analog of the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|UPRmt]] and of mammalian [[Mitohormesis]]. The shared logic — a mitonuclear retrograde signal that reprograms nuclear transcription toward stress resistance and metabolic flexibility — is conserved from yeast to humans and is a cornerstone of longevity research in [[S. cerevisiae]].

The connection to [[Adrenochrome]] and redox biology is the general principle that mitochondrial stress signals induce cytoprotective, antioxidant programs that limit reactive oxygen species leakage. By tuning TCA-cycle and glutathione-precursor flux, the Rtg1 pathway helps maintain reducing capacity, indirectly buffering the [[Oxidative Stress]] that drives catecholamine autoxidation such as the conversion of [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]].

> [!info] Partner swap
> Rtg3 can pair with Rtg2/3-independent factors, but the Rtg1–Rtg3 bHLH heterodimer is the DNA-binding engine of the RTG response.
