# cco-1

**cco-1** encodes the *C. elegans* ortholog of cytochrome c oxidase subunit V (COX5, complex IV of the electron-transport chain). As a structural and regulatory subunit of cytochrome c oxidase, cco-1 is required for efficient mitochondrial respiration and the final reduction of oxygen to water.

Tissue-specific knockdown of *cco-1* (classically in body-wall muscle or neurons) produces a localized respiratory deficiency that, rather than remaining cell-autonomous, triggers a **cell-non-autonomous** stress signal. Stressed cells release [[Mitokines]] — secreted factors, notably the mitochondrial-derived peptide humanin and the small protein HPO (FGF21-like in mammals) — that travel to distal tissues and activate the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPRmt) systemically. This inter-tissue communication extends [[Lifespan]] and enhances stress resistance organism-wide.

> [!info] Systemic mitohormesis
> cco-1 knockdown is a canonical demonstration that mitochondrial stress in one tissue can be broadcast to others via mitokines, a key mechanism of systemic [[Mitohormesis]].

Because complex IV sits downstream of the redox chain, cco-1 perturbation also perturbs [[Superoxide anion]] handling, linking respiratory architecture to redox set-point.
