# 2-deoxy-D-glucose

**2-deoxy-D-glucose (2DG)** is a synthetic glucose analog in which the 2-hydroxyl group is replaced by hydrogen, preventing it from progressing through glycolysis.

## Chemical Properties & Mechanism of Action

- Taken up by glucose transporters ([[GLUT4]]) and phosphorylated by hexokinase to 2DG-6-phosphate, which cannot be further metabolized and accumulates, competitively inhibiting hexokinase and phosphoglucose isomerase.
- **Net effect:** blockade of [[Glycolysis]] and a transient drop in ATP that mimics [[Caloric Restriction]] and energy stress, activating [[AMPK]] and stress-response programs.

## Mitohormesis & Longevity Relevance

> [!info] Mitohormetic ROS signaling
> Rather than being purely toxic, low-dose 2DG forces cells to shift toward mitochondrial [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]], producing a mild, transient burst of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]]. This low-level oxidative signal induces adaptive antioxidant and stress-defense responses — the hallmark of [[Mitohormesis]] — and extends [[Lifespan]] in *C. elegans*. The concept dovetails with the redox-adaptation themes central to the [[Adrenochrome]] topic, where controlled [[Oxidative Stress]] can be protective (a biphasic, hormetic dose–response) while excess is damaging.

## Applications

- Research tool to inhibit glycolysis and probe the Warburg effect in [[Cancer]] cells.
- Investigated (often in combination) as an antitumor and caloric-restriction-mimetic agent.
