# Cysteinyldopa

[[Cysteinyldopa]] refers to the family of thioether adducts formed when the sulfhydryl group of [[Cysteine]] attacks the electrophilic quinone ring of [[Dopaquinone]]. The predominant isomer, 5-S-cysteinyldopa, is the first stable product of the pheomelanogenic branch of melanogenesis and is subsequently oxidized and polymerized into pheomelanin—the sulfur-containing, yellow-to-red form of [[Melanins]] distinct from eumelanin.

## Formation and Redox Significance

Cysteinyldopa formation is a detoxification shunt: by trapping dopaquinone, cysteine prevents the quinone from undergoing redox cycling that would generate [[Superoxide anion]] and [[Oxidative Stress]]. However, pheomelanin itself is a weaker antioxidant than eumelanin and, under UV exposure, can generate reactive species, linking high pheomelanin content to photocarcinogenesis. The branching ratio between cysteinyldopa (pheomelanin) and [[5,6-Dihydroxyindole]] (eumelanin) is governed by intracellular cysteine availability and [[Glutathione]] levels.

> [!note]
> Cysteinyldopa sits at the same catechol-quinone branch point as the adrenochrome/aminochrome pathway; both reflect how cells manage electrophilic ortho-quinones through thiol capture versus redox polymerization.

## Clinical and Neuromelanin Relevance

Urinary and plasma cysteinyldopa are established biomarkers of melanocyte activity and are used in monitoring metastatic melanoma. In the brain, cysteine adducts of catechol quinones analogous to cysteinyldopa may moderate the oxidative burden of [[Dopamine]] and [[Epinephrine]] metabolism, influencing [[Neuromelanin]] composition and dopaminergic neuron survival in the [[Substantia Nigra]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]].
