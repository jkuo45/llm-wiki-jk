# Nitrobenzene

Nitrobenzene (C₆H₅NO₂) is a pale-yellow aromatic liquid used industrially as a solvent and as a precursor to aniline and other chemicals. It is lipophilic and readily absorbed through skin, lungs, and gut, making occupational and accidental exposure a recognized hazard. Systemically, nitrobenzene is reductively metabolized — principally in erythrocytes and the liver — through a series of nitroso- and hydroxylamine intermediates to aniline and, critically, to a ferrihemoglobin-generating species.

The toxic hallmark is acquired [[Methemoglobinemia]]. The reductive metabolites (notably phenylhydroxylamine) oxidize [[Hemoglobin]]'s ferrous (Fe²⁺) heme iron to the ferric (Fe³⁺) state, producing [[Methemoglobin]], which cannot bind oxygen and shifts the [[Oxygen-dissociation curve]] leftward, impairing tissue oxygen delivery. Cyanosis, dyspnea, and in severe cases central nervous system depression result. Because the same metabolites can re-reduce methemoglobin in a redox cycle, nitrobenzene poisoning can be persistent and recurrent.

Nitrobenzene's metabolic relationship to [[Aniline dyes]] (manufactured from it) explains their shared methemoglobinemia profile. The broader redox theme connects to adrenochrome science through the chemistry of heme and catecholamine oxidation: just as nitrobenzene drives ferrihemoglobin formation, [[Oxidative Stress]] drives catecholamine autoxidation to [[Adrenochrome]]. Both processes illustrate how a single oxidizing insult propagates through a redox-active endogenous carrier.

> [!warning] Redox cycling
> Phenylhydroxylamine both oxidizes and reduces hemoglobin, creating a self-sustaining methemoglobin cycle.
