# Diaphorase

[[Diaphorase]] is a historical/functional name for **[[NADH-dependent methemoglobin reductase]]** (also called [[Cytochrome b5 reductase]]), the flavoprotein enzyme responsible for the continuous reduction of [[Methemoglobin]] back to functional [[Hemoglobin]] in red blood cells.

## Structure & Mechanism of Action

- An [[FAD]]-containing flavoenzyme that oxidizes [[NADH]] and transfers electrons to cytochrome b5.
- **Pathway:** reduced cytochrome b5 donates an electron to the ferric (Fe³⁺) iron of [[Methemoglobin]], returning it to the ferrous (Fe²⁺) state that can bind oxygen. This NADH–cytochrome b5 reductase system accounts for the bulk (~95–99%) of physiological methemoglobin reduction.
- A minor [[NADPH]]-dependent methemoglobin reductase pathway exists but is normally inactive — it becomes the therapeutic route exploited by [[Methylene blue]].

## Physiological & Clinical Relevance

> [!info] Methemoglobinemia
> Under normal conditions ~1–2% of hemoglobin spontaneously autoxidizes to methemoglobin and is rapidly reduced by diaphorase. **Congenital deficiency** of cytochrome b5 reductase causes hereditary [[Methemoglobinemia]] with chronic [[Cyanosis]]. In acute toxic (oxidant-induced) methemoglobinemia, [[Methylene blue]] is reduced to leucomethylene blue by NADPH, which then non-enzymatically reduces methemoglobin — effectively substituting for the diaphorase system.

This redox-restoration enzyme is central to the [[Adrenochrome]] topic's focus on iron/heme oxidation, [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], and the reversal of oxidative damage.
