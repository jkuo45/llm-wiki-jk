# Metanephrine

**Metanephrine** is the O-methylated metabolite of [[Epinephrine]] (adrenaline), formed by the enzyme [[COMT|catechol-O-methyltransferase]] (COMT). Its counterpart, normetanephrine, is derived analogously from [[Norepinephrine]].

## Biochemistry & Pathway

- **Formation:** [[COMT]] transfers a methyl group from [[S-adenosylmethionine]] to the 3-hydroxyl of epinephrine's catechol ring, yielding metanephrine. This O-methylation is a principal route of catecholamine inactivation, working in parallel with oxidative deamination by [[Monoamine oxidase|MAO]].
- **Downstream fate:** metanephrine is further deaminated and oxidized to vanillylmandelic acid (VMA), the terminal urinary catecholamine metabolite.
- **Localization:** much metanephrine is produced continuously within [[Chromaffin cells]] of the [[Adrenal medulla]], independent of catecholamine release.

## Clinical & Adrenochrome Relevance

> [!info] Diagnostic biomarker
> Because metanephrines are produced steadily by chromaffin tumor tissue, plasma-free and 24-hour urinary metanephrines are the most sensitive biochemical test for **pheochromocytoma** and paraganglioma. [[COMT]] genotype (COMT Val158Met) influences the rate of catecholamine O-methylation.

Metanephrine represents the **non-oxidative** branch of [[Epinephrine]] disposal. This contrasts with the **oxidative/autoxidation** branch central to the [[Adrenochrome]] topic, in which epinephrine is instead converted through [[Aminochromes]] to [[Adrenochrome]] with generation of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — making metanephrine an important reference point for how much adrenaline is safely methylated versus oxidized.
