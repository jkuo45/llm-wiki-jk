# BluB-like

**BluB-like** enzymes are bacterial proteins homologous to BluB (5,6-dimethylbenzimidazole synthase), the radical SAM enzyme that builds the lower ligand of vitamin B₁₂. Metagenomic and structural studies identified BluB-like biocatalysts in soil and gut-associated microbes that can bind and **cleave glucosepane**, the most abundant and chemically stable advanced glycation cross-link in aged human tissues.

Glucosepane forms irreversibly between lysine and arginine side chains via a glucose-derived dicarbonyl, and its accumulation stiffens collagen and elastin in skin, lens, and vasculature — contributing to [[Arterial Stiffening]] and diabetic complications. Because glucosepane resists mammalian enzymatic repair, BluB-like enzymes are of major interest as **cross-link breakers**: recombinant or engineered variants can hydrolyze the glucosepane bridge, potentially reversing matrix rigidification.

> [!tip] Therapeutic prospect
> A glucosepane-degrading enzyme would be the first mechanism-based AGE-breaker, complementary to inhibitors such as [[Alagebrium]] and [[Aminoguanidine]], which blunt new cross-link formation rather than removing existing ones.

Characterization of BluB-like specificity and in vivo delivery (e.g., topical or enzymatic) remains an active research frontier in geroscience.
