# Anaphylaxis

**Anaphylaxis** is a severe, rapidly progressing, systemic allergic reaction that can be life-threatening within minutes of exposure to an allergen (foods, insect venom, drugs, latex). It is driven by IgE-crosslinking on mast cells and basophils, causing massive degranulation with release of histamine, leukotrienes, and tryptase, leading to vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, bronchoconstriction, and hypotension.

**[[Epinephrine]]** (adrenaline) is the unambiguous first-line treatment and the only medication shown to reduce mortality; it is given intramuscularly (typically via an auto-injector such as [[EpiPen]] or [[Symjepi]]) without delay. Epinephrine reverses bronchoconstriction, constricts vasculature, and suppresses further mediator release. Because epinephrine solutions can oxidize to [[Adrenochrome]] and adrenolutin on light/heat exposure, formulation stability and purity are clinically critical — degraded product loses potency precisely when needed.

> [!warning] Formulation integrity
> Epinephrine autoinjectors must be protected from light and heat; oxidation to adrenochrome-like species reduces efficacy, making redox stability a genuine patient-safety issue.

Airway support, antihistamines, corticosteroids, and intravenous fluids are adjuncts, but none substitute for prompt epinephrine. Biphasic reactions can recur hours later, warranting observation.
