# Toren Finkel

**Toren Finkel** is an American physician-scientist whose research has shaped the understanding of mitochondrial biology in aging, cardiovascular disease, and cellular stress signaling. He is Professor of Medicine at the University of Pittsburgh and Director of the Center for Metabolism and Mitochondrial Medicine (CM3). Previously, he served as Chief of the Cardiovascular Branch at the NHLBI/NIH.

## Career & Background

Finkel earned his MD and PhD from Johns Hopkins, completed residency at Johns Hopkins Hospital and cardiology fellowship at Massachusetts General Hospital, then joined the NHLBI as Chief of the Cardiovascular Branch before moving to the University of Pittsburgh in 2013 to direct the Center for Metabolism and Mitochondrial Medicine.

## Research Contributions

### Mitohormesis

Finkel is best known for co-developing the **mitohormesis** concept—the idea that mild mitochondrial stress activates adaptive signaling pathways that extend lifespan. In his landmark 2014 *Cell Metabolism* review with [[Jeanho Yun]], he showed that low-level mitochondrial perturbation triggers compensatory transcriptional and metabolic programs that enhance cellular resilience and delay aging. This work established that mitochondrial hormetic stress operates through the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPRmt) and [[SKN-1]]/[[Nrf2]] signaling, shifting the paradigm from viewing mitochondrial dysfunction solely as pathogenic to recognizing its adaptive, pro-longevity potential.

### Reactive Oxygen Species & Redox Signaling

A major theme of Finkel's work is the nuanced role of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (ROS) in cellular physiology. His lab demonstrated that ROS function as essential second messengers, not merely unavoidable byproducts of oxidative metabolism. Finkel has argued that the dose, duration, and subcellular location of ROS determine whether they promote health or drive pathology, helping reconcile why antioxidant therapies have largely failed while genetic enhancement of endogenous ROS can paradoxically extend lifespan.

### PINK1/Parkin Mitophagy & Mitochondrial Quality Control

Finkel has made key contributions to understanding the [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] mitophagy pathway. His group elucidated how mitochondrial depolarization triggers PINK1 to recruit Parkin to the outer mitochondrial membrane, leading to ubiquitination and autophagic clearance of damaged organelles. This work established mitophagy as critical for mitochondrial homeostasis in heart, brain, and skeletal muscle.

### Mitochondrial NAD+ & SIRT3

Finkel's lab investigated mitochondrial [[NAD+]] and [[SIRT3]] in aging, showing that age-related NAD+ decline impairs SIRT3 activity, causing hyperacetylation of mitochondrial proteins and compromised metabolic flux. This connected NAD+ biology to mitochondrial dysfunction in aging, providing rationale for [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] and [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide]] supplementation to preserve mitochondrial function.
