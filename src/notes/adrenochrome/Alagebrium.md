# Alagebrium

**Alagebrium (ALT-711)** is a synthetic thiazolium-derived compound developed as a prototype **AGE-breaker** — a drug designed to cleave preexisting advanced glycation end-product cross-links rather than merely preventing new ones. Its parent chemical class (2,3-disubstituted thiazolium) was identified from studies of aminoguanidine analogs, but alagebrium's novelty was its ability to break established protein–protein cross-links, particularly those involving glucose-derived and carbonyl adducts on [[Collagen]] and vascular matrix.

In preclinical and early clinical studies, alagebrium improved arterial compliance, reduced [[Arterial Stiffening]], and enhanced left-ventricular diastolic function, effects attributed to reversal of AGE-mediated matrix rigidification. It also showed promise in cardiac hypertrophy and vascular remodeling models.

> [!info] Breaker vs. inhibitor
> Unlike [[Aminoguanidine]], which blocks AGE *formation*, alagebrium targets *existing* cross-links — a complementary anti-glycation strategy. The enzymatic [[BluB-like]] glucosepane breakers represent a newer extension of this concept.

Development of alagebrium was ultimately discontinued for commercial reasons rather than definitive safety failure, but it remains the proof-of-concept AGE-breaker informing current cross-link–targeted geroscience.
