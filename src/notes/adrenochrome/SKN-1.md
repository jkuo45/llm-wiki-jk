# SKN-1

SKN-1 is the *Caenorhabditis elegans* ortholog of mammalian [[NRF2]] (NF-E2-related factor 2), a cap'n'collar basic leucine zipper transcription factor that governs the conserved antioxidant and detoxification response. Under basal conditions SKN-1 is retained in the cytoplasm through association with the BTB–Kelch repressor WDR-23 (the homolog of [[Keap1]]); upon electrophilic or oxidative insult, the repressor is inactivated and SKN-1 translocates to the nucleus to induce phase-II enzymes, xenobiotic transporters, and proteostasis genes.

SKN-1 is a central node in the [[Mitohormesis]] framework: mild mitochondrial dysfunction or low-level [[Oxidative Stress]] — including the modest [[Superoxide anion]] leak from compromised respiration — activates SKN-1, driving a cytoprotective transcriptional program that extends [[Lifespan]]. Genetic studies show that SKN-1 activity is required for the longevity conferred by mitochondrial mutants (e.g., *isp-1*, *clk-1*) and by dietary restriction, placing it downstream of the redox signal that links mitochondrial state to systemic stress resistance.

The adrenochrome/redox relevance is direct: SKN-1/NRF2 target genes include glutathione biosynthesis, [[Glutathione|glutathione]]-dependent reductases, and quinone reductases that detoxify aminochromes and limit the autoxidation of catecholamines such as [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. By boosting the cellular reducing environment and electrophile-detoxifying capacity, the SKN-1 program buffers precisely the oxidative load that drives aminochrome formation, making it a key endogenous defense against adrenochrome accumulation.

> [!info] Conservation
> NRF2/SKN-1 is the most conserved eukaryotic oxidative-stress transcription factor and a major longevity determinant.
