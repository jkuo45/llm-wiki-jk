Neutrophils (polymorphonuclear leukocytes, or PMNs) can catalyze the oxidation of adrenaline (epinephrine) to adrenochrome via reactive oxygen species (ROS) they produce, particularly in inflammatory conditions.

pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

This is based on legitimate but relatively old scientific research (primarily from the 1980s), not conspiracy theories. Adrenochrome itself is a real oxidation product of adrenaline, but claims of it being harvested for psychoactive or other exotic purposes lack scientific support.Key Scientific Findings

- Mechanism: Activated neutrophils generate superoxide radicals and other ROS through their NADPH oxidase system (and potentially myeloperoxidase, MPO). These oxidants drive the conversion of adrenaline to adrenochrome. This was shown to be the major catabolic pathway for adrenaline in the presence of stimulated neutrophils, accounting for over 80% of oxidation at low micromolar concentrations (vs. the usual COMT/MAO pathways).
    
    pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
    
- Experimental evidence:
    - Stimulated neutrophils (e.g., with latex beads or chemotactic peptides like fMLP) rapidly oxidize adrenaline to adrenochrome, detectable by spectrophotometry (at 480 nm, its purple color) or HPLC.
    - The reaction is inhibited by superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase, and azide, confirming ROS involvement.
    - Cell-free medium from activated neutrophils retains this oxidizing ability, indicating released factors (ROS or enzymes) drive it.
        
        pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
        
- In vivo relevance: Researchers detected adrenochrome-like compounds in neutrophil-rich fluids, such as synovial fluid from rheumatoid arthritis patients and serum from myocardial infarction patients (where neutrophil activation occurs). This suggests a role in inflammatory or stress-related conditions.
    
    link.springer.com
    

One study proposed this as a potential catabolic route for adrenaline in inflammatory diseases, with possible links to stress-induced heart issues (via adrenochrome or its metabolites like adrenolutin).

link.springer.com

Context and Limitations

- Adrenochrome basics: It forms readily when adrenaline is oxidized (in vitro or in vivo) by agents like silver oxide, air, or ROS. It has a distinctive violet color. Early (1950s–1970s) hypotheses linked it to schizophrenia, but these were not substantiated.
    
    en.wikipedia.org
    
- Neutrophil role is niche: This is not a primary or major systemic source of adrenochrome in healthy physiology. Adrenaline oxidation occurs via multiple routes. Neutrophil-driven production is prominent locally at sites of inflammation, infection, or tissue damage where neutrophils accumulate and release ROS.
- Related enzymes: Myeloperoxidase (MPO, abundant in neutrophils) may contribute to catecholamine oxidation in some contexts, though the primary driver in the cited studies is ROS.
    
    jstage.jst.go.jp
    
- Modern research: This pathway is occasionally referenced in studies of oxidative stress, cardiovascular effects, or neutrophil function, but it is not a major active area of research today. Adrenochrome assays have even been used historically to measure ROS production (though with caveats about specificity).
    
    sciencedirect.com

Myeloperoxidase (MPO) is a heme-containing enzyme stored primarily in the azurophilic granules of neutrophils (and to a lesser extent in monocytes/macrophages). It plays a central role in the oxidative burst during innate immune responses.

pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Core Oxidative MechanismMPO uses hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂, generated by NADPH oxidase) as a substrate to catalyze the two-electron oxidation of halides (mainly Cl⁻, also Br⁻ and SCN⁻) into hypohalous acids:

- Primary reaction (halogenation cycle): MPO + H₂O₂ + Cl⁻ → HOCl (hypochlorous acid) + H₂O + native MPO.  
    HOCl is a potent antimicrobial oxidant that damages microbial proteins, lipids, and DNA.
    
    pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
    

MPO also operates a peroxidase cycle, performing one-electron oxidations of a wide range of organic substrates (e.g., tyrosine → tyrosyl radical, urate, ascorbate, nitrite → NO₂•, phenols, catecholamines, and others). This generates substrate radicals that can initiate further chain reactions, including lipid peroxidation and protein modification.

sciencedirect.com

MPO cycles through intermediates:

- Compound I (highly oxidizing, ferryl-oxo + porphyrin radical): formed from native MPO + H₂O₂.
- Compound II (less reactive ferryl-oxo): involved in one-electron oxidations.
- It can also form Compound III and has "NO oxidase" activity, consuming nitric oxide and contributing to endothelial dysfunction.
    
    sciencedirect.com
    

MPO and Catecholamine Oxidation (Adrenaline/Epinephrine to Adrenochrome)In the context of neutrophils and adrenochrome:

- Activated neutrophils produce ROS (superoxide, H₂O₂) and release MPO. While superoxide and derived ROS drive much of the adrenaline oxidation to adrenochrome, MPO can directly contribute.
- Studies show MPO + H₂O₂ oxidizes epinephrine to adrenochrome (a red/purple pigment) via a free-radical intermediate (likely o-semiquinone). This was observed in cell-free systems and linked to MPO's peroxidase activity.
    
    jstage.jst.go.jp
    
- Early neutrophil studies noted that azide (an MPO inhibitor) partially blocks adrenaline oxidation, suggesting MPO's involvement alongside ROS. Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) or direct peroxidase action may play a role, though it was not the sole or dominant pathway in all experiments.
    
    researchgate.net
    
- Proposed in vivo relevance: At sites of inflammation/trauma (where neutrophils accumulate), MPO could convert local epinephrine to adrenochrome. One hypothesis (from older literature) suggests this might aid hemostasis/wound healing via radical polymerization, though this is speculative.
    
    jstage.jst.go.jp
    

This fits into broader catecholamine oxidation under oxidative stress, potentially relevant in inflammatory cardiac conditions (e.g., myocardial infarction with neutrophil infiltration).

jmcc-online.com

Broader Pathophysiological Roles in Oxidation

- Antimicrobial defense: HOCl and other oxidants kill pathogens in phagosomes.
- Tissue damage and inflammation: Excess MPO/HOCl oxidizes host proteins (e.g., 3-chlorotyrosine, 3-nitrotyrosine), lipids, and lipoproteins (LDL → more atherogenic; HDL → dysfunctional). This links MPO to atherosclerosis, endothelial dysfunction (via NO consumption), and plaque instability.
    
    pubs.acs.org
    
- Cardiovascular and other diseases: Elevated MPO levels/activity associate with increased oxidative stress, cardiovascular risk, and conditions involving chronic inflammation. It serves as both a biomarker and mediator.
    
    ahajournals.org
    
- MPO also oxidizes other biomolecules (e.g., estrogens, serotonin, xenobiotics) and can generate protein radicals or cross-links.

Regulation and InhibitionMPO activity is tightly controlled (e.g., by pH, halide availability, antioxidants). Inhibitors target the enzyme for therapeutic potential in inflammatory diseases (e.g., suicide inhibitors or compounds that trap Compound II).

sciencedirect.com

Summary: MPO is a powerful oxidant generator via HOCl and radical formation. Its role in catecholamine (adrenaline) oxidation to adrenochrome is real but secondary/context-dependent compared to direct ROS in neutrophils. This contributes to local oxidative environments in inflammation, with potential protective (antimicrobial) and damaging (tissue injury) effects. Modern research focuses more on its cardiovascular and chronic disease implications than on adrenochrome per se.
