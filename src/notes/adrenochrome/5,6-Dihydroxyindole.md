# 5,6-Dihydroxyindole

[[5,6-Dihydroxyindole]] (5,6-DHI) is a reducing indole derivative produced from [[Dopachrome]] during melanogenesis; its carboxylic acid analog, 5,6-dihydroxyindole-2-carboxylic acid (DHICA), arises via dopachrome tautomerase. Both are oxidized and polymerized by [[Tyrosinase]] and related oxidases into eumelanin, the dark, nitrogen-rich form of [[Melanins]]. 5,6-DHI is the prototypical eumelanin monomer and a major constituent of [[Neuromelanin]].

## Redox and Polymerization Chemistry

5,6-DHI is a potent reducing agent that auto-oxidizes readily, donating electrons to oxygen and generating [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] including [[Superoxide anion]] and [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]. This oxidative polymerization is what cross-links the indole units into the heterogeneous, metal-chelating eumelanin polymer. The redox activity of DHI-derived melanin is double-edged: it can scavenge radicals but also, during its formation, amplifies local oxidative load.

> [!info]
> Neuromelanin in the [[Substantia Nigra]] is built from DHI/DHICA and catechol-derived quinones; its accumulation parallels the aminochrome chemistry of [[Adrenochrome]] and [[Dopaminochrome]] in catecholaminergic neurons.

## Link to the Aminochrome Axis

The DHI pathway and the adrenochrome/aminochrome pathway converge on catechol quinone chemistry: [[Dopamine]] and [[Epinephrine]] are both converted to ortho-quinones and indolequinones that polymerize into pigment. Uncontrolled 5,6-DHI oxidation thus contributes to the same protein cross-linking and oxidative damage implicated in [[Parkinson's Disease]] and the broader adrenochrome toxicity narrative.
