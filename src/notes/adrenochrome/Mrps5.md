# Mrps5

**Mrps5** (mitochondrial ribosomal protein S5) encodes a small subunit component of the mammalian mitochondrial ribosome (mitoribosome), which translates the 13 mtDNA-encoded subunits of the oxidative-phosphorylation machinery. Like other mitochondrial ribosomal proteins, Mrps5 is essential for assembly of respiratory complexes I, III, IV, and V.

Genetic knockdown or partial loss of Mrps5 reduces mitochondrial translation capacity, producing a mild respiratory deficiency that is sensed as proteotoxic stress. This activates the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPRmt): the ATF5/ATFS-1 transcription factor relocalizes to the nucleus and induces mitochondrial chaperones, proteases, and antioxidant defenses, a state that has been associated with extended [[Lifespan]] and improved stress resistance in model systems. Mrps5 therefore belongs to a class of "mitohormetic" longevity genes — including *cco-1*, *isp-1*, and *clk-1* — whose calibrated impairment prolongs healthspan.

> [!info] Translation vs. respiration
> Sublethal inhibition of mitoribosomal subunits (Mrps5) phenocopies pharmacological mitochondrial translation blockers such as doxycycline and actinonin, confirming a shared UPRmt-driven longevity mechanism.

Human MRPS5 mutations have been reported in isolated mitochondrial disorders, underscoring the dosage sensitivity of mitoribosome function.
