# QueE-like

QueE-like enzymes are radical-S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) proteins distantly related to QueE (7-carboxy-7-deazaguanine synthase), an enzyme of the queuosine/tetrapyrrole-related biosynthetic family. Metagenomic and structural screens identified QueE-like homologs in certain soil bacteria and fungi that, unlike the canonical nucleotide biosynthetic enzyme, possess a repositioned active site suited to cleaving the stable lysine–arginine imidazolium crosslink known as [[Glucosepane]]. Glucosepane is one of the most abundant advanced glycation end-product (AGE) crosslinks in human tissue, accumulating relentlessly in long-lived proteins such as collagen and elastin and contributing to vascular and skin stiffening with age.

The enzymatic mechanism appears to use a radical intermediate generated by reductive cleavage of SAM to abstract a hydrogen from the crosslink, enabling rearrangement and hydrolytic release of the crosslinked amino acids. In functional assays, QueE-like enzymes can grow on synthetic glucosepane as a sole nutrient source, and recombinant preparations degrade glucosepane in vitro. Because glucosepane is exceptionally resistant to mammalian [[Glyoxalase System]] and other endogenous repair pathways, these biocatalysts are being explored as research tools and as the basis for "crosslink breakers" aimed at reversing [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGE]]-mediated tissue stiffening.

The relevance to adrenochrome and redox biology is the shared theme of glycative and oxidant damage: AGEs and their receptors ([[RAGE]]) amplify [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] and oxidative signaling, while carbonyl stress overlaps with the autoxidation of catecholamines such as [[Epinephrine]] into [[Adrenochrome]]. Enzymatic clearance of glucosepane addresses the glycative arm of this dual burden.

> [!tip] Therapeutic angle
> AGE crosslink breakers complement carbonyl traps (e.g., [[Pyridoxamine]]) by removing pre-formed damage rather than preventing new formation.
