# Doxycycline

**Doxycycline** is a broad-spectrum tetracycline-class antibiotic that, at concentrations above its antimicrobial IC₅₀, inhibits mitochondrial translation by binding the mitochondrial 28S (39S) ribosomal subunit and blocking aminoacyl-tRNA accommodation. This selectively impairs synthesis of the 13 mtDNA-encoded respiratory-chain subunits, gradually depleting complexes I, III, IV, and V and shifting cellular metabolism toward glycolysis.

The resulting respiratory deficiency is a classic trigger of the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response]] (UPRmt). In *C. elegans*, doxycycline-mediated mitochondrial translation inhibition activates the ATFS-1 transcriptional program, induces mitochondrial chaperones and proteases, and extends [[Lifespan]]; similar UPRmt engagement and healthspan benefits have been reported in mammalian models. Because doxycycline acts on mitochondria rather than nuclear ribosomes, it is a widely used pharmacological tool to probe mitohormesis and retrograde signaling.

> [!info] Therapeutic duality
> Beyond infection, doxycycline is repurposed as an anti-angiogenic and matrix-metalloproteinase-modulating agent in oncology and as a suppressant of aneurysm expansion. Its mitochondrial effects also intersect with senotherapeutic strategies that leverage mild proteotoxic stress.

The compound is generally well tolerated but, like other tetracyclines, can cause photosensitivity, gastrointestinal upset, and — rarely — intracranial hypertension.
