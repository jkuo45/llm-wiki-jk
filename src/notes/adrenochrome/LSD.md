# LSD

**LSD** (lysergic acid diethylamide, LSD-25) is a potent, long-acting
serotonergic psychedelic of the ergoline class. It is among the most
pharmacologically potent substances known: active doses in humans begin around
25–50 micrograms, and effects last 8–12 hours with a characteristic
tolerance that resets after roughly two weeks of abstinence.

> [!info] Discovery
> Albert Hofmann at Sandoz Laboratories in Basel first synthesized LSD in
> November 1938, while researching ergot-derived lysergic acid compounds for
> analeptic use. On 16 April 1943 he absorbed a trace amount through his skin
> during resynthesis, and on 19 April 1943 he deliberately ingested 250 µg and
> rode home on a bicycle — the ride commemorated every 19 April as Bicycle Day.
> The compound was distributed to researchers as D-lysergic acid diethylamide
> (LSD-25), and the acronym became the name.

## Mechanism

LSD is a **partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors**, with lower affinity
at 5-HT1A, 5-HT2C, 5-HT6, and 5-HT7, and some dopamine D2 activity. Its
hallucinogenic and perceptual effects are attributed principally to 5-HT2A
signalling at layer V pyramidal neurons of the visual cortex, where the pathway
involves cortical glutamate, dopamine, and the thalamocortical system.

The structural similarity between LSD, mescaline-type phenethylamines, and
[[Adrenochrome]] — an indole-containing oxidation product of
[[Epinephrine]] — is the entire basis of the [[Adrenochrome Hypothesis]], which
proposed that abnormal catecholamine oxidation generates an endogenous
psychotomimetic accounting for [[Schizophrenia]]. The link is structural, not
mechanistic: adrenochrome's psychotomimetic activity in humans is unproven, and
the hypothesis remains rejected by mainstream psychiatry while persisting as a
historical and orthomolecular research thread.

> [!warning] The "mind-expanding" era is not the current evidence base
> Much of the vault's LSD content derives from 1950s–60s self-experimentation by
> [[Humphry Osmond]] and [[Abram Hoffer]], conducted under the ethical norms of
> the period but not by modern standards. Those single-dose LSD-plus-psychotherapy
> studies in [[Alcoholism]] reported large relapse-reduction effects that have not
> been reproduced in contemporary controlled trials. Contemporary psychedelic
> research has moved overwhelmingly to psilocybin and MDMA, not LSD — partly for
> psychological-set reasons and partly because LSD's duration and anxiety
> liability make it less clinically tractable.

## Therapeutic History

LSD's clinical career ran roughly 1950–1970:

- **Psychosis modelling.** LSD administered to healthy volunteers was used to
  generate states phenomenologically comparable to acute
  [[Schizophrenia]], in the belief that a shared mechanism existed. The
  comparison was phenomenological rather than mechanistic, and it did not
  survive as an explanatory model.
- **Alcohol use disorder.** Osmond and Hoffer at Saskatchewan Hospital ran
  single- to two-dose LSD sessions with psychotherapy, reporting durable
  abstinence. Results were uncontrolled and expectation effects cannot be
  excluded.
- **Psychotherapy adjunct.** LSD was trialled as a facilitator for psychodynamic
  work by Humphry Osmond, Aldous Huxley, and others through the 1960s.
- **Prohibition.** Recreational enthusiasm in the 1960s, association with
  destabilization during the Vietnam era, and growing concern about
  chromosome damage and teratogenicity in rodents drove prohibition. Research
  collapsed and has only recently resumed at scale.

> [!info] Receptor pharmacology as the modern model
> Psychedelics act through 5-HT2A at doses that do not produce the substantial
> 5-HT2A occupancy of the older antipsychotics they were once assumed to mimic.
> Consequently the older literature's attribution of antipsychotic and
  LSD-like effects to the same receptor is now considered oversimplified: the
  relevant distinction is intrinsic efficacy and the global neuronal context,
  not receptor occupancy.

## Mechanisms of Action (proposed)

Observed effects include altered sensory processing and perceptual
amplification, synesthesia, ego dissolution, memory integration, and a
distant/"objective" quality to ordinary objects. The functional imaging
literature points to a shift from the default mode network toward
transient, broad-band connectivity — decreased modularity and increased
integration — rather than to any single hyperactive region. The integrative
framing matters because it explains both the characteristic benefits and the
characteristic liability: the same global connectivity change that enables
insight enables anxiety and destabilization in predisposed individuals.

## Connections
- [[Adrenochrome]] — the indole-structure similarity between LSD and this
  oxidation product of epinephrine is what motivated the adrenochrome model of
  psychosis, alongside mescaline. The comparison is structural analogy only.
- [[Adrenochrome Hypothesis]] — LSD and mescaline were the experimental
  tripwires for this hypothesis: their phenomenology was used to argue that
  schizophrenia was a chemically generated state, with adrenochrome as the
  endogenous agent. Modern psychiatry does not accept the model.
- [[Humphry Osmond]] — Osmond self-administered LSD, worked with Hoffer on the
  alcoholism trials, and coined the term "psychedelic". The Saskatchewan
  single-dose studies are the best-known origin point of psychedelic-assisted
  therapy.
- [[Abram Hoffer]] — Hoffer and Osmond jointly developed the adrenochrome
  hypothesis and co-ran the LSD-assisted alcoholism studies. For Hoffer, the
  LSD work and the adrenochrome work were one programme, not two.
- [[Schizophrenia]] — the disease LSD was repeatedly used to model by
  phenomenology, and the disease the adrenochrome hypothesis was built to
  explain. That framing is historically central and scientifically unresolved.
- [[Serotonin]] — 5-HT2A agonism is LSD's primary mechanism; serotonergic
  signalling in the visual cortex and thalamocortical loops accounts for the
  perceptual phenomenology.
- [[Epinephrine]] — its oxidation product, adrenochrome, is the compound whose
  proposed endogenous psychotomimetic properties motivated the whole
  research programme.
