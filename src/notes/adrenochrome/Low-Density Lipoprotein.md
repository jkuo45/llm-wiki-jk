# Low-Density Lipoprotein

**Low-density lipoprotein (LDL)** is the principal cholesterol-transporting lipoprotein, carrying a single ApoB-100 protein and a core of cholesteryl esters and triglycerides. Elevated plasma LDL is the cardinal risk factor for [[Atherosclerosis]], but the unmodified particle is relatively inert; pathogenicity emerges once LDL is retained in the arterial intima and chemically modified.

Two convergent modifications dominate: **oxidation** (by myeloperoxidase, lipoxygenases, and [[Superoxide anion]]) and **glycation** (glucose/dicarbonyl attack producing [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]] on ApoB-100). Glycated-oxidized LDL is avidly taken up by macrophage scavenger receptors, seeding [[Foam Cells]] and fatty streaks. AGEs on LDL also engage [[RAGE]], amplifying [[Inflammation]] and [[Oxidative Stress]] within the plaque.

> [!tip] Longevity lever
> Lowering LDL (statins, diet) and limiting dietary AGEs both reduce atherogenic modification, a core cardiovascular-aging intervention. Sirtuin and [[GlyNAC]] pathways further protect LDL from oxidative modification.

Small, dense LDL subspecies are especially prone to glycation and oxidation, explaining why particle number and quality matter beyond total cholesterol.
