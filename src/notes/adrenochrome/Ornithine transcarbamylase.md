# Ornithine transcarbamylase

Ornithine transcarbamylase (OTC) is a nuclear-encoded, mitochondrial matrix enzyme that catalyzes the second step of the urea cycle, condensing carbamoyl phosphate with ornithine to form citrulline and releasing inorganic phosphate. It is the only urea-cycle enzyme located in the mitochondrion, and its deficiency is the most common inborn error of the urea cycle, causing hyperammonemia. Because OTC must be imported, folded, and oligomerized into its trimeric active form within the matrix, it is a useful model for studying protein-folding stress in that compartment.

When newly imported OTC precursors fail to fold correctly, exposed hydrophobic regions and aggregation-prone intermediates activate the [[Mitochondrial Unfolded Protein Response|mitochondrial unfolded protein response (UPRmt)]]. This retrograde signaling program shifts transcription toward mitochondrial chaperones (e.g., HSP60/HSP10), matrix proteases, and antioxidant defenses, while transiently slowing general protein translation to relieve folding load. Experimental expression of aggregation-prone OTC mutants in *C. elegans* and mammalian cells is a classic trigger used to map UPRmt circuitry, including the [[HAF-1]] peptide exporter and the transcription factors that induce protective genes.

The link to [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Adrenochrome]] biology is through the broader theme of mitochondrial proteostasis: a robust UPRmt blunts mitochondrial ROS production, supporting the [[Mitohormesis]] buffering systems that limit catecholamine autoxidation. Conversely, chronic matrix protein-folding overload — as might arise from accumulated damage in aging — can exhaust the response and tip the redox balance toward oxidation of [[Epinephrine]] to adrenochrome and other aminochromes.

> [!warning] Clinical relevance
> OTC deficiency illustrates how a single mitochondrial folding defect propagates to systemic ammonia toxicity, underscoring why matrix proteostasis surveillance matters.
