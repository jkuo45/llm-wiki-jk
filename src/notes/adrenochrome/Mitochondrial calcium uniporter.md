# Mitochondrial calcium uniporter

The **mitochondrial calcium uniporter (MCU)** is a highly selective, pore-forming ion channel of the inner mitochondrial membrane that mediates the rapid uptake of cytosolic Ca²⁺ into the matrix. It is the central pore subunit of the larger uniporter holocomplex, which also includes the regulatory subunits MCUb, EMRE (essential MCU regulator), and the Ca²⁺-sensing gatekeepers MICU1 and MICU2. Under basal cytosolic Ca²⁺, MICU1/2 keep the pore closed; when local Ca²⁺ rises at endoplasmic-reticulum–mitochondria contact sites, they relieve inhibition, allowing cooperative, high-capacity Ca²⁺ influx.

Matrix Ca²⁺ is a potent allosteric activator of three NADH-generating dehydrogenases — pyruvate dehydrogenase, isocitrate dehydrogenase, and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase — thereby coupling excitatory or hormonal signals to ATP production. However, sustained Ca²⁺ overload depolarizes the inner membrane, elevates [[Mitochondria]]l reactive oxygen species, and can open the mitochondrial permeability transition pore, committing the cell to necrosis or apoptosis.

> [!info] Redox intersection
> The MCU and its regulators are themselves redox-sensitive. Oxidant stress promotes S-glutathionylation and altered gating, embedding the uniporter within the same [[Oxidative Stress]] circuits that govern [[Mitohormesis]] and adrenochrome-linked redox cycling.

In longevity contexts, calibrated mitochondrial Ca²⁺ and ROS signaling participates in the retrograde and unfolded-protein responses that underlie mitohormetic conditioning. [[Hemoglobin]] is included here only to flag the distinct, erythrocyte-localized Ca²⁺ handling that does not involve MCU.
