# Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptor

Retinoic-acid-inducible protein I-like receptors (RLRs) are a family of cytosolic pattern-recognition receptors — principally RIG-I (DDX58), MDA5 (IFIH1), and LGP2 (DHX58) — that survey the cytoplasm for non-self RNA. RIG-I and MDA5 contain helicase domains that bind viral RNA features (5′-triphosphate termini, double-stranded regions, or panhandle structures) and a C-terminal repressor domain that, upon RNA engagement, undergoes a conformational change exposing the N-terminal caspase-recruitment (CARD) domains.

Signaling proceeds when the RLR CARDs interact with the mitochondrial outer-membrane adaptor [[MAVS]] (also called VISA/IPS-1), nucleating a prion-like MAVS filament that recruits [[TRAF6]] and [[TRAF2]] as well as TBK1/IKKε and the IKK complex. This bifurcates into IRF3/IRF7 activation, driving type-I interferon production, and NF-κB activation, driving [[Inflammation|pro-inflammatory cytokines]]. MAVS therefore places RLR signaling at the mitochondrion, the same organelle central to redox and [[Oxidative Stress]] homeostasis.

The connection to adrenochrome/redox biology is that viral infection and the ensuing interferon/cytokine storm amplify oxidative burden: activated immune cells generate ROS, [[Nitric Oxide]] and peroxynitrite, and systemic inflammation accelerates the autoxidation of catecholamines such as [[Epinephrine]] to [[Adrenochrome]]. MAVS-localized signaling also intersects with mitophagy and mitochondrial dynamics, linking innate immunity to the [[Mitohormesis]] and quality-control programs that defend against aminochrome accumulation. RLR/MAVS thus couple pathogen sensing to the broader redox–proteostasis network relevant to aging.

> [!info] MAVS localization
> Anchoring MAVS to mitochondria integrates antiviral signaling with cellular energy and redox state.
