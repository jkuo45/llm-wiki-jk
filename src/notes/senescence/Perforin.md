# Perforin

**Perforin** (encoded by *PRF1*) is a calcium-dependent, pore-forming glycoprotein stored in the cytotoxic granules of [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]] and [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]]. Upon immunological-synapse formation it oligomerizes in the target-cell membrane to form pores that deliver [[Granzyme B|granzymes]] into the cytosol, initiating apoptosis. Perforin deficiency causes severe familial haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, underscoring its non-redundant role in cytotoxic lymphocyte function.

## Loss in senescent CD8+ T cells

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> High-senescence-score CD8+ T cells from UC patients show lower expression of the canonical cytotoxic molecules GZMB and PRF1 alongside a prominent [[SASP]]. FK866-treated CD8+ T cells show reduced perforin production (flow cytometry) and impaired proliferative capacity, while IFN-γ and TNF-α secretion is enhanced. In OT-1 TCR-transgenic CD8+ T cells, FK866 impairs perforin production and proliferation and blunts IFN-γ secretion upon cognate OVA-peptide stimulation.

Reduced perforin is thus part of a functional decoupling in [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|senescent CD8+ T cells]]: cytotoxic effector capacity falls while an antigen-independent pro-inflammatory [[SASP]] rises. The retained inflammatory output, rather than cytotoxic clearance, is what amplifies mucosal injury in [[Ulcerative Colitis]].
