# Viral Replication

**Viral Replication** is the multistep process by which viruses multiply inside host cells, comprising attachment, entry, genome replication, protein synthesis, assembly, and release. It is tightly opposed by the host [[Innate Immune System]], whose antiviral effector programs restrict viral production.

## Innate Antiviral Control and NAD+ Metabolism

As the innate immune response activates against infection, the activation of [[PARP]]s increases due to extensive [[DNA Damage]] and IFN-induced MARylation (mono-ADP-ribosylation) of target viral proteins. This [[Type I Interferon]]-linked PARP response is required for inhibition of viral replication. The antiviral effect, however, is reversed by the ADP-ribosylhydrolase macrodomain of the viral nonstructural protein nsp3, whose de-MARylation activity is required for [[SARS-CoV-2]] virulence.

> [!info] NAD+ Metabolome as Antiviral Battleground
> Key events in the innate immune response to viral infection occur within the infected cell's NAD+ metabolome. Because PARP activity consumes NAD+, boosting NAD+ content through nicotinamide and NR kinase pathways may restore antiviral PARP functions and support control of viral replication.

## Mechanism Summary

- IFN-induced PARP activation → MARylation of viral proteins → inhibition of viral replication.
- Viral nsp3 macrodomain removes MAR groups → reversal of inhibition → enhanced virulence.
- Sustained PARP activity depletes NAD+, coupling antiviral defense to cellular NAD+ metabolism.

## Therapeutic Implications

[[NMN]] and [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] are proposed to replenish NAD+ and restore antiviral PARP functions, supporting innate immune defense during viral infection. Understanding PARP–macrodomain antagonism also informs antiviral drug design targeting viral de-MARylation activity.
