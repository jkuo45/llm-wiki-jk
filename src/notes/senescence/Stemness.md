# Stemness

**Stemness** describes the capacity of cells for self-renewal and differentiation potential that defines [[Stem Cells]]. It is governed by an interconnected transcription-factor network that maintains pluripotency and dedifferentiation competence, and its modulation underlies both regenerative plasticity and pathological cancer stemness.

## IL-6/JAK/STAT3 and Reprogramming

[[IL-6]] is a central driver of in vivo reprogramming toward pluripotency and stemness via [[JAK-STAT Signaling]] and [[STAT3]]. STAT3 transcriptionally sustains the core pluripotency factors [[Oct4]], [[Nanog]], and [[Sox2]], promoting a dedifferentiated, stem-like state in recipient cells. This underlies both wound-healing plasticity and, pathologically, [[Cancer Stem Cells|Cancer Stem Cell]] formation.

> [!important] SASP as a Fate-Shaping Signal
> Because IL-6 is a major [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] component, the senescent secretome can actively reprogram neighboring cells toward stemness — a dual-edged consequence linking senescence to tissue repair and cancer risk.

## Role in Senescence & Tissue Context

Senescent cells, through SASP-mediated IL-6/JAK/STAT3 signaling, can induce stem-like properties in the tumor microenvironment, contributing to cancer stemness and therapy resistance. Conversely, the same signaling supports regenerative stemness during wound healing. Blocking IL-6/JAK/STAT3 signaling can interrupt this reprogramming, highlighting a targetable axis connecting senescence, inflammation, and cell-fate control.

## Clinical Relevance

Manipulating stemness is central to [[Cellular Reprogramming]] and [[Partial Reprogramming]] strategies for regenerative medicine, while restraining cancer stemness is a therapeutic goal in oncology. The IL-6–STAT3–stemness axis is a validated point of pharmacological intervention.
