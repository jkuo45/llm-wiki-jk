# Dextran Sulfate Sodium

**Dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)** is a synthetic sulfated polysaccharide of dextran. Administered orally in drinking water, it is directly toxic to colonic epithelial cells and disrupts the [[Intestinal Barrier|intestinal barrier]], permitting luminal microbes and antigens to trigger mucosal inflammation. Because it is simple, reproducible and dose-titratable, DSS colitis is one of the most widely used murine models of [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]], particularly [[Ulcerative Colitis]]. Acute protocols cause epithelial injury and acute inflammation; cyclical administration produces chronic colitis with crypt architectural distortion and ulceration.

## Use in the Ye 2026 study

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> A chronic colitis model was induced by cyclical 3% DSS in drinking water (7 days DSS followed by 14 days distilled water, repeated twice, then a final 7 days of DSS). Colitic mice show weight loss, colon shortening and severe mucosal ulceration with crypt distortion, alongside increased SA-β-gal+ senescent T cells in the colon and, less prominently, in spleen and mesenteric lymph nodes.

The model underpins three findings: [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic therapy]] ([[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]]) attenuates weight loss, disease activity index and colon weight; [[Adoptive Transfer|adoptive transfer]] of [[FK866]]-induced [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|senescent CD8+ T cells]] accelerates disease; and NAD+ precursor supplementation had previously been reported to alleviate DSS-induced colitis. The authors note the model mimics key but not all features of human UC.
