# Immunosenescence
Immunosenescence refers to the gradual deterioration of the immune system associated with aging, leading to increased susceptibility to infections, autoimmune diseases, and cancer.
**Immunosenescence** (or [[Immune Aging]]) is the systemic deterioration of immune function that accompanies [[Aging]].

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> Immunosenescence is not solely driven by chronological aging: metabolic dysregulation, oxidative stress and genomic instability can precipitate premature immunosenescence. In [[Ulcerative Colitis]], mucosal [[NAD+ Metabolic Reprogramming]] drives a pathogenic population of [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells]] whose [[SASP]] amplifies chronic mucosal inflammation, and the NAD+–senescence signature predicts primary non-response to biologic therapy.

## Hallmarks
- Impaired immune reactions to new antigens.
- Perturbed memory responses, especially in [[T Cell]] immunity.
- Accumulation of memory T cell subsets with [[Senescence]]-like features.
- Accompanied by [[Inflammaging]], a chronic state of innate immune activation.
- Premature, non-chronological accumulation of senescent T cells in chronic inflammatory disease ([[Ulcerative Colitis]]; Ye et al., 2026).
## Reversal
- Clinical implementation of [[Autophagy Inducer|autophagy inducers]] (e.g., [[Rapamycin]], [[Metformin]], [[Spermidine]]) provides potential to partially reverse systemic immunosenescence.
- A T cell–bone marrow axis of immunosenescence drives hematopoietic aging: [[Cytotoxic CD4+ T Cells]] with mitochondrial stress accumulate in the [[Bone Marrow]] and impose myeloid skewing of [[Hematopoiesis]] through [[CCL5]]–[[CCR5]] signaling; pharmacological blockade with [[Maraviroc]] normalizes myelopoiesis and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio in aged mice (Gabandé-Rodríguez et al., 2026, *Nat Aging*).
