# Lymphopenia

**Lymphopenia** is an abnormally low blood count of [[Lymphocyte]]s, and a characteristic feature of severe [[SARS-CoV-2]] infection. It is part of a maladaptive immune response in which exaggerated [[Pro-inflammatory Cytokines|proinflammatory cytokine]] production drives a pulmonary cytokine storm while circulating CD4+ and CD8+ [[T Cell]] numbers decline dramatically.

## Pathophysiology

During the adaptive immune response to SARS-CoV-2, [[CD38]] is overexpressed in both CD4+ and CD8+ lymphocytes. CD38 is an NADase that consumes [[NAD+]], and its upregulation further exaggerates NAD+ depletion in activated lymphocytes. Drastic NAD+ loss impairs [[Sirtuins]] function and drives increased production and release of proinflammatory cytokines, [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], and macrophage infiltration, perpetuating both lymphopenia and hyperinflammation.

## Mechanism Summary

- Viral-induced immune activation → CD38 upregulation → accelerated NAD+ consumption → sirtuin dysfunction → proinflammatory cytokine and ROS production.
- Drastic NAD+ depletion impairs immune cell energy metabolism and exacerbates T-cell attrition.

## Therapeutic Implications

Replenishing body NAD+ content could restore energy levels and impaired sirtuin function, potentially rebalancing the maladaptive immune response. [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] and [[NMN]] are proposed as supportive strategies in infections characterized by NAD+ depletion and lymphopenia. Lymphopenia also generalizes to age-related immune decline: [[Immunosenescence]] and reduced adaptive immunity predispose older adults to severe outcomes, making lymphopenia a bridge between infection biology and immune aging.
