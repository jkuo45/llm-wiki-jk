# Adaptor Protein

An **adaptor protein** is a non-enzymatic scaffold that couples receptor engagement to downstream kinase cascades by assembling multiprotein signaling complexes. The canonical example in innate immunity is **[[MyD88]]**, a cytoplasmic adaptor that is recruited to [[Toll-like Receptor]] (TLR) and [[IL-1 Receptor]] TIR domains upon ligand binding, where it nucleates the helical [[Myddosome]] complex.

## Mechanism

MyD88 contains an N-terminal death domain and a C-terminal [[TIR domain]]. Homotypic TIR–TIR interactions recruit MyD88 to activated receptors, while death-domain interactions recruit [[IRAK4]] and [[IRAK1]]. The resulting oligomeric scaffold activates TAK1 and the IKK complex, culminating in [[NF-κB]] nuclear translocation and pro-inflammatory transcription. Because it lacks enzymatic activity, MyD88's function is entirely architectural: it brings kinases into proximity for trans-autophosphorylation and subsequent [[TRAF6]]-mediated signaling.

## Role in Senescence

In cellular senescence, surface-bound [[IL-1α]] engages IL-1 receptors and recruits MyD88, sustaining the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] through constitutive NF-κB activation. MyD88 therefore sits at the interface of [[Innate Immune System]] signaling and senescent-cell inflammatory output, and its loss blunts SASP amplification.

## Clinical Relevance

MyD88 is required for protective immunity to many pathogens; its deficiency predisposes to pyogenic infections, while activating mutations (e.g., MYD88 L265P) drive constitutive NF-κB signaling in B-cell malignancies. Adaptor proteins such as MyD88 are validated therapeutic targets for autoinflammatory and inflammatory conditions.
