# Lamins

**Lamins** are type V intermediate-filament proteins that assemble into the meshwork of the nuclear lamina underlying the inner [[Nuclear Envelope]]. They provide structural support to the [[Nucleus]], anchor chromatin, and organize nuclear pore complexes. Lamins are classified as A-type ([[Lamin A]] and Lamin C, encoded by the [[LMNA Gene]]) and B-type ([[Lamin B1]], Lamin B2).

## Structure & Function

Each lamin has an α-helical central rod flanked by globular head and tail domains, forming coiled-coil dimers that polymerize into higher-order filaments. Beyond mechanics, lamins regulate gene expression by tethering heterochromatin, influencing [[Senescence|cellular senescence]] and cell fate decisions. Reduced lamin B1 expression is a hallmark of replicative senescence.

## Role in Apoptosis

Lamins are major substrates of executioner caspases. [[Caspase-3]] and other [[Executioner Caspase]]s cleave lamins at conserved sites, depolymerizing the lamina and driving the nuclear collapse and chromatin condensation that characterize [[Apoptosis]]. The directionality is Caspase-3 acting as the operative agent upon Lamins rather than the reverse; this direct molecular event is a precise, often druggable point of control that can decisively shift cell fate.

## Pathology & Aging

Mutations in [[LMNA Gene]] cause laminopathies including [[Hutchinson-Gilford Progeria Syndrome]], where production of the truncated [[Progerin]] protein disrupts the lamina and accelerates aging phenotypes. Because lamins connect nuclear mechanics to senescence, chromatin organization, and apoptosis, they are central nodes linking aging, cell death, and cellular senescence.
