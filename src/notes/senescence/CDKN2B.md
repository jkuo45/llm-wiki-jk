# CDKN2B (p15^INK4b^)

**CDKN2B** encodes **p15^INK4b^** (cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor 2B), a member of the INK4 family of CDK inhibitors that specifically binds and inhibits [[CDK4]] and [[CDK6]], preventing their association with D-type cyclins. This maintains [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] in its hypophosphorylated, growth-suppressive state, enforcing G1 cell cycle arrest. CDKN2B is a key effector of [[TGFβ]]-mediated growth arrest and plays a critical role in [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]].

## Mechanism of Action

- **CDK4/6 inhibition**: p15^INK4b^ binds CDK4 and CDK6, preventing cyclin D–CDK complex formation and Rb phosphorylation.
- **G1 arrest**: By maintaining hypophosphorylated Rb, p15^INK4b^ sequesters E2F transcription factors, silencing genes required for S-phase entry.
- **TGF-β target**: CDKN2B is a direct transcriptional target of [[TGFβ]] signaling via [[SMAD3]]/[[SMAD4]] complexes binding to SMAD-binding elements in the CDKN2B promoter.
- **Epigenetic regulation**: The CDKN2B locus is subject to [[DNA Methylation]] and [[Polycomb Group Proteins|PRC2]]-mediated H3K27me3 silencing; its derepression is a hallmark of senescence.

> [!info] p15^INK4b^ in the INK4 Family
> The INK4 family comprises four members: [[p16INK4A|p16^INK4a^]] (CDKN2A), p15^INK4b^ (CDKN2B), p18^INK4c^ (CDKN2C), and p19^INK4d^ (CDKN2D). All specifically inhibit CDK4/6 but have distinct expression patterns and regulatory mechanisms. p15^INK4b^ is the primary CDK inhibitor induced by TGF-β, while p16^INK4a^ is the primary senescence-associated CDK inhibitor induced by oncogenic stress.

## Role in Senescence

> [!important] Source: Acosta et al., 2013 (PMID 23636316)
> TGF-β family ligands (TGF-β1, activin A, BMP2, GDF15) are major mediators of [[Paracrine Senescence]]. They signal through TGFBR1/ALK4/ALK5/ALK7 and [[SMAD2]]/[[SMAD3]] to induce [[CDKN2B|p15^INK4b]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]] in receiving cells. TGFBR1 inhibitors prevented SMAD2/3 phosphorylation, blunted paracrine arrest, and correlated with impaired p15^INK4b and p21^CIP1 induction. In a PanIN mouse model, conditional deletion of TGF-βR1 attenuated OIS — confirming that TGF-β → p15^INK4b/p21^CIP1 signaling is essential for senescence-mediated tumour suppression.

- p15^INK4b^ is induced by TGF-β during paracrine senescence, acting as a key effector of the growth arrest signal.
- It cooperates with [[p21 CIP1|p21^CIP1]] (induced by both TGF-β and [[p53]] pathways) to enforce the cell cycle arrest.
- CDKN2B loss or silencing (e.g., by promoter methylation) is a mechanism of senescence bypass in some cancers.
- CDKN2B is located on chromosome 9p21, adjacent to CDKN2A (p16^INK4a^/p14^ARF^), in a region frequently deleted or silenced in cancer.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- **Cancer**: CDKN2B is frequently silenced by promoter methylation or deleted in leukaemias, lymphomas, gliomas, and other cancers. Its re-expression by demethylating agents (e.g., decitabine, 5-azacytidine) can restore growth arrest.
- **Myeloid malignancies**: CDKN2B methylation is a prognostic marker in myelodysplastic syndromes and acute myeloid leukaemia.
- **Atherosclerosis**: CDKN2B variants at the 9p21 locus are the strongest common genetic risk factor for coronary artery disease, though the mechanism involves regulation of neighbouring genes (ANRIL, CDKN2A) rather than p15^INK4b^ directly.
- **TGF-β pathway disorders**: Loss of TGF-β → p15^INK4b^ signaling contributes to fibrosis, cancer progression, and impaired senescence-mediated tumour suppression.
