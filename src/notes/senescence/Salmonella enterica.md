# Salmonella enterica

**Salmonella enterica** is a Gram-negative, facultative anaerobic enteric bacterium responsible for a spectrum of human disease from gastroenteritis to typhoid fever. Beyond its clinical importance, *S. enterica* is historically significant in sirtuin biology: the first bacterial sirtuin was discovered in this organism.

## CobB and Prokaryotic Sirtuins

The founding prokaryotic sirtuin was found in *Salmonella enterica* and named **CobB** due to its involvement in cobalamin (vitamin B12) biosynthesis and propionic acid catabolism. CobB demonstrated that sirtuin-like [[NAD+]]-dependent deacetylases are not restricted to eukaryotes but are also present in prokaryotes, where they regulate various metabolic pathways. In bacteria, one or two sirtuins are typically found, and CobB serves as the archetype of prokaryotic sirtuin biology.

> [!info] Evolutionary Significance
> CobB's discovery showed that the NAD+-dependent deacetylation mechanism is evolutionarily ancient, predating the seven human [[Sirtuins]]. This supports a conserved link between NAD+ metabolism, acetylation, and metabolic regulation across domains of life.

## Mechanism

Like eukaryotic sirtuins, CobB couples deacetylation of acetyl-lysine substrates to NAD+ cleavage, producing deacetylated substrate, nicotinamide, and O-acetyl-ADP-ribose. By regulating acetylated metabolic enzymes, CobB modulates central carbon metabolism and connects nutrient availability to enzyme activity in bacteria.

## Clinical Relevance

Understanding bacterial sirtuins informs antibiotic development and metabolic engineering, while the sirtuin–NAD+ connection in *S. enterica* provides a model system for studying conserved NAD+-dependent regulation relevant to aging research.
