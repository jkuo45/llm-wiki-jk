# Myddosome

The **myddosome** is the helical oligomeric signaling platform assembled by the [[Adaptor Protein]] [[MyD88]] with the kinases [[IRAK4]] and [[IRAK1]]/IRAK2. It serves as the scaffolding core of [[IL-1 Receptor|IL-1R]]/[[Toll-like Receptor]] signaling, activating downstream kinases such as [[TAK1]] and transcription factors including [[NF-κB]].

## Assembly & Structure

Myddosome formation is driven by death-domain homotypic interactions. Upon ligand binding, MyD88 is recruited to the receptor TIR domain and nucleates a three-layered helical structure: a MyD88 death-domain layer, an IRAK4 layer, and an IRAK1/2 layer. This supramolecular architecture brings multiple kinase domains into proximity, enabling sequential trans-autophosphorylation and full activation of IRAK4 and IRAK1, which then recruits [[TRAF6]] to propagate NF-κB and MAPK signaling.

> [!info] Structural Rationale
> The helical stoichiometry concentrates signaling capacity: clustering of IRAK4 and IRAK1 within the myddosome ensures robust and thresholded activation, making the complex a validated drug target for inflammatory and autoimmune disease.

## Role in Senescence

In cellular senescence, myddosome formation underlies [[IL-1α]]-driven [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]. Surface-bound IL-1α on senescent cells engages IL-1 receptors, triggering myddosome assembly that translates receptor engagement into inflammatory transcription. Loss of myddosome components (e.g., IRAK1) suppresses SASP output, confirming the complex's functional necessity in senescent cell inflammatory signaling.

## Clinical Relevance

The myddosome is a defining structural feature of MyD88-dependent signaling and a validated drug target. Pharmacological disruption of myddosome assembly is explored to dampen excessive inflammation without globally ablating innate immunity.
