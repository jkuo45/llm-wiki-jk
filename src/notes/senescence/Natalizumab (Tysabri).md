# Natalizumab (Tysabri)

**Natalizumab** (Tysabri) is a humanized monoclonal antibody that blocks the interaction between [[VCAM-1]] and VLA-4 (α4β1 integrin) by antagonizing the alpha-4 [[Integrin]] subunit, reducing T-cell trafficking across the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] into the central nervous system.

## Mechanism of Action

Natalizumab binds the α4 subunit of VLA-4, preventing leukocyte adhesion to VCAM-1 on brain endothelial cells and thereby inhibiting immune-cell entry into the CNS. This dampens CNS inflammation and dramatically reduces relapses in [[Multiple Sclerosis]], validating the VCAM-1/VLA-4 pathway as a driver of neuroinflammation.

## Clinical Profile & Risks

The directionality of the drug is integrin blockade → reduced leukocyte entry. However, broad alpha-4 inhibition also affects gut homing of immune cells, and natalizumab is associated with progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy ([[PML]]), a potentially fatal demyelinating disease caused by JC virus reactivation. The risk is highest with prolonged use, prior immunosuppression, and JC virus seropositivity, and it limits the drug's use to patients managed under strict risk-stratification protocols.

> [!warning] Safety Caveat
> Although natalizumab is highly effective in MS, its PML risk requires careful JC virus antibody monitoring and limits duration of therapy — a central trade-off of broad adhesion-molecule blockade.

## Therapeutic Implications

Natalizumab's efficacy established anti-adhesion therapy as a cornerstone of MS treatment. Its safety profile illustrates the challenge of targeting immune-cell trafficking without compromising CNS and gut immune surveillance, informing development of more selective adhesion-molecule inhibitors.
