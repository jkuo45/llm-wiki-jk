# The Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype and Its Physiological and Pathological Implications

> [!abstract] Abstract
> [[Senescence]] is a state of terminal growth arrest associated with the upregulation of different cell cycle inhibitors, mainly [[p16INK4A|p16]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21]], structural and metabolic alterations, chronic [[DNA Damage Response|DNA damage responses]], and a hypersecretory state known as the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]]. The SASP is the major mediator of the paracrine effects of senescent cells in their tissue microenvironment and of various local and systemic biological functions.

## Introduction

[[Senescence]] is a state of stable terminal proliferation arrest. Because cells can transit to a senescent state as a consequence of various stressors, different types of senescence have been historically named after their inducer:

- **Replicative senescence** — cells passaged in culture until they reach irreversible proliferation arrest
- **[[Oncogene-Induced Senescence]] (OIS)** — cells overexpressing an oncogene, primarily [[RAS]]
- **Therapy-induced senescence (TIS)** — cells exposed to high but non-lethal doses of drugs or chemicals
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction-induced senescence (MiDAS)** — cells undergoing profound mitochondrial perturbations
- **Immunologically induced senescence** — cells exposed to excessive pro-inflammatory factors

All these states share common marks but are also associated with unique features that underline the potential for a large heterogeneity of senescence phenotypes in vivo.

A major common feature of senescent cells is the upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) inhibitors, primarily [[p21 CIP1|p21]]^CIP1^ (encoded by [[CDKN1A]]) and [[p16INK4A|p16]]^INK4a^ ([[CDKN2A]]). Persistent inhibition by these proteins of CDK–cyclin complexes results in hypophosphorylated [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]], p107 and p130 proteins and consequently in the downregulation of [[E2F]] target genes, which are essential for cell cycle progression.

> [!info] Senescence Markers
> Blocks of mitosis in senescent cells lead to multinucleation, irregular nuclear size and loss of expression of the nuclear lamina protein [[Lamin B1]]. Moreover, Rb mediates the formation and accumulation of [[Senescence-Associated Heterochromatin Foci|SAHF]], which are distinct chromatin structures that suppress transcription.

[[DNA Damage Response|DNA damage responses]] (DDR) that cannot be resolved are major inducers of senescence through serine phosphorylation and [[p53]] activation. Senescent cells exhibit priming to undergo apoptosis but survive because of the upregulation of various anti-apoptotic mechanisms. Pharmacological or genetic inhibition of anti-apoptotic proteins is sufficient to trigger apoptosis — a concept exploited for the development of [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic therapy]].

> [!tip] Key Concept
> The [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] includes various molecules — proteins, lipids, extracellular vesicles and non-coding nucleic acids — that participate in mediating various senescence-associated biological functions in health and disease.

## Composition of the SASP

### Proteins

As the first-discovered SASP components, proteins are by far the most characterized among all SASP factors. Major SASP proteins include interleukins and cytokines, chemokines, growth factors, and extracellular matrix proteases.

#### Interleukins and Cytokines

[[IL-1α]] and [[Interleukin 1β|IL-1β]] are among the most widely studied SASP factors. They can be secreted into the extracellular environment or intracellularly activate other key SASP interleukins such as [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]], which can reinforce proliferation arrest, induce [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]] and mediate immune responses. The cleavage of IL-1α by [[Caspase-5]] or [[Caspase-11]] is essential for the regulatory functions of the SASP.

> [!note] IL-1α Translocation
> Cleaved IL-1α can rapidly move from the cell surface to the nucleus, depending on copper and/or calcium homeostasis and on the calcium-binding protein [[S100A13]], and activates transcription of other pro-inflammatory SASP factors. Moreover, miR-146a and/or miR-146b act downstream of IL-1α to regulate IL-6 and IL-8 in senescent cells.

[[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]] mediate [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence|OIS]] in an autocrine manner, thereby reinforcing the tumour-suppressive function of senescence. In addition to their autocrine mechanisms of function, IL-6 and IL-8 are essential mediators of the paracrine effects of a variety of senescent cells in different pathophysiological contexts.

[[Interleukin 11|IL-11]] is a SASP factor associated with ageing, vitamin D deficiency and [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis|pulmonary fibrosis]] that seems to be activated as a consequence of downregulation of [[SIRT1]]. IL-11 signals to senescent fibroblasts and ultimately leads to the development of a pro-fibrotic phenotype.

[[Interleukin 33|IL-33]] is an IL-1β-dependent SASP interleukin, the secretion of which from senescent hepatic stellate cells promotes [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] development.

[[BAFF]] (TNFSF13B) is an immunity-modulating cytokine secreted by senescent fibroblasts and monocytic leukaemia cells to reinforce the upregulation of IL-6 and other SASP factors.

#### Chemokines

[[CCL2]] (MCP-1) is a SASP factor regulated by IL-1α and IL-1β and a strong chemoattractant for the migration and infiltration of monocytes and macrophages. CCL2 can be negatively regulated by [[Bmi-1]] through an IL-1α-mediated mechanism in senescent mesenchymal stromal cells.

[[CCL5]] contributes to non-small-cell lung cancer progression upon its secretion by TIS cancer cells and impairs preantral follicle development secreted by senescent theca and interstitial cells during natural ageing.

[C-X-C motif ligand]([[CXCL1]], [[CXCL2]], [[CXCL3]]) chemokines are often SASP factors. CXCL1, CXCL2 and CXCL3 secreted by senescent stellate cells in the liver bind to [[CXCR2]] and perform paracrine functions in the liver and pancreas.

> [!important] CXCR2 Feedback Loop
> Interestingly, [[CXCR2]] also mediates and reinforces senescence induction upstream of [[p38 MAPK]] and downstream of [[p53]] and the [[DNA Damage Response|DDR]].

[[CXCL5]], secreted by blastocysts originating from aged oocytes, also binds to CXCR2 and is detrimental to pre-implantation embryos used for in vitro fertilization in women of older reproductive age.

[[CXCL10]]: ROS-induced senescent hepatocytes secrete CXCL10 to enhance the activity of natural killer cells by binding to [[CXCR3]]. TIS endothelial cells secrete [[CXCL11]], which increases breast cancer aggressiveness.

#### Growth Factors

[[TGFβ]] (Transforming Growth Factor-beta) is a SASP factor that, in aged mice, is secreted by senescent cells in the fracture callus to reduce the number of mesenchymal progenitor cells and delay healing of tibial-bone fractures. TGFβ is also an inducer of [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]] that promotes SASP upregulation and immune-suppressive conditions in hypoxic tumour microenvironments.

[[GDF15]] (Growth–Differentiation Factor 15), which belongs to the TGFβ superfamily, contributes to colon carcinogenesis in the tumour microenvironment and promotes osteoarthritis from senescent chondrocytes, and its serum level is highly correlated with age and sex in individuals with age-related frailty.

[[HGF]] (Hepatocyte Growth Factor) is a SASP factor secreted by senescent fibroblasts that inhibits cell competition-mediated cancer suppression mechanisms.

Other fibroblast-associated SASP factors are proteases and matrix metalloproteinases ([[MMP1]], [[MMP-3]], [[MMP-9]], [[MMP-12]]), the latter being associated with detrimental effects on senescent dermal fibroblasts during and following tissue damage, particularly noted for their contribution to skin ageing.

### Bioactive Lipids

The secretion of leukotrienes from senescent lung fibroblasts was initially discovered in [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]]. SASP leukotrienes have various detrimental pro-inflammatory functions, for example, in osteoporosis during ageing. SASP leukotrienes can be pro-fibrotic depending on the expression of cysteinyl leukotriene receptors on the surface of responding cells.

In contrast to driving inflammation and fibrosis, other SASP lipids, primarily prostaglandins, have roles in the repair of tissue injury, such as in cutaneous wound healing, renal, hepatic and myocardial injury repair, and prevention of excessive lung fibrosis. Following their metabolic conversion to the oxylipin dihomo-15d-PGJ2, prostaglandins contribute to reinforcing senescence-associated proliferation arrest.

### Small Extracellular Vesicles

Small extracellular vesicles, especially exosomes (30–150 nm), have been recently identified in the secretome of senescent cells and shown to mediate some of the paracrine effects of senescent cells such as cancer promotion and [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]]. Exosome absolute levels increase in various types of senescent cells. The content of SASP exosome is quite heterogeneous, including proteins, lipids and noncoding nucleic acids, mainly [[miRNA]]. Moreover, exosomes from senescent cells promote cancer cell proliferation through [[Ephrin Type A Receptor 2|EPHA2]].

### Non-coding Nucleic Acids

The secretion of non-coding nucleic acids by senescent cells is largely dependent on exosomes, which include [[miRNA]] and cytoplasmic chromatin DNA fragments (CCFs). miRNAs were shown to mediate the communication between senescent fibroblasts and keratinocytes in human skin. Secreted CCFs elicit [[DNA Damage Response|DNA damage responses]] in recipient cells.

## Regulation of the SASP

The SASP was originally described as a consequence of a transcriptomic programme regulated by various components of the [[DNA Damage Response|DDR]], which upregulates the expression of pro-inflammatory proteins. Later, several additional DDR-independent pathways were shown to activate distinct categories of SASP proteins.

### The p53–p21 and p16–Rb Pathways

Stable proliferation arrest is a key characteristic of senescent cells, and the [[p53]]–[[p21 CIP1|p21]] and [[p16INK4A|p16]]–[[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] pathways are its major regulators. In addition to their role in growth arrest, these pathways are crucial for SASP regulation.

> [!note] p53 and SASP
> In most contexts, [[p53]] appears to have a role in restraining the production of pro-inflammatory SASP factors. For example, loss of p53 amplifies pro-inflammatory SASP factors in replicative senescence, TIS, and OIS fibroblasts and p53 activation in senescent cells by treatment with [[Nutlin 3a]] attenuates the secretory phenotype in TIS fibroblasts.

Similarly, [[p16INK4A|p16]] overexpression or long-term exposure to CDK4 or CDK6 inhibitors induced a DDR-free senescent state without proinflammatory SASP factors but with a unique p53-dependent SASP profile (p53-arm) that included secretory proteins encoded by direct p53 target genes (for example, [[IGFBP3]]) and several interferon pathway factors (for example, [[ISG15]]).

> [!important] p21 and Immuno-surveillance
> [[p21 CIP1|p21]] supports an early secretory phenotype that includes [[CXCL14]] and [[IGFBP3]], which puts stressed or senescent cells under immunosurveillance, and this process is mediated by [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] activation.

### The DDR and NF-κB

The transcription factor [[NF-κB]] is a master regulator of SASP interleukins and cytokines, chemokines, growth factors, and other factors. Notably, disruption of the NF-κB pathway allows cells to escape senescence-associated proliferation arrest, possibly as a consequence of suppressing [[IL-6]]-mediated and [[IL-8]]-mediated autocrine activities.

The DNA-break repair protein [[PARP1]] was initially shown to mediate the NF-κB-associated SASP; however, a later study demonstrated that the [[ATM]]–PARP1–IKK (also known as inhibitor of NF-κB) axis is acutely activated in advance of NF-κB signalling. [[IκBζ]], which is a selective co-activator of NF-κB for a subset of target genes, is a major regulator of SASP genes (for example, _IL6_ and _IL8_).

[[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]] is a transcription factor that cooperates with NF-κB to regulate the expression of [[CXCR2]] and pro-inflammatory SASP genes in OIS. [[Protein Kinase D|PKD]] is a major sensor of oxidative stress that interacts with NF-κB to modulate SASP gene expression, including IL-6 and IL-8.

Moreover, cell surface proteins, such as [[CD36]] (also known as platelet glycoprotein 4) and [[CD40L]], are also essential for the activation of NF-κB and its downstream SASP, which might be crucial for mediating external signals from senescence inducers. Finally, other DDR factors, including [[ATM]]–[[CHK2]], ATR and [[GATA4]], also promote pro-inflammatory SASP.

### The p38 MAPK and mTOR Pathways

In cancer, [[p38 MAPK]] signalling is constitutively activated by oncogenic RAS and its effector MEK mediates premature senescence through upregulation of p53 and [[p16INK4A|p16]]. p38 MAPK was the first protein shown to activate an NF-κB-mediated SASP independent of the DDR. In senescent fibroblasts residing in the tumour microenvironment, p38 MAPK stabilizes SASP transcripts, thereby favouring tumorigenesis.

Following chemotherapy, levels of the transcription factor ZSCAN4 are elevated in senescent stromal cells through [[ATM]] activation of [[TAK1]] (also known as MAP3K7), and a TAK1–ZSCAN4 complex has a key role in promoting the SASP by activating p38 MAPK and [[mTORC1|mTOR]] signalling.

> [!tip] mTOR and SASP
> [[mTORC1|mTOR]] promotes the production of SASP proteins through two mechanisms: it promotes translation of membrane-bound [[IL-1α]] and IL-1α-mediated NF-κB transcriptional activities; and it promotes the translation of [[MAPKAPK2|MK2]], which can phosphorylate and inhibit the mRNA decay activator protein [[ZFP36L1]], thereby stabilizing transcripts of many SASP factors, including IL-1α. Accordingly, the mTOR inhibitor [[Rapamycin]] was shown to reduce the pro-inflammatory SASP.

In physiological hypoxia conditions, [[AMPK]] inhibits mTOR to suppress the secretion of detrimental SASP factors. By contrast, the [[JAK]]–[[STAT3]] pathway, which interacts with both p38 MAPK and mTOR pathways, was shown to promote a pro-inflammatory SASP and age-related frailty.

### Cytosolic DNA–cGAS–STING Pathway

The [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] complex promotes the SASP through interferon signalling. Downregulation of the DNA-degrading enzyme [[DNASE2]] and three-prime repair exonuclease 1 ([[TREX1]]) in senescent cells results in reduced degradation and thus in accumulation of cytoplasmic DNA, triggering cGAS–STING.

> [!important] cGAS–STING Activation
> During this process, both [[TOP1]] cleavage complexes (TOP1cc) and [[G3BP1]] have crucial roles in assisting cGAS recognition of and binding to CCFs, which is essential for downstream SASP regulation. Inhibition of cGAS or STING greatly suppresses the activation of NF-κB in TIS, indicating that NF-κB is part of the pathway.

[[TLR2]] is an innate immunity sensor that is upregulated in senescent cells and is essential for the activation of an immunity-regulating SASP during OIS through the cGAS–STING pathway. Levels of the retrotransposable element [[LINE-1]] are elevated during late senescence, and cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA triggers interferon signalling in long-term senescence cells, reinforcing the production of SASP factors.

> [!warning] Mitochondrial DNA Release
> In senescent cells with minority mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (miMOMP), the [[BAX]] and [[BAK]] macropores allowed the mitochondrial inner membrane to herniate into the cytosol to release mitochondrial DNA, leading to activation of cGAS–STING and SASP. Cytoplasmic DNA released from mitochondria and cGAS–STING were shown to be the drivers of ageing-associated and SASP-like low-grade inflammation.

### Pathways Dedicated to Distinct SASP Profiles

As discussed above, p53 reactivation or accumulation and long-term CDK4 and CDK6 inhibition by [[p16INK4A|p16]] overexpression or exposure to drugs can lead to a senescent state without a conventional NF-κB-driven pro-inflammatory SASP but with unique profiles that are directly dependent on p53 or p16. Similarly, MiDAS is associated with a distinct secretory phenotype that is dependent on p53.

Overexpression of the [[Notch]]1 intracellular domain (N1ICD; active NOTCH1) induces a unique SASP profile lacking the pro-inflammatory module, which is partly dependent on TGFβ signalling. In models of prostate-cancer senescence induced by loss of the phosphatase tumour suppressor PTEN, further deletion of TIMP1, which encodes metalloproteinase inhibitor 1, reprogrammes the SASP to have a pro-tumorigenic function, suggesting the existence of molecular switches that regulate different SASP modules.

### Role of Epigenetic Networks

A series of epigenetic changes have been implicated in SASP regulation:

- The RNA m^6^A-methyltransferase complex [[METTL3]]–[[METTL14]] promotes the expression of SASP factors by bringing enhancers in close proximity to promoters of SASP genes that contain NF-κB binding sites, a process that is independent of its m^6^A modification function.
- [[CEBPα|C/EBPα]] is a SASP regulator similar to C/EBPβ. C/EBPα binds to senescence-activated enhancers to promote the expression of SASP genes in replicative senescence fibroblasts.
- In OIS, global enhancer remodelling was identified, and [[BRD4]] was shown to be recruited to senescence-activated enhancers, contributing to the expression of SASP factors without interfering with growth arrest.

> [!note] Histone Modifications and SASP
> Post-translational modifications of histones are crucial for the establishment of the SASP. In senescent stromal cells, upregulation of the [[KDM4]] protein family reduced histone H3 Lys9 (H3K9) and H3K36 methylation and promoted the expression of SASP factors. [[EZH2]], the catalytic subunit of polycomb repressive complex 2, was shown to repress the expression of pro-inflammatory SASP factors, including [[CCL2]], CXCL9 and [[CXCL10]], in senescent tumour cells.

The deacetylase [[SIRT1]] was shown to reduce H3K9 and H4K16 acetylation in the promoter regions of _IL-6_ and _IL-8_. The histone variant macroH2A1 positively regulates the expression of SASP genes, but endoplasmic reticulum stress and high ROS levels induced by the SASP contribute to the removal of macroH2A1 in a negative feedback loop.

High-mobility group protein A (HMGA) and HMGB are chromatin-regulating factors. Downregulation of HMGB proteins is crucial for the establishment of a senescent state. [[HMGB1]] depletion or loss from the nucleus resulted in the upregulated expression of SASP genes. Conversely, HMGB2 was shown to protect SASP genes from the formation of SAHF, and its loss led to silencing of a subset of SASP genes. HMGA1 was upregulated in OIS and promoted the expression of [[NAMPT]], which enhanced [[NAD+]]-mediated AMPK inhibition, thereby suppressing p53 and leading to the activation of a p38 MAPK-associated and NF-κB-associated pro-inflammatory SASP.

### Additional Regulators

- **[[miRNA]]**: miR-34a contributes to the SASP (for example, by IL-6 secretion) in senescent vascular smooth muscle cells of aged aortas, and higher expression of miR-125a in lung tissues of individuals with [[Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease|COPD]] promotes high levels of SASP factors. By contrast, miR-15b inhibits a [[SIRT4]]-mediated SASP in radiation-induced or replicative senescence dermal fibroblasts.
- **lncRNAs**: The lncRNA _MIR31HG_ binds to Y-box-binding protein 1 and predisposes it to phosphorylation by ribosomal protein S6 kinases, which is crucial for inducing the translation of IL-1α and the downstream activation of SASP factors.
- **PDK4**: Promotes aerobic glycolysis and contributes to lactate production, which drives ROS-mediated SASP production.
- **Iron**: Iron reduction by the iron chelator deferiprone can suppress pro-inflammatory SASP factors such as [[CCL2]] and [[Interleukin 11|IL-11]].

## Temporal Dynamics of the SASP

The induction of different SASP components is a temporally dynamic and intricately regulated process. Whereas the [[p53]]–[[p21 CIP1|p21]] pathway is an early regulator of senescence, [[NF-κB]] and [[p16INK4A|p16]] are normally induced in later phases, and LINE1-driven interferon signalling is considered a mark of deep senescence.

> [!info] Notch-Mediated SASP Switch
> [[Notch]] signalling is crucial for the switch from an early (for example, [[TGFβ]]) to a late (for example, [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]]) SASP by suppressing [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]], which further interferes with [[IL-1α]] and NF-κB. A multi-omics study has shown that the transcription factor [[AP-1]] defines the temporal dynamics of the senescence-associated transcriptional network, including the SASP.

## Determinants of SASP Heterogeneity

### Senescence Inducers

The type of stimulus triggering senescence has a dominant role in determining SASP composition. OIS (caused by activation of [[RAS]], RAF or MYC) significantly increases SASP gene expression compared to other senescence inducers, sometimes even by thousands-fold increases at the mRNA level. In TIS, the type of therapy highly influences SASP composition — interventions leading to genotoxic stress promote a pro-inflammatory senescent state, whereas pharmacological CDK4 and CDK6 inhibitors do not trigger expression of NF-κB target genes.

### Cell Type

Transcriptomics data studies have shown that the SASP produced by dermal fibroblasts, melanocytes and keratinocytes is diverse despite cells originating from the same tissue. Fibroblasts are highly secretory cells by default, and their secretory machinery might support a higher SASP compared to other cell types. Less than 50% of SASP proteins were shared between fibroblasts and epithelial cells.

### The Tissue Environment

Environmental conditions, including different physiological oxygen levels, metabolic rates and nutrient availability, have a crucial role in the establishment and composition of the SASP in different tissues and tissue regions. Physiological hypoxia conditions (1% oxygen) or hypoxia-mimetic drugs were shown to reduce the expression of pro-inflammatory SASP factors. High glucose levels in culture media or blood induce senescence and SASP. In vivo, different compositions of dietary macronutrients significantly affected the level of SASP in mouse livers, with a high-fat content promoting higher expression of inflammatory factors.

## Biological Functions of SASP

### Paracrine Propagation of Senescence

In OIS cells, SASP components, including [[IL-1α]], [[TGFβ]], [[VEGF]], [[CCL2]] and CCL20, autocrinally reinforce senescence-associated growth arrest but can additionally induce [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine proliferation arrest]] by binding to their specific receptors on receiving cells. Interestingly, [[Notch]] signalling is essential for the establishment of paracrine senescence and weakens the SASP produced by secondary senescent cells, thereby preventing the surge of continuous senescence induction.

Exosomes were shown to induce paracrine senescence through [[IFITM3]], which is highly expressed in exosomes from the plasma of elderly individuals and is a potential ageing biomarker.

### Tissue Remodelling and Repair

Senescent cells contribute to different stages of development. Senescence markers, such as SA-β-gal, [[p53]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21]], are upregulated in developing forelimbs and neural tube, and the SASP factor [[TGFβ]] is a crucial mediator of this senescence-associated function.

> [!tip] Wound Healing
> Senescent cells and their SASP contribute to wound healing through modulation of both vascularization and matrix production. In response to a cutaneous wound, senescent fibroblasts and endothelial cells increased early, inducing myofibroblast differentiation through the secretion of [[PDGFAA]]. Removing senescent cells 24 h following injury decreased myofibroblast differentiation and delayed wound closure.

Regeneration of kidney and heart also benefits from cellular senescence. Removal of senescent cells within the first week of renal ischaemia in a mouse model inhibited the recovery of renal function, suggesting that cellular senescence has a regenerative role during the early stages of response to acute kidney injury. Similarly, the removal of senescent cells using a senolytic drug impeded neonatal heart regeneration and resulted in the formation of fibrotic scars.

Partial hepatectomy promotes senescence in hepatic stellate cells, and removal of these senescent cells disturbs liver regeneration owing, in part, to a lack of [[IL-6]]- and [[CXCL2]]-dependent stimulation of hepatocyte proliferation. A recent study of salamander limb regeneration demonstrated a detailed biological role of cellular senescence in tissue regeneration.

### Interaction with the Immune System

> [!important] SASP and Immunity
> SASP factors, such as [[IL-6]] and [[Interleukin 1β|IL-1β]], have pivotal roles in modulating the immune response. When [[TGFβ]] is present, the SASP factors IL-6 and IL-1β promote the differentiation of naive T cells to a [[TH17]] phenotype through the activation of the transcription factors [[STAT3]] or RORγt.

SASP-mediated recruitment of macrophages, natural killer cells, neutrophils and T cells is considered beneficial for limiting tumorigenesis and removing apoptotic cells and cell debris to initiate tissue remodelling. However, senescent cells can also directly affect immune cells and cause immune dysfunction. The SASP can also stimulate the generation of myeloid-derived immunity suppressor cells in bone marrow and spleen, which may suppress distinct components of innate and adaptive immunity.

Cellular senescence involves de-repression of [[LINE-1]] elements, which activate a type I interferon response that acts on innate immunity cells. This response contributes to [[Inflammaging]] and the maintenance of the SASP. The [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] pathway, which is essential for cellular senescence, also directly drives chronic inflammation and immune dysfunction during ageing.

### Impact on Ageing

Senescent cells gradually accumulate during ageing and contribute to ageing-related diseases through the SASP. Removal of naturally occurring senescent cells along with their SASP significantly improved the health span of aged animals. Interventions targeting senescent cells and the SASP also benefit the outcomes of age-related [[Osteoarthritis]] and [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular diseases]]. In clinical settings, a panel of circulating SASP proteins was shown to predict the adverse events of older individuals (60–90 years old). Moreover, the SASP profile in urinary extracellular vesicles can distinguish between older individuals with or without [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson disease]].

### Cancer and Cancer Therapies

> [!warning] Double-Edged Sword
> The SASP is a double-edged sword in cancer and cancer therapies, particularly with regards to immunosurveillance and immune regulation, as some SASP factors can trigger immune surveillance, whereas others trigger immune evasion.

Activation or restoration of [[p53]] induces an immunogenic SASP (including, for example, CSF1, [[CCL2]], IL-15, [[CXCL1]], [[CXCL14]] and [[IGFBP3]]), which contributes to macrophage-mediated or CD4^+^ T cell-mediated tumour regression in lymphoma, sarcoma and liver cancer mouse models. Moreover, combinatorial therapies using a MEK inhibitor with CDK4 and CDK6 inhibitors induce SASP in _Kras_-mutant lung and pancreatic cancer models, thereby facilitating elimination of cancer cells by natural killer cells and CD8^+^ T cells, respectively.

Conversely, NF-κB-associated SASP factors seem to be more pro-tumorigenic. Studies with OIS or TIS cellular senescence models showed that DDR-induced senescence contributes to tumour growth and invasion in breast cancer, thyroid cancer and glioblastoma models. NF-κB-associated [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]] secretion have been involved in almost every aspect of tumorigenesis, including proliferation, metastasis, angiogenesis, immune evasion and stemness. [[CCL2]] promotes immune evasion by attracting CCR2^+^ myeloid cells. CCL5 activates MYC–cyclin D and [[HGF]] cooperates with the MMPs to promote cancer cell proliferation. Together with IL-6 and [[CXCL5]], the SASP factor [[VEGF]] stimulates the formation of blood vessels in the cancer microenvironment. [[CXCL12]] and MMPs were demonstrated to cooperatively facilitate cancer cell metastasis.

## Biomarkers and Senomorphic Therapy

### Clinical Biomarkers

In the past few years, levels of some of the most common SASP factors have been used as surrogate markers for the burden of senescence in humans. The plasma level of SASP proteins, such as [[IL-6]], [[MMP-9]] and [[GM-CSF]], has been measured to evaluate the efficacy of [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic therapy]] ([[Dasatinib]] plus [[Quercetin]]) in individuals with diabetic kidney disease. Urine levels of IL-6, [[CCL2]], MMP7 and MMP8 have been used to assess the extent of negative correlation of the SASP with the geroprotector protein α-klotho in individuals with [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]] undergoing senolytic treatment.

> [!info] SASP Atlas
> The SASP Atlas is an unbiased and comprehensive database of soluble factors identified by proteomics, which proposes a set of proteins ([[GDF15]], stanniocalcin 1, serpin proteins and MMPs) for use in combination as a biomarker of senescence in human plasma. More recent studies in humans demonstrated that five SASP factors ([[GDF15]], RAGE, [[VEGF]]A, PARC (also known as [[CCL18]]) and [[MMP-2]]) are strongly associated with mortality and that the plasma level of a panel of SASP factors is predictive of COVID-19 severity.

### Pharmacological SASP Inhibition

> [!tip] Senomorphic vs Senolytic
> Senolytic therapies aim to selectively eliminate senescent cells. By contrast, [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic therapies]] focus on modulating senescence-associated properties without directly leading to cytotoxicity. In certain circumstances, senotherapies seem to provide a more beneficial outcome than senolytics, particularly in cases where senescent cells contribute to maintaining tissue structure.

Various drugs inhibit the SASP by targeting SASP-regulating pathways:

- **[[Glucocorticoids]]**: Block pro-inflammatory SASP by impairing IL-1α-mediated NF-κB activity
- **[[Metformin]]**: Inhibits NF-κB signalling in fibroblasts, macrophages, and senescent endothelial cells
- **Olive phenols**: Reduce cGAS–STING–NF-κB pro-inflammatory SASP
- **[[Nutlin 3a]]**: Suppresses pro-inflammatory SASP by stabilizing p53
- **p38 MAPK/MK2 inhibitors**: Inhibit SASP from cultured fibroblasts from older donors
- **[[Rapamycin]]**: Suppresses SASP by interfering with mTOR and p38 MAPK–IL-1α–NF-κB pathways
- **Hypoxia-mimetic compounds**: Block mTOR through AMPK activation, suppress pro-inflammatory SASP
- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Inhibits pro-inflammatory SASP cytokines
- **[[Apigenin]]**: Reduces breast cancer aggressiveness by inhibiting NF-κB-dependent SASP
- **Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs)**: Lamivudine (3TC) inhibits LINE1 propagation
- **[[Ruxolitinib]]**: JAK1/2 inhibitor; reduces SASP cytokines

### Dietary Interventions and Physical Exercise

> [!tip] Lifestyle Interventions
> Different lifestyle choices, behaviours and dietary habits affect accumulation of senescent cells and production of SASP factors. Mice fed a high-fat diet showed elevated levels of pro-inflammatory SASP factors. Conversely, dietary restriction through a 30% decrease in caloric intake can reduce DNA damage, NF-κB and SASP levels during renal ageing.

Exercise training was shown to be an effective approach to reducing senescence burden. A 12-week training programme improved the physical performance of older adults, which was correlated with reduced levels of senescent CD3^+^ T cells, and a specific SASP panel in the blood was used as a biomarker for predicting responders to exercise intervention.

## Conclusions and Perspectives

The SASP is heterogeneous and highly dynamic, and it mediates various beneficial and detrimental biological functions. However, mechanisms underlying SASP activation and composition during physiological and pathological conditions in vivo remain poorly understood. The continuous presence of pro-inflammatory SASP factors is considered detrimental for tissue homeostasis and is implicated in the development of various diseases but the same factors, when transiently expressed, might be important regulators of interaction with the immune system and for tissue remodelling and repair.

> [!quote] Key Insight
> Measuring the abundance of SASP products at individual time points might not be enough to discriminate between beneficial and detrimental senescent cells in vivo, making the use of the SASP as a biomarker of declining health a challenge.

An interesting approach to improving healthy longevity could be the use of lifestyle interventions that lead to SASP reduction in individuals predisposed to accumulation of senescent cells. The senomorphic effect of the intervention might eventually lead to reduction of the senescence burden by decreasing the likelihood of promoting [[Paracrine Senescence|paracrine senescence]].
