# LAG-3

**LAG-3** (lymphocyte-activating gene 3, CD223) is a type-I transmembrane inhibitory receptor of the immunoglobulin superfamily, structurally related to CD4. It is expressed on activated [[T Cell|T cells]], [[Regulatory T cells|Tregs]] and [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]], and binds MHC class II, FGL1 and other ligands to suppress TCR signalling and cytokine production. LAG-3 is a canonical marker of [[T Cell Exhaustion|exhausted T cells]] and a clinically validated checkpoint target (e.g. relatlimab, an anti-LAG-3 antibody used with anti-[[PD-1]] therapy).

## Negative marker for senescence vs exhaustion

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> The senescent state is phenotypically distinct from [[T Cell Exhaustion|T cell exhaustion]]: classical exhaustion checkpoint markers PDCD1 ([[PD-1]]), CTLA4 ([[CTLA-4]]), LAG3 and HAVCR2 show no differential expression between high- and low-senescence-score CD8+ T cells. FK866-treated cells also skew away from an effector state without upregulating the canonical exhaustion marker PD-1.

LAG-3 therefore functions as a *negative discriminator* in this study: the pathogenic [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|senescent CD8+ T cells]] of [[Ulcerative Colitis]] are defined by [[p16]], [[p21 CIP1|p21]], [[KLRG1]] and a [[SASP]], not by checkpoint upregulation. The distinction carries therapeutic implications — the authors argue for [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic clearance]] in chronic inflammatory disease rather than checkpoint blockade, which targets exhaustion in cancer.
