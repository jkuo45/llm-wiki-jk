# Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype

The **senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP)** is the major mediator of the paracrine effects of [[Senescence|senescent cells]] in their tissue microenvironment and of various local and systemic biological functions. It is a hypersecretory state characterized by the upregulation of hundreds of factors, including cytokines, chemokines, growth factors, extracellular matrix proteases, bioactive lipids, small extracellular vesicles, and non-coding nucleic acids.

The SASP was first described in 2001 by Krtolica et al., who proposed that senescent cells secrete factors that modulate biological activities both locally and systemically. By 2008, multiple laboratories independently defined the SASP (or "senescence-messaging secretome") primarily as a collection of pro-inflammatory and growth-stimulating proteins.

## Composition of the SASP

### Proteins

Proteins are the most characterized SASP components, comprising interleukins and cytokines, chemokines, growth factors, and extracellular matrix proteases.

#### Interleukins and Cytokines

- [[IL-1α]] and [[Interleukin 1β|IL-1β]]: Among the most widely studied SASP factors. They can be secreted into the extracellular environment or intracellularly activate other key SASP interleukins such as IL-6 and IL-8. The cleavage of IL-1α by [[Caspase-5]] or [[Caspase-11]] is essential for SASP regulatory functions. Cleaved IL-1α moves from the cell surface to the nucleus depending on copper/calcium homeostasis and the calcium-binding protein [[S100A13]], activating transcription of other pro-inflammatory SASP factors.
- [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]]: Mediate [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence]] in an autocrine manner, reinforcing tumor-suppressive function of senescence. Essential mediators of paracrine effects of senescent cells in different pathophysiological contexts.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Acid_ceramidase_modulates_the_lipid_profile_and_ex|Acid ceramidase modulates the lipid profile… (Soriano-Castell et al., 2026)]]
> Beyond the classic paracrine/autocrine roles, [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]] act as **ferroptotic instructive signals**: recombinant IL-6/IL-8 (2:1) or co-culture with [[Senescent Cells|senescent cells]] induces [[Acid ceramidase]] (ASAH1) up-regulation and significantly elevates [[Lipid Peroxidation|lipid peroxidation]] and [[Ferroptosis|ferroptotic cell death]] in neighboring proliferative cells. This reveals a SASP→ACase→pro-ferroptotic lipid profile axis that spreads both senescence *and* ferroptotic vulnerability through tissue.
- [[Interleukin 11|IL-11]]: SASP factor associated with ageing, vitamin D deficiency, and [[Pulmonary Fibrosis]]. Activated as a consequence of downregulation of [[SIRT1]]. IL-11 signals to senescent fibroblasts leading to pro-fibrotic phenotype.
- [[Interleukin 33|IL-33]]: IL-1β-dependent SASP interleukin; secretion from senescent hepatic stellate cells promotes [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] development.
- [[BAFF]] (TNFSF13B): Immunity-modulating cytokine secreted by senescent fibroblasts and monocytic leukaemia cells to reinforce upregulation of IL-6 and other SASP factors.

#### Chemokines

- [[CCL2]] (MCP-1): Regulated by IL-1α and IL-1β; strong chemoattractant for monocytes and macrophages. Negatively regulated by [[Bmi-1]] through IL-1α-mediated mechanism in senescent mesenchymal stromal cells.
- [[CCL5]]: Contributes to non-small-cell lung cancer progression upon secretion by therapy-induced senescent cancer cells; impairs preantral follicle development during natural ageing.
- [[CXCL1]], [[CXCL2]], [[CXCL3]]: Secreted by senescent stellate cells in the liver; bind to [[CXCR2]] and perform paracrine functions in the liver and pancreas.
- [[CXCL5]]: Secreted by blastocysts from aged oocytes; binds to CXCR2 and is detrimental to pre-implantation embryos used for in vitro fertilization in older women.
- [[CXCL10]]: Secreted by ROS-induced senescent hepatocytes; enhances natural killer cell activity by binding to [[CXCR3]].
- [[CXCL11]]: Secreted by therapy-induced senescent endothelial cells; increases breast cancer aggressiveness.
- [[CXCL12]] (SDF-1): A homeostatic ELR− chemokine and SASP factor that binds [[CXCR4]] and [[ACKR3]]. In vascular aging, *Cxcl12* [[MRNA|mRNA]] is the most highly upregulated SASP factor in senescent [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]] — it causally drives [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial dysfunction]] by suppressing [[Nitric Oxide|NO]], promoting mitochondrial [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], and inducing [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition|EndoMT]]. [[Fisetin]] normalizes CXCL12 to improve vascular function. Also a well-established pro-metastatic factor that cooperates with [[MMP|MMPs]] to guide cancer metastasis.
- [[CXCL14]]: In p21-driven SASP, promotes immunosurveillance by recruiting macrophages.

#### Growth Factors

- [[TGFβ]] (Transforming Growth Factor-beta): Secreted by senescent cells in aged fracture callus; reduces mesenchymal progenitor cells and delays bone healing. Inducer of paracrine senescence that promotes SASP upregulation and immune-suppressive conditions in hypoxic tumour microenvironments.
- [[GDF15]] (Growth-Differentiation Factor 15): Contributes to colon carcinogenesis; promotes osteoarthritis from senescent chondrocytes; serum level highly correlated with age and sex in individuals with age-related frailty.
- [[HGF]] (Hepatocyte Growth Factor): Secreted by senescent fibroblasts; inhibits cell competition-mediated cancer suppression mechanisms.
- [[VEGF]] (Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor): Stimulates angiogenesis in the cancer microenvironment.

#### Proteases

- [[MMP1]], [[MMP-3]], [[MMP-9]], [[MMP-12]] (Matrix Metalloproteinases): Associated with detrimental effects on senescent dermal fibroblasts during tissue damage; contribute to skin ageing; cooperatively facilitate cancer cell metastasis.

### Bioactive Lipids

- **Leukotrienes**: Secreted from senescent lung fibroblasts; discovered in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Have pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic functions depending on cysteinyl leukotriene receptor expression.
- **Prostaglandins**: Have roles in tissue injury repair (cutaneous wound healing, renal, hepatic, myocardial injury repair) and prevention of excessive lung fibrosis. Metabolic conversion to oxylipin dihomo-15d-PGJ2 reinforces senescence-associated proliferation arrest.
- **Oxylipins**: Signaling lipids whose biosynthesis is elevated in senescent cells. Dihomo-15d-PGJ2 activates [[HRAS]] and its effectors [[ERK1/2]] through cell-autonomous mechanisms.

### Small Extracellular Vesicles

Small extracellular vesicles, especially exosomes (30–150 nm), mediate paracrine effects including cancer promotion and [[Paracrine Senescence]]. Exosome absolute levels increase in various senescent cells. Content is heterogeneous, including proteins, lipids, and microRNAs. Exosomes promote cancer cell proliferation through [[Ephrin Type A Receptor 2|EPHA2]].

### Non-coding Nucleic Acids

- **miRNAs**: Mediate communication between senescent fibroblasts and keratinocytes in human skin. Context-dependent positive and negative effects on SASP establishment.
- **Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments (CCFs)**: Elicit DNA damage responses in recipient cells.
- **LINE1 Retrotransposable Elements**: Elevated during late senescence; cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA triggers interferon signalling, reinforcing SASP factor production. Mark of deep senescence.

## Regulation of the SASP

### The p53–p21 and p16–Rb Pathways

- [[p53]]: In most contexts, restrains production of pro-inflammatory SASP factors. Loss of p53 amplifies pro-inflammatory SASP factors. Nutlin 3a (MDM2 inhibitor stabilizing p53) attenuates the secretory phenotype.
- [[p21 CIP1|p21]]: Supports an early secretory phenotype that includes CXCL14 and [[IGFBP3]], placing stressed cells under immunosurveillance, mediated by [[Retinoblastoma Protein|Rb]] activation.
- [[p16INK4A|p16]]: Overexpression or long-term CDK4/6 inhibition induces a DDR-free senescent state without pro-inflammatory SASP but with a unique p53-dependent SASP profile (PASP) including IGFBP3 and [[ISG15]]. Essential for expression of SASP factors in OIS and TIS cells.

### The DDR and NF-κB

- [[NF-κB]]: Master regulator of SASP interleukins, cytokines, chemokines, growth factors. Disruption allows cells to escape senescence-associated proliferation arrest.
- [[ATM]]–[[PARP1]]–IKK axis: Acutely activated in advance of NF-κB signalling.
- [[IκBζ]]: Selective co-activator of NF-κB for a subset of SASP target genes (e.g., IL6 and IL8).
- [[CEBPβ|C/EBPβ]]: Cooperates with NF-κB to regulate CXCR2 and pro-inflammatory SASP genes.
- [[Protein Kinase D|PKD]]: Major sensor of oxidative stress interacting with NF-κB to modulate SASP gene expression.
- [[CD36]] and [[CD40L]]: Cell surface proteins essential for NF-κB activation and downstream SASP.
- [[GATA4]]: Activates NF-κB via [[TRAF3IP2]]; also promotes pro-inflammatory SASP through inhibition of autophagy of GATA4.

### The p38 MAPK and mTOR Pathways

- [[p38 MAPK]]: First protein shown to activate NF-κB-mediated SASP independent of DDR. Stabilizes SASP transcripts in tumour microenvironment. Activated by TAK1–ZSCAN4 complex following chemotherapy.
- [[mTORC1|mTOR]]: Promotes SASP protein production through two mechanisms: (1) promotes translation of membrane-bound IL-1α and IL-1α-mediated NF-κB transcriptional activities; (2) promotes translation of [[MAPKAPK2|MK2]], which phosphorylates and inhibits [[ZFP36L1]], stabilizing SASP factor transcripts. [[Rapamycin]] reduces pro-inflammatory SASP.
- [[AMPK]]: In physiological hypoxia, inhibits mTOR to suppress detrimental SASP factors.
- [[JAK]]–[[STAT3]]: Interacts with p38 MAPK and mTOR; promotes pro-inflammatory SASP and age-related frailty.

### Cytosolic DNA–cGAS–STING Pathway

- [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING]] complex: Promotes SASP through interferon signalling. Activated by cytoplasmic DNA from downregulated [[DNASE2]] and [[TREX1]]. Both [[TOP1]] cleavage complexes and [[G3BP1]] assist cGAS recognition of CCFs.
- [[Toll-like Receptor 2|TLR2]]: Innate immunity sensor upregulated in senescent cells; essential for immunity-regulating SASP during OIS through cGAS–STING.
- **LINE1 retrotransposable elements**: Elevated during late senescence; cytoplasmic LINE1 cDNA triggers interferon signalling.
- **Minority mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (miMOMP)**: [[BAX]] and [[BAK]] macropores allow mitochondrial inner membrane herniation to release mitochondrial DNA, activating cGAS–STING and SASP.

### Mitochondrial Metabolic Checkpoint

Mitochondrial metabolism independently gates the SASP through an epigenetic mechanism. Senescent cells rely on mitochondrial pyruvate metabolism to supply the acetyl-CoA used for [[H3K27ac|H3K27ac]] deposition at SASP loci:

- Pyruvate enters mitochondria via the [[MPC|mitochondrial pyruvate carrier]] (MPC1/MPC2) and feeds citrate synthesis.
- Citrate is exported to the cytosol by the mitochondrial citrate carrier [[SLC25A1]], where [[ACLY]] regenerates the acetyl-CoA pool for H3K27ac.
- Inhibiting [[SLC25A1]] (with [[CTPI2]]) or [[MPC]] depletes this pool, actively removes H3K27ac from SASP enhancers, and selectively suppresses SASP without killing senescent cells.
- Exogenous [[Acetate]] bypasses the block via [[ACSS2]], restoring acetylation and SASP — demonstrating acetyl-CoA availability, not mitochondrial integrity, is limiting.

> [!important] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP|Mitochondrial metabolism and epigenetic crosstalk drive SASP]]
> This mitochondrial citrate–acetyl-CoA checkpoint is **independent of mtDNA and cGAS–STING signaling**: mtDNA depletion or STING inhibition ([[STING Inhibitors|SN011]]) does not prevent H3K27ac loss upon SLC25A1 inhibition. The two arms are genetically separable and converge on SASP gene activation. CTPI2 administration to aged mice reduced age-related [[Frailty]] and improved healthspan, establishing the metabolic arm as a druggable target for SASP modulation.

### Epigenetic Regulation

- [[METTL3]]–[[METTL14]]: m6A-methyltransferase complex promotes SASP factor expression by bringing enhancers close to promoters of SASP genes with NF-κB binding sites.
- [[BRD4]]: Recruited to senescence-activated enhancers, contributing to SASP factor expression.
- [[CEBPα|C/EBPα]]: Binds to senescence-activated enhancers to promote SASP genes.
- [[KDM4]]: Reduces H3K9 and H3K36 methylation, promoting SASP factors.
- [[EZH2]] (PRC2): Represses pro-inflammatory SASP factors (CCL2, CXCL9, CXCL10).
- [[SIRT1]]: Reduces H3K9 and H4K16 acetylation in IL-6 and IL-8 promoter regions.
- **macroH2A1**: Positive regulator of SASP genes; removed in negative feedback loop by ER stress and ROS.
- **HMGB1** and **HMGB2**: HMGB1 depletion upregulates SASP genes; HMGB2 protects SASP genes from SAHF-mediated silencing.
- **HMGA1**: Upregulated in OIS; promotes NAMPT expression enhancing NAD+-mediated AMPK inhibition.

### Additional Regulators

- **miRNAs**: miR-34a contributes to SASP in vascular smooth muscle cells; miR-125a promotes SASP in COPD lung tissues; miR-15b inhibits SIRT4-mediated SASP.
- **lncRNAs**: MIR31HG binds YBX1, crucial for IL-1α translation and downstream SASP activation.
- **PDK4**: Promotes aerobic glycolysis and lactate production driving ROS-mediated SASP.
- **Iron**: Iron chelation by deferiprone suppresses pro-inflammatory SASP factors (CCL2, IL-11).

### Regulation of Lipid SASP

- [[PTGS2]] (COX2): Transcriptionally upregulated; dependent on p53; cooperatively regulates pro-inflammatory SASP.
- [[ALOX5]]: Converts PUFAs to leukotrienes; expressed in lung fibroblasts contributing to pulmonary fibrosis.
- [[cPLA2]]: Phosphorylated by p38 MAPK; provides free PUFAs for leukotriene and prostaglandin generation.

### Regulation of Exosome SASP

- [[RAB35]]: GTPase controlling exosome secretion through multivesicular body pathway.
- [[STEAP3]] (TSAP6) and [[VPS32]] (SNF7): p53 targets regulating exosome production during senescence.
- [[IFITM3]]: Highly expressed in exosomes from senescent cells; mediates paracrine senescence.
- [[EPHA2]]: Receptor tyrosine kinase upregulated in secreted exosomes; essential for pro-tumorigenic effects.

## Temporal Dynamics

The SASP is temporally dynamic and intricately regulated:

1. **Early phase**: p53–p21 pathway activated; TGFβ secretion dominant (Notch-dependent switch)
2. **Intermediate phase**: NF-κB and p16 induced; IL-6 and IL-8 secretion increases
3. **Deep senescence**: LINE1-driven interferon signalling reinforces pro-inflammatory SASP

Notch signalling is crucial for the switch from early (TGFβ) to late (IL-6, IL-8) SASP by suppressing C/EBPβ. The transcription factor AP1 defines temporal dynamics of the senescence-associated transcriptional network.

## SASP Heterogeneity

### Determinants

- **Senescence inducers**: OIS (RAS, RAF, MYC activation) significantly increases SASP gene expression compared to other inducers. Therapy type influences composition (genotoxic stress promotes pro-inflammatory SASP; CDK4/6 inhibitors do not trigger NF-κB targets).
- **Cell type**: Fibroblasts are highly secretory; dermal fibroblasts, melanocytes, keratinocytes, and endothelial cells produce diverse SASP profiles. Less than 50% of SASP proteins are shared between fibroblasts and epithelial cells.
- **Tissue environment**: Physiological hypoxia (1% O2) reduces pro-inflammatory SASP; high glucose induces senescence and SASP; dietary macronutrients affect SASP levels (high-fat promotes inflammatory factors); SASP gene expression varies across tissues (eye, kidney, heart, spleen, lung, liver, colon).

## Biological Functions

### Paracrine Senescence

[[Paracrine Senescence|SASP components]] (IL-1α, TGFβ, VEGF, CCL2, CCL20) autocrinally reinforce senescence-associated growth arrest and induce paracrine proliferation arrest in neighbouring cells. Notch signalling is essential for establishing paracrine senescence and weakens the SASP produced by secondary senescent cells. Exosomes induce paracrine senescence through IFITM3.

### Tissue Remodelling and Repair

Senescent cells and their SASP contribute to wound healing through modulation of vascularization and matrix production. Transient increases in senescent cells during wound healing secrete [[PDGFAA]] to induce myofibroblast differentiation. The SASP promotes a pro-regenerative response through induction of cell plasticity in keratinocytes. Regeneration of kidney, heart, liver, and salamander limbs benefits from cellular senescence.

### Immune System Interaction

SASP factors promote immune cell migration and modulate immune cell phenotype and function. IL-6 and IL-1β with TGFβ promote T cell differentiation to [[TH17]] phenotype. SASP-mediated recruitment of macrophages, natural killer cells, neutrophils, and T cells is beneficial for limiting tumorigenesis. However, excessive SASP factors can suppress clearance of senescent cells by impairing macrophage phagocytosis and stimulate myeloid-derived suppressor cells.

### Impact on Ageing

Senescent cells gradually accumulate during ageing and contribute to ageing-related diseases through the SASP. Removal of naturally occurring senescent cells significantly improved health span of aged animals. A panel of circulating SASP proteins predicts adverse events in older individuals (60–90 years old). The SASP profile in urinary extracellular vesicles can distinguish between older individuals with or without Parkinson disease.

### Cancer

The SASP is a double-edged sword in cancer:

- **Beneficial**: Activation or restoration of p53 induces immunogenic SASP (CSF1, CCL2, IL-15, CXCL1, CXCL14, IGFBP3) contributing to macrophage-mediated or CD4+ T cell-mediated tumour regression. SASP factors contribute to propagation of senescence and secondary cell death in tumours.
- **Detrimental**: NF-κB-associated SASP factors are pro-tumorigenic. IL-6 and IL-8 promote proliferation, metastasis, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and stemness. CCL2 promotes immune evasion. VEGF stimulates angiogenesis. CXCL12 and MMPs facilitate metastasis.

## Biomarkers and Senomorphic Therapy

### Clinical Biomarkers

SASP factors serve as surrogate markers for senescence burden. The SASP Atlas proposes GDF15, stanniocalcin 1, serpin proteins, and MMPs as biomarker panel. Five SASP factors (GDF15, RAGE, VEGFA, PARC/CCL18, MMP2) are strongly associated with mortality. Plasma SASP factor levels predict COVID-19 severity.

### Senomorphic Therapies

[[Senomorphic Therapy|Senomorphic therapies]] modulate senescence-associated properties without cytotoxicity:

- **Glucocorticoids**: Block pro-inflammatory SASP by impairing IL-1α-mediated NF-κB activity
- **[[Metformin]]**: Inhibits NF-κB signalling in fibroblasts, macrophages, and senescent endothelial cells
- **Olive phenols**: Reduce cGAS–STING–NF-κB pro-inflammatory SASP
- **[[Nutlin 3a]]**: Suppresses pro-inflammatory SASP by stabilizing p53
- **p38 MAPK/MK2 inhibitors**: Inhibit SASP from cultured fibroblasts
- **[[Rapamycin]]**: Suppresses SASP by interfering with mTOR and p38 MAPK–IL-1α–NF-κB pathways
- **Hypoxia-mimetic compounds**: Block mTOR through AMPK activation, suppress pro-inflammatory SASP
- **[[Resveratrol]]**: Inhibits pro-inflammatory SASP cytokines
- **Apigenin**: Reduces breast cancer aggressiveness by inhibiting NF-κB-dependent SASP
- **Nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs)**: Lamivudine (3TC) inhibits LINE1 propagation, reducing cGAS–STING–NF-κB SASP
- **[[Autophagy Inducer|Autophagy inducers]]** (e.g., [[Rapamycin]], [[Metformin]], [[Spermidine]]): Clear damaged organelles and limit inflammasome activation, suppressing SASP and [[Inflammaging]]; [[Autophagy]] itself suppresses SASP by clearing damaged organelles and restraining inflammasome activation.

### Lifestyle Interventions

- High-fat diet elevates pro-inflammatory SASP factors; can be rescued by senescent cell removal
- Dietary restriction (30% caloric decrease) reduces DNA damage, NF-κB, and SASP levels during renal ageing
- Exercise training reduces senescent cell burden and SASP levels; specific SASP panel predicts exercise intervention responders

## Interaction with Apoptosis

SASP and [[Apoptosis]] interact in complex, bidirectional ways. [[Senescent Cells]] (which produce SASP) are typically resistant to apoptosis, while SASP factors can promote or inhibit apoptosis in neighboring cells.

### Resistance to Apoptosis
Senescent cells upregulate senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs), allowing them to persist and continuously secrete SASP. Key mechanisms include:
- Upregulation of anti-apoptotic proteins from the [[Bcl-2]] family (e.g., BCL-2, BCL-XL, BCL-W, Mcl-1).
- Activation of survival pathways (e.g., [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], [[Unfolded Protein Response|UPR]]).
- Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction (SAMD).

### Paracrine Effects on Apoptosis
- **Pro-apoptotic**: Factors like [[IL-6]] and [[TNFα|TNF-α]] can enhance T-cell apoptosis, impairing immune clearance.
- **Anti-apoptotic**: SASP can confer apoptosis resistance to some neighboring cells via paracrine signaling.

### Shared Mitochondrial Mechanisms
Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP) is a key connector:
- Sublethal stress (miMOMP) in senescent cells releases [[mtDNA]] → activates [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] → drives SASP without full cell death.
- This creates a "mitochondrial apoptotic stress" state that sustains SASP while the cell resists complete apoptosis.

## Clinical Relevance

### Senolytic Combination Therapy

The SASP is the therapeutic target of [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytics]] — drugs that selectively eliminate senescent cells. [[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]] (D+Q) reduces SASP burden in aged mice and has shown clinical benefit in [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]], [[Diabetic Kidney Disease]], and [[Osteoarthritis]].

### Biomarker Potential

Circulating SASP factors ([[IL-6]], [[IL-8]], [[MMP-9]], [[PAI-1]]) serve as biomarkers of biological age and senescent cell burden.

### Galectin-9

> [!info] Source: Dong et al. 2024 (PMC11365203)
> Tarallo et al. first reported that senescent cells secrete [[Galectin-9]] in melanoma. Galectin-9 has immunosuppressive effects in the TME, promoting T cell apoptosis, increasing Treg and M2 macrophage polarization, and impairing anti-tumor immune responses. Some reports suggest Galectin-9 can also inhibit melanoma metastasis, highlighting context-dependent roles.

### Extracellular Vesicles

> [!info] Source: Dong et al. 2024 (PMC11365203)
> [[Extracellular Vesicles]] have emerged as crucial intermediaries within SASP. Suppression of small EV release induces DNA damage and apoptosis-like death in senescent cells. The vesicular secretome induces diverse effects depending on cellular environment, driven by varied EV cargos (proteins, nucleic acids, lipids). Since EVs are found in all bodily fluids, they are valuable targets for liquid biopsy and potential treatment of age-related diseases.
