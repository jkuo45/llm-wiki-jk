# Inflammatory Pain

**Inflammatory Pain** is pain arising from tissue injury and inflammation, in which immune-derived mediators sensitize [[Nociceptor]]s at the site of injury and within the dorsal horn, producing spontaneous pain, hyperalgesia, and allodynia.

## Pathophysiology

Inflammatory mediators including [[Prostaglandin E2]], bradykinin, and cytokines sensitize nociceptor terminals, while spinal mechanisms involving [[Glutamate]] and the [[NMDA receptor]] amplify nociceptive transmission and produce central sensitization. This glutamatergic component is a key target for analgesic agents, and pain-evoked expression of the immediate-early gene c-Fos in the superficial dorsal horn (laminae I–II) is a standard readout of nociceptive activation.

## Honokiol in Inflammatory Pain

[[Honokiol]], a biphenolic neolignan, attenuates inflammatory pain in preclinical models. It reduces formalin-induced paw-licking behavior in the inflammatory phase and thermal hyperalgesia in mice, blocks nociceptive signaling evoked by NMDA, [[Glutamate]], substance P, and [[Prostaglandin E2]], and decreases formalin-induced c-Fos expression in superficial laminae (I–II) of the L4–L5 lumbar dorsal horn. Compared with [[Magnolol]], honokiol is more selective for NMDA-induced behavioral responses and hyperalgesia.

## Therapeutic Implications

The analgesic effect of honokiol is multifactorial, involving both the peripheral [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] cascade and central glutamatergic/NMDA receptor pathways. Its actions illustrate how anti-inflammatory and neuromodulatory mechanisms converge, and position [[Neuroinflammation]] as a shared driver of chronic pain and age-related sensory dysfunction.
