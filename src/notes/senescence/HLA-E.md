# HLA-E

**HLA-E** is a non-classical (class Ib) major histocompatibility complex molecule characterized by limited polymorphism and a specialized role in innate and adaptive immune regulation. Rather than presenting a diverse peptide repertoire, HLA-E primarily presents leader peptides derived from other MHC class I molecules and functions chiefly as an **immune checkpoint ligand**. Its upregulation is exploited by senescent and cancer cells to escape immune clearance.

## Structure & Function

- A heavy chain associated with β2-microglobulin, structurally similar to classical MHC-I but with a restricted groove that preferentially binds nonameric signal-sequence peptides from HLA-A, -B, -C, and -G.
- Surface expression of HLA-E reports on the integrity of the overall MHC-I biosynthetic pathway, allowing immune cells to sense "missing self."

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- HLA-E is the ligand for the inhibitory receptor **[[NKG2A]]/[[CD94]]** heterodimer on [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]] and [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]]; engagement delivers an inhibitory signal that **blunts cytotoxic killing**.
- It also engages the activating receptor NKG2C/CD94 with lower affinity, but the dominant physiological effect in the senescence/cancer context is inhibitory.
- Expression is stabilized by peptide availability and can be induced by inflammatory signaling, including [[Interferon-gamma|IFN-γ]] and SASP cytokines.

## Role in Senescence / SASP

- HLA-E is a key node of **cancer and senescent-cell immune evasion**: the SASP cytokines [[IL-6]] and [[IL-8]] upregulate HLA-E, engaging NKG2A/CD94 to suppress NK- and CD8+ T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity.
- By dampening [[Senescence Surveillance|immune surveillance]], HLA-E upregulation allows senescent cells to persist and continue secreting the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]], and enables tumor cells to escape clearance.
- This complements other SASP-driven evasion mechanisms such as recruitment of [[Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells|myeloid suppressor cells]].

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- The HLA-E–NKG2A axis is an active immuno-oncology target: **monalizumab** (anti-NKG2A) aims to release NK/CD8+ T-cell suppression and restore anti-tumor immunity.
- HLA-E overexpression is associated with poor prognosis in several cancers and may serve as a biomarker of immune escape.
- Blockade of HLA-E/NKG2A is being explored to enhance immune-mediated clearance of senescent cells (a senotherapeutic strategy).
