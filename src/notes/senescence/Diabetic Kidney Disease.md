# Diabetic Kidney Disease

**Diabetic Kidney Disease** (DKD) is a progressive complication of [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] and a leading cause of [[Chronic Kidney Disease]]. It is characterized by glomerular and tubular injury driven by chronic [[Hyperglycemia]], [[Inflammation]], and extracellular matrix remodeling culminating in [[Kidney Fibrosis]] and loss of renal function.

## Pathophysiology & Senescence

Accumulating evidence implicates cellular senescence in DKD progression. Senescent renal cells within the [[Kidney]] acquire a [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]], secreting pro-inflammatory cytokines and matrix-remodeling factors that propagate fibrotic signaling and renal dysfunction. Chronic hyperglycemia acts as a stressor that induces premature senescence of mesangial, tubular, and endothelial cells, linking metabolic stress to senescence-driven matrix deposition and inflammation.

## Senolytic Therapy

The senolytic combination of [[Dasatinib]] and [[Quercetin]] (D+Q) has been tested in a pilot study for DKD (NCT02848131) based on the hypothesis that clearing [[Senescent Cells]] and dampening the SASP would attenuate renal fibrosis. Early results demonstrated feasibility, safety, and reduced senescent-cell burden with biomarker improvements, but clinical confidence remains preliminary pending larger powered trials. D+Q is among the few senolytic regimens with human feasibility data in a metabolic kidney indication.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Beyond senolytics, standard of care includes glycemic control, renin–angiotensin–aldosterone blockade, and SGLT2 inhibitors. Senolytic and senomorphic approaches, including [[Senolytics]] and senescent-cell-targeted drugs, are positioned as adjunct strategies to address the residual fibrotic burden not covered by metabolic and hemodynamic therapy.
