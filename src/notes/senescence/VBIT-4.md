# VBIT-4

**VBIT-4** is a small molecule that binds [[VDAC|VDAC1]] (voltage-dependent anion channel 1) and prevents its oligomerization. VDAC1 oligomers form a large mitochondrial outer-membrane pore through which [[mtDNA]] and other pro-apoptotic factors can escape; VBIT-4 thereby inhibits apoptosis and protects mitochondrial integrity. It has been used to probe VDAC1-dependent mtDNA release, interferon signalling and inflammation in autoimmune and neurodegenerative models. A 2025 preprint has questioned the specificity of VBIT-4, reporting membrane-disruptive activity, so mechanistic interpretations require appropriate controls.

## Use in the Ye 2026 study

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> To test whether [[mtDNA]] leakage contributes upstream of [[STING]] activation, [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]] were pre-treated with VBIT-4, an inhibitor of VDAC1 oligomerization (the macropore responsible for mtDNA release). VBIT-4 abolished cytosolic mtDNA accumulation, reduced the SA-β-gal+ senescent fraction, lowered expression of Cgas and Sting1, and diminished phosphorylated [[STING]] and [[IRF3]]. These results position mtDNA release as the step coupling NAD+ exhaustion-induced [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial damage]] to [[cGAS-STING Pathway]]-driven [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|CD8+ T-cell senescence]].

VBIT-4 is used here as a mechanistic probe rather than a therapeutic: it separates the mitochondrial-damage step from the innate DNA-sensing step in the NAD+–mitochondria–cGAS-STING axis of [[Ulcerative Colitis]].
