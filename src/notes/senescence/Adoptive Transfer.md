# Adoptive Transfer

**Adoptive transfer** is an experimental technique in which cells are isolated from a donor, manipulated or treated ex vivo, and infused into a recipient so that the in-vivo consequences of that specific cell population can be tested. In immunology it is used to establish causal roles for lymphocyte subsets, to track engraftment and homing, and (clinically, as adoptive cell therapy) to deliver tumour-reactive T cells. Its interpretive strength is that it isolates a cell-intrinsic effect from the donor's broader tissue environment.

## Use in the Ye 2026 study

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> To verify the direct pathogenic role of senescent CD8+ T cells in vivo, [[FK866]]-induced senescent [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]] (100 nM, 72 h; n = 6) were adoptively transferred into mice with [[Dextran Sulfate Sodium|DSS]]-induced colitis. Recipients showed accelerated disease — more severe weight loss and higher disease activity index scores — versus vehicle-transfer and control-CD8+ T-cell groups. Flow cytometry of colonic lamina propria showed expansion of total and CD8+ T cells (but not CD4+, myeloid or B cells), increased [[KLRG1]]+[[CD27]]- senescent CD8+ T cells, and elevated IFN-γ-producing CD8+ T cells.

The experiment establishes sufficiency: NAD+ dysregulation-driven [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|senescent CD8+ T cells]], characterized by an enhanced pro-inflammatory [[SASP]], directly exacerbate intestinal inflammation in [[Ulcerative Colitis]]. It complements the loss-of-function evidence from [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic therapy]] and anti-CD8 depletion.
