# C178

**C-178** is a small-molecule, covalent antagonist of [[STING]]. It modifies Cys91 in the transmembrane domain of murine STING, blocking the palmitoylation that is required for STING clustering and activation at the Golgi; this retains STING in a signalling-incompetent state and suppresses [[TBK1]]/[[IRF3]] phosphorylation and downstream type-I interferon and pro-inflammatory transcription. Its Cys91-dependent mechanism makes it largely selective for mouse STING, which is relevant because the Ye 2026 experiments use primary murine [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]]. Related palmitoylation inhibitors include H-151 and SN-011.

## Use in the Ye 2026 study

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> To validate the cGAS-STING mechanism, [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]] were treated with the STING agonist [[diABZI]] or the inhibitor C178. Immunoblotting showed [[FK866]] activated the pathway (upregulated [[cGAS]] and [[IRF3]], increased phosphorylated [[STING]]); conversely, blockade of STING with C178 rescued FK866-induced upregulation of [[p16]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21]]. Flow cytometry showed C178 attenuated the pro-inflammatory IFN-γ/TNF-α phenotype induced by NAD+ restriction.

C178 provides the loss-of-function half of the pharmacological pair with [[diABZI]]; together with resistance of [[STING]]-knockout CD8+ T cells to FK866, it establishes [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] signalling as required for [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|CD8+ T-cell senescence]] in the [[Ulcerative Colitis]] model.
