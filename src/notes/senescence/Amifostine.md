# Amifostine

**Amifostine** (WR-2721, Ethyol) is a phosphorothioate prodrug used as a cytoprotective agent during [[Radiation Therapy]] and certain chemotherapies. It is dephosphorylated at the cell membrane by alkaline phosphatase to its active free-thiol form, WR-1065, which accumulates selectively in normal tissues.

## Mechanism of Action

The active thiol WR-1065 acts as a potent scavenger of [[Reactive Oxygen Species]], particularly [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] produced by ionizing radiation. By donating hydrogen atoms to neutralize the highly reactive HO• radical, WR-1065 spares DNA and proteins from radiation-induced [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[DNA Damage]]. Its selective uptake by normal tissues (driven by differential alkaline phosphatase activity and membrane transport) underlies its cytoprotective profile, preferentially shielding healthy tissue rather than tumor.

## Pharmacodynamics & Side Effects

Amifostine also promotes the reductive repair of damaged DNA and scavenges other free radicals, complementing its direct HO• neutralization. The main dose-limiting toxicities are [[Hypotension]] and nausea, which constrain its clinical use. Despite these limitations, it remains the prototypical selective radioprotectant and a reference compound for redox-based normal-tissue protection, including radiation-induced xerostomia.

## Applications

- Amifostine is approved to reduce the incidence of moderate-to-severe xerostomia in patients undergoing post-operative radiation for head and neck cancer.
- Its paradigm of selectively protecting normal tissue while preserving antitumor efficacy is relevant to senotherapeutics, where sparing non-senescent cells during [[Senolytic Therapy]] is a parallel design goal.
