# MHC Class Ib

**MHC Class Ib** molecules are the non-classical arm of [[Major histocompatibility complex class I|MHC class I]] glycoproteins. Unlike the highly polymorphic classical class Ia molecules (HLA-A, -B, -C), class Ib molecules (HLA-E, -F, -G) are oligomorphic and present a restricted set of peptides — often leader peptides derived from other MHC class I molecules — to innate lymphoid receptors rather than to conventional αβ T-cell receptors.

## Structure & Function

MHC class Ib molecules maintain the class I architecture of an α chain with three domains complexed with β2-microglobulin, but their limited polymorphism restricts the peptide repertoire. This specialization underpins dedicated ligand–receptor interactions with innate receptors such as [[NKG2A]]/[[CD94]] on [[Natural Killer Cells]]. HLA-E is the canonical example: it presents class I leader-sequence peptides and its engagement with CD94/NKG2A delivers an inhibitory signal that is part of the standard NK-cell licensing and surveillance machinery.

## Role in Senescence & Immune Evasion

In cellular senescence, upregulation of HLA-E becomes a mechanism of [[Immune Evasion]] that protects arrested cells from cytotoxic lymphocytes. By engaging the inhibitory NKG2A/CD94 receptor, senescent cells dampen NK- and T-cell-mediated elimination, undermining [[Senescence Surveillance]]. The class Ib identity enables this checkpoint-like function, positioning non-classical MHC molecules at the interface of senescence immunobiology and immune escape.

## Clinical Relevance

Because class Ib molecules modulate NK-cell activity, they are targets for therapeutic modulation of innate immunity, including in cancer, viral infection, and senescence-based immunotherapy.
