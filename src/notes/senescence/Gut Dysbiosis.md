# Gut Dysbiosis

**Gut Dysbiosis** is an imbalance in the composition, diversity, and function of the [[Gut Microbiome]] that is associated with intestinal inflammation, impaired [[Intestinal Barrier]] function, and systemic disease. It is implicated in inflammatory bowel conditions, metabolic syndrome, and age-related inflammation.

## NMN-Mediated Remodeling

[[NMN]] (nicotinamide mononucleotide) reverses gut dysbiosis and protects the intestinal tract. In a dextran sodium sulfate colitis mouse model, NMN given by gavage (1 mg/g body weight) increased microbial abundance and diversity, improved mucus secretion and tight-junction protein expression, and attenuated intestinal mucosal permeability. Long-term NMN treatment reshaped microbiota composition, increasing butyric-acid-producing bacteria such as Ruminococcaceae UCG-014 and Prevotellaceae NK3B31 as well as Akkermansia muciniphila, while decreasing potentially harmful taxa such as Bilophila and Oscillibacter.

## Mechanism

These changes restore intestinal homeostasis and barrier integrity through microbiota remodeling, increasing production of [[Short-chain Fatty Acids]] and beneficial metabolites that reinforce the mucosal barrier. Because NMN replenishes [[NAD+]] and supports [[Sirtuins]] activity, NAD+-dependent mechanisms are thought to underlie part of the microbiota-modulating and gut-protective effect, linking energy metabolism to gut ecology.

## Clinical Relevance

Targeting dysbiosis through NAD+ precursors is a promising strategy for inflammatory bowel disease and for dampening gut-derived inflammation in aging. The gut–immune axis connects dysbiosis to systemic [[Inflammation]] and immunosenescence, making the microbiome a modifiable node in age-related inflammatory burden.
