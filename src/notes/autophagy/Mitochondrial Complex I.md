# Mitochondrial Complex I

**Mitochondrial Complex I** (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) is a key enzyme complex in the [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] electron transport chain.

## Inhibition by Metformin
- [[Metformin]] inhibits complex 1, leading to an increased AMP:ATP ratio.
- This subsequently activates [[AMPK]].
- Inhibition reduces mitochondrial [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] production, oxidative stress, and [[Senescence]].

## Immune Impact
- Reducing ROS production via complex 1 inhibition dampens the secretion of proinflammatory [[Cytokines|cytokines]] such as [[IL-6]] and [[IL-1β]].

## Function

Mitochondrial Complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase) is the largest ETC entry point. Its dysfunction generates ROS and triggers [[Mitophagy]].

## MYC^high^ Selectivity & Cancer Vulnerability

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]]
> Inhibition of [[Complex I]] with [[Metformin]], [[BAY 87-2243]], [[Mubritinib]], or rotenone depletes both [[NAD+]] and ATP levels independently of MYC status. However, MYC^high^ cells are selectively sensitive to Complex I inhibition because they rely on Complex I-regenerated NAD+ to sustain a glutamine-fueled [[TCA cycle]]. Q-site (ubiquinone-binding site) inhibitors show the highest MYC selectivity.

The MYC^high^-selective vulnerability arises from the coupling of elevated [[OXPHOS]] to anabolic demand: [[MYC]] rewires the TCA cycle to run on [[Glutamine]] carbon, making NAD+ regeneration by Complex I indispensable. Co-inhibition of Complex I and [[Glutaminolysis]] (e.g., with [[CB-839]]) is [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic lethal]] in MYC^high^ breast cancer cells and PDX models.
