# Reticulophagy

**Reticulophagy** (also called ER-phagy) is the selective [[Autophagy]] of the
endoplasmic reticulum: the process by which ER membrane, including discrete
sheet and tubule domains, is engulfed and delivered to the [[Lysosome]] for
degradation. It is one arm of the broader framework of
[[Selective Autophagy|selective autophagy]], distinguished from [[Mitophagy]]
and [[Lipophagy]] by its receptor-independent-ER-resident receptor machinery.

> [!info] Why the ER needs its own pathway
> The ER is an unusually large and plastic organelle whose area is hard to
> calibrate during [[ER Stress]], nutrient flux, and [[Lipophagy|overlapping
> vesicular trafficking]]. Because its function depends on folding capacity and
> Ca²⁺ handling, surplus or damaged ER membrane must be removed rather than
> merely unfolded. Reticulophagy is that quality-control route.

## Mechanism

Reticulophagy is a form of [[Macroautophagy]] using double-membrane
[[Autophagosome|autophagosomes]]. The distinguishing question is which cargo
receptor tethers the ER to the forming phagophore.

- **FAM134B / RETREG1** — the best-characterised receptor. A reticulon-family
  ER membrane protein with an N-terminal reticulon homology domain that
  anchors it to the ER and mediates membrane curvature, and an LIR motif that
  binds [[LC3]]. Its central role in selective ER-phagy and disease is well
  documented.
- **ATL3**, an ER-shaping GTPase and ATL family member, is both a receptor and
  a membrane-remodeling factor acting on ER tubules.
- **TEX264**, an ER membrane protein with a GyrI-like LIR motif, is the
  receptor most directly tied to basal, unstressed turnover rather than
  stress-induced ER-phagy.
- **CCPG1** and **SEC62** act as adaptors under specific stress conditions
  (including ER stress in plants and nutrient starvation in mammals).
- **Reticulons (RTN1/RTN2/RTN3)** contribute membrane-remodeling capacity and
  influence receptor availability.

The core machinery overlaps with canonical autophagy: [[Atg5]]–[[Atg7]]-mediated
LC3 lipidation on the phagophore, [[Atg8|LC3]]–[[FIP200]]/RUBCN scaffolding,
and autophagosome–lysosome fusion. In mammals, ULK1 complex formation at the
ER is an early, rate-setting step; the [[mTOR]] complex and [[AMPK]] regulate
it, so reticulophagy is under the same nutrient and energy control as bulk
autophagy.

Tuning is set by **reticulon 3 (RTN3)** availability and by the relative
abundance of TEX264 versus FAM134B at the ER surface, which determines whether
ER is removed by piecemeal tubule-specific excision or by larger-scale
envelope engulfment.

## Physiological Function

Basal reticulophagy runs constitutively to keep ER volume matched to secretory
load. It is upregulated by [[Unfolded Protein Response|ER stress]], nutrient
deprivation, and infection. Degraded ER membrane components — especially
phospholipids — are recycled, and loss of ER-phagy produces ER expansion.

Beyond bulk removal, FAM134B-mediated ER-phagy has specific signalling
consequences: it has been reported to suppress [[NF-κB]] activation by clearing
damaged or over-expanded ER and reducing ER stress, and to limit mitochondrial
calcium overload by reducing inositol trisphosphate receptor abundance at mitochondria-associated
ER contact sites.

> [!warning] ER-phagy has a double life
> Enlarging the ER by blocking reticulophagy can be adaptive in some models
> (providing membrane for secretion or for [[Lipid Droplet|lipid droplet]]
> biogenesis), so "more ER-phagy" is not unconditionally better. The direction
> of benefit is tissue- and stress-dependent, and the claim that ER-phagy
> activation is uniformly geroprotective is not established.

## Disease Relevance

Dysregulated ER-phagy is implicated in neurodegeneration (reticulon and
receptor mutations causing hereditary spastic paraplegia and motor neuron
disease, with defective [[Autophagy|autophagic]] clearance of ER in
[[Neurodegeneration]]), liver disease including NAFLD and alcohol-related
injury, inflammatory bowel disease and other [[Inflammation|inflammatory]]
conditions where ER-phagy restrains inflammasome and NF-κB signalling, and in
[[Aging]] where ER-phagy capacity declines alongside lysosomal competence.
[[Fibrosis]] and [[Apoptosis]] signalling are also intertwined: ER
expansion can trigger [[Apoptosis]], and ER-phagy is an alternative disposal
route that competes with it.

## Connections
- [[ER Stress]] — the defining stimulus: accumulated misfolded protein and Ca²⁺ depletion are relieved in part by removing surplus ER membrane, which is why ER-phagy sits downstream of the unfolded protein response.
- [[Unfolded Protein Response]] — the [[Unfolded Protein Response|three arms]] of the response adjust synthesis, folding capacity, and degradation; ER-phagy handles the structural half of that adaptation.
- [[Selective Autophagy]] — reticulophagy is the ER-specific instance of the receptor-mediated cargo-selection framework shared with mitophagy and lipophagy.
- [[LC3]] — FAM134B, TEX264, and CCPG1 all tether the ER to the phagophore by binding LC3 on the nascent autophagosome through LIR motifs.
- [[Lysosome]] — terminal degradative compartment; lysosomal capacity and position determine whether ER-phagic flux completes or stalls, which is the basis for the TFEB/TFE3 connection.
- [[Mitophagy]] — the parallel selective pathway with its own receptors and an overlapping machinery; the two are frequently induced together under nutrient stress and share the mTOR/AMPK upstream gate.
- [[mTOR]] — mTORC1 suppresses ULK1 complex assembly and therefore basal ER-phagy, so nutrient availability gates ER turnover.
- [[Aging]] — declining lysosomal flux and reduced ER-phagy efficiency are part of the age-related loss of organelle quality control that accompanies [[Epigenetics and aging|epigenetic drift]] and [[Senescence|cellular senescence]].
- [[Inflammation]] — because FAM134B ER-phagy restrains NF-κB activation, failing ER-phagy converts chronic ER stress into chronic inflammatory signalling.
