# LAMP2

**LAMP2** (lysosome-associated membrane glycoprotein 2, CD107b; gene *LAMP2*, Xq28) is one of the most abundant and most heavily glycosylated lysosomal membrane proteins. About 70% of its mass is carbohydrate in the form of N-linked and O-linked glycan chains, all with terminal galactose residues that carry the lysosomal targeting signal.

## Structure and Isoforms

LAMP2 has a short cytoplasmic tail, a single membrane-spanning segment, and a large glycosylated luminal domain organized into two homologous domains separated by a hinge. That hinge is the flexible element that allows the heavily glycosylated domains to form and release vesicle-like multimeric assemblies.

Alternative splicing of the *LAMP2* transcript generates three isoforms with distinct C-termini:

| Isoform | Distinct C-terminus | Principal role |
|---|---|---|
| LAMP-2A | 24 amino acids | Receptor for [[Chaperone-Mediated Autophagy]]; retained on the inner lysosomal membrane |
| LAMP-2B | 96 aa, includes a GYGLPI motif targeting the Golgi | Glycosylation of lysosomal enzymes, maturation of late endosomes and autophagosomes |
| LAMP-2C | 24 aa | Relieves dityrosine-mediated retention of immature lysosomes, so they can depart the endosome |

## Chaperone-Mediated Autophagy

> [!info] The receptor that makes CMA selective
> All proteins entering the lysosome by CMA contain a pentapeptide KFERQ-like targeting motif. Cytosolic HSC70 ([[HSPA8]]) recognizes the motif and delivers the substrate to LAMP-2A, which is the receptor that both binds it and translocates it across the lysosomal membrane. Because selectivity is motif-based, CMA degrades about 30% of the cytosolic proteome under prolonged nutrient deprivation (Dice, *Autophagy* 2007; PMID 17404494).

CMA activity is regulated largely by the abundance and availability of LAMP-2A: it can be raised by transcriptional induction under oxidative stress, or by reduced degradation and redistribution of the protein from the lumen to the membrane. LAMP-2A can also be sequestered into cholesterol-rich membrane microdomains where it is **inactive**, and it exits those domains when CMA is activated.

> [!important] CMA declines with age — through the receptor
> The age-related reduction in CMA is traced directly to LAMP-2A: aging brings increased LAMP-2A degradation and a reduced ability of the protein to reinsert into the lysosomal membrane. Lowering LAMP-2A levels by RNAi reduces CMA and secondarily activates macroautophagy, leaving cells more susceptible to oxidative and other stresses (Dice 2007). This makes the LAMP-2A pool a genuine candidate target for geroprotective intervention.

## Autophagosome Maturation and Disease

LAMP-2B is required for the maturation of late endosomes and autophagosomes into autolysosomes, recruiting components of the fusion machinery. Loss of LAMP-2 causes characteristic accumulation of autophagic vacuoles in affected tissue — the histologic signature of Danon disease.

Danon disease is a rare X-linked cardiomyopathy caused by *LAMP2* deficiency, with additional skeletal myopathy, cognitive defects, and visual problems. The prevailing reading of the mouse and cellular data is that loss of the **LAMP-2B** isoform in macroautophagy is what drives the phenotype, and that LAMP-2 isoforms serve distinct autophagy-related functions that a bulk "LAMP2 deficiency" model conflates (Rowland et al., *J Cell Sci* 2016; PMID 27165304).

LAMP-2 also participates in immune signaling: it binds and traffics CD40, and it is reported as a receptor for cholera toxin B subunit and for certain viral glycoproteins.
