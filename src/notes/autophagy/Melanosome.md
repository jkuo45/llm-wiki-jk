# Melanosome

## Overview

The melanosome is a biosynthetic and secretory [[Lysosome-Related Organelle]] of melanocytes in which melanin is synthesized, stored, and transferred to keratinocytes. Like canonical lysosomes, melanosomes receive cargo through the endosomal trafficking machinery; unlike lysosomes, they mature into a dedicated pigment-storage compartment. Mitochondria regulate melanosome growth and maturation rather than degradative capacity, through membrane contacts, ATP supply, phosphoinositide signaling, and the repurposing of mitochondrial dynamics proteins.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Melanosomes are biosynthetic and secretory organelles; mitochondria regulate their growth and maturation rather than degradative capacity. Mitochondria and melanosomes form dynamic physical contacts that increase during biogenesis, and disruption of [[MFN2]] or mitochondrial ATP production impairs contact formation and melanosome maturation.

## Mitochondria–Melanosome Contacts

Mitochondria establish dynamic contacts with melanosomes; contact frequency rises during melanosome biogenesis. The tether includes [[MFN2]], which also mediates mitochondria–ER contacts and mitochondrial outer-membrane fusion. A more recent identification describes a tethering complex in which the ER Ca²⁺ sensor [[STIM1]] interacts with MFN2 to promote mitochondria–melanosome contacts required for efficient maturation. These findings place direct membrane apposition between mitochondria and LROs in the same functional category as mitochondria–lysosome contacts: a platform for communication rather than a reflection of organelle proximity.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> A tethering complex involving the ER Ca²⁺ sensor [[STIM1]] and [[MFN2]] promotes mitochondria–melanosome contacts and is required for efficient melanosome maturation, providing a molecular basis for the physical interaction first observed during melanosome biogenesis.

## MFF-Dependent Division and Maturation

[[MFF]] is a mitochondrial fission adaptor that buds from mitochondria and localizes to melanosomes. At melanosomes it promotes actin-dependent division and maturation. Loss of MFF produces enlarged, immature melanosomes, revealing an extramitochondrial function for a classical mitochondrial dynamics protein. The observation that both MFF, which supports fission, and MFN2, which supports fusion, participate in mitochondria–melanosome contacts that promote biogenesis indicates that mitochondrial dynamics proteins have been repurposed for LRO remodeling.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Mitochondrial [[MFF]] buds from mitochondria, localizes to melanosomes, and promotes actin-dependent melanosome division and maturation; MFF loss results in enlarged, immature melanosomes. This extends MFF function beyond mitochondrial fission to control of LRO size.

## Phosphoinositide Control

PIKfyve and its product [[PtdIns(3,5)P2]] regulate melanosome biogenesis. Inhibition of PIKfyve blocks maturation and causes accumulation of immature melanosomal intermediates. The same lipid controls lysosomal ion homeostasis and [[TRPML1]]-dependent Ca²⁺ signaling in canonical lysosomes, supporting the interpretation that related phosphoinositide modules have been adapted to distinct organelle functions.

## Unresolved Questions

The mechanism by which mitochondrial ATP production influences contact formation is not resolved in the review. Whether the STIM1–MFN2 tether and the MFF budding pathway operate sequentially or in parallel during maturation is not established. The extent to which actin-dependent division depends on Ca²⁺ signaling at the contact interface remains to be tested.
