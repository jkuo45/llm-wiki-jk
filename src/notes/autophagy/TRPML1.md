# TRPML1

**TRPML1** (Mucolipin-1) is a lysosomal calcium channel that regulates the efflux of Ca2+ from the [[Lysosome]] lumen, essential for [[Lysosome]] biogenesis, trafficking, and reformation.

## Function

TRPML1 (MCOLN1) is a Ca2+-permeable non-selective cation channel on late endosomes and [[Lysosome]]s, activated by [[PtdIns(3,5)P2]].
## Autophagy Role

TRPML1-mediated Ca2+ release from lysosomes activates [[TFEB]] via [[calcineurin]]-dependent dephosphorylation. It also regulates autophagosome-lysosome fusion and lysosomal reformation. Loss-of-function causes [[Mucolipidosis IV]].

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Reactive oxygen species stimulate TRPML1-dependent lysosomal Ca²⁺ release, providing a candidate link between mitochondrial oxidative stress and lysosomal adaptation. At [[Mitochondria-Lysosome Contact Site|mitochondria–lysosome contacts]], Ca²⁺ released through TRPML1 crosses the outer mitochondrial membrane through VDAC1 and the inner membrane through [[Mitochondrial calcium uniporter|MCU]]. Restoring TRPML1-dependent Ca²⁺ release rescues lysosomal acidification, hydrolytic capacity, and mitochondrial physiology in cellular and animal models of mitochondrial disease; genetic or pharmacological disruption of TRPML1 alters mitochondrial Ca²⁺ handling, quality control, and survival across disease models.
