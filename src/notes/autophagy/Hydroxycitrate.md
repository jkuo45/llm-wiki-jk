# Hydroxycitrate

**(-)-Hydroxycitric acid** (HCA) is a polyhydroxycarboxylic acid found as the principal
acid in the fruit rinds of *Garcinia cambogia*, *G. indica* and *G. atroviridis*. It is
best known as a competitive inhibitor of [[ATP Citrate Lyase|ATP-citrate lyase]] (ACLY)
and as the putative active ingredient of over-the-counter Garcinia weight-loss
supplements.

## Chemistry and Classification

HCA is a structural analogue of [[Citrate]] bearing an additional hydroxyl group on the
central carbon. The naturally abundant enantiomer is the (−)-isomer; the (+)-form is
essentially inactive. It is a competitive inhibitor with respect to citrate, and its
inhibition is relieved by raising substrate concentration.

## Mechanism of Action

- **The blocked reaction** — ACLY is a cytosolic homotetramer (~1101 residues per
  subunit) that cleaves citrate exported from the mitochondrion into
  [[Acetyl-CoA]] and oxaloacetate, consuming ATP. It is the sole committed step linking
  the TCA cycle to cytosolic lipid synthesis.
- **Consequence for lipogenesis** — Removing cytosolic acetyl-CoA starves
  [[Fatty Acid Synthase| fatty acid]] and cholesterol synthesis, and reduces
  malonyl-CoA derived from it. Feeding animals a lipogenic, high-carbohydrate diet plus
  HCA suppresses fatty acid synthesis, lipogenesis, food intake and weight gain.
- **Broader metabolic effects** — HCA also promotes ketogenesis, glycogenesis,
  gluconeogenesis and lipid oxidation in rodent studies, and has been reported to reduce
  acetyl-CoA supply and lipid droplet accumulation in cultured adipocytes. Because
  acetyl-CoA is also the substrate for histone acetylation, sustained ACLY inhibition
  has epigenetic consequences, and ACLY itself is transcriptionally driven by
  [[SREBP-1c]] and sterol-regulatory elements.
- **Caloric restriction mimetic logic** — Lowering acetyl-CoA availability while
  preserving ATP signalling mimics the low-energy state of caloric restriction without
  the need for food restriction, placing HCA among the compounds catalogued in
  [[Caloric Restriction Mimetics]] alongside [[Rapamycin]], [[Resveratrol]],
  [[Spermidine]], [[Trehalose]], [[Lithium]] and [[2-deoxy-D-glucose]].

## Clinical and Practical Limitations

> [!warning] Efficacy is contested
> Human trials of Garcinia/HCA are small, short and inconsistent. A 16-week
> double-blind randomised trial in subjects with visceral-fat-accumulation obesity
> reported significant reductions in visceral, subcutaneous and total fat area by CT
> versus placebo, with no severe adverse events; other systematic reviews conclude the
> weight-loss effect is trivial and not clinically meaningful. Dose, extract
> bioavailability and the co-formulation of other ingredients vary widely between
> products.

> [!warning] Tolerability and interactions
> Garcinia products have been associated with reported [[Serotonin Syndrome]]-like
> serotonergic events, headache, nausea and dizziness, and rare cases of
> [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma|hepatic injury]]-type reactions have been described. Because
> HCA also modestly inhibits [[MAO|monoamine oxidase]]-adjacent pathways in some
> preparations, caution is advised with serotonergic drugs.

## Connections
- [[ATP Citrate Lyase]] — Hydroxycitrate's entire pharmacology is competitive inhibition
  of this cytosolic citrate-cleaving enzyme; the entire weight-loss rationale rests on
  the resulting cytosolic acetyl-CoA restriction.
- [[Caloric Restriction Mimetics]] — HCA is listed among CRMs because cutting
  de novo lipogenesis reproduces part of the low-energy signalling of dietary
  restriction without reducing intake.
- [[Acetyl-CoA]] — The metabolite whose supply HCA depletes; the same pool that feeds
  fatty acid synthesis, cholesterol synthesis and histone acetylation.
- [[Citrate]] — Citrate is both HCA's structural parent and the ACLY substrate that
  competes with it for the active site.
- [[SREBP-1c]] — SREBP-1c drives the lipogenic programme that ACLY executes, so
  transcriptional control of ACLY and pharmacological control of ACLY converge on the
  same output.
- [[Obesity]] — Hydroxycitrate's only mainstream clinical use is as an unproven
  anti-obesity supplement, with efficacy disputed across trials.
- [[Insulin Resistance]] — Lipogenesis-lowering compounds are of interest in metabolic
  disease because hepatic steatosis and insulin resistance are bidirectionally linked.
