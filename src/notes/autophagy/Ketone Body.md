# Ketone Body

**Ketone bodies** are the three water-soluble compounds the [[Liver|liver]] produces from fatty acids when carbohydrate supply is limited: **acetoacetate**, **β-hydroxybutyrate** and **acetone**. They are exported in blood to extrahepatic tissue, are converted back into [[Acetyl-CoA]] there, and enter the [[Citric Acid Cycle|citric acid cycle]] as alternative redox fuel. The synthetic process is [[Ketogenesis]]; their catabolism is *ketolysis*.

## Chemistry

| Species | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Acetoacetate** | The central, obligate intermediate. It is produced from acetyl-CoA via mitochondrial HMG-CoA synthase (mitochondrial isoform) and HMG-CoA lyase, and it is either reduced to β-hydroxybutyrate or decarboxylated to acetone. |
| **β-Hydroxybutyrate (β-OHB)** | Chemically not a ketone but a ketone body; the dominant circulating species in fasting. Its ratio to acetoacetate reflects the mitochondrial NADH/NAD⁺ state, so it is a redox readout as much as a fuel. |
| **Acetone** | A spontaneous, non-enzymatic decarboxylation product of acetoacetate; exhaled, and detectable on the breath. Its conversion to methylglyoxal by CYP2E1 during fasting or ketoacidosis links ketone metabolism to dicarbonyl chemistry. |

Hepatic production is fed by [[Fatty acid oxidation|β-oxidation]] generating acetyl-CoA and NADH; the liver cannot use ketones because it lacks **SCOT** (sulphide:quinone oxidoreductase / OXCT1), the transferase step. Extrahepatic uptake is carrier-mediated (OAT-family monocarboxylate transporters) and rate-limiting, which explains why the brain — where SCOT is absent in most regions — takes weeks of ketogenic adaptation to increase ketone use, while heart and skeletal muscle use ketones immediately.

## Signalling and Non-Fuel Roles

> [!info] Ketones as signals
> - **GPR109A (HCAR2)** on adipocytes and myeloid cells is a niacin/ketone receptor; its activation promotes [[Niacin|niacin]]-type signalling, raises cAMP, and dampens [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] and [[Aging|age-related]] myeloid phenotypes.
> - **β-OHB inhibits class I and IIa [[Histone Deacetylase|HDACs]]**, and the derived modification β-hydroxybutyrylation is deposited on histone lysine residues — a direct bridge between a metabolite and the [[Histone]]/chromatin machinery.
> - **Antioxidant effects**: ketones can raise glutathione-dependent capacity and reduce net oxidative stress in some contexts.
> - **NLRP3 inflammasome inhibition** has been reported downstream of HCAR2 signalling, linking ketone metabolism to [[Inflammation|inflammasome]] biology.

> [!warning] Double-edged
> The metabolic claim that ketones are uniformly "healthy" is contested. More recent work shows β-OHB can act as an alternative substrate that *supports* tumour growth in colourectal cancer, and that increased ketone oxidation shifts mitochondrial redox state toward NADH and ROS. Evidence quality varies by tissue and clinical context; treat universal ketone benefits as unsettled.

## Clinical Use

Ketone elevation is the objective of the [[Ketogenic Diet|ketogenic diet]] and of [[Fasting|fasting]] protocols, both of which lower glucose and insulin and raise lipolysis; the intended signalling targets are [[Autophagy]] and [[mTORC1]] suppression. Clinically validated indications remain narrow — drug-resistant epilepsy is the strongest — while type 2 diabetes, cardiometabolic disease, and cognitive or neurodegenerative applications are still being tested. Because tumour cells in many cancers express low levels of SCOT and 3β-hydroxybutyrate dehydrogenase, ketogenic strategies in oncology are explicitly double-edged.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - The Beneficial and Adverse Effects of Autophagic Response to Caloric Restriction and Fasting|The Beneficial and Adverse Effects of Autophagic Response to Caloric Restriction and Fasting]] — reports that most cancer cells carry reduced levels of the enzymes catabolising ketone bodies (SCOT and 3β-OHBD) compared with normal cells, uses ketogenic diets for weight maintenance in patients on therapy, and frames ketone-induced autophagy as beneficial in some settings and growth-supporting in others.

## Connections

- [[Ketogenesis]] — Ketogenesis is the hepatic synthetic process; ketone bodies are its products, and the two notes are read together.
- [[Hepatocyte]] — The hepatocyte is the exclusive producer of ketone bodies and, lacking SCOT, cannot consume them — the compartmental asymmetry that makes ketones a circulating fuel rather than a liver metabolite.
- [[Acetyl-CoA]] — Both entry routes into ketone metabolism terminate at acetyl-CoA: synthesis condenses acetyl-CoA units, and ketolysis regenerates acetyl-CoA before entry into the citric acid cycle.
- [[Methylglyoxal]] — During fasting and ketoacidosis, CYP2E1 oxidises acetone to methylglyoxal, coupling the ketogenic state to dicarbonyl and glycation chemistry.
- [[Histone Deacetylase]] — β-hydroxybutyrate's inhibition of class I/IIa HDACs is the metabolic-chromatin link at the centre of interest in ketone biology.
- [[Mitochondria]] — β-OHB oxidation regenerates NADH and feeds the electron transport chain; this is why ketone metabolism is simultaneously a fuel pathway and a redox variable.
- [[Autophagy]] — Ketogenic states and fasting both lower mTORC1 activity and raise autophagic flux, which is the mechanistic bridge from fasting to renewal programs in the vault.
- [[NAD+]] — Ketone-driven changes in the mitochondrial NADH/NAD⁺ ratio sit upstream of NAD⁺-dependent sirtuin signalling.
