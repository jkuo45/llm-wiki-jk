# MYC

**MYC** (c-Myc) is a bHLH-LZ transcription factor that heterodimerizes with [[MAX]] to regulate cell proliferation, growth, metabolism, and [[Autophagy]].

## Structure & Interaction
MYC contains an N-terminal transactivation domain, a central region, and a C-terminal bHLH-LZ domain. It binds E-box sequences (CACGTG) as a MYC-[[MAX]] heterodimer. [[MXD]] (Mad) family proteins compete for MAX binding, antagonizing MYC activity.

## Autophagy & Lysosomal Regulation
MYC competes with [[TFEB]] for binding to [[CLEAR Element]] motifs, repressing lysosomal and autophagy gene transcription. This antagonism positions MYC as a negative regulator of [[Autophagy]] and [[Lysosomal Biogenesis]], promoting anabolic growth over catabolic recycling. MYC overexpression suppresses the CLEAR network.

## Broader Functions
MYC controls [[Cell Cycle]] progression (via [[Cyclin D]]-[[CDK4]]/[[CDK6]], [[E2F]]), [[Ribosome Biogenesis]], [[Metabolism]] (glycolysis, glutaminolysis), and [[Apoptosis]] (via [[p53]]-[[ARF]]). It is one of the most frequently activated oncogenes in human cancer.

## Metabolic Reprogramming & OXPHOS Coupling

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]]
> MYC induces elevated [[OXPHOS]] in cancer cells, coupling mitochondrial respiration to anabolic growth. High MYC activity shifts the [[TCA cycle]] carbon source from [[Glucose uptake|glucose]] to [[Glutamine]], rendering the TCA cycle mostly glutamine carbon-driven. [[Complex I]]-regenerated [[NAD+]] sustains this glutamine-fueled TCA cycle, creating a selective vulnerability: co-inhibition of complex I and [[Glutaminolysis]] is [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic lethal]] in MYC^high^ tumors.

MYC enhances glucose catabolism and the pentose phosphate pathway while diverting glucose-derived carbon away from the TCA cycle. Instead, MYC drives [[Glutaminolysis]], promoting influx of glutamine-derived [[α-Ketoglutarate]] into the TCA cycle. This metabolic switch meets the anabolic demand of MYC-driven cells by simultaneously promoting [[OXPHOS]] for bioenergy and the TCA cycle for biomass production.

## Pathology
MYC is amplified or overexpressed in >50% of human cancers including [[Burkitt's Lymphoma]], [[Breast Cancer]], [[Colorectal Cancer]], and [[Pancreatic Cancer]]. MYC-driven tumors are often addicted to [[Glutamine Metabolism]] and show altered autophagy. In [[triple-negative breast cancer]], about half of tumors contain high proportions of MYC^high^ cells, which represent the most aggressive subtypes.
