# Erβ

**Erβ** (estrogen receptor β, encoded by ESR2) is one of the two nuclear [[Estrogen Receptor]] subtypes, along with ERα. It mediates many of the physiological actions of [[Estrogen]] in the brain, vasculature, and metabolic tissues.

## Structure and Signaling
Erβ is a ligand-activated nuclear receptor that, upon [[Estrogen]] binding, dimerizes and regulates transcription of target genes through estrogen response elements. It also engages non-genomic signaling. Erβ is expressed in the hippocampus, cortex, vascular endothelium, and reproductive tissues, with tissue distribution distinct from ERα.

## Regulation of SIRT1
Erβ signaling upregulates [[SIRT1]] expression and activity, linking estrogens to SIRT1-mediated metabolic and neuroprotective programs. Estrogen-replacement models show increased [[SIRT1]] levels and reduced oxidative damage. In the sirtuin literature, Erβ is tabulated as a positive regulator of [[SIRT1]].

> [!info] Estrogen–SIRT1 Axis
> Erβ acts as an upstream positive regulator of [[SIRT1]], coupling estrogen signaling to NAD+-dependent deacetylation, mitochondrial function, and neuroprotection — a pathway relevant to sex differences in [[Aging]] and age-related disease.

## Physiological and Clinical Relevance
Through [[SIRT1]] upregulation, Erβ signaling promotes stress resistance, mitochondrial biogenesis, and reduced [[Oxidative Stress]] in multiple tissues. This pathway is implicated in the neuroprotective and cardiometabolic benefits of estrogen and in sex-specific trajectories of [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegenerative Disease]], although the balance of ERα versus Erβ signaling is context-dependent.
