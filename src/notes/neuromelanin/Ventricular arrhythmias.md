# Ventricular arrhythmias

**Ventricular arrhythmias** are abnormal, often rapid cardiac rhythms originating in the ventricular myocardium, ranging from premature ventricular contractions to ventricular fibrillation, and are a leading cause of sudden cardiac death, particularly in the setting of [[Heart Failure]] and ischemic heart disease.

## Pathophysiology
Ventricular arrhythmias arise from re-entry circuits, abnormal automaticity, and triggered activity in the ventricular myocardium. Myocardial ischemia, [[Oxidative Stress]], ion-channel dysfunction, and structural remodeling such as [[Ventricular Remodeling]] and [[Cardiac Hypertrophy]] promote an arrhythmogenic substrate.

## Modulation by Resveratrol
[[Resveratrol]] reduces ventricular arrhythmias in preclinical models, likely via antioxidant, ion-channel, and [[Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase|eNOS]]-modulating actions. Directionally, resveratrol sits upstream of reduced arrhythmogenesis.

> [!info] Electrophysiological Protection
> The anti-arrhythmic effect of resveratrol may reflect stabilization of cardiac ion channels, reduced [[Oxidative Stress]], and improved nitric oxide signaling in the myocardium, adding an electrophysiological benefit to its cardiovascular profile.

## Clinical Context
The anti-arrhythmic effect is observed in animal studies with uncertain human translation. These findings support resveratrol's reputation as a cardioprotective compound, though relevance to human arrhythmia remains to be established in clinical settings.

## Clinical Significance
Reducing ventricular arrhythmia burden is a key goal in [[Heart Failure]], [[Cardiomyopathy]], and post-infarct care. Nutritional and pharmacological strategies that limit oxidative and ischemic injury may complement standard anti-arrhythmic therapy.
