# Cardiac Function

**Cardiac function** refers to the ability of the heart to pump blood, determined by contractility, preload, afterload, and heart rate, and reflected clinically in ejection fraction and stroke volume.

## NAD+ and Cardiac Function
NAD+ homeostasis is critical for myocardial energetics, since the heart relies heavily on [[Mitochondria]] and [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] for the ATP that powers contraction. A strong beneficial effect of [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR) was discovered in mouse models of [[Heart Failure]], with preservation of cardiac function and limitation of cardiac remodeling, effects associated with maintained [[NAD+]] levels in the heart.

> [!info] Myocardial NAD+ Restoration
> NR supplementation normalizes the myocardial NAD+/NADH ratio and, by improving NAD+ homeostasis and activating NMRK2, can prevent the deterioration of cardiac function and adverse remodeling in a mouse model of dilated [[Cardiomyopathy]] leading to [[Heart Failure]].

## Mechanisms
By replenishing NAD+ through the salvage and phosphorylation pathways, NR supports [[SIRT1]] and mitochondrial [[SIRT3]] deacetylase activity, which regulate energetics, redox homeostasis, and stress resistance in the [[Cardiomyocyte]]. These actions oppose pathological hypertrophy and [[Ventricular Remodeling]].

## Clinical Significance
The preservation of cardiac function by NAD+ precursors has generated interest in NR and related [[NAD+ Biosynthesis]] intermediates as adjunctive therapies for [[Heart Failure]] and other age-related cardiovascular decline, though clinical trials remain at early stages.
