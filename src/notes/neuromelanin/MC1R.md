# MC1R

[[MC1R]] (Melanocortin 1 Receptor) is a G protein-coupled receptor encoded by the MC1R gene on chromosome 16q24.3. Best known for regulating cutaneous pigmentation—by switching [[Melanocytes]] from pheomelanin to eumelanin synthesis—MC1R has unexpectedly emerged as a pleiotropic factor linking pigment biology to [[Parkinson's Disease]] risk and to dopaminergic neuron survival, bridging skin and brain melanogenesis.

## Structure and Canonical Function

MC1R is a seven-transmembrane [[Receptor]] coupled to Gsα, stimulating adenylyl cyclase and cAMP upon binding α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH). High cAMP activates [[Tyrosinase]] and the eumelanogenic program in [[Melanogenesis]], producing protective dark pigment. Loss-of-function variants (e.g., R151C, R160W, D294H) typical of red hair/fair skin reduce signaling, shifting synthesis toward [[Pheomelanin]]. These variants also raise [[Melanoma]] risk.

## MC1R in the Brain and Parkinson's Disease

Epidemiological studies report that MC1R variants, independent of pigmentation, associate with increased PD risk and earlier age at onset. Mechanistically, MC1R is expressed on astrocytes and neurons; its activation can dampen [[Neuroinflammation]] and oxidative injury. Red-hair variants may impair this protective signaling, leaving dopaminergic neurons of the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] more vulnerable. Intriguingly, MC1R signaling may also modulate [[Neuromelanin]]-containing neuron resilience, since both skin and brain melanogenesis share the [[Tyrosinase]]-dependent catechol oxidation pathway.

## Therapeutic Implications

MC1R agonists (e.g., α-MSH analogs, [[Melanocortins]]) are being explored preclinically for [[Neuroprotection]], reducing microglial activation and improving mitochondrial function. The melanoma–PD association—both tied to MC1R and to [[Melanocytes]]/neuromelanin biology—suggests shared pathogenic themes in pigment-cell systems. MC1R thus exemplifies how a pigment gene can influence neurodegeneration.
