# Proteinuria

**Proteinuria** is the presence of abnormally high amounts of protein in the urine, reflecting impaired [[Kidney]] filtration barrier integrity. It is a cardinal sign of glomerular disease and a strong predictor of renal and [[Cardiovascular Disease]] risk.

## Pathophysiology
Proteinuria arises when the glomerular filtration barrier — endothelial cells, basement membrane, and podocytes — is damaged, allowing albumin and other proteins to escape into the filtrate. Tubular reuptake becomes saturated, and filtered protein itself contributes to tubulointerstitial injury and fibrosis. It often accompanies reduced [[Glomerular Filtration Rate]] and is a central feature of [[Diabetic nephropathy]].

## Dietary AGEs and Proteinuria
Sebekova and colleagues found that long-term consumption of [[Advanced Glycation End Products]] (AGEs) in rats leads to a dose-dependent increase in proteinuria that over time could induce renal damage. High long-term consumption of AGEs is also associated with higher fasting glucose, insulin, and serum AGEs, as well as increased AGE localization and [[RAGE]] staining in ovarian tissue.

> [!warning] High-AGE Diet and the Kidney
> Chronic dietary AGE excess reproduces in animals some of the renal and metabolic features seen in aging and diabetes, including dose-dependent proteinuria, supporting the toxicity of a high-AGE diet to the kidney.

## Clinical Significance
Proteinuria is a prognostic biomarker that guides therapy in diabetic and hypertensive nephropathy, where interventions (e.g., ACE inhibitors) target its reduction. The AGE–proteinuria link connects dietary glycation load to [[Aging]], [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], and progressive renal dysfunction.
