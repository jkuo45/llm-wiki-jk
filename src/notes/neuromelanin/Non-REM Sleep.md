# Non-REM Sleep

**Non-REM (NREM) sleep** is the phase of sleep characterized by slow, synchronized cortical activity and reduced arousal, and is subdivided into stages N1–N3. It is essential for restorative functions including synaptic homeostasis and metabolic clearance. NREM alternates with REM sleep across the [[Circadian Rhythm]].

## Regulation
NREM sleep is generated by sleep-promoting neurons, including the ventrolateral preoptic area (VLPO) of the hypothalamus, which inhibit wake-promoting regions. The [[GABA_A receptor]]-mediated inhibitory neurotransmission of [[GABA]] is central to NREM sleep generation, and benzodiazepines promote sleep through this receptor.

## Modulation by Honokiol
[[Honokiol]] promotes NREM sleep and shortens sleep latency. In mouse studies, honokiol at 10 and 20 mg/kg significantly decreased sleep latency and increased the amount of NREM sleep without affecting REM sleep or EEG power density, and it excited sleep-promoting neurons in the ventrolateral preoptic area. These effects occur through the benzodiazepine site of the [[GABA_A receptor]].

> [!info] GABAergic Sleep Promotion
> Honokiol's NREM-promoting action is mediated at the benzodiazepine site of the [[GABA_A receptor]], exciting VLPO sleep-promoting neurons and shortening sleep latency without altering REM sleep or EEG power density.

## Clinical Significance
The combined sleep-promoting and anxiolytic actions of honokiol suggest a potential role as an anesthetic adjunct. Enhancing NREM sleep is also relevant to cognitive function, metabolic health, and the clearance of metabolic waste from the brain, connecting sleep to [[Aging]] and neurodegeneration.
