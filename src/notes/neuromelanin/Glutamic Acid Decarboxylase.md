# Glutamic Acid Decarboxylase

**Glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD)** is the enzyme that catalyzes the decarboxylation of [[Glutamate]] to synthesize [[GABA]], the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain. It exists as two isoforms, GAD65 and GAD67, encoded by separate genes.

## Structure and Function
GAD requires pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (vitamin B6) as a cofactor. GAD67 is constitutively active and maintains basal [[GABA]] levels, whereas GAD65 is associated with synaptic vesicles and provides GABA for regulated release. Both isoforms are highly expressed in GABAergic [[Neuron]] populations.

## Modulation by Honokiol
[[Honokiol]] increases the activity of glutamic acid decarboxylase in the cortex and hippocampus of mice. By enhancing GAD65 and GAD67 activity, honokiol can augment the brain's synthesis of the inhibitory neurotransmitter [[GABA]], which may complement its direct positive allosteric modulation of [[GABA_A receptor]]s.

> [!info] Dual GABAergic Mechanism
> Honokiol enhances both GABA synthesis (via increased GAD activity) and GABA signaling (via [[GABA_A receptor]] positive allosteric modulation). This combined effect on GABAergic tone is consistent with honokiol's central depressant, anticonvulsant, anxiolytic, and sleep-promoting pharmacological profile.

## Clinical Relevance
Reduced GAD activity is associated with epilepsy and anxiety disorders, making GAD a therapeutic node for enhancing inhibitory tone. GAD is also a major autoantigen in stiff-person syndrome and type 1 diabetes, where anti-GAD antibodies are diagnostic markers. Honokiol's GAD enhancement is relevant to its [[Epilepsy]]-relevant and anxiolytic actions.
