# Haloperidol

[[Haloperidol]] is a first-generation (typical) antipsychotic of the butyrophenone class, widely used to treat schizophrenia, acute psychosis, and agitation, and for antiemesis. It exerts its therapeutic effect primarily as a potent antagonist of the [[D2 receptor]] (dopamine D2 receptor) in the mesolimbic and nigrostriatal pathways. Beyond receptor blockade, haloperidol has drawn neurotoxicological interest because it can be metabolically converted into the pyridinium neurotoxin [[HPP+]].

## Pharmacodynamics and Pharmacokinetics
Haloperidol blocks central D2-like receptors, relieving positive psychotic symptoms but also disrupting the nigrostriatal dopaminergic tone that regulates movement—accounting for its high liability for extrapyramidal side effects and drug-induced parkinsonism. It is extensively metabolized in the liver by CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 (via reduction, N-dealkylation, and oxidation) and undergoes carbonyl reduction. A minor but consequential pathway generates the pyridinium ion HPP+ (4-(4-chlorophenyl)-1-[4-(4-fluorophenyl)-4-oxobutyl]pyridinium).

## Chemical Process: Formation of HPP+
Haloperidol's keto-reduced metabolite is oxidized to a pyridinium species structurally analogous to [[MPP+]] (the active toxin of [[MPTP]]). Like MPP+, HPP+ is a charged, membrane-impermeant cation that can be taken up by dopaminergic neurons via the dopamine transporter and accumulates in mitochondria, where it inhibits complex I of the electron transport chain. This mechanistic parallelism to MPTP/MPP+ provides a molecular rationale for haloperidol-associated parkinsonism and for rare cases of tardive dyskinesia and neurotoxicity.

## Physiological and Clinical Relevance
Haloperidol remains valuable clinically, but its dopaminergic blockade produces acute dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, and, with chronic use, tardive dyskinesia. The HPP+ hypothesis adds a mitochondrial/oxidative dimension: if HPP+ forms in sufficient amounts, it may directly damage the same [[Substantia Nigra]] [[Neuromelanin]] neurons that degenerate in [[Parkinson's Disease]]. This has motivated research into whether haloperidol use accelerates PD risk or severity, and into whether CYP2D6 genotype (affecting HPP+ formation) modulates vulnerability.

## Pathology and Research Implications
The structural kinship between HPP+ and MPP+ makes haloperidol a useful pharmacological tool for modeling selective dopaminergic neurotoxicity, and a cautionary example of how an antipsychotic can engage [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Oxidative Stress]]. Mitigation strategies include using lower-potency or atypical antipsychotics when parkinsonism risk is a concern. Overall, haloperidol illustrates the intersection of receptor pharmacology and metabolic neurotoxicology in catecholaminergic neuron injury.
