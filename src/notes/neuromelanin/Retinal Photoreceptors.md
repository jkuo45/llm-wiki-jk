# Retinal Photoreceptors

**Retinal photoreceptors** are the light-sensitive [[Neuron|neurons]] of the retina — rods for dim-light vision and cones for color and high-acuity vision. They have extremely high metabolic demand and densely packed [[Mitochondria]], making them exceptionally vulnerable to energetic and oxidative stress.

## Mitochondrial Dependence
Photoreceptors rely on [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] for the ATP required for phototransduction and outer segment renewal. [[SIRT3]] and [[SIRT5]] are both critical for the health of retinal photoreceptors. SIRT3 is a mitochondrial NAD+-dependent deacetylase that supports ATP homeostasis, [[ROS]] detoxification, and neuroprotection.

## NAD+ and Degeneration
SIRT3 activity is sensitive to the reduction of [[NAD+]] content detected in multiple disorders with retinal degeneration, including age-associated dysfunction, [[Diabetic Retinopathy]], and light-induced degeneration in mice.

> [!info] NAD+–SIRT3 in Photoreceptor Survival
> Increasing mitochondrial [[NAD+]] to support [[SIRT3]] is one proposed mechanism of [[Nicotinamide Riboside]]'s neuroprotective effects. This suggests a potential therapeutic role for NR in disorders that include photoreceptor degeneration.

## Clinical Relevance
Photoreceptor loss underlies the irreversible vision loss of retinitis pigmentosa, macular degeneration, and [[Diabetic Retinopathy]]. Strategies that maintain mitochondrial NAD+, such as NAD+ precursor supplementation, are being investigated to preserve photoreceptor viability and function.
