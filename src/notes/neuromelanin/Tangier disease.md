# Tangier disease

**Tangier disease** is a rare autosomal-recessive disorder caused by loss-of-function mutations in [[ABCA1]], the cholesterol transporter central to [[Cholesterol Efflux]]. It is characterized by extremely low [[HDL]] and accumulation of cholesteryl esters in [[Macrophages]] and other tissues.

## Genetic Basis
[[ABCA1]] mutations cause Tangier disease; the defect blocks cholesterol efflux, preventing transfer of cellular cholesterol to apolipoprotein A-I for HDL particle formation. This produces the hallmark extremely low HDL and tissue lipid accumulation.

## Clinical Presentation
The transport defect leads to characteristic enlarged orange tonsils (from cholesteryl ester-laden macrophages), hepatosplenomegaly, and peripheral neuropathy. Corneal clouding and premature [[Atherosclerosis]] may also occur. The accumulation of lipid-laden macrophages reflects impaired reverse cholesterol transport from peripheral tissues.

> [!info] Gene-Dosage Spectrum
> Incomplete versus complete loss of function yields a spectrum of severity, and modifier genes influence phenotype. Heterozygotes show intermediate HDL reduction, illustrating a gene-dosage effect and the clinical spectrum between Tangier disease and familial HDL deficiency.

## Pathophysiology
Loss of ABCA1-mediated [[Cholesterol Efflux]] produces [[Foam Cells]] in tonsils, spleen, and arterial walls. The resulting disruption of [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]] links the disease to lipid metabolism and [[Atherosclerosis]]. Directionally, transporter loss leads to lipid accumulation and neuropathy.

## Clinical Significance
Tangier disease is a natural experiment establishing ABCA1 as critical for HDL biogenesis and [[Cholesterol Efflux]], with implications for [[HDL]] metabolism, cardiovascular risk, and macrophage cholesterol handling.
