# Noise-Induced Hearing Loss

**Noise-induced hearing loss (NIHL)** is sensorineural hearing loss caused by exposure to loud sound, which damages cochlear hair cells and auditory nerve fibers through mechanical and metabolic stress, including [[Oxidative Stress]] and mitochondrial dysfunction.

## Pathophysiology
Acoustic overstimulation generates excessive reactive oxygen species ([[ROS]]) in the cochlea, triggering hair cell apoptosis, synaptic loss between hair cells and spiral ganglion neurons, and neurite retraction. The resulting [[Axonal Degeneration]] of auditory nerve fibers contributes to permanent threshold shifts.

## NAD+ and Prevention
[[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR) prevents noise-induced hearing loss and neurite retraction from hair cells in the inner ear through a [[SIRT3]]-dependent mechanism, in which the NR-induced increase in [[NAD+]] supports the activity of the mitochondrial deacetylase [[SIRT3]].

> [!info] SIRT3-Mediated Protection
> NR's elevation of cochlear [[NAD+]] activates the mitochondrial deacetylase [[SIRT3]], which supports ATP homeostasis and ROS detoxification, protecting hair cells and their neurites from noise-induced degeneration.

## Broader Relevance
This illustrates the broader neuroprotective and therapeutic potential of NR, which also protects neurons from excitotoxicity-induced [[Axonal Degeneration]]. SIRT3 activity is sensitive to NAD+ reductions seen in retinal degeneration disorders, and NR may therefore have applications for a variety of sensory and neurodegenerative conditions, including [[Diabetic Retinopathy]] and [[Retinal Ganglion Cells|retinal ganglion cell]] dysfunction.
