# Ifosfamide-induced Encephalopathy

**Ifosfamide-induced encephalopathy** is an acute neurotoxic syndrome caused by the alkylating chemotherapeutic [[Ifosfamide]], manifesting as confusion, somnolence, hallucinations, seizures, and in severe cases coma. It is caused by the metabolite chloroacetaldehyde, which depletes central [[Glutathione]] and impairs neuronal metabolism.

## Pathophysiology
Ifosfamide is metabolized by hepatic cytochromes, and its neurotoxic metabolite chloroacetaldehyde interferes with mitochondrial respiration and amino acid metabolism in the brain, disrupting the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] and causing cerebral dysfunction. Risk factors include renal impairment, hypoalbuminemia, and prior neurotoxicity.

## Treatment with Methylene Blue
Methylene blue is widely used off-label for the treatment of ifosfamide-induced encephalopathy. For this indication, methylene blue is typically administered intravenously at a dosage of 50 mg every 4 hours until symptom resolution, a regimen supported by case series and institutional experience. Dosing is individualized based on clinical response, with symptom resolution often occurring within 10 minutes to 24 hours.

> [!tip] Clinical Regimen
> Intravenous methylene blue (commonly 50 mg every 4 hours) is the standard off-label intervention for ifosfamide-induced encephalopathy, with most patients improving within 10 minutes to 24 hours; prophylaxis may be used in high-risk patients.

## Clinical Significance
Prompt recognition and treatment with methylene blue reduce the morbidity of this reversible neurotoxicity. The efficacy is attributed to methylene blue's redox properties, which accept electrons and restore mitochondrial function, mitigating the metabolic disturbance caused by chloroacetaldehyde. Methylene blue also inhibits [[Monoamine oxidase]], further altering neurotransmitter balance.
