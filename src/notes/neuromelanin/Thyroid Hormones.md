# Thyroid Hormones

**Thyroid hormones** — chiefly [[Triiodothyronine]] (T3) and thyroxine (T4) — are the principal endocrine regulators of metabolic rate. They set the metabolic tone of the body by regulating mitochondrial oxygen consumption and cellular [[Sodium Potassium ATPase|sodium-potassium ATPase]] activity in most tissues.

## Metabolic Actions
Thyroid hormones increase the transcription of genes involved in [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] biogenesis and [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]], raising basal oxygen consumption. They stimulate [[Sodium Potassium ATPase]] activity, contributing substantially to cellular energy expenditure, and influence the activity of uncoupling proteins and substrate cycles, all of which feed into whole-body energy expenditure.

## Thyroid Status and Resting Metabolic Rate
[[Resting Metabolic Rate]] (RMR) is modulated by thyroid hormones. Both hypothyroid and hyperthyroid states shift RMR substantially, and within the normal range thyroid status contributes to inter-individual variability in resting metabolic rate.

> [!info] Thyroid and RMR
> Hypothyroid states lower RMR while hyperthyroid states raise it; even within the euthyroid range, thyroid hormone levels explain part of the inter-individual variance in resting metabolic rate.

## Limits of Hormonal Regulation
However, training-induced changes in RMR are generally not mediated by changes in thyroid hormone levels, and short-term exercise does not consistently alter circulating thyroid hormone concentrations, indicating that the post-exercise RMR elevation is driven by other factors, such as [[Proton Leak]] and sympathetic activity. This dissociation is important for interpreting metabolic adaptation to exercise.

## Clinical Significance
Thyroid hormone excess or deficiency produces profound metabolic phenotypes, connecting thyroid status to [[Energy Balance]], obesity, and the metabolic complications of aging. Thyroid signaling also interacts with [[Mitochondria]]-centered processes such as [[Proton Leak]] and adaptive [[Thermogenesis]].
