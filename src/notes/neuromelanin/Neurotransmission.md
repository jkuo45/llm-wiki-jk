# Neurotransmission

[[Neurotransmission]] is the process by which a presynaptic neuron releases chemical messengers—[[Neurotransmitters]]—that diffuse across the [[Synapse]] and bind postsynaptic (or presynaptic autoreceptor) receptors to alter the electrical state of the target cell. It is the fundamental unit of intercellular communication in the nervous system. In the neuromelanin field, the catecholaminergic transmission mediated by [[Dopamine]], [[Norepinephrine]], and [[Epinephrine]] is of central interest, because the same molecules that signal also serve as precursors to [[Neuromelanin]] and as substrates for oxidative stress.

## The Synaptic Cycle

1. **Synthesis & storage:** Catecholamines are synthesized from [[L-Tyrosine]] → [[L-DOPA]] → dopamine (and further to norepinephrine/epinephrine) and packaged into synaptic vesicles by VMAT2.
2. **Release:** An action potential triggers Ca²⁺ influx, vesicle fusion, and exocytosis of transmitter into the cleft.
3. **Receptor activation:** Dopamine acts on D1/D2-family [[Receptor|receptors]]; norepinephrine/epinephrine on adrenergic receptors, modulating excitability and second-messenger cascades.
4. **Termination:** Reuptake via the [[Dopamine Transporter]]/NET and enzymatic breakdown by [[Monoamine oxidase B]] and COMT clear the signal.

## Link to Neuromelanin and Oxidative Stress

High rates of catecholamine turnover in pigmented neurons mean abundant cytosolic dopamine and its oxidation products ([[Aminochromes]], dopaquinone). Spontaneous auto-oxidation and MAO-B catabolism generate [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] and quinones that polymerize into [[Neuromelanin]]. Thus the very efficiency of dopaminergic [[Neurotransmission]] fuels pigment formation and redox burden—a trade-off that may explain the selective vulnerability of these neurons in [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Relevance to Neurotoxins and Disease

Toxins exploit transmission: [[MPP+]] hijacks the [[Dopamine Transporter]] to enter neurons, and [[Manganese]] disrupts GABA/dopamine balance. Loss of dopaminergic transmission underlies the motor signs of PD, treated by boosting signaling with [[L-DOPA]]. Understanding transmission dynamics is therefore inseparable from understanding pigment and degeneration.

## Research Techniques

Electrophysiology, amperometry, optogenetics, and microdialysis are used to study catecholamine release and uptake, while [[PET scan]] and DAT imaging quantify presynaptic integrity in vivo.
