# Arterial Thrombosis

**Arterial thrombosis** is the pathological formation of a platelet-rich thrombus within an artery, typically over ruptured or eroded atherosclerotic plaques, and is a leading cause of [[Ischemic Stroke]] and myocardial infarction.

## Pathophysiology
Arterial thrombi are dominated by platelets and fibrin rather than the red-cell–rich clots of venous thromboembolism. Endothelial injury exposes subendothelial collagen, triggering platelet adhesion, activation, and aggregation, with thrombin generation stabilizing the clot. Risk factors include [[Atherosclerosis]], smoking, hypertension, diabetes, and dyslipidemia.

## Honokiol and Antithrombotic Activity
The lignan [[Honokiol]] is a potent inhibitor of arterial thrombosis, acting through endothelial cell protection and stimulation of prostacyclin production, which opposes platelet activation and vasoconstriction. This antithrombotic effect complements its neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory profile.

> [!warning] Bleeding Risk
> Honokiol's antithrombotic action is a safety consideration. Caution is advised in coagulopathic patients, those with hemorrhagic stroke or hemorrhagic transformation after [[Ischemic Stroke]], patients on [[Warfarin]] or low-molecular-weight heparin, and individuals with clotting disorders such as hemophilia or von Willebrand deficiency. The bleeding risk must be weighed against the neuroprotective benefits before clinical use.

## Clinical Significance
The antiplatelet, antithrombotic, and endothelial-protective actions position honokiol as a candidate adjunct for vascular and [[Cardiovascular Disease]], though clinical translation remains preliminary.
