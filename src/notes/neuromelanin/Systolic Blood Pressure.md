# Systolic Blood Pressure

**Systolic blood pressure (SBP)** is the peak arterial pressure reached during ventricular systole, reflecting cardiac output and arterial stiffness. It is a major modifiable risk factor for stroke, [[Myocardial Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury|myocardial infarction]], and [[Cardiovascular Disease]], and [[Hypertension]] is defined largely by elevated SBP.

## NAD+ and Vascular Function
[[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR) supplementation reduces systolic blood pressure and aortic stiffness, two clinically important risk indicators of cardiovascular function and health. These effects may occur through nuclear and cytosolic [[SIRT1]] activation, since [[NAD+]] is an obligate substrate for the deacetylase [[SIRT1]], which has been implicated in the maintenance of healthy vascular function, including endothelium-dependent vascular relaxation.

> [!info] SIRT1 and the Vasculature
> NAD+ supports [[SIRT1]]-mediated deacetylation of endothelial targets, promoting [[Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase|eNOS]] activity and nitric-oxide–dependent vasodilation, which lowers [[Systolic Blood Pressure]] and arterial stiffness.

## Mechanisms
In mouse models, NR-induced reduction of SBP and aortic stiffness was documented alongside improved [[NAD+]] homeostasis. Elevated NAD+ boosts [[SIRT1]] activity in the vascular endothelium, increasing [[Nitric Oxide]] bioavailability, improving [[Endothelial Dysfunction|endothelial function]], and reducing aortic stiffening. These changes are captured clinically by [[Pulse Wave Velocity]], a measure of arterial stiffness.

## Clinical Significance
Reducing SBP is a cornerstone of cardiovascular risk reduction. NAD+ precursors represent a promising, emerging strategy for improving vascular aging and lowering SBP, complementing established antihypertensive therapies.
