# Major histocompatibility complex class I

[[Major histocompatibility complex class I]] (MHC-I) molecules are cell-surface glycoproteins present on virtually all nucleated cells. They sample intracellular peptides (8–10 residues) generated by the proteasome, load them onto the peptide-binding groove via the [[TAP]] transporter in the endoplasmic reticulum, and present them to CD8⁺ cytotoxic [[Lymphocytes]]. In the healthy brain, MHC-I expression is low and tightly regulated; its aberrant re-expression on neurons has emerged as a surprising feature of [[Neuroinflammation]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Structure and Classical Function

MHC-I is a heterodimer of a polymorphic heavy chain (encoded by HLA-A, -B, -C in humans) non-covalently associated with β₂-microglobulin. The peptide-receptive complex surveys the intracellular proteome so that infected or malignant cells can be eliminated by cytotoxic T cells. In the CNS, synaptic MHC-I also participates in activity-dependent refinement of neural circuits during development.

## MHC-I in Parkinson's Disease and Neuromelanin

Post-mortem studies of PD brains reveal upregulated neuronal MHC-I in the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]], particularly in [[Neuromelanin]]-containing dopaminergic neurons. Several mechanisms converge: (1) [[Oxidative Stress]] and proteasome inhibition increase misfolded-protein presentation; (2) [[Alpha-synuclein]] aggregates and extracellular [[Neuromelanin]] released from dying neurons activate microglia, which secrete IFN-γ that induces MHC-I; and (3) the pigment itself may serve as a source of neo-antigens or oxidized peptides. Surface MHC-I could therefore render pigmented neurons visible to infiltrating or perivascular cytotoxic T cells, adding an adaptive immune dimension to a historically "purely innate" neurodegenerative process.

## Pathological and Therapeutic Implications

The MHC-I axis implies that [[Neuroinflammation]] in PD is not limited to microglia but may include antigen-specific lymphocyte attack. Genetic association studies have linked certain HLA haplotypes to PD risk, consistent with immune involvement. Therapeutically, modulating MHC-I presentation or T-cell trafficking is being explored, though care is required not to compromise immune surveillance. This reframes [[Neuromelanin]] not only as a redox buffer but as a potential immunogenic cue.
