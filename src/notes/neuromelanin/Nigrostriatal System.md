# Nigrostriatal System

The [[Nigrostriatal System]] is one of the four major dopaminergic pathways of the brain, projecting from the [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] (SNpc) in the midbrain to the dorsal striatum ([[Caudate Nucleus]] and [[Putamen]]). It is the principal circuit governing voluntary motor control and is the system whose degeneration defines [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD). The heavy [[Neuromelanin]] content of SNpc neurons is closely linked to their selective vulnerability.

## Anatomy and Circuit Organization

The SNpc contains large, heavily pigmented dopaminergic neurons (A9 cell group) whose axons travel through the median forebrain bundle to terminate in the striatum, where dopamine is released from [[Synaptic Vesicles]] into the synaptic cleft. The striatum is the input nucleus of the [[Basal Ganglia]], receiving glutamatergic input from cortex and projecting via the direct and indirect pathways to the globus pallidus and substantia nigra pars reticulata, ultimately modulating thalamocortical motor output. Dopamine acts on D1 receptors (direct pathway, facilitating movement) and D2 receptors (indirect pathway, suppressing movement), maintaining a balanced motor set-point.

## Dopamine Synthesis, Storage, and Release

Within SNpc neurons, dopamine is synthesized from [[L-DOPA]] by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, packaged into vesicles by [[Vesicular Monoamine Transporter 2]] (VMAT2), and released in an activity-dependent manner. Cytoplasmic dopamine that escapes vesicular storage is vulnerable to auto-oxidation, generating [[Quinones]] and contributing to [[Neuromelanin]] accumulation over the lifespan. The tight regulation of cytosolic dopamine by VMAT2 is a key determinant of both pigment load and neuronal resilience.

## Selective Vulnerability and Parkinson's Disease

The progressive, asymmetric loss of nigrostriatal dopaminergic terminals produces the cardinal motor triad of PD: resting tremor, [[Rigidity]], and [[Bradykinesia]]. Postmortem PD brains show >50–70% loss of striatal dopamine and marked depletion of pigmented SNpc neurons. Multiple factors converge on this vulnerability: high dopamine turnover (a source of [[Oxidative Stress]]), high neuromelanin and metal burden, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] (especially complex I deficiency), and impaired [[Lysosomal Dysfunction|lysosomal]] clearance of [[Alpha-synuclein]]. The [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]] hypothesis posits that lifelong pigment accumulation ultimately overwhelms degradative capacity in these neurons.

## Research and Clinical Relevance

The nigrostriatal system is the target of nearly all current PD pharmacotherapy: [[L-DOPA]] replenishes striatal dopamine, while dopamine agonists and MAO-B/[[COMT]] inhibitors modify its signaling or metabolism. In vivo imaging of this pathway—using [[Positron Emission Tomography]] with dopamine transporter or vesicular tracers—provides early diagnosis and progression monitoring. Deep brain stimulation of downstream basal ganglia targets compensates for the circuit imbalance.
