# Glomerular Filtration Rate

**Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** is the volume of plasma filtered by the glomeruli per unit time and is the standard measure of [[Kidney]] function. Estimated GFR (eGFR) is derived from serum [[Creatinine]] and is used clinically to stage chronic kidney disease.

## Renal Function and AGE Clearance
The [[Kidney]] is central to regulating circulating levels of advanced glycation end products (AGEs), since declining kidney function reduces AGE clearance. Vlassara and colleagues studied 325 healthy participants and 66 participants with kidney disease and found that serum CML and methylglyoxal were higher in older participants, and that serum [[CML]] correlated negatively with estimated GFR and positively with age.

> [!info] Kidney–AGE Axis
> The inverse relationship between [[CML]] and eGFR indicates that the kidney is central to regulating circulating [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGE]] levels. Age-related decline in renal function contributes to the progressive accumulation of AGEs in the elderly.

## Clinical Significance
Reduced eGFR identifies reduced excretory reserve, and the resulting AGE accumulation drives [[Inflammation]], [[Oxidative Stress]], and vascular damage, connecting declining GFR to cardiovascular risk. [[Albuminuria]] and falling GFR are complementary markers of nephron injury in conditions such as [[Diabetic nephropathy]].

## Relationships
Age-related GFR decline is a normal physiologic trajectory, but accelerated decline is pathological. Monitoring eGFR alongside markers like [[CML]] and [[Methylglyoxal (MG)]] helps capture the interaction between renal aging and glycation stress.
