# Synaptic plasticity

[[Synaptic plasticity]] is the capacity of synapses to change their strength in response to patterns of activity. It is the cellular substrate of learning, memory, and adaptive motor control. In the basal ganglia, dopaminergic signaling from [[Neuromelanin]]-bearing [[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] neurons powerfully modulates striatal plasticity, and its disruption is a core feature of [[Parkinson's Disease]] (PD).

## Forms and Mechanisms

The best-characterized forms are long-term potentiation (LTP) and long-term depression (LTD), mediated by alterations in AMPA/NMDA receptor number, trafficking, and presynaptic release probability. Dopamine acts as a permissive, neuromodulatory signal: via D1 receptors it favors LTP in the direct pathway (facilitating movement), while D2 receptor activation promotes LTD in the indirect pathway. Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) further shapes circuits by correlating pre- and postsynaptic firing. Underlying molecular cascades involve Ca²⁺ influx, CaMKII, protein kinase A, and local protein synthesis at dendrites.

## Neuromelanin and Dopamine Modulation

Because the dopamine that drives striatal plasticity is released by neurons laden with [[Neuromelanin]], the pigment indirectly governs plasticity. Neuromelanin buffers cytosolic catechols and metals, but as it accumulates toward the [[Pathogenic Threshold of Neuromelanin]], associated [[Oxidative Stress]] and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] can dysregulate dopamine release and receptor signaling. Reduced vesicular storage by [[Vesicular Monoamine Transporter 2]] raises cytosolic dopamine and [[Quinones]], perturbing the neuromodulatory tone required for normal plasticity. Thus pigment load and plasticity are mechanistically coupled.

## Plasticity in Parkinson's Disease

In PD, loss of nigrostriatal terminals flattens dopaminergic modulation, degrading the balance of striatal LTP/LTD and impairing motor learning, habit formation, and compensation. This synaptic failure precedes massive cell loss and underlies [[Bradykinesia]] and [[Rigidity]] before structural degeneration is complete. Notably, [[L-DOPA]] and dopamine agonists can restore some plasticity, but fluctuating plasma levels may induce maladaptive plasticity (dyskinesias).

## Research Relevance

Therapeutics aiming to preserve or reinstate physiological synaptic plasticity—rather than simply elevating dopamine—are a frontier in PD research, including glutamatergic modulators, [[Deep Brain Stimulation]], and α-synuclein-targeted agents that protect [[Synaptic Vesicles]] and [[Alpha-synuclein]]-mediated vesicle cycling.
