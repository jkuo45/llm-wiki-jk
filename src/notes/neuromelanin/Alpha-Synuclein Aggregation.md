# Alpha-Synuclein Aggregation

[[Alpha-Synuclein Aggregation]] is the process by which soluble [[Alpha-synuclein]] monomers misfold and self-associate into oligomers, protofibrils, and insoluble fibrils. These aggregates are the principal component of [[Lewy Bodies]] and are considered a central driver of neuronal dysfunction in [[Parkinson's Disease]].

## Overview

α-Synuclein is an intrinsically disordered, natively unfolded protein that normally populates the presynaptic terminal, where it regulates SNARE complex assembly and vesicle cycling. Under conditions of increased concentration, oxidative modification, or mutation (e.g., [[A53T]]), the protein samples β-sheet-rich conformations that nucleate aggregation. The process follows a nucleation–elongation kinetics: a rate-limiting nucleation step gives way to rapid fibril growth.

## Mechanism of Aggregation

The aggregation pathway proceeds from monomer → dimer/oligomer → protofibril → fibril → higher-order assembly. The membrane-bound α-helical form competes with the cytotoxic β-sheet form; factors such as [[Oxidative Stress]], metal ions (iron, copper), and catecholamine-derived quinones from [[Neuromelanin]] metabolism can stabilize aggregation-prone intermediates. Mature fibrils deposited in Lewy bodies are relatively inert, whereas soluble oligomers are increasingly viewed as the most toxic species, impairing [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], proteasome activity, and synaptic function.

## Seeding and Propagation

α-Synuclein aggregates can template the conversion of native protein in a prion-like manner, supporting the "braak staging" hypothesis that pathology spreads anatomically from the olfactory bulb and [[Locus Coeruleus]]/[[Substantia Nigra Pars Compacta]] along connectomic routes. Released aggregates may be taken up by neighboring neurons, perpetuating spread.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

Aggregation severity correlates with dopaminergic neuron loss and motor symptoms such as [[Bradykinesia]]. Therapeutic strategies target aggregation at multiple points: reducing SNCA expression (antisense oligonucleotides, gene silencing), stabilizing monomers, blocking seeding, enhancing clearance via [[Autophagy]] and [[Lysosomal Dysfunction]] correction (e.g., [[Ambroxol]] for GBA1-linked disease), and active/passive immunotherapy to clear aggregates. Biomarkers such as α-synuclein in cerebrospinal fluid and seed-amplification assays are entering clinical use.
