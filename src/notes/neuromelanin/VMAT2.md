# VMAT2

**VMAT2** (vesicular monoamine transporter 2, gene *SLC18A2*) is the
presynaptic vesicular uptake transporter of monoaminergic neurons. It uses a
proton gradient generated by a V-type ATPase to move cytosolic monoamines from
the cytoplasm into the lumen of small synaptic vesicles, and it is the rate-
limiting step in vesicular release.

> [!important] Why the transporter matters as much as the transmitter
> Cytosolic monoamine that is not packaged is not stored and not protected —
> it is the substrate for monoamine oxidase. VMAT2 therefore determines not only
> how much transmitter is released, but how much oxidises spontaneously between
> release events. This storage-protection role is the mechanistic bridge between
> dopamine handling and the oxidative chemistry of the neuromelanin and
> adrenochrome pathways.

## Structure and Mechanism

VMAT2 is a 12-transmembrane-domain integral membrane protein of the major
facilitator superfamily, an antiporter that exchanges monoamine (out) for two
protons (in). Two isoforms exist: **VMAT1**, largely restricted to endocrine
and peripheral secretory cells including adrenal chromaffin cells and platelets,
and **VMAT2**, the neuronal isoform, confined to the brain monoaminergic
neurons — noradrenergic, serotonergic, and the dopaminergic cells of the
[[Substantia Nigra]], [[Ventral Tegmental Area]], and [[Striatum]].

Upstream synthesis is the tyrosine hydroxylase step converting [[Tyrosine]] to
L-DOPA, then [[Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase|Aromatic L-amino acid
decarboxylase]] to dopamine; dopamine is then a substrate for VMAT2 and for
[[Monoamine oxidase]] in the cytosol.

## Pharmacology

Blockade depletes stored monoamine rather than blocking the receptor, which
distinguishes VMAT2 inhibitors from receptor antagonists.

- **Reserpine** — the classic irreversible VMAT inhibitor, once used
  antihypertensively and as a psychotropic; its central and peripheral actions
  produce depression and parkinsonism from monoamine depletion.
- **Tetrabenazine, deutetrabenazine, valbenazine** — the modern
  tetrabenazine-class VMAT2 inhibitors, FDA-approved for
  [[Dyskinesia|tardive dyskinesia]] associated with chronic dopamine
  receptor blockade (e.g. from [[Haloperidol]]). Their mechanism is described as
  reducing dopamine release in the motor striatum, shifting the balance from
  "go" to "stop" circuits.
- **Deprenyl and selegiline** act at monoamine oxidase rather than at VMAT2,
  and their anti-parkinsonian effect likewise comes from raising
  cytosolic/vesicular dopamine availability.

> [!warning] Not interchangeable with dopamine receptor blockers
> VMAT2 inhibition causes depletion and therefore a withdrawal-type syndrome —
> depression, akathisia, parkinsonism, sedation. Dopamine receptor blockade
> instead causes compensatory hyperdyskinesia. The therapeutic logic is
> opposite even though both drugs are used for abnormal movement.

## Relevance to Dopaminergic Degeneration

Because cytosolic dopamine is the substrate both for
[[Dopaminochrome]]/[[Neuromelanin]] chemistry and for oxidative stress, VMAT2
occupancy is a modifier of dopaminergic vulnerability. Transgenic animals
overexpressing VMAT2 show greater resistance to MPTP-induced
[[Parkinson's Disease|parkinsonism]] and to dopamine-induced oxidative stress,
consistent with the idea that tighter vesicular sequestration is
neuroprotective — one of the arguments raised for vesicular storage as a
therapeutic target in neurodegeneration. Conversely, loss of VMAT2 function
depletes stores and produces a parkinsonian phenotype.

## Connections
- [[Dopamine]] — dopamine is the principal CNS substrate of VMAT2, and VMAT2-dependent vesicular sequestration is the main defence against cytosolic dopamine oxidation.
- [[Monoamine oxidase]] — competes with VMAT2 for the same cytosolic monoamine pool, so vesicular uptake efficiency directly determines how much substrate MAO sees.
- [[Substantia Nigra]] — the nigrostriatal dopamine neurons are uniquely dependent on VMAT2 for both transmission and protection from dopamine-derived oxidative chemistry.
- [[Dopaminochrome]] — the quinone intermediate that arises when cytosolic dopamine autoxidises is the gateway to both neuromelanin and adrenochrome-type chemistry.
- [[Parkinson's Disease]] — VMAT2 activity determines susceptibility of dopaminergic neurons to dopamine-induced oxidative injury, and VMAT2 inhibitors themselves can produce a parkinsonian syndrome.
- [[Dyskinesia]] — tardive dyskinesia is the one indication where depleting stored dopamine by blocking VMAT2 is the therapeutic mechanism rather than a side effect.
- [[Synapse]] — VMAT2 defines the vesicular pool that the action potential releases; it sits upstream of release, not at the receptor.
