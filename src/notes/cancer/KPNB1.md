# KPNB1

## Overview

**KPNB1** (Karyopherin Subunit Beta 1), also known as importin-β1, is a nuclear transport receptor that mediates import of proteins containing nuclear localization signals (NLS) into the nucleus. It was identified as a druggable oncogene in epithelial ovarian cancer through in vivo loss-of-function screens (Kodama et al., *PNAS* 2017, PMID: 28811376).

## Function

KPNB1 is the primary importin responsible for transporting cargo proteins from the cytoplasm into the nucleus. It forms a heterodimer with importin-α to recognize NLS-bearing cargo and interacts with nucleoporins at the nuclear pore complex for translocation.

## Role in Cancer

> [!important] Druggable Oncogene
> shRNA and CRISPR in vivo screens identified KPNB1 as essential for ovarian cancer cell survival. Ivermectin, an FDA-approved antiparasitic, blocks cell cycle and induces apoptosis through a KPNB1-dependent mechanism.

- KPNB1 is overexpressed in multiple cancer types
- In ovarian cancer, KPNB1 inhibition causes cell cycle arrest and apoptosis
- Synergy with paclitaxel: ivermectin + paclitaxel almost completely inhibited tumor growth in vivo
- KPNB1 is required for nuclear transport of cell cycle regulators and survival factors

## Therapeutic Targeting

- [[Ivermectin]] is a known KPNB1 inhibitor identified through mechanistic studies
- Combination with microtubule-targeting agents (paclitaxel) shows strong synergy in preclinical models
