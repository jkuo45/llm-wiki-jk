## Overview

Xp11 translocation renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is a distinct subtype of kidney cancer defined by chromosomal translocations involving the TFE3 locus at Xp11.2. It characteristically arises in children and young adults and displays clear-cell or papillary morphology.

## Molecular Pathogenesis

[[TFE3]] is recurrently involved in Xp11.2 translocation RCC, in which it fuses with partner genes such as ASPSCR1, PRCC, or SFPQ, creating chimeric transcription factors that drive tumorigenesis. The fusion proteins typically retain the TFE3 DNA-binding domain and are constitutively active, deregulating [[MiT-TFE family|MiT/TFE]] target genes.

> [!info] Constitutive activation
> Because the fusion retains the TFE3 DNA-binding domain while gaining constitutive activity, MiT/TFE transcriptional programs — including lysosomal and autophagic gene networks — are aberrantly driven in these tumors.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

These tumors present like other RCCs with hematuria, flank pain, or incidentally on imaging, but occur at younger ages and are often more aggressive in adults. Diagnosis relies on histology, fluorescence in situ hybridization, or sequencing to detect TFE3 rearrangement. Detection of the rearrangement is important for correct diagnosis and classification.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Treatment follows RCC paradigms, including nephrectomy and systemic therapy. Because these tumors are distinct at the molecular level, targeting the downstream MiT/TFE program — including metabolic and autophagic dependencies — is an active area of investigation, and TFE3 status is emerging as a prognostic biomarker.
