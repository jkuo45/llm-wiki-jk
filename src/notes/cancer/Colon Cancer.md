# Colon Cancer

**Colon Cancer** is a malignancy arising from the epithelial lining of the large intestine (colon), most commonly from adenomatous polyps through the classic [[Adenoma]]-Carcinoma Sequence. It is a major subset of [[Colorectal Cancer]] and represents the third most commonly diagnosed cancer worldwide, with approximately 1.9 million new cases and over 900,000 deaths annually. Peak incidence occurs between ages 65–75, with roughly equal sex distribution. Global lifetime risk is approximately 4–5%, with marked geographic variation — highest in developed nations (North America, Europe, Australia) and rising in Asia and South America as dietary patterns Westernize.

## Risk Factors

**Age** is the dominant risk factor — over 90% of cases are diagnosed after age 50. **Hereditary syndromes** account for ~5–10% of cases: [[Familial Adenomatous Polyposis]] (FAP), caused by germline [[APC]] mutations, confers near-100% lifetime risk; [[Lynch Syndrome]] (Hereditary Non-Polyposis Colorectal Cancer, HNPCC), caused by germline mutations in [[DNA Mismatch Repair]] genes ([[MLH1]], [[MSH2]], [[MSH6]], [[PMS2]]), confers a 40–80% lifetime risk, predominantly right-sided. Long-standing [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]] (IBD), particularly [[Ulcerative Colitis]] and [[Crohn's Disease]], elevates risk proportional to disease duration and extent. [[Obesity]], [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Cigarette Smoking]], and heavy [[Alcohol Consumption]] each confer approximately 1.2–1.5-fold increased risk. Dietary factors include high consumption of [[Red Meat|red]] and [[Processed Meat|processed meats]], low dietary fiber, and low [[Folate]] intake. [[Aspirin]] and other [[NSAIDs]] show chemopreventive benefit, particularly in Lynch Syndrome carriers.

## Adenoma-Carcinoma Sequence

The canonical **Vogelstein model** describes the stepwise accumulation of genetic alterations driving the transition from normal colonic epithelium to invasive carcinoma:

1. **APC loss (chromosome 5q)** — Inactivating mutation of the [[APC]] tumor suppressor gene is the initiating event, leading to aberrant [[Wnt]]/β-catenin signaling and forming dysplastic aberrant crypt foci.
2. **KRAS activation (chromosome 12)** — Oncogenic [[KRAS]] mutation (most commonly codon 12 or 13) constitutively activates the [[MAPK]]/[[ERK]] pathway, driving proliferation of intermediate adenomas.
3. **SMAD4 loss (chromosome 18q)** — Deletion of [[SMAD4]] (and neighboring [[DCC]]) disrupts [[TGFβ]] signaling, enabling escape from growth-inhibitory signals in late adenomas.
4. **TP53 loss (chromosome 17p)** — Loss of [[TP53]] ([[p53]]) function abolishes [[DNA Damage]] checkpoints and [[Apoptosis]], permitting the final transition to invasive carcinoma.

An alternative pathway, the **serrated pathway**, involves [[BRAF]] V600E mutation and [[CpG Island]] Methylator Phenotype (CIMP), leading to microsatellite instability through [[MLH1]] promoter hypermethylation — characteristic of right-sided, MSI-H tumors.

## Screening

[[Colonoscopy]] is the gold standard, with a sensitivity >95% for adenomas and early cancers. Current guidelines recommend initiating screening at age 45 (average risk) every 10 years. Polypectomy during colonoscopy reduces colorectal cancer incidence by 76–90% and mortality by ~53%. Alternative modalities include **Fecal Immunochemical Testing (FIT)** annually, **Fecal Occult Blood Testing (FOBT)** annually, and **CT Colonography** (virtual colonoscopy) every 5 years. Multitarget stool DNA testing ([[Cologuard]]) every 3 years offers intermediate sensitivity. Emerging modalities include blood-based [[cfDNA]] methylation assays (e.g., [[SEPT9]]).

## Histology

[[Adenocarcinoma]] accounts for >95% of colon cancers, characterized by glandular formation with varying differentiation. Subtypes include **mucinous adenocarcinoma** (>50% extracellular mucin; ~10%; worse prognosis), **signet ring cell carcinoma** (>50% signet ring cells; ~1%; poor prognosis), **medullary carcinoma** (associated with MSI-H; favorable prognosis), **neuroendocrine carcinoma** (rare; aggressive), and **squamous cell carcinoma** (extremely rare). Histologic grade, lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion, tumor budding, and [[Mismatch Repair]] status ([[MSI]]/[[dMMR]]) are critical prognostic features. [[CK7]]/[[CK20]]/[[CDX2]] immunohistochemistry is used to confirm colonic origin in metastatic workup.

## Treatment

**Localized Disease (Stages I–III)**:
- **Surgical resection** — segmental [[Colectomy]] with en bloc [[Lymphadenectomy]] (removal of ≥12 lymph nodes). [[Laparoscopic]] and [[Robotic]] approaches offer equivalent oncologic outcomes with faster recovery.
- **Adjuvant chemotherapy** — indicated for Stage III (node-positive) and high-risk Stage II (T4, poor differentiation, <12 lymph nodes sampled, lymphovascular invasion, perforation, obstruction). Standard regimen: **FOLFOX** ([[5-Fluorouracil]]/[[Leucovorin]]/[[Oxaliplatin]]) for 3–6 months. [[CapeOx]] ([[Capecitabine]]/[[Oxaliplatin]]) is an oral alternative. [[Irinotecan]]-containing regimens are not standard in the adjuvant setting.

**Metastatic Disease (Stage IV)**:
- **Chemotherapy backbones**: [[FOLFOX]] or [[FOLFIRI]] ([[5-Fluorouracil]]/[[Leucovorin]]/[[Irinotecan]]), interchangeable as first-line, with switch-maintenance strategies.
- **Anti-EGFR** ([[Cetuximab]], [[Panitumumab]]) — restricted to [[RAS]] wild-type ([[KRAS]]/[[NRAS]] exon 2, 3, 4) left-sided tumors; confers ~8-month survival benefit.
- **Anti-VEGF** ([[Bevacizumab]]) — added to chemotherapy regardless of RAS status; improves PFS and OS. [[Aflibercept]] and [[Ramucirumab]] (anti-VEGFR2) are second-line options.
- **MSI-H/dMMR**: [[Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors]] — [[Pembrolizumab]] (first-line, approved) and [[Nivolumab]] ± [[Ipilimumab]] show durable responses (ORR ~40–50%) independent of RAS/BRAF status.
- **BRAF V600E-mutant**: [[Encorafenib]] + [[Cetuximab]] ± [[Bintrafusp alfa]] — approved after prior therapy; significantly improved OS vs. standard chemotherapy.
- **HER2 amplification**: [[Trastuzumab]] + [[Lapatinib]] or [[Pertuzumab]] + [[Trastuzumab]] — active in RAS wild-type, HER2-amplified tumors.
- **Refractory setting**: [[TAS-102]] (Trifluridine/Tipiracil, [[Lonsurf]]), [[Regorafenib]] (oral multikinase inhibitor), or [[Fruquintinib]] in China — each modestly extends OS (~1.5–2.4 months). [[Pembrolizumab]] for TMB-high tumors irrespective of MSI status.

**Liver-limited metastases** — approximately 15–25% of Stage IV patients are candidates for curative-intent liver resection (segmentectomy or hemihepatectomy), with 5-year survival rates of 35–60% after R0 resection. Perioperative chemotherapy (FOLFOX) is standard. [[Radiofrequency Ablation]] (RFA), [[Microwave Ablation]], and [[Yttrium-90]] radioembolization are alternative local therapies.

## Prognosis

Prognosis depends on stage at diagnosis and molecular subtype:

| Stage | 5-Year Survival | Description |
|-------|----------------|-------------|
| I | ~90% | Tumor invades submucosa/muscularis propria (T1–T2, N0, M0) |
| IIA–IIC | ~75–87% | Tumor invades through muscularis propria (T3–T4, N0, M0) |
| IIIA–IIIC | ~40–70% | Node-positive (any T, N1–N2, M0) |
| IV | ~10–15% | Distant metastasis (any T, any N, M1) |

MSI-H/dMMR tumors carry a more favorable Stage II–III prognosis (HR ~0.65) but derive no benefit from 5-FU alone. [[BRAF]] V600E mutation confers worse prognosis in metastatic disease (median OS ~12 months without targeted therapy). Liver-only metastatic disease with curative-intent resection offers 5-year survival of 35–60%. Baseline [[CEA]] ([[Carcinoembryonic Antigen]]) level is an independent prognostic factor.
