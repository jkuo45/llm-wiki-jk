# TGF-β3

**Transforming growth factor beta 3** (TGF-β3) is a secreted cytokine of the TGF-β superfamily, closely related to TGF-β1 and TGF-β2. Like its siblings, it signals through the TGF-β receptor complex (TGFBR1/TGFBR2) and SMAD2/3 transcription factors to regulate cell differentiation, embryogenesis, tissue repair, and immune tolerance. Its biology is context-dependent: in normal epithelia and early tumors it restrains proliferation, whereas in established cancers it often switches to a pro-invasive, immunosuppressive role.

## Structure and Signaling

TGF-β3 is synthesized as a latency-associated precursor and activated extracellularly. Binding to the type II receptor recruits and phosphorylates the type I receptor, which activates SMAD2/3; these complex with SMAD4 and translocate to the nucleus to modulate gene expression. The cytokine carries an N-terminal region with an **RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) integrin-binding motif**, a feature exploited in engineering.

## Role in Cancer

In early tumorigenesis, TGF-β signaling enforces cytostasis and apoptosis, functioning as a [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]] pathway. As tumors evolve, they frequently acquire resistance to these brakes while co-opting TGF-β for [[EMT]], stromal remodeling, [[Angiogenesis]], and [[Metastasis]]. High TGF-β3 in the tumor microenvironment correlates with poor prognosis in several cancers via fibroblast activation and T-cell exclusion.

## Use in Anticancer Peptide Engineering

In the design of tumor-selective lytic peptides, an N-terminal alpha-helical **RGD peptide motif (RGD1-melittin)** derived from TGF-β3 was engineered onto [[melittin]]. The RGD sequence directs binding to αvβ3/αvβ5 [[Integrins]] overexpressed on angiogenic endothelium and some tumor cells, enhancing selectivity and reducing off-target toxicity of the membranolytic payload. This illustrates repurposing a native cytokine motif for targeted delivery.

## Therapeutic Landscape

TGF-β pathway inhibitors (antisense galunisertib, ligand traps such as fresolimumab, anti-TGFBR antibodies) are in clinical evaluation, largely combined with checkpoint blockade, though success has been limited by the pathway's pleiotropy.
