# Exosomes

## Overview

Exosomes are small extracellular vesicles (30-150 nm) of endosomal origin, released by all cell types. They carry proteins, [[lipids]], [[mRNA]], [[microRNA]], and [[DNA]], mediating intercellular communication in physiology and disease, including [[Cancer]] and [[Senescence]].

## Biogenesis

1. **Formation**: Inward budding of the [[endosomal membrane]] generates intraluminal vesicles within multivesicular bodies (MVBs).
2. **Cargo loading**: ESCRT complex-dependent and independent mechanisms sort cargo into exosomes.
3. **Release**: MVBs fuse with the [[plasma membrane]], releasing exosomes into the extracellular space.
4. **Uptake**: Target cells internalize exosomes via [[endocytosis]], [[phagocytosis]], or direct membrane fusion.

## Role in Senescence

Senescent cells secrete exosomes with altered cargo composition (senescence-associated exosomes). These exosomes carry [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] factors, [[pro-inflammatory cytokines]], [[DNA fragments]], and [[microRNAs]] (e.g., [[miR-146a]], [[miR-21]]) that can:
- Paracrine spread of senescence to neighboring cells
- Modulate the [[Tumor Microenvironment]]
- Transfer [[Cytoplasmic Chromatin Fragments|cytoplasmic chromatin fragments]] that activate [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] in recipient cells

## Role in Cancer

- **Tumor promotion**: Tumor-derived exosomes transfer oncoproteins, promote [[Angiogenesis]], establish pre-metastatic niches, and suppress antitumor immunity.
- **Tumor suppression**: Can deliver tumor antigens to [[dendritic cells]] for cross-presentation.
- **Biomarkers**: Exosomal [[DNA]], [[RNA]], and proteins in liquid biopsies for cancer diagnosis and monitoring.
- **Therapeutic vehicles**: Engineered exosomes as drug delivery systems.
