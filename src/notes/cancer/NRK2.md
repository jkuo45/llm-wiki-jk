# NRK2 (Nicotinamide Riboside Kinase 2)

## Overview

**NRK2** (Nicotinamide Riboside Kinase 2, also known as _Nmrk2_, _Itgb1bp3_, or _Mibp_) is a muscle-specific [[NAD+]] biosynthetic enzyme that phosphorylates [[Nicotinamide Riboside|nicotinamide riboside (NR)]] to [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide|nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)]] in the salvage pathway. It is normally activated upon stress to support NAD+ biosynthesis and is involved in extracellular matrix remodeling, [[Muscle Stem Cell|myogenesis]], and [[Skeletal Muscle|muscle regeneration]].

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Chemotherapy NAD+ Loss Pöllänen 2026]]
> Paradoxically, despite these protective roles, muscle Nrk2 is suppressed in [[Cachexia|cachexia]], potentially compromising [[NAD+]] homeostasis and other physiological functions and thereby contributing to muscle wasting.

## Structure & Function

NRK2 belongs to the nicotinamide riboside kinase family, which also includes the ubiquitously expressed [[NRK2|NRK1]]. NRK2 is predominantly expressed in skeletal muscle and cardiac tissue, where it serves as a stress-responsive enzyme for NAD+ biosynthesis via the NR → NMN → NAD+ pathway.

- **Substrate specificity**: Phosphorylates nicotinamide riboside (NR) to NMN
- **Tissue distribution**: Muscle-specific (skeletal muscle, cardiac muscle)
- **Upstream regulation**: PPARα is a known transactivator; cytokine-driven [[Inflammation|inflammation]] and activin signaling suppress expression

## Role in Muscle Wasting

> [!important] Nrk2 as a Biomarker of Muscle Mass
> Muscle Nrk2 expression dynamically tracks changes in muscle mass, reflecting transitions between catabolic and anabolic muscle states. It may serve as a predictor of muscle mass or atrophy, with potential utility for monitoring treatment responses.

### Chemotherapy-Induced Downregulation

Nrk2 is the most responsive [[NAD+]] biosynthetic enzyme to [[Chemotherapy]], showing consistent downregulation across all models with ongoing or developing muscle loss:

- [[Cisplatin]]: −93% (p<0.001)
- [[FOLFIRI]]: −84% (p<0.001)
- [[FOLFOX]]: −92% (p<0.001)

Nrk2 downregulation is detectable as early as 24 hours after FOLFIRI treatment, preceding any measurable muscle loss. This positions Nrk2 as an early indicator of chemotherapy-induced muscle wasting.

### Cancer Cachexia

A meta-analysis by Zhao et al. encompassing 13 studies across multiple mouse models (C26, KPC, LLC, KPP, KPC GEMM) identified Nrk2 as consistently downregulated in skeletal muscle across all cancer cachexia models, with the most pronounced suppression in the C26 model.

### Human Conditions

- **Cachexia**: _NRK2_ ranked among the top 20 most differentially expressed transcripts in cachectic pancreatic cancer patients
- **[[Sarcopenia]]**: Reduced _NRK2_ expression in elderly sarcopenic patients
- **Critical illness myopathy**: Reduced _NRK2_ expression compared to healthy counterparts
- **Inactivity**: Strongly decreased upon bed rest or limb immobilization
- **Resistance training**: Significantly upregulated, consistent with its role as a marker of anabolic muscle states

### Therapeutic Reversal

Cachexia-targeted interventions that increase muscle mass restore Nrk2 expression:
- Activin receptor ligand blockade
- [[IL-6]] deficiency
- [[FOXO]] inhibition
- GDF15 neutralization

## NAD+ Biosynthesis Context

NRK2 operates in the [[NAD+]] salvage pathway alongside [[NAMPT]] and [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide|NMNAT]] enzymes:

```
NR → (NRK2) → NMN → (NMNAT) → NAD+
```

In skeletal muscle, NAD+ is primarily generated via the salvage pathway through [[NAMPT]]. NRK2 provides a stress-responsive supplementary route that becomes critical during regeneration and metabolic stress.

> [!warning] Paradoxical Suppression
> Despite its protective roles in NAD+ biosynthesis and muscle regeneration, Nrk2 is paradoxically suppressed in cachexia — potentially compromising NAD+ homeostasis and contributing to muscle wasting. The precise upstream regulators mediating this suppression remain to be fully defined.
