# FBW7

F-box/WD repeat-containing protein 7 (FBW7, also called CDC4 or FBXW7) is the substrate-recognition subunit of the SCF^FBW7^ (SKP1–CUL1–F-box) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. It directs the polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of a roster of potent oncoproteins, thereby functioning as a context-dependent [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]].

## Structure & Domains

FBW7 contains an N-terminal F-box motif that binds the SKP1 adaptor of the [[SCF Complex]], and a C-terminal β-propeller of seven WD40 repeats that recognizes phosphorylated degron motifs (the "phosphodegron") on target proteins. Three tissue-specific isoforms (FBW7α, β, γ) localize to the nucleus, cytoplasm, and nucleolus, respectively, allowing compartment-specific substrate targeting.

## Mechanism of Action & Targets

FBW7 recognizes substrates only after they are primed by kinase phosphorylation, typically at conserved threonine/serine residues. Key substrates include:
- **[[MYC]]** (oncogenic transcription factor)
- **[[Cyclin E]]** (cell-cycle progression)
- **[[Mcl-1]]** (anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family protein)
- **[[c-JUN]]**, **[[Notch1]] intracellular domain**, **[[SREBP]]**, and **[[Aurora A]]**

By degrading these proteins, FBW7 restrains proliferation, promotes [[Apoptosis]], and limits metabolic and mitotic fitness. FBW7 itself is regulated by [[Akt]]/SGK phosphorylation (which impairs substrate binding) and by [[USP28]] deubiquitinase that can stabilize it.

## Physiological Function

FBW7 maintains genome stability, controls stem-cell self-renewal, and couples growth-factor signaling to protein turnover. It is essential during development and tissue homeostasis.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

FBW7 is mutated or epigenetically silenced in ~6% of human cancers, including [[Colorectal Cancer]], [[T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia]], [[Breast Cancer]], and [[glioblastoma]]. Loss of FBW7 stabilizes [[MYC]] and [[Mcl-1]], driving unrestrained growth and resistance to apoptosis — directly engaging multiple [[Hallmarks of Cancer]]. Because FBW7 loss sensitizes tumors to replication stress and CHK1 inhibition, it is being explored as a predictive biomarker. Restoring FBW7 activity (e.g., via USP28 inhibitors) is an emerging therapeutic strategy.

## Connections

- [[SCF Complex]]: The cullin-RING ligase to which FBW7 provides substrate specificity.
- [[Mcl-1]]: Anti-apoptotic substrate degraded by FBW7.
- [[MYC]]: Oncogenic transcription factor targeted for degradation.
- [[Tumor Suppressor Gene]]: FBW7 acts as a haploinsufficient tumor suppressor.
- [[Apoptosis]]: FBW7 loss stabilizes Mcl-1, blocking cell death.
- [[Cyclin E]]: Cell-cycle substrate whose accumulation follows FBW7 loss.
- [[Akt]]: Phosphorylates FBW7 to inhibit its function.
