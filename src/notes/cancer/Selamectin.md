# Selamectin

**Selamectin** is a semi-synthetic [[Avermectin]]-family macrocyclic lactone developed by Pfizer as a broad-spectrum topical endectocide approved for use in dogs and cats (brand names Revolution, Stronghold). It is one of the closest veterinary analogues of [[Ivermectin]] — same chloride-channel mechanism and largely overlapping spectrum — but is formulated for young puppies/kittens, covers fleas and ear mites, and penetrates the mammalian CNS less avidly, giving it a wider margin in dogs carrying the [[P-gp]] (MDR1/ABCB1) mutation.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

Selamectin is the mono-saccharide 5-oxime avermectin: 25-cyclohexyl-5-deoxy-22,23-dihydro-5-hydroxyiminoavermectin B1. Removing one of the two oleandrose sugars of the natural avermectin scaffold reduces oral absorption, which is exploited by making it a topical (spot-on) product that is absorbed transdermally into the systemic circulation. Along with [[Doramectin]] and [[Moxidectin]], it is counted among the avermectin-family compounds reviewed alongside ivermectin in the vault's oncology-repurposing document.

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

Like ivermectin, selamectin opens invertebrate glutamate- and GABA-gated chloride channels, producing sustained Cl⁻ influx, neuronal hyperpolarization, flaccid paralysis, and death of the parasite. Mammalian chloride channels and the intact blood–brain barrier confer selectivity, and — critically for canine safety — selamectin shows substantially lower brain penetration than ivermectin even in P-glycoprotein-deficient (Mdr1a/b and Bcrp knockout) mice (Geyer et al., 2009, cited in the vault's ivermectin review). After topical application it is absorbed systemically, achieving prolonged plasma exposure suitable for once-monthly dosing.

## Applications

### Veterinary Use in Dogs and Cats

Approved monthly topical use against:
- **Heartworm** (*Dirofilaria immitis*) prevention — the ivermectin-like core use;
- **Fleas** (*Ctenocephalides* spp.) and **ear mites** (*Otodectes cynotis*);
- **Sarcoptic mange** (*Sarcoptes scabiei*);
- **Roundworms** (*Toxocara canis/cati*) and **hookworms** (*Ancylostoma* spp.).

It is not effective against ticks or tapeworms. Label studies support use in ivermectin-sensitive (collie-type) breeds at the approved dose.

### Safety in Dogs and the MDR1 Gene

Selamectin is a P-gp substrate, but its reduced CNS penetration makes it safer than ivermectin in dogs homozygous for the *ABCB1-1Δ* (MDR1) deletion found in collies, Australian shepherds, Shelties, and other herding breeds. Approved doses are well tolerated, including in breed-safety label studies; as with all macrocyclic lactones, accidental overdose or concurrent use of other P-gp inhibitors should still be avoided in affected breeds.

### Cancer Repurposing

The vault's ivermectin review notes that selamectin (like the rest of the avermectin family, including [[Doramectin]]) has reported anticancer activity in preclinical models, positioning it within the [[Drug Repositioning]] literature centered on [[Ivermectin]] and its relatives.
