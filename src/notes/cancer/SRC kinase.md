# SRC kinase

**SRC kinase** (or **Src**, product of the *SRC* proto-oncogene) is a non-receptor cytoplasmic [[Tyrosine Kinase]] and the founding member of the SRC family of kinases (SFKs: Src, Yes, Fyn, Lyn, Lck, Blk, Hck, Fgr, Frk). Originally identified as the cellular homolog of the v-Src oncogene carried by Rous sarcoma virus, c-Src is a prototype [[Oncogene]] that integrates signals from receptor [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors|tyrosine kinases]], integrins, and G-protein-coupled receptors to drive cell proliferation, survival, motility, and [[Angiogenesis]].

## Structure and Regulation

Src is organized into an N-terminal myristoylation/palmitoylation motif (for membrane association), an SH3 domain, an SH2 domain, a catalytic kinase domain, and a short C-terminal tail containing Tyr527. In the inactive state, the phosphorylated tail (Tyr527, by Csk kinase) binds intramolecularly to the SH2 domain, and the SH3 domain engages the kinase domain, locking Src in a closed conformation. Dephosphorylation of Tyr527 (by phosphatases such as PTP1B or SHP-1) or phosphorylation of activation-loop Tyr416 releases the clamp and activates the kinase. This conformational switch is a classic example of intramolecular autoinhibition.

## Mechanism of Action and Signaling

Active Src phosphorylates a broad set of substrates on tyrosine residues, including focal adhesion kinase (FAK), p130Cas, [[Caspase-8]], STAT transcription factors, and receptor tyrosine kinases such as [[HER2]] and EGFR. Notably, Src phosphorylates [[Caspase-8]] at Tyr380, which blocks caspase-8 maturation and provides a direct biochemical mechanism for [[Apoptosis]] suppression — linking Src activity to resistance to death-receptor-induced cell death. Through FAK and Cas, Src also remodels the actin cytoskeleton and promotes [[EMT]] and [[Metastasis]].

## Role in Cancer

Src is overexpressed or hyperactivated in numerous malignancies including colorectal, breast, pancreatic, and [[Gastric Cancer]]. Its pro-tumorigenic outputs encompass sustained proliferation, evasion of [[Apoptosis]], increased invasiveness, and stimulation of tumor vasculature. Because Src sits downstream of many receptor tyrosine kinases, it is an attractive node for combination therapy.

## Therapeutic Targeting

Several small-molecule [[Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors]] (e.g., dasatinib, saracatinib, bosutinib) inhibit Src alongside related kinases. Although single-agent activity has been modest, Src inhibition is explored in combination with [[Chemotherapy]], EGFR/HER2 blockers such as [[Trastuzumab]], and anti-angiogenic agents. The caspase-8 phosphorylation axis remains a mechanistic rationale for combining Src inhibition with death-receptor agonists.
