## Overview

VEGFR2 (KDR/Flk-1) is the principal receptor tyrosine kinase mediating VEGF-driven angiogenesis. It is expressed predominantly on blood vascular endothelium and transduces signals controlling endothelial survival, proliferation, migration, and vascular permeability.

## Structure & Function

VEGFR2 is a receptor tyrosine kinase with an extracellular ligand-binding domain and an intracellular kinase domain. It is activated by [[VEGF]]-A homodimers, with [[VEGFc]] also engaging VEGFR2 under certain conditions.

## Dual Receptor Usage of VEGF-C

At higher concentrations or after proteolytic processing, [[VEGFc]] also engages VEGFR2 on blood vascular endothelium, supporting [[Angiogenesis]] in addition to its lymphatic actions. This dual receptor usage broadens its angiogenic potential within the [[SASP]] and explains how VEGF-C can drive both blood- and lymphatic-vessel formation, depending on the local processing environment.

> [!info] Shared ligand, distinct outputs
> Processed VEGF-C acts as a shared ligand across VEGFR2 and VEGFR3 systems, with distinct downstream signaling outputs depending on the receptor engaged.

## Clinical Relevance

VEGFR2 signaling is a central node in tumor [[Angiogenesis]]. It is the primary target of approved anti-angiogenic therapies, including bevacizumab (anti-VEGF-A) and tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as sunitinib and sorafenib that block VEGFR2 kinase activity.
