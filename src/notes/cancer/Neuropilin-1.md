# Neuropilin-1

**Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1)** is a transmembrane co-receptor originally characterized in axon guidance that has emerged as a pivotal player in [[Angiogenesis]], vascular permeability, and receptor-mediated tumor targeting. It lacks intrinsic kinase activity and functions by associating with signaling partners such as VEGFR2 and plexins.

## Structure and Ligands

NRP-1 contains an extracellular a1/a2 and b1/b2 domain arrangement, a transmembrane segment, and a short cytoplasmic tail. It binds class 3 semaphorins (via the a1/a2 domain) and VEGF family isoforms (notably VEGF165, via the b1/b2 domain) through distinct sites. Critically, NRP-1 recognizes the C-end Rule (CendR) motif present on tumor-homing peptides such as [[iRGD peptide]], mediating tissue penetration.

## Mechanism of Action

As a VEGF co-receptor, NRP-1 amplifies [[VEGF]]-driven [[Angiogenesis]] and increases vascular permeability by potentiating VEGFR2 signaling and by regulating endothelial fenestration. In tumors, NRP-1 is overexpressed on both endothelial and cancer cells, where it supports neo-vascularization, survival, and [[Metastasis]]. The CendR interaction is exploited therapeutically: iRGD binds αv integrins and then engages NRP-1 to trigger transendothelial and interstitial transport, enhancing the tumor penetration of co-administered drugs and nanoparticles.

## Physiological and Pathological Function

Physiologically, NRP-1 guides neuronal and cardiovascular development. Pathologically, its upregulation in [[Cancer]] fosters a leaky, pro-invasive vasculature and serves as a gateway for tumor-targeted delivery. NRP-1 also modulates immune evasion and TGF-β signaling in the stroma.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

NRP-1 is a biomarker of angiogenesis and a target for vascular-disrupting and peptide-guided therapies. iRGD-based delivery systems leveraging NRP-1 are in preclinical and early clinical evaluation to improve the intratumoral distribution of cytotoxic and immunotherapeutic agents, addressing a major limitation of conventional [[Chemotherapy]].
