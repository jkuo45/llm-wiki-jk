## Overview

Cyclin D1 (encoded by CCND1) is a regulatory subunit of the cyclin D–CDK4/6 complexes that drive the G1/S transition by phosphorylating and inactivating the [[Rb]] tumor suppressor. It is among the most frequently overexpressed cyclins in human cancers.

## Structure & Function

Cyclin D1 contains the cyclin box domain required for [[CDK4]] binding and activation. Cyclin D1/CDK4 phosphorylation of [[Rb]] releases E2F transcription factors, enabling S-phase gene expression. Its abundance is tightly controlled by mitogenic signaling and by the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway.

## Interaction with SIRT1

Beyond cell cycle control, the cyclin D1/CDK4 complex and the cyclin B–Cdk1 complex regulate [[SIRT1]] by phosphorylation — massive phosphorylation is required for SIRT1's cell-cycle function. Conversely, cyclin D1 opposes [[SIRT1]]-dependent regulation of cell-cycle genes in proliferative contexts, and cyclin D1 is listed among negative SIRT1 regulators (Sasaki et al., 2008, PLoS One 3:e4020, PMID 19015735).

> [!info] Bidirectional control
> Cyclin D1 both depends on and shapes SIRT1 activity: CDK-mediated phosphorylation primes SIRT1 for its cell-cycle roles, while cyclin D1 acts as a negative regulator of SIRT1-dependent gene regulation.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

Cyclin D1 overexpression drives unchecked proliferation in breast, mantle cell, and other cancers. [[CDK4]]/[[CDK6]] inhibitors such as abemaciclib and palbociclib target its effector pathway, and CDK4/6 blockade is standard care in hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. The cyclin D1–SIRT1 link suggests sirtuin modulation may intersect with cell-cycle therapy responses.
