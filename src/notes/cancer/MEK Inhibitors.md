# MEK Inhibitors

**MEK inhibitors** are allosteric small molecules that inhibit the dual
specificity kinases MEK1 (MAP2K1) and MEK2 (MAP2K2), the two convergence
points of the RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK (MAPK) cascade. They are among the most
selective antitumour agents available, because the MEK allosteric pocket
adjacent to the ATP site has no close homologues in the kinome.

## Mechanism of Action

RAS-GTP activates RAF, which phosphorylates and activates MEK1/2, which
phosphorylate [[ERK]] (ERK1/2). Activated ERK translocates to the nucleus and
drives the transcription programmes underlying proliferation, differentiation
and survival.

Selumetinib, cobimetinib and trametinib bind a unique allosteric pocket on
the N-terminal domain of MEK1/2 adjacent to the ATP site. This locks MEK in
a catalytically inactive conformation and prevents RAF-induced
phosphorylation — so unlike ATP-competitive inhibitors they act
allosterically and with exceptional selectivity. Selumetinib inhibits MEK1/2
with an IC50 of ~14 nM and fails to inhibit other kinases up to 10 µM.

> [!info] Why MEK, not ERK
> MEK sits at the focal point of upstream activating mutations, phosphorylates
> only ERK1/2, and has a structure favourable to selective inhibition — all
> three arguments favour targeting this tier rather than ERK itself.

## Approved Agents

- **Trametinib** (Mekinist) — reversible, highly selective, ATP-noncompetitive
  allosteric inhibitor. First approved 2013 for BRAF V600E/V600K unresectable
  or metastatic melanoma; later for BRAF-mutant non-small cell lung cancer and
  thyroid cancer, as monotherapy or with dabrafenib. Long half-life permits
  once-daily dosing and sustained pathway inhibition across the interval.
- **Cobimetinib** (Cotellic) — approved with vemurafenib for BRAF V600-positive
  melanoma and histiocytic cancers. Binds the µ-opioid receptor.
- **Selumetinib** (Koselugo) — approved for symptomatic inoperable plexiform
  neurofibromas in NF1 (paediatric, then expanded to adults in November 2025),
  dosed 25 mg/m² twice daily by body surface area.
- **Binimetinib** — MEK1/2 inhibitor approved with encorafenib for BRAF-mutant
  metastatic melanoma.

## Toxicity Profile

Class effects concentrate in what the pathway normally does to skin, heart and
eye: rash, photosensitivity, peripheral oedema, cardiomyopathy and reduced
ejection fraction, and ocular toxicity (retinopathy, visual disturbance).
Cobimetinib additionally carries a haemorrhage risk, and its CYP/transporter
profile (CYP2C8/2C9/2C19 inhibition, CYP3A4 induction, OATP1B1/1B3, P-gp and
BCRP inhibition) drives interaction complexity.

## Resistance & Combinations

Roughly 80% of BRAF-mutant melanomas become BRAF-inhibitor resistant through
MAPK reactivation; because BRAF-inhibitor-resistant cells remain sensitive to
MEK blockade, dual BRAF+MEK inhibition is standard and is approved in three
combinations (vemurafenib+cobimetinib, dabrafenib+trametinib,
encorafenib+binimetinib). Dual inhibition also delays MAPK-reactivation
resistance relative to single agents.

Resistance routes to MEK inhibitors include RAF1 (CRAF) bypass, NRAS or
MEK1/2 activating mutations, and feedback reactivation of RTKs.

## Non-Oncology and Emerging Uses

Selumetinib's NF1 approval is the clearest proof that MAPK-pathway blockade
works outside Ras-mutant cancer: NF1 loss disables neurofibromin's negative
regulation of RAS, so tumours are RAS-driven but not RAS-mutant. Combining a
MEK inhibitor with CDK4/6 inhibitors induces [[SASP]] in Kras-mutant lung and
pancreatic models and facilitates NK- and CD8-mediated clearance — an
exploitable therapeutic window.
