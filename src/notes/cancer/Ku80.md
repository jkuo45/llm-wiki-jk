# Ku80

**Ku80** (also called Ku86, gene *G22P*) is one of two subunits of the **Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer**, an abundant and evolutionarily conserved DNA-binding complex with a central role in genome integrity (Fell & Schild-Poulter, *Mutat Res* 2015; PMID 25795113).

## Structure and Mechanism

Ku is a ring-shaped heterodimer formed by intertwining the Ku70 and Ku80 polypeptide chains. The resulting central hole binds double-stranded DNA ends with very high affinity in a **sequence-independent** manner — the signature property that lets Ku occupy essentially any broken end in the genome.

> [!info] The universal break sensor
> Because Ku does not care about sequence, it is the first factor to arrive at virtually every DNA double-strand break. At the break it acts as a scaffold, docking and activating essentially all other classical NHEJ components in sequence.

## Role in Classical NHEJ

DNA double-strand breaks are repaired in mammals predominantly by NHEJ and [[Homologous Recombination]], with the balance set by cell-cycle phase. Ku performs the initial steps:

1. **End recognition and binding** — Ku loads onto the two-stranded break.
2. **Recruitment and activation of processing enzymes** — Ku directly and indirectly interacts with nucleases that process the ends (notably Artemis and the Ku80-associated nucleases) and with the ligation machinery.
3. **Scaffold assembly** — Ku tethers [[DNA-PKcs]], forming the DNA-PK holoenzyme, which drives both end processing and end joining. [[Ku70]] is the larger, catalytically inactive partner; together with DNA-PKcs it constitutes DNA-PK.

> [!important] Ku is also a signal-transduction node
> Beyond ligation, Ku signals to the DNA damage response machinery, modulating checkpoint activation and the apoptotic decision — coupling break repair to cell-cycle control and cell death.

## Telomere Protection: the Ku Paradox

Ku also localizes to telomeres, where, rather than promoting end joining, it **protects chromosome ends from being recognized as double-strand breaks**. This blocks their fusion, recombination, and degradation. The same structural feature — indiscriminate end binding — is used protectively at telomeres and destructively at accidental breaks; capping vs. joining depends on the chromatin context, with shelterin cooperating at telomeres.

## Disease Relevance

- **Cancer** — NHEJ determines the outcome of DSB-inducing cancer therapies; defective or altered NHEJ contributes to radiosensitization strategies that exploit synthetic lethality.
- **Neurodegeneration** — mutant huntingtin has been shown to interact with Ku70, promote its ubiquitination and degradation, and destabilize the Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer, impairing NHEJ and worsening DNA damage in Huntington's disease iPSC-derived neural progenitors (Chiu et al., *Stem Cell Res Ther* 2025; PMID 40770351).
- **V(D)J recombination** — Ku participates in the programmable double-strand breaks of lymphocyte antigen receptor rearrangement; class switch recombination and V(D)J joining are additionally DNA-PKcs-dependent.

## Connections

- [[Ku70]] — the larger, complementary subunit; the obligate heterodimer partner with distinct but interacting functions.
- [[DNA-PKcs]] — the catalytic kinase of the DNA-PK holoenzyme, activated by and essential alongside Ku for end processing and joining.
- [[Non-homologous End Joining]] — the pathway Ku initiates, and the main DSB repair route in mammalian cells.
- [[Homologous Recombination]] — the alternative, template-dependent DSB repair pathway; the choice between them depends on cell-cycle phase.
- [[DNA Repair]] — Ku sits at the entry point of the DSB repair network.
- [[DNA Damage]] — Ku both responds to and helps resolve double-strand breaks.
- [[Telomere]] — the paradoxical site where Ku protects ends from the joining it otherwise performs.
- [[p53]] — the checkpoint and apoptosis axis to which Ku signals.
- [[Apoptosis]] — Ku-mediated DNA damage signaling influences the cell-death decision after a break.
- [[53BP1]] — a key NHEJ factor acting downstream of Ku-bound breaks at chromatin.
- [[ATM]] and [[ATR]] — the apical damage-response kinases whose checkpoint signaling Ku modulates.
- [[Radiotherapy]] — NHEJ competence determines the survival of irradiated tumor cells.
- [[DNA Ligase IV]] — the final step of NHEJ in which Ku-loaded ends are sealed.
