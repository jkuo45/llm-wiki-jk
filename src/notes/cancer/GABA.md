# GABA

γ-Aminobutyric acid (GABA) is the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter in the adult mammalian central nervous system, synthesized from [[Glutamate]] by glutamic acid decarboxylase (GAD) and degraded by GABA transaminase. Although best known for neuronal inhibition, GABA and its receptors are increasingly implicated in cancer biology and in the pharmacology of anthelmintics such as [[Ivermectin]].

## Chemistry & Metabolism

GABA is a four-carbon non-protein amino acid. In neurons, vesicular GABA is released into the synapse and binds ionotropic GABA_A (Cl−-permeable) and GABA_C receptors and metabotropic GABA_B receptors, hyperpolarizing the postsynaptic membrane. GABA signaling is terminated by reuptake via GABA transporters (GATs) and catabolism to succinate semialdehyde.

## Relationship to Ivermectin & GluCls

Early hypotheses proposed GABAergic receptors as the anthelmintic target of [[Ivermectin]], but it is now established that the primary invertebrate target is the [[Glutamate-gated Cl- channels]] (GluCls), not vertebrate GABA receptors. Vertebrate GABA_A receptors are far less sensitive to avermectins, contributing to the selective safety margin of ivermectin. This distinction is important in the repurposing of ivermectin as an [[Anticancer]] agent, where its actions are mediated by mammalian targets such as [[PAK1]], [[YAP1]], and [[Akt]]/[[mTOR]] rather than GABAergic blockade.

## Physiological Function

GABA maintains neuronal excitability balance and modulates neuroendocrine and autonomic circuits. In the periphery, GABA is produced by immune and stromal cells.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

GABAergic signaling exerts tumor-modulatory effects. GABA can inhibit proliferation of some carcinomas via GABA_B receptor–mediated suppression of [[cAMP]]/ERK and can influence [[Glioblastoma]] growth, where GABA release by tumor cells shapes the peritumoral excitatory/inhibitory balance and seizure propensity. GABA receptors are being explored as modifiers of the [[Tumor Microenvironment]] and neuro-immune axis. Understanding that ivermectin does not act primarily through GABA in vertebrates clarifies its anticancer mechanism and safety profile.
