## Overview

The VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) family comprises structurally related cysteine-knot growth factors, including VEGF-A, VEGF-B, placental growth factor, and the lymphatic-specific ligands VEGF-C and VEGF-D. These ligands coordinate vascular and lymphatic development and remodeling through receptor tyrosine kinases [[VEGFR2]] and [[VEGFR3]].

## Structure & Classification

Family members share a cysteine-knot fold and dimerize to engage their receptors. [[VEGFc]] (VEGF-C) is structurally related to [[VEGF]]-A but has distinct receptor usage and lymphatic specificity. The family membership underpins receptor interactions, particularly with VEGFR-3 and, after proteolytic processing, VEGFR-2.

## Role in Senescence & the SASP

The family's classification situates it among the growth-factor arm of the [[SASP]], and in senescence family members such as VEGF-C are early-induced angiogenic and lymphangiogenic factors. This dual receptor capacity distinguishes the vascular functions of individual family members.

> [!info] SASP vascular arm
> The early induction of VEGF-family ligands during senescence supports both blood- and lymphatic-vessel remodeling around senescent tissue, linking the SASP to tissue microarchitecture.

## Clinical Relevance

VEGF-A-VEGFR2 signaling is a cornerstone of tumor [[Angiogenesis]], targeted by bevacizumab and tyrosine kinase inhibitors. VEGF-C-VEGFR3 signaling drives lymphatic metastasis, making the lymphatic axis an emerging therapeutic target.
