# Apis mellifera

***Apis mellifera***, the Western or European [[Honeybee]], is a hymenopteran insect of immense ecological and economic importance as a pollinator. Within the cancer knowledge base it is principally relevant as the natural source of [[Bee Venom]] and its major component [[melittin]], both of which have been extensively studied for anticancer properties.

## Biology & Products

Worker *A. mellifera* produce a complex venom apparatus; the secreted venom is a cocktail of peptides (melittin ~50–60%, apamin, adolapin), enzymes (phospholipase A₂), and amines. Beyond venom, the species yields honey, pollen, propolis, and royal jelly — the basis of [[apitherapy]].

## Anticancer Relevance of Bee Products

- **Melittin**: The principal cytotoxic peptide; it disrupts membranes, activates [[Phospholipase A2]], and triggers [[Apoptosis]] and [[Inflammation]] signaling in tumor cells. It can inhibit [[Wnt/β-catenin]] and [[NF-κB]] pathways and impair [[Metastasis]] and [[Angiogenesis]].
- **Apoptosis Induction**: Studies in breast, lung, liver, and glioblastoma models show melittin reduces viability and colony formation.
- **Selectivity**: Some evidence suggests melittin preferentially affects transformed over normal cells, though cardiotoxicity limits systemic use; nanoparticle delivery is an active mitigation strategy.

## Physiological & Ecological Context

*Apis mellifera* is also a model organism for toxin, behavior, and immunity research. Pesticide and pathogen stresses on bee populations (e.g., varroa mite) are themselves subjects of toxicological study with mechanistic overlap to cellular stress responses relevant in [[Cancer]] biology.

## Research & Therapeutic Outlook

Bee venom therapy remains largely preclinical/complementary; rigorous trials are needed. Its inclusion here reflects the broader interest in repurposing natural products as low-cost anticancer agents, paralleling work on [[Ivermectin]] and [[Albendazole]].
