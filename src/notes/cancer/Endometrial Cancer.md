# Endometrial Cancer

**Endometrial cancer (EC)** is a malignancy arising in the epithelial lining of the uterus. It is the most common gynecological cancer in high-income countries and the seventh most common cancer in women overall, with over 420,000 new cases annually worldwide and a median age at diagnosis of 63–66. Incidence is rising in high-income countries with population aging and rising obesity, and it is not negligible in low- and middle-income settings.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

EC is unusually strongly hormonally driven — up to 40–50% of cases may be attributable to excess estrogen, more than for most hormone-dependent cancers — and metabolically driven.

**Risk factors (all increase lifetime unopposed estrogen exposure):** elevated BMI, late menopause, early menarche, nulliparity, infertility, polycystic ovary syndrome, hormone replacement therapy, [[Tamoxifen]], [[Obesity]], [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] and hypertension. Postmenopausal women with elevated estrogen from adipose aromatisation of androstenedione to estrone have a 2–12-fold increased risk; the elevated risk persists even on estrogen-lowering aromatase inhibitors. An estimated 20–57% of EC is related to excess body weight, and ~14% of cases could be prevented by normal body weight. **Protective:** progestins, pregnancy, smoking, physical activity.

**Pathway.** Physiologically, estrogen stimulates endometrial proliferation while progesterone inhibits it and promotes implantation differentiation. Unopposed estrogen drives simple → complex hyperplasia → **endometrial intraepithelial neoplasia (EIN)**, formerly atypical hyperplasia, the recognised premalignant lesion, then endometrioid carcinoma with stromal and myometrial invasion. Obesity and hyperinsulinaemia act by lowering sex hormone-binding globulin, raising free estrogen; insulin resistance may additionally be an independent factor. In type I tumours, AKT activation downstream of PIK3CA phosphorylates and activates the estrogen receptor, allowing estrogen-independent ER transcription — this is the proposed reason PIK3CA-mutant tumours are over-represented among ER-positive, well-differentiated luminal cancers.

> [!important] Bokhman type I / type II (1983)
> **Type I** (~70–80% of cases): estrogen-dependent, endometrioid or mucinous, well to moderately differentiated, arises in hyperplasia, typically pre/perimenopausal or early postmenopausal, ER/PR-positive, favourable prognosis.
> **Type II** (~10–20%): estrogen-independent, arises in **atrophic** endometrium or from endometrial glandular dysplasia, usually serous, clear cell or grade 3 endometrioid, in older, non-obese, often parous, non-white women, hormone-receptor-negative, high-grade, deeply invasive, early metastatic, poor prognosis.
> The dualistic model has always shown substantial overlap in individual tumours and never gained routine pathologist acceptance — it has been superseded by molecular classification.

**Genetic syndromes.** Germline MMR mutations (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, or EPCAM deletion) cause Lynch syndrome; lifetime EC risk to age 70 is ~33% overall and 15–71% depending on gene, lowest for PMS2. Germline [[PTEN]] loss causes Cowden syndrome / PTEN hamartoma tumour syndrome with ~19–28% lifetime EC risk, highest in younger women; endometrioid histology predominates.

## Molecular Classification (TCGA)

TCGA's integrative genomic/transcriptomic/proteomic analysis — the first gynecological cancer so profiled — stratified EC into four subgroups, now embedded in ESGO/ESTRO/ESP guidelines and the 2023 FIGO staging revision:

| Subgroup | Hallmarks | Prognosis |
|---|---|---|
| **POLE ultramutated** | Exonuclease-domain POLE mutations; very high mutational burden; all endometrioid histology, ~50% presenting high-grade | Excellent; supports adjuvant de-escalation |
| **MSI-hypermutated / MMR-deficient** | Loss of MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2 by mutation or epigenetic silencing; ~18 × 10⁻⁶ mut/Mb; ~25–30% of cases. Most common sporadic cause is **MLH1 promoter hypermethylation**. Also co-occurs with PTEN, PIK3CA, KRAS, ARID1A, CTNNB1. Patients tend to be older, more obese, more advanced at diagnosis | Intermediate; predicts checkpoint-inhibitor response |
| **Copy-number low (NSMP)** | ~40% of cases; prototypical type I — low/intermediate-grade endometrioid; MSS, no POLE or TP53 mutation; recurrent PTEN, PIK3CA, CTNNB1, ARID1A, PIK3R1 | Intermediate-to-favourable |
| **Copy-number high (p53-abnormal)** | ~2.9 × 10⁻⁶ mut/Mb; 73.3% serous morphology; TP53, PPP2R1A, HER2/ERBB2 amplification, PIK3CA, PTEN; extensive chromosomal instability | Poor; needs intensive therapy |

> [!warning] Only POLE requires sequencing
> All other subgroup assignments — MMR deficiency, p53 abnormality, CTNNB1, ER/PR — can be made by immunohistochemistry plus targeted POLE exonuclease sequencing. This is why molecular classification is now routinely achievable in standard pathology laboratories. Note also that up to ~95% of MSI tumours carry PI3K-pathway alterations (*PTEN*, *PIK3CA*, *PIK3R1*), and that PIK3CA and PTEN alterations are mutually exclusive within a tumour.

## Clinical Presentation & Treatment

- **Presentation** is usually abnormal uterine bleeding; most type I tumours present early, whereas type II tumours more often present at an advanced stage. Diagnosis is by endometrial biopsy; surgery with staging is primary treatment, with adjuvant radiotherapy, [[Chemotherapy]] and targeted agents chosen by molecular subtype and risk.
- **Targeted/immunotherapy by subtype:**
  - HER2-amplified p53-abnormal and serous carcinoma — trastuzumab added to carboplatin-paclitaxel.
  - MMR-deficient — checkpoint inhibitors [[Pembrolizumab]] and dostarlimab; for MMR-proficient recurrent disease, lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab.
  - ER-positive NSMP — endocrine (letrozole) plus [[Everolimus]].
  - Rare NTRK fusions — larotrectinib or entrectinib.
  - PARP inhibitors are of interest given MSI, with efficacy shown in PTEN-deficient cell lines; benefit is unproven in clinical practice.
- **Prognostic caveat:** high-grade endometrioid morphology can coexist with a POLE mutation and an excellent prognosis, so morphology-based management alone may overtreat — a central reason the molecular classification displaced Bokhman.

> [!warning] Type assignment and risk factor overlap
> Two large prospective studies (Norwegian million-women and case-control) *did* find elevated BMI associated with type II cancer as well (RR 1.94 for obese women), by pooling grade 3 endometrioid with serous and clear cell and comparing against healthy controls rather than against type I. The clean "type I = obese, type II = non-obese" framing is therefore a simplification, not a rule.

## Connections
- [[Estrogen]] — the dominant causal exposure; unopposed estrogen drives proliferation through hyperplasia to EIN to endometrioid carcinoma, and PIK3CA-AKT signalling activates ER independently of ligand.
- [[PIK3CA]] — one of the most frequently altered genes in EC (~71% of significantly mutated genes in TCGA endometrioid tumours) and mutated in up to ~95% of MSI tumours alongside PTEN; mutually exclusive with PTEN.
- [[PTEN]] — the most frequently altered gene in EC (~94% in the TCGA significantly-mutated-gene analysis); PTEN loss is an early event and germline loss drives Cowden syndrome.
- [[p53]] — TP53 mutation defines the copy-number-high/p53-abnormal subgroup with the worst prognosis; p53 abnormality also entered the 2023 FIGO staging revision.
- [[MLH1]] — the commonest sporadic cause of MMR deficiency via promoter hypermethylation, and a Lynch syndrome gene; MMR IHC loss is what makes EC eligible for checkpoint blockade.
- [[KRAS]] — recurrent in type I and MSI tumours alongside PTEN and PIK3CA, participating in the RAS-ERK axis that cooperates with the PI3K pathway.
- [[Obesity]] — the strongest single risk factor and the dominant driver of rising incidence, acting via adipose aromatisation to estrone.
- [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] — independently associated with EC risk, partly through hyperinsulinaemia lowering sex hormone-binding globulin.
- [[Tamoxifen]] — an exogenous estrogenic SERM that increases EC risk and is listed among unopposed-estrogen exposures.
- [[HER2]] — amplified in a subset of serous/p53-abnormal tumours and the target for trastuzumab addition, making ERBB2 status actionable.
- [[Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors]] — the standard of care for MMR-deficient disease, and the reason MMR testing is mandatory rather than optional.
- [[Homologous Recombination]] — MMR deficiency and POLE ultramutation are both DNA-repair-context biomarkers that determine prognosis and treatment response in EC, alongside BRCA/PARP strategy considerations.
- [[Angiogenesis]] — VEGF/angiogenic signalling is a validated therapeutic target in recurrent and advanced EC.
- [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] — EC expresses a compact set of hallmarks; its defining peculiarity is that oestrogen dependence and immune/genomic context (MSI, POLE) are both unusually strong determinants of outcome.
