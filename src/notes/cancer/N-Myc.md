## Overview

N-Myc (encoded by MYCN) is a member of the MYC family of basic helix-loop-helix transcription factors, alongside c-Myc ([[MYC]]) and L-Myc. It is a key driver of neural crest development and a major oncogene in neuroblastoma and other pediatric tumors.

## Structure & Function

N-Myc heterodimerizes with [[MAX]] to bind E-box sequences and regulate transcriptional programs controlling cell proliferation, growth, and metabolism. Its expression is normally tightly restricted during development, and its abundance is regulated at multiple levels, including protein stability and post-translational modification.

## Regulation by SIRT1

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates N-Myc, modulating its stability and transcriptional output in proliferation and tumor contexts. Acetylation-dependent control of N-Myc affects its degradation and activity, linking the NAD+-dependent deacetylase machinery to MYC-driven oncogenic programs.

> [!info] Acetylation-based oncogene control
> SIRT1-dependent deacetylation couples cellular energy/NAD+ status to N-Myc protein stability, connecting sirtuin biology to proliferation and tumor growth.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

MYCN amplification is a defining feature of high-risk neuroblastoma and is associated with aggressive disease and poor prognosis. N-Myc drives proliferation and metabolic reprogramming, making its regulation by deacetylation a potential therapeutic lever. SIRT1 modulators are being explored for their impact on N-Myc-driven tumors.
