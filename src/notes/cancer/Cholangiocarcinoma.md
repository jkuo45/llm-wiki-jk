# Cholangiocarcinoma

**Cholangiocarcinoma** is a malignant tumor originating from the bile duct epithelium (cholangiocytes), occurring inside or outside the liver. It is the second most common primary liver cancer after hepatocellular carcinoma. Because it is typically diagnosed at advanced stages and is refractory to conventional therapy, cholangiocarcinoma carries a dismal prognosis, driving intense interest in repurposed agents such as [[Ivermectin]].

## Subtypes & Etiology

Cholangiocarcinoma is anatomically classified into intrahepatic (iCCA), perihilar (pCCA, Klatskin tumors), and distal (dCCA) forms, each with distinct biology and risk factors. Established risk factors include primary sclerosing cholangitis, liver fluke infection (*Opisthorchis viverrini*, *Clonorchis sinensis*), chronic biliary inflammation, cirrhosis, hepatitis B/C, and metabolic syndrome. Recurrent [[Inflammation]] and bile acid–driven injury promote cholangiocyte proliferation and [[Oxidative Stress]], fostering malignant transformation. Driver alterations commonly involve *IDH1/2*, *FGFR2* fusions, *BRAF*, *KRAS*, and *TP53*.

## Mechanism & Pathobiology

Tumor cholangiocytes (e.g., the KKU cell lines) display constitutive proliferative and survival signaling through [[EGFR]], [[MAPK Signaling]], PI3K–AKT, and IL-6/STAT3 axes. The desmoplastic stroma secretes factors that further drive invasion and chemoresistance. These features render cholangiocarcinoma notoriously resistant to the backbone agent [[Gemcitabine]] combined with cisplatin.

## Role of Ivermectin

[[Ivermectin]] has shown potent anticancer activity against cholangiocarcinoma in preclinical models. Mechanisms include: (1) **inhibition of proliferation** in a dose- and time-dependent manner in KKU214 and related lines; (2) **cell-cycle arrest** in S phase accompanied by induction of [[Apoptosis]]; and (3) **overcoming resistance**, as gemcitabine-resistant cells remain highly sensitive to ivermectin. Ivermectin's pleiotropic actions — PAK1 inhibition, mitochondrial depolarization, and disruption of survival signaling — make it a candidate repositioned therapy, especially for refractory or drug-resistant disease.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Standard care is surgical resection for localized disease, with systemic [[Chemotherapy]] (gemcitabine/cisplatin) for advanced cases and emerging targeted agents (FGFR2 inhibitors, IDH1 inhibitors) for molecularly defined subsets. Repurposing [[Ivermectin]] alone or with chemotherapy is under investigation to bypass resistance. Biomarkers such as CA19-9 aid monitoring, and liquid biopsy for *FGFR2* or *IDH* alterations guides precision approaches.
