# FADD

**FADD** (Fas-associated via death domain) is a critical adaptor protein that bridges death receptors to initiator caspases in the [[Extrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]].

## Function
FADD contains both a death domain (DD) and a death effector domain (DED).
- **DD binding**: The DD allows FADD to bind to activated death receptors like [[Fas]], [[TNFR1]] (via [[TRADD]]), [[DR4]], and [[DR5]].
- **DED binding**: The DED allows FADD to recruit pro-[[Caspase-8]], leading to the formation of the **Death-Inducing Signaling Complex** (DISC) and the subsequent activation of caspase-8.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - FLIP the Switch Regulation of Apoptosis and Necroptosis by cFLIP|Tsuchiya et al. 2015]]
> FADD is sub-stoichiometric to procaspase-8 at native DISCs: quantitative mass spectrometry finds several-fold more procaspase-8 than FADD at TRAIL- and CD95L-induced DISCs, with very little [[c-FLIP]] — consistent with short DED-chain oligomers rather than a 1:1 receptor–adaptor–caspase unit (Dickens; Schleich). Other work caps the (procaspase-8 + c-FLIP):FADD ratio near 2:1, arguing against long death-effector filaments (Majkut). FADD also nucleates the [[Ripoptosome]] ([[RIPK1]]–FADD–caspase-8–c-FLIP) independently of a receptor, and a TLR3–[[TRIF]]–caspase-8 complex can activate procaspase-8 with RIPK1 but *without* FADD.

## Role in Cancer
Downregulation of FADD is observed in several cancers, such as thyroid and lung carcinomas, contributing to resistance against death receptor-mediated apoptosis.

## Role in Erythrocyte Death
FADD is retained in mature erythrocytes, which keep the Fas/FasL → FADD → [[Caspase-8]] arm of the extrinsic pathway even though they have lost the mitochondrial machinery. In aged red cells [[Fas]] colocalizes with lipid-raft markers and oxidative stress drives Fas translocation into rafts, DISC assembly and caspase-8/-3 activation. Cigarette smoke extract triggers this cascade: [[p38 MAPK]] phosphorylation initiates Fas oligomerization and FADD recruitment, and erythrocytes from smokers show elevated p-p38 and assembled Fas-FADD complexes. In erythrocyte necroptosis, FADD instead assembles a RIPK1/FADD/caspase-8 necrosome in which caspase-8 activity *prevents* death — the mirror image of nucleated cells, and the reason caspase-8 acts as the fate switch between [[Eryptosis]] and [[Erythronecroptosis]].

## Connections
- [[Extrinsic Pathway]]: The essential adaptor for most death receptor signaling.
- [[Caspase-8]]: Recruited and activated by FADD.
- [[Fas]] / [[TNFR1]] / [[DR4]] / [[DR5]]: Receptors that utilize FADD.
- [[DISC]]: The signaling complex centered on FADD.
- [[Ripoptosome]]: Receptor-independent RIPK1–FADD–caspase-8–c-FLIP platform FADD nucleates when cIAPs fall.
- [[c-FLIP]]: DED-chain partner whose isoform ratio sets whether the FADD complex signals apoptosis, survival, or necroptosis.
- [[RIPK1]]: Death-domain partner exposing its DD to bind FADD during ripoptosome assembly.
- [[TRIF]]: TLR3 adaptor that can activate caspase-8 through a RIPK1-dependent, FADD-independent complex.
- [[Eryptosis]]: The Fas/FADD/caspase-8 arm retained in mature erythrocytes.
- [[Erythronecroptosis]]: The RIPK1/FADD/caspase-8 necrosome in which FADD participates with the opposite sign.
- [[p38 MAPK]]: Initiates DISC assembly downstream of cigarette smoke extract.
