# Temozolomide

## Chemical Properties & Classification

**Temozolomide** (TMZ; also referenced as AZD2218) is a rationally designed imidazotetrazine alkylating agent, a prodrug of the active species 5-(3-methyltriazen-1-yl)imidazole-4-carboxamide (**MTIC**). It belongs to the class of triazene alkylators alongside dacarbazine and procarbazine, and its chlorinated predecessor is the nitrosourea mitozolomide. The parent compound is an inert, lipophilic molecule; spontaneous ring-opening at physiological pH — accelerated above pH 7 — releases MTIC, which then decays to a methyldiazonium ion that transfers a methyl group to DNA.

Temozolomide is chemically stable in the stomach and modestly acidic environments and requires no enzymatic activation, which is why it can be given orally. It is also relatively water-soluble, and it crosses the [[Blood-Brain Barrier]] — a decisive property for a brain tumour.

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

- **Mechanism**: The methyldiazonium ion methylates the N7 position of guanine (~70% of adducts, repaired by [[Nucleotide Excision Repair]] and tolerated) and the O6 position of guanine (~5%, the toxic, mismatch-relevant lesion). O6-methylguanine mispairs with thymine and is normally removed by the suicide protein **O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT)**, the key determinant of TMZ sensitivity.
- **Kinetics**: Oral bioavailability is ~100%, with peak concentrations within 1–2 hours and an elimination half-life of ~1.8 hours. MTIC is short-lived (minutes), so damage accrues close to dosing. Exposure is roughly linear, and clearance is affected by body surface area, food, and CYP450 metabolism.
- **Cellular consequences**: Replication-blocking O6-methylguanine lesions trigger mismatch-repair-dependent futile cycles, arrest, and [[Apoptosis]]; in dividing tumour cells this dominates over repair. Non-lethal stress also produces senescence, autophagy, and mitotic catastrophe.

## Applications

- **Glioblastoma**: Concomitant radiotherapy plus daily TMZ (Stupp regimen) followed by adjuvant TMZ is the standard of care for newly diagnosed [[Glioblastoma]], improving survival over radiotherapy alone. TMZ is used in recurrent disease, in elderly or frail patients as an alternative to radiotherapy, and in trials of neoadjuvant or combination approaches.
- **Schedule**: Densely fractionated dosing (5 days on, 23 off) exploits the higher proportion of cycling tumour cells and allows haematopoietic recovery.
- **Resistance**: The dominant mechanism is MGMT-mediated repair of O6-methylguanine; MGMT promoter methylation predicts benefit, and its absence of methylation marks resistant disease. Other routes include hypermutator mismatch-repair-deficient phenotypes acquired under therapy, altered DNA-repair capacity, and detoxification.
- **Toxicity**: Myelosuppression (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia) is dose-limiting and requires blood monitoring; nausea, fatigue, alopecia, and mild hand-foot syndrome are common. Rare but serious events include *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (hence routine prophylaxis during extended courses) and opportunistic infection. Concern for potential secondary myeloid malignancy and gonadal toxicity underlies the age restrictions in some protocols.

> [!info] Why TMZ and not nitrosoureas?
> Its oral availability, ease of administration, absence of vesicant and pulmonary toxicity, and predictable dose reduction of myelosuppression made TMZ the cytotoxic backbone of modern neuro-oncology.
