# PAMP

## Overview

Pathogen-Associated Molecular Patterns (PAMPs) are conserved molecular motifs found in microorganisms but not in host cells. They are recognized by [[Pattern Recognition Receptors|pattern recognition receptors]] (PRRs) of the [[Innate Immune System]], triggering an inflammatory response. Common PAMPs include [[LPS]], flagellin, peptidoglycan, lipoteichoic acid, viral [[nucleic acids]], and fungal glucans.

## PRRs That Recognize PAMPs

- **[[TLR4]]**: Recognizes [[LPS]] from Gram-negative bacteria.
- **[[TLR5]]**: Recognizes flagellin.
- **[[TLR2]]** (with TLR1/TLR6): Recognizes peptidoglycan, lipoteichoic acid, and lipoproteins.
- **[[TLR3]]/[[TLR7]]/[[TLR8]]/[[TLR9]]**: Recognize viral and bacterial nucleic acids.
- **[[NOD-like receptors]]** (NLRs): Recognize bacterial peptidoglycan fragments.
- **[[RIG-I]]/[[MDA5]]**: Recognize viral RNA in the cytosol.
- **[[cGAS]]**: Recognizes cytosolic [[DNA]] of microbial origin.

## PAMP vs DAMP

| Feature | PAMP | DAMP |
|---------|------|------|
| Origin | Microbial | Host-derived |
| Examples | LPS, flagellin, viral RNA | ATP, HMGB1, DNA, uric acid |
| Receptors | TLRs, NLRs, RLRs | TLRs, RAGE, cGAS, NLRP3 |
| Function | Detect infection | Detect tissue damage |

## Role in Cancer

Chronic infection-driven inflammation via PAMP recognition contributes to [[Tumorigenesis]] (e.g., [[H. pylori]]→[[Gastric Cancer]], [[HPV]]→[[cervical cancer]], [[HBV]]/[[HCV]]→[[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]]). Conversely, synthetic PAMP analogs (e.g., [[Imiquimod]]/TLR7 agonist, [[BCG]]/TLR2/4 agonist) are used as cancer immunotherapies to activate antitumor immunity.

## Connections

- [[DAMP]]: Host-derived counterparts; both activate overlapping PRR pathways
- [[cGAS]]: Senses both microbial (PAMP) and host (DAMP) DNA in the cytosol
- [[Innate Immune System]]: Activated by PAMPs and DAMPs through PRRs
- [[Inflammation]]: Downstream consequence of PAMP recognition
