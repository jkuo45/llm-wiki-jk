# Fibronectin

Fibronectin is a high-molecular-weight (~440–500 kDa) dimeric glycoprotein of the extracellular matrix (ECM) that mediates cell–matrix adhesion, migration, and differentiation. Produced as a soluble plasma form by hepatocytes and as an insoluble cellular form by fibroblasts and other cells, fibronectin is a foundational scaffold of the [[Tumor Microenvironment]].

## Structure & Domains

Each fibronectin monomer is built from repeating modules — Types I, II, and III — folded into domains with distinct binding activities. The most famous is the Type III connecting segment (IIICS) that contains the universal **[[RGD peptide]]** motif, the binding site for [[Integrins]] α5β1, αvβ3, and αvβ5. Other domains bind collagen, heparin, [[Fibrinogen]], and cell-surface receptors. Alternative splicing of the IIICS region generates isoforms (e.g., extra domain A/B) that are upregulated during development and wound healing and in cancer.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Fibronectin binds integrins and triggers outside-in signaling that activates [[FAK]], [[Src]], [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]], and the [[MAPK]]/ERK pathways, regulating cytoskeleton remodeling, survival, and proliferation. Fibronectin fibrillogenesis is itself force-dependent: cell contractility exposes cryptic binding sites, creating a positive feedback loop that stiffens the matrix. This mechanical signaling is a key driver of [[EMT]] and invasion.

## Physiological Function

Fibronectin organizes the provisional matrix during embryogenesis, wound healing, and hemostasis, guiding cell migration and tissue morphogenesis.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

In cancer, fibronectin is remodeled and overexpressed at tumor borders and in the stroma, supporting [[Angiogenesis]], [[Metastasis]], and [[Chemotherapy]] resistance. The alternatively spliced extra domain A (EDA) form is a damage-associated molecular pattern that activates toll-like receptors and fuels [[Inflammation]]. Fibronectin-based [[Biomarker]]s (plasma fibronectin, oncofetal fibronectin) and integrin-targeted therapies (e.g., cilengitide, an αvβ3/αvβ5 antagonist) have been evaluated in [[glioblastoma]] and other tumors. Fibronectin also promotes cancer-associated fibroblast activation, a central node in the [[Tumor Microenvironment]].
