# Moxidectin

**Moxidectin** is a macrocyclic lactone endectocide of the **milbemycin** class (a methoxime derivative of nemadectin, from *Streptomyces cyanogriseus*). Although chemically a milbemycin, it is grouped with the [[Avermectin]] family throughout the vault's ivermectin literature. Veterinarily it is used for long-acting heartworm prevention and intestinal-nematode control in dogs, and it is also the first new drug approved for human onchocerciasis after ivermectin. It acts like [[Ivermectin]] but is more potent per milligram and persists far longer in the body — an advantage for dosing frequency and a reason its dose margin is narrower than [[Milbemycin Oxime]]'s.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

Moxidectin is a 23-methoxime milbemycin: it shares the 16-membered macrocyclic lactone ring of the avermectins but lacks the disaccharide (oleandrose) substituents characteristic of true avermectins. The removal of the sugars increases lipophilicity (log P ≈ 5.3), which drives its very long half-life and accumulation in adipose tissue. Products include **ProHeart 6 / ProHeart SR** (6-month, slow-release injectable heartworm prevention for dogs) and **Advantage Multi** (topical moxidectin + imidacloprid for dogs and cats).

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

Moxidectin, like ivermectin, is an agonist at invertebrate glutamate- and GABA-gated chloride channels, causing sustained chloride influx, paralysis, and death of nematodes and arthropods. Relative to ivermectin it is roughly 100-fold more lipid-soluble, distributes widely into fat, and is cleared much more slowly — an elimination half-life of several weeks in dogs, which is what enables the six-month ProHeart dosing schedule. This persistence is a double-edged sword: beneficial for prolonged cover, but any overdose cannot be quickly cleared.

## Applications

- **Heartworm prevention** (*Dirofilaria immitis*): monthly topical (Advantage Multi) or 6-month injectable (ProHeart 6) in dogs.
- **Intestinal nematodes**: hookworms, roundworms, and (with the topical formulation) whipworms.
- **Sarcoptic mange**: Advantage Multi is labeled for mite control (demodicosis and sarcoptic mange) in dogs.
- **Human onchocerciasis** (river blindness): EMA-approved (2018) for *Onchocerca volvulus* — the vault's ivermectin review cites Prichard & Geary 2019 on moxidectin utility against parasitic nematodes and anthelmintic resistance.

## Safety in Dogs and the MDR1 Gene

Moxidectin is a P-glycoprotein ([[P-gp]]/ABCB1) substrate. Dogs homozygous for the *ABCB1-1Δ* (MDR1) deletion — collies, Australian shepherds, Shelties, and related herding breeds — clear macrocyclic lactones from the CNS poorly, so moxidectin toxicity becomes a real risk, particularly with the injectable sustained-release product, which cannot be withdrawn. Label heartworm-preventive doses are generally tolerated even in these breeds, but because moxidectin is more potent and longer-acting than ivermectin, **off-label dosing is more dangerous**, and [[Milbemycin Oxime]] is widely regarded as the safer macrocyclic choice for MDR1-affected dogs. ProHeart 6 has additionally had product-safety scrutiny (adverse-event reports and a 2004–2005 FDA market withdrawal and reformulation), so injectable use warrants a veterinary decision.

## Cancer Repurposing

The vault's ivermectin review catalogs moxidectin among the macrocyclic lactones with emerging (largely preclinical) anticancer-repurposing interest, consistent with the family-wide [[Drug Repositioning]] literature centered on [[Ivermectin]].
