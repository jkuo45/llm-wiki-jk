# Avermectin

**Avermectins** are a family of 16-membered macrocyclic lactones produced by the soil actinomycete *Streptomyces avermectinius*. Discovered through the pioneering work of [[Satoshi Ōmura]] and [[William C. Campbell]] — recognized by the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine — avermectins are among the most important anthelmintic and insecticidal natural products ever developed. [[Ivermectin]] is the best-known semi-synthetic derivative.

## Chemical Structure & Classification

Avermectins consist of a macrocyclic lactone ring fused to a disaccharide (α-L-oleandrose)₂. Naturally occurring avermectins (A1a, A2a, B1a, B2a and their minor homologs) differ in substitution; the B1a component is the precursor to ivermectin (22,23-dihydroavermectin B1). They act as agonists of glutamate-gated and γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA)-gated chloride channels in invertebrates.

## Pharmacological Action

In parasites, avermectins bind invertebrate glutamate- and GABA-gated chloride channels, causing sustained Cl⁻ influx, paralysis, and death. Mammalian CNS channels are structurally distinct and poorly penetrated (especially with the blood–brain barrier intact), underpinning their selectivity and safety margin.

## Relevance to Cancer Research

Beyond its antiparasitic role, avermectin itself shows direct **anticancer activity** — it inhibits cancer-cell proliferation and, like ivermectin, engages the [[Glutamate-gated Cl- channels]] target that underlies the class's selective safety. Ivermectin, the flagship avermectin, is under active investigation for **anticancer repositioning**:
- Inhibits the [[Wnt/β-catenin]], [[AKT/mTOR]], and [[PAK1]] oncogenic pathways.
- Impairs [[Mitochondrial Respiration]] and promotes [[Apoptosis]] in tumor cells.
- Enhances the efficacy of conventional [[Chemotherapy]] and may overcome [[Multidrug Resistance]].
- Decades of safe use (via programs like the [[African Programme for Onchocerciasis Control]]) support repurposing trials.

## History & Development

Avermectin discovery emerged from a systematic screen of soil microbes at the [[Kitasato Institute]] and was developed commercially by [[Merck & Co. Inc]]. The drug donation program underpinned global elimination efforts against onchocerciasis and lymphatic filariasis.
