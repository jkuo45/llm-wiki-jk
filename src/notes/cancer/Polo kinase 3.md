# Polo kinase 3

Polo kinase 3 (Plk3) is the stress-responsive member of the [[Polo-like kinase]] family, also catalogued as [[PLK3]]. It phosphorylates the anti-apoptotic protein [[Bcl-xL]] at Ser49 during cell cycle progression and at checkpoint states, modulating Bcl-xL's capacity to restrain [[Apoptosis]]. This distinct phosphorylation site (Ser49, versus the Ser62 targeted by [[PLK1]]) reflects Plk3's role in stress- and checkpoint-coupled death regulation rather than mitotic execution.

## Structure & Mechanism

Plk3 contains an N-terminal catalytic kinase domain and a C-terminal polo-box domain for substrate targeting, analogous to other polo kinases. It is induced by genotoxic and oxidative stress and activated through stress-kinase cascades. Phosphorylation of [[Bcl-xL]] at Ser49 by Plk3 alters the protein's anti-apoptotic potency, tipping damaged cells toward mitochondrial apoptosis and thereby functioning as a checkpoint enforcement mechanism.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

By coupling Bcl-xL phosphorylation to cell-cycle checkpoints, Plk3 eliminates cells with unresolved damage, protecting against transformation. Loss or silencing of Plk3 in [[Cancer]] permits continued survival of stressed cells, contributing to the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]] (evasion of apoptosis, genomic instability). Plk3 therefore acts as a tumor-suppressive stress sensor.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Plk3/Bcl-xL(Ser49) phosphorylation is a candidate biomarker of stress-induced apoptosis and a conceptual therapeutic node; restoring Plk3 activity may sensitize tumors to [[Chemotherapy]] and radiation. Care must be taken to distinguish Plk3 from the oncogenic mitotic kinase [[PLK1]] when interpreting Polo-kinase biology.
