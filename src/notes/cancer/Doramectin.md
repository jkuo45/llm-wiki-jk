# Doramectin

**Doramectin** is a semi-synthetic [[Avermectin]] (25-cyclohexylavermectin B1) developed by Pfizer for veterinary use, principally in **livestock** (cattle, sheep, swine) under the brand name Dectomax. It behaves pharmacologically like [[Ivermectin]] — same chloride-channel target and endectocide spectrum — but carries a cyclohexyl group that extends its elimination half-life, enabling single-dose long-acting control of parasites in production animals. It is **not a dog-labeled product**; although it is used off-label in dogs for some mite infestations, its narrow margin in [[P-gp]]-deficient breeds makes it a poor choice for collie-type (MDR1) dogs.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

Doramectin is a 25-cyclohexyl derivative of avermectin B1, retaining the full avermectin structure (16-membered macrocyclic lactone with an oleandrose disaccharide). The cyclohexyl substituent increases lipophilicity relative to ivermectin, slowing clearance and prolonging tissue persistence — the property exploited for single-injection parasite control programs in cattle and swine (injectable and pour-on formulations).

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

Like ivermectin, doramectin agonizes invertebrate glutamate- and GABA-gated chloride channels, producing sustained Cl⁻ influx and parasite paralysis/death; mammalian CNS selectivity depends on channel differences and the intact blood–brain barrier, protected by P-glycoprotein efflux. Its longer elimination half-life (days in cattle, with a large volume of distribution into fat) supports extended-duration efficacy relative to ivermectin.

## Applications

- **Cattle and swine**: broad-spectrum control of gastrointestinal and pulmonary nematodes, grubs, lice, mites, and flies (Dectomax).
- **Off-label use in dogs**: occasional veterinary use against sarcoptic mange and other mites; not approved, and not meaningfully used for heartworm prevention in companion-animal practice.
- **Cancer repurposing**: the vault's ivermectin review records doramectin among the avermectin-family compounds with reported (preclinical) anticancer activity.

## Safety in Dogs and the MDR1 Gene

Doramectin is a P-glycoprotein ([[P-gp]]/ABCB1) substrate, and dogs carrying the *ABCB1-1Δ* (MDR1) deletion — collies, Australian shepherds, Shelties, and other herding breeds — are at risk of neurotoxicity (mydriasis, ataxia, tremors, coma) even at modest doses. Because doramectin is built for large animals, the typical dosing volumes and concentrations are impractical and hazardous to apply to dogs, and companion-animal clinicians who need an ivermectin-like drug in an MDR1-affected dog should prefer [[Milbemycin Oxime]] or [[Selamectin]] at labeled doses.
