## Overview

Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is the most common malignancy of the oral cavity, arising from the mucosal epithelium and driven by tobacco, alcohol, and human papillomavirus exposure. Despite advances in surgery and chemoradiation, outcomes for advanced disease remain poor, prompting interest in molecular targets.

## Molecular Pathophysiology

In OSCC, [[SIRT3]] is overexpressed, and its downregulation has antiproliferative activity and sensitizes carcinoma cells to radiation and to [[Cisplatin]]. Directionally, elevated SIRT3 supports tumor growth and therapy resistance in this cancer type, while SIRT3 loss restores sensitivity.

> [!warning] Context-dependent sirtuin function
> This contrasts with SIRT3's broader tumor-suppressive role in mouse models, showing that sirtuin function is context-dependent: a single sirtuin can be protective or oncogenic depending on tumor type and molecular background.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

OSCC presents as non-healing ulcers or masses of the tongue, floor of mouth, or buccal mucosa, often with cervical lymph node involvement. Diagnosis is confirmed by biopsy; molecular profiling increasingly includes assessment of sirtuin expression, which may hold prognostic and predictive value.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Standard care combines surgical resection, radiation, and systemic therapy. The finding that SIRT3 loss sensitizes cells to radiation and cisplatin identifies [[SIRT3]] as a candidate therapeutic target, and SIRT3 inhibition is being explored as a radiosensitizing strategy.
