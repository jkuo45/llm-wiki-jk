# BAY 87-2243

**BAY 87-2243** (CAS 1227158-85-1) is a ubiquinone-binding site (Q-site) dependent inhibitor of mitochondrial [[Complex I]] (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase). Originally developed as a hypoxia-inducible factor-1 (HIF-1) pathway inhibitor, BAY 87-2243 was later rediscovered as a potent Complex I inhibitor with an aromatic ring chain topology critical for Q-site binding.

## Mechanism of Action

BAY 87-2243 binds the ubiquinone-binding site (Q-site) of [[Complex I]], blocking electron transfer from NADH to ubiquinone. This leads to:
- Increased NADH:NAD+ ratio (reductive stress)
- Depletion of [[NAD+]] and ATP
- [[AMPK]] activation
- Collapse of the [[TCA cycle]] in glutamine-dependent cells

## MYC^high^ Selectivity

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]>
> [[BAY 87-2243]] showed the highest MYC selectivity (alongside [[Mubritinib]]) in a drug screen of 526 oncology compounds across [[triple-negative breast cancer|TNBC]] cell lines. Molecular docking analysis confirmed high Q-site binding affinity. In MYC^high^ TNBC cells, BAY 87-2243 induced prominent [[AMPK]] activation, consistent with Complex I inhibition as its primary mechanism.

## Structural Relationship

BAY 87-2243, [[Mubritinib]], and the clinically tested IACS-10759 share an aromatic ring chain topology — a molecular scaffold critical for Q-site Complex I inhibition. This structural class represents a distinct mechanism from biguanide Complex I inhibitors like [[Metformin]].
