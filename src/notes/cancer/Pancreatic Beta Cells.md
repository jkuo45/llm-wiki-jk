## Overview

Pancreatic beta cells are endocrine cells located in the pancreatic islets of Langerhans that synthesize and secrete [[Insulin]] in response to glucose, maintaining blood glucose homeostasis. Their destruction or dysfunction is central to both type 1 and type 2 diabetes.

## Function & Physiology

Beta cells sense glucose via uptake and metabolism, coupling glycolytic flux to insulin granule exocytosis. Glucose-stimulated insulin secretion requires intact mitochondrial function and a coordinated signaling network that includes ERK and STAT3 pathways.

## Cytokine-Mediated Injury & Humanin Protection

In type 1 diabetes, progressive accumulation of [[Lymphocytes]] within the pancreatic islets leads to beta-cell destruction. The cytokines [[IL-1β]], [[TNFα]], and [[Interferon-gamma]] released by T cells and activated [[Macrophages]] induce beta-cell [[Apoptosis]]. [[Humanin]] — a mitochondrial-derived peptide — decreases serum starvation- and cytokine-induced apoptosis in the pancreatic beta-cell line NIT-1, protecting against these cytokines in an [[ERK]]- and [[STAT3]]-dependent manner.

> [!info] Protective mechanism
> Humanin's survival signal in beta cells is transduced through ERK and STAT3, linking mitochondrial-derived peptide signaling to cytokine resistance and islet preservation.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

Humanin improves glucose tolerance and delays the onset of diabetes in NOD mice, a model of type 1 diabetes. In NOD mouse pancreata, humanin treatment dramatically suppressed inflammation and lymphocyte infiltration. This suggests mitochondrial-derived peptides may be therapeutic targets for type 1 diabetes.
