# Rituximab

**Rituximab** (Rituxan, MabThera) is a chimeric IgG1κ monoclonal [[Antibody]] that binds the extracellular loop of **CD20**, a B-cell-restricted membrane protein expressed from pre-B-cell stage onward and largely absent from plasma cells and hematopoietic stem cells. It is the archetypal targeted cancer agent in haematological malignancy and a standard B-cell-depleting immunotherapy in autoimmune disease.

## Mechanism of Action

CD20 has no known physiological ligand and, unlike most surface targets, **is not internalised** on antibody binding — so rituximab acts almost entirely from the cell surface. Killing occurs through several cooperating mechanisms:

- **Complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC)** — the exposed Fc region recruits C1q and assembles the membrane attack complex.
- **Antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC)** — FcγRIIIa on macrophages and NK cells drives release of granzymes and perforin.
- **Apoptotic and non-apoptotic signalling** — CD20 is a scaffold for the Lyn–Fyn–Syk–BLNK–PLCγ2 and PI3K–Akt axes; ligation promotes mitochondrial destabilisation, caspase-independent death, and in some settings [[Apoptosis]].
- **Fc-mediated functions** — agglutination of CD20-bearing cells, and depletion of B cells by FcγR-dependent phagocytosis.

> [!info] B-cell depletion is deep and long
> Because CD20 is absent on plasma cells and [[Stem Cells|hemopoietic stem cells]], the B-cell pool recovers by re-emergence of naive precursors over months. This dissociation of humoral protection from antibody production is the basis of rituximab's use in [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], granulomatosis with polyangiitis, idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura, pemphigus vulgaris, myasthenia gravis, and antibody-mediated transplant rejection — and also of its late humoral immunodeficiency.

## Clinical Use

In oncology, rituximab is standard first-line therapy for [[Lymphoma]] subtypes (diffuse large B-cell, follicular, mantle cell) and combined with chemoimmunotherapy or [[Venetoclax]]-based regimens in [[Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia]]. In haematology it is also used in immune thrombocytopenia, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, and post-transplant antibody-mediated rejection.

## Adverse Effects and Caveats

> [!warning] Not a benign antibody
> - **Infusion reactions**: cytokine release during infusion; mitigated by pre-medication and slow rate (e.g. acetaminophen, antihistamine, corticosteroid).
> - **Late-onset neutropenia**: can occur weeks to months after the last dose, sometimes with no preceding infection.
> - **Hepatitis B reactivation**, including fulminant hepatitis — screen all patients for HBsAg/anti-HBc before dosing.
> - **Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy**, rare but reported; **hypogammaglobulinaemia** with repeated courses.
> - Avoid live vaccines during and after therapy; immunoglobulin levels should be checked after repeated cycles.

## Resistance

Resistance arises from loss of CD20 surface expression (antigen shedding, CD20-negative relapse), blockade of the effector arm (increased [[Complement System|complement]] regulators CD55/CD59), Fc receptor polymorphisms affecting ADCC strength, and alternative B-cell survival signalling such as PI3K–AKT or BTK-dependent pathways. Dual-targeting agents (obinutuzumab with chlorambucil, CAR-T cells directed to CD19/CD20) attempt to bypass some of these routes.

## Connections

- [[Lymphoma]] — Rituximab defines modern first-line therapy for B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphomas, where it is combined with cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin and vincristine or with lenalidomide, and its addition converted curable-disease categories into chronic ones.
- [[Venetoclax]] — Venetoclax-based regimens were designed in de novo and relapsed CLL on a backbone of rituximab or obinutuzumab, and the two are frequently paired; venetoclax-associated [[Tumor Lysis Syndrome]] risk is stratified in the same patients.
- [[Methotrexate]] — Rituximab and low-dose methotrexate form a standard rituximab-based remission-maintenance combination in rheumatoid arthritis and are combined in selected anti-drug-antibody protocols in enzyme-replacement settings.
- [[Pompe Disease]] — In Pompe disease, rituximab is used experimentally with methotrexate to suppress anti-drug antibodies to GAA enzyme replacement therapy.
- [[Bortezomib]] — Both are antibody-based targeted agents in myeloma and lymphoma that act on distinct, non-overlapping death pathways (surface signalling vs. proteotoxic stress).
