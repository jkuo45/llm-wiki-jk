# Annexin V

**Annexin V** is a ~35 kDa member of the annexin family of calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding proteins. It binds with high affinity to [[Phosphatidylserine]] (PS), a phospholipid normally restricted to the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane. During early [[Apoptosis]], PS is externalized by scramblases (e.g., Xkr8, TMEM16F), and Annexin V conjugated to fluorescent or biotin tags is the canonical reagent for detecting this "eat-me" signal.

## Structure & Binding Mechanism

Annexin V folds into a compact core of four α-helical repeats surrounding a type II calcium-binding site. Ca²⁺ bridges conserved acidic residues to the negatively charged PS headgroup. In the presence of Ca²⁺, Annexin V binds PS with nanomolar affinity and forms two-dimensional arrays on membranes, enabling robust labeling without membrane permeabilization.

## Role in Apoptosis Assays

The **Annexin V / propidium iodide (PI)** dual-stain is the standard flow-cytometry assay:
- **Annexin V⁺ / PI⁻** → early apoptotic.
- **Annexin V⁺ / PI⁺** → late apoptotic / secondary necrotic.
- **Annexin V⁻ / PI⁻** → viable.
- **Annexin V⁻ / PI⁺** → necrotic.

This distinguishes [[Apoptosis]] from [[Necrosis]] and complements [[Caspase-3]] activation assays. In studies of [[melittin]] and [[Bee Venom]], Annexin V/PI staining characterizes death kinetics and subtype, revealing dose-dependent shifts between apoptotic and necrotic fates.

## Physiological Function

Endogenous Annexin V participates in anticoagulation (binding PS on activated platelets), membrane repair, and vesicle trafficking. Its PS-binding property is co-opted experimentally as a death marker.

## Cancer Research Relevance

Annexin V assays are indispensable for screening [[Chemotherapy]] agents, [[BH3 mimetics]], and natural products (e.g., [[melittin]], [[Apis mellifera]] venom) for pro-apoptotic activity. Imaging variants (Annexin V-Cy5.5) enable non-invasive detection of therapy-induced apoptosis in tumors, serving as an early pharmacodynamic biomarker.
