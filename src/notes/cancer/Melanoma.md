# Melanoma

**Melanoma** is an aggressive [[Cancer|malignant]] neoplasm arising from [[Melanocytes|melanocytes]] — the pigment-producing cells of the skin and mucous membranes. It accounts for the majority of skin-cancer deaths due to its high propensity for early [[Metastasis]] and its capacity to evade immune surveillance. The incidence of melanoma has been rising steadily worldwide, with UV radiation exposure being the principal environmental risk factor.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Melanoma development is driven by genetic and epigenetic alterations that activate proliferative signaling pathways:

- **BRAF V600E mutations**: Present in ~50% of cutaneous melanomas; constitutively activates the MAPK/ERK pathway. Paradoxically, [[BRAF]] activation in melanocytes of benign nevi (moles) induces [[Senescence|oncogene-induced senescence (OIS)]], restraining progression — rare cell variants that bypass OIS evolve into melanoma.
- **NRAS mutations**: Present in ~20% of melanomas; activates RAS-MAPK and PI3K-AKT signaling.
- **Loss of CDKN2A**: The [[p16INK4A|p16]]/p14ARF tumor suppressor locus is frequently inactivated, enabling escape from senescence.
- **UV-induced DNA damage**: Characteristic C>T transitions at dipyrimidine sites; defects in DNA repair promote mutagenesis.
- **Epigenetic reprogramming**: DNA methylation changes and histone modifications contribute to tumor heterogeneity and drug resistance.

> [!important] Senescence as a Double-Edged Sword
> Senescent melanocytes in nevi are held in check by OIS driven by BRAF V600E. However, the SASP produced by these senescent cells — particularly [[IL-8]] and [[GROα]] — can stimulate the proliferation of rare premalignant cells that have escaped senescence, potentially driving melanoma development.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

- **ABCDE criteria**: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter >6 mm, Evolving shape
- **Breslow thickness**: Primary prognostic factor; determines staging and surgical margins
- **LDH**: Serum lactate dehydrogenase level; prognostic marker in metastatic disease
- **Sentinel lymph node biopsy**: Standard staging procedure for intermediate-thickness melanomas
- **Molecular biomarkers**: BRAF/NRAS mutation status, PD-L1 expression, tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) density

## Therapeutic Landscape

- **Surgery**: Wide local excision with appropriate margins; sentinel lymph node biopsy for staging
- **Targeted therapy**: [[BRAF]] inhibitors (vemurafenib, dabrafenib) combined with MEK inhibitors (trametinib) for BRAF-mutant melanoma
- **Immunotherapy**: Checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD-1: nivolumab, pembrolizumab; anti-CTLA-4: ipilimumab) have transformed outcomes
- **TIL therapy**: Adoptive cell transfer of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes for advanced disease
- **Radiation**: Adjuvant radiation for high-risk disease; palliative for metastases

> [!tip] Senolytic Potential
> Senescent melanocytes accumulate in aging skin and may contribute to a pro-tumorigenic microenvironment via SASP. Senolytic strategies could potentially clear these cells and reduce melanoma risk in aging populations.
