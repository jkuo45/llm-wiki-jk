# Mebendazole

**Mebendazole** is a [[Benzimidazole]] anthelmintic used for decades against a range of intestinal nematode infections (ascariasis, pinworm, hookworm, trichuriasis, and others). It has become a prominent example of [[Drug Repositioning]] in oncology because it interferes with [[Microtubule]] assembly by binding [[Tubulin]] and modulates several oncogenic signaling pathways, showing preclinical and early clinical activity against [[Cancer]] types such as [[glioblastoma]], [[Colorectal Cancer]], and [[Lung Cancer]].

## Chemistry & Mechanism of Action

Mebendazole is a carbamate-substituted benzimidazole. Its antiparasitic and anticancer actions derive largely from binding β-tubulin at the colchicine site, preventing microtubule polymerization. In rapidly dividing cells this disrupts mitotic spindle formation, arrests the [[Cell Cycle]] at G2/M, and triggers [[Apoptosis]] and mitotic catastrophe. Unlike taxanes (which stabilize microtubules), benzimidazoles inhibit polymerization, yielding a distinct mechanistic and toxicity profile.

## Pathway Modulation

Beyond tubulin, mebendazole inhibits multiple kinases and signaling networks that cancer cells co-opt:
- **Hedgehog signaling:** suppression limits GLI-driven proliferation in medulloblastoma/glioblastoma contexts.
- **Wnt/β-catenin:** attenuation reduces survival and stemness programs.
- **[[NFκB]]:** inhibition lowers expression of anti-apoptotic and inflammatory target genes.

This multi-target engagement makes mebendazole attractive where single-pathway resistance emerges.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Mebendazole's favorable safety record and oral availability facilitate repurposing. Preclinical studies show growth inhibition of glioblastoma, colorectal, lung, and melanoma models, often through microtubule catastrophe plus pathway suppression. Its ability to cross the blood–brain barrier (limited but meaningful) is particularly relevant to brain tumors such as glioblastoma, where standard therapies penetrate poorly.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Several clinical trials have evaluated mebendazole alone or with standard [[Chemotherapy]] in patients with glioblastoma and other refractory solid tumors, generally for feasibility, tolerability, and signal of disease stabilization. Related benzimidazoles ([[Albendazole]], [[Fenbendazole]], [[Oxfendazole]]) share the tubulin-binding mechanism and are explored similarly. Combinations with microtubule-stabilizing or apoptotic agents are under study to deepen responses.
