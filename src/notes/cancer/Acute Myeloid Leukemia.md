## Overview

Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) is a hematologic malignancy characterized by clonal expansion of immature myeloid blasts that fail to differentiate, leading to bone marrow failure and impaired hematopoiesis. It is the most common acute leukemia in adults and arises through the sequential acquisition of mutations in [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cells]].

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Recurrent mutations in the DNA methyltransferase [[DNMT3a]] occur in approximately 20% of AML cases, with the R882H hotspot being the most frequent. These mutations reduce methyltransferase activity, disrupting DNA methylation at regulatory regions controlling stem cell behavior. The resulting epigenetic dysregulation cooperates with additional mutations — including in genes such as [[p53]], FLT3, NPM1, and IDH — to drive leukemogenesis. Critically, [[DNMT3a]]-mutant clones are frequently detectable in preleukemic states, linking age-associated [[Clonal Hematopoiesis]] to overt AML.

> [!info] Preleukemic origin
> [[DNMT3a]]-mutated clones can persist in the bone marrow years before diagnosis, surviving chemotherapy and seeding relapse — a key rationale for targeting the mutant clone rather than only the blast population.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

Patients typically present with fatigue, cytopenias, infection, and bleeding. Diagnostic workup relies on morphology, flow cytometry, cytogenetics, and molecular profiling; mutational status of [[DNMT3a]], FLT3, NPM1, and TP53 informs risk stratification and treatment selection.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Standard-of-care therapy combines intensive induction chemotherapy with consolidation, often followed by allogeneic stem cell transplantation in eligible high-risk patients. Emerging targeted approaches include FLT3 inhibitors, IDH inhibitors, and venetoclax-based regimens. The persistence of preleukemic [[DNMT3a]]-mutant clones remains a major challenge for durable remission.
