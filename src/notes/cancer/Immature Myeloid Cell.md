## Overview

Immature myeloid cells are myeloid-lineage cells that have not completed terminal differentiation. Under steady-state conditions they are scarce, but they expand dramatically under pathological stress including cancer and chronic inflammation. Their classification reflects an arrested differentiation state rather than a single lineage, with polymorphonuclear and monocytic subsets recognized.

## Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells

The most functionally important population of immature myeloid cells is the [[Myeloid-Derived Suppressor Cells]] (MDSCs) — a heterogeneous population with potent immunosuppressive activity. MDSCs suppress T-cell and NK-cell function through multiple mechanisms, including arginine depletion, reactive oxygen species, and induction of regulatory T cells.

> [!warning] Immunosuppressive brake
> In senescence contexts, MDSCs are recruited by SASP factors and blunt anti-tumor and anti-senescence immunity, shifting the immune balance away from clearance of damaged cells.

## Role in Aging, Inflammation & Cancer

In the tumor microenvironment, immature myeloid cells are recruited by chemokines and cytokines and contribute to immune evasion, angiogenesis, and therapy resistance. In senescence contexts, MDSCs are recruited by SASP factors and blunt anti-tumor and anti-senescence immunity, shifting the immune balance away from clearance. This positions immature myeloid cells as a key immunosuppressive cell type bridging aging, inflammation, and cancer.

## Clinical Significance

MDSC abundance correlates with poor prognosis and reduced checkpoint-inhibitor efficacy in many cancers, making them an attractive therapeutic target. Strategies to deplete or differentiate immature myeloid cells — restoring terminal maturation into [[Macrophages]] and dendritic cells — are under active investigation.
