## Overview

The TGFβ superfamily is a large family of structurally related cytokines, including TGF-βs, activins/inhibins, bone morphogenetic proteins, and growth differentiation factors. These ligands regulate cell fate, proliferation, differentiation, and inflammation through serine/threonine kinase receptor signaling.

## Ligands & Signaling Mechanism

Members signal through heteromeric type I/type II serine/threonine kinase receptors, which phosphorylate receptor-activated [[SMAD]] transcription factors (e.g., [[SMAD2]], [[SMAD4]]). Signaling output is tightly controlled by ligand availability, receptor expression, and inhibitory SMADs. The family includes [[TGF-β3]], activin A, BMP2, and [[GDF15]].

## Role in Senescence & the SASP

[[Activin A]] — a homodimer of inhibin beta-A (INHBA) subunits — is a member of this superfamily. Like other family members, it signals through serine/threonine kinase receptors to regulate cell fate, proliferation, and inflammation. Its classification situates it among the SASP factors with paracrine arrest-inducing capacity, alongside TGF-β1, BMP2, and [[GDF15]].

> [!info] Shared paracrine function
> Activin A is a structurally defined ligand distinct from TGF-β1 but shares functional overlap, particularly in inducing cell-cycle inhibitors in receiving cells during paracrine senescence.

## Clinical Relevance

TGFβ superfamily signaling is deeply implicated in fibrosis, immunosuppression, and tumor progression. TGFβ pathway inhibitors are in clinical development, while modulation of activin/myostatin signaling is explored in cachexia and muscle wasting.
