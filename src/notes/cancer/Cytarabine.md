# Cytarabine

**Cytarabine** (cytosine arabinoside, ara-C, 1-β-D-arabinofuranosylcytosine; trade name Cytosar) is a cytidine analog antimetabolite and the backbone of induction chemotherapy for [[Acute Myeloid Leukemia]].

## Mechanism of Action

Cytarabine is a pyrimidine nucleoside analog in which the ribose sugar is replaced by arabinose, inverting the 2′-OH stereochemistry. Like other cytidine analogs it is a **prodrug**:

1. **Activation** — [[Deoxycytidine Kinase]] performs the first, rate-limiting phosphorylation; nucleoside diphosphate and triphosphate kinases complete the ladder to ara-CTP.
2. **DNA incorporation** — ara-CTP competes with dCTP for [[DNA Polymerase]]. The stereochemical inversion makes the 3′-OH a poor acceptor for the next phosphodiester bond, so once ara-C is incorporated, chain elongation **stalls**.
3. **Self-amplification** — stalled chains trigger [[DNA Damage]] checkpoints and repair attempts, generating lesions and breaks that become lethal.
4. **S-phase specificity** — incorporation requires ongoing DNA synthesis, so cytotoxicity tracks the fraction of cells in S phase. This is why cytarabine efficacy is fundamentally a kinetic problem: exposure duration relative to cycle time matters as much as dose.

> [!warning] Cytarabine-induced neurotoxicity has a specific mechanism
> A 2025 mechanistic study showed that cytarabine causes DNA double-strand breaks during **TET-mediated active 5-methylcytosine demethylation**, by interrupting TDG-dependent base excision repair; these breaks are then converted into deletions and translocations by DNA ligase 4. Postmitotic Purkinje and Golgi cells in the cerebellum are the only neuronal populations with high damage levels, because they carry high methylated cytosine and express TET targets in highly transcribed gene bodies controlling movement coordination — which explains the long-recognized cerebellar neurotoxicity of cytarabine. Gemcitabine causes only single-strand breaks in neurons, repaired by DNA ligase 3 with minimal toxicity, providing a rational explanation for the differing toxicity profiles of closely related agents (Liu et al., *Nature* 2025; PMID 40562930).

## Clinical Use

- **AML induction and consolidation** — cytarabine at high doses ("Hi-C") with an anthracycline remains standard induction for fit adults, with an anthracycline-free backbone of low-dose cytarabine plus venetoclax and azacitidine for older or unfit patients.
- **Liposomal combination** — CPX-351 (Vyxeos), a liposomal 5:1 molar encapsulation of cytarabine with daunorubicin, is approved for newly diagnosed therapy-related AML or AML with myelodysplasia-related changes, and showed benefit over conventional 7+3 in the pivotal trial along with reduced intestinal and cardiotoxicity.
- **Other uses** — high-dose cytarabine in CNS-directed therapy for acute lymphoblastic leukemia and CNS lymphoma.

## Toxicity

Dose-limiting and characteristic toxicities:

- **Bone marrow suppression** — profound and prolonged neutropenia and thrombocytopenia from S-phase-specific killing of marrow precursors; the toxicity that limits both dose and schedule.
- **Cerebellar and cortical neurotoxicity** — the mechanism is now understood mechanistically as TET/BER-orchestrated double-strand break formation (above). Cumulative dosing matters.
- **Conjunctival and corneal irritation** — a common topical toxicity, often managed with eye drops during infusion.
- **Non-hematologic** — nausea, mucositis, hepatic and renal dysfunction, and rare pulmonary toxicity.

## Connections

- [[Deoxycytidine Kinase]] — performs the first and rate-limiting phosphorylation that switches cytarabine from an inert nucleoside into cytotoxic triphosphates.
- [[Acute Myeloid Leukemia]] — the disease for which cytarabine-based induction is standard of care.
- [[DNA Polymerase]] — the direct target of incorporated ara-CTP; chain elongation stalls because of the arabinose 2′-stereochemistry.
- [[DNA Damage]] — stalled replication forks and repair attempts at incorporated ara-C generate the lesions that become lethal.
- [[Chemotherapy]] — cytarabine is a core antimetabolite backbone and defines the Hi-C dosing paradigm.
- [[Apoptosis]] — replication catastrophe from stalled forks and unrepaired lesions executes via programmed cell death.
- [[Cell Cycle]] — S-phase specificity is the pharmacological basis for cytarabine's schedule dependence.
- [[Pancreatitis]] — an uncommon but well-recognized toxicity of cytarabine, distinct from the drug's marrow effects.
- [[Radiotherapy]] — cytarabine is frequently combined with or sequenced against radiation in CNS disease, with additive marrow and neurotoxicity.
- [[Decitabine]] — a related deoxycytidine analog also activated through the deoxycytidine salvage entry point, but a hypomethylating agent rather than a cytotoxic one.
- [[Nitric Oxide]] and [[Reactive Oxygen Species]] — cytarabine metabolite generation contributes to oxidative and nitrosative stress in some settings, a lesser-studied strand compared with the replication-stall mechanism.
