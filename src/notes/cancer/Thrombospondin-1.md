## Overview

Thrombospondin-1 (TSP-1, encoded by THBS1) is a large matricellular glycoprotein that modulates cell-matrix interactions, cell adhesion, survival, inflammation, and angiogenesis. It is secreted by platelets, endothelial cells, and macrophages, particularly under stress.

## Structure & Domains

TSP-1 is a homotrimer containing multiple functional domains, including the N-terminal domain, procollagen-like and type I (properdin-like) repeats, EGF-like repeats, and a C-terminal domain. These domains mediate binding to diverse receptors including CD36, CD47, integrins, and proteoglycans.

## CD36-Mediated Signaling

The [[CD36]] receptor binds thrombospondin-1, and this interaction activates [[FYN]] kinase and [[p38 MAPK]] signaling that influences cell adhesion, survival, and inflammation. Thrombospondin-1 thus acts as a CD36 ligand that shapes [[Macrophages|macrophage]] behavior. The directionality is ligand binding to intracellular signaling.

> [!warning] Receptor promiscuity
> Thrombospondin-1 has many other receptors (e.g., [[Integrins]], CD47), so CD36 is one node in a broader network rather than the sole mediator of TSP-1 function.

## Physiological & Pathological Roles

TSP-1-CD36 signaling can influence macrophage phagocytosis, [[Angiogenesis]], and platelet function, expanding CD36's role beyond lipid uptake. TSP-1 is a major anti-angiogenic factor in the tumor microenvironment and is implicated in thrombosis, wound healing, and age-related inflammation.
