# Trametinib

Trametinib (Mekinist, GSK1120212) is a selective, reversible, allosteric inhibitor of MEK1 and MEK2, the dual-specificity kinases immediately upstream of ERK1/2. It was the first MEK inhibitor granted FDA approval, on 29 May 2013, for BRAF V600E- or V600K-positive unresectable or metastatic [[Melanoma]].

> [!info] Target, not the lesion
> Trametinib does not bind the ATP pocket of MEK. It binds an allosteric site adjacent to the ATP site in a domain unique to MEKs, stabilising the inactive conformation of the kinase. Because it competes with neither ATP nor substrate, its potency is insensitive to the ATP concentration and it is unusually selective — MEK1/2 IC50 values are in the low nanomolar range while the broader kinome is spared.

## Mechanism of Action

The RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK ([[MAPK]]) cascade transmits growth-factor signals from a membrane receptor to the nucleus:

1. [[RAS]] is GTP-loaded in response to receptor tyrosine kinase signalling.
2. RAS activates RAF (BRAF, CRAF, ARAF), which phosphorylates and activates MEK1/2.
3. MEK1/2 dual-phosphorylate ERK1/2 on a Thr-X-Tyr motif.
4. ERK phosphorylates hundreds of substrates, including transcription factors, ribosomal proteins and cytoskeletal regulators, driving proliferation and survival.

Trametinib interrupts step 3, so ERK output collapses regardless of which upstream kinase drove it. This is why the drug works in RAS-mutant as well as RAF-mutant tumours, and why it does not by itself block the upstream signalling event.

> [!warning] The affinity trap of the class
> MEK inhibition relieves ERK-dependent negative feedback onto RAS and RAF, which frequently drives RAS-GTP loading and, in BRAF-mutant cells, paradoxical RAF activation and/or RAF-MEK alternate splicing into larger, drug-resistant isoforms. Combination with a BRAF inhibitor such as dabrafenib is therefore not optional in current practice; trametinib monotherapy is now largely historical.

## Pharmacokinetics

Trametinib has among the most favourable profiles in the MEK class: long effective half-life (roughly 3.5–4 days at steady state), high oral bioavailability (~72%), and dose-independent, CYP3A4-independent clearance. That permits once-daily 2 mg dosing with a single loading dose and allows co-administration with CYP3A4 substrates or inducers without adjustment. It is metabolised predominantly by deacetylation to an inactive carboxylate, followed by glucuronidation.

## Clinical Role

- **BRAF V600-mutant melanoma**: FDA-approved as a single agent (2013) and, far more importantly, in combination with dabrafenib (2014). The combination delays resistance, prolongs progression-free survival and reduces the emergence of BRAF inhibitor resistance relative to BRAF inhibitor monotherapy.
- **NSCLC**: approved with dabrafenib for BRAF V600E-positive metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.
- **Anaplastic thyroid carcinoma**: with dabrafenib for locally advanced or metastatic BRAF V600E disease.
- **Histopathology-driven use**: BRAF V600E status, not histology, now determines eligibility, so tumour-agnostic tissue-agnostic pathways carry this combination.
- **Low-grade serous ovarian carcinoma** and other MAPK-altered rare tumours use MEK inhibition off-label or in trials.
- Combination trials with checkpoint blockade are prominent: MEK inhibition raises tumour antigen presentation and T-cell infiltration, and the resulting combination has proved far more effective in BRAF-mutant melanoma than either agent alone.

> [!tip] The reactive-oxygen-species angle
> MEK inhibitors have a documented off-target effect on the electron transport chain, producing mitochondrial ROS that contribute to their cytotoxicity. This connects their mechanism to the vault's mitochondrial and oxidative-stress work, and is one reason MEK inhibition has been reported to intersect with [[Autophagy]] and senescence programs.

## Toxicity

The class-limiting toxicities are dermatologic, gastrointestinal, ocular and cardiopulmonary:

- **Rash, dermatitis and acneiform eruption** — the most common adverse reaction, sometimes severe.
- **Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting**.
- **Ocular**: blurred vision, visual disturbance, central serous-like chorioretinopathy; serious events are rare but require early ophthalmology referral.
- **Cardiomyopathy** — a decline in left ventricular ejection fraction, which is why a baseline and periodic echocardiogram is required. This risk is higher with trametinib than with BRAF inhibitors.
- **Interstitial lung disease and pneumonitis**.
- **Peripheral oedema, fatigue, pyrexia**.
