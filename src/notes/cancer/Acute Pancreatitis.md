## Overview

Acute pancreatitis is a sudden inflammatory disorder of the pancreas in which premature activation of digestive enzymes triggers autodigestion, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative damage, and a systemic inflammatory response. Severity ranges from mild, self-limiting disease to severe necrotizing pancreatitis with multi-organ failure.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Gallstones and alcohol are the most common triggers. Pathologically, acinar cell injury releases activated proteases, generating [[Oxidative Stress]] and mitochondrial dysfunction that amplify [[Inflammation]] within the gland. Gut microbiota dysbiosis exacerbates injury: in mouse models, normobiotic fecal microbiota transplantation raised serum [[NMN]] levels, enhancing pancreatic [[NAD+]] biosynthesis.

## Mitochondrial & Metabolic Axis

[[NMN]] derived from the gut microbiota ameliorates acute pancreatitis injury in experimental models. Mechanistically, NMN elevates [[NAD+]] and activates the SIRT3-PRDX5 pathway, which mitigates pancreatic mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative damage, and inflammation in a partially [[SIRT3]]-dependent manner. This positions microbiota-produced NAD+ precursors as an important mediator of the protective effects of fecal microbiota transplantation.

> [!tip] Clinical implication
> The gut–pancreas axis suggests that microbiota composition — and NAD+ precursor availability — can shape the severity of acute pancreatitis, offering a dietary or microbiota-targeted avenue for intervention.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Management is largely supportive (fluid resuscitation, analgesia, nutritional support). Identifying patients at risk of severe disease remains a clinical priority. The NAD+/SIRT3 axis is emerging as a druggable pathway, though evidence is predominantly preclinical.
