# Capecitabine

**Capecitabine** is an oral [[Prodrug]] of the antimetabolite [[5-Fluorouracil]] (5-FU), developed so that cytotoxic 5-FU exposure could be delivered systemically without the severe gut and hepatic first-pass injury that makes oral 5-FU itself unusable. It is a fluoropyrimidine carbamate, marketed as Xeloda, and is a standard component of therapy in [[Colorectal Cancer|colorectal]], [[Colon Cancer|colon]], [[Rectal Cancer|rectal]] and metastatic [[Breast Cancer]].

## Mechanism of Activation

Three sequential enzymatic steps convert capecitabine to 5-FU, each catalysed by an enzyme that is enriched in tumour tissue:

1. **Carboxylesterase** hydrolyses capecitabine to 5'-deoxy-5-fluorocytidine (5'-DFCR), largely in the liver.
2. **Cytidine deaminase** converts 5'-DFCR to 5'-deoxy-5-fluorouridine (5'-DFUR) — the step limiting capecitabine's hepatic inactivation relative to tegafur.
3. **Thymidine phosphorylase** cleaves 5'-DFUR to 5-FU, and this enzyme is high in many carcinomas relative to surrounding normal tissue, giving roughly a 3-fold higher 5-FU concentration in colorectal tumour than in adjacent healthy tissue after oral dosing (PMID 11286326).

> [!warning] Tumour-preferential activation is not absolute
> The concentration advantage is well documented in primary colorectal tumour but does not hold in liver metastases, where 5-FU concentrations are still antitumour-sufficient. The mechanism is a relative enrichment, not a tumour-specific trap.

## Pharmacodynamics

5-FU is converted intracellularly to FdUMP, FUTP and FdUTP, which injure cells by two routes:

- **Thymidylate synthase inhibition** — FdUMP forms a stable ternary complex with [[Thymidylate Synthase]] and the folate cofactor 5,10-methylene-THF, depleting dTMP and producing "thymineless death". This is the basis for synergy with [[Folinic acid]] (leucovorin) and the basis for sensitisation by [[FOLFOX]].
- **RNA and DNA incorporation** — FUTP is incorporated into RNA, disrupting RNA processing and protein synthesis; FdUTP and FUTP are incorporated into DNA, causing strand breaks and triggering [[DNA Damage]] response and [[Apoptosis]]. Inhibition of ribonucleotide reduction is another point of indirect interference.

Resistance arises chiefly through increased thymidylate synthase, reduced thymidine phosphorylase, and catabolism of 5-FU itself.

## Pharmacokinetics & Toxicology

Capecitabine is rapidly and almost completely absorbed (Tmax ~1.5–2 h) with a short parent-drug half-life of ~0.75–0.9 h. Because 5-FU is generated continuously from the circulating 5'-DFUR pool, oral capecitabine reproduces the pharmacodynamics of continuous 5-FU infusion rather than of bolus dosing. Interpatient variability in pharmacokinetics is high (27–89%), largely from variability in the activating and catabolic enzymes.

The rate-limiting catabolic enzyme is **dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD; gene *DPYD*)**, which reduces 5-FU to dihydrofluorouracil. Pre-treatment toxicity signature: severe diarrhoea, mucositis, neutropenia, hand-foot syndrome and neurotoxicity.

> [!important] Pharmacogenetics
> Partial or complete *DPYD* deficiency occurs in ~3–5% of Northern European populations and converts standard dosing into potentially fatal toxicity. Regulatory guidance (FDA label; CPIC) is to test *DPYD* genotype before starting and to avoid capecitabine in complete deficiency.

Other clinically relevant points: cholestatic/hepatotoxic injury, transient *CPK* elevation, and — relevant to the vault's [[Warfarin]] note — a documented interaction with oral vitamin K antagonists (clinically significant *PT*/INR rises and bleeding). Combination partners include [[trastuzumab]], [[docetaxel]], [[paclitaxel]], [[Gemcitabine]], [[Cyclophosphamide]], and the colorectal doublets FOLFOX (oxaliplatin + folinic acid) and FOLFIRI (irinotecan + folinic acid).
