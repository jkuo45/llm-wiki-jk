## Overview

VEGFR3 (Flt-4) is the receptor tyrosine kinase that mediates lymphangiogenesis. It is expressed predominantly on lymphatic endothelium and is the primary receptor through which [[VEGFc]] promotes lymphatic vessel formation and sprouting.

## Structure & Function

VEGFR3 is a receptor tyrosine kinase that binds members of the [[VEGF Family]], most notably [[VEGFc]] and VEGF-D. Signaling through VEGFR3 promotes lymphatic endothelial cell survival, proliferation, and migration.

## Canonical VEGF-C/VEGFR3 Axis

[[VEGFc]] signals primarily through VEGFR3 on lymphatic endothelium, promoting lymphangiogenesis and lymphatic vessel sprouting. This receptor usage distinguishes VEGF-C from [[VEGF]]-A's preferential VEGFR2 engagement and underlies its role in lymphatic remodeling around senescent tissue.

> [!info] Canonical function
> The relationship between VEGF-C and VEGFR3 is a canonical, well-established function in lymphatic biology.

## Role in Senescence & the SASP

In the [[SASP]], VEGF-C-VEGFR3 signaling contributes to lymphatic vessel formation and potentially to immune cell trafficking routes. The lymphatic axis around senescent tissue may shape local immune access and clearance of damaged cells.

## Clinical Relevance

VEGF-C/VEGFR3 signaling promotes lymphatic metastasis in many cancers, and blockade of this axis is being explored to prevent lymphatic dissemination. VEGFR3 is also relevant to lymphedema and developmental lymphatic disorders.
