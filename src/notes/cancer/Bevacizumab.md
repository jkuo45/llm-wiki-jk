# Bevacizumab

Bevacizumab (Avastin) is a humanised IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds all isoforms of [[VEGF|VEGF-A]] and neutralises its activity. It was the first agent approved on the rationale of inhibiting tumour [[Angiogenesis]] rather than directly targeting tumour-cell proliferation or DNA.

> [!info] How it works
> Tumours outgrow their blood supply, become hypoxic, and switch on [[Hypoxia|HIF-1α]]-driven transcription. VEGF-A is the principal effector of this switch, secreted by tumour cells and by tumour-associated stromal and myeloid cells, and it acts on endothelial cells through VEGFR1 and [[VEGFR2]] to drive proliferation, migration, survival and — critically — vascular permeability. Bevacizumab intercepts the ligand, so endothelial cells never receive the signal and the tumour cannot recruit or maintain new vessels. Existing vessels are not destroyed; the drug blocks new sprouting and induces regression of immature vessels.

The mechanistic prediction that anti-VEGF would shrink tumours produced disappointing single-agent results. The drug's clinical value depends almost entirely on combination with cytotoxic chemotherapy, where blocking angiogenesis deprives tumour cells of oxygen and nutrients at the same time as the chemotherapy damages them — a mechanism sometimes described as vascular normalisation improving drug delivery.

## Clinical Role

Approved indications span a wide range of tumour types, nearly all as maintenance or combination therapy:

- Metastatic [[Colon Cancer]] — the original approval, in combination with fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy.
- Non-small cell lung cancer (with carboplatin/paclitaxel or pemetrexed), later withdrawn in some settings for lack of benefit in the maintenance setting.
- Glioblastoma (with radiotherapy and temozolomide), an unusual label based on a survival benefit in the pivotal trial.
- Renal cell carcinoma (with interferon), ovarian cancer (with carboplatin/paclitaxel, platinum-sensitive relapse), and cervical cancer.
- Biosimilars have expanded access considerably in recent years.

> [!warning] Why the same trial failed twice
> Two large breast-cancer trials (E2100, AVF2772) were stopped early for futility and harm, and the breast indication was never granted, despite the drug's activity in other settings. The standard reading is that the tumour types chosen were not sufficiently angiogenic to be VEGF-dependent — the same marker that makes it work in clear cell renal carcinoma makes it fail elsewhere. This is the clearest clinical demonstration that angiogenesis is not a universal cancer dependency.

## Adverse Effects

Bevacizumab carries a distinctive toxicity profile with a strong mechanistic logic:

- **Hypertension** — the most common adverse event, from blockade of endothelial NO signalling and vessel stiffening.
- **Proteinuria** — dose-dependent, from glomerular injury; high-grade (grade 3/4) proteinuria occurs in roughly 2% of patients.
- **Haemorrhage** — mucosal bleeding (epistaxis is common) and serious gastrointestinal, intracranial, pulmonary and aortic bleeding; fatal cases occurred in early trials.
- **Impaired wound healing** — a leading reason for avoiding perioperative use; surgery is typically deferred until at least 8 weeks after the last dose.
- **Gastrointestinal perforation** — rare but potentially fatal, and the reason it is contraindicated in unresected abdominal malignancy.
- **Arterial thromboembolism** — myocardial infarction, stroke, peripheral arterial events.
- **Venous thromboembolism**, pulmonary embolism.
- **Congestive heart failure** in breast cancer exposure, and **ovarian torsion**.
- **Rare but important**: osteonecrosis of the jaw, intestinal perforation, and reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome.
- **Hypothyroidism** is reported with prolonged exposure.
- **Proteinuria, neutropenia and febrile neutropenia** are more frequent when combined with chemotherapy than with the antibody alone.

> [!info] Predictive biomarkers are absent
> No biomarker has been validated to select patients for bevacizumab. VEGF levels, microvessel density and hypertension as a pharmacodynamic marker have all failed to validate robustly. This contrasts with the situation for the kinase inhibitors that dominate modern [[Targeted Therapy]].

## Connections

- [[VEGF]] — the target ligand; bevacizumab is the monoclonal-antibody counterpoint to the small-molecule VEGF receptor kinase inhibitors and the anchor of the vault's antiangiogenic axis.
- [[VEGFR2]] — the endothelial receptor through which VEGF signals; blocking the ligand versus blocking the receptor is the central design comparison in this therapeutic class.
- [[Angiogenesis]] — the process being suppressed, and the mechanism by which a tumour grows beyond a few millimetres.
- [[Hypoxia]] — the upstream stimulus that induces VEGF; HIF-1α stabilisation is the switch bevacizumab holds off.
- [[Targeted Therapy]] — bevacizumab is the founding antiangiogenic targeted agent and the template for vascular-oncology drug development.
- [[Colon Cancer]] — the indication that produced the original 2004 approval.
- [[Brain Cancer]] — the glioblastoma indication, where it was approved with radiotherapy and temozolomide.
- [[Kidney]] — the organ whose glomerular injury produces the characteristic proteinuria toxicity.
- [[Proteinuria]] — the most recognisable renal toxicity of VEGF blockade, and a mechanistically direct readout of on-target effect.
- [[Hypertension]] — the most common adverse event and a consequence of the same endothelial signalling blockade.
- [[Wound Healing]] — the clinical axis most often disrupted in practice, since antiangiogenic therapy and surgery interact badly.
- [[Chemotherapy]] — bevacizumab is nearly always administered with cytotoxic chemotherapy rather than as monotherapy.
- [[Inflammation]] — the VEGF axis is bidirectional with inflammation, which is why bevacizumab has been explored in inflammatory and fibrotic indications.
