## Overview

Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (PJS) is a rare autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline loss-of-function mutations in STK11, encoding the serine/threonine kinase [[LKB1]]. It is characterized by hamartomatous polyps of the gastrointestinal tract, mucocutaneous pigmentation, and a markedly elevated risk of multiple cancers.

## Etiology & Pathophysiology

Directionally, [[LKB1]] deficiency sits upstream of this cancer predisposition. LKB1 is a critical [[Tumor Suppressor]] and master metabolic regulator that activates the [[AMPK]] energy-sensing pathway. Because LKB1 loss compromises [[AMPK]] activation, cells fail to properly sense energy stress, promoting growth and genomic instability. The connection of LKB1 to [[AMPK]] and sirtuin axes explains the metabolic features of the syndrome.

> [!info] Metabolic tumor suppression
> LKB1-AMPK signaling links nutrient sensing to growth control; its loss uncouples energy stress detection from growth restraint, explaining both the metabolic phenotype and cancer risk in PJS.

## Clinical Presentation & Diagnostics

Patients present with recurrent abdominal pain, intussusception, and intestinal polyps, often in childhood. Characteristic mucocutaneous pigmentation appears on the lips, buccal mucosa, and digits. The hamartomatous polyps are a defining feature, with elevated lifetime risks of colorectal, breast, pancreatic, and gynecologic cancers.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Management is surveillance-based — regular endoscopy, imaging, and cancer screening — aimed at early detection. The syndrome illustrates the clinical importance of the LKB1-AMPK-sirtuin network, and AMPK activators and metabolic interventions are being explored for their preventive potential.
