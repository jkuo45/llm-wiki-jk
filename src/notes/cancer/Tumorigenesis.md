# Tumorigenesis

## Overview

Tumorigenesis (carcinogenesis) is the process by which normal cells acquire genetic and [[epigenetic]] alterations that drive progressive transformation into malignant cells. It proceeds through [[Initiation]], [[Promotion]], and [[Progression]] stages, characterized by acquisition of the [[Hallmarks of Cancer]].

## Stages

1. **Initiation**: Irreversible [[DNA damage]] (mutations) in proto-oncogenes (e.g., [[KRAS]], [[MYC]]) or tumor suppressor genes (e.g., [[p53]], [[RB1]], [[APC]]).
2. **Promotion**: Clonal expansion of initiated cells driven by [[growth factors]], [[cytokines]], and [[inflammatory]] signals.
3. **Progression**: Accumulation of additional mutations, [[genomic instability]], [[angiogenic]] switch, [[EMT]], and acquisition of invasive and [[metastatic]] capacity.

## Role of Senescence

[[Senescence]] acts as a barrier to tumorigenesis by permanently arresting proliferation of pre-malignant cells. This is mediated by [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence]] (OIS), triggered by [[KRAS]] G12V, [[BRAF]] V600E, or other oncogenic insults. However, the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] from senescent cells can paradoxically promote tumorigenesis by creating a pro-inflammatory, immunosuppressive microenvironment.
