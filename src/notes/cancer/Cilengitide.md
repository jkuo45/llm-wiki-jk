# Cilengitide

**Cilengitide** (EMD 121974) is a cyclic [[RGD peptide]] derivative that acts as a potent, selective antagonist of the αvβ3 and αvβ5 [[Integrins]]. It was principally developed as an anti-[[Angiogenesis]] agent for the treatment of [[glioblastoma]] and other solid tumors by blocking integrin-mediated endothelial and tumor cell survival signaling.

## Chemistry & Pharmacology

Cilengitide is a cyclized pentapeptide (c(RGDf(NMe)V)) in which the Arg-Gly-Asp motif mimics the integrin-binding sequence of matrix proteins such as vitronectin and osteopontin. Cyclization and N-methylation confer proteolytic stability and high affinity for αvβ3/αvβ5. By occupying the ligand-binding pocket, cilengitide prevents integrin engagement with the extracellular matrix, disrupting downstream focal adhesion kinase (FAK) and PI3K–AKT survival signaling required for new vessel formation.

## Mechanism of Action

During [[Angiogenesis]], αvβ3 and αvβ5 are upregulated on sprouting endothelium and on some tumor cells. αvβ3 binds angiopoietin-1 and cooperates with vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) signaling; αvβ5 transactivates VEGF receptor-2. Cilengitide competitively blocks these interactions, inducing endothelial cell detachment, anoikis, and vessel regression. It also interferes with pericyte recruitment and may sensitize tumors to [[Chemotherapy]] and radiation by normalizing or pruning the tumor vasculature. Note that, unlike CendR peptides, cilengitide lacks the C-terminal penetration switch and acts primarily at the vascular adhesion step.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Cilengitide advanced to phase III trials in newly diagnosed [[glioblastoma]] (combined with temozolomide/radiotherapy) based on promising phase II activity, but the pivotal trials did not meet overall-survival endpoints in the unselected population, though a molecularly defined MGMT-unmethylated subgroup showed possible benefit. It remains a reference tool compound for integrin-targeted therapeutics and is studied in combination with VEGF-pathway inhibitors and integrin-directed imaging. Lessons from cilengitide underscore the need for biomarker selection (e.g., integrin expression, MGMT status) in anti-angiogenic drug development.
