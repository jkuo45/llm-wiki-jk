# Andy Crump

**Andy Crump** is a scientist, writer, and science communicator who has documented the discovery and global health impact of [[Ivermectin]], the avermectin-derivative antiparasitic discovered by [[Satoshi Ōmura]] and [[William C. Campbell]]. Crump's work centers on the [[Kitasato Institute]] and the collaborative story of how a microbial natural product from *Streptomyces avermectinius* became one of the most widely used medicines in human history.

## Contributions

Crump has authored books and extensively cited historical accounts tracing ivermectin from soil bacterium to Nobel-recognized therapy. His writing situates the drug within the broader history of [[Avermectin]] research and the public–private partnership with [[Merck & Co. Inc]], which established the donation program that delivered billions of treatments for onchocerciasis and lymphatic filariasis.

## Relevance to the Cancer Knowledge Base

Ivermectin has emerged as a candidate for **anticancer repositioning**, and Crump's historical documentation of its safety, pharmacology, and global distribution informs that research translation:

- Ivermectin inhibits the [[Wnt/β-catenin]], [[AKT/mTOR]], and [[PAK1]] signaling axes exploited by tumors.
- It impairs [[Mitochondrial Respiration]] and promotes [[Apoptosis]] in cancer models.
- Decades of population-scale safety data (from APOC and similar programs) de-risk clinical repurposing trials.

Thus Crump's chronicling of ivermectin provides essential provenance for its investigation as a low-cost [[Chemotherapy]] adjunct.

## Science Communication

Crump exemplifies the role of clear communication in translating basic microbiology into public health impact — a model relevant to how cancer research findings reach clinicians and patients.
