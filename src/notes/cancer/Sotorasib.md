## Overview

Sotorasib (AMG 510) is the first approved covalent inhibitor of the [[KRAS]] G12C mutant, targeting the previously undruggable KRAS oncoprotein. It is indicated for KRAS G12C-mutant non-small cell lung cancer and has shown activity in other G12C-driven solid tumors.

## Mechanism of Action

[[KRAS]] is a small GTPase and among the most commonly mutated oncogenes, constitutively driving MAPK and PI3K output when mutated. Sotorasib covalently binds the mutant cysteine in the KRAS G12C switch-II pocket, locking KRAS in its inactive GDP-bound state and blocking downstream signaling. The directionality runs from Sotorasib toward KRAS — Sotorasib is the active agent and KRAS the recipient of the effect.

> [!info] Active agent vs. target
> Clinically, Sotorasib positions itself as a therapeutic modality against mutant KRAS; efficacy is shaped by biomarker selection and the eventual emergence of resistance.

## Clinical Applications

Sotorasib has demonstrated clinical efficacy in previously refractory [[non-small-cell lung cancer]] harboring KRAS G12C mutations, and it is being studied in combination with immune checkpoint inhibitors and other MAPK pathway agents.

## Challenges & Resistance

Acquired resistance arises through secondary KRAS mutations, MET amplification, and MAPK pathway reactivation, motivating combination strategies and next-generation inhibitors. Response correlates with G12C allele selection and co-mutation profile, reinforcing the importance of biomarker-driven patient selection.
