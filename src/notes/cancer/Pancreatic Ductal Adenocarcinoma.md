## Overview

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is the most common and lethal form of [[Pancreatic Cancer]], characterized by dense desmoplasia, late diagnosis, and profound therapy resistance. It is driven by initiating mutations — most often in KRAS — followed by loss of tumor suppressors.

## Pathophysiology & Oncogenic Signaling

PDAC develops through sequential accumulation of mutations in oncogenes and tumor suppressors, with activating KRAS mutations present in over 90% of cases. A hallmark is the induction of oncogene-driven [[Cellular Senescence]] in premalignant lesions, which can either restrain or promote progression depending on the surrounding metabolic and inflammatory milieu.

> [!warning] Context-dependent NAD+ effects
> In an oncogenic mouse model, NMN supplementation (500 mg/kg body weight daily for 13 days) significantly increased the secretory phenotype associated with pro-inflammatory senescence, promoting PDAC progression. This cautions that NAD+ precursor supplementation could accelerate tumorigenesis in certain premalignant settings even as it confers benefits in normal aging and metabolic disease.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

Patients typically present with jaundice, weight loss, and abdominal pain. Diagnosis relies on cross-sectional imaging, endoscopic ultrasound with biopsy, and CA 19-9 as a serum marker. Most patients present with unresectable or metastatic disease.

## Therapeutic Landscape

Standard care includes surgical resection for localized disease, followed by adjuvant chemotherapy (FOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine-based). The context-dependent interplay between [[NAD+]] metabolism and cancer biology remains an active research question, with the balance between healthspan benefits and tumor promotion requiring careful evaluation.
