## Overview

MCPIP1 (monocyte chemoattractant protein-induced protein 1), also known as Regnase-1 and encoded by ZC3H12A, is an endoribonuclease that degrades specific messenger RNAs and microRNAs. It is a critical negative regulator of inflammatory responses.

## Structure & Function

MCPIP1 contains a conserved PIN-domain RNase fold responsible for its catalytic activity. Through its RNase activity it destabilizes a set of cytokine and inflammation-related mRNAs, directly limiting inflammatory gene expression.

## Mechanism: The miR-9/SIRT1 Axis

MCPIP1 alleviates inflammatory responses by promoting [[SIRT1]] expression through modulation of [[MicroRNA|miR-9]]. Directionally, MCPIP1 sits upstream of increased SIRT1. Higher SIRT1 then suppresses [[NF-κB]]-driven inflammation, creating an anti-inflammatory circuit centered on SIRT1 induction.

> [!info] RNA-protein control of sirtuins
> MCPIP1's regulation of the miR-9/SIRT1 axis illustrates how RNA-binding proteins control sirtuin abundance — a non-enzymatic route to modulating NAD+-dependent deacetylation.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

This pathway is relevant to chronic inflammation, where MCPIP1 levels are altered in [[Macrophages]] and other immune cells. Loss of MCPIP1 activity promotes unrestrained inflammation and autoimmune pathology, while its regulation of the SIRT1/NF-κB axis links RNA metabolism to metabolic and aging-related inflammatory programs.
