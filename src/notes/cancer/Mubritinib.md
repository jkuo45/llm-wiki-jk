# Mubritinib

**Mubritinib** (TAK-165, CAS 366017-09-6) is a ubiquinone-binding site (Q-site) dependent inhibitor of mitochondrial [[Complex I]] (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase). Originally developed as a receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor, mubritinib was later rediscovered as a potent Complex I inhibitor with an aromatic ring chain topology critical for Q-site binding.

## Mechanism of Action

Mubritinib binds the ubiquinone-binding site (Q-site) of [[Complex I]], blocking electron transfer from NADH to ubiquinone. This inhibits [[NAD+]] regeneration from NADH, leading to:
- Increased NADH:NAD+ ratio (reductive stress)
- Depletion of ATP
- [[AMPK]] activation
- Collapse of the [[TCA cycle]] when combined with [[Glutaminolysis]] inhibition

## MYC^high^ Selectivity

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]]
> [[Mubritinib]] showed the highest MYC selectivity (largest difference in drug sensitivity score between MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups) in a screen of 526 oncology drugs across [[triple-negative breast cancer|TNBC]] cell lines. Even at low nanomolar concentrations (10 nM), mubritinib induced significant MYC-associated cell death in glutamine-deprived conditions. The 10 nM dose showing MYC^high^ selective cytotoxicity is ~20x lower than the plasma concentration reported to delay AML development in mouse studies.

## In Vivo Efficacy

In MYC^high^ TNBC PDX models (MAXFTN MX1 and HCI-002), mubritinib monotherapy showed weak or no antitumor effect. However, the combination of mubritinib + [[CB-839]] (glutaminase inhibitor) persistently inhibited tumor growth, producing 2–5x smaller tumors than the control group. The antitumor effect was achieved without systemic toxicity.

## Clinical Context

Mubritinib (TAK-165) was previously studied in clinical trials for HER2-expressing tumors (NCT00034281). Its rediscovery as a Q-site Complex I inhibitor, combined with the MYC^high^ selectivity data, provides a biological rationale for dose de-escalation studies in MYC^high^ cancers.
