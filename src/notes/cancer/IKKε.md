# IKKε

**IKKε** (IκB kinase epsilon, also termed IKK-i) is an inducible member of the IκB kinase family distantly related to [[IKKalpha|IKKα]] and [[IKKβ]]. Unlike the classical IKK complex, IKKε is not a stable component of the NEMO-associated signalosome but is transcriptionally induced by type I interferons and viral infection. It functions in both innate antiviral immunity and oncogenic signaling, and it can phosphorylate the key inhibitor of apoptosis [[XIAP]], driving its degradation and thereby lowering the threshold for [[Apoptosis]].

## Structure & Regulation

IKKε is a serine/threonine kinase with an N-terminal kinase domain and a C-terminal scaffold/oligomerization region. Its expression is induced through interferon regulatory factor (IRF) pathways downstream of Toll-like receptors and RIG-I-like receptors. IKKε activation requires phosphorylation at activation-loop residues and is reinforced by TBK1, its closest paralog, with which it shares overlapping substrates in the type I IFN response (IRF3/IRF7).

## Mechanism of Action

A defining apoptotic function of IKKε is the phosphorylation of [[XIAP]], a potent endogenous caspase inhibitor. Phosphorylation marks XIAP for degradation, relieving caspase suppression and sensitizing cells to death stimuli. This mechanism is particularly relevant during virus-induced apoptosis, where IKKε helps eliminate infected cells. IKKε also contributes to NFκB and IFN signaling by phosphorylating IKKα/β-associated substrates and IRFs, linking pathogen sensing to both inflammatory and cell-death programs.

## Physiological & Pathological Function

IKKε is normally expressed at low levels in resting tissues but is strongly induced during viral infection as part of the host defense program. In cancer, IKKε is frequently amplified or overexpressed in subsets of breast (especially basal-like/triple-negative), ovarian, and lung tumors, where it promotes proliferation, [[EMT]], and [[Metastasis]] through NFκB- and IRF-dependent target genes. Its ability to degrade XIAP couples antiviral death signaling to tumor cell vulnerability.

## Clinical & Research Relevance

Because IKKε overexpression supports tumor growth and therapy resistance, it is under investigation as a therapeutic target. Selective IKKε/TBK1 inhibitors (e.g., amlexanox, BX795, nedisertib) have shown antitumor activity in IKKε-driven models and can synergize with agents that rely on apoptosis induction. The XIAP-degradation function suggests IKKε activation or mimicry could sensitize resistant tumors to death ligands and cytotoxic [[Chemotherapy]].
