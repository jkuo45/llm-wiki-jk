## Highlights

- [[MYC]] induces elevated [[OXPHOS]] in breast cancer cells
- [[Complex I]]-regenerated [[NAD+]] couples OXPHOS to the [[TCA cycle]] to fuel anabolic growth
- MYC rewires the TCA cycle to run on [[Glutamine]] to meet biosynthetic demand
- Co-inhibition of complex I and [[Glutaminolysis]] is synthetic lethal in MYC^high^ tumors

## Summary

In many cancers, stably elevated [[MYC]] levels drive sustained activation of anabolic programs and the [[Cell Cycle]], creating opportunities for the [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic-lethal]] targeting of MYC^high^ tumors. Enhanced [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] respiration is a hallmark of MYC overexpressing cancer cells. Mitochondrial respiration sustains the [[TCA cycle]] by regenerating [[NAD+]] through [[Complex I]]-mediated oxidation of NADH, supporting the anabolic demand of MYC-driven cells. Metabolic carbon tracing reveals that MYC shifts the TCA cycle carbon source from [[Glucose uptake|glucose]] to [[Glutamine]]. Inhibition of the glutamine-fueled TCA cycle using NAD+-depleting complex I inhibitors promotes MYC-dependent [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic lethality]] in breast cancer cells. In mouse models of MYC^high^ tumors, combined inhibition of complex I and [[Glutaminolysis]] produces persistent suppression of tumor growth. Altogether, the elevated respiration of MYC^high^ cells supports a glutamine carbon-enriched TCA cycle that meets anabolic demand, rendering MYC^high^ tumors selectively vulnerable to mitochondrial respiration and glutaminolysis inhibitors.

> [!important] Key Finding
> MYC^high^ breast cancers are selectively vulnerable to combined inhibition of [[Complex I]] and [[Glutaminolysis]] — a [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic lethal]] strategy that collapses the glutamine-fueled [[TCA cycle]] by depleting [[NAD+]].

## Introduction

[[MYC]] is a basic helix-loop-helix leucine zipper (bHLH-Zip) transcription factor and one of the most studied cancer driver genes. MYC protein is frequently expressed at elevated levels in human cancer, and the dominant presence of MYC^high^ cells in cancer associates with aggressive behavior, treatment resistance, and unfavorable prognosis. For example, in [[Breast Cancer]], about half of the tumors contain high proportions of MYC^high^ cells, and typically, these tumors represent the most aggressive subtypes such as [[triple-negative breast cancer]] (TNBC), Luminal B tumors, breast cancer metastases, and drug-resistant tumors.

Elevated levels of MYC possess oncogenic activity, which deregulates a variety of cellular programs that control the [[Cell Cycle]] and proliferation, [[DNA Repair|DNA replication]] and chromatin structure, metabolic pathways, cell adhesion, immunity, [[Apoptosis]], and many biosynthetic pathways. Some MYC-reprogrammed pathways create specific vulnerabilities, which form a rationale for therapeutic intervention via [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic lethal strategies]]. Currently investigated targets, which upon inhibition predominantly or selectively harm MYC^high^ cells include, for example, cyclin-dependent kinases, anti-apoptotic [[Bcl-2 family]] members, mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1/2 ([[mTOR]]), aurora-B kinase, and Polo-like kinase 1.

The principle of [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic lethal strategies]] is to identify and target pathways and phenotypes enriched in the cancer cells — to direct the harmful effects of drugs on cancer cells while simultaneously sparing healthy cells to avoid drug-related adverse effects.

> [!info] Synthetic Lethality Context
> Some of the most selective MYC^high^-associated cellular phenotypes include metabolic dependencies, such as those rendering cancer cells reliant on specific nutrients. [[MYC]] overexpression has been shown to mediate dependence on non-essential amino acids such as [[Glutamine]] and cysteine. MYC-induced glutamine and cysteine dependence is thought to arise from the onset of [[Anaplerosis|glutamine anaplerosis]] in MYC^high^ cells, as several reports have shown that MYC enhances [[Glutaminolysis]], which promotes influx of glutamine-derived [[α-Ketoglutarate]] into the [[TCA cycle]].

In rapidly proliferating cells, many TCA cycle intermediates are being extracted for biosynthesis, and the glutamine [[Anaplerosis|anaplerosis]], as a process, replenishes the otherwise dwindling TCA cycle intermediates through the provision of sufficient carbon supply for the continued TCA cycle. The sustained TCA cycle consequently generates reducing equivalents (NADH, FADH2), which in turn drive the mitochondrial [[Electron Transport Chain|respiratory chain]] and generate biosynthetic precursors for lipids and nucleotides.

The MYC target gene expression signatures or "MYC activity" signatures may provide robust and quantitative metrics for evaluation of the MYC^high^ status of cancer cells to complement semi-quantitative and often antibody quality-dependent protein-based assays. Here, we identify a MYC signature that is highly correlated with MYC protein levels in a panel of 14 TNBC cell lines. Both transcriptomic and biochemical analyses revealed enhanced [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] respiration in the MYC^high^ cells. The association between the MYC^high^ activity and enhanced respiration was corroborated in a global analysis of the transcriptomes of cancer cell lines and tumor samples. Thus, high mitochondrial respiration appears as a universal MYC^high^ phenotype, present in TNBC and a wide variety of cancer cell lines of different origins.

> [!tip] Metformin Sensitivity
> The MYC^high^ TNBC cells were preferentially sensitive to [[Metformin]], a weak mitochondrial respiratory [[Complex I]] inhibitor, which resulted in diminished proliferation together with apoptotic priming. Mechanistically, MYC induces a metabolic reprogramming, shifting glutamine over glucose as the TCA cycle's carbon source. Inhibition of complex I with metformin or ubiquinone (Q) reduction site-blocking specific complex I inhibitors strongly repressed the [[Glutaminolysis|glutaminolysis-dependent]] TCA cycle and selectively promoted death in MYC^high^ TNBC cells.

In summary, high MYC levels are coupled to high mitochondrial respiration rate and a striking metabolic shift toward a glutamine-fueled TCA cycle in [[Breast Cancer|breast cancer]]. The MYC^high^ [[OXPHOS]]^high^ breast cancer phenotype marks a metabolic vulnerability targetable with low doses of [[Complex I]] inhibitors alone or in combination with clinical stage inhibitors of [[Glutamine]] metabolism.

## Results

### Transcriptional signature that defines MYC^high^ TNBC cells

MYC activity signatures have been used, for example, to define subtypes of [[Breast Cancer|breast cancer]] with the highest MYC activity for trials with MYC pathway-directed drugs, to predict clinical outcomes among MYC-associated cancers, and more recently, to define metabolic alterations that associate with MYC^high^ areas within mouse and human tumors.

To define which published transcriptional MYC activity signatures correlate best with high levels of MYC protein expression, we determined the population mean MYC protein expression level by high content immunofluorescence (IF) analysis in 15 [[triple-negative breast cancer|TNBC]] cell lines. TNBC represents 10%–15% of all breast cancers and is an aggressive subtype with the lowest 5-year relative survival rate among different breast cancer subtypes.

In addition to IF, bulk RNA-seq was performed in triplicate for the same cell lines to profile transcriptomic levels of MYC target genes. We compared the population mean MYC protein expression levels obtained via IF to MYC protein data from western blot analysis and mass spectrometry. We found significant Spearman correlation between the IF and western blot results and between the IF and mass spectrometry assays. Thus, our results demonstrate that cancer cell lines can be robustly and consistently categorized to MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ expressors via multiple protein-based assays.

![](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117629/asset/ffaf2783-da88-48f2-88bb-5abd7d72aacf/main.assets/gr1_lrg.jpg)

Figure 1 Stratification of TNBC cells to MYC high and MYC low groups

Consistent with recent proteogenomic studies indicating overall low correlation between gene amplifications and corresponding protein expression, we did not find significant correlation between high *MYC* gene amplification status and a high level of MYC protein or mRNA. However, *MYC* mRNA levels did correlate with the MYC^high^ protein status. To explore whether the published MYC target gene signatures correlate with the MYC^high^ protein status in TNBC cells, we used two gene set enrichment analysis tools: the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) software and the correlation adjusted mean rank gene set test (CAMERA).

MDA-MB-157 cells expressed low MYC protein and mRNA levels as well as the lowest score for MYC target signatures (molecular signature database (MSigDB); Broad Institute). Therefore, MDA-MB-157 cells were used as a reference cell line in all subsequent analyses. From GSEA analysis, 10 MYC target gene sets (GSs) were selected, and the normalized enrichment score (NES) was used to rank the 15 TNBC cell lines according to their inferred MYC activity status.

We chose five GSs for further analysis and correlated their NES values to the MYC protein expression scores (PESs). The best association between the NES and PES values was observed when GS-4 (HALLMARK\_MYC\_TARGETS\_V1) and GS-5 (HALLMARK\_MYC\_TARGETS\_V2) were combined into a larger MYC V1V2 set. The combined set contains 240 unique genes after removing overlapping entries between the two hallmark GSs. The CAMERA analysis confirmed the best PES correlation for MYC V1V2. The observed strong correlation between the MYC V1V2 GS and the MYC PES status supported the use of MYC V1V2 set as an MYC activity-based biomarker for stratification of the TNBC cells into MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups.

The highest MYC activity was attributed to a group of BT-549, HCC1395, HCC1937, HCC1806, and HCC38 cell lines (NES > 2; FDR < 0.0001). However, the HCC38 cell line was excluded from the MYC^high^ set as an outlier. Lowest MYC scores (MYC^low^) were attributed to a group of Hs578T, MDA-MB-453 and MDA-MB-157 cells. MYC V1V2 showed correlation with *MYC* mRNA levels, but not with those of MYC's binding partner [[MAX]]. Together, our analyses reveal the MYC V1V2 GS as a strong RNA-based biomarker for MYC activity across multiple TNBC cell lines.

To explore molecular differences between the MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ TNBC groups, we first performed GSEA analysis on 50 hallmark molecular signatures in the MSigDB. Notably, the hallmark [[OXPHOS|oxidative phosphorylation]] (OXPHOS) set was among the significantly enriched gene sets in the MYC^high^ group. As expected, the MYC^high^ group was also enriched with GSs of MYC targets, the cell cycle, and cell growth-related signatures.

We next investigated whether the MYC^high^ status of cells has a discernible impact on the expression of genes that encode [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] proteins. For GSEA analysis, we chose a GS representing MYC-regulated nuclear genes that encode mitochondrial proteins. The analysis revealed a significant enrichment of MYC-regulated mitochondrial targets in the MYC^high^ group.

### MYC^high^ cancer cells show increased mitochondrial respiration

To further explore the association between MYC^high^ status and enrichment of mitochondrial respiration GSs, we compiled a list of 26 mitochondrial respiration-relevant GSs identified from various data repositories (MitoCarta3.0; MSigDB; Reactome). Next, we sourced RNA-seq data for cell lines from cancer cell line encyclopedia (CCLE; Broad Institute, 1009 cell lines) and for TNBC samples from The Cancer Genome Atlas program (TCGA; NIH, 105 samples) and computed with the ssGSEA method the ESs both for the MYC V1V2 signature and separately for all 26 mitochondrial respiration GSs.

Pearson's correlation analysis revealed a significant positive correlation between the MYC^high^ status and 24 out of 26 mitochondrial respiration GSs in the CCLE data. In the TCGA patient data, MYC^high^ status positively correlated with 21 mitochondrial respiration GSs out of 26. The most significantly enriched GSs included OXPHOS, OXPHOS assembly factors, [[Complex I|complex I]] and IV as well as [[Electron Transport Chain|ETC]]-related GSs.

![](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117629/asset/d7e02330-e86e-4cbc-be93-9f1b26c0c024/main.assets/gr2_lrg.jpg)

Figure 2 Mitochondrial respiration is increased in MYC high TNBCs and MYC-activated cells

To determine whether high MYC activity couples with elevated mitochondrial respiration, we measured the rate of mitochondrial respiration in the MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups of TNBC cells using high-resolution respirometry (HRR). The HRR analyses revealed cell line-specific variation within both MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups for routine respiration, complex IV-specific respiration, and leak respiration rates. Notably, the routine respiration rate was statistically higher in the MYC^high^ than in the MYC^low^ TNBC cells.

To assess the impact of inducible MYC activation on mitochondrial respiration, oxygen consumption was measured in MCF10A MycER cells using the Seahorse XF Cell Mito Stress Test. MYC activation significantly increased basal respiration, consistent with the elevated respiration observed in TNBC cells. Direct MYC inhibition with EN4 blunted both basal and maximal respiration in MYC^high^ TNBC cells, demonstrating that elevated mitochondrial respiration activity is MYC-dependent.

To characterize the mitochondrial content in the TNBC cells, we measured multiple mitochondrial mass markers. First, we measured mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) levels, which were comparable between the MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups. Furthermore, we quantified the expression of outer-membrane markers, TOMM20 and VDAC1. These data collectively suggest that the elevated respiration rates observed in MYC^high^ cells reflect enhanced functional activity or metabolic reprogramming rather than a simple increase in mitochondrial mass.

### Metformin, a weak mitochondrial complex I inhibitor, suppresses proliferation and primes apoptosis preferentially in MYC^high^ TNBC cells

[[Metformin]] acts as a weak [[Complex I]] inhibitor in cell cultures at high concentrations. In accordance, addition of metformin in the millimolar range efficiently inhibited complex I-dependent respiration, with no difference observed between the MYC^low^ and MYC^high^ TNBC cells.

To explore whether complex I inhibition by metformin has different biological effects in MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups of TNBC cells, we defined the proliferation and apoptotic status of metformin-treated MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ TNBC cell lines stratified according to the MYC V1V2 signature. Metformin inhibited proliferation in both MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups of cells. A trend for stronger inhibition of proliferation was observed in the MYC^high^ group.

We also measured the level of trimethylation of histone 3 at lysine 9 (H3K9me3), a post-translational modification involved in the formation of transcriptionally silent heterochromatin in senescent cells. Metformin treatment strongly and selectively induced H3K9me3 marks in the MYC^high^ TNBC cells. Nevertheless, even though metformin inhibited the proliferation of MYC^high^ cells in short-term and long-term assays as well as promoted the senescence-associated H3K9me3 marks and senescence-associated beta-galactosidase activity, the inhibitory effect of metformin on proliferation was reversible.

![](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117629/asset/7859c50f-9c31-4e7d-a528-2d1025c88d2a/main.assets/gr3_lrg.jpg)

Figure 3 Metformin inhibits proliferation, promotes senescence-associated features, and induces BAK activation in MYC high TNBC cells

Activation of MYC primes mammary epithelial and tumor cells to [[Apoptosis|apoptotic cell death]] through a mechanism that involves conformational activation of the pro-apoptotic [[Bcl-2 family]] protein [[BAK]]. Conformational activation exposes the N-terminal part of mitochondrially localized BAK, which can be detected with N-terminus-directed neoepitope-specific antibodies.

> [!info] BAK Activation by Metformin
> Metformin induced apoptotic priming observed as increased [[BAK]] activation in MYC-induced MCF10A MycER cells, while [[BAX]] did not get activated. In breast epithelial cells, conformational activation of BAK reflects mitochondrial apoptotic sensitization rather than execution of apoptosis, whereas downstream events such as BAX activation or caspase cleavage typically occur only when apoptosis is triggered by additional stimuli.

Metformin induced prominent BAK activation in half of the tested TNBC cell lines, with 40%–60% of the cells staining BAK positive at 72h timepoint. All these cell lines were MYC^high^ cell lines. The BAK response to metformin was less prominent in the two MYC^low^ cell lines. This suggests that MYC sensitizes cells to metformin's pro-apoptotic effects.

### MYC^high^ TNBC cells are specifically sensitive to Q site-blocking complex I inhibitors

Next, we asked whether the MYC-associated enhanced mitochondrial respiration generates therapeutic vulnerabilities. For this purpose, we performed a drug screen with FIMM Oncology Collection including 526 investigational and approved oncology drugs for TNBC cell lines stratified as MYC^high^ and MYC^low^.

The analyses revealed 105 compounds with high DSS (>10) for MYC^high^ cell lines. These hits included drugs with previously reported [[Synthetic Lethality|synthetic lethal]] activity with MYC such as inhibitors for cyclin-dependent kinases (Dinaciclib), mammalian target of rapamycin complex 1/2 (Sapanisertib), and Polo-like kinase 1 (Volasertib and GSK-461364).

The drugs which showed the highest MYC selectivity (highest difference in DSS between MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ groups) were BAY 87–2243 (CAS 1227158-85-1) and [[Mubritinib]] (TAK 165; CAS 366017-09-6).

![](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117629/asset/5d4b65af-02a4-481d-9627-027dc09cc03a/main.assets/gr4_lrg.jpg)

Figure 4 Drug sensitivity screen to identify drugs with selectivity for MYC high TNBC cells

> [!important] Q-Site Complex I Inhibitors
> Interestingly, the top two MYC^high^-selective hits, [[BAY 87-2243]] and [[Mubritinib]], were originally considered as hypoxia-inducible factor-1 (HIF-1) pathway and receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitors, respectively. However, later studies have rediscovered BAY 87–2243 and mubritinib as ubiquinone-binding site (Q-site) dependent inhibitors of mitochondrial respiratory [[Complex I]]. BAY 87–2243 and mubritinib, as well as a clinically tested Q-site inhibitor IACS-10759, share an aromatic ring chain topology, a molecular scaffold critical for complex I inhibition.

[[Complex I]], a.k.a NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase is the first enzyme of the mitochondrial [[Electron Transport Chain|electron transport chain]] (ETC); it provides an entry point for electrons to the respiratory chain by oxidizing NADH to [[NAD+]]. In the process of [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] respiration, complex I translocates four protons across the inner membrane per molecule of oxidized NADH, helping to build the electrochemical potential difference used to produce ATP. Consistent with this mode of complex I action, we observed that inhibition of complex I with [[Metformin]], [[BAY 87-2243]], [[Mubritinib]], or rotenone in MCF10A MycER cells led to a notable depletion of both NAD+ and ATP levels. The level of inhibition was similar in these cells with or without MYC activation. Therefore, these experiments show that pharmacological inhibition of complex I leads to reductive stress (increased NADH:NAD+ ratio) and loss of ATP, independently of the MYC status.

### MYC enriches the TCA cycle with glutamine-derived carbon

[[NAD+]] is an essential coenzyme in multiple pathways, including the [[TCA cycle]], in which dehydrogenase enzymes oxidize their substrates by reducing NAD+. Therefore, the inhibition of mitochondrial [[Complex I]] in MYC-activated cells may lead to disruption in NAD-dependent pathway activity and affect cell survival. To understand how MYC influences the function of the TCA cycle and whether it is affected by complex I inhibitors, we explored the use of glucose- and glutamine-derived carbons in metabolic ^13^C enrichment analysis.

We first analyzed the enrichment of glucose-derived carbons in [[Glycolysis|glycolysis]] and TCA cycle intermediates using U-^13^C-labeled glucose tracing in MCF10A MycER cells. The pulse isotope tracing showed that MYC induction increased the enrichment of ^13^C isotopologues of glycolytic intermediates such as glucose-6P M + 6, fructose-6P M + 6 and PEP M + 3. In addition, MYC activation enhanced the enrichment of ^13^C in metabolites of pentose phosphate pathway and glycerol-3-phosphate biosynthesis pathway. Lactate cellular biosynthesis levels (lactate M + 3 isotopologue) were reduced in MYC-activated cells. Because the tracer analysis quantified intracellular metabolite pools, this observation does not necessarily reflect total lactate production due to rapid lactate export. To address this, we measured extracellular acidification rate (ECAR) in MCF10A MycER cells using Seahorse analysis. Basal ECAR values were significantly elevated in MYC-activated cells, indicating increased extracellular acidification consistent with enhanced lactate secretion.

![](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117629/asset/3e4582e4-e384-402f-9ff8-1ccd71dd9d9f/main.assets/gr5_lrg.jpg)

Figure 5 Glucose catabolism and TCA cycle with \[U- 13 C\]glucose tracer

Next, we investigated the incorporation of glucose-derived carbons into the [[TCA cycle]] in the presence and absence of active [[MYC]]. We observed that MYC activation reduces the glucose-derived ^13^C M + 2 enrichment in [[α-Ketoglutarate|α-ketoglutarate]], succinate, fumarate, and malate, suggesting a possible rewiring of carbon metabolism in the TCA cycle.

[[Glutamine]] also serves as a carbon source for the TCA cycle, initially hydrolyzed by [[Glutaminase]] to produce glutamate, subsequently converted into [[α-Ketoglutarate|α-ketoglutarate]] before entering the TCA cycle. To follow glutamine carbon enrichment in the cells with or without active MYC, we used ^13^C-labelled glutamine, finding that MYC activation significantly increases the glutamate M + 5 levels and its relative abundance to glutamine M + 5. A similar increase was observed in the downstream TCA cycle intermediates, with the M + 5 isotopologue of α-ketoglutarate and the M+4 isotopologues of succinate, fumarate, and malate, as expected from the first clockwise turn of the TCA cycle. These findings suggest that MYC rewires glutamine pathway to support carbon utilization in the TCA cycle.

![](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117629/asset/2cd85ac4-03a5-454f-aaab-6c4afc638841/main.assets/gr6_lrg.jpg)

Figure 6 Complex I inhibitors impede MYC-dependent enhanced glutamine carbon incorporation into the TCA cycle

Next, we asked whether [[Complex I]] inhibition disrupts glutamine-derived carbon enrichment in the [[TCA cycle]] of MYC-activated cells. In the stable isotope ^13^C-glutamine tracing, we found that the complex I inhibitors increased [[α-Ketoglutarate|α-ketoglutarate]] M + 5 levels and decreased TCA cycle intermediate M + 4 and M + 2 isotopologues. This indicates that complex I inhibition impedes the incorporation of glutamine-derived carbons into the TCA cycle intermediates, regardless of MYC status.

> [!warning] Mechanism of Complex I Inhibition
> Importantly, complex I inhibition does not prevent glutamine utilization itself. [[Glutamine]] is still converted to glutamate and subsequently to [[α-Ketoglutarate|α-ketoglutarate]], as evidenced by the accumulation of α-ketoglutarate observed upon complex I inhibition. Instead, inhibition of complex I limits the oxidative reactions of the [[TCA cycle]], thereby preventing efficient incorporation of glutamine-derived carbon into downstream TCA intermediates.

### MYC-reprogrammed TCA cycle exposes synthetic lethal opportunities for complex I inhibitors

Finally, we explored the potency of [[Complex I]] inhibition to induce death in cells with high MYC activity. As complex I inhibition limits glutamine-fueled TCA cycle without completely blocking [[Glutamine]] utilization, we reasoned that MYC^high^ cells might be particularly vulnerable when mitochondrial respiration is inhibited simultaneously with perturbations in glutamine metabolism.

We first analyzed the effects of complex I inhibition alone or in glutamine-deprived conditions in MCF10A MycER cells using the CellTox Green cell death assay. We observed a prominent MYC-dependent induction of cell death in glutamine-depleted conditions or in the presence of the [[Glutaminase]] inhibitor [[CB-839]].

![](https://www.cell.com/cms/10.1016/j.celrep.2026.117629/asset/d4ec5a91-3bc0-4c15-8ee5-e4d83b9d0512/main.assets/gr7_lrg.jpg)

Figure 7 Complex I inhibition with glutamine deprivation preferentially kills MYC high breast cancer cells, inhibiting tumor growth

We further tested the lethal effects of complex I inhibition in endogenously MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ TNBC cell lines. The experiments revealed consistently significant induction of cell death in two out of four MYC^high^ cells grown in glutamine-restricted conditions. In the group of MYC^low^ TNBC, two out of three cell lines did not show a response to the treatments. The results with endogenously MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ TNBC cell lines are consistent with the idea that MYC^high^ TNBC cells are more sensitive to complex I inhibition and glutamine restriction. Notably, complex I inhibition together with glutaminase inhibition did not suppress MYC or [[MAX]] expression, indicating that the cell death is not caused by the downregulation of these proteins.

> [!note] Variability in Response
> It should be noted that even if these results suggest an MYC-preferred action for complex I inhibitors in a small group of TNBC cells, there was variability in the responses within both MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ TNBC groups. This variability is likely a reflection of the genetic heterogeneity of TNBC cells, which has its own cell line-specific impact on drug responses. Therefore, future studies with a larger panel of cell lines are needed to precisely define the predictive value of the MYC^high^ molecular phenotype for cellular response to complex I inhibitors.

To validate the observed enhanced antitumor activity of complex I inhibition in MYC^high^ tumors *in vivo*, we analyzed a panel of patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models of TNBC. We chose MAXFTN MX1 as a model of MYC^high^ TNBC for the *in vivo* experiments. As a second MYC^high^ TNBC model, we used HCI-002 PDX.

In both models of MYC^high^ TNBC, [[Mubritinib]] + [[CB-839]] combination significantly inhibited tumor growth. On day 25, the relative volumes of mubritinib + CB-839 combination-treated tumors were about two (MAXFTN MX1) or five times (HCI-002) smaller than in the control group. The antitumor effect was achieved without systemic toxicity, with no changes observed in body weights or serum toxicity markers. Consistent with the tumor growth analysis, the longest survival times were observed in the combination-treated cohorts.

Taken together, these results indicate that combined inhibition of [[Complex I]] and [[Glutaminase|glutaminase]] suppresses tumor growth in MYC^high^ TNBC PDX models. Although [[CB-839]] monotherapy showed limited antitumor activity in these models, the enhanced efficacy observed in the combination-treated groups supports the proposed functional interaction between mitochondrial complex I inhibition and [[Glutamine]] metabolism.

## Discussion

Here, we demonstrate that MYC V1V2 activity signature accurately predicts the MYC protein expression status in [[triple-negative breast cancer|TNBC]] cell lines. This signature tool enabled stratification of TNBC cell lines into MYC^high^ and MYC^low^ activity groups. MYC^high^ cells showed the enrichment of [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle]] activity signatures as well as the enrichment of [[DNA Repair]], cell growth ([[mTOR]]), stress, and interferon response GSs. Interestingly, the MYC V1V2 signature also indicated association with enhanced [[OXPHOS]] and enrichment of MYC-regulated mitochondrial targets.

Consistent with the enriched OXPHOS signatures, MYC^high^ cells showed generally elevated [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] respiration rates. A strong positive association between MYC V1V2 activity and OXPHOS- or respiration-related signatures was observed across the analyzed datasets. Analysis of 1009 cell lines in the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia and 105 TNBC tumor samples in TCGA demonstrated that increasing MYC V1V2 activity is accompanied by progressively stronger enrichment of mitochondrial respiration programs.

> [!info] Beyond the Warburg Effect
> High respiratory activity in cancer cells might sound counterintuitive to the concept of the [[Warburg Effect]] that emphasizes the use of non-mitochondrial glycolytic pathways for energy production in tumor cells, even if oxygen is present. However, it is well established today that many cancers have elevated mitochondrial activity, as indicated by the enrichment of mitochondrial metabolic signatures or enhanced mitochondrial energy generation. Respiration proficiency has been reported, for example, in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (OXPHOS-DLBCL subtype), melanoma, colorectal cancer, and glioblastoma.

Why would high MYC activity promote mitochondrial respiratory activity? We propose here that the higher respiration rate is part of the MYC's metabolic program geared to facilitate the anabolic function of the [[TCA cycle]]. Mitochondrial respiration produces not only ATP through [[OXPHOS]] but also [[NAD+]] from the mitochondrial pools of NADH via [[Complex I]] activity. The generation of mitochondrial NAD+ is, in turn, indispensable for the function of the TCA cycle due to the TCA cycle's dehydrogenase enzymes, which use NAD+ as a hydride acceptor.

Consistent with the idea that mitochondrially generated NAD+ is crucial for sustaining the TCA cycle, we demonstrate here that all tested complex I inhibitors reduced the NAD+/NADH ratio, and concomitantly the levels of TCA intermediates, exposing a general stagnation of the TCA cycle. Importantly, the TCA cycle is amphibolic, meaning that it has a catalytic function but also an anabolic function crucial for highly proliferative cells such as cancer cells. For example, the TCA metabolites are used for the synthesis of amino acids, fatty acids, sterols as well as purines and pyrimidines. Therefore, coupling of high MYC activity to enhanced respiration benefits cell proliferation in at least two ways: it simultaneously promotes the OXPHOS for production of bioenergy and the use of the TCA cycle for production of biomass.

> [!tip] MYC-Driven Metabolic Switching
> The metabolic analysis revealed significant differences in the cells' utilization of glucose- and glutamine-derived carbon in the presence or absence of active [[MYC]]. In the presence of active MYC, the glucose-derived carbons were metabolized through [[Glycolysis|glucose catabolism]] and pathways branched from the glycolytic pathway. MYC enriched glucose-derived carbon in the glycolytic pathway and the pentose phosphate pathway, supporting purine and pyrimidine nucleobase synthesis. MYC also enriched glucose-derived carbon in the glycerol-3-phosphate synthesis pathway, which contributes to glycerolipid synthesis. Notably, high MYC activity drastically reduced the enrichment of glucose-derived carbon in the [[TCA cycle]]. The levels of unlabeled (M + 0) TCA cycle intermediates were essentially similar between the cells with and without active MYC, with the exception of [[α-Ketoglutarate|α-ketoglutarate]] (M + 0), which was increased upon MYC activation. α-ketoglutarate can be derived from [[Glutamine]], and further metabolic ^13^C tracer analysis with glutamine-derived carbon showed that MYC significantly enhances the presence of glutamine-derived carbon in all steps of the TCA cycle. Together, these findings suggest that in the presence of active MYC, cells prioritize glucose-derived carbon through glucose catabolism and its branched pathways, while relying on glutamine as the primary carbon source for the TCA cycle.

Here, we show that high MYC levels promote a switch from the glucose-derived carbon to glutamine-derived carbon as a fuel for the TCA cycle, rendering the TCA cycle mostly glutamine carbon-driven. Therefore, glutamine dependency of MYC^high^ cells should be considered as a major MYC-dependent therapeutic vulnerability.

We demonstrate that the MYC-driven, glutamine-fueled TCA cycle can be blocked with [[Complex I]] inhibitors, selectively sensitizing high MYC activity cells to cell death even at low nanomolar doses. Complex I is a major contributor to mitochondrial [[NAD+]] regeneration, which is indispensable for the function of the [[TCA cycle]]. Therefore, we propose that the lethal action of complex I inhibitors toward the cells with glutamine-fueled TCA cycle arises from a deficiency in the oxidizing power of NAD+, leading to the impairment of the TCA cycle.

> [!warning] Clinical Translation Challenges
> Several different types of mitochondrial [[Complex I]] inhibitors have been tested for the treatment of cancer in clinical trials. No results are available for [[BAY 87-2243]] or [[Mubritinib]], but the results with structurally related IACS-010759 have raised safety concerns as the treated patients have presented a similar spectrum of symptoms observed in patients with mitochondrial diseases caused by deficit [[OXPHOS]]. Therefore, for clinical success in the treatment of cancer, the efficacy of safe [[Metformin]]-like drugs should be improved without simultaneously increasing the risk for adverse effects. Alternatively, biomarkers should be established to identify cancer types sensitive to low doses of ubiquinone-site targeted drugs. Here, we show data that could inspire metformin or CI inhibitor combination strategies with glutaminase inhibitors such as [[CB-839]] for the treatment of MYC^high^ cancers.

With the caution that any comparison of the drug effects between cell culture conditions and *in vivo* is extremely artifactual, the 10 nM dose showing MYC^high^ selective cytotoxicity is about 20 times lower concentrations than the plasma concentration of mubritinib reported to delay the development of AML in mouse studies. Thus, these results may offer a biological rationale for testing low doses of [[Mubritinib]] or other complex I inhibitors in the treatment of MYC^high^ cancers or cancer models through dose de-escalation design.

Finally, our mouse studies demonstrate relatively weak (MAXFTN MX1) or no antitumor effect (HCI-002) for [[Mubritinib]] as a single agent, while the combination of mubritinib + [[CB-839]] persistently inhibited tumor growth. To contextualize these findings, previous work demonstrated that the [[Glutaminase]] inhibitor CB-839 suppresses [[Glutamine]] metabolism and tumor growth in selected TNBC models, supporting the concept of glutamine dependency in subsets of breast cancer. However, subsequent clinical trials of CB-839 in unselected TNBC populations have shown limited efficacy, highlighting the challenge of patient stratification. Our findings suggest that MYC^high^ status identifies a biologically distinct subgroup characterized by enhanced glutamine-fueled TCA cycling and mitochondrial respiration. In this context, dual targeting of [[Glutaminolysis]] and [[Complex I]] produces significantly greater tumor inhibition in MYC^high^ PDX models than CB-839 alone, supporting a biomarker-guided combination strategy over monotherapy.

### Limitations of the study

The functional studies involving [[Complex I]] and [[Glutaminase]] inhibition were performed in cell culture systems and immunocompromised mouse models. While these models enabled mechanistic analysis of tumor-intrinsic metabolic dependencies, they do not fully recapitulate the interactions between tumor cells and an intact immune microenvironment. Because metabolic inhibitors can also influence immune cell metabolism and function, future studies in immunocompetent models will be important to define the broader systemic and immunological consequences of these therapeutic strategies and to evaluate their impact on anti-tumor immunity.
