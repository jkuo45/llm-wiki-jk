# Glutamine

[[Glutamine]] (Gln or Q) is the most abundant free amino acid in human blood plasma and intracellular fluid. It is classified as a **conditionally essential amino acid**, meaning that while it can be synthesized by the body under normal conditions, demand may exceed supply during periods of physiological stress, such as critical illness, trauma, or rapid cell proliferation.

## Structure & Properties

As an amino acid, glutamine contains both an amino group (-NH₂) and a carboxyl group (-COOH). Its side chain contains an amide group, which makes it a key nitrogen carrier in the body. It is synthesized from [[Glutamate]] by the enzyme [[Glutamine Synthetase]] (GS).

## Metabolic Role & Pathways

Glutamine is a versatile metabolic intermediate:

- **Nitrogen Transport**: It carries nitrogen from peripheral tissues (like [[Skeletal Muscle]]) to the [[Kidneys]], [[Liver]], and [[Small Intestine]].
- **Anaplerosis**: Through the enzyme [[Glutaminase]] (GLS), glutamine is converted back to [[Glutamate]], which then enters the [[Tricarboxylic Acid Cycle|TCA cycle]] as α-ketoglutarate. This process, known as glutaminolysis, is crucial for maintaining mitochondrial function when glucose availability is low.
- **Biosynthesis**: It provides nitrogen for the synthesis of nucleotides (purines and pyrimidines), amino sugars, and other amino acids.

## Role in Cancer

Many [[Cancer|cancers]] exhibit a high dependency on glutamine for growth and survival, a phenomenon known as **glutamine addiction**.

- **Fuel Source**: Cancer cells use glutamine as a primary carbon and nitrogen source to fuel the [[TCA cycle]] and support rapid [[Biomass Synthesis|biomass accumulation]].
- **Redox Balance**: Glutamine metabolism supports the production of [[Glutathione]], a major intracellular antioxidant, helping cancer cells manage oxidative stress.
- **Signaling**: The [[MYC]] oncogene is a key regulator of glutamine metabolism, upregulating glutamine transporters (e.g., [[ASCT2|SLC1A5]]) and glutaminase expression.

### MYC-Driven Carbon Switching

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Complex I drives glutamine-dependent TCA cycle to support viability of MYChigh breast cancer cells (NAD+)]]
> [[MYC]] rewires the [[TCA cycle]] to run primarily on [[Glutamine|glutamine-derived]] carbon, shifting away from glucose-derived carbon. Metabolic ^13^C tracing shows that MYC activation significantly increases glutamine-derived carbon enrichment in all TCA cycle intermediates (glutamate M+5, [[α-Ketoglutarate|α-ketoglutarate]] M+5, succinate/fumarate/malate M+4). Meanwhile, glucose-derived carbon is diverted to [[Glycolysis]], the pentose phosphate pathway, and glycerolipid synthesis. This metabolic switch renders MYC^high^ cells dependent on glutamine for TCA cycle function and vulnerable to combined [[Complex I]] + [[Glutaminolysis]] inhibition.

## Physiological Function

In healthy individuals, glutamine is vital for:
- **Immune Function**: It is the primary fuel for rapidly dividing immune cells, including [[T cells]] and [[B cells]].
- **Gut Integrity**: It supports the proliferation of enterocytes in the [[Small Intestine]], maintaining the mucosal barrier.
- **Acid-Base Balance**: In the [[Kidneys]], glutamine metabolism helps neutralize excess acid by producing ammonia.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Critical Illness**: Glutamine supplementation is often used in clinical settings (e.g., for patients with severe burns or trauma) to support immune function and gut health.
- **Cancer Therapy**: Targeting glutamine metabolism (e.g., using [[Glutaminase]] inhibitors like [[CB-839]]) is an active area of research in [[Oncology]].
