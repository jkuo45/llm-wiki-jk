# Taxanes

**Taxanes** are a class of plant-derived antimitotic chemotherapy agents — [[paclitaxel]], [[docetaxel]] and the semisynthetic cabazitaxel — that kill dividing cells by stabilising [[Microtubule|microtubules]]. Paclitaxel was isolated from the bark of *Taxus brevifolia* in the late 1960s; taxanes and the structurally unrelated [[Vinca Alkaloids]] remain, roughly half a century later, the two pillars of microtubule-targeting chemotherapy.

## Mechanism of Action

Taxanes bind the **taxane site** on β-tubulin, a pocket on the **inner lumen-facing surface** of the microtubule — structurally distinct from the vinca site on the lattice and from the colchicine site on soluble tubulin. Binding promotes lateral contacts between protofilaments and suppresses depolymerisation:

- Microtubules become **excessively stable** and functionally frozen.
- Suppression of **dynamic instability** — the treadmilling that drives spindle function — activates the spindle assembly checkpoint.
- Cells arrest in **G2/M and metaphase**, unable to progress through the checkpoint even with chromosomes attached.
- Prolonged arrest triggers intrinsic apoptosis through [[MOMP]] and [[Caspase-9]], or, in some contexts, mitotic slippage into senescence.

> [!important] Stabilising versus destabilising — the key contrast with vinca alkaloids
> Both classes are called "antimitotics," but they act at different sites and by different mechanisms. Vinca alkaloids bind the microtubule lattice and prevent polymer growth; taxanes bind the lumen and prevent depolymerisation. Because the taxane site requires polymerised microtubule to exist, tubulin-targeting agents that destroy microtubules — colchicine, vinca alkaloids at high concentration — antagonise taxane activity rather than substituting for it. This also explains why taxane resistance does not confer vinca cross-resistance.

## Pharmacodynamics

At low nanomolar concentrations taxanes **stabilise** existing microtubules; at higher concentrations they also promote tubulin assembly into unstable non-functional polymers. The antitumour effect in vivo is attributed mainly to the suppression of dynamics rather than to bulk polymer mass.

## Resistance

- **β-tubulin isotype shifts** — increased expression of βIII and βV tubulin, and decreased α-tubulin, is the best-characterised class resistance mechanism; both drug-binding pockets sit on α/β heterodimers, so altered stoichiometry changes the number of available sites.
- **P-glycoprotein (ABCB1) efflux** — a major and highly characteristic taxane resistance mechanism, less prominent than in vinca resistance at low drug concentrations but dominant at clinically achievable levels.
- **TUBB3 upregulation** — an emerging biomarker candidate correlating with taxane insensitivity.
- **Apoptotic-priming failure** — as documented for paclitaxel, failure of the prolonged mitotic arrest to kill (e.g. MCL-1 stabilisation, or deficient E3 ligase activity) underlies resistance independent of drug efflux.

## Clinical Uses

- **Breast cancer** — paclitaxel and docetaxel are standard in both neoadjuvant and adjuvant settings, and as taxane–platinum combinations in metastatic disease.
- **Ovarian, lung and prostate cancer** — paclitaxel, and docetaxel for castration-resistant prostate cancer.
- Cabazitaxel for taxane-resistant metastatic prostate cancer; nab-paclitaxel (albumin-bound) is used where Cremophor solvent toxicity limits dosing.

## Adverse Effects

- **Peripheral neuropathy** — dose-dependent, cumulative, and often the dose-limiting toxicity; sensory > motor, frequently irreversible at high cumulative doses.
- **Myelosuppression** — neutropenia is common; febrile neutropenia requires dose modification.
- **Fluid retention and hypersensitivity** — largely Cremophor EL–related and much reduced with nab-paclitaxel and cabazitaxel.
- Arthralgia, myalgia, alopecia, mucositis, and rare interstitial lung disease.

## Connections

- [[Microtubule]] — taxanes act by suppressing microtubule dynamics; this is the structural basis of the whole antimitotic effect.
- [[Tubulin]] — the taxane binding pocket sits on the α/β tubulin heterodimer, and β-tubulin isotype expression is the dominant resistance determinant.
- [[Mitosis]] — dynamic instability is what the spindle checkpoint reads; freezing it produces metaphase arrest.
- [[Vinca Alkaloids]] — the mechanistically contrasting microtubule-targeting class, which serves as the natural comparison for taxane site specificity and resistance spectrum.
- [[Paclitaxel]] and [[docetaxel]] — the two taxanes in routine clinical use, the first being the original natural product and the second a semisynthetic 10-deacetylbaccatin III derivative with greater potency at the cost of more neutropenia.
- [[Chemotherapy]] — taxanes are a standard cytotoxic class and, with platinum agents, the backbone of many first-line solid tumour regimens.
- [[P-glycoprotein]] — ABCB1 efflux is the second canonical taxane resistance mechanism and explains much multidrug resistance.
- [[Apoptosis]] — prolonged metaphase arrest is converted into death via the intrinsic pathway; failure of this conversion is a resistance route.
