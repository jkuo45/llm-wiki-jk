## Overview

MEK (MAPK/ERK kinase) refers to the dual-specificity kinases MEK1 and MEK2 that lie immediately upstream of ERK in the [[MAPK Signaling]] cascade. MEK is the point at which RAS-RAF signals are integrated into proliferative output, making it a central node in tumor biology.

## Structure & Function

MEK1/2 are dual-specificity kinases capable of phosphorylating both tyrosine and threonine residues in the activation loop of [[ERK1_2]]. They are themselves activated by RAF kinases (e.g., [[RAF1]], [[BRAF]]) downstream of [[KRAS]] and other RAS family GTPases.

## Role in the RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK Cascade

[[ERK1_2]] are the terminal effectors of the RAS-RAF-MEK cascade that phosphorylate nuclear and cytoplasmic targets to drive division. The directionality runs from MEK toward ERK1/2 — MEK is the active agent and ERK1/2 the recipient of the effect. This directional link makes the interaction a meaningful node in tumor biology, with implications for disease progression and for identifying therapeutic or biomarker opportunities.

> [!info] Signal integration point
> MEK is where upstream RAS-RAF signals converge and are amplified before reaching ERK, making it an attractive pharmacological bottleneck in MAPK-driven tumors.

## Pathological & Clinical Relevance

Constitutive MAPK activation — through mutant [[KRAS]], [[BRAF]], or [[RAF1]] — drives many cancers. MEK inhibitors (e.g., trametinib, selumetinib) are approved for BRAF-mutant melanoma and used in combination with BRAF inhibitors; resistance frequently emerges via ERK reactivation, motivating triple blockade strategies.
