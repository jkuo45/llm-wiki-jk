# FOLFOX

## Overview

**FOLFOX** is a combination [[Chemotherapy]] regimen consisting of three agents:
- **5-Fluorouracil (5-FU)**: an antimetabolite that inhibits thymidylate synthase
- **Leucovorin** (folinic acid): a folate derivative that enhances 5-FU activity
- **Oxaliplatin**: a third-generation platinum-based compound that forms DNA adducts

FOLFOX is a standard regimen for [[Cancer|colorectal cancer]] and is also used in gastric, pancreatic, and other gastrointestinal cancers.

## Role in NAD+ Metabolism and Muscle Wasting

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Chemotherapy NAD+ Loss Pöllänen 2026]]
> FOLFOX treatment in mice preserves body weight but induces mild muscle loss and [[NRK2|Nrk2]] downregulation despite preserved [[NAD+]] levels.

### Long-Term (5-Week) Treatment

- Maintains stable body weight but exhibits mild muscle loss
- Does not alter NAD+ metabolites or their ratios in skeletal muscle
- Reduces muscle [[NRK2|Nrk2]] expression by −92% (p<0.001) despite preserved NAD+ levels
- Accompanied by upregulation of the ubiquitously expressed isoform [[NRK2|Nrk1]]
- In liver: modest NAD+ loss (−15%, p=0.043) with broader alterations in NAD+ biosynthesis, including increased _Tdo_ and _Nampt_ and decreased _Naprt_

> [!tip] Uncoupling of Nrk2 from NAD+ Levels
> FOLFOX-induced Nrk2 downregulation despite preserved NAD+ levels suggests that Nrk2 repression may precede NAD+ depletion or become functionally uncoupled from NAD+ biosynthesis. This positions Nrk2 as an early warning marker of potential metabolic vulnerability.
