# RELA

## Overview

RELA (p65) is one of the subunits of the [[NF-κB]] transcription factor complex. It mediates the transcriptional response to inflammatory cytokines, stress, and infection, driving expression of pro-inflammatory genes. Constitutive or excessive RELA activity underlies chronic inflammation.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT6]] represses RELA/p65 transcriptional activity via chromatin deacetylation. By deacetylating histones at NF-κB target loci (and/or the RELA protein itself), SIRT6 reduces recruitment and transactivation of pro-inflammatory genes. Directionally, SIRT6 sits upstream of suppressed RELA-driven transcription.

> [!important] Tamping inflammation
> SIRT6-mediated chromatin deacetylation at NF-κB target promoters is a key mechanism restraining chronic inflammation, linking NAD+/sirtuin status to inflammatory gene control.

## Physiological Function

SIRT6-dependent repression of RELA contributes to metabolic homeostasis and protects against chronic, low-grade inflammation that accompanies [[Aging]] and age-related disease. This complements [[SIRT1]]'s deacetylation of p65, another route for dampening NF-κB signaling.

## Clinical Relevance

Dysregulated NF-κB/RELA activity is implicated in atherosclerosis, insulin resistance, and neurodegeneration. Interventions that raise NAD+ and activate sirtuins (e.g., [[NMN]], [[Sirtuin Activators]]) are proposed to restrain RELA-driven inflammation, though [[Sirtuin Inhibitors]] also have context-dependent roles in cancer immunity.
