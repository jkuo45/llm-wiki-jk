# FXR

## Overview

The farnesoid X receptor (FXR; NR1H4) is a nuclear receptor activated by bile acids. It is a master regulator of bile acid synthesis, transport, and enterohepatic circulation, and also controls triglyceride, glucose, and cholesterol metabolism. [[SIRT1]] regulates FXR, linking [[NAD+]] status to cholesterol and metabolic homeostasis.

## Mechanism

Bile acids bind and activate FXR, which heterodimerizes with the retinoid X receptor to regulate gene transcription. SIRT1-mediated deacetylation modulates FXR activity, adjusting its transcriptional output in response to cellular NAD+ and energy status. Through this regulation, SIRT1 couples nutrient/energy sensing to the bile-acid signaling that governs lipid and glucose handling.

> [!info] Metabolic integration
> SIRT1→FXR regulation connects NAD+ availability to cholesterol homeostasis, hepatic lipid metabolism, and metabolic adaptation, bridging sirtuin biology and bile-acid endocrinology.

## Physiological Function

FXR controls hepatic bile acid synthesis (via CYP7A1), biliary secretion, and gallbladder filling, and coordinates lipid, glucose, and energy metabolism. Its regulation by SIRT1 means that fasting-induced rises in NAD+ can adjust bile-acid and cholesterol metabolism accordingly.

## Clinical Relevance

FXR is a therapeutic target in cholestatic liver diseases and metabolic dysfunction; synthetic FXR agonists (e.g., obeticholic acid) are used clinically. The SIRT1–FXR connection suggests NAD+ status may influence FXR-dependent metabolic outcomes, relevant to [[Metabolic Syndromes|metabolic syndrome]] and hepatic steatosis.
