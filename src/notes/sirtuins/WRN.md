# WRN

## Overview

WRN is a member of the RecQ family of DNA helicases and the protein mutated in [[Werner Syndrome]], a human progeroid disorder of premature aging. WRN possesses both helicase and exonuclease activities and functions in DNA replication, recombination, and repair.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates and activates the Werner syndrome helicase WRN, linking SIRT1 to [[DNA Repair]] and genomic stability. Acetylation represses WRN's helicase activity; SIRT1-dependent deacetylation relieves this inhibition, enabling WRN to participate in resolving DNA structures during repair.

> [!important] SIRT1–WRN axis
> SIRT1 deacetylation activates WRN helicase, coupling NAD+/sirtuin status to genome maintenance. Loss of this regulation mimics aspects of premature aging.

## Physiological Function

WRN promotes resolution of double-strand breaks and replication-associated DNA structures (e.g., via [[Homologous Recombination]] and base-excision repair pathways). By activating WRN, SIRT1 coordinates DNA repair with metabolic state, protecting genome integrity during stress and aging.

## Clinical Relevance

Werner syndrome is characterized by accelerated aging, genomic instability, and cancer predisposition. The SIRT1–WRN connection suggests that maintaining NAD+ levels and sirtuin activity may support genome maintenance and counteract age-related DNA damage.
