# Rifampicin

## Overview

Rifampicin (rifampin) is a broad-spectrum antibiotic, a mainstay of tuberculosis therapy, and a potent inducer of hepatic drug-metabolizing enzymes. Its enzyme-induction properties make it a model perpetrator of drug–drug interactions.

## Mechanism

Rifampicin activates the pregnane X receptor (PXR), upregulating [[Cytochrome P450]] enzymes, including [[CYP3A4]], and conjugating/transporting enzymes in the [[Liver]]. By increasing the activity of these metabolic enzymes, rifampicin can accelerate the metabolism of co-administered drugs, reducing their half-life and systemic exposure.

> [!important] Drug–drug interaction principle
> Rifampicin-induced CYP450 activity can accelerate the metabolism of compounds such as urolithin A, shortening their half-life. This illustrates how hepatic enzyme inducers alter the clearance and efficacy of co-administered agents.

## Pharmacological Applications

This induction principle applies broadly: antibiotics, antifungal drugs, and anticancer drugs are reported to interact with CYP450 enzymes, competitively inhibiting or inducing them and thereby disrupting metabolism and clearance of other agents. Careful management of rifampicin-based regimens is required to maintain therapeutic drug levels.

## Clinical Relevance

Because [[Urolithin A]] and other polyphenol metabolites are substrates of hepatic metabolism, co-administration with rifampicin may reduce their bioavailability and biological effects, relevant to supplementation and to patients on multi-drug regimens.
