# TRPM2

**TRPM2** (Transient Receptor Potential Melastatin-Related Channel 2) is a calcium-permeable non-selective cation channel. It is expressed highly in the [[Brain]], immune system, and cardiovascular system. TRPM2 functions as an oxidative stress sensor, and its prolonged activation leads to intracellular calcium overload and subsequent cell death (apoptosis). TRPM2 is unique among ion channels because it is directly gated by a metabolite of sirtuin catalysis — providing a rare, direct coupling between the [[Sirtuins]] and membrane excitability.

## Structure and Gating Mechanism

TRPM2 is a ~170 kDa subunit forming tetrameric channels. It contains an N-terminal MHR (Meltastatin Homology Region) domain, six transmembrane segments with a pore loop, and a remarkable C-terminal extension bearing an enzymatic **NUDT9-H homology domain**. This domain lacks catalytic activity but serves as a ligand-binding site for ADP-ribose derivatives. TRPM2 opens in response to intracellular ADP-ribose, and its sensitivity is dramatically potentiated by [[OAADPr]] (2′-O-acetyl-ADP-ribose) and by warm temperatures and cytosolic Ca2+. H2O2 and other oxidants elevate the intracellular ADP-ribose/NAD+ metabolic pool, engaging TRPM2 as a heat- and redox-coupled cation channel.

## Activation by OAADPr

TRPM2 is unique among ion channels because it is directly gated by sirtuin reaction metabolites:

- **Gating by OAADPr**: The intracellular C-terminal domain of TRPM2 contains an enzymatic NUDT9-H homology region. This domain binds to **[[OAADPr]]** (2′-O-acetyl-ADP-ribose), a unique by-product of NAD⁺-dependent deacetylation catalyzed by sirtuins.
- **Sirtuin Link**: Deacetylation reactions by sirtuins like [[SIRT2]] and [[SIRT3]] generate OAADPr. High-stress stimuli trigger increased sirtuin activity, leading to a localized rise in OAADPr. This gates TRPM2 channels, leading to sodium and calcium influx.
- **Cell Death Pathway**: Experiments using cell-death inducing agents show that cell death is markedly reduced by sirtuin inhibitors or by decreasing the expression of [[SIRT2]] or [[SIRT3]], directly linking sirtuin deacetylation by-products to TRPM2-mediated cell survival/death decisions.

This creates a feedback loop in which sirtuin activation — normally cytoprotective — can, under extreme stress, generate enough OAADPr to open TRPM2 and trigger calcium-dependent death, a built-in fail-safe against irreparably damaged cells.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

TRPM2 mediates cytokine release in immune cells, insulin secretion in β-cells, and neuronal vulnerability to ischemia and [[Oxidative Stress]]. Its OAADPr sensitivity ties membrane physiology to the [[NAD+]] redox economy and the sirtuin network, making it a sensor of the same metabolic state that sirtuins monitor. Dysregulation of TRPM2 contributes to neurodegeneration (stroke, Alzheimer's), inflammatory disease, and glycemic dysfunction, underscoring its cross-topic importance to [[Aging]] and [[Neurodegeneration]].
