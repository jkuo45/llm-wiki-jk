# ELK4

[[ELK4]] (also known as **SAP-1**) is an [[ETS]]-domain transcription factor belonging to the ternary complex factor (TCF) subfamily that also includes [[ELK1]] and ELK3. It is an ERK/mitogen-activated protein kinase target that regulates immediate-early gene expression. ELK4 has a well-characterized role in the transcriptional response to growth factor signaling and has been implicated in tumor biology.

## Structure & Function

- Contains a conserved ETS DNA-binding domain that recognizes purine-rich GGAA/T motifs and an SRF interaction domain for associating with the serum response factor on the *c-fos* serum response element.
- Phosphorylated by ERK1/2 and other MAPKs at its transcriptional activation domain, linking receptor tyrosine kinase signaling to gene transcription.

## Role in Sirtuin Biology

In the context of sirtuin-mediated tumorigenesis, [[SIRT7]] was found to be stabilized at the promoters of its target genes by interaction with the [[ETS]]-family transcription factor ELK4. ELK4-dependent tethering allows SIRT7 to deacetylate lysine 18 of [[Histone H3]] ([[H3K18ac|H3K18]]), thereby repressing genes with tumor suppressor function. This pathway maintains cancer hallmarks such as anchorage-independent growth and loss of contact inhibition.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- Elevated/activating ELK4 signaling contributes to oncogenic transcriptional programs downstream of MAPK in various cancers.
- The ELK4–SIRT7 axis suggests that ELK4 inhibition could antagonize SIRT7-driven H3K18 deacetylation and the repression of tumor suppressor genes.
