# PEPCK

## Overview

Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK) catalyzes the committed step of [[Gluconeogenesis]], converting oxaloacetate to phosphoenolpyruvate. It is a key control point for hepatic glucose output and for glyceroneogenesis in adipose tissue.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT2]] activates PEPCK via deacetylation, a regulatory axis that also involves [[FOXO]] transcription factors, c-Myc, and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase. SIRT2-mediated deacetylation of PEPCK increases gluconeogenic capacity, influencing transcription, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and [[NADPH]]/redox balance.

> [!info] Metabolic integration
> SIRT2 deacetylation of PEPCK coordinates glucose production with pentose-phosphate-pathway flux (via G6PD), coupling gluconeogenesis to NADPH generation and redox status.

## Physiological Function

PEPCK is essential for maintaining blood glucose during fasting and for lactate/glycerol recycling. SIRT2-dependent regulation connects cellular NAD+ status and sirtuin activity to whole-body glucose homeostasis, opposing or complementing the insulin-dependent control of gluconeogenic gene expression.

## Clinical Relevance

Dysregulated PEPCK activity contributes to hyperglycemia in [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]]. SIRT2 modulation is being explored as a metabolic intervention, though its effects are context-dependent (see [[Phosphoglycerate Mutase]] for SIRT2's pro-glycolytic, tumor-promoting face).
