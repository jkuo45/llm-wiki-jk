# CCNDBP1

## Overview

CCNDBP1 (cyclin D-binding protein 1; also known as GCIP) is a protein that binds [[Cyclin D]] and is implicated in the control of [[Cell Cycle]] progression, differentiation, and cellular stress responses. Within sirtuin biology, CCNDBP1 is a downstream target of [[SIRT6]]: SIRT6 deacetylates and activates CCNDBP1, providing a direct mechanistic link between the chromatin-associated deacetylase and proliferative control.

## Mechanism

SIRT6, a [[Sirtuins]] family member with NAD+-dependent deacetylase activity, removes acetyl groups from CCNDBP1, converting it to a more active form. The deacetylation is coupled to [[NAD+]] hydrolysis, so the resulting CCNDBP1 activation is metabolically sensitive to the cellular NAD+ pool. Activated CCNDBP1 then participates in cyclin D-related signaling, influencing the balance between proliferation and differentiation, and contributing to the cellular response to genotoxic or metabolic stress.

## Physiological Function

Through CCNDBP1, SIRT6 couples nutrient and energy status to the cell-cycle machinery. This is consistent with SIRT6's broader roles in maintaining [[Genomic Instability|genomic stability]], suppressing tumorigenic proliferation, and coordinating DNA-repair outputs (see also [[WRN]] and [[XPA]] for SIRT6/SIRT1 DNA-repair connections). Dysregulation of the SIRT6–CCNDBP1 axis could contribute to uncontrolled proliferation in cancer and to impaired stress adaptation during aging.

> [!tip] Clinical relevance
> Because SIRT6 suppresses proliferation in part through CCNDBP1, loss of SIRT6 or failed CCNDBP1 deacetylation may release cell-cycle brakes, a feature relevant to cancer biology and to age-related proliferative dysregulation.

## Open Questions

Whether CCNDBP1 activation downstream of SIRT6 operates primarily at the level of cell-cycle entry, differentiation commitment, or stress survival, and how it integrates with other SIRT6 targets, remains only partially characterized.
