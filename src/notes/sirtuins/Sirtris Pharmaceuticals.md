# Sirtris Pharmaceuticals

[[Sirtris Pharmaceuticals]] was a biotechnology company (Cambridge, MA) founded in 2004 around the biology of sirtuins and aging. It originated the high-throughput screens and medicinal chemistry campaigns that produced the [[STACs|STAC]] (sirtuin-activating compound) series of synthetic SIRT1 activators, including [[SRT1720]], [[SRT1460]], and [[SRT2183]], as well as [[SRT2104]] which advanced to clinical testing. Alongside [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]]'s identification as a polyphenolic STAC, Sirtris' programs put sirtuin modulation on the map as a drug-discovery strategy for aging and metabolic disease.

## History & R&D

- The company screened large compound libraries for SIRT1 activators, identifying imidazothiazole-class SRT compounds that are ~100-fold more potent than resveratrol.
- Acquired by GlaxoSmithKline in 2008 for approximately USD 720 million, catalyzing both academic and commercial interest in sirtuin pharmacology.

## Legacy & Scientific Controversy

- The SRT series demonstrated broad metabolic benefits (insulin sensitivity, mitochondrial biogenesis, lifespan in diet-induced obese mice).
- Sirtris' activation mechanism claims were challenged: activation frequency correlated with fluorescent substrate artifacts, and resveratrol-like compounds may act indirectly via the [[cAMP]]-[[Epac1]]-[[AMPK]] axis rather than direct SIRT1 binding. This controversy shaped the later, more rigorous STAC chemistry.
