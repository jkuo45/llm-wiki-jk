# DLD (dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase)

## Overview

Dihydrolipoamide dehydrogenase (DLD) is a flavoprotein (E3) subunit shared by the mitochondrial α-ketoacid dehydrogenase complexes, including [[Pyruvate Dehydrogenase]], α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and branched-chain ketoacid dehydrogenase. It reoxidizes the dihydrolipoyl groups generated during decarboxylation, regenerating the lipoamide cofactor and transferring electrons to NAD⁺.

## Mechanism

DLD couples the reoxidation of dihydrolipoamide to the reduction of [[NAD+]] to NADH, functioning as the terminal electron-transfer step of the multienzyme complexes that feed carbon into the [[TCA cycle]]. The mitochondrial deacetylase [[SIRT3]] deacetylates DLD, supporting α-ketoacid dehydrogenase complex activity and mitochondrial metabolism.

> [!info] SIRT3 regulation
> By deacetylating DLD, SIRT3 enhances flux through pyruvate and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenases, coupling mitochondrial NAD+ availability to oxidative decarboxylation and ATP production.

## Physiological Function

Through its role in the dehydrogenase complexes, DLD supports glucose and amino-acid oxidation, the [[Citric Acid Cycle]], and ATP synthesis. Its SIRT3-dependent activation illustrates how mitochondrial sirtuins tune core metabolic enzymes in response to [[NAD+]] status, aligning catabolic flux with energetic demand.

## Pathology

DLD deficiency causes a rare inborn error of metabolism characterized by lactic acidosis, neurological dysfunction, and movement disorders. Impaired DLD activity may also contribute to age-related mitochondrial dysfunction.
