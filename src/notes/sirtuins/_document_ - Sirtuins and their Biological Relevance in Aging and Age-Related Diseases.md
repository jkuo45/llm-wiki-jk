## Abstract

[[Sirtuins]], initially described as histone deacetylases and gene silencers in [[Saccharomyces cerevisiae|yeast]], are now known to have many more functions and to be much more abundant in living organisms. The increasing evidence of sirtuins in the field of [[aging|ageing]] and [[age-related diseases]] indicates that they may provide novel targets for treating diseases associated with [[aging]] and perhaps extend human [[lifespan]]. Here, we summarize some of the recent discoveries in sirtuin biology that clearly implicate the functions of sirtuins in the regulation of aging and age-related diseases. Furthermore, human sirtuins are considered promising therapeutic targets for anti-aging and ageing-related diseases and have attracted interest in scientific communities to develop small molecule activators or drugs to ameliorate a wide range of ageing disorders. In this review, we also summarize the discovery and development status of sirtuin-targeted drug and further discuss the potential medical strategies of sirtuins in delaying aging and treating age-related diseases.

---

In 1979, the discovery of mating-type regulator 1 (MAR1) in *[[Saccharomyces cerevisiae|Saccharomyces cerevisiae]]* was reported \[[^1]\]. Lack of this protein resulted in the inhibition of silencing of HM loci, which control mating type and sterility in yeast. Three more proteins with similar functions were discovered later in 1979, and the nomenclature was unified, thus creating a family of silent information regulator proteins, *Sirs* \[[^2]\]. Emerging interest in sirtuins occurred in 1999 when it was reported that *[[Sir2 (yeast)|Sir2]]* overexpression could extend yeast [[lifespan|lifespans]] by as much as 70% \[[^3]\]. Further research revealed that [[sirtuins|sirtuins]] overexpression also leads to [[lifespan|lifespan]] extension in other model organisms, such as *[[Caenorhabditis elegans|Caenorhabditis elegans]]* and *[[Drosophila melanogaster|Drosophila melanogaster]].*

Shortly thereafter, it was shown that sirtuins appear to be conserved from yeast to mammals, however the complexity of their function increases with the complexity of the organism \[[^4]\]. In yeast, in addition to the chief representative, *Sir2*, there are four more homologous proteins \[[^2]\], and the positive effect of sirtuins activity can be attributed to the increase in genomic stability. In mammals there are seven enzymes belonging to the sirtuin family, *sirt1-7*. Phylogenetic analysis of 60 core domains from different eukaryotes and prokaryotes places the mammalian sirtuins into four different classes (I—IV). *Sirt1, sirt2* and *sirt3* are known as Class I sirtuins, which groups all yeast sirtuins and also at least one of the *sir2* -related proteins in most eukaryotes. Class I is divided in three sub-classes: a, b and c. *Sirt1* belongs to Class Ia which also includes *Sir2* and *Hst1* from S. cerevisiae, *C. elegans Sir* -2.1 and *D. melanogaster* D. mel1. *Sirt2* and *sirt3* reside in Class Ib, together with yeast *Hst2*, fly D. mel2 and other fungi and protozoa sirtuins. *Sirt4* is part of Class II, which also includes sirtuins from bacteria, insects, nematodes, mould fungus and protozoans. *Sirt5* is the mammalian member of Class III sirtuins, distributed widely in all prokaryotes either bacteria or archaea. Finally, Class IV contains *sirt6* and *sirt7* in two different sub-classes IVa and IVb respectively; and unlike Class III, sirtuins of this class are not present in prokaryotes, but are broadly distributed in metazoans, plants and vertebrates \[[^5]\].

In addition, mammalian [[sirtuins|sirtuins]] also differ in their sub-cellular localization, and some sirtuins can relocalize depending on the cell or tissue type, the developmental stage, metabolic status, and certain stress conditions. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* is localized to the nucleus \[[^6]\], but it shuttles to the cytoplasm when required to act on cytoplasmic targets, such as during inhibition of insulin signaling \[[^7]\]. In contrast, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* is cytoplasmic. It deacetylates tubulin microtubules \[[^8]\] and transcription factors those shuttle from the cytoplasm to the nucleus \[[^9]\]. *[[SIRT3|Sirt3]]*, *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* and *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* are active in the [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] \[[^10]\] by participating in the regulation of [[Adenosine Triphosphate|Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)]] synthesis, metabolism, [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] and intracellular signaling \[[^11]\]. Among them, *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* may be moved between the nucleus and mitochondria under cellular stress \[[^2]\]. *[[SIRT6|Sirt6]]* is a nuclear protein, although it is also present in the endoplasmic reticulum, where it deacetylates [[TNF-α]] \[[^12]\]. *[[SIRT7|Sirt7]]* is a nuclear protein that is mostly expressed in the [[Nucleolus|nucleolar]] regions \[[^13]\].

## Overview of sirtuins functions

[[Sirtuins]] belong to the class III [[histone deacetylases]] (HDACs) \[[^14]\]. The sirtuin family shares a highly conserved catalytic domain, and exerts [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]]-dependent protein deacylase and/or [[ADP-ribosylation|ADP ribosyltransferase]] activities \[[^15], [^16]\]. Studies indicate that, from yeast to humans, sirtuins contain a highly conserved catalytic core domain formed by 275 amino acids, and N,C-terminal extensions which is variable in length and sequence, and can affect the binding with interacting partners, mediate interactions with other sirtuin forms, and direct cellular localization. In addition to their well-studied roles as lysine deacetylases, certain sirtuins can also remove other acyl modifications from lysine residues, including propionyl, butyryl, malonyl, succinyl and the lengthy fatty-acid derived myristoyl and palmitoyl groups \[[^18] - [^22]\]. In addittion, sirtuins possess NAD <sup>+</sup> -dependent deacetylase, deacylase, desuccinylase, demalonylase, deglutarylase, ADP-ribosyltransferase activities, etc. and regulate many processes in vivo, including metabolism, DNA repair, metastasis, apoptosis, translation, promoting longevity and protecting against cancer via altering substrate activity, localization, stability and protein-protein interactions \[[^23]\].

*[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* is the closest to yeast *[[Sir2 (yeast)|Sir2]]* in terms of sequence and enzymatic activity and is also the most extensively studied mammalian sirtuin at present. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* deacetylates a diverse array of cellular proteins, including histones, transcription factors, DNA repair proteins, [[Autophagy|autophagy]] factors, and others, like [[FOXO3a|FOXO3a]], [[PPAR-α|PPARα]], PGAM-1, SREBP1, FXR, [[PGC-1α|PGC-1α]], [[NF-κB|NF-κB]], etc \[[^24]\] to modulate [[Metabolism|metabolism]], stress responses, and other cellular processes \[[^25]\]. *In vitro, [[SIRT1|sirt1]]* possesses deacylase activity, although the functional significance of this activity *in vivo* remains unclear \[[^26]\].

*[[SIRT2|Sirt2]]* mainly functions in [[Mitosis|mitosis]]. *[[SIRT2|Sirt2]]* regulates mitotic progression by controlling the activity of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome. When DNA damage emerges, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* may halt cell division, effectively guarding the cell against erroneous replication. *[[SIRT2|Sirt2]]* also plays an important role in controlling the [[Cell Cycle|cell cycle]]. In fact, an increase in *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* activity significantly delays cell cycle progression \[[^27]\]. In addition, the overall effect of *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* upregulation on carbohydrate and lipid metabolism is similar to that of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]*, promoting [[Gluconeogenesis|gluconeogenesis]] through deacetylation of [[PEPCK|phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK)]] \[[^28]\] and inhibiting [[Adipocyte Differentiation|adipocyte differentiation]] through deacetylation of [[FOXO1|FoxO1]] \[[^9], [^29]\]. Furthermore, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* also has [[Anti-inflammatory|anti-inflammatory]] effects \[[^30]].

*[[SIRT3|Sirt3]]* is a [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] enzyme, and it deacetylates and activates mitochondrial enzymes to regulate diverse mitochondrial functions, such as [[Adenosine Triphosphate|ATP]] production, [[Reactive Oxygen Species|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]] management, β-oxidation, [[Ketogenesis|ketogenesis]], and [[Apoptosis|cell death]] \[[^31]\]. The metabolic actions of *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* on carbohydrate and lipid metabolism are similar to those of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* (e.g., stimulation of gluconeogenesis, inhibition of lipogenesis, activation of [[Fatty Acid Oxidation|fatty acid oxidation]], and some neuroprotective actions) \[[^32]\]. Furthermore, *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* has also been related to adaptive [[Thermogenesis|thermogenesis]] because of its regulation in both white and [[Brown Adipose Tissue|brown adipose tissue]] by [[Caloric Restriction|caloric restriction (CR)]] and cold exposure \[[^33]\].

Unlike the other sirtuins, *[[SIRT4|Sirt4]]* is a [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] sirtuin lacking *in vitro* deacetylase activity \[[^34]\]. Biologically, *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* functions in many important processes, particularly in [[Glutamine|glutamine]] and [[Fatty Acid Metabolism|fatty acid metabolism]] \[[^35]\]. *[[SIRT4|Sirt4]]* is also thought to regulate [[Adenosine Triphosphate|ATP]] homeostasis. *[[SIRT4|Sirt4]]* improves the efficacy of ATP synthesis by inhibiting the oxidative phosphorylation uncoupler [[ANT2|ANT2]] \[[^36]\].

*[[SIRT5|Sirt5]]* is a [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] enzyme \[[^20]\]. It is now known that newly discovered PTMs removed by *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* can regulate the activity of enzymes affecting the [[Redox|redox]] status of cells and energy utilization, but we have just started to learn about the exact influence of *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* on these pathways. In addition to the deacylase activities of *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]*, recent studies have suggested that *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* can mediate protein desuccinylation, demalonylation, and deglutarylation \[[^21], [^37], [^38]].

*[[SIRT6|Sirt6]]* deacetylates specific cellular targets: [[H3K9ac|H3K9Ac]] and H3K56Ac, the [[DNA Repair|DNA repair]] factor [[CtIP|CtIP]], and the acetyltransferase [[GCN5|GCN5]] \[[^39]\]. Recent studies have shown that *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* also has a deacylase activity \[[^19]\]and interacts physically with some non-histone proteins - not only through deacylation, but also through direct physical interaction (PIA), inhibition of their binding at the target gene promoters (IATGP) and destabilization of their binding at the target gene promoters (DATGP)\[[^40]\]. Through these functions, *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* plays essential roles in metabolic homeostasis, [[Inflammation|inflammation]], stress responses, and [[Genomic Stability|genomic stability]] \[[^41]\].

*[[SIRT7|Sirt7]]* is the only sirtuin localized to the [[Nucleolus|nucleolus]] and is a component of the [[RNA Polymerase I|RNA polymerase I (Pol I)]] transcriptional machinery. By interacting with [[RNA Polymerase I|RNA Pol I]] and histones, *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* regulates the transcription of [[rDNA|rDNA]] in mammalian cells \[[^42]\]. In addition, *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* can mediate histone desuccinylation \[[^43]\].

## Sirtuins and aging

[[Aging|Ageing]] is a conserved phenomenon across all species and imposes an ever-increasing risk of dysfunction and death in older organisms. Growing evidences have shown that [[sirtuins]] are essential factors those delay [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]] and extends the organismal [[lifespan]] through the regulation of diverse cellular processes. Therefore, in the review, we summarize the evidences and controversies regarding the roles of different sirtuins on aging and lifespan extension, and systematically elucidate the functions and pathways of sirtuins on aging and lifespan extension.

The link between sirtuins and [[longevity|longevity]] was first established 20 years ago in yeast, in which the complex of *Sir2/3/4* extended the replicative [[lifespan]] of [[Saccharomyces cerevisiae|S. cerevisiae]] \[[^44], [^45]\]. Research interests increased after a report showed that extra copies of *[[Sir2 (yeast)|Sir2]]*, a member of sirtuins in budding yeast [[Saccharomyces cerevisiae|Saccharomyces cerevisiae]], extended the [[lifespan]] by 30% by preventing the formation of extrachromosomal DNA circles \[[^3]\]. Subsequently, more and more research shows that sirtuins can regulate longevity in numerous lower organisms, especially yeast *[[Sir2 (yeast)|Sir2]]* and its homologues, which extend the [[lifespan]] of budding yeast *S. cereviseasae*, worms *[[Caenorhabditis elegans|C. elegans]]*, fruit flies *[[Drosophila melanogaster|D. melanogaster]]*, and mice \[[^46] - [^48]\]. So far, The prolongevity effect of *sir2* has been confirmed in higher organisms, while the mechanisms of exerting prolongevity effects are different from that in yeast, including changes in [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial function and biogenesis]], suppression of [[Inflammation|inflammation]], and regulation of [[Genomic Stability|genomic stability]] \[[^49]\].

Though several reports have challenged this theory, [[sirtuins]] have long been recognized as regulators of [[Aging|ageing]], and overexpression of some sirtuins has been shown to extend [[lifespan]] in several organisms. The suppression of [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]] by sirtuin is mainly mediated through delaying the age-related telomere attrition, sustaining genome integrity and promoting [[DNA Repair|DNA damage repair]] \[[^50]\]. According to reports, *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* deacetylates histones H3, H4 and H1 and more than 50 non-histone proteins, including DNMT1, transcription factors and [[DNA Repair|DNA repair]] proteins \[[^2]\]. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* and *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* were shown to be recruited to the damaged sites and promote [[DNA Repair|DNA repair]] through deacetylating the repair proteins such as poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase ([[PARP-1|PARP-1]]), [[Ku70|Ku70]], NBS, and Werner ([[WRN|WRN]]) helicase \[[^12], [^51] - [^52]\]. In mammals, sirtuin upregulation can work in a context-, tissue-, and particular sirtuin-dependent manner \[[^53]\], and not all laboratories have managed to repeat the initial life span extending effect of sirtuins upregulation \[[^54], [^55]\]. In addition, sirtuins are found to especially interact with all the major conserved longevity pathways, such as [[AMPK|AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK)]], [[Insulin/IGF-1 Signaling|insulin/IGF-1 signaling (IIS)]], [[mTOR|target of rapamycin (TOR)]], and forkhead box O ([[FOXO|FOXO]]). Of these, FOXO transcription factor is the most fascinating target of sirtuin. In *[[Caenorhabditis elegans|C. elegans]]*, the extension of [[lifespan]] by elevation of sir-2.1 was shown to be dependent on [[DAF-16|daf-16]], the homologue of FOXO in worms \[[^56] - [^57]\]. Considering that FOXO is a major component in the IIS cascade to promote lifespan extension and stress resistance, several evidences have reported the association of the IIS pathway with the prolongevity effect of sirtuin. In *[[Caenorhabditis elegans|C. elegans]]*, the deletion of sir-2.1 had no effect on the [[lifespan]] of a long-living daf-2 mutants \[[^58]\]. In mammals, the relationship of IIS and sirtuin has also been well investigated. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* is reported to play a crucial role in metabolic homeostasis and IIS \[[^59], [^60]\]. AMPK signaling belongs to the protein kinase family and restores cellular energy levels. Increased AMPK activity is known to extend the lifespan of some model organisms. The mutation of AMPK (aak-2) in *C. elegans* abrogated the lifespan extension by sir-2.1 expression \[[^61]\], indicating that AMPK also contributes to the sirtuin-induced lifespan extension. *Sirt1* activates AMPK through the direct deacetylation of LKB1, a regulator of AMPK \[[^62]\]. In addition, AMPK contributes to the prolongevity effect of IIS, suggesting that these longevity pathways intricately cross-talk with each other. Apart from these, several other molecules are also reported to mediate lifespan extension by sirtuin overexpression, including 14-3-3, kat-1, hcf-1, and cts-1 in *C. elegans*. In addition, a study of mutant screening reported that loss-of-function mutations of ketoacyl thiolase (kat-1) resulted in premature aging and fully suppressed the lifespan extension exerted by overexpression of sir-2.1 \[[^63]\]. Also, host cell factor-1 (hcf-1), a nuclear co-repressor of FOXO, was shown to act downstream of sir-2.1 to modulate the lifespan in C. elegant \[[^64]\]. Furthermore, mitochondrial regulators such as cts-1 and fzo-1, and the mitochondrial unfolded protein response (UPRmt) gene hsp-6, were reported to be increased by sir-2.1 overexpression, and the knock-down of UPRmt regulator ubl-5 using RNAi almost completely suppressed the lifespan extension by sir-2.1 overexpression \[[^65]\]. Among the mammalian [[sirtuins]], *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* has been the most extensively characterized for its role in [[Aging|aging]]. Although much of the attention has gone to *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* and its protective effects against the onset of chronic diseases, its effect on [[longevity|longevity]] remains unconvincing. Sirtuins other than *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* are also reported to exert a prolongevity effect. Recently, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* has been found to be a key modulator of [[Aging|ageing]], and it extends [[lifespan]] in the [[BubR1|BubR1]] mice model\[[^66]\]. Additionally, *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* is the only sirtuin that has been shown to be associated with [[Aging|human aging]]; some (but not all) studies have linked polymorphisms in the *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* genomic locus to survival in elderly individuals \[[^54], [^55], [^67], [^68]\]. However, no pan- or tissue-specific transgenic animal models overexpressing *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* to determine whether *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* overexpression confers [[lifespan]] extension or protects against age-associated pathologies have been described in the literature currently, and some newer studies failed to confirm these correlations in other populations \[[^55], [^68]\]. In contrast, recent work on *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* suggests that this sirtuin might hold the most potential for actual life-span extension \[[^47]\]. Loss of *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* causes severe metabolic defects and rapid [[Aging|aging]] \[[^69]\]. In addition, global *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* depletion contributes to premature [[Aging|ageing]], especially in the backbone, [[White Adipose Tissue|white adipose tissue]] and the [[Heart|heart]] \[[^70], [^71]\].

However, it is clear that we are just starting to appreciate the importance of identifying specific processes in aging regulated by the different members of the sirtuin family in a tissue-, cell type-, and gene specific context. Thus, identifying these processes might be necessary to gain a better understanding of their role and fill in the current knowledge gaps in the field.

Although there has been emerging debate on the role of [[sirtuins]] in [[Aging|ageing]] and [[lifespan]] extension, mounting evidence suggests that [[sirtuins]] are indeed the critical modulators of [[Aging|ageing]] and [[Age-Related Diseases|aging-related diseases]] via different signalling pathways. The human sirtuin isoforms, *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* -7, are considered attractive therapeutic targets for [[Age-Related Diseases|ageing-related diseases]] listed in [Table 3](#T3-ad-11-4-927), including [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]], [[Metabolic Syndrome|metabolic syndrome]], [[Cardiomyopathy|cardiomyopathies]], non-alcoholic hepatic steatosis, hyperinsulinism-induced dyslipidaemia, [[Chronic Inflammation|chronic inflammation]], [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]], and some types of [[Cancer|cancer]] \[[^72], [^73]\]. ## Sirtuins and neurodegenerative disease

Although the body’s organ systems experience a general decline in function with age, perhaps the most emotionally and physically devastating decline is associated with the CNS. Impaired CNS function, with its effects on cognition, memory, hearing, balance, and motor control, can lead to a rapid loss of quality of life. In recent years, the risk of [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative disease]] (e.g., [[Alzheimer’s Disease|Alzheimer’s disease (AD)]], [[Parkinson’s Disease|Parkinson’s disease (PD)]], [[Huntington’s Disease|Huntington’s disease (HD)]], and others) has increased sharply with age \[[^74]\]. Several studies have revealed the important roles of [[sirtuins]] in neuronal development and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative disease]] \[[^75], [^76]\]. Though the ability of sirtuins to ameliorate CNS-specific disorders is still a very inchoate area of investigation, the initial findings offer significant promise.

Progress in understanding the neurobiological benefits of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* has been focused on animal models of different human CNS diseases. In mouse models of [[Alzheimer’s Disease|Alzheimer’s disease]], brain-specific knockout of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* caused a significant elevation in β-amyloid plaques and reactive gliosis \[[^77]\]. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* also conferred [[Neuroprotection|neuroprotection]] in three different mouse models of [[Huntington’s Disease|Huntington’s disease]] \[[^78], [^79]\]. Brain-specific deletion of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* exacerbated the neurotoxicity associated with mutant huntingtin protein, whereas overexpression of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* attenuated the toxicity. In addition, in a mouse model of [[Parkinson’s Disease|Parkinson’s disease]], overexpression of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* reduced α-synuclein aggregates, reduced gliosis, and attenuated lethality \[[^80]\]. In a mouse model of injury-induced axonal degeneration, [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] biosynthesis and its support of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* activity were shown to be essential in preventing axonal loss following axonal transection \[[^81], [^82]\].

Inhibition of *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* by pharmacological and genetic means in invertebrate and cell culture models has suggested potential neuroprotective benefits \[[^83]\]. Unfortunately, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* knockout mice lacked a remarkable phenotype related to neurodegeneration \[[^84]\], calling into question the effect of *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* on neuronal health in mammals.

Less data is available for other sirtuins. It has been found that short-term AD treatment with extracellular Aβ1-42 oligomers enhanced the expression of the *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* gene, but prolonged treatment affected all three [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] isoforms (*[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* to *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]*), suggesting that links between [[APP|APP]]/Aβ and sirtuins might be more complex, and possibly reciprocal \[[^85]\].

## Sirtuins and cardiovascular diseases

In addition, [[Aging|aging]] results in well-defined phenotypic changes which lead the [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular system]] to develop diseases even in the absence of traditional risk factors \[[^86]\]. [[Sirtuins]] have been associated with [[Cardiovascular Disease|vascular diseases]] in humans, such as carotid plaques \[[^87]\], carotid intima media thickness \[[^88]\], arterial stiffness \[[^89]\], and plaque area and morphology \[[^90]].

Whole body augmentation of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* activity confers resistance to many [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular]] sequelae associated with [[Metabolic Syndrome|metabolic syndrome]]. Study indicated that endothelial *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* age-dependent depletion or its inactivation is a frequent companion of many [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular diseases (CVDs)]] \[[^91]\]. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* is highly expressed in [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]], and activation of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* in endothelial tissues may be beneficial in the protection of endothelial cell function with age \[[^92]\]. Conversely, *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* insufficiency results in greater [[Foam Cell|foam cell]] formation and [[Atherosclerosis|atherosclerotic]] lesioning \[[^93]\]. Age-related loss of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* protein expression in human [[Vascular Smooth Muscle Cell|VSMC]] correlates with a loss of capacity for vascular repair, diminished stress response, and increased [[Cellular Senescence|senescence]] \[[^94], [^95]\]. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* appears to counteract [[Atherosclerosis|atherosclerosis]] by inhibiting VSMC hypertrophy \[[^96]\] and neointima formation and protecting against DNA damage, medial degeneration, and [[Hypertension|hypertension]] \[[^97], [^98]\]. In summary, *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* acts as a cardioprotective molecule that protects from [[Aging|aging]], induces resistance against hypertrophic and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stresses]], inhibits [[Apoptosis|cardiomyocyte apoptosis]], and regulates cardiac energy metabolism \[[^99], [^100]\]. However, the role of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* on [[longevity|longevity]] and human [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular diseases]] is not fully convincing, so further studies are needed.

Hashimoto-Komatsu et al. showed that *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* mediates microtubule reorganization induced by [[Angiotensin II|Ang II]] and cyclic stretch in [[Endothelial Cells|endothelial cells]], suggesting that *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* is a key regulator of endothelial remodeling \[[^101]\]. However, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* knockout mice have no [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiac]] abnormalities. Thus, further studies are warranted to precisely define the role of *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* in cardiac contractile function under physiological and pathological conditions. *[[SIRT3|Sirt3]]* is necessary to prevent [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] dysfunction and [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]] during [[Aging|ageing]] \[[^102], [^103]\]. Overexpression of *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* blunts [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]] by decreasing [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] via upregulation of endogenous antioxidants (like [[MnSOD|Mn-SOD2]] and catalase). *[[SIRT4|Sirt4]]* is found to be specifically enriched in the [[Heart|heart]], kidney, brain, and liver. Furthermore, *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* can also negatively modulate [[Insulin Secretion|insulin secretion]], [[Fatty Acid Oxidation|fatty acid oxidation]], and mitochondrial gene expression in [[Cardiomyocyte|cardiomyocytes]] and the liver, although the mechanism remains elusive and in vivo data are actually absent \[[^104]\]. Variants in *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* gene have been found to be associated with the risk of carotid plaque development \[[^94]\]. In addition, Liu et al. have demonstrated in vivo that *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* plays a critical role in regulating [[Cell Viability|cell viability]] in [[Cardiomyocyte|cardiomyocytes]] \[[^105]\]. Future studies are required to investigate the correlations between *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* level and on the effect of *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* on various pathologic pathways of [[Cardiovascular Disease|CVDs]].

Based on the striking phenotype of *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* knockout mice, which are predisposed to accelerated [[Cellular Senescence|senescence]], significant researches have shown in vivo that *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* can also regulate [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]] and age-related [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular]] alterations \[[^106], [^107]\]. Among nuclear sirtuins, *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* and *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* play an important role in prevention and delay of [[Cardiovascular Disease|CVDs]] \[[^108]\]. In fact, *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* expression blocks the development of [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]] and [[Heart Failure|heart failure]] \[[^109], [^110]\], whereas some data suggested that *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* promoted [[Cardiomyocyte|cardiomyocyte]] hypertrophy \[[^111]\]. To date, the molecular events through which *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* exerts a protective role at [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular]] level, regulating the endothelial cell and [[Cardiomyocyte|cardiomyocyte]] response to stress, reducing [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] and [[Hyperglycemia|hyperglycemia]], are still unclear. Vakhrusheva et al. have demonstrated that *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* plays an important role in preventing progressive functional deterioration of the [[Heart|heart]] \[[^71]\]. *[[SIRT7|Sirt7]]* deletion leads to various pathological changes in the [[Heart|heart]], which further aggravates with age, including [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|heart hypertrophy]], [[Fibrosis|fibrosis]], lipofuscin accumulation and [[Inflammatory Cardiomyopathy|inflammatory cardiomyopathy]].

However, there are many unsolved issues regarding the function of sirtuins at cardiovascular level, and undoubtedly, more work is needed to understand the role of the different sirtuins in cardiac and vascular cell biology before they can be considered as a valuable therapeutic target against age-related cardiovascular diseases.

## Sirtuins and metabolic diseases

Furthermore, declines in basal metabolic rate and physical activity contribute to an elevated incidence of [[Insulin Resistance|insulin resistance]], [[Obesity|obesity]], and [[Metabolic Syndrome|metabolic syndrome]] with age \[[^112]\]. Sensing of the metabolic state and regulation of the sirtuin function and expression are critical components of the metabolic machinery. Thus, activation of pathways that restores [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin sensitivity]] and improves the utilization of glucose and [[Fatty Acid Metabolism|fatty acids]] would be of benefit in stemming the pathologies associated with age-related metabolic dysfunction \[[^113]\]. In the next review, we summarize an overview and update on the function of different sirtuin in metabolism, and further touch the correlation between each sirtuin and disorders of metabolism.

Many studies have indicated that *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* is an important target for mitigating metabolic dysfunction. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* is directly involved on metabolic pathways such as [[Lipogenesis|lipogenesis]], stimulation of [[Fatty Acid Oxidation|fatty acid β-oxidation]], and [[Gluconeogenesis|gluconeogenesis]]. Its overexpression is thought to be beneficial and generates phenotypes in mice similar to [[Caloric Restriction|calorie restriction]] conditions. All the major [[Mitochondria|mitochondrial]] processes including the krebs cycle, the [[Fatty Acid Metabolism|fatty acid metabolism]], the antioxidant response, the amino acid catabolism, and so on, are regulated by the balance of N"-lysine acetylation/deacetylation. Several transgenic models have shown that heightened *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* activity protects against the metabolic derangement associated with [[Obesity|obesity]] \[[^114]\]. *[[SIRT1|Sirt1]]* and *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* activators can prevent and reverse [[Insulin Resistance|insulin resistance]] and [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetic complications]], and have been proven to be promising therapeutic targets for [[Diabetes Mellitus|type 2 diabetes (T2D)]] \[[^115] - [^116]\]. In addition, the protective effects of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* may occur through attenuation of [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] responses, as *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* overexpression mitigates HFD-induced [[Hepatic Steatosis|hepatic steatosis]] and adipose tissue specific inflammation \[[^117], [^118]\].

Compared to *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]*, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* is abundant in [[Adipocytes|adipocytes]]. Current evidence suggests a role for *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* in regulating adipose tissue development and function. *[[SIRT2|Sirt2]]* activates the [[PEPCK|PEPCK]] via deacetylation and enhances [[Gluconeogenesis|gluconeogenesis]] during times of glucose deprivation \[[^119]\]. Meanwhile, recent studies have proposed that, with regard to [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin sensitivity]], *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* may act in opposing roles in different tissues \[[^120]\]. Thus, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* activation may prove to be protective against [[Obesity|obesity]], and its role in metabolic homeostasis deserves further exploration.

*[[SIRT3|Sirt3]]* may regulate cellular energy status both at transcriptional level in the nucleus and by posttranscriptional mechanisms in [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]], and its expression is higher in metabolically active tissues including brain, liver, [[Heart|heart]], [[Brown Adipose Tissue|brown adipose tissue]] and [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] \[[^121]\]. *[[SIRT3|Sirt3]]* functions by activating important enzymes during [[Caloric Restriction|CR]], such as 3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl- CoA synthase 2 for generation of [[Ketogenesis|ketones]] \[[^122]\] and long chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase for the oxidation of long-chain [[Fatty Acid Oxidation|fatty acids]] \[[^123]\]. *[[SIRT3|Sirt3]]* also activates [[Glutamate Dehydrogenase (GDH)|glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH)]], facilitating [[Gluconeogenesis|gluconeogenesis]] from amino acids\[[^124]\]. In addition, *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* indirectly destabilizes the transcription factor [[HIF1α|HIF1α]] and subsequently inhibits [[Glycolysis|glycolysis]] and glucose oxidation \[[^125]\]. Intriguingly, recent studies have shown that *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* levels in pancreatic islets are reduced in patients afflicted with [[Diabetes Mellitus|type 2 diabetes]] \[[^126]\], and *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* overexpression in pancreatic β-cells promotes [[Insulin Secretion|insulin secretion]] and abrogates [[Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress|endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress]] that is connected to β-cell dysfunction and [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] \[[^127]\].

In contrast to *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]*, hepatic *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* expression declines slightly during [[Caloric Restriction|caloric restriction (CR)]] and increases in genetic models of [[Diabetes Mellitus|diabetes]] \[[^34], [^128], [^129]\]. Little is known about the physiological relevance of *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* and its role in metabolism. *[[SIRT4|Sirt4]]*, initially reported as a unique [[ADP-ribosylation|ADP ribosyltransferase]], appears to blunt [[Insulin Secretion|insulin secretion]] by reducing [[Glutamate Dehydrogenase (GDH)|GDH]] activity \[[^130]\]. In addition to GDH, a diverse range of *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* targets are identified in the regulation of [[Insulin Secretion|insulin secretion]], including ADP/ATP carrier proteins, insulin-degrading enzymes, [[ANT2|ANT2]] and ANT3 \[[^130]\]. Recently, Haigis and coworkers showed that *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* promoted [[Lipid Synthesis|lipid synthesis]] and inhibition of [[Fatty Acid Oxidation|fatty acid oxidation]] by deacetylation of malonyl CoA decarboxylase \[[^131]\].

In contrast to other sirtuins, *[[SIRT5|Sirt5]]* displays deacetylase and [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] dependent demalonylase and desuccinylase activities. *[[SIRT5|Sirt5]]* facilitates [[Glycolysis|glycolysis]] by demalonylating the glycolytic enzyme [[GAPDH|glyceraldehyde phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH)]] \[[^38]\]. A recent study proposed that *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* might be positively correlated with [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin sensitivity]], the biological significance of which still remains to be confirmed \[[^132]\].

The indication of the connection between *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* and metabolism was first provided by Mostoslavsky et al. who showed that *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* -deficient mice had a loss of subcutaneous fat, [[Lymphopenia|lymphopenia]] and acute [[Hypoglycemia|hypoglycemia]] \[[^133]\]. Conversely, one recent study revealed that *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* overexpression protects mice from diet-induced [[Obesity|obesity]], showing increased [[Glucose Tolerance|glucose tolerance]] and reduced fat accumulation \[[^134]\]. In addition, *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* may positively mediate glucose stimulated [[Insulin Secretion|insulin secretion]] and overexpression of *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* enhances [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin sensitivity]] in [[Skeletal Muscle|skeletal muscle]] and liver, which implicates that *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* may act as an attractive therapeutic target for [[Diabetes Mellitus|T2D]] \[[^135]\].

In addition, *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* knockout mice were resistant to [[Glucose Intolerance|glucose intolerance]], and [[Insulin Sensitivity|insulin sensitivity]] was improved in *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* knockout mice receiving a [[High-Fat Diet|high-fat diet]] \[[^46]\], revealing a novel role for *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* in glucose metabolism.

## Sirtuins and Cancer

Based upon statistics from the National Cancer Institute, 54% of all [[Cancer|cancer]] cases occur in people over the age of 65. [[Cancer]] is recognized as an [[Age-Related Diseases|age-related disease]] and occurs in an exponentially increasing pattern in elderly individuals. In recent years, [[Cancer]] has become a grim challenge for us. [[Cancer]] seems to be one of the biggest hurdles in our way to longer and healthier [[Aging|ageing]] lives.

Currently, accumulating evidence has shown that the aberrant [[Epigenetics|epigenetic]] activation of [[sirtuins]] signaling pathways contributes to tumor carcinogenesis and may be potential therapeutic targets for future treatments and biomarkers in predicting prognosis in cancers \[[^23], [^136]\]. Interestingly, sirtuins seem to have a [[Dual Role in Cancer|dual role in cancer]] \[[^137]\]. On the one hand, some sirtuins help protect [[DNA|DNA]] from damage and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]], maintain [[Genomic Stability|genomic stability]], limit replicative life-span, and protect organisms against [[Cancer|cancer]]. On the other hand, some data suggest that the promotion of [[Cell Survival|cell survival]] under stress conditions by sirtuins could be directly involved in [[Tumorigenesis|tumorigenesis]], as it would inhibit [[Cellular Senescence|senescence]] and allow unchecked cell division \[[^138]].

The role of sirtuins, especially *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* in [[Carcinogenesis|carcinogenesis]], appears to be opposing and complicated. Under normal conditions, in response to stress or to [[DNA Damage|DNA damage]], *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* might promote [[Cell Survival|cell survival]] via [[Cell Cycle Arrest|cell cycle arrest]], [[DNA Repair|DNA repair]], or inhibition of [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]. If the stress signal becomes chronic or the levels of damage cross a certain threshold, *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* could induce [[Cellular Senescence|cell senescence]] and prevent [[Carcinogenesis|carcinogenesis]] \[[^114]\]. However, following chronic stress or [[DNA Damage|DNA damage]], the loss of a [[Tumor Suppressor|tumor suppressor]] or of any other checkpoint-related factor could cause an imbalance in these regulatory processes and induce *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* overexpression beyond a critical limit. The aberrant overexpression of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* would in turn contribute to transformation and tumor formation by promoting cell growth and inhibiting [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] \[[^139]\].

In fact, *sirt1* acts as a tumor keystone, and its level and action maintain a fine and delicate balance between suppression and promotion of oncogenesis. It is plausible that *sirt1* acts as a suppressor and then a promoter (or vice versa) depending on the stage and situation of tumourigenesis. The dual role of sirtuins in different cancers have has been summarized and shown in [Table 5](#T5-ad-11-4-927). Studies have shown that *sirt1* is upregulated in many human cancer cell lines, as well as in tissues collected from patients suffering from various types of cancer (e.g., lung cancer, prostatic cancer, colon cancer, breast cancer, ovarian cancer, leukaemia, neuroblastomas, osteo-sarcomas, etc.), suggesting that sirtuins might be cancer therapeutic targets and the *sirt1* inhibition in cancer cells could possibly inhibit cancer cell growth \[[^140] - [^141]\]. Other research, however, points to a tumor suppressive role for *sirt1*. Certain cancer types, such as oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), restoration of *sirt1* levels in these cells results in inhibition of tumor growth \[[^142]\]. In other cancer mouse models, *sirt1* can protect against the development of intestinal tumors in a β-catenin-driven colon cancer model \[[^143]\], sarcomas, lymphomas, teratomas, and carcinomas arising from deletion of p53 \[[^144]\], HFD-induced hepatocarcinomas \[[^114]\], and age-associated spontaneous tumor development \[[^114]\]. In addition, other studies reported only slightly elevated *sirt1* activity (in some thyroid cancers) or unchanged activity in some lung cancers, colon cancers, gastric cancers, urinary bladder cancers, and skin cancers \[[^144]\].

Similar to *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]*, *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* is either a positive or a negative regulator of the [[Tumorigenesis|tumorigenic process]]. The expression of *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* has been found downregulated in several cancers, such as gliomas, [[Breast Cancer|breast cancer]], head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and esophageal adenocarcinoma, and elevated in others, such as neuroblastoma, pancreatic cancer, and acute myeloid leukemia \[[^145], [^146]\].

The case of *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* is more complex. Several studies indicate that *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* is a [[Tumor Suppressor|tumor suppressor]], mainly through mechanisms linked to [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative response]], energetic balance, and metabolic regulation \[[^11], [^147]\]. *[[SIRT3|Sirt3]]* expression is decreased in many human cancers, especially in 40% of human [[Breast Cancer|breast]] and ovarian cancers \[[^106], [^125]\]. However, in specific cancer types, *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* turns out to be an [[Oncogene|oncogene]] and promote tumorigenensis \[[^148], [^149]\].

*[[SIRT4|Sirt4]]* mRNA levels were reduced in several human cancers, such as small cell lung carcinoma \[[^150]\], gastric cancer \[[^151]\], [[Breast Cancer|breast cancer]] and leukemia \[[^152]\]. Lower *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* expression is associated with shorter survival time in lung tumor patients \[[^153]\]. A recent study has also shown that *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* is a crucial regulator of [[Stress Resistance|stress resistance]] in cancer cells and *[[SIRT4|sirt4]]* loss sensitizes cells to [[DNA Damage|DNA damage]] or [[Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress|ER stress]] \[[^154]\].

Similar to other sirtuins, *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* has been considered as a potential [[Oncogene|oncogene]] or [[Tumor Suppressor|tumor suppressor]]. *[[SIRT5|Sirt5]]* acts as a potential [[Oncogene|oncogene]], and is overexpressed in non-small cell lung cancer \[[^155]\] and ovarian carcinoma \[[^156]\]. However, *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* also emerged as a [[Tumor Suppressor|tumor suppressor]] in squamous cell carcinoma \[[^157]\] and endometrial carcinoma \[[^158]].

*[[SIRT6|Sirt6]]* is thought to be a significant [[Tumor Suppressor|tumor suppressor protein (TSP)]], given its major role as a guardian of [[Genomic Stability|genome stability]]\[[^159], [^160]\]. *[[SIRT6|Sirt6]]* overexpression induces intense [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] in cancer cells but not in normal cells, which makes it an attractive ‘‘target’’ for future antineoplastic medications \[[^161]\]. In addition, as with *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]*, *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* plays both [[Dual Role in Cancer|tumor suppressing and promoting roles]]. *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* expression is downregulated in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and colon, pancreatic, liver and non-small cell lung cancers \[[^157], [^162], [^163]\]. Conversely, increased *[[SIRT6|sirt6]]* expression has been reported in human skin squamous cell carcinoma and pancreatic, prostate and [[Breast Cancer|breast cancers]], whose high expression suggests a poor prognosis and [[Chemotherapy Resistance|chemotherapy resistance]]\[[^164] - [^165]].

Although *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* has received comparatively less attention than other sirtuins, *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* appears to have been regarded as a potential [[Oncogene|oncogene]] for its upregulation in all the cancer types studied so far, such as thyroid cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, bladder cancer and colorectal cancer \[[^166] - [^167]\]. *[[SIRT7|Sirt7]]* can contribute to the maintenance of a transformed cell phenotype in tumor cells by suppressing the expression of some [[Tumor Suppressor|TSPs]] \[[^15]\]. However, *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* does not contribute to the initiation of [[Carcinogenesis|carcinogenesis]], which has been experimentally proven, as no correlation between *[[SIRT7|sirt7]]* upregulation in normal cells and their susceptibility to transformation has been found \[[^6], [^15]].

## Small molecule activators of sirtuins

Dietary restriction (DR)/CR (the reduction of calorie intake without causing malnutrition) is the only known intervention able to increase the lifespan in many species, including yeast, fruit flies, nematodes, fish, rats, mice, hamsters and dogs \[[^168], [^169]\] and possibly even primates \[[^170]\].

Since [[sirtuins]] is commonly believed to mediate the beneficial effects of [[Caloric Restriction|CR]], the activators of sirtuins are considered to mimic these beneficial effects and are hence attractive therapeutics for [[Age-Related Diseases|age-related diseases]] \[[^171]\]. The search for molecules those activates sirtuins began more than a decade ago. These [[STACs|sirtuin-activating compounds (STACs)]] are mainly divided into two categories. One is the use of exogenous activators, the other is replenishment of the cellular [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] \[[^73], [^172]\]. The first STACs were discovered for *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* in 2003, and the most potent of which was [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]]. This initial discovery was important because it proved that allosteric activation of [[sirtuins]] was possible \[[^172]\]. Treatment with [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]] and its derivatives brought some beneficial effects of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* induction without applying [[Caloric Restriction|CR]] \[[^173], [^174]\]. Moreover, studies have shown that the sirtuin activator [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]] has chemo preventive activity against various [[Cancer|cancers]], including leukemia, DMBA-induced mammary tumors in rats, skin cancer, and prostate cancer \[[^175] - [^176]\]. Following the discovery of [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]], a few researchers tried to find other selective activators \[[^174]\]. High-throughput screening and medicinal chemical efforts have since identified more than 14,000 STACs from a dozen chemical classes, including several classes of plant derived metabolites such as flavones, stilbenes, chalcones, and anthocyanidins, which directly activate *[[SIRT1|sirt1]] in vitro* \[[^172]\]. Synthetic STACs include a number of agents such as imidazothiazoles (e.g., [[SRT1720|SRT1720]]) \[[^177]\], thiazolopyridines (e.g., STAC-2), benzimidazoles (e.g., STAC-5), bridged ureas (e.g., STAC-9 ), cilostazol \[[^178]\], paeonol \[[^179]\], statins \[[^180]\], hydrogen sulfide \[[^181], [^182]\] and persimmon \[[^183]\]. All of these chemical classes activate *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* by lowering the Km value of the substrate through a K-type allosteric activation mechanism\[[^184], [^185]\]. In addition, there are some natural anti-ageing compounds, such as quercetin, butein, fisetin, kaempferol, catechins and proanthocyanidins \[[^186]\].

An alternative approach to activating [[sirtuins]] is to regulate [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] level by activating enzymes involved in biosynthesis of [[NAD+|NAD]] or by inhibiting the [[CD38|CD38 NAD hydrolase]] \[[^187] - [^189]\]. It has been known since 2003 that upregulation of the [[NAD Salvage Pathway|NAD salvage pathway]], which recycles [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] from [[Nicotinamide|NAM]], can extend [[lifespan]] and mimic [[Caloric Restriction|calorie restriction]] in yeast \[[^190], [^191]\]. [[NAD+|NAD]]-boosting molecules constitute a newer class of [[STACs]] those are gaining attention as a way to restore [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] levels in elderly individuals and potentially activate all seven sirtuins with a single compound. Examples of [[NAD+|NAD]]-boosting molecules include [[NMN|NMN]], [[Nicotinamide Riboside|nicotinamide riboside]] \[[^192] - [^193]\], and inhibitors of [[CD38|CD38]] such as [[Apigenin|apigenin]] \[[^188]\], [[Quercetin|quercetin]] \[[^188]\] and GSK 897-78c \[[^194]\]. In addition, targeting the enzymes that regulate [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] levels, such as [[CD38|CD38]], CD157 and [[NAMPT|NAMPT]], may also be worth exploring for their therapeutic potential. Moreover, malate dehydrogenase, [[MDH1|MDH1]], which is involved in energy metabolism and reduces NAD+ to NADH during its catalytic reaction, also plays a critical role in [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]].

## Small molecule inhibitors of sirtuins

The inhibition of sirtuins has attracted more interest as a potential therapeutic anticancer strategy. Here, the fundamental principle of developing inhibitors is that they should have two characteristics: they should be potent and selective. The mechanism of a potent inhibitor is as follows: combining of the inhibitor and the sirtuin may be easier than that of Nε-acyl-lysine and the sirtuin, and the inhibitors may be processed by the sirtuin into a non-catalytic intermediate which can bind tightly to the active site of the sirtuin to prevent the normal binding and processing of the substrate \[[^195]\]. A selective inhibitor should perfectly inhibit one certain sirtuin, for example *[[SIRT2|sirt2]],* while not reacting with other sirtuins. Based on these 2 principles, there are some inhibitors already designed, such as [[Nicotinamide|NAM]] and thioacyllysine-containing compounds. However, there are still limits for such inhibitors.

Except for the mechanism-based inhibitor discussed above, there is another common way to develop an inhibitor for sirtuin, which is by chemical library screening. In this way, thousands of molecules can be screened. Of these screened molecules, there are 5 compounds potent and selective enough, including [[Sirtinol|sirtinol]] and its analogues, [[Splitomicin|splitomicin]] and its derivatives, indole derivatives, and [[Tenovin-6|tenovin]] and its analogues, to presumably work by noncovalently binding to the sirtuin active site and blocking substrate binding.

Compound 6 was found to be a highly potent inhibitor of the deacetylation reaction catalysed by *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]*. Compound 6 may have much less inhibitory effect on reactions catalysed by *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* and *[[SIRT3|sirt3]]* compared with *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]*. [[SirReal2|SirReal 2]] and Compound 7 were screened from an internal compound library, and were demonstrated to be potent *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* inhibitors \[[^196], [^197]\]. Compound 8 is another potent and selective *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* inhibitor. X-ray analysis showed that when this compound binds to the active domain of *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]*, it may form a rigid cyclic structure by intramolecular hydrogen bonding. Therefore, it may tightly bind with *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]*, highly potently and selectively inhibiting the activity of *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* \[[^195]\]. In addition, AK-7 is a brain-permeable selective *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* inhibitor. It is [[Neuroprotection|neuroprotective]] *in vitro*, by reducing polyglutamine inclusions and cholesterol levels in neurons \[[^198]\]. [[Tenovin-6|Tenovin-6]], [[Salermide|salermide]] and benzodeazaoxaflavin are small-molecule inhibitors of *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* and *[[SIRT2|sirt2]]* \[[^199], [^200]\]. Suramin inhibits *[[SIRT5|sirt5]]* by binding into the B- and C-pockets of the [[NAD+|NAD<sup>+</sup>]] binding site as well as to the substrate-binding site \[[^201]\]. In total, segmented inhibition or activation of [[sirtuins]] activity might confer therapeutic potentials in the future, mainly because of its double-edged sword effects in [[Cancer|cancer cells]].

In addition, Natural compounds present in the diet, classed as functional food/nutrients, are a great promise for health and [[longevity|longevity]] promotion and prevention of [[Age-Related Diseases|age-related chronic diseases]] \[[^202]\]. Such compounds are nontoxic, easy to use and commonly available and could be included into a normal diet for long lasting supplementation. Several reports emphasized that dietary supplementation of [[Polyphenols|polyphenols]] may protect against [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative]], [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular]], inflammatory, metabolic diseases and [[Cancer|cancer]] by enhancing *[[SIRT1|sirt1]]* deacetylase activity. However, in humans, the therapeutic and pharmacological potential of these natural compounds remains to be validated in clinical conditions.

## Implications and future perspectives

Since the discovery of the yeast *Sir2*, sirtuins have been focused on their functions in ageing and age-related disease. Accumulating studies have revealed that sirtuins exert profound protective functions against ageing-related pathologies and the degeneration of tissues and organs in elderlyindividuals. Their multivalent roles in blocking the development of ageing and ageing-related diseases further mark them as promising targets for developing interventions for several age-associated pathologies.

The intense efforts in the past ~15 years to develop isoform specific small molecule modulators of the sirtuins have yielded key insights into the pathophysiological roles of sirtuins and have seen great strides in understanding the enzymatic reaction mechanisms. Initial drug development efforts focused on *sirt1* and *sirt2* have yielded a number of potent *sirt2* inhibitors and *sirt1* activators, which have now been passed the first clinical trials, with evidence of safety and efficacy.

However, there is little standardization of the disease models currently used to study sirtuin therapeutic biology, leading to contradictory reports with limited cross-validation of the findings. There is still a long way to go for a molecule to be applied in treatment, with hurdles of regarding side effects, stability, and selectivity. The field of sirtuin modulators has clearly matured into an exciting path of drug development that holds the promise for treating common and rare diseases, with considerable unmet demand.
