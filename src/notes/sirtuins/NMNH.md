# NMNH

## Overview

NMNH (dihydronicotinamide mononucleotide) is the reduced form of [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide]] (NMN). It has been shown to promote [[NAD+ Biosynthesis|NAD+ synthesis]], joining [[NRH]] (reduced nicotinamide riboside) in an emerging class of reduced NAD+ precursors.

## Mechanism

NMNH carries the reduced pyridine ring (dihydronicotinamide), which is converted to NAD+ through the salvage machinery, typically via NAD+ kinase/adenylylation steps in the [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]]. Like NRH, NMNH bypasses some of the rate-limiting steps of the amidated routes, providing an alternative substrate for NAD+ replenishment.

> [!info] Flexibility of NAD+ metabolism
> Reduced precursors NMNH and NRH demonstrate that the NAD+ metabolic cycle is more flexible than initially recognized, offering alternative routes beyond the classic precursors [[Tryptophan]], nicotinamide, nicotinic acid, NMN, and [[Nicotinamide Riboside]].

## Physiological Function

Reduced NAD+ precursors expand the toolkit for restoring NAD+ levels across tissues. Since NAD+ declines with [[Aging]] and is central to [[Sirtuins|sirtuin]] activity, compounds such as NMNH are of interest for preventing age-related NAD+ loss.

## Research Applications

NMNH and NRH are being investigated for their potency, oral bioavailability, and tissue distribution relative to NMN and NR. [[Adenosine Kinase]]-catalyzed phosphorylation of NRH to NMNH links these reduced precursors into a connected biosynthetic network.
