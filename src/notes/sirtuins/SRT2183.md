# SRT2183

[[SRT2183]] is a synthetic small-molecule [[STACs|STAC]] (sirtuin-activating compound) developed by [[Sirtris Pharmaceuticals]] as a potent [[SIRT1]] activator. It belongs to the imidazothiazole class and is discussed together with [[SRT1720]] and [[SRT1460]] as the potent second-generation STACs reported after the polyphenolic activators (compound **8** in Fig. **3** of the review).

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- **Class**: Imidazothiazole; second-generation synthetic STAC.
- **Target**: [[SIRT1]] activator.
- **Selectivity/Potency**: More potent than resveratrol in deacetylase-based SIRT1 activation assays.

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

- SRT2183 mimics many transcriptional effects of resveratrol (deacetylation of SIRT1 substrates such as [[p53]]), shifting cells toward stress resistance and oxidative metabolism.
- Mechanism controversy: as with other STACs, activation was frequently only demonstrable with fluorescently tagged substrates. Given its metabolic similarity to resveratrol, SRT2183 may act *in vivo* partly through indirect pathways (e.g., the [[cAMP]]-[[Epac1]]-[[AMPK]] axis) rather than direct [[SIRT1]] binding.

## Applications

- Research tool in metabolic and longevity research to model SIRT1-dependent effects.
- STACs of this class were reported to possess anticancer activity, though the contribution of SIRT1 activation vs. off-target activity is unresolved.
