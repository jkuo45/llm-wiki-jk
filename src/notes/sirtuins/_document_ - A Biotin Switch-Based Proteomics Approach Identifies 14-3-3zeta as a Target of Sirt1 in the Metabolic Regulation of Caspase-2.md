# A Biotin Switch-Based Proteomics Approach Identifies 14-3-3ζ as a Target of Sirt1 in the Metabolic Regulation of Caspase-2

**A Biotin Switch-Based Proteomics Approach Identifies 14-3-3ζ as a Target of Sirt1 in the Metabolic Regulation of Caspase-2** (Andersen et al., *Molecular Cell* 2011;43(5):834–842, PMID 21884983 — cover story) adds a third caspase to the [[Sirtuin-Caspase Crosstalk]]: the initiator [[Caspase-2]], regulated metabolically through a SIRT1–[[14-3-3]]ζ axis.

## Key findings

> [!important] SIRT1 deacetylates 14-3-3ζ to keep caspase-2 suppressed
> [[SIRT1]] deacetylates **14-3-3ζ** (lysine 49), maintaining its binding to [[Caspase-2]]. Acetylation of 14-3-3ζ triggers its **dissociation from caspase-2**, allowing caspase-2 activation. SIRT1 inhibition therefore **sensitizes cells to caspase-2-dependent death** and overrides caspase-2 suppression by nutrient abundance.

- **Proteomics**: A biotin-switch strategy identified cytoplasmic SIRT1 substrates clustered in glycolysis/metabolism, oxidative stress, cytoskeletal dynamics, and apoptosis.
- **Metabolic logic**: Pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) flux → NADPH/G6P promotes **SIRT1 activity** toward 14-3-3ζ; G6P supplementation in *Xenopus* egg extract enhanced 14-3-3ζ-directed sirtuin activity and caspase-2/14-3-3ζ binding. Declining PPP activity → 14-3-3ζ acetylation → release → caspase-2 activation.
- **Two-layer caspase-2 control**: 14-3-3ζ binds caspase-2 in a phosphorylation-dependent manner, protecting **Ser135** (phosphorylated by [[CaMKII]]) from dephosphorylation — acetylation of 14-3-3ζ disengages this shield.
- **Cancer context**: In breast tumor cell lines SIRT1 is primarily **cytoplasmic** (vs. nuclear in normal breast tissue); SIRT1 inhibition there causes rapid caspase-2/14-3-3ζ dissociation and markedly enhances **paclitaxel** sensitivity — therapeutically relevant during mitotic arrest when transcription is not ongoing.

## Significance

Extends the crosstalk beyond the executioner/initiator caspases of apoptosis (caspase-3/9 cleaving SIRT1) to a **metabolic, cytoplasmic** mode: SIRT1 transmits nutrient abundance into apoptotic resistance by guarding 14-3-3ζ-mediated caspase-2 inhibition. Cytoplasmic SIRT1 thus has both pro-death (post-cleavage) and pro-survival (metabolic) roles depending on context.

## Connections

- [[SIRT1]]: Deacetylates 14-3-3ζ (K49) to maintain caspase-2 suppression.
- [[14-3-3]]: 14-3-3ζ isoform — acetylation state controls caspase-2 binding.
- [[Caspase-2]]: Apical caspase whose suppression by 14-3-3ζ is SIRT1-dependent.
- [[CaMKII]]: Phosphorylates caspase-2 Ser135; the shield 14-3-3ζ protects.
