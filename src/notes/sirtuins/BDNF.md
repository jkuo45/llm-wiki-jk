# BDNF

[[BDNF]] (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor) is a member of the [[Neurotrophin|neurotrophin]] family of growth factors that supports neuronal survival, differentiation, synaptic plasticity, and [[Neurogenesis|neurogenesis]]. It is expressed throughout the [[Brain|brain]], particularly in the [[Hippocampus|hippocampus]] and [[Cortex|cortex]], and is critical for learning, memory, and mood regulation. Mature BDNF is proteolytically cleaved from a larger pro-BDNF precursor; the mature form promotes survival and synaptic strengthening, whereas pro-BDNF can signal through p75^NTR^ to induce pruning and long-term depression.

## Structure and Signaling

BDNF signals primarily through the receptor tyrosine kinase TrkB (tropomyosin-related kinase B), triggering phosphorylation cascades including MAPK/ERK, PI3K–Akt, and PLCγ. These pathways converge on transcription factors such as [[CREB]], which drives the expression of synaptic and survival genes. BDNF–TrkB signalling strengthens excitatory synapses, facilitates long-term potentiation, and supports adult hippocampal neurogenesis—cellular substrates of memory and resilience.

## Sirtuin Connections

- [[SIRT1]] directly regulates [[BDNF]] transcription by deacetylating and repressing [[Hes1 and Hey2|Hes1]] (a transcriptional repressor of BDNF), thereby relieving inhibition and promoting neuronal survival and [[Neurogenesis|neurogenesis]].
- [[SIRT1]] also activates [[PGC-1α]], which co-activates the [[BDNF]] promoter, linking [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] to neurotrophic support—a coordination of energy metabolism and synaptic health.
- [[Exercise]] raises both [[BDNF]] and [[NAD+]] levels, and SIRT1 may mediate exercise-induced BDNF upregulation in the hippocampus.
- [[Caloric Restriction]] and [[Intermittent Fasting]] elevate [[BDNF]] through SIRT1/FoxO-dependent pathways, contributing to cognitive resilience against aging.

## Irisin Regulation

The myokine [[Irisin]], cleaved from the membrane protein [[FNDC5]] during exercise, is a potent upstream inducer of BDNF in the hippocampus. Irisin crosses the blood-brain barrier and upregulates BDNF expression through a mechanism dependent on endothelial integrin αVβ5 and the FAK/eNOS signaling pathway. In the hippocampus, FNDC5-derived irisin stimulates BDNF and downstream mediators of hippocampal function including Npas4, c-Fos, and Arc. This irisin→BDNF axis is now recognized as a core molecular mechanism by which exercise enhances learning, memory, and neurogenesis, and it may underlie the protective effects of physical activity against [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]]. Irisin also promotes [[Amyloid Beta|amyloid-β]] clearance via microglial eHsp90α, providing an additional neuroprotective mechanism beyond BDNF induction.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

BDNF is a central effector of experience-dependent plasticity: enriched environment, exercise, and learning all increase BDNF. Reduced BDNF levels are implicated in [[Depression]] and anxiety disorders, where SIRT1 activation and BDNF induction may underlie antidepressant effects. BDNF loss is a feature of [[Neurodegeneration]] in [[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]], and [[Huntington's Disease]]; SIRT1-mediated BDNF induction is neuroprotective and a target of caloric-restriction and resveratrol research. BDNF also interacts with calcium signalling—e.g., via channels such as [[TRPM2]]—linking activity-dependent Ca²⁺ transients to trophic support.

## Clinical and Research Relevance

BDNF is investigated as a biomarker of neuropsychiatric and neurodegenerative disease and as a surrogate for pro-plasticity interventions. Strategies that elevate BDNF through SIRT1/NAD⁺-dependent mechanisms (exercise, fasting, [[Resveratrol]], [[Nicotinamide Mononucleotide|NMN]]) are active areas of geroscience and neurotherapeutics.
