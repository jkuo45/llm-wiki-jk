# Deacetylation

[[Deacetylation]] is the hydrolytic removal of an acetyl group (–COCH₃) from a substrate, most importantly from **Nε-lysine acetyl residues** on proteins. In the longevity/longevity-medicine context it is dominated by the **sirtuin (class III HDAC) deacetylases**, which couple lysine deacetylation to [[NAD+]] consumption, thereby translating the cellular energy/redox state into broad transcriptional and metabolic reprogramming.

## Chemical Mechanism

- **Sirtuin-catalyzed**: The sirtuin active site binds the acetyl-lysine and [[NAD+]]; NAD+ is cleaved to nicotinamide + 2′-*O*-acetyl-ADP-ribose, transferring the acetyl group to the ADP-ribose and freeing deacetylated lysine (PMID 12434184).
- **Opposing enzymes**: [[Acetylation|Acetyltransferases]] (e.g., p300/CBP, GCN5/KATs) add acetyl groups; the acetyl/deacetyl balance sets the "acetylome."

## Biological Roles

- **Chromatin & transcription**: Deacetylation of histones (e.g., [[Histone H4]], H3) generally condenses chromatin and represses transcription; deacetylation of non-histone targets ([[p53]], [[FOXO]], [[PGC-1α]], [[TFEB]]) modulates stress responses, metabolism, and autophagy.
- **Metabolic sensing**: Because it consumes [[NAD+]], sirtuin deacetylation is activated by fasting, exercise, and [[Caloric Restriction]] (when not yet a note) and inhibited by high NAD+/NADH (low NAD+).
- **Autophagy & quality control**: Deacetylation of autophagic machinery and [[Beclin1]]/[[LC3]]-associated regulators lowers the threshold for autophagic flux.

> [!warning] NAD+ stoichiometry
> Every deacetylation event hydrolyzes one [[NAD+]] to nicotinamide + O-acetyl-ADP-ribose. Sustained sirtuin activation therefore depends on [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]] capacity and niacin availability.

## Physiological & Clinical Relevance

- **Aging**: Declining NAD+ limits deacetylation, contributing to [[Aging]] and [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]]; restoring NAD+ or using [[Sirtuin Activators]] is a major longevity strategy.
- **Therapeutics**: Sirtuin-activating and NAD+-boosting interventions aim to recalibrate the acetylome for metabolic and neuroprotection.
