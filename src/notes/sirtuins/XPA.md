# XPA

## Overview

XPA (xeroderma pigmentosum group A protein) is a central factor in the [[Nucleotide Excision Repair]] (NER) pathway. It recognizes and verifies bulky DNA lesions — such as UV-induced photoproducts — and nucleates assembly of the repair complex.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates XPA, enhancing DNA-damage recognition. Acetylation of XPA modulates its DNA-binding affinity; SIRT1-dependent deacetylation restores efficient lesion recognition, improving NER capacity. This positions XPA as a SIRT1-controlled checkpoint in the repair of transcription-blocking damage.

> [!info] Damage recognition
> Deacetylation by SIRT1 sharpens XPA's lesion-binding, coupling NAD+-dependent sirtuin activity to nucleotide excision repair and genome integrity.

## Physiological Function

XPA is required for both global-genome and transcription-coupled NER. Its SIRT1-dependent regulation links metabolic state to DNA repair capacity, supporting genome stability during oxidative and UV stress.

## Clinical Relevance

XPA mutations cause xeroderma pigmentosum, characterized by extreme UV sensitivity, skin cancer predisposition, and neurodegeneration. Enhancing SIRT1 activity (e.g., via NAD+ precursors) may bolster NER capacity and counteract age-related DNA damage accumulation.
