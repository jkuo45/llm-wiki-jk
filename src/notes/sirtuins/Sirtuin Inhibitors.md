# Sirtuin Inhibitors

[[Sirtuin Inhibitors]] are small-molecule agents that block the catalytic activity of sirtuin deacetylases. Because sirtuins can act as oncogenes or tumor suppressors depending on context, sirtuin inhibition is a context-dependent therapeutic strategy, most clearly for the pro-oncogenic [[SIRT1]] and the cancer-promoting roles of [[SIRT2]] and [[SIRT7]].

## Chemical Classes

- **Nicotinamide/NAD⁺ mimics**: Endogenous inhibitor [[Nicotinamide|nicotinamide]], carbamido-NAD⁺.
- **Substrate peptide-derived**: Thioacetyllysine-bearing fragments.
- **β-Naphthol derivatives**: [[Sirtinol]], [[Salermide]], [[Cambinol]], [[Splitomicin]].
- **Indoles**: [[EX-527]] (potent, selective SIRT1 inhibitor).
- **Tenovins**: [[Tenovins]] (tenovin-1, [[Tenovin-6]]) engaging SIRT1/SIRT2/p53.
- **Suramin analogs**: [[Suramin]], [[NF675]].
- **Kinase-inhibitor scaffolds**: Bisindolylmaleimides ([[Ro31-8220]]) and indolinones ([[GW5074]]).
- **Other synthetic**: [[AGK2]], [[JGB1741]], [[AC93253]], 1,4-dihydropyridines, thiobarbiturates.
- **Natural products**: [[Aristoforin]], [[Amurensin G]], [[Tanikolide]].

## Therapeutic Rationale

SIRT1 inhibition reactivates [[p53]] and proapoptotic [[FOXO]] signaling and cooperates with [[HDAC inhibitors]] and chemotherapeutics to induce apoptosis in leukemia and other cancer cells. SIRT2 inhibition (e.g., [[AC93253]]) suppresses [[Akt]] signaling and causes mitotic defects, but may counteract microtubule-inhibitor therapy.

## Assay Methods

Inhibitors are identified through [[Sirtuin Fluorimetric Activity|Fluor de Lys fluorescence assays]], homogeneous fluorescent deacetylase assays, charcoal binding assays, radiolabeled nicotinamide-release assays, and cell-based Western blot/ELISA readouts of substrate acetylation ([[p53]], [[Histone H3|histone 3]], α-[[Tubulin|tubulin]]).
