# FOXO3

## Overview

FOXO3 (also FOXO3a) is a member of the [[FOXO Transcription Factors|FOXO family]] of forkhead transcription factors that regulate stress resistance, metabolism, cell-cycle arrest, and [[Autophagy]]. It is one of the most robust genetic correlates of human longevity.

## Mechanism

FOXO3 and [[SIRT1]] form a feed-forward stress-resistance loop. [[Acetylation]] of FOXO3 by acetyltransferases promotes expression of pro-apoptotic targets; deacetylation by SIRT1, which is [[NAD+]]-dependent, shunts FOXO3 toward stress-resistance and metabolic gene programs instead. In parallel, FOXO3 transactivates the SIRT1 gene, reinforcing the loop. This mutually reinforcing pair is central to longevity-associated signaling (Brunet et al., 2004, PMID 15066266).

> [!important] Feed-forward loop
> SIRT1 deacetylates FOXO3 → stress-resistance transcription; FOXO3 upregulates SIRT1 → sustained cytoprotection. This positive feedback couples NAD+ status to a durable stress-resistance phenotype.

## Physiological Function

Via SIRT1-dependent deacetylation, FOXO3 programs antioxidant defense (e.g., [[MnSOD]]), metabolic adaptation, and cellular maintenance — outputs consistent with longevity. FOXO3 activity is opposed by insulin/[[Akt]] signaling, which phosphorylates FOXO3 and sequesters it in the cytoplasm, linking nutrient sensing to stress-response gene expression.

## Clinical Relevance

Polymorphisms in FOXO3 are repeatedly associated with human longevity. Modulation of the SIRT1–FOXO3 axis (e.g., by [[Sirtuin Activators]] or NAD+ precursors such as [[NMN]]) is proposed to bolster stress resistance in aging.
