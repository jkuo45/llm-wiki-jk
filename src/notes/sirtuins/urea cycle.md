# urea cycle

## Overview

The urea cycle is a [[Liver]] mitochondrial and cytosolic pathway that converts toxic ammonia (from amino-acid catabolism) into urea for excretion. It consists of five enzymes: carbamoyl phosphate synthetase 1 ([[CPS1]]), ornithine transcarbamylase, argininosuccinate synthetase, argininosuccinate lyase, and arginase.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT5]], a mitochondrial desuccinylase/demalonylase, participates in urea cycle regulation. As a desuccinylase/demalonylase it regulates urea cycle enzymes (notably CPS1), ketogenesis, and redox enzymes (Du 2011 PMID 22076378; Nakagawa 2009 PMID 19410549). SIRT5-mediated desuccinylation of CPS1 activates the enzyme, coupling ammonia detoxification to mitochondrial NAD+ and acylation status.

> [!info] Acylation control
> SIRT5 removes succinyl and malonyl groups from urea cycle enzymes such as CPS1, tuning ammonia handling and connecting protein acylation to nitrogen metabolism.

## Physiological Function

The urea cycle is essential for nitrogen balance during fasting and high-protein feeding. SIRT5-dependent regulation adapts urea production to metabolic demand, while also influencing ketogenesis and mitochondrial redox homeostasis.

## Clinical Relevance

Urea cycle defects cause hyperammonemia with severe neurological consequences. Understanding SIRT5-mediated acylation control of the cycle informs metabolic interventions and the biology of liver dysfunction in aging and disease.
