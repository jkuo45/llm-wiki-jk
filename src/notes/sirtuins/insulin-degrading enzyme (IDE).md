# insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE)

## Overview

Insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE; insulysin) is a zinc metalloprotease best known for cleaving [[Insulin]] and other small peptides, thereby regulating insulin clearance. Beyond hormone degradation, IDE degrades the tumor suppressor [[PTEN]], placing it in the control of [[PI3K]]/[[Akt]] signaling.

## Mechanism

As an outcome of cellular stresses, [[SIRT4]] controls PTEN stability via IDE. Directionally, SIRT4 sits upstream of PTEN protein abundance: SIRT4 activity influences IDE-mediated degradation of PTEN, so SIRT4-dependent control of PTEN stability links mitochondrial sirtuin activity to oncogenic signaling under stress conditions.

> [!warning] Dual role
> IDE degrades both insulin (beneficial in limiting hyperinsulinemia) and PTEN (potentially harmful, as PTEN is a tumor suppressor). SIRT4's control of this balance shows how a mitochondrial enzyme can tune a cytoplasmic protease to influence both metabolism and cancer risk.

## Physiological Function

Through PTEN, the SIRT4–IDE axis modulates the PI3K/Akt pathway, a central regulator of cell growth, survival, and metabolism. Because PTEN opposes PI3K signaling, reducing PTEN abundance via IDE derepresses Akt activity, which may promote proliferation under stress.

## Clinical Relevance

PTEN is among the most frequently lost or inactivated tumor suppressors in cancer. Understanding SIRT4–IDE–PTEN regulation suggests that mitochondrial sirtuin status could influence tumor suppressor function and therapeutic responses.
