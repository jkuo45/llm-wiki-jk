# Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase 1B (PTP1B/PTPN1)

**Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase 1B (PTP1B/PTPN1)** is a 50 kDa non-receptor tyrosine phosphatase that dephosphorylates and inactivates the [[Insulin Receptor]] (IR) and [[Insulin Receptor Substrate]] (IRS) proteins, serving as a major negative regulator of [[Insulin Signaling]]. As one of the first identified classical tyrosine phosphatases, PTP1B is a validated metabolic drug target whose activity is held in check by the [[SIRT1]] arm of the [[NAD+]] longevity network.

## Structure and Catalytic Mechanism

PTP1B is an endoplasmic-reticulum–associated phosphatase anchored to the cytosolic face of the ER via its C-terminal tail. Its catalytic domain contains the signature HCX5R motif, in which the catalytic cysteine performs nucleophilic attack on the phosphotyrosine substrate, forming a cysteinyl-phosphate intermediate that is subsequently hydrolyzed. By removing the activating phosphates from the insulin receptor and IRS proteins, PTP1B terminates the insulin signal. Its proximity to the ER — where internalized IR localizes after endocytosis — positions PTP1B to efficiently dephosphorylate the receptor en route to degradation.

## SIRT1 Regulation

[[SIRT1]] downregulates PTP1B expression and activity, thereby increasing [[Insulin Sensitivity]] and promoting glucose homeostasis. SIRT1-mediated repression of PTP1B occurs through transcriptional mechanisms involving [[FOXO]] factors and [[PGC-1α]], linking [[NAD+]]-dependent deacetylase activity to insulin signaling enhancement. By deacetylating FOXO and PGC-1α, SIRT1 reconfigures the transcriptional program away from PTP1B and toward oxidative metabolism, effectively sensitizing cells to insulin. This provides a molecular explanation for why [[Resveratrol]] and [[Caloric Restriction]] — both SIRT1-activating interventions — improve glucose tolerance.

## Physiological and Pathological Role

PTP1B is a validated therapeutic target for [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]] and [[Obesity]]. Knockout of PTP1B in mice enhances insulin sensitivity, improves glucose tolerance, and confers resistance to diet-induced obesity. PTP1B also regulates [[Leptin Signaling]] by dephosphorylating JAK2, making it a dual regulator of both insulin and leptin action. Its overexpression or hyperactivity contributes to systemic insulin resistance, while its inhibition is consistently associated with improved metabolic healthspan — linking PTP1B to the broader [[Longevity]] and metabolic-aging literature.

## Clinical Relevance

Small-molecule PTP1B inhibitors have been pursued for type 2 diabetes and obesity, though achieving selectivity and oral bioavailability has been challenging. Indirect PTP1B suppression via SIRT1 activation ([[Resveratrol]], [[NAD+]] precursors) offers an alternative route. Given PTP1B's role in both insulin and leptin resistance, its modulation sits at the crossroads of metabolic and [[Aging]] therapeutics.
