## Overview

Tenovin-6 is a small-molecule inhibitor of the [[Sirtuin]] family of NAD+-dependent deacetylases. It was discovered in a screen for compounds that activate p53 by preventing its deacetylation, and it exerts this effect by directly blocking sirtuin catalytic activity. Unlike the many agents that activate sirtuins, Tenovin-6 is valued as a chemical probe precisely because it inhibits them.

## Mechanism / Structure

- Tenovin-6 blocks NAD+-dependent deacetylation in the sirtuin catalytic pocket, thereby increasing acetylation of sirtuin substrates.
- It inhibits [[SIRT2]] most potently (IC50 ≈ 10 µM), making SIRT2 its principal target. By suppressing SIRT2-mediated deacetylation, Tenovin-6 can reactivate [[p53]] and protect neurons in models of neurodegeneration where SIRT2 overactivity is deleterious.
- It also inhibits [[SIRT3]] with moderate potency (IC50 ≈ 67 µM), weaker than its SIRT2 effect. This broader sirtuin inhibition contributes to its anticancer phenotype, but selectivity across SIRT1/2/3 is limited, so isoform-specific interpretations require caution.

> [!warning] Limited selectivity
> Tenovin-6's overlapping inhibition of SIRT1, SIRT2, and SIRT3 means that biological outcomes cannot be assigned to a single isoform without complementary genetic validation. It is best used as a broad-spectrum sirtuin probe rather than a selective SIRT2 tool.

## Role in Cancer & Neurobiology

- By inhibiting SIRT2 and SIRT3, Tenovin-6 disrupts deacetylation-driven survival and metabolic programs in cancer cells, contributing to growth suppression and apoptosis — the basis of its anticancer phenotype.
- In neuroscience, inhibition of SIRT2 (whose overactivation is linked to [[Neurotoxicity]] in neurodegenerative contexts) is pursued for Parkinson's and Huntington's disease, where excessive deacetylation impairs microtubule and protein-homeostasis defenses.

> [!info] p53 reactivation
> Tenovin-6's original mechanistic claim is rescue of p53 acetylation. Because several sirtuins (notably SIRT1) deacetylate and dampen p53, broad sirtuin blockade by Tenovin-6 can disinhibit p53 transcriptional activity, a property exploited in oncology research.

## Clinical & Research Significance

Tenovin-6 remains primarily a research chemical probe rather than a clinical candidate, owing to modest potency, off-target liability, and poor isoform selectivity. Its derivatives (the [[Tenovins]] family) have been optimized for improved activity. It is nevertheless a foundational tool for dissecting sirtuin biology and for validating sirtuin-inhibition hypotheses in cancer and neurodegeneration.
