# p73

**p73** (Tumour Protein p73, encoded by *TP73*) is a structural and functional homologue of the tumor suppressor [[p53]]. Like p53, p73 is a transcription factor that can induce cell cycle arrest or trigger apoptotic cell death in response to [[DNA Damage]] and other cellular stress signals. A member of the p53 family (along with p53 and p63), p73 contains a transactivation domain, a DNA-binding domain, an oligomerization domain, and a C-terminal sterile alpha motif (SAM). It is a key mediator of developmental regulation and stress-induced apoptosis, and it is directly regulated by the sirtuin [[SIRT1]] through deacetylation.

## Isoform Diversity

The *TP73* locus gives rise to two classes of isoforms via alternative promoter usage and splicing. The full-length TAp73 isoforms (TAp73α, β, γ) contain the N-terminal transactivation domain and can activate p53-like target genes, whereas the ΔNp73 isoforms lack this domain and act as dominant-negative inhibitors of both TAp73 and p53. This isoform balance determines whether p73 conveys tumor-suppressive or oncogenic signals, and is a critical consideration when interpreting p73 function in disease.

## Regulation by SIRT1

p73 is directly targeted and regulated post-translationally by [[SIRT1]]:

- **Physical Interaction**: p73 physically associates with the nuclear sirtuin [[SIRT1]].
- **Deacetylation**: [[SIRT1]] deacetylates p73, primarily at C-terminal lysines. Acetylation of p73 is required for its optimal DNA binding and transactivation; deacetylation reverses this.
- **Inhibition of Transcriptional Activity**: SIRT1-mediated deacetylation of p73 suppresses its transcriptional transactivation activity. This acts to prevent p73-mediated pro-apoptotic target gene expression, thereby inhibiting apoptotic cell death in response to cellular stress.

The p73–SIRT1 relationship mirrors the better-studied p53–SIRT1 axis, positioning SIRT1 as a general negative regulator of the p53 family under conditions where survival is favored (e.g., nutrient stress, [[Caloric Restriction]]). By dampening p73, SIRT1 promotes cellular tolerance to stress at the cost of suppressed apoptotic clearance of damaged cells.

## Physiological and Pathological Relevance

p73 is essential for neuronal development, cilia formation, and tumor suppression. Germline *TP73* disruption in mice causes hippocampal dysgenesis and chronic infections, underscoring its non-oncogenic roles. In cancer, the p73 status — particularly the TAp73/ΔNp73 ratio — influences chemosensitivity. SIRT1-mediated suppression of TAp73 may protect normal cells during stress but could also shield tumor cells from therapy-induced apoptosis, analogous to the pro-survival versus tumor-promoting duality of [[SIRT1]] activity in [[Aging]] and [[Cancer]].
