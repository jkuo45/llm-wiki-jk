# GDH (Glutamate Dehydrogenase)

## Overview

Glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH; GLUD1) is a mitochondrial enzyme that catalyzes the reversible oxidative deamination of [[Glutamate]] to [[α-Ketoglutarate]], providing a key entry of amino-acid carbon into the [[TCA cycle]]. It is a central node in amino-acid catabolism and anaplerosis.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT4]] mono-ADP-ribosylates GDH, inhibiting its oxidative deamination of glutamate to α-ketoglutarate. This slows anaplerotic carbon entry into the [[TCA cycle]], restraining amino-acid-derived ATP production. The inhibition is nutrient-responsive: under nutrient-replete conditions, SIRT4 activity is high, acting as a mitochondrial brake on amino-acid catabolism.

> [!info] Mitochondrial brake
> SIRT4 mono-ADP-ribosylation of GDH suppresses glutamate→α-ketoglutarate flux, limiting anaplerosis, mitochondrial ATP generation, and amino-acid-stimulated insulin secretion.

## Physiological Function

Because GDH-derived α-ketoglutarate fuels mitochondrial oxidative metabolism, SIRT4-dependent GDH inhibition restrains glucose- and amino-acid-stimulated [[Insulin]] secretion in pancreatic β-cells. This positions SIRT4 as a regulator of nutrient-driven hormonal output, opposing the oxidative, catabolic programs that [[SIRT3]] promotes.

## Clinical Relevance

Loss-of-function mutations in GLUD1 cause hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia (hyperinsulinism-hyperammonemia syndrome), underscoring GDH's role in insulin secretion. The antagonistic SIRT3/SIRT4 balance — see [[SIRT3-SIRT4 Ratio]] — is emerging as a determinant of mitochondrial health and metabolic resilience.
