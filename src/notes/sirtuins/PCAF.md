# PCAF

## Overview

PCAF (p300/CBP-associated factor; KAT2B) is a [[Histone Acetyltransferase]] (HAT) that acetylates histones and transcription factors, generally activating gene expression. It partners with the coactivators [[P300]] and CBP in regulating muscle-gene transcription during myogenesis.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT1]] physically interacts with and deacetylates PCAF within regulatory complexes at muscle-gene promoters. By deacetylating PCAF, SIRT1 reduces its HAT activity and its contribution to muscle-gene activation. Directionally, SIRT1 enzymatically inhibits PCAF, counterbalancing [[Acetylation]] during myogenic regulation.

> [!info] Tuning acetyltransferases
> SIRT1's regulation of PCAF shows it tunes acetyltransferase partners, not only deacetylating substrates directly. This bidirectional control shapes the acetylation-deacetylation balance that determines transcriptional output.

## Physiological Function

During myogenesis, PCAF acetylation promotes expression of muscle-specific genes (e.g., through coactivation with [[MyoD]]). SIRT1-dependent PCAF deacetylation restrains this program, ensuring a balanced myogenic response. This regulation is part of SIRT1's broader control of the acetylation balance that shapes transcriptional programs in metabolism, stress, and differentiation.

## Clinical Relevance

Because both PCAF and SIRT1 are implicated in metabolic and inflammatory gene programs, their antagonism may influence muscle maintenance during aging, cachexia, and metabolic disease. Pharmacological modulation of HAT activity is being explored alongside [[Sirtuin Activators]]/[[Sirtuin Inhibitors]].
