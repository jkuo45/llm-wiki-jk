# H3K56ac

**H3K56ac** denotes the acetylation of lysine 56 on **[[Histone H3]]**. Unlike the N-terminal tail marks (e.g., [[H3K9ac]]), K56 sits in the globular **αN** domain near the DNA entry/exit point of the nucleosome, where acetylation destabilizes nucleosome–DNA contacts and loosens chromatin. H3K56ac decorates newly synthesized histone H3, coupling its deposition to nucleosome assembly during [[DNA replication]] (S phase) and DNA repair. Its timely removal by NAD⁺-dependent sirtuin deacetylases is a central determinant of [[Genomic Instability|genomic stability]], stress resistance, and lifespan regulation.

## Structural Location & Functional Role

- **Globular-domain placement**: K56 lies within the α-helix region at the core–DNA interface; acetylation neutralizes the lysine charge and weakens histone–DNA interactions, promoting histone deposition and unwinding of nucleosomal DNA.
- **Replication-coupled deposition**: In yeast and mammals, H3K56ac is loaded onto newly synthesized H3 during S phase by specialized chaperones (yeast [[Asf1]]), marking chromatin assembly behind the replication fork.
- **Dual transcriptional character**: H3K56ac is canonically associated with newly deposited, assembly-competent histones and genes induced after DNA damage; at some loci its accumulation tracks with transcription-permissive states, but its signature function is assembly and repair rather than steady-state activation.

## Writing and Erasing the Mark

- **Yeast**: The H3-specific acetyltransferase [[Rtt109]] (yeast) writes H3K56ac together with [[Asf1]] and Vps75 (Han et al., *Science* 2007, PMID 17272723); the redundant deacetylases [[Hst3]] and [[Hst4]] and the founding sirtuin [[Sir2 (yeast)]] erase it.
- **Mammals**: The [[CBP]]/p300 acetyltransferases write H3K56ac (Das et al., *Nature* 2009, PMID 19270680); removal is carried out by the sirtuins [[SIRT1]], [[SIRT2]], [[SIRT3]], and [[SIRT6]].

## Regulation by Sirtuins

- **[[SIRT6]]** — the principal H3K56ac deacetylase. SIRT6 removes H3K56ac from chromatin at double-strand breaks and target promoters, promoting compaction, DSB-repair factor recruitment, and gene silencing (Michishita et al., *Nature* 2008, PMID 18354480; Yang et al., *Cell Cycle* 2009, PMID 19667707). Loss of H3K56ac deacetylation in SIRT6-deficient cells is a driver of genomic instability.
- **[[SIRT1]]** — deacetylates H3K56 to promote compacted, silenced chromatin; SIRT1 redistribution and H3K56ac changes link the mark to aging (Oberdoerffer et al., *Cell* 2008, PMID 19041753). In a double-blind RCT, resveratrol increased [[SIRT1]] expression and significantly reduced H3K56ac content (Bo et al., *Acta Diabetol* 2018, PMID 29492703).
- **[[SIRT2]]** — deacetylates H3K56ac in human cells, contributing to mitotic chromatin organization and genome stability.
- **[[SIRT3]]** — deacetylates H3K56ac, a mark of newly synthesized histones and histone turnover.

> [!info] SIRT6 activators act through H3K56ac
> Pharmacological SIRT6 activation with [[UBCS039]], [[MDL-800]], [[MDL-801]], or [[Cyanidin]] rapidly lowers both [[H3K9ac]] and H3K56ac, compacting chromatin and facilitating DNA double-strand break repair (source: [[_document_ - sirtuins (resveratrol), gemini|Sirtuins and Resveratrol]]; [[_document_ - sirtuins in health and disease s41392-022-01257-8|Sirtuins in Health and Disease]]).

## Role in DNA Replication, Repair & Genome Stability

- **Replication fidelity**: Yeast cells lacking the writers (Rtt109/Asf1) or lacking deacetylase capacity (*hst3 hst4* double mutants) show severe sensitivity to genotoxins and replication blocks, reflecting the mark's role in coupling nucleosome assembly to fork progression.
- **DSB repair**: At damage sites, SIRT6 deacetylates H3K56ac, enabling chromatin compaction and recruitment of repair factors such as [[53BP1]], [[CtIP]], and [[γ-H2AX]]-associated machinery. H3K56ac dysregulation is associated with spontaneous DNA damage and genomic instability (Yuan et al., *Cell Cycle* 2009, PMID 19411845).
- **Acetylation balance is critical**: Both persistent hyperacetylation (loss of deacetylases) and acetylation-resistant mutations compromise genome integrity, indicating that precise, spatially regulated levels—not simply the absolute amount—are required for stability.

## Aging & Longevity

- **Yeast longevity**: H3K56ac is a classic aging marker in yeast. Either permanent acetylation (*hst3 hst4* deletion, Hst3 overexpression) or an acetylation-resistant K56 mutant shortens replicative lifespan; one extra copy of a correct H3K56 deacetylase extends lifespan. This argues that survival requires precisely tuned levels of the mark, not simply low or high (Tsuchiya et al., 2006; Dang et al., *Nature* 2009, PMID 19516333; Feser et al., *Mol Cell* 2010, PMID 20832751).
- **Mammalian aging**: H3K56ac is redistributed during aging in human cells, tracking with sites of genomic instability and heterochromatin erosion (O'Sullivan et al., 2010). SIRT-activating compounds and [[Resveratrol]] suppress age-related H3K9/H3K56 hyperacetylation and promote heterochromatin formation (Oberdoerffer et al., 2008; [[_document_ - Epigenetic changes during aging and their reprogramming potential|Epigenetic changes during aging]]).

## Clinical & Translational Relevance

- **Biomarker of sirtuin activation**: Peripheral H3K56ac content decreased significantly with resveratrol-induced [[SIRT1]] elevation in type 2 diabetes patients, positioning H3K56ac as a candidate pharmacodynamic readout for SIRT1-activating interventions (Bo et al., 2018, PMID 29492703).
- **Precision targeting**: Because both excess and absence of H3K56ac are toxic, therapeutic strategies aim to restore normal sirtuin-mediated turnover of the mark (especially via SIRT6) rather than simply abolish or elevate it.

## Connections

- [[SIRT6]] — Principal deacetylase of H3K56ac; removal at DSBs and promoters enables repair, compaction, and genomic stability.
- [[SIRT1]], [[SIRT2]], [[SIRT3]] — Additional mammalian deacetylases of H3K56ac (chromatin silencing, mitotic organization, histone turnover).
- [[Histone H3]] — Parent histone carrying the K56 mark.
- [[H3K9ac]] — The two principal SIRT6 histone targets; both compacted upon SIRT6 activation.
- [[CBP]] — CBP/p300 acetyltransferases write H3K56ac in mammals.
- [[Sir2 (yeast)]] — Founding sirtuin and yeast H3K56 deacetylase; Hst3/Hst4 redundant enzymes.
- [[53BP1]], [[CtIP]], [[γ-H2AX]] — DSB-repair axis gated by SIRT6-mediated H3K56ac removal.
- [[UBCS039]], [[MDL-800]], [[MDL-801]], [[Cyanidin]] — SIRT6 activators that lower H3K56ac and promote DNA repair.
- [[Resveratrol]] — Lowers H3K56ac via SIRT1 in a clinical RCT.
- [[Genomic Instability]], [[Acetylation]] — Consequence of dysregulated H3K56ac and the PTM class to which it belongs.
