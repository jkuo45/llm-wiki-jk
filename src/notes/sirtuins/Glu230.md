## Overview

Glutamate 230 (E230, denoted Glu230) is a conserved negatively charged residue located in the N-terminal domain (NTD) of [[SIRT1]], the mammalian NAD+-dependent protein deacetylase. It is a critical determinant of the allosteric binding site engaged by small-molecule activators such as [[Resveratrol]].

## Structure & Role

- **Resveratrol binding site:** Biochemical and structural studies indicate that resveratrol binding to the SIRT1 NTD specifically requires the E230 residue; mutagenesis of Glu230 abolishes the activating effect of resveratrol on SIRT1 catalytic activity.
- **Allosteric modulation:** Rather than occupying the catalytic pocket, resveratrol engages this distal site to enhance substrate affinity, illustrating a non-classical activation mechanism for sirtuins.

## Physiological & Pathological Relevance

Because [[SIRT1]] is a central longevity and metabolic regulator, the Glu230-dependent activation mechanism is directly relevant to sirtuin-activating strategies (STACs) explored for healthy aging, metabolic disease, and cardiovascular protection. Residues such as E230 are key to designing selective sirtuin modulators.
