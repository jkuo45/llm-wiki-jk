# Caspase-mediated changes in Sir2α during apoptosis

**Caspase-mediated changes in Sir2α during apoptosis** (Ohsawa & Miura, *FEBS Letters* 2006;580(25):5875–5879, doi:10.1016/j.febslet.2006.09.051, PMID 17027980). FEBS Letters is open-archive; this note is compiled from the retrieved full text (abstract, results, figure legends, and discussion).

> [!important] Central claim
> Mammalian Sir2α ([[SIRT1]]) — a stress-protective, anti-apoptotic deacetylase — is itself **directly cleaved by both initiator and executioner caspases** and **relocalizes from the nucleus to the cytoplasm** during apoptosis. Cleavage and dislocation therefore occur **throughout** the apoptotic process, not at a single step: the death program actively dismantles the survival factor that restrains it ([[Sirtuin-Caspase Crosstalk]]).

## Background

- Sir2α protects cells from stress-induced apoptosis: it deacetylates [[FOXO3]]/[[FOXO4]], attenuating FOXO-induced apoptosis and potentiating FOXO-induced cell-cycle arrest; increased Sir2α activity also reduces [[p53]]-mediated apoptosis and slows cellular senescence.
- Because FOXO3 and p53 interact under oxidative stress, the authors reasoned Sir2α might raise viability by shifting the FOXO/p53 response away from death.
- SIRT1/Sir2α-deficient cells are known to be hypersensitive to pro-apoptotic stimuli — ionizing radiation, staurosporine, and oxidative stress — making SIRT1 itself a plausible caspase target once death is committed.

## Results

### 1. Sir2α is cleaved in a caspase-dependent manner (Fig. 1)

In apoptotic cells Sir2α appears as a lower-molecular-weight band; the cleavage is caspase-dependent. The authors conclude that Sir2α is **directly regulated by multiple caspases throughout the apoptotic process** — i.e., not a single upstream or downstream event but continuous substrate processing.

### 2. Sir2α translocates from nucleus to cytoplasm (Fig. 2)

- In normally growing HeLa cells, transfected Sir2α is **nuclear** (consistent with prior localization reports).
- Upon apoptotic stimulation (**TNF-α**), Sir2α appears in the **cytoplasm of dying cells**.
- Both **dominant-negative Caspase-9** and **[[Bcl-xL]]** kept Sir2α in the nucleus — placing the translocation **downstream of apoptosome activation and MOMP** and establishing it as caspase-dependent.

### 3. In vivo correlate — pyknotic nuclei (Fig. 3)

In the ventricular zone of **Ara-C-treated embryonic mouse brain** (Hoechst 33342 counterstain), cells with cytoplasmic Sir2α were overwhelmingly dying: **of 150 cytoplasmic-Sir2α cells, over 130 had pyknotic nuclei**. Cytoplasmic Sir2α is thus a marker of terminal apoptosis *in vivo*, not a transfection artifact.

## Discussion (key points)

- One of the histone deacetylases, Sir2α, is cleaved by caspases and released from nucleus to cytoplasm during caspase-dependent apoptosis.
- Because Sir2α is cleaved by **multiple caspases — including [[Caspase-9]] (initiator) and [[Caspase-3]] (executioner)** — its cleavage and dislocation must occur **throughout** apoptosis, unlike single-step substrates.
- Interpretation: during stress the cell first uses Sir2α to repair and defend; if that fails, the caspase cascade removes it. Cleavage of an anti-apoptotic nuclear protein is functionally consistent with apoptosis' ordered self-dismantling.
- The cytoplasmic fate of cleaved SIRT1 became mechanistically important later: cytoplasmic SIRT1 is **pro-apoptotic** (caspase-dependent, deacetylase-independent), so the cleavage is not merely loss-of-function but a fate flip; and under severe DNA damage the cleavage site was mapped to **DEPDVP(704–709)** with coupled [[KAP1|TRIM28]]-mediated degradation (see [[_document_ - Post-translational Modification in Control of SIRT1 Stability during DNA Damage Response]]).

## Methods (as reported)

HeLa transfection with Sir2α expression vector; TNF-α apoptotic stimulation; dominant-negative Caspase-9 and Bcl-xL co-expression; immunofluorescence (anti-Sir2α + Hoechst 33342); Ara-C treatment of embryonic mouse brain with pyknosis scoring (intact / weakly / strongly fragmented nuclei among 150 cytoplasmic-Sir2α cells).

## Significance

This paper established the **caspase → sirtuin** direction of the sirtuin–caspase relationship: caspases do not merely escape SIRT1's anti-apoptotic control — they destroy it, in parallel with their cleavage of [[PARP1]], DNA-PKcs, and ATM, ensuring the apoptotic commitment is irreversible.

## Connections

- [[SIRT1]]: the caspase substrate — cleaved and relocalized nucleus→cytoplasm during apoptosis.
- [[Caspase-9]]: initiator caspase cleaving Sir2α; dominant-negative caspase-9 blocks cleavage and keeps Sir2α nuclear.
- [[Caspase-3]]: executioner caspase cleaving Sir2α throughout the apoptotic process.
- [[Bcl-xL]]: blocks the cleavage/relocalization upstream at the mitochondrial gate.
- [[FOXO3]] / [[FOXO4]] / [[p53]]: the anti-apoptotic programs Sir2α normally restrains.
- [[Apoptosis]]: the process in which SIRT1 dismantling occurs.
- [[KAP1|TRIM28]]: later shown to degrade cleaved SIRT1 in the DNA-damage response.
