# spliced X-box binding protein 1

## Overview

Spliced X-box binding protein 1 (XBP1s) is the active, transcriptionally competent isoform of [[XBP1]], generated by IRE1-mediated splicing during the [[Unfolded Protein Response]]. XBP1s directs [[Endoplasmic Reticulum]] (ER) stress adaptation by upregulating ER chaperones and folding machinery.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT1]] deacetylates XBP1s downstream of an AMPKα-dependent pathway in macrophages. Deacetylated XBP1s then inhibits [[NLRP3]] inflammasome assembly. Directionally, SIRT1 modifies XBP1s, which in turn suppresses NLRP3, coupling ER-stress responses to sirtuin control of inflammation.

> [!important] Stress-pathway integration
> AMPKα → SIRT1 → XBP1s deacetylation → NLRP3 inhibition: this axis integrates ER stress and innate immune signaling at the intersection of metabolism and immunity.

## Physiological Function

By restraining NLRP3-driven IL-1β production, the SIRT1–XBP1s axis limits sterile inflammation. This is a key mechanism in atherosclerotic and metabolic disease, where chronic ER stress and inflammasome activation drive pathology, illustrating how SIRT1 coordinates multiple inputs to restrain inflammatory signaling.

## Clinical Relevance

Chronic ER stress and inflammasome activation contribute to atherosclerosis, metabolic syndrome, and neurodegeneration. Enhancing SIRT1 activity (e.g., via NAD+ precursors or [[Sirtuin Activators]]) is proposed to reinforce XBP1s-mediated suppression of inflammation.
