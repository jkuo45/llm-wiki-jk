# Phosphoglycerate Mutase

## Overview

Phosphoglycerate mutase (PGAM1) is a glycolytic enzyme that converts 3-phosphoglycerate to 2-phosphoglycerate, a central step of [[Glycolysis]]. Beyond its catalytic role, PGAM1 regulates the pentose-phosphate pathway by controlling 3-phosphoglycerate levels, a key inhibitor of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT2]] deacetylates phosphoglycerate mutase at Lys100, activating it. Enhanced PGAM1 activity channels glycolytic carbon toward NADPH production and promotes tumor growth, particularly in lung cancer. Deacetylation of Lys100 relieves inhibitory acetylation, increasing the enzyme's catalytic rate.

> [!warning] Two-faced sirtuin
> SIRT2's deacetylation of PGAM1 accelerates glycolysis and NADPH generation, fueling anabolism in cancer. This illustrates SIRT2's context-dependent, often harmful metabolic face versus the protective [[SIRT1]]/[[SIRT3]]/[[SIRT6]] axis.

## Physiological Function

In normal cells, PGAM1 supports glycolytic flux and coordination with the [[Pentose Phosphate Pathway]]. SIRT2-dependent activation amplifies this coupling, favoring NADPH and ribose production that support proliferation.

## Clinical Relevance

PGAM1 is overexpressed in many cancers and is a candidate therapeutic target. The SIRT2–PGAM1 interaction suggests SIRT2 inhibition may limit tumor glycolysis, though systemic SIRT2 inhibition must be weighed against its other, sometimes protective, roles.
