# SIRT1: Protein Roles at the Nexus of Health, Disease, and Therapeutics

**Brahim Chaqour** (Journal of Cell Communication and Signaling, 2026). https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ccs3.70086

> [!abstract]
> Silent mating type information regulators ([[Sirtuins|sirtuins and SIRTs]]) have emerged as central nodes linking cellular metabolism to stress adaptation and diseases. [[SIRT1]] stands out as a ubiquitously expressed, [[NAD+|NAD⁺]]-dependent deacetylase with remarkably broad and sometimes paradoxical functions. Beyond its canonical roles in chromatin regulation and [[DNA Repair|DNA repair]], SIRT1 regulates key pathways controlling metabolism, inflammation, circadian rhythms, [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]], [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], immune response, and cellular survival. SIRT1 modulates, through [[Deacetylation|deacetylation]], a diverse network of substrates including [[Histone H3|histones H3/H4]], [[Tubulin|tubulin]], [[BMAL1]], [[p53]], [[NF-κB|NF-κB]], [[FOXO|FOXO]], [[PGC-1α]], and [[Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase|eNOS]], yet its functional outcomes are highly context dependent. Although SIRT1 activation is often protective in metabolic, cardiovascular, and neurovascular settings, its role in cancer remains dualistic, acting as both tumor suppressor and promoter. Similarly, its contribution to longevity remains unresolved. Pharmacological targeting of SIRT1, through compounds such as [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]] and synthetic modulators, has generated considerable enthusiasm, but challenges related to specificity, bioavailability, and off-target effects persist. SIRT1 is discussed here as a molecular "shapeshifter," whose context-dependent actions demand more precise biomarker-guided strategies for clinical translation.

## 1. Introduction

[[Sirtuins|Silent mating type information regulators (sirtuins) proteins (aka SIRTs)]] form a family of [[NAD+|NAD⁺]]-dependent protein lysine [[Deacetylation|deacetylases]] with or without mono-ADP-ribosylase activity. They are expressed across organisms and share an evolutionarily conserved catalytic region of 275 residues while differing in their N- and C-terminal domains, which define substrate specificity and subcellular localization. [[Sirtuins|SIRTs]] play crucial physiological roles in diverse tissues and organs including skin, muscle, skeleton, adipose, lymph, blood, nervous system, lungs, liver, kidneys, pancreas, heart, prostate, and ovaries. Each sirtuin homolog binds specific molecular targets, generating unique biological effects.

Several issues of the _Journal of Cell Communication and Signaling (JCCS)_ have featured studies highlighting the critical role of [[SIRT1]] in cardiovascular disease models. Yin et al. identified a mechanistic link between [[SIRT1]], the gut microbiota-derived metabolite [[Trimethylamine N-oxide|trimethylamine N-oxide/TMAO]], and the inflammatory response in vascular smooth muscle cells. Their findings suggest that SIRT1 may attenuate atherosclerotic plaque formation and slow disease progression by dampening inflammation and modulating SM22α expression. Begum et al.'s study suggested that the anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and wound healing properties of dehydrozingerone can be attributed, at least in part, to the upregulation of [[SIRT1]]. In a separate study, Liu et al. identified a novel diabetic retinopathy (DR)-associated circular RNA, cPWWP2A, that functions as a competing endogenous RNA sequestering miR-579; through this interaction, cPWWP2A promotes DR-associated retinal vascular dysfunction by upregulating [[SIRT1]]. These studies exemplify a growing body of research exploring the regulation, biological functions, and therapeutic potential of [[SIRT1]]-based interventions.

[[SIRT1]] is the most studied sirtuin mainly for its ubiquitous expression and its involvement in the regulation of a wide range of cellular responses. In mammalian cells, SIRT1 promotes [[Deacetylation|deacetylation]] of [[Histone H1|histone H1 at lysine 26]], [[Histone H4|histone H4 at lysine 16]], and [[Histone H3|H3 at lysine 9 and K79]]. The latter also promotes DNA hypomethylation. [[Deacetylation|Deacetylation]] of histone tails and spreading of hypomethylated H3-K79 favors the formation of tightly compacted chromatin and gene silencing, a hallmark of SIRT1 molecular activities. Because histone deacetylation is associated with decreased chromatin accessibility, the [[Deacetylation|histone deacetylase activity]] of SIRT1 was directly associated with the regulation of telomeric chromatin, gene expression, and the dynamic chromatin association of [[DNA Repair|DNA repair factors]].

[[Telomere|Telomere shortening]] is a deleterious process that occurs over the lifespan and has been linked to the concomitant decrease of [[SIRT1]] expression with age. Similarly, age-related DNA hypomethylation linked to reduced DNA methyltransferase activity have been correlated with reduced SIRT1 activity and decreased longevity. However, the interplay between SIRT1 expression and the rate of aging remains outstanding. The best studies highlighting a cause-and-effect relationship between SIRT1 expression and longevity stemmed from lower organisms, as genetic overexpression of SIRT1 was linked to increased lifespan of yeast, worms, and flies. In mice, earlier studies showed that overexpression of the [[SIRT1|Sirt1 gene]] led to beneficial phenotypes. Studies by several groups showed that mice engineered to overexpress SIRT1 or express additional copies of SIRT1 have improved organ function, increased disease resistance, and longevity.

> [!info] Longevity paradox
> While SIRT1 overexpression extends lifespan in lower organisms and improves health in mice, the longevity benefit in humans remains controversial. By contrast, SIRT1 *deficiency* is consistently linked to increased risk of age-related diseases and metabolic disorders.

Although the longevity benefits of SIRT1 overexpression remain controversial, SIRT1 deficiency has been consistently linked to an increased risk of age-related diseases and heightened susceptibility to metabolic disorders. [[Aging|Aging]] is accompanied by a progressive decline in SIRT1 activity, leading to reduced expression of core circadian clock components and disruption of central circadian regulation. This disruption is considered an early hallmark of premature aging and is implicated in the pathogenesis of metabolic and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]]. In the liver, SIRT1 deacetylates [[PGC-1α]] and amplifies the expression of the circadian transcription factors [[BMAL1]] and [[CLOCK]] in the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus. A key transcriptional target of the core circadian regulators BMAL1 and CLOCK is [[NAMPT]], the rate-limiting enzyme in the [[NAD+|NAD⁺ salvage pathway]] from nicotinamide. As a result, [[NAD+|NAD⁺]] is synthesized in a circadian oscillatory manner, establishing a rhythmic pattern of SIRT1 activation and deactivation in alignment with the circadian cycle.

The study by Yin et al. on the role of SIRT1 in influencing the inflammatory effects of the gut microbiome and ensuing atherosclerotic lesions is corroborated by other studies emphasizing SIRT1 effects on metabolism and metabolic syndrome. SIRT1 transgenic mice were protected from diabetes and diet- or genetically induced obesity. Similarly, transgenic mice with constitutive expression of SIRT1 are resistant to developing liver steatosis whereas their heterozygous counterparts are prone to developing this disease. Paradoxically, another study showed that SIRT1-null mice presented higher glucose tolerance and protection from fatty liver induced by LXR agonists. Polymorphisms of SIRT1 play a role in susceptibility to [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus|type 2 diabetes mellitus]] and vascular complications of diabetes. Zhang et al. demonstrated that endothelium-specific overexpression of SIRT1 decreases [[Atherosclerosis|atherosclerosis]] in apolipoprotein E-deficient mice. Other studies provided additional evidence that activation of SIRT1 can improve metabolic disturbances.

Mechanistically, the diverse biological roles of SIRT1 in the cardiovascular system are attributed to its interaction with multiple molecular targets. SIRT1 functions as a deacetylase, modulating over 40 known substrates involved in inflammation, stress resistance, [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]], fatty acid metabolism, insulin secretion, glucose production, and lipid homeostasis. Its targets include, among many others, [[PPARγ|PPAR-γ]] and its coactivator [[PGC-1α]], protein tyrosine phosphatases, [[FOXO|FOXO transcription factors]], [[AMPK|AMP-activated protein kinase]], CREB-regulated transcription coactivator 2, [[Endothelial Nitric Oxide Synthase|endothelial nitric oxide synthase]], [[p53]], myogenic differentiation factor, [[Liver X Receptor|liver X receptor]], and transcription factor [[E2F1]].

SIRT1 exerts antioxidative effects, notably through the [[Deacetylation|deacetylation]] of [[p53]], which upregulates [[MnSOD|manganese superoxide dismutase]] expression. In cardiomyocytes, SIRT1-mediated p53 deacetylation downregulates pro-apoptotic proteins [[BAX|Bax]] and [[Caspases|caspase-3]], thereby reducing [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]], an effect especially relevant in the border zone following myocardial infarction. Additionally, SIRT1 modulates [[FOXO|FOXO transcription factors]], shifting their activity from promoting apoptosis to enhancing resistance to [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress]] and promoting [[Autophagy|autophagy]], thus supporting cell survival. A positive feedback loop has been identified between SIRT1 and [[FoxO1|FOXO1]]: SIRT1 deacetylates FOXO1, enhancing its transcriptional activity, whereas FOXO1, in turn, activates SIRT1 expression by binding to a regulatory site in the SIRT1 promoter. These findings underscore the tissue- and organ-specific effects of SIRT1.

> [!info] SIRT1 as a "shapeshifter"
> SIRT1 promotes [[DNA Repair|DNA damage repair]], cell survival, metabolic control, [[Redox Homeostasis|oxidative stress]] and [[Inflammation|inflammation]] control—largely protective. Yet in oncology its effects are dualistic: the same deacetylase activity can suppress or promote tumorigenesis depending on cellular context.

Despite controversial findings about SIRT1 effects in different experimental models, the molecular effects of SIRT1 as a promoter of [[DNA Repair|DNA damage repair]], cell survival, and metabolism, and control of [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress]] and [[Inflammation|inflammation]] support a rather protective role of SIRT1 against metabolic diseases. A race to identify SIRT1 modulators ensued with the prospect to treat various diseases and maintain physiological balance. These efforts lead to the identification of several compound [[Polyphenols|polyphenols]] as Sirtuin-activating compounds ([[STACs|STACs]]) including [[Resveratrol|resveratrol (RSV)]], flavonoids, terpenes, ellagic acid, [[Quercetin|quercetin]], anthocyanins, and [[Curcumin|curcumin]]. RSV (3,5,4′-trihydroxystilbene), commonly found in fruits, vegetables, and nuts, stood out as particularly representative of the STACs. RSV is highly lipophilic and poorly water soluble. It has high intestinal absorption and undergoes rapid metabolization by the gut microbiota. The plasma concentration of RSV metabolites increases up to 20-fold immediately after high-dose administration. After absorption, RSV accumulates in the liver where it is bound to serum albumin and low-density lipoproteins.

Similarly, RSV shows protective effects against vascular occlusive disease by inhibiting neointima formation and enhancing endothelial function. It does so primarily through the activation of the [[NRF2|Nrf2]]/[[HO-1|HO-1]] pathway, which boosts antioxidant defenses and reduces [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress]]. RSV helps counteract [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative damage]] caused by high glucose levels. In mice fed a high-fat diet, RSV restores Nrf2-dependent vasodilation, reduces [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress]] and [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] in femoral arteries, and improves endothelial function. Additionally, in a mouse model of [[Atherosclerosis|atherosclerosis]], RSV inhibits the expression of [[ICAM-1]], a key factor in monocyte adhesion, through modulation of Nrf2/ARE signaling.

> [!warning] Oncology duality
> [[SIRT1]] is upregulated in breast, prostate, skin, lung, colon, and liver cancers as well as lymphomas and leukemia, promoting cancer cell growth, survival, and invasiveness. SIRT1 deacetylates tumor suppressors like [[p53]], blunting their function and increasing chemoresistance. Inhibiting SIRT1 may therefore be antitumorigenic—yet conflicting evidence shows SIRT1 can also act as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting β-catenin and [[NF-κB|NF-κB]] signaling. Context dictates outcome.

It should be noted that by regulating cellular processes such as [[DNA Repair|DNA repair]], cell cycle, [[Autophagy|autophagy]], [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]], metabolic regulation, [[Inflammation|inflammation]], and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress]] control, [[Sirtuins|SIRTs]] also regulate tumorigenesis. These effects are highly context-dependent and its role in tumor suppression and growth remains controversial. SIRT1 was found to be up-regulated in breast, prostate, skin, lung, colon, and liver cancer as well as in lymphomas and leukemia and was suggested to promote cancer cell growth, survival, and invasiveness. Likewise, chemotherapy and drug resistance have been linked to SIRT1 upregulation. SIRT1 induces [[Deacetylation|deacetylation]] of tumor suppressor genes like [[p53]]. As a result, p53 loses its tumor suppressor function and increases cancer cell growth while inhibiting their [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]. Therefore, inhibiting SIRT1 protein activity may prevent loss of p53 function and exert an antitumorigenic effect.

However, many aspects of SIRT1 in tumor growth are still ambiguous and controversial. Conflicting evidence suggests SIRT1 can also act as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting β-catenin and [[NF-κB|NF-κB]] signaling. Along these lines, [[Resveratrol|RSV]] has demonstrated anticancer effects, as evidenced by its ability to inhibit the growth of a broad range of tumor cells. These include cancers of the lymphoid and myeloid lineages, multiple myeloma, and malignancies of the breast, prostate, stomach, colon, pancreas, and thyroid, as well as melanoma, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, ovarian carcinoma, and cervical carcinoma. Its growth-suppressing activity is associated with its propensity to induce cell-cycle arrest, increase the expression of [[p21 CIP1|p21/Cip1/WAF1]], [[p53]], and [[BAX|Bax]], and decrease the levels of survivin, cyclin D1, cyclin E, [[Bcl-2|Bcl-2]], Bcl-xL, cIAPs, and [[Caspases|caspases]]. Overall, a better understanding of the biological chemistry of SIRT1 activators and inhibitors as well as their off-target effects will pave the road for their potential therapeutic usefulness.

## 2. Conclusions and Future Perspectives

[[SIRT1]] plays pivotal regulatory roles in cellular processes such as [[Apoptosis|apoptosis inhibition]], [[Aging|aging delay]], [[Reactive Oxygen Species|oxidative stress response]], [[Inflammation|inflammation control]], and tumor progression. Increasing evidence supports the therapeutic relevance of [[Sirtuins|SIRTs]] modulators in a range of diseases, including cancer, cardiovascular, autoimmune, and neuroendocrine disorders. Although several well-characterized modulators such as [[Resveratrol|RSV]] and [[SRT2104]] have demonstrated promise, many novel agents remain at the preclinical stage due to challenges related to specificity, bioavailability, safety, and clinical translation. Future research should prioritize the design of more selective and potent modulators, improve delivery systems, and explore combination therapies. Additionally, integrating genomics and biomarker profiling into clinical strategies could facilitate personalized treatment regimens. Interdisciplinary collaboration among researchers in biology, pharmacology, genetics, and clinical medicine will be essential to overcome current limitations.

Importantly, the role of SIRT1 in neurovascular and [[Neurodegenerative Diseases|neurodegenerative diseases]] remains incompletely understood and warrants further investigation, particularly given its involvement in neuronal survival, vascular integrity, and metabolic regulation. A deeper mechanistic understanding of SIRT1 function within the neurovascular unit will be critical for identifying disease-relevant targets and therapeutic windows. In parallel, the development of tailored context-dependent strategies to pharmacologically modulate SIRT1 activity in these systems will be essential to maximize therapeutic benefit while minimizing off-target effects.

Integration of biomarker profiling, multi-omics, and system-level approaches into clinical research may further enable personalized therapeutic strategies. Interdisciplinary collaboration across biology, pharmacology, genetics, and clinical research will be key to advancing SIRT1-targeted interventions. As precision medicine evolves, refined modulation of SIRT1 and related pathways holds considerable promise for improving disease prevention and treatment outcomes.

> [!note] Translational takeaway
> SIRT1 is a high-value but context-dependent therapeutic node. Biomarker-guided, tissue-specific modulation—rather than blanket activation or inhibition—is the proposed path to clinical utility.
