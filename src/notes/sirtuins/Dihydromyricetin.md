# Dihydromyricetin

**Dihydromyricetin (DHM)**, also known as **Ampelopsin**, is a natural flavonoid (flavanonol/dihydroflavonol) found at high concentrations in *Ampelopsis grossedentata* (vine tea / rattan tea). It is one of the most accessible dietary **[[SIRT3]] activators**, with notable metabolic, hepatoprotective, neuroprotective, and anti-inflammatory properties.

## Mechanism of Action — SIRT3 Activation

DHM activates [[SIRT3]], the primary mitochondrial deacetylase, through direct or expression-level effects:

- **MnSOD Deacetylation**: Like [[Honokiol]], activated SIRT3 deacetylates MnSOD (SOD2) at [[Lys68]] and K122, dramatically enhancing mitochondrial ROS scavenging.
- **Mitochondrial Function**: SIRT3 activation by DHM improves mitochondrial respiration and reduces oxidative damage.
- **AMPK Co-activation**: DHM also activates [[AMPK]], which synergistically increases [[NAD+]] levels (via NAMPT upregulation) and further potentiates SIRT3 activity.

## Physiological Impacts

- **Hepatoprotection**: Reduces alcohol-induced liver damage; widely studied for alcohol metabolism (inhibits ADH and ALDH pathways; reduces acetaldehyde accumulation).
- **Metabolic health**: Improves insulin sensitivity and glucose uptake; reduces hepatic steatosis in metabolic syndrome models.
- **Neuroprotection**: Reduces neuroinflammation; shows promise in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's models.
- **Anti-alcohol intoxication**: Widely marketed for reducing intoxication severity and hangover; works partly via GABA-A receptor modulation and alcohol metabolism.
- **Anti-inflammatory**: Inhibits NF-κB and NLRP3 inflammasome pathways.

## Dietary Sources and Bioavailability

DHM's key advantage over many sirtuin activators is its **practical dietary achievability**:

| Source | DHM Content | Quantity for ~300 mg DHM |
|---|---|---|
| *Ampelopsis grossedentata* (vine/rattan tea) dry leaves | 20–40% of dry extract | **1.0–1.5 g dry leaves** (~1–2 tea bags) |
| Grapes, berries, hops | Trace amounts | Impractically large quantities |

**~300 mg DHM from 1–1.5 g dry vine tea leaves** makes it one of the most feasible SIRT3-activating dietary interventions.

**Bioavailability**: DHM is fairly well absorbed, though like many flavonoids it undergoes some gut metabolism. Bioavailability is enhanced with fat-containing meals.
