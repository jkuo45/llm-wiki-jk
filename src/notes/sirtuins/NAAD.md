# NAAD

## Overview

NAAD (nicotinic acid adenine dinucleotide) is a metabolic intermediate of the deamidated [[Preiss-Handler Pathway]] of [[NAD+ Biosynthesis]], in which nicotinic acid is converted via NAAD to NAD+. In humans, NAAD is a sensitive biomarker of increased NAD+ metabolism after supplementation with [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR).

## Mechanism

When NR is ingested, a substantial fraction is processed through the deamidated route: NR is converted to nicotinic acid, which is then adenylated to NAAD, with NAAD amidated to NAD+. Because NAAD is elevated only when the Preiss-Handler branch is active, it serves as a readout distinguishing the deamidated route from the direct [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]] (NAMPT/NMNAT).

> [!info] Biomarker value
> NR supplementation raised NAAD ~4.5-5-fold in blood; in one study NAAD was undetectable before NR but rose ~2,900% after single doses up to 1 g NR, corroborating mouse data that the deamidated pathway predominates after NR intake.

## Physiological Function

NAAD itself is primarily an intermediate, but its measurement provides non-invasive evidence that supplemented NAD+ precursors engage the deamidated metabolic route in humans. This informs how efficiently dietary precursors replenish the NAD+ pool in aging.

## Clinical Relevance

Blood NAAD is increasingly used as a pharmacodynamic biomarker in human trials of NAD+ precursors ([[NMN]], NR), supporting dosing and efficacy assessment in interventions targeting NAD+ decline with [[Aging]].
