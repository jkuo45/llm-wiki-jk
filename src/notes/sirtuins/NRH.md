# NRH

## Overview

NRH (dihydronicotinamide riboside) is the reduced form of [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR). It has recently been shown to promote [[NAD+ Biosynthesis|NAD+ synthesis]] and is more stable than NR, which degrades to [[Nicotinamide]] in the blood. NRH raises NAD+ more potently and rapidly than NR in cultured cells (5-10-fold) and in mice.

## Mechanism

NRH enters the salvage machinery: it is phosphorylated by [[Adenosine Kinase]] to [[NMNH]], which is then converted to NAD+ via downstream adenylylation steps in the [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]]. This route bypasses the NAMPT-dependent amidated pathway, providing an alternative, [[NMRK1]]-independent entry point. Endogenous NRH has been detected in the liver of mouse models, establishing it as a natural NAD+ precursor.

> [!important] Reduced-precursor route
> NRH→NMNH→NAD+ is a non-canonical, NMRK-independent biosynthetic route. Its stability and potency make NRH a promising oral NAD+ precursor.

## Physiological Function

NRH expands the repertoire of NAD+ precursors beyond the amidated molecules nicotinamide, [[NMN]], and NR. Its discovery illustrates the redundancy of NAD+ biosynthetic routes and suggests multiple dietary and pharmacological compounds can maintain intracellular NAD+ levels across tissues.

## Research Applications

NRH is being studied for preventing NAD+ decline in [[Aging]] and disease. Its faster, more potent NAD+ elevation relative to NR makes it an attractive candidate, though its handling by [[Purine Nucleoside Phosphorylase]] and tissue metabolism requires further characterization.
