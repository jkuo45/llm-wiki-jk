# Sirt1 Regulates Aging and Resistance to Oxidative Stress in the Heart

**Alcendor RR, Gao S, Zhai P, Zablocki D, Holle E, Yu X, Tian B, Wagner TE, Vatner SF, Sadoshima J.** *Circulation Research* 2007;100(10):1512–1521. PMID 17446436. https://doi.org/10.1161/01.RES.0000267723.65696.4a

> [!important] Key finding — dose-dependent (hormetic) cardiac SIRT1 activity
> Using heart-specific Sirt1 transgenic mice on the α-MHC promoter, low (2.5-fold, line 39) to moderate (7.5-fold, line 40) overexpression **retarded** age-dependent cardiac hypertrophy, apoptosis, fibrosis, dysfunction, and senescence-marker expression, and conferred resistance to [[Paraquat]]-induced [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] via [[FoxO1]]-dependent [[Catalase]] upregulation. In contrast, high (12.5-fold, line 53) overexpression increased apoptosis, hypertrophy, and oxidative stress while depleting [[NAD+]], suppressing [[PGC-1α]], [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]], and [[ATP]] — inducing [[Cardiomyopathy]]. The beneficial effects of [[SIRT1|Sirt1]] are thus confined to a narrow, low-to-moderate dose window.

## Abstract

[[Sir2 (yeast)|Sir2]], a class III histone deacetylase, mediates lifespan extension in model organisms and prevents [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] in mammalian cells. However, beneficial functions of Sir2 remained to be shown in mammals *in vivo* at the organ level, such as the heart. The authors addressed this using transgenic mice with heart-specific overexpression of [[SIRT1|Sirt1]], a mammalian homolog of Sir2. Sirt1 was significantly upregulated (4- to 8-fold) in response to pressure overload and [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] in nontransgenic adult mouse hearts. Low (2.5-fold) to moderate (7.5-fold) overexpression of Sirt1 attenuated age-dependent increases in [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]], [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]]/fibrosis, cardiac dysfunction, and expression of senescence markers. In contrast, a high level (12.5-fold) of Sirt1 increased apoptosis and hypertrophy and decreased cardiac function, thereby stimulating the development of [[Cardiomyopathy|cardiomyopathy]]. Moderate overexpression of Sirt1 protected the heart from oxidative stress induced by [[Paraquat|paraquat]], with increased expression of antioxidants such as [[Catalase|catalase]] through forkhead box O ([[FOXO|FoxO]])-dependent mechanisms, whereas high levels of Sirt1 increased oxidative stress in the heart at baseline. Thus, mild to moderate expression of Sirt1 retards aging of the heart, whereas a high dose of Sirt1 induces cardiomyopathy. Furthermore, although high levels of Sirt1 increase oxidative stress, moderate expression of Sirt1 induces resistance to oxidative stress and apoptosis. These results suggest that [[SIRT1|Sirt1]] could retard aging and confer stress resistance to the heart *in vivo*, but these beneficial effects are observed only at low to moderate doses (up to 7.5-fold) of Sirt1.

Extrinsic and intrinsic factors cooperate in determining the rate of aging and the aging phenotype. Because aging reduces organ function and increases disease risk, elucidating the mechanisms controlling aging has significant clinical implications. Increasing evidence suggests that evolutionarily conserved molecular mechanisms regulate lifespan in animals. For example, [[Caloric Restriction|caloric restriction]] prolongs the lifespan of organisms from yeast to primates, possibly through [[Sir2 (yeast)|Sir2]]-dependent mechanisms. Genetic alterations causing metabolic effects similar to caloric restriction, including mutation/deletion of *Cyr1*/*Sch9*, also cause lifespan extension in yeast. Similarly, inhibition of insulin-like growth factor ([[IGF-1|IGF-I]]) signaling, including *Daf-2* mutation in *Caenorhabditis elegans*, causes lifespan extension mediated by a loss of suppression of *Daf-16* or forkhead box O ([[FOXO|FoxO]]) transcription factors, which regulate antioxidant expression and DNA damage repair ([[GADD45]]). In mammals, Ames and Snell dwarf mice lacking GH/IGF-I signaling and IGF-I receptor heterozygous knockout mice live longer, and systemic overexpression of *[[Klotho|klotho]]*, a hormone that inhibits insulin/IGF-I signaling, extends lifespan in mice. Another important mechanism controlling aging is [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]]. Overexpression of antioxidant molecules, including mitochondrial [[Catalase|catalase]] and [[Thioredoxin|thioredoxin]], induces lifespan extension. Mice deficient in either *mclk1* or *p66shc* ([[P66shc|p66^shc]]), both involved in mitochondrial electron transfer, live longer. Importantly, these longevity factors also confer stress resistance to the organism — the accumulation of which leads to longer lifespan.

Yeast [[Sir2 (yeast)|Sir2]], an [[NAD+|NAD^+]]-dependent protein deacetylase and a founding member of the sirtuin family, functions in a wide array of cellular processes, including gene silencing, longevity, muscle differentiation, and DNA damage repair. Stimulation of Sir2 by overexpression or sirtuin-activating compounds such as [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]] is sufficient to induce prolonged lifespan in yeast, *Caenorhabditis elegans*, *Drosophila melanogaster*, and mice. [[SIRT1|Sirt1]], a mammalian ortholog of Sir2, provides protection against apoptosis and plays an essential role in mediating survival of cardiac myocytes and neurons under stress *in vitro*. Sirt1-deficient mice rarely survive postnatally and exhibit developmental abnormalities in several organs, including the heart. Pancreatic β-cell–specific overexpression of Sirt1 enhances ATP production, enhancing insulin secretion in response to glucose in transgenic mice, suggesting Sirt1 potentially improves glucose metabolism. Judging from the generally cell-protective function of Sir2 in model organisms, Sirt1 may prevent aging and play a protective role in mammalian cells *in vivo*. Importantly, it remains to be shown, without relying on pharmacological interventions, that specific activation of Sirt1 confers antiaging and stress-resistance benefits to mammalian cells *in vivo*. In fact, recent evidence suggests that nonreplicating yeast cells without Sir2 exhibit greater stress resistance in extremely long-lived yeast mutants, suggesting Sir2 could make cells more prone to stress under some conditions. Furthermore, overexpression of Sir2 family proteins does not extend replicative lifespan in normal human fibroblasts or prostate epithelial cells but rather promotes replicative senescence in response to chronic cellular stress via a *p19^ARF*-dependent mechanism in mouse embryonic fibroblasts. These reports suggest the molecular mechanism mediating lifespan extension in lower organisms may not work as expected in higher organisms. Thus, it is important to examine whether Sir2 can retard *in vivo* aging of mammalian organs, such as the heart, the major component of which is terminally differentiated cardiac myocytes.

To elucidate the *in vivo* function of Sirt1 in the heart, the authors generated transgenic mice with cardiac specific overexpression of Sirt1 (Tg-Sirt1). The central hypothesis was that [[SIRT1|Sirt1]] mediates antiaging and cell-protective effects in the heart *in vivo*.

## Materials and Methods

Tg-Sirt1 mice were generated on an FVB background using the α-myosin heavy chain (α-MHC) promoter. All experiments involving animals were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at the New Jersey Medical School. [[ATP|ATP content]] and [[Citrate Synthase|citrate synthase]] activity were measured using assay kits from Sigma. DNA microarray analyses were conducted using a GeneChip Mouse Genome 430 2.0 probe array (Affymetrix). Data are reported as mean±SEM. Statistical analyses between groups were done by 1-way ANOVA, and when probability values were significant, differences among group means were evaluated using *t* test with Bonferroni's correction. A probability value of <0.05 was considered significant.

## Results

### Sirt1 Is Upregulated in Response to Stress in the Heart

Expression of Sirt1 is upregulated in the dog heart during heart failure. The authors examined whether Sirt1 is upregulated in response to stresses. In mice, the level of Sirt1 in the heart was significantly upregulated after 2 and 4 weeks of pressure overload (5.5- and 8.8-fold) and by [[Paraquat|paraquat]] injection (4.3-fold), which induces [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] in the heart (Figure 1A). Expression of Sirt1 in the heart was also significantly greater (2.9-fold) in old (20.8 years) than in young (6.1 years) monkeys (Figure 1B).

> [!info] Figure 1 — Stress and aging upregulate cardiac Sirt1
> Heart homogenates were prepared from mice subjected to [[Transverse aortic constriction|transverse aortic constriction (TAC)]] for 2 or 4 weeks or paraquat (PQ) injection for 2 weeks, or from young and old monkeys, and subjected to immunoblot analyses with anti-Sirt1, anti-actin, or anti-GAPDH antibodies. Sirt1 normalized to actin was increased ~5.5×/8.8× after TAC and ~4.3× after paraquat, and ~2.9× in old vs young monkey hearts.

### Basal Characterization of Tg-Sirt1

Three lines (39, 40, and 53) of Tg-Sirt1 were generated with different levels of Sirt1 in the heart (2.5-, 7.5-, and 12.5-fold over nontransgenic \[NTg\] mice, respectively) (Figure 2A). Immunoblot analyses of multiple organs confirmed heart-specific overexpression (Figure 2B). Initial characterization used mice aged 6 months. Postmortem analyses showed lines 39 and 40 had normal heart size, whereas line 53 showed an enlarged left atrium and left ventricle (LV) (Figure 2C). LV enlargement in line 53 was confirmed echocardiographically (LV end-diastolic dimension, LVEDD). Baseline LV weight (LVW)/body weight and LVW/tibial length (LVW/TL) ratios, indices of [[Cardiac Hypertrophy|cardiac hypertrophy]], were normal in lines 39 and 40 but significantly greater in line 53. There was no significant change in hypertrophy-associated ("fetal type") gene expression in lines 39 or 40. In contrast, expression of β-MHC, [[Atrial natriuretic peptide|atrial natriuretic factor]], and α-skeletal actin was significantly upregulated, whereas α-MHC was significantly downregulated, in line 53. These results indicate that **high levels of Sirt1 overexpression induce cardiac hypertrophy *in vivo***.

Echocardiographic analyses showed normal chamber size and cardiac function in lines 39 and 40. In line 53, however, the LV was significantly dilated and both LV ejection fraction (LVEF) and percentage fractional shortening were significantly reduced; LV end-diastolic pressure was elevated while contraction (dP/dt) and relaxation (−dP/dt) rates were reduced; and lung weight/body weight ratio was elevated, suggesting lung congestion. Thus, 2.5- to 7.5-fold overexpression does not affect baseline cardiac chamber size or function, whereas 12.5-fold overexpression induces LV chamber dilation, hypertrophy, and dysfunction, mimicking [[Cardiomyopathy|cardiomyopathy]].

Histological analyses of LV myocardium showed no significant fibrosis in lines 39 or 40 at 6 months; line 53 showed significantly increased LV fibrosis. TUNEL analyses showed the frequency of TUNEL-positive nuclei was significantly lower in lines 39 and 40, but significantly elevated in line 53, indicating myocardial [[Apoptosis|apoptosis]] is suppressed by mild-to-modest overexpression but enhanced by a high level of Sirt1.

> (A) Hematoxylin/eosin staining; (B) picric acid Sirius red (PASR) staining quantifying fibrosis; (C) TUNEL/DAPI staining showing apoptosis was lower in lines 39/40 and elevated in line 53 vs NTg.

### Mild to Moderate Overexpression of Sirt1 Retards Age-Dependent Changes in the Heart

Because Sirt1 is overexpressed heart-specifically, overexpression may not be sufficient to extend whole-animal lifespan. Kaplan–Meier analysis showed no significant difference in mortality in lines 39 or 40 vs NTg, whereas line 53 mice died significantly earlier and rarely survived more than 250 days.

Increases in hypertrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and apoptosis are common age-dependent cardiac changes. Histopathological analysis of all three lines at 2 to 3, 6 to 7, and 18 months showed: cardiac myocyte size and LVW/TL in line 53 were greater than NTg from 2 to 3 months and even greater at 6 to 7 months; lines 39 and 40 showed smaller age-dependent increases in cell size and LVW/TL, with cell size at 18 months significantly smaller than NTg. Similar results were obtained for age-dependent increases in fibrosis and myocyte apoptosis. The same trend held for age-dependent declines in cardiac function — LVEDD and LV end-systolic dimension (LVESD) in NTg were greater at 18 months than at earlier ages, whereas in lines 39 and 40 they were significantly smaller than NTg at 18 months. Line 53 showed lower LVEF and fractional shortening from 2 to 3 months onward, whereas lines 39 and 40 exhibited significantly greater LVEF and fractional shortening than NTg at 18 months.

> [!tip] Conclusion — dose dependence of cardiac antiaging
> Although high levels of Sirt1 stimulate the development of [[Cardiomyopathy|cardiomyopathy]], **mild to moderate expression of Sirt1 retards aging-induced histological changes and LV dysfunction in the heart**.

### Moderate Overexpression of Sirt1 Stimulates Cell-Protective Molecules and Inhibits Aging Markers

Expression of the INK4/ARF family proteins, inducers of [[Cellular Senescence|cellular senescence]], increases with age in many rodent organs including the heart. Protein levels of [[p15INK4b|p15^INK4b]] and p19^ARF were significantly lower in lines 39 and 40, but not line 53, than in NTg at 2 to 3 and 8 to 12 months (Figure 6A, 6B). A similar result was obtained for [[p53]] (Figure 6C). Expression of [[Bcl-2]] and [[Bcl-xL]], both antiapoptotic molecules, was elevated in lines 39, 40, and 53, with the greatest expression in line 40 (Figure 6D).

DNA microarray analyses (deposited in GEO, accession GSE7407) identified cell-protective molecules and potential aging regulators upregulated in line 40, including heat shock protein ([[HSP70|Hsp]]40/70/90), telomere reverse transcriptase, telomere repeat binding factor 2, [[Klotho]], and [[Werner Syndrome|Werner syndrome protein]].

### Tg-Sirt1 Line 53 Mice Have Lower Cardiac ATP Content

Although cardiac [[ATP|ATP content]] in line 40 was not significantly different from NTg, line 53 exhibited significantly lower ATP at 2 to 3 months (Figure 7A). The ratio of phospho-[[AMPK|AMPK]]/total AMPK was significantly elevated in line 53, but not line 40, consistent with energy starvation. Line 40 showed higher, whereas line 53 lower, [[Citrate Synthase|citrate synthase]] activity, suggesting depression of [[Mitochondrial Function|mitochondrial function]] by high Sirt1 (Figure 7B). Electron microscopy showed a significantly smaller number of [[Mitochondria|mitochondria]] in line 53, suggesting reduced [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] (Figure 7C). mRNA expression of [[PGC-1α]], a master cofactor of mitochondrial biogenesis, was dose-dependently reduced by Sirt1 overexpression, with line 53 exhibiting ≈50% reduction (Figure 7D).

### Tg-Sirt1 Mice Are Protected Against Oxidative Stress

Because line 40 exhibited reduced baseline apoptosis and aging markers, the authors treated mice with [[Paraquat|paraquat]] for 14 days. Paraquat significantly reduced LV contraction in NTg but not line 40, and the extent of cardiac myocyte apoptosis after paraquat was significantly greater in NTg than in Tg-Sirt1 (Figure 8A). Paraquat increased [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]] in NTg hearts ([[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine|8-OHdG]] staining and [[Malondialdehyde|malondialdehyde]] content, established oxidative-stress markers), but these increases were abolished in line 40 (Figure 8B). Paraquat-induced increases in [[Catalase|catalase]] expression were greater in line 40 than in NTg (Figure 8C). Conversely, tissue oxidative stress was significantly enhanced in line 53 even at baseline (Figure 8D), indicating high-level Sirt1 overexpression enhances oxidative stress.

> [!info] Figure 8 — Paraquat oxidative-stress challenge
> Line 40 Tg-Sirt1 and NTg mice were treated with i.p. 10 mg/kg paraquat for 2 weeks. (A) TUNEL apoptosis was significantly lower in Tg-Sirt1; (B) [[8-oxo-2'-deoxyguanosine|8-OHdG]] staining and [[Malondialdehyde|malondialdehyde (MDA)]] content rises were abolished in line 40; (C) paraquat-induced catalase induction was greater in line 40; (D) 8-OHdG staining was elevated in line 53 even at baseline.

Mechanistically, the authors tested whether [[FoxO1|FoxO]] mediates catalase upregulation, since Sirt1 stimulates oxidative-stress resistance through FoxO in fibroblasts. Overexpression of either Sirt1 or constitutively active FoxO1a in cultured cardiac myocytes stimulated [[Catalase|catalase]] expression, whereas upregulation was inhibited in the presence of dominant negative FoxO1a — suggesting **FoxO1a plays an important role in mediating Sirt1-induced catalase upregulation**, which in part suppresses myocardial damage caused by oxidative stress in line 40.

## Discussion

Aging hearts exhibit unique histological and biochemical features: increases in apoptosis and necrosis, proliferation of myocyte nuclei, increased myocyte volume, and connective tissue accumulation; aging also affects INK4/ARF family senescence-factor expression, and induction of cell-protective mechanisms (antioxidants, heat shock proteins) is attenuated in aging hearts. As a result, aging cardiac myocytes cannot be transcriptionally reprogrammed in response to increased workload. Optimal therapeutic interventions should prevent cell death and accumulation of senescent myocytes.

Although mechanisms inducing lifespan extension may slow whole-organism aging, whether each longevity factor prevents organ/cell aging in mammals remained untested. Heart-specific expression of a longevity factor (dPTEN or dFoxO) prevents age-dependent decline of heart function in *Drosophila*, indicating longevity factors may act autonomously to prevent aging of individual organs. Overexpression of [[Catalase|catalase]] targeted to mitochondria retarded aging-induced cardiac damage in mice. These results suggest Sirt1 prevents cardiac aging when 2.5- to 7.5-fold overexpressed; more studies are needed to determine whether this is a general property of longevity mechanisms or molecule-specific.

> [!note] IGF-I trade-off and cardiotropism caveats
> Given the apparent cardioprotective effects of the IGF-I–Akt axis (at least short term), whether inhibiting IGF-I signaling — which extends mouse lifespan — prevents mammalian cardiac aging without impairing function remains to be tested. IGF-I may represent a trade-off between current benefits and later costs in senescence, like inotropic agents and their long-term exacerbation of heart failure. Cardiac-specific overexpression of mammalian sterile 20-like kinase 1 ([[Mst1|Mst1]]) induces dilated [[Cardiomyopathy|cardiomyopathy]], yet a homolog of Mst1 extends lifespan in *C. elegans* through DAF-16 phosphorylation — an example of possible divergence between lower organisms and mammals in longevity/stress-resistance mechanisms.

The antiaging and stress-resistance effects of Sirt1 could be mediated by well-established longevity mechanisms ([[FOXO|FoxO]] family transcription factors) as well as nonspecific upregulation of cytoprotective pathways. Whether stimulation of Sirt1 is sufficient to extend mammalian lifespan remains untested — heart-specific overexpression was not sufficient to induce lifespan extension in mice.

> [!warning] High-dose Sirt1 is not cardioprotective
> Line 53 (12.5-fold) spontaneously developed heart failure with hypertrophy, apoptosis, fibrosis, and oxidative stress. [[ATP|ATP content]], [[Citrate Synthase|citrate synthase]] activity, and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]] were all reduced, while [[PGC-1α]] protein was suppressed — suggesting high Sirt1 induces mitochondrial dysfunction, which may increase oxidative stress. High Sirt1 may also consume [[NAD+]], depleting it — a dose-dependent adverse effect documented for [[PARP1|poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase-1]], another NAD^+-dependent enzyme, where high doses lead to NAD^+ depletion. Because NAD^+ is required for mitochondrial respiration, NAD^+ depletion could lead to ATP deficiency, cellular dysfunction, and cell death.

Expression of Sirt1 is upregulated 3- to 9-fold in response to stresses and 2.9-fold in old monkey hearts. Such modest upregulation is likely a compensatory mechanism expected to retard aging and inhibit apoptosis without causing mitochondrial dysfunction or NAD^+ depletion. It would be important to keep Sirt1 overexpression modest if upregulation is considered as a therapeutic option.

> [!tip] Hormetic basis of SIRT1's dose window
> Resistance to intrinsic and extrinsic stressors correlates with lifespan across species. This positive correlation, also seen in mice with genetic mutations and in animals pretreated with sublethal stress, is a phenomenon known as [[Hormesis|hormesis]]. Under the hormesis hypothesis, longevity factors could be upregulated in response to low-grade stress and confer stress resistance. Sirt1 was upregulated in failing hearts and in animals subjected to [[Caloric Restriction|caloric restriction]]. Here Tg-Sirt1 exhibited resistance to oxidative stress. Previously [[Thioredoxin|thioredoxin 1]] — an antioxidant and among the few that prolong mouse lifespan when overexpressed systemically — is upregulated in response to pressure overload and acts as an antihypertrophic factor and stimulator of mitochondrial function. This raises the possibility that stimulating known longevity mechanisms could be a new modality of heart-failure treatment, by increasing cardiac resistance to pathologic insults.

In summary, 2.5- to 7.5-fold overexpression of [[SIRT1|Sirt1]] has antiaging and stress-resistance effects, whereas higher levels induce [[Cardiomyopathy|cardiomyopathy]], possibly through mitochondrial dysfunction, *in vivo*. Although stimulation of Sirt1 may be considered as an antiaging therapy for the heart, careful evaluation of dosage seems essential to best use its therapeutic potential.

## Key Insights for the Wiki

- **Hormetic dose-response for cardiac SIRT1:** 2.5–7.5-fold overexpression is protective/antiaging; 12.5-fold is pathogenic ([[Cardiomyopathy|cardiomyopathy]]). A canonical example of a [[Hormetic Window|hormetic window]] for a sirtuin in a mammalian organ.
- **Sirt1 is a stress-responsive longevity factor** upregulated 3–9-fold by pressure overload/paraquat and ~2.9-fold in aged monkey hearts — consistent with a compensatory/adaptive mechanism.
- **FoxO1a→Catalase axis:** moderate Sirt1 induces antioxidant [[Catalase|catalase]] via [[FoxO1|FoxO1a]]-dependent transcription, conferring resistance to [[Paraquat|paraquat]]-induced oxidative injury.
- **High-dose mitochondrial toxicity:** 12.5-fold Sirt1 suppresses [[PGC-1α]] and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis|mitochondrial biogenesis]], lowers [[ATP|ATP]] and [[Citrate Synthase|citrate synthase]], elevates phospho-[[AMPK|AMPK]], and may deplete [[NAD+]].
- **Anti-senescence markers:** moderate Sirt1 lowers [[p15INK4b|p15^INK4b]], p19^ARF ([[Cellular Senescence|INK4/ARF]]), and [[p53]], while raising [[Bcl-2]]/[[Bcl-xL]].
