# Malonyl-CoA decarboxylase

## Overview

Malonyl-CoA decarboxylase (MCD; MLYCD) converts malonyl-CoA to acetyl-CoA, reducing malonyl-CoA levels. Malonyl-CoA is a potent inhibitor of carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT1), the rate-limiting transporter for [[Fatty acid oxidation]] import into [[Mitochondria]]; by degrading malonyl-CoA, MCD promotes fatty-acid oxidation.

## Mechanism

In hepatocytes, [[SIRT4]] represses PPARα signaling and inhibits malonyl-CoA decarboxylase, shifting liver metabolism away from mitochondrial fatty-acid utilization and toward anabolic lipid conservation. Directionally, SIRT4 sits upstream of suppressed [[Beta-Oxidation]]. This metabolic brake counterbalances [[SIRT3]], which promotes beta-oxidation; the relative activity of the two mitochondrial sirtuins determines whether the liver oxidizes or stores fat.

> [!important] Metabolic brake
> SIRT4 inhibits malonyl-CoA decarboxylase → malonyl-CoA accumulates → CPT1 inhibited → reduced fatty-acid oxidation and increased lipid storage.

## Physiological Function

MCD is a key regulator of the malonyl-CoA/CPT1 axis controlling the switch between lipid oxidation and lipid synthesis. Its SIRT4-dependent suppression favors lipid conservation under nutrient surplus.

## Clinical Relevance

Under chronic nutrient surplus, sustained SIRT4 activity and consequent MCD inhibition contribute to [[Hepatic Steatosis]] (fatty liver). Conditions that shift the [[SIRT3-SIRT4 Ratio]] toward SIRT3 favor fat burning, a principle relevant to metabolic-disease interventions.
