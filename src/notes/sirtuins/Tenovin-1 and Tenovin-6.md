# Tenovin-1 and Tenovin-6

## Overview

Tenovin-1 and tenovin-6 are small-molecule [[Sirtuin Inhibitors]] targeting [[SIRT1]] and [[SIRT2]]. They were identified in a phenotypic screen for p53-activating compounds and are among the earliest synthetic sirtuin inhibitors studied for anticancer activity.

## Mechanism

Tenovins block SIRT1/SIRT2-mediated deacetylation of [[p53]], leading to p53 hyperacetylation and activation. Acetylated p53 is more stable and transcriptionally active, reinstating tumor-suppressive gene programs in cancer cells. Directionally, tenovin-mediated sirtuin inhibition sits upstream of p53 activation and tumor suppression.

> [!important] Sirtuin inhibition → p53 activation
> By blocking SIRT1/SIRT2 deacetylation of p53, tenovins stabilize and activate p53, promoting apoptosis and growth arrest in p53-wild-type tumors — a strategy shared with HDAC inhibitors.

## Pharmacological Properties

Tenovin-6 shows improved cellular potency and is used experimentally to probe sirtuin functions in metabolism and cancer. Their strong anticancer activity is attributed to p53 reactivation, illustrating the direct link between sirtuin inhibition, p53 acetylation state, and cancer therapy.

## Clinical Relevance

Tenovins illustrate a key dichotomy: sirtuin activators are pursued for longevity and metabolic health, while sirtuin inhibitors (tenovins, EX-527, cambinol) are explored as cancer therapeutics, particularly in p53-competent tumors. See also [[Tenovin-6]] and [[Tenovins]] notes.
