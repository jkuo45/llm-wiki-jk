# SRT1460

[[SRT1460]] is a synthetic small-molecule [[STACs|STAC]] (sirtuin-activating compound) developed by [[Sirtris Pharmaceuticals]] as a potent [[SIRT1]] activator. It belongs to the imidazothiazole class (structurally unrelated to [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]]) and is often discussed together with [[SRT1720]] and [[SRT2183]] as the second-generation STACs identified through high-throughput screening of SIRT1 activity.

## Chemical Properties & Classification

- **Class**: Imidazothiazole; second-generation synthetic STAC (compound **6** in Fig. **3** of the review).
- **Target**: [[SIRT1]] activator.
- **Selectivity**: Reported to be more potent than resveratrol as a SIRT1 activator in biochemical assays.

## Pharmacodynamics & Pharmacokinetics

- SRT1460 activates SIRT1 in deacetylase assays and recapitulates many metabolic hallmarks of SIRT1 activation (e.g., deacetylation of [[p53]] and [[PGC-1α]]-associated pathways).
- Its mechanism of action is debated: like other [[Allosteric Activator|allosteric]] STACs, activation could frequently only be demonstrated with fluorescently tagged substrates, raising the possibility of assay artifacts. Given its metabolic similarity to resveratrol, SRT1460 may act *in vivo* at least partly through indirect pathways (e.g., the [[cAMP]]-[[Epac1]]-[[AMPK]] axis) rather than by direct SIRT1 binding.

## Applications

- Research tool to probe SIRT1-dependent effects in metabolic and [[Aging|aging]] models.
- These STACs were reported to have anticancer activity, though the contribution of SIRT1 activation versus off-target effects remains unresolved.
