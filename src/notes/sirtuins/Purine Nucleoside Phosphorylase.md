# Purine Nucleoside Phosphorylase

## Overview

Purine nucleoside phosphorylase (NP/PNP) is a salvage-pathway enzyme that cleaves the glycosidic bond of nucleosides, releasing the free base. In NAD+ metabolism, NP converts [[Nicotinamide Riboside]] (NR) into [[Nicotinamide]] (NAM), which can then be recycled to [[NAD+]] via [[NAMPT]] and [[NMNAT]].

## Mechanism

NP-mediated cleavage of NR to NAM is believed to be the variable step that degrades NR in the gut, potentially explaining the low and interindividually variable oral bioavailability of NR. NAM is the most common NAD+ precursor in mammals; at higher doses it can exert [[Sirtuin Inhibitors|sirtuin-inhibiting]] effects. The balance between the direct [[NMRK1]] pathway and the NP/NAMPT route determines how efficiently supplemented NR is converted to NAD+ in different tissues.

> [!info] Variable step
> NP cleavage of NR→NAM in the gut competes with direct NR phosphorylation by NMRK1, creating a tissue- and individual-dependent split that governs NR bioavailability and NAD+ yield.

## Physiological Function

The NP/NAMPT route provides an alternative to the direct NMRK-dependent salvage of NR. Because NAM generated by NP feeds the classic [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]], NP activity shapes how precursor supplements (NR, [[NMN]]) are handled systemically.

## Clinical Relevance

Understanding NP activity helps predict which individuals respond to NR supplementation and informs strategies (e.g., formulation, timing, combination with NAMPT modulation) to improve NAD+ precursor bioavailability in aging.
