# Glutamate dehydrogenase

## Overview

Glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH; GLUD1) is a mitochondrial matrix enzyme catalyzing the reversible oxidative deamination of [[Glutamate]] to [[α-Ketoglutarate]], with NAD(P)+ as cofactor. It is a major conduit for amino-acid carbon into the [[TCA cycle]] (anaplerosis) and a determinant of ammonia homeostasis and [[Insulin]] secretion.

## Mechanism

[[SIRT4]] inhibits GDH by mono-ADP-ribosylation, reducing the conversion of glutamate to α-ketoglutarate and limiting anaplerotic carbon entry into the TCA cycle. Directionally, SIRT4 enzymatically suppresses GDH activity within [[Mitochondria]], acting as a metabolic brake that restrains amino-acid-stimulated insulin secretion and mitochondrial ATP generation.

> [!important] SIRT3/SIRT4 antagonism
> Because SIRT4 also inhibits mitochondrial trifunctional protein α (MTPα), glutamine metabolism, and PPARα signaling, it broadly opposes the oxidative, catabolic programs that [[SIRT3]] promotes. The balance between these mitochondrial sirtuins is an emerging determinant of metabolic resilience.

## Physiological Function

By gating glutamate flux, GDH links amino-acid abundance to mitochondrial energy production and to glucose-stimulated insulin secretion in pancreatic β-cells. SIRT4-dependent inhibition therefore couples nutrient status to hormonal output and lipid conservation under nutrient-replete conditions.

## Clinical Relevance

GDH activating mutations cause hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia. The SIRT4–GDH interaction positions mitochondrial sirtuin signaling as a regulator of whole-body glucose handling and a potential target in metabolic disease.
