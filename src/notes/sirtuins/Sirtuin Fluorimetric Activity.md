# Sirtuin Fluorimetric Activity

[[Sirtuin Fluorimetric Activity|Sirtuin fluorimetric activity assays]] are the most commonly used methods to screen molecules as possible sirtuin inhibitors. These coupled assays detect sirtuin-mediated deacetylation of a fluorophore-modified substrate; upon deacetylation the substrate becomes sensitive to a developer reagent (or trypsin), releasing a measurable fluorophore (e.g., aminomethylcoumarin, AMC).

## Assay Formats

- **Fluor de Lys**: Deacetylation sensitizes the substrate; addition of a developer produces a fluorophore measured on a plate reader.
- **Homogeneous fluorescent deacetylase assay**: ZMAL (fluorescent acetyl-lysine derivative) is deacetylated to ZML, which is cleaved by trypsin to release fluorescent AMC.
- **SIRT1 Fluorescence Polarization (FP)**: Mass/disorder change of a biotinylated, fluorescently tagged p53-derived peptide is read by fluorescence polarization.

## Limitations

The aminomethylcoumarin moiety of fluorogenic substrates can affect binding of small molecules to the enzyme, potentially producing false activation/inhibition results. This underlies the controversy over [[STACs|STAC]] activation (e.g., [[Resveratrol|resveratrol]], [[SRT1460]], [[SRT1720]], [[SRT2183]]), so results are often confirmed with other assays (charcoal binding, radiolabeled nicotinamide release, capillary electrophoresis, bioluminescence NAD⁺).

## Applications

- High-throughput screening of compound libraries for sirtuin inhibitors (e.g., identification of [[EX-527]], [[Tenovins|tenovins]], [[JGB1741]], [[Sirtinol|sirtinol]] analogues, [[Cambinol|cambinol]] analogues).
- Hit confirmation and structure-activity relationship studies.
