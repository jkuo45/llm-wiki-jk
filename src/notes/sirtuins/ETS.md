# ETS

The **ETS** (E-Twenty-Six) family is a large group of eukaryotic transcription factors defined by a conserved ~85 amino-acid winged helix-turn-helix DNA-binding domain that recognizes purine-rich GGAA/T core DNA motifs. ETS proteins control genes involved in proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and migration and are frequently deregulated in cancer, either by overexpression, chromosomal translocation, or as downstream effectors of MAPK/RAS signaling.

## Structure & DNA Binding

- The ETS domain comprises three α-helices and a four-stranded β-sheet; a winged turn contacts the DNA major groove and flanking sequences provide specificity.
- Family members bind cooperatively with partner factors (e.g., SRF, ternary complex factors) to composite response elements.

## Members & Functions

- Subfamilies include ETS (E26), TCF (ternary complex factors [[ELK1]], [[ELK4]]), and ETS2/ETS-related groups.
- Regulate immediate-early gene expression downstream of growth factor signaling and drive lineage-specific gene programs (e.g., prostate via ETS fusions TMPRSS2-ERG).

## Role in Sirtuin Biology

The ETS family transcription factor [[ELK4]] is central to the pro-oncogenic activity of [[SIRT7]]: ELK4 stabilizes SIRT7 at target gene promoters, where SIRT7 deacetylates [[Histone H3]] at [[H3K18ac|lysine 18]], repressing tumor suppressor gene expression.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- ETS gene fusions and overexpression are diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers in prostate cancer, Ewing sarcoma (EWS-ETS fusions), and leukemias.
- ETS transcription factors are candidate therapeutic targets because their DNA-binding domains are druggable by small molecules that block DNA engagement.
