01 · Framing

Damage vs. Hormetic Reading

Adrenochrome has two honest readings in the vault. The first is the damage reading: a redox-cycling catecholamine oxidation product that generates ROS, depletes glutathione, and is hypothesised to drive ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes and dopaminergic neurons. The second — the protocol reading developed here — is the hormetic one: a low-dose redox stressor whose ROS output lands on the very adaptive machinery the cell uses to build resilience, namely mitohormesis, autophagy, and sirtuins. The same node read as a trigger rather than a target.

A prior study traced how that trigger reaches the three adaptive programs. This page widens the lens to the protocol that aims to steer the trigger: methylene blue (MB) as an electron-shunt amplifier, aminoguanidine (AG) as an aminochrome-quencher / AGE blocker, and the supporting stack (carbazochrome, ascorbic acid, urolithin A, spermidine, rapamycin, fisetin, creatine, resveratrol) that the outline document proposes around the mitohormetic-redox-relay (MRR), glyco-oxidative-proteostasis (GOPS) and SASP-remodeling-aminochrome (SRAC) mechanisms. Every one of those agents now has a fresh trace against the same build.

Primary study question

How is each protocol agent wired to the adrenochrome node — by a direct chemical edge (reaction, derivation, reduction) or by shared redox/injury bridges — and where does each sit relative to the trigger across the spectral seam?

Secondary: (a) do the protocol's core agents (MB, AG) couple to the trigger more tightly than the adaptive programs themselves? (b) which coupling mode is a genuine single-bridge choke? (c) does the protocol reproduce, extend, or contradict the four-node baseline?

Hormesis is a dose hypothesis

A knowledge graph records coupling, not dose-response. Calling adrenochrome a "hormetic trigger" is a claim about the low-dose regime — that its ROS/electrophile chemistry engages adaptive signalling rather than overwhelming it. The network analysis on this page is agnostic to dose and stands on its own; the hormetic claim must be adjudicated against primary literature (see §10).

03 · Coupling ladder

The headline: the protocol core is the trigger's tightest pharmacological neighbourhood

Confidence-weighted Personalized PageRank seeded at Adrenochrome ranks every traced node in one ladder. The protocol core lands in the graph's top-15 — tighter than any adaptive program:

Aminoguanidine #4 (0.01401) · Methylene blue #8 (0.00929) · Carbazochrome #14 (0.00716) · Ascorbic Acid #15 (0.00689) — versus the adaptive programs at #89 / #93 / #305. Reversing the seed, Adrenochrome ranks #3 when walked from Aminoguanidine and #7 from Methylene blue — again tighter in both directions than any adaptive arm (#60 / #58 / #147).

Walk mass already "knows" the protocol

The gap between the quencher/amplifier family (top-15) and the systemic modulation stack (#59–#181) is not noise: the direct-reaction agents inherit the trigger's own walk mass, while the modulators reach it only through intermediate programs and bridges. This is the network's fingerprint-of-role reading, and it re-ranks the "top agents" an empirical practitioner might otherwise assume from the outline alone.

04 · Agent traces

The core pair, then the chemistry pair

Aminoguanidine — the quencher wired by chemistry

AG is the only protocol agent coupled to the trigger by a stored chemical reaction: Aminoguanidine --[reacts_with|0.9]→ Adrenochrome (from the US4501923A preparation patent), a multiplicity-1 direct edge with no bridge. The reaction product is itself a graph node — Adrenochrome monoaminoguanidine (AMM, community #98, size 6) — AG's only shared neighbour with the trigger (Jaccard 0.012; Adamic–Adar 0.558).

Structurally, AG is adaptive-side: Fiedler +0.0021. Effective resistance to the trigger 0.186 (z −1.64 / −1.80). PPR #4 forward, #3 reverse — the single tightest exogenous coupling to adrenochrome measured anywhere in the study.

Methylene blue — the amplifier without a chemistry edge

MB reaches the trigger through three co-shortest paths, bridges Complex I, Oxidative Stress, and Hemolysis — no direct reaction edge. Yet it is the graph's strongest missing-edge proposer to the trigger: Adamic–Adar 2.641, the highest in any trace so far (shared Complex I / Oxidative Stress / Hemolysis neighbourhood), against a Jaccard of 0.031. The stored wiring says why:

Methylene blue --enhances(0.94)--> Mitochondrial Respiration

So MB couples to the trigger through the same redox channels the trigger itself exploits — Complex I --reduces(0.9)--> Adrenochrome and Oxidative Stress --drives(0.95)--> Adrenochrome formation are both stored. Read as an amplifier: MB does not "make" adrenochrome; it presses the very Complex-I / oxidative drivers that do. Fiedler +0.0013 (adaptive side); PPR #8 / #7; effective resistance 0.164 (z −1.68 / −1.70).

MB ⇄ AG share exactly two neighbours: Monoamine oxidase and Nitric Oxide Synthase (Jaccard 0.027)

Both MB and AG each carry an MAO-inhibition edge (0.92 / 0.9) and an NOS-family edge (MB inhibits sGC/NOS; AG blocks NO production and inhibits iNOS while sparing eNOS). The two core agents are not wired to each other chemically in this graph — they are co-agents on shared intermediaries, which is structurally consistent with a two-lever protocol rather than a single reaction cascade.

The chemistry pair — Carbazochrome & Ascorbic Acid

Two more direct edges complete the chemical family around the trigger: Carbazochrome --is_an_intermediate_for(0.95)--> Adrenochrome and Ascorbic Acid --reduces(0.75)--> Adrenochrome (Ascorbate additionally inhibits(0.9) Adrenochrome formation). Both are adaptive-side-opposite: they sit on the trigger side of the seam (Carbazochrome −0.0008, Ascorbic Acid −0.0023), grouping with the redox partners of the o-quinone rather than with the systemic programs. Their shared neighbourhood — effectively only Adrenochrome — gives Jaccard(Carbazochrome, Ascorbic Acid) = 0.143, the highest pairwise overlap in the entire study.

Their coupling depths differ sharply: Ascorbate PPR #15, effective resistance 0.282 (z −1.48); Carbazochrome PPR #14 but effective resistance 0.747 (z −0.70) — the farthest of any direct-edge node, because carbazochrome is a k-core-2 pendant (degree 2) bobbing at the graph's edge inside the AMM community. The direct edge is real; the walk mass is thin.

Three coupling modes, one story

  • Chemical reaction (AG reacts_with) — direct, single-bridge-free, tightest flow.
  • Derivation / reduction (Carbazochrome is_an_intermediate_for, Ascorbate reduces) — direct but flow-thin (pendant / reducer nodes).
  • Functional modulation via shared bridges (MB, and the stack) — no direct edge; rides Complex I / oxidative-stress / ROS circuitry the trigger itself drives.

06 · Structural findings

The direction trap, single-bridge chokes, and the sweep

Three arms, each with a single rate-limiting bridge

Arm A (mitohormetic signalling):  Mitohormesis --requires(0.90)--> ROS --generates(0.95)--> Adrenochrome
Arm B (autophagic clearance):    Autophagy --associated_with(0.95)--> Neuromelanin --sequesters(0.8)--> Adrenochrome
Arm C (sirtuin/NAD+):            Sirtuins --suppress(0.86)--> SASP <--is_regulated_by(0.96)-- NF-κB <--inhibits(0.6, AMBIGUOUS)-- Adrenochrome

Arms A and B are short, high-confidence, and mechanistically direct; Arm C is longer, passes through the graph's top-betweenness node (SASP), and terminates in the quartet's lowest-confidence edge. Run C confirms the baseline: note the stored requires confidence now reads 0.9 (an earlier log printed 0.92) — a small triple drift worth registering in §09.

⚠️ The direction-inversion trap on Arm C (standing)

The analysis script prints NF-κB --[inhibits|0.6]→ Adrenochrome, but the stored triple is the reverse: Adrenochrome —inhibits→ NF-κB (0.6, AMBIGUOUS, from the senotherapeutic-agent document). Context: the senomorphic hypothesis — adrenochrome's electrophilic o-quinone chemistry modifies p65/IKK cysteines, blunting NF-κB-driven SASP. Read correctly, Arm C says sirtuins and adrenochrome are two parallel levers on the same SASP/NF-κB axis, not a production-control chain.

Entity fragmentation depresses the sirtuin arm (standing)

The graph carries ≥ 7 NF-κB variants as separate nodes (NF-κB 48, NF-kB 1, NF-kappaB 2, NF-κB p65, RelA/p65 (NF-κB subunit), NF-kappa B signaling, NF-κB signaling pathway). Fragmentation splits walk mass and contributes to Sirtuins' zero-Jaccard isolation — quantified as remediation Phase 0b. It also deflates the NF-κB-mediated paths of the stack agents (Fisetin via COMT, Resveratrol via SIRT1/SIRT3 are partly independent).

Two results fall out:

  1. Arms A and B behave like true single-bridge chokes — cutting either hop collapses ~20–33% of that arm's coupling while leaving the other arms untouched.
  2. Arm C barely depends on its nominal bridge (−4.7%), and deleting SASP actually increases Mito/Auto→Adrenochrome flow. SASP acts as a competing attractor soaking up walk mass; removing it redistributes flow rather than severing a route. Arm C reads as axis co-regulation, not a production-control chain.

07 · Spectral seam

One seam splits the chemistry family from the program layer

λ₂ (algebraic connectivity) = 0.0463. With every traced node on the same Fiedler axis, the split the four-node study found generalizes:

Trigger side (−): Creatine −0.0024, Ascorbic Acid −0.0023, Adrenochrome −0.0015, Fisetin −0.0010, Carbazochrome −0.0008, Resveratrol −0.0006.

Adaptive side (+): Methylene blue +0.0013, Aminoguanidine +0.0021, Autophagy +0.0033, Urolithin A +0.0033, Mitohormesis +0.0034, Rapamycin +0.0036, Spermidine +0.0037, Sirtuins +0.0043.

Reading: the chemistry layer — the trigger, its prodrug/derivative (carbazochrome), its reducer (ascorbate), and COMT-substrate modulatees (fisetin, resveratrol) — sits on the negative side; the systemic program layer — both protocol core agents (MB, AG) plus the anti-aging stack and all three adaptive programs — sits positive. The protocol thus reaches across the weakest functional seam rather than living on one side of it.

H2 generalized — with a caveat

H2 stated the trigger sits alone opposite the adaptive trio. It generalizes to: the trigger sits opposite the protocol's systemic agents too, but with its own chemical family. Caveat: Fiedler coordinates of peripheral nodes (Carbazochrome degree 2, Ascorbic Acid degree 6) are the least stable under rebuild; a re-run should report sign stability for the low-degree members before over-reading the chemistry-vs-program split.
