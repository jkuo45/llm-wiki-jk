Ivermectin × Fenbendazole — Complementary Anticancer Mechanisms & the Sirtuin Connection Preclinical

Two repurposed antiparasitics attack fundamentally different nodes of cancer biology — ivermectin at kinase / signaling hubs, fenbendazole at structural / metabolic machinery. Their primary mechanisms barely overlap, which is precisely why they are discussed as a potential drug-repositioning combination. This page turns the task-output synthesis into interactive charts, then traces where the sirtuins — the NAD+-coupled metabolic rheostat — intersect the pair.

The complementary thesis: one drug removes signals, the other removes machinery

Complementarity, not redundancy, is the entire basis of the pairing.

Ivermectin — signaling & kinase hubs

Promotes proteasomal degradation of [[PAK1]], a hub upstream of [[Akt]]/[[mTOR]], [[NF-κB]], [[STAT3]] and [[Wnt]]/[[β-Catenin]]; independently inhibits [[YAP1]] (Hippo) and P-gp efflux. Also disrupts mitochondria directly (ΔΨm collapse, [[BAX|Bax]]↑/[[Bcl-2]]↓) and induces three death modes: apoptosis, autophagy, pyroptosis.

Fenbendazole — structural & metabolic machinery

Binds the colchicine site of β-[[Tubulin]] → mitotic arrest; down-regulates [[GLUT1]] and inhibits [[Hexokinase 2]] → Warburg blockade; suppresses [[MDM2]]/MdmX → p53 activation; induces ROS via the MEK3/6–p38 axis.

Why pairing makes sense

Kinase-addicted cells fall to ivermectin; metabolically-addicted cells fall to fenbendazole. Their downstream effects converge on mitochondria, redox, cell cycle and metabolism — from different doors — while ivermectin's P-gp blockade and fenbendazole's NADPH/glutathione collapse make the two a potential dose-sparing, resistance-averse pair.

Antiparasitic origin → repositioned anticancer mechanisms

Ivermectin did not start as an anticancer drug. The graph trace (src/tasks/task_output_ivermectin_cancer_14_JUL_2026.md) recovers the full lineage: a macrocyclic lactone born as a mass-drug-administration anthelmintic pivots, through the fact that humans lack its invertebrate-specific target, into a repurposed anticancer agent. The bridge is pharmacological — one safe scaffold whose vertebrate-sparing dose window leaves headroom to hit conserved mammalian survival kinases.

antiparasitic lineage & mechanism pivot — vertebrate safety margin repositioned mammalian targets honest gap — preclinical only

Antiparasitic origin branch

Ivermectin is a semi-synthetic dihydro derivative of [[Avermectin]], a 16-membered macrocyclic lactone from Streptomyces avermectinius, discovered at the [[Kitasato Institute]] by [[Satoshi Ōmura]] and [[William C. Campbell]] (2015 Nobel Prize). Its anthelmintic mechanism is allosteric agonism of [[Glutamate-gated Cl- channels]] (GluCls) — invertebrate-exclusive channels where Cl− influx hyperpolarizes and paralyzes the parasite.

The pivot — a target humans lack

Humans have no GluCls. Vertebrate GABAA/glycine receptors need roughly ~100× higher ivermectin concentrations, so therapeutic plasma levels spare the host (Glutamate-gated Cl- channels.md:21). This built-in safety margin — underwritten by decades of mass drug administration (OCP, APOC, the Mectizan program) — is what makes repurposing trials medically defensible.

Repositioned mammalian targets

At therapeutic concentrations the same molecule engages mammalian targets instead of GluCls: primary host kinase [[PAK1]] (proteasomal degradation), Hippo-effector [[YAP1]], the survival axis [[Akt]]/[[mTOR]], [[STAT3]] and the efflux pump [[P-gp]]. It induces [[Apoptosis]], [[Autophagy]] and [[Pyroptosis]], drives immunogenic cell death ([[HMGB1]] release), and suppresses [[Cancer Stem Cells]] via the PAK1–STAT3 axis.

Headline finding. The wiki corpus frames ivermectin strictly as a repurposed therapeutic agent, not a primary preventive: every note places it in treatment/adjuvant contexts (inhibiting proliferation, metastasis, angiogenesis; reversing multidrug resistance). No note addresses primary prevention (reducing cancer incidence in healthy people) — the preventive framing is inference beyond the corpus.

Sirtuin intersections — where the pair meets the NAD+ rheostat

The seven sirtuins are NAD+-dependent deacylases whose output is gated by the very redox and metabolic state these drugs perturb. Four hubs intersect the combination in concrete, graph-backed ways: SIRT1 (p53 / NF-κB / PAK1), SIRT2 (tubulin), SIRT3 (mitochondrial ROS) and SIRT6 (anti-Warburg). Green = reinforces the combination; red = opposes / context-dependent.

SIRT1

the p53 / NF-κB / PAK1 rheostat Nucleus + cytosol

Reinforces the combination

  • Deacetylates p53 (Lys379) → restrains its pro-apoptotic output; p53 reciprocally inhibits SIRT1, so fenbendazole's p53 stabilization self-amplifies by lowering SIRT1
  • Deacetylates RelA/p65 (NF-κB) and STAT3 → a third pathway into the survival axes both drugs already suppress
  • Inhibits [[HIF-1α]] and [[mTOR]], deacetylates [[ULK1]] / [[Beclin1|Beclin-1]] / [[Atg5]]-[[Atg7|7]] / [[LC3]] → [[Warburg Effect|anti-Warburg]] + pro-autophagy, aligned with ivermectin's PAK1→Akt→mTOR arm
  • SIRT1-mediated PAK1 deacetylation at K420 hinders glioblastoma growth — the same PAK1 node ivermectin degrades, hit via a separate post-translational route

Opposes / context

  • Chronic SIRT1 activity blunts p53 and drives chemoresistance (tamoxifen via FoxO1/MDR2; cisplatin via VEGF-A)
  • High-SIRT1 tumors blunt fenbendazole's p53 lever; partners [[c-Myc]]/HIF-1α in some contexts

SIRT2

the tubulin node Cytosol

Reinforces the combination

  • A tubulin deacetylase (α-tubulin K40) — it sits on the same cytoskeleton node fenbendazole destabilizes at β-tubulin
  • Inhibits p53 → SIRT2 inhibition deepens fenbendazole's p53 activation alongside MDM2/MdmX suppression
  • Deacetylates NF-κB → aligned with both drugs' NF-κB suppression

Opposes / open question

  • Acetylated α-tubulin marks stable polymers — SIRT2's deacetylase activity governs the very dynamic instability fenbendazole disrupts; directionality vs the microtubule block is unresolved
  • Inhibited by Akt and Wnt (both suppressed by ivermectin) → the combination may indirectly shift SIRT2 activity; whether that helps or hurts is context-dependent

SIRT3

the mitochondrial guardian — a sensitivity switch Mitochondria

Reinforces the combination

  • Low-SIRT3 tumors (~40% of breast/ovarian cancers) run high mitochondrial ROS — a redox-primed state that should sensitize to both drugs' oxidative / mitochondrial attack
  • Destabilizes HIF-1α → anti-Warburg, aligned with fenbendazole's GLUT1/HK2 program

Opposes / context

  • High SIRT3 clears ROS (SOD2/MnSOD, IDH2) and maintains mitochondria (Complex I, PDH) — the exact levers ivermectin collapses (ΔΨm, respiration, ATP)
  • High-SIRT3 cells are the likely resistance context for the pair's ROS-dependent death

SIRT6

fenbendazole's nuclear twin Nucleus (heterochromatin)

Reinforces the combination (strongest intersection)

  • Corepresses HIF-1α and suppresses GLUT1 / HK2 transcription → anti-glycolysis at the transcriptional level, while fenbendazole hits the same gene products at the protein level — one axis, two levels
  • p53 activates SIRT6 → fenbendazole's p53 stabilization feeds back to raise SIRT6, reinforcing glycolysis / NF-κB / HIF-1α suppression (a clean synergy loop)
  • Suppresses NF-κB (H3K9 at target promoters) and inhibits [[JAK2]]/STAT3 → joins both drugs

Opposes / context

  • Requires NAD+ — oxidative-stress PARP consumption may starve it exactly when needed
  • Its austerity / DNA-repair program can confer chemoresistance in established tumors
Secondary isoforms: SIRT4 (glutamine-addiction brake; upregulates p16/p21 — aligned with fenbendazole's p21 arrest) · SIRT5 (glutaminolysis / glycolysis driver — its inhibition would help) · SIRT7 (ribosome biogenesis / cell-cycle driver — oncogenic; less connected to this pair).

The NAD+ gate — redox rewires the sirtuin rheostat mid-treatment

Sirtuins are NAD+-consuming enzymes, and NAD+ is also a glycolytic cofactor (GAPDH). The pair's oxidative and metabolic attack therefore does not just read sirtuin activity — it rewrites the NAD+ pool that gates it. This is the most hypothesis-generating intersection and should be read as a hypothesis, not established fact.

ivermectin contribution fenbendazole contribution NAD+ pool / PARP sirtuin output

Upside of NAD+ drain

Drug-induced oxidative stress → PARP consumes NAD+ → sirtuin output falls. This lowers SIRT1's p53 deacetylation (reinforces fenbendazole), SIRT2's p53 inhibition, and SIRT3's ROS scavenging — amplifying the pair's death signals.

Downside of NAD+ drain

The same drain lowers SIRT6 (anti-Warburg reinforcement lost) and SIRT1 (NF-κB / STAT3 suppression lost) — two of the most desirable anti-tumor sirtuin outputs. Net sirtuin behavior during treatment is a function of redox state, not a constant.

Read-out

Any claim that "the combination boosts sirtuins" or "blocks sirtuins" is over-simplified. The honest framing: the pair shifts the NAD+/NADH and NADPH/glutathione pools, and each sirtuin's sign flips accordingly — a context-dependent, isoform-specific outcome.

Cross-cutting caveats & the bottom line

Everything is preclinical. Every mechanism cited here is from cell lines and xenograft models (Tang et al. 2020; Nguyen et al. 2024). There are no clinical trials of either compound as an anticancer agent. The combination rationale is a mechanistic hypothesis.
The autophagy paradox. Ivermectin's autophagic death is the primary mode in breast cancer but protective in melanoma; context decides whether autophagic flux kills or shields. Dose and tumor type will dictate net effect.
Sirtuins flip sign. SIRT1/3/6 are anti-tumor in some settings and chemoresistance factors in others. The pair's NAD+ drain makes this worse: the same redox stress that empowers p53 / ROS also starves SIRT6 — so "sirtuin support" cannot be prescribed as a blanket strategy.
SIRT2 directionality is unresolved. SIRT2 sits on the tubulin node fenbendazole attacks, but whether its deacetylation of α-tubulin amplifies or counteracts the microtubule block is an open question.
Bottom line. Ivermectin and fenbendazole are a mechanistically complementary pair — signaling hub vs structural/metabolic machinery — with convergent mitochondria, redox, cell-cycle, and metabolic endpoints. The most concrete sirtuin links are SIRT6 (fenbendazole's anti-Warburg twin, with a p53→SIRT6 feedback loop), SIRT1 (p53 / NF-κB / PAK1 rheostat), SIRT3 (mitochondrial ROS sensitivity switch), and SIRT2 (tubulin node) — all gated by the NAD+ pool the pair itself rewrites. Treat the sirtuin dimension as hypothesis-generating until tested in vivo.
