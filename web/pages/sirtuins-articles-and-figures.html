Foundational Review — Michan & Sinclair (2007)

The landmark review that defined sirtuin biology — enzymology, the seven mammalian sirtuins, and their cellular functions.

From: "Sirtuins in mammals: insights into their biological function" (2007) — NIH Public Access manuscript

Figure 1 · Enzymology

Sirtuin Enzymatic Activities

Sirtuins are NAD+-dependent deacetylases and mono-ADP-ribosyl transferases regulating a wide array of proteins involved in metabolism and cell survival. The ε-acetyl-lysine residues of target proteins serve as substrates; deacetylation generates 2′-O-acetyl-ADP-ribose (OAADPr) as a by-product. NAM, nicotinamide.
Hydrolysis of one NAD+ molecule yields one nicotinamide and one OAADPr — the coupling that links sirtuin activity to cellular energy status. For SIRT4 and SIRT6, mono-ADP-ribosyl transfer is the main enzymatic activity.
Source: PMC2753453 ↗
Figure 2 · The Seven Sirtuins

The Seven Mammalian Sirtuins

Mammals have seven sirtuins, SIRT1–7, each characterized by a conserved ~275-residue NAD+-dependent catalytic core that may act preferentially as an ADP-ribosyl transferase (ART) and/or NAD+-dependent deacetylase (DAC), flanked by variable N- and C-terminal sequences. SIRT1, SIRT6 and SIRT7 are predominantly nuclear, SIRT2 cytoplasmic, and SIRT3–SIRT5 mitochondrial.
Reprinted from Frye (Biochem. Biophys. Res. Commun. 273:793–798, 2000) with permission — the canonical map of the sirtuin family and its compartmental organisation.
Source: PMC2753453 ↗
Figure 3 · Phylogeny

Phylogenetic Classification of Mammalian Sirtuins

Phylogenetic analysis of ~60 sirtuin core domains places the mammalian sirtuins into four classes: SIRT1–SIRT3 (Class I), SIRT4 (Class II), SIRT5 (Class III), and SIRT6/SIRT7 (Class IV). Class II, III and U sirtuins appear to have evolved earliest.
SIRT4 and SIRT5 may thus be the most ancient mammalian sirtuins, while Class I and Class IV sirtuins are present only in eukaryotes.
Source: PMC2753453 ↗
Figure 4 · Cell Map

Cellular Functions of Mammalian Sirtuins

Nuclear SIRT1 deacetylates histones H1/H3/H4 as well as p300, p53, MyoD/MEF2, PPAR-γ and Tat; SIRT6 regulates base excision repair in heterochromatin; SIRT7 activates RNA Pol I in the nucleolus; SIRT2 shuttles into the nucleus during mitosis (H4K16). Cytosolic SIRT1 activates AceCS1; mitochondrial SIRT3 activates AceCS2, and SIRT4 ADP-ribosylates GDH to inhibit amino-acid-stimulated insulin secretion.
The classic "cell map" of the sirtuin field — one family, three compartments, dozens of substrates — still frames how sirtuin outputs are organised today.
Source: PMC2753453 ↗
Figure 5 · Apoptosis & Survival

SIRT1 in Apoptosis & Cell Survival

SIRT1 deacetylates p53, Ku70, E2F1, p73 and FOXO factors to suppress apoptosis and promote survival. The mapped pathways include p53/HIC1 feedback, Ku70-mediated Bax sequestration, FOXO-driven stress resistance, and inhibition of NF-κB (RelA/p65) transactivation.
Whether SIRT1 acts as a tumour suppressor or an oncogene in vivo was unresolved in 2007 — the question that launched the sirtuin–cancer field.
Source: PMC2753453 ↗
Figure 6 · Neuroanatomy

SIRT1 & SIRT2 Localize Differentially in the Brain

Immunofluorescence in cortex, hippocampus and striatum shows SIRT1 (green) co-localizing with the neuronal marker NeuN in neurons, whereas SIRT2 does not label neurons — it co-localizes with myelinated axonal fibres and oligodendrocytes in white matter.
The distinct expression patterns foreshadowed functions beyond metabolism: SIRT1 in neuroprotection and SIRT2 in myelination/glial biology — and their later links to neurodegeneration.
Source: PMC2753453 ↗

Network Diagrams

Integrated views of the sirtuin regulatory network across cellular compartments and NAD+ metabolism.

From: "The Sirtuin Network: Linking NAD+ Metabolism, Mitochondrial Function, and Metabolic Homoeostasis" (2025)

Figure 1 · Compartment Map

The Compartmentalised Sirtuin Network

Nuclear sirtuins (SIRT1, SIRT6, SIRT7) orchestrate transcriptional programs controlling metabolism and inflammation. Mitochondrial sirtuins (SIRT3, SIRT4, SIRT5) directly regulate metabolic flux and redox homoeostasis. Cytosolic SIRT2 connects metabolic signalling with cytoskeletal and cell-cycle processes. NAD+ metabolism dynamically modulates sirtuin activity across compartments.
Central to this system, NAD+ metabolism enables coupling of cellular energy status to coordinated metabolic responses across glucose/lipid metabolism, inflammatory signalling, and mitochondrial function.
Source: PMC13364290 ↗
Figure 2 · NAD+ Metabolism

NAD+ as Central Regulator of Sirtuin Activity

NAD+ is produced through de novo (tryptophan → kynurenine), Preiss–Handler (nicotinic acid), and salvage (nicotinamide → NMN via NAMPT) pathways. Beyond its role as a redox cofactor, NAD+ serves as essential substrate for sirtuins, directly linking its availability to their enzymatic activity across compartments.
Changes in NAD+ levels across cellular compartments dynamically regulate the sirtuin network, influencing mitochondrial function, metabolic pathways, and stress responses. Age-related NAD+ decline (via CD38 upregulation) compromises sirtuin function.
Source: PMC13364290 ↗
Figure 2 · Disease Network

SIRT1, SIRT3, and SIRT6 Attenuate Age-Associated Diseases

Schematic overview of how SIRT1, SIRT3, and SIRT6 protect against neurodegeneration, metabolic dysfunction, inflammation, cancer, and cardiovascular disease. Tumor suppressor and oncogenic properties have been reported for each protein. SIRT1 and SIRT6 overexpression extend longevity in mice.
This figure from the landmark Giblin et al. (2014) review summarizes the evidence that mammalian sirtuins act as "guardians of healthspan" across the major hallmarks of ageing.
Source: PMC4077918 ↗

Chromatin Biology & DNA Repair

Sirtuins as epigenetic regulators — deacetylating histones to promote heterochromatin formation, telomere maintenance, and DNA damage repair.

From: "Sirtuins, a promising target in slowing down the ageing process" (2017) & "Rejuvenating Sirtuins" (2013)

Figure 1 · Chromatin

Role of SIRT1 and SIRT6 in Chromatin Condensation

Both sirtuins promote heterochromatin formation through three mechanisms: (1) deacetylation of H3K9 enabling its trimethylation and HP1α binding, (2) SIRT1 decreases p300 histone acetyltransferase activity, and (3) SIRT1 activates SUV39H1 methyltransferase by deacetylating K266. Arrows = positive regulation; T-bars = inhibition. Thick arrows indicate changes during ageing.
H3K9 deacetylation by SIRT1 and SIRT6 is essential for heterochromatin formation. Loss of this function during ageing contributes to genomic instability, aberrant gene expression, and cellular senescence.
Source: PMC5514220 ↗
Figure 3 · Interactome

Sirtuin Protein Interacting Partners & Substrates

Examples of sirtuin interacting partners and substrates relevant to the pathways outlined in the review: metabolism (PGC-1α, FOXO, NF-κB), chromatin biology (H3K9, H3K56, H4K16), DNA repair (Ku70, WRN, CtIP), cell survival (p53, survivin, c-MYC), and mitochondrial function (SOD2, IDH2).
The overlapping substrate usage among SIRT1, SIRT3, and SIRT6 — sometimes with opposing functional outcomes — underscores the context-dependent nature of sirtuin biology and the difficulty of pan-sirtuin therapeutic strategies.
Source: PMC4077918 ↗

Lifespan & Healthspan

Key experimental evidence for sirtuin-mediated lifespan extension in mice — brain-specific SIRT1 and whole-body SIRT6 overexpression.

From: "Sirtuins: Guardians of Mammalian Healthspan" (2014)

Figure 1 · Lifespan

SIRT1 & SIRT6 Overexpression Extend Mouse Lifespan

Panel A: SIRT1 overexpressed in dorsomedial/lateral hypothalamic nuclei (DMH/LH) extends lifespan of both sexes by upregulating the orexin type 2 receptor (OX2R) via deacetylation of NK2 homeobox 1 (NKX2-1). Panel B: Whole-body SIRT6 overexpression extends male lifespan by inhibiting IGF-1/AKT signaling cascade, including downstream pAKT, pFOXO1, pFOXO3, and pS6 kinase.
Brain-specific SIRT1 overexpression (BRASTO mice) increases median lifespan ~11% and mimics calorie restriction. SIRT6 overexpression extends male median lifespan by 14.5% and 9.9% in two independent strains — a male-specific effect linked to reduced serum IGF-1 levels.
Source: PMC4077918 ↗

Protein Structures

Crystal structures of human sirtuin isoforms resolved by X-ray crystallography, revealing the conserved Rossmann-fold domain, zinc-binding domain, and cofactor/substrate binding sites.

From: "Sirtuins: Shedding light on structure, function and regulation" (2023) & "Rejuvenating Sirtuins" (2013)

Figure 1 · SIRT1

Structure of SIRT1 (PDB: 4IG9)

The NAD+ binding region consists of a core six-stranded parallel β-sheet (strands β1–3, β7–9) and eight helices. The helical module (αC–F) and Zn2+-binding unit (β4–6, αI) form the smaller domain. The C-terminal regulatory region (CTR) creates a β-hairpin covering a hydrophobic core.
SIRT1 has strong deacetylase activity and modulates anti-ageing signalling with AMPK. It promotes healthy ageing, protects against metabolic-syndrome-linked cancers, and delays neurodegeneration.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 2 · SIRT2

Structure of SIRT2 (PDB: 3ZGO)

The catalytic core is an elongated structure with a larger Rossmann-fold domain (six β-strands, six α-helices) and a smaller domain comprising a helical module (α3–α6) and a zinc-binding region (β4–6, α9). A large groove at the domain intersection forms the active site cleft.
SIRT2 has both mono-ADP-ribosyl transferase and deacetylase activity. It controls metabolic reprogramming during induced pluripotency and mediates tubulin acetylation. Upregulated in NSCLC and metastatic melanoma.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 3 · SIRT3

Structure of SIRT3 (PDB: 3GLS)

SIRT3 has a two-domain structure: a large Rossmann-fold domain for NAD+ binding and a smaller domain consisting of a helical bundle and zinc-binding region created by two extending loops from the large domain. A groove between the zinc-binding and helical bundle may participate in protein–protein interactions.
The principal mitochondrial deacetylase. Full-length SIRT3 is inactive; cleavage of the N-terminal 101 residues by mitochondrial processing peptidase activates it. Suppresses tumor growth via HIF-1α regulation and ROS suppression.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 4 · SIRT5

Structure of SIRT5 (PDB: 2NYR)

Made up of 14 α-helices and 9 β-strands arranged in two globular domains. The larger Rossmann-fold domain contains a core β-sheet of six parallel strands and nine α-helices. The smaller zinc-binding domain has a unique insertion: helix α9 and a 16-residue preceding loop specific to human SIRT5.
SIRT5 has weak deacetylase activity but is a potent desuccinylase/demalonylase/deglutarylase. Its deacetylation activity is unusually resistant to nicotinamide inhibition — relevant for drug design.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 5 · SIRT6

Structure of SIRT6 (PDB: 3K35)

Two globular domains with eight α-helices and nine β-strands. The Rossmann-fold domain features a six-stranded parallel β-sheet. The smaller zinc-binding domain is formed by two extending loops. Notably, SIRT6 lacks the cofactor-binding loop found in other sirtuins — replaced by a single helix (α3) with NAD+-binding residues.
SIRT6 is a mono-ADP-ribosyl transferase essential for telomere maintenance, DNA repair, and inhibition of glycolysis and ROS. Its compact small domain (no helix bundle) may underlie its unique NAD+ binding properties.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 6 · SIRT7

Structure of SIRT7 (PDB: 5IQZ)

The SIRT7 N-terminal domain (NTD) consists of three helices (α1–α3) connected by a short 310 helix. α1 is the longest helix (35 residues, Gly5–Arg37). α2 and α3 are nearly perpendicular to α1. The NTD shows structural similarities to transcription factor regulators and may participate in DNA binding.
SIRT7 has deacetylase activity critical for ageing and hematopoietic cell lifespan. It regulates rDNA transcription, pre-rRNA cleavage, and acts as a heterochromatin stabiliser counteracting stem cell ageing.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 1 · SIRT3 Complex

X-ray Structure of SIRT3 with Acetyl-CoA Synthetase 2

Human SIRT3 in complex with ADPR bound to the Acetyl-coenzyme A synthetase 2 (AceCS2) peptide containing thioacetyl lysine (PDB: 3GLT). Color-coded: red = Rossmann-fold domain, yellow = zinc-binding domain, green = cofactor-binding loop. Surfaces of substrate-binding (purple) and NAD+-binding (light blue) sites are highlighted.
This co-crystal structure reveals the productive binding mode of the acetyl-lysine substrate and NAD+ cofactor in the extended cleft between the two domains — a template for structure-based drug design.
Source: PMC3549556 ↗

Pharmacology — Activators & Inhibitors

Chemical structures of small molecules that modulate sirtuin activity, from natural polyphenols to synthetic STACs and inhibitors.

From: "Sirtuins: Shedding light on structure" & "Rejuvenating Sirtuins" & "The Sirtuin System: The Holy Grail of Resveratrol?"

Figure 7 · Activators

Sirtuin Activating Compounds (STACs)

Structures of five sirtuin activators: (a) buteīn, (b) isoliquiritigenin, (c) piceatannol, (d) resveratrol, and (e) SRT1720. The first four are natural polyphenols; SRT1720 is a synthetic STAC with substantially more potent SIRT1 activation.
Plant polyphenols activate recombinant SIRT1. Resveratrol replicates calorie restriction effects in lower organisms and improves insulin resistance in HFD mice. Synthetic STACs (imidazo[1,2-b]thiazole core) activate SIRT1 more potently.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 8 · Inhibitors

Sirtuin Inhibiting Compounds (STICs)

Structures of seven sirtuin inhibitors: (a) Splitomicin, (b) HR73, (c) Sirtinol, (d) AGK2, (e) Cambinol, (f) Salermide, and (g) Suramin. Most inhibit SIRT1 and/or SIRT2; some also affect SIRT3 and SIRT5.
Up-regulated SIRT1 in cancer cell lines makes inhibitors potential therapeutics. Suramin inhibits SIRT1/SIRT2 at nanomolar concentrations. Cambinol is effective against Burkitt lymphoma. Salermide causes p53-independent apoptosis in cancer cells.
Source: PMC9805487 ↗
Figure 1 · Resveratrol & CAD

Resveratrol's Effects on Coronary Artery Disease Risk

Comprehensive diagram summarizing the proposed mechanisms by which resveratrol influences factors contributing to coronary artery disease (CAD). Resveratrol modifies a large number of atherogenic pathways including oxidative stress, inflammation, lipid metabolism, endothelial function, and platelet aggregation.
The "French Paradox" — reduced CVD in French populations with moderate wine consumption — motivated research into resveratrol as a cardioprotective polyphenol. It is a known SIRT1 activator with anti-proliferative and anti-inflammatory properties.
Source: PMC3613783 ↗

Substrates & Chemical Probes

Chemical structures of sirtuin inhibitors and activators from the cancer drug discovery perspective, covering diverse scaffolds identified through screening, rational design, and natural product analysis.

From: "Rejuvenating Sirtuins: The Rise of a New Family of Cancer Drug Targets" (2013)

Figure 2 · Inhibitors

Sirtuin Inhibitor Chemical Space

Structural diversity of sirtuin inhibitors spanning: nicotinamide (endogenous product inhibitor), sirtinol, salermide, cambinol, splitomicin, kinase inhibitors (Ro31-8220, GW5074), EX-527 (nanomolar-selective SIRT1 inhibitor), suramin (nanomolar dual SIRT1/SIRT2), and NF675 (20-fold SIRT1 selectivity).
Inhibitors bind different sirtuin pockets depending on the isoform and scaffold. EX-527 is the most potent and selective SIRT1 inhibitor known. Suramin occupies the nicotinamide ribose pocket (B-pocket), C-pocket, and part of the substrate-binding site (co-crystal with SIRT5).
Source: PMC3549556 ↗
Figure 3 · Activators

Sirtuin Activating Compounds (STACs)

Natural polyphenolic activators (1–5): resveratrol, piceatannol, buteīn, quercetin, and myricetin. Synthetic STACs (6–8): SRT1460, SRT1720, and SRT2183 — chemically diverse and more potent than the natural compounds. Non-polyphenolic allosteric activators have also been reported.
Controversy: SIRT1 activation by these compounds was often only demonstrated with fluorescently tagged substrates. Resveratrol's in vivo effects may be indirect via the cAMP–Epac1–AMPK pathway rather than direct SIRT1 binding. The specific amino acid in SIRT1 targeted by STACs has been identified — mutation at this site abolishes STAC response.
Source: PMC3549556 ↗

Fig 1 · You & Wang 2025

The Multidimensional SIRT3 Regulatory Network

SIRT3 sits at the center of mitochondrial homeostasis: (A) as a redox regulator it activates the antioxidant enzymes MnSOD2, catalase, GPX and PRDX against ROS; (B) it supports DNA repair through the NHEJ pathway in concert with PARP1, Ku70/80, XRCC1 and DNA ligase 4; (C) it modulates apoptosis by deacetylating Bcl-2, delaying cytochrome c release and downstream caspase activation; (D) it integrates metabolic flux by promoting TCA-cycle activity, fatty-acid oxidation, pyruvate-to-lactate conversion, and ketone-body production.
Fills the "SIRT3 longevity untested in vivo" gap with the 2025 state of the field: SIRT3 exerts multimodal control over mitochondria, redox and epigenetics — yet no SIRT3-overexpressing lifespan study has been reported, so the in vivo longevity question remains open.
Source: PMC12374834 ↗
Fig 3 · You & Wang 2025

SIRT3 in Aging-Related Pathologies

Panel (A) links SIRT3 to cellular senescence itself: aging-associated mitochondrial dysfunction and nuclear instability are countered by SIRT3's control of nuclear architecture, chromatin remodeling and proteostasis, which influences senescence, stem-cell exhaustion and genome instability. Further panels cover neurodegeneration and age-related hearing loss (B), renal pathophysiology (C), cardiovascular protection via PINK1-mediated mitophagy (D), metabolic dysregulation (E) and oncogenic processes (F).
Directly relevant to senescence biology: the cellular-senescence panel (A) frames SIRT3 as a modulator of the senescence program rather than a passive bystander of mitochondrial aging.
Source: PMC12374834 ↗
Fig 1 · Dikalova 2020

Sirt3 Overexpression Protects Against Ang II Hypertension

The global Sirt3-overexpressing (Sirt3OX) transgenic mouse — the closest existing SIRT3 gain-of-function model — was infused with angiotensin II (0.7 mg/kg/day): telemetric systolic blood pressure stayed lower in Sirt3OX mice than in wild-type littermates (insert: aortic Sirt3 immunoblot). Sirt3OX also preserved endothelial-dependent relaxation to acetylcholine, reduced aortic superoxide (DHE-HPLC) and maintained endothelial nitric oxide bioavailability (EPR).
Provides the in vivo gain-of-function evidence missing from SIRT3 research: Sirt3 protects vascular function under hypertensive stress, but no lifespan data exist for this model — the "SIRT3 longevity in vivo" gap remains open.
Source: PMC7035170 ↗
Fig 6 · Dikalova 2020

Sirt3 Sets Vascular Senescence Markers

Western blots of aortic TERT, p21 and SA-β-gal in 6-month-old wild-type, Sirt3−/− and Sirt3OX mice: Sirt3 deletion raised p21 and SA-β-gal while lowering telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT); Sirt3 overexpression reversed this pattern. Systolic blood pressure was also elevated in Sirt3−/− mice at baseline.
A direct link between a sirtuin and the senescence phenotype in the vessel wall — SA-β-gal and p21 are the canonical senescence markers of vascular aging.
Source: PMC7035170 ↗
Fig 7 · Wu 2024

Succinylomics Pins CPT2 as SIRT5's FAO Substrate

Integrated metabolomics and succinylomics of diabetic (HFD/STZ) hearts: Sirt5−/− myocardium showed hundreds of hyper-succinylated peptides enriched in mitochondrial fatty-acid oxidation (FAO) enzymes (KEGG). The analysis flowchart (D) and tables (E) converge on CPT2; immunoprecipitation confirmed increased CPT2 succinylation with reduced CPT2 enzymatic activity in Sirt5−/− hearts.
Fills the "SIRT5-specific disease mechanisms" gap: SIRT5 desuccinylates CPT2 to sustain fatty-acid oxidation, preventing lipotoxic cardiomyopathy — [[CPT2]] is suggested as a new entity note for the vault.
Source: PMC11091707 ↗
Fig 8 · Wu 2024

CPT2 Lys424 Succinylation Drives Cardiac Lipotoxicity

Mass spectrometry identified Lys424 as the key succinylated site on CPT2 (conserved across species). Adenoviral expression of the succinylation-resistant CPT2 K424R mutant in Sirt5−/− hearts restored CPT2 activity, rescued FAO measured by Seahorse, and reduced lipid droplets and triglyceride content in cardiomyocytes under palmitate stress.
The K424R rescue is the causal proof: a single succinylation site mediates SIRT5-dependent FAO control in diabetic cardiomyopathy — a mechanistic, not merely correlative, fill for the SIRT5 gap.
Source: PMC11091707 ↗
Fig 3 · Li 2024

SIRT5 Desuccinylates TOM1 at K48

In hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)-injured cardiomyocytes, SIRT5 overexpression lowered and knockdown raised TOM1 succinylation; exogenous and endogenous co-immunoprecipitation showed a direct SIRT5–TOM1 interaction. The succinylation site was mapped to K48, and the K48R mutant abolished SIRT5-dependent regulation of TOM1 protein stability (CHX chase).
The first mechanistic SIRT5–autophagy link in the heart: TOM1 desuccinylation couples SIRT5 to autophagic flux — [[TOM1]] is suggested as a new entity note.
Source: PMC11363360 ↗
Fig 5 · Li 2024

SIRT5 Alleviates Myocardial Injury In Vivo

In rat models of myocardial infarction / ischemia-reperfusion injury, SIRT5 overexpression shrank the TTC-stained infarct area and improved myocardial histology (H&E). Western blots of the four treatment groups show SIRT5 raising TOM1 and the LC3-II/I ratio while lowering TOM1-succ and p62 — consistent with autophagy activation in the injured myocardium.
In vivo validation that SIRT5's desuccinylase activity translates into cardioprotection, connecting the SIRT5–autophagy axis to cardiac mitophagy and the sirtuin–heart disease literature.
Source: PMC11363360 ↗
Fig 2 · Lagunas-Rangel 2025

Sirtuins That Promote Mitophagy

Positive regulators: SIRT1 activates FOXO1/FOXO3 to induce mitochondrial-degradation genes; SIRT3 enhances mitophagy via FOXO3, deacetylates TFAM (which interacts with LC3 to seed mitophagosome formation), and supports PARKIN recruitment by promoting HK2 dissociation from VDAC1 and deacetylating ATP5IF1; SIRT6 acts indirectly by activating AMPK and thereby FOXO3.
Fills the "SIRT6/SIRT7 in autophagy & mitophagy" gap with a systematic overview — the PINK1/[[PARKIN]] axis is suggested as a new entity note.
Source: PMC12594717 ↗
Fig 4 · Lagunas-Rangel 2025

Differential Sirtuin Roles in Mitophagy

A family-level map: SIRT1, SIRT3 and SIRT6 generally act as positive regulators of mitophagy, while SIRT2, SIRT4, SIRT5 and SIRT7 more commonly limit it. The subcellular localization of each sirtuin is depicted (nuclear, cytosolic, mitochondrial), highlighting compartment-specific functions.
The polarity map directly informs the under-studied isoform gaps: SIRT4 and SIRT7 emerge as inhibitory nodes whose mechanistic details "need further study".
Source: PMC12594717 ↗
Fig 2 · Raza 2024

Aging Declines SIRT7 Across Tissues

Aging lowers SIRT7 expression and activity in multiple tissues and cell types — brain, heart, lungs, liver, colon, skin, leukocytes, and stem cells (hair follicle stem cells, hematopoietic stem cells and neural stem cells).
Anchor evidence for the "SIRT7 under-studied in aging" gap: SIRT7 decline is a conserved feature of organismal aging, motivating its investigation in cellular senescence.
Source: PMC11281815 ↗
Fig 10 · Raza 2024

Sirt7 Deficiency Accelerates Murine Aging

Sirt7 knockout mice age prematurely with a disease-associated shorter lifespan; defects and disorders are observed across brain, heart, liver, kidney, adipose tissue, colon, bone, reproductive organs, blood, hair and ears.
The in vivo aging evidence for SIRT7: unlike SIRT3, SIRT7 has a loss-of-function lifespan phenotype in mice — a key contrast for the vault's sirtuin longevity notes.
Source: PMC11281815 ↗
Fig 4 · Tran 2025

SIRT7 Loss Sends NUCKS1 to Chromatin in Senescence

In IMR90 fibroblasts, SIRT7 immunoprecipitates with the HMG-box transcription factor NUCKS1. In etoposide-induced senescence — when SIRT7 is degraded by the E3 ligase TRIP12 — NUCKS1 translocates into the nucleus and onto chromatin (immunofluorescence, compartmental proteomics, chromatin fractionation), correlating with increased NUCKS1 acetylation.
A molecular explanation for the senescent epigenome: SIRT7 loss during senescence promotes NUCKS1 acetylation and chromatin binding — [[NUCKS1]] is suggested as a new entity note.
Source: PMC12225685 ↗
Fig 5 · Tran 2025

NUCKS1 Depletion Delays Senescence

Knockdown of NUCKS1 in IMR90 cells reduced β-galactosidase positivity after etoposide treatment and lowered IL-8 expression; RNA-seq showed 2,738 differentially expressed genes enriched in NF-κB (RELA) and CEBPβ targets, and NUCKS1 acetylation mimetics raised IL-6, IL-8 and MMP3.
Functional proof that the SIRT7–NUCKS1 axis drives the inflammatory SASP — a central theme in sirtuin–senescence research — and that SIRT7 loss is a trigger rather than a consequence of senescence.
Source: PMC12225685 ↗
Graphical Abstract · Zhang 2024

The SIRT7–KLF4 Axis in Endothelial Homeostasis

In healthy pulmonary artery endothelial cells, SIRT7 deacetylates KLF4 at K228, stabilizing it and maintaining endothelial homeostasis and pulmonary vascular function. In pulmonary hypertension, SIRT7 is reduced, KLF4 acetylation rises and KLF4 is ubiquitinated and degraded — driving endothelial dysfunction and vascular remodeling.
A defined tissue-specific SIRT7 mechanism — links to the existing [[Klf4]] entity note and fills the "SIRT7 cardiovascular" gap with a therapeutically targetable axis.
Source: PMC10981524 ↗
Fig 1 · Zhang 2024

SIRT7 Deficiency Exacerbates Pulmonary Hypertension

SIRT7 mRNA is reduced in lung tissue of pulmonary-arterial-hypertension patients (GEO datasets GSE113439, GSE117261) and in Sugen5416/hypoxia (SuHx) mouse lungs. Sirt7 knockout and endothelial-targeted AAV-shSirt7 knockdown worsened right ventricular systolic pressure, the Fulton index and pulmonary arterial wall thickness in SuHx mice.
Human + mouse evidence that SIRT7 loss is causal in PH — fills the SIRT7 cardiovascular gap with both loss- and gain-of-function data (AAV-Sirt7 delivery and NR treatment reverse the phenotype).
Source: PMC10981524 ↗
Fig 1 · Yang 2023

SIRT2 Is Elevated in Failing Human Hearts

SIRT1, SIRT2, SIRT3 and SIRT6 levels in mouse hearts after trans-aortic constriction: SIRT2 rose while the other sirtuins did not. SIRT2 protein was also increased in explanted human hearts from patients with dilated cardiomyopathy and with ischemic heart failure compared with healthy controls.
Directly relevant to the "SIRT2 context-dependence" gap: SIRT2 is the only sirtuin consistently elevated in failing hearts, supporting its maladaptive role under cardiac stress.
Source: PMC10558204 ↗
Fig 6 · Yang 2023

Nrf2 Deletion Reverses Protection; AGK2 Protects the Heart

After ischemia-reperfusion, ejection fraction and fractional shortening were better in Sirt2−/− mice but returned to wild-type levels in Sirt2−/−/Nrf2−/− double knockouts — Nrf2 is the protective mediator. Pharmacological inhibition with the selective SIRT2 inhibitor AGK2 (40 mg/kg, twice weekly, protocol in C) improved echo measures of cardiac remodeling after TAC.
The pharmacological arm fills the SIRT2 gap's therapeutic side: SIRT2 inhibition — not just deletion — protects against hypertrophy and maladaptive remodeling.
Source: PMC10558204 ↗
Fig 2 · Grzeczka 2025

SIRT5's PTM Network and Its Failures

Diagram of the consequences of aberrant post-translational modifications in SIRT5's substrate network: failed regulation of SOD1 (superoxide dismutase 1) weakens antioxidant defense; SDHA (succinate dehydrogenase subunit A) and GCDH (glutaryl-CoA dehydrogenase) defects reduce ATP production; GAPDH dysregulation disturbs carbohydrate metabolism.
Review-level synthesis for the "SIRT5 understudied in disease" gap: SIRT5's desuccinylase/demalonylase/deglutarylase network is central to cardiac energy homeostasis.
