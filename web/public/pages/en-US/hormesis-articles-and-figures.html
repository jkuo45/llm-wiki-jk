The bibliometric-scale review of hormesis in disease: two centuries of history from Schulz to Calabrese, publication and citation dynamics, collaboration networks, journal and keyword landscapes, hotspot mapping, and applications across environment, biology and medicine.

Two Centuries of Hormesis: Milestones

Developmental history of hormesis from Paracelsus ("the dose makes the poison") through Schulz's yeast stimulation, Southam & Ehrlich (1943), Luckey's radiation hormesis, to Calabrese's modern biphasic framework (stimulatory zone ~0.1–10× NOAEL) — the temporal milestones that turned dismissed anomalies into a general biological principle.

POS & Conditioning — Oliveira et al. (2018)

"Preparation for oxidative stress" — the transient antioxidant surge (SOD, catalase, GST) that hypoxia-tolerant animals mount across 8 phyla — argued as a new case of physiological conditioning hormesis, with explicit hormetic-zone criteria (≥10% inverted-U or ≥3% J-curve with return).

From: "Is 'Preparation for Oxidative Stress' a Case of Physiological Conditioning Hormesis?" (Oliveira et al., Front. Physiol. 9:945, 2018)

Figure 1 · Oliveira 2018

POS Mechanism & the Hormetic Zone

(A) Under hypoxia the mitochondrial electron-transport redox state shifts reduced, favoring superoxide production (electron availability, not O2, is limiting); redox imbalance inhibits phosphatases (PTP1B, PTEN) and engages kinases, activating redox-sensitive transcription factors (NF-κB, FoxOs, Nrf2) and phosphorylating antioxidant enzymes. (B) Limited insult (hypoxia, freezing, dehydration, estivation) opens the hormetic zone with maximum protection at "conditioned re-oxygenation"; longer/stronger exposure flips into the harmful zone.
Counter-intuitive core claim verified across scallops (SOD +15–50% at 12 h, −40–60% by day 7–21), carp and shrimp (clean inverted-U curves): hypoxia increases oxidant formation, and the antioxidant surge is the hormetic response — graphics adapted from Servier Medical Art.
Source: PMC6082956 ↗

Dietary Hormesis & Hormetins — NPJ Sci Food (2025), Brosimine B (2025)

The safest, most accessible hormesis: whole-food matrices of hormetins (resveratrol, curcumin and kin) whose cumulative effect exceeds isolated compounds — quantified end-to-end in one exemplar, Brosimine B, from chemical structure through biphasic viability curves to OGD neuroprotection via ROS suppression and catalase rescue.

From: "Dietary hormesis: beyond nutrition and energy supply" (npj Sci. Food, 2025) & "Brosimine B and the biphasic dose-response" (Fonseca et al., Front. Pharmacol., 2025)

Figure 1 · Dietary Hormesis 2025

Food's Tripartite Role — Nutrition, Energy, Hormesis

Food serves three functions: providing nutrients, supplying energy, and — via hormetins embedded in the food matrix — triggering adaptive stress responses. The matrix (fiber, co-constituents) modulates hormetin bioavailability and metabolism, so whole-food effects differ from isolated-compound assays (BioRender illustration).
Conceptual anchor for the [[Dietary Hormesis]] ↔ [[Hormetin]] ↔ [[Brosimine B]] entity chain: whole-food context → chemical units → quantified exemplar, in that order on this page.
Source: PMC12280001 ↗
Figure 1 · Fonseca 2025

Brosimine B: The Molecule

Chemical structure of Brosimine B, isolated from ethanolic extract (1:10 m/v) of Brosimum acutifolium bark after 48 h percolating maceration in 96° ethanol — the hormetin whose full dose-response the paper quantifies.
Identity first: every downstream curve (viability, ROS, catalase) is anchored to this structure and extraction protocol — the reproducibility unit of dietary-hormesis claims.
Source: PMC12012618 ↗
Figure 2 · Fonseca 2025

Biphasic Viability: 1–100 µM

Retinal cells in normal culture treated with 1, 5, 10, 25, 50 and 100 µM Brosimine B for 24 h: viability rises at low doses and falls at high doses — the textbook inverted-U, measured point by point.
The raw observation before modeling: stimulation and inhibition coexist in one titration, ruling out monotonic toxicity or tonic benefit.
Source: PMC12012618 ↗
Figure 3 · Fonseca 2025

Fitting the Hormetic Curve: Peak at 10.2 µM

Hormetic model fit to the viability data (blue observed points): baseline 0.715, amplitude 0.217, peak concentration x0 = 10.2 µM, spread σ = 6.5 µM — x0 marks maximal stimulation and σ the width of the hormetic zone.
Quantification is what separates hormesis from hand-waving: four fitted parameters turn "low stimulates, high inhibits" into a testable window (peak ± spread) for the neuroprotection assays that follow.
Source: PMC12012618 ↗
Figure 4 · Fonseca 2025

OGD Protection at the Hormetic Peak

Embryonic retinal cells subjected to oxygen-glucose deprivation (OGD) lose viability at 3, 6 and 24 h; 10 µM Brosimine B — the fitted peak — rescues viability (107.0% ± 6.2% at 3 h, p < 0.0001; protection persisting at 6 h), translating the normoxic hormetic peak into ischemic protection.
The payoff of fitting first: the protective dose was predicted by the curve, not found by screening — hormetic modeling as experimental design.
Source: PMC12012618 ↗
Figure 5 · Fonseca 2025

ROS Suppression (A–C: 3, 6, 24 h)

10 µM Brosimine B lowers ROS in OGD cultures — significantly at 3 h (269.0% ± 54.0% vs. OGD control), with 6 h and 24 h panels completing the time course — implicating redox modulation in the protective mechanism.
Mechanism layer one: the hormetin blunts the very oxidant burst that POS physiology (previous section) treats as the hormetic signal — protection via pre-tuned redox, not ROS elimination per se.
Source: PMC12012618 ↗
Figure 6 · Fonseca 2025

Catalase Rescue (A–C: 3, 6, 24 h)

OGD depresses catalase activity (56.2% ± 6.1% at the nadir, p < 0.001); 10 µM Brosimine B restores it (124.0% ± 17.2%), with panels A–C spanning 3, 6 and 24 h — endogenous antioxidant defense, not just ROS scavenging, carries the protection.
Direct echo of the POS story: catalase induction as the conserved hormetic effector, here driven by a dietary molecule in mammalian retina rather than by hypoxia in molluscs — and the vault's [[Brosimine B]] ↔ [[Preparation for Oxidative Stress]] link made quantitative.
Source: PMC12012618 ↗

Trained Immunity — Kim et al. (2025)

DAMP-driven innate memory as dose-dependent hormesis: low/acute DAMP exposure trains (mTORC1–HIF-1α–glycolysis, H3K4me3/H3K27ac), high/chronic exposure tolerizes (AMPK–SIRT1–autophagy, H3K9me3) — with central (bone-marrow) and peripheral (tissue) arms, DAMP-specific signaling routes, and a DAMP × receptor × metabolism reference table.

From: "DAMP-driven trained immunity: metabolic and epigenetic reprogramming in critical illness and chronic inflammation" (Kim et al., Front. Immunol. 16:1669054, 2025)

Figure 1 · Kim 2025

Central vs. Peripheral Training

DAMPs (trauma, burns, ischemia) imprint memory two ways — central (left): bone-marrow HSPCs reprogrammed via IL-1β/oxidized lipids into myeloid-biased, pro-inflammatory output for months; peripheral (right): tissue monocytes/macrophages (heme, HMGB1, uric acid) rewire metabolism and chromatin, responding stronger to re-infection or reinjury (BioRender).
Timescale is the hormetic dimension here: the same DAMP imprints weeks-long marrow memory and days-long tissue memory — dose × duration × compartment jointly set train-vs-tolerize.
Source: PMC12682684 ↗
Figure 2 · Kim 2025

Priming, Training, Tolerance — Kinetics

(A) Priming: transient, re-stimulation before the first program ends gives an enhanced immediate response. (B) Trained immunity (TRIM): persists after return to metabolic homeostasis — delayed re-challenge augmented. (C) Tolerance: same timescale as priming but re-stimulation blunted; LPS tolerization is canonical.
The classification the whole review hangs on — and a hormetic reframe: priming/training/tolerance are points on one dose–time surface, with β-glucan even reversing LPS tolerance.
Source: PMC12682684 ↗
Figure 3 · Kim 2025

Enhancer Grammar of Memory

Chromatin states encoding memory: highly accessible + H3K4me3/H3K27ac at enhancer and promoter = actively transcribing; primed enhancers (H3K4me1, little H3K27ac) = poised for rapid activation, the trained signature; latent enhancers (H3K27me3/closed) = silent or tolerance-associated. Latent regions acquiring TF binding + marks on stimulation respond faster next time.
Causal chain cited: H3K27ac tunes transcriptional burst kinetics; G9a inhibition (↓H3K9me2) augments training in monocytes and BCG recipients; lncRNA UMLILO scaffolds H3K4me3/H3K27ac at chemokine loci.
Source: PMC12682684 ↗
Figure 4 · Kim 2025

mTORC1 vs. AMPK: The Metabolic Fork

mTORC1 activation + glycolysis → trained immunity (SETD7/MLL3/4-mediated H3K4me3/H3K27ac, heightened re-challenge); AMPK-driven OXPHOS/FAO → tolerance (LSD1 remodeling, blunted cytokines); priming sits poised between them, resolving by context. β-glucan training runs PI3K–Akt–mTOR–HIF-1α; tolerance runs itaconate–TET and AMPK catabolism.
The vault's shared-logic link made explicit: the same mTOR/AMPK–ROS axis runs POS, exercise hormesis and immune training — one wiring diagram, three vocabularies.
Source: PMC12682684 ↗
Figure 5 · Kim 2025

The Dose Makes the Memory: Train vs. Tolerize

Low/acute DAMP or PAMP doses → mTORC1/HIF-1α glycolysis → trained immunity (more cytokines, H3K4me3/ H3K27ac). High/chronic doses → AMPK/SIRT1/autophagy → tolerance (suppressed inflammation, FAO, H3K9me3). The most explicitly hormetic figure in the review: a biphasic immune dose-response with named molecular mediators on each arm.
Pairs with Brosimine B's viability curve two sections up: same inverted logic, different readout (cytokines instead of survival) — hormesis as a cross-scale pattern, not a single assay.
Source: PMC12682684 ↗
Figure 6 · Kim 2025

DAMP-Specific Routes to Memory

oxLDL → CD36/TLR4 → mTOR–HIF-1α → glycolytic training; HMGB1 → RAGE → AMPK/SIRT1 tolerance or mTOR training by dose/context; heme → SYK–ROS → mTOR-independent training (rapamycin-insensitive); mtDNA → TLR9–STING → autophagy/mTOR inhibition → tolerant state (BioRender).
Receptor usage + signal integration + context imprint tailored memories — the DAMP × receptor × metabolism table (Table 1: oxLDL, HMGB1, vimentin, hyperglycemia, heme, urate, mtDNA, S100A4) behind the diagram is a vault-ready reference.
Source: PMC12682684 ↗
Figure 7 · Kim 2025

mTOR as Train/Tolerize Integrator

(A) Upstream: oxLDL–CD36/TLR4–mevalonate–IGF1R and dectin-1–Syk–AKT converge on mTOR → HIF-1α, glycolysis, H3K4me3/H3K27ac → training; feedback brakes (AMPK, PTEN, SIRT1, STING/autophagy via MTING) suppress mTOR under chronic DAMP load. (B) Inhibitory nodes enforced in tolerance: AMPK/PTEN/SIRT1 plus mtDNA–STING autophagy loop locking in mTOR repression, OXPHOS and cytokine shutdown (BioRender).
Capstone diagram: every DAMP in Figure 6 enters this integrator — therapeutic handles (rapamycin nanobiologics, metformin, statins, HDAC/methyltransferase inhibitors) are node interventions on this map.
Source: PMC12682684 ↗

Exercise Hormesis — Liu & Zhao (2026)

Redox–exercise crosstalk in neurodegeneration: exertion-generated ROS as the hormetic signal — Nrf2-driven antioxidant/detox enzymes plus BDNF synthesis for adaptive neuroprotection — with the context-dependence (antioxidant supplements can blunt the signal) and Parkinson's-model evidence laid out.

From: "Redox-exercise crosstalk in neurodegenerative diseases: mechanistic perspectives" (Liu & Zhao, Front. Nutr., 2026)

Figure 1 · Liu 2026

Exercise → ROS → Nrf2 + BDNF → Neuroprotection

Core mechanism: exercise-generated redox signal activates Nrf2 (antioxidant/detoxifying enzyme program) and catalyzes BDNF synthesis — jointly driving adaptive neuroprotection in neurodegenerative disease models, alongside autophagy and mitohormesis arms.
The exercise instantiation of the POS logic: a transient oxidant pulse (this time from muscle work, not hypoxia) pre-tunes defenses — Nrf2 here plays the role FoxOs/NF-κB play in Oliveira's diagram.
Source: DOI 10.3389/fnut.2026.1850414 ↗
Figure 2 · Liu 2026

When Antioxidants Backfire: Context-Dependence

Exercise × antioxidant-supplementation interaction map: exogenous antioxidants can blunt the very redox signal exercise depends on — suppressing Nrf2/BDNF induction and neuroprotection — so supplementation context (dose, timing, disease state, including human AD exercise trials) decides whether redox support helps or cancels adaptation.
The hormetic-zone warning in clinical form: more antioxidant is not monotonically better — the right of the inverted-U applies to interventions too, a point with direct trial-design consequences.
Source: DOI 10.3389/fnut.2026.1850414 ↗
Figure 3 · Liu 2026

Redox–Antioxidant Interactions in PD Models

Experimental Parkinson's-model evidence map: how redox status and antioxidant interventions interact in PD systems — which nodes rescue dopaminergic readouts, which are neutral, and where exercise and redox modulation converge or conflict.
Disease grounding for the mechanism figures: PD as the test case where exercise hormesis, Nrf2/BDNF signaling and antioxidant pharmacology meet measurable neuronal outcomes.
Source: DOI 10.3389/fnut.2026.1850414 ↗

Plant Eustress — Mickky et al. (2026)

Salt eustress in sunflower microgreens: 25 mM NaCl stimulates yield, photosynthesis and nutrition (+58.7% fresh yield, +27% carbs/protein), 50 mM weakly stimulates, 75–100 mM suppresses with oxidative damage — a whole-plant inverted-U with numbers, and a direct nutritional bridge back to dietary hormesis.

From: "Salt eustress modulates physiological responses and enhances yield, nutritional quality, and antioxidant capacity in sunflower microgreens" (Mickky et al., BMC Plant Biol., 2026) — vault holds the abstract stub; figures harvested from the open-access publisher page.

Figure 1 · Mickky 2026

Growth Dose-Response: 25 mM Stimulates

Growth parameters across 0–100 mM NaCl: 25 mM NaCl raises shoot fresh yield 58.7%, dry yield 55.4%, shoot length 48.3%, root length 25.7%, leaf area 81.4% and specific leaf area 19.7%; 50 mM still improves growth but less; 75–100 mM significantly reduce it — the left and right arms of the inverted-U in one panel set.
Phenotype-level hormesis with agronomic meaning: the stimulatory zone is a yield gain, not just a biomarker wiggle — eustress as a cultivation input.
Source: DOI 10.1186/s12870-026-09786-y ↗
Figure 2 · Mickky 2026

Physiology Follows the Same Curve

Physiological status (photosynthesis and transpiration rates, chlorophyll content, relative water content, water-use efficiency) tracks the growth curve: improved at 25 mM, deteriorating at 75–100 mM — photosynthetic machinery, not just biomass, is hormetically tuned.
Mechanism above phenotype: eustress-level salt keeps carbon assimilation and water relations in the stimulatory zone, the physiological basis for the yield gain in Figure 1.
Source: DOI 10.1186/s12870-026-09786-y ↗
Figure 3 · Mickky 2026

Redox Biomarkers: Quiet at 25 mM, Alarming at 100 mM

Stress biomarkers (proline, catalase activity, lipid peroxidation, H2O2): at 25 mM, proline rises 5.7% while catalase, lipid peroxidation and H2O2 stay flat — stimulation without oxidative cost; higher doses drive marked lipid peroxidation and H2O2 accumulation, the distress signature.
The POS parallel in plants: low-dose salt primes (proline up, no damage), high-dose salt overwhelms — same redox-zone logic as Oliveira's animal panel, with proline playing an osmoprotectant role.
Source: DOI 10.1186/s12870-026-09786-y ↗
Figure 4 · Mickky 2026

Nutrition Up: Carbs, Protein, Antioxidants

Nutritional quality and antioxidant capacity (proximate composition, minerals, fatty-acid profile, antioxidant assays): 25 mM NaCl raises total carbohydrates 27.2% and total proteins 27.6% alongside antioxidant capacity — eustress improves the food itself, not only its biomass.
The bridge back to dietary hormesis: plant eustress enriches the hormetin content of food — the [[Eustress]] ↔ [[Dietary Hormesis]] vault link, quantified on the plate.
Source: DOI 10.1186/s12870-026-09786-y ↗
