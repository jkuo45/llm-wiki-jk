GTPase signaling — the cell's switchboard

GTPases are the cell's fast, reversible switches. A small GTPase is on when it holds GTP and off when it holds GDP; the energy of hydrolysis is what lets one molecule recruit many effectors before it shuts off again. The same architecture is reused for growth signaling ([[RAS]] → ERK), actin organisation ([[RhoA]], [[Rac1]], [[Cdc42]]), membrane identity ([[Rab5]] → [[Rab7]]), coat assembly ([[Arf1]]), and the physical scission of membranes ([[Dynamin]]). Because a switch that cannot be turned off is a disease — oncogenic Ras, a missing brake ([[GAP]]), a GTPase that never reaches its membrane — the GTPase cycle sits upstream of most of what this wiki cares about: autophagy flux, ROS, and the cytoskeleton that senescence and stress leave behind.

One cycle, dozens of families

Almost every fast decision a cell makes is made by a nucleotide switch. The biochemistry is stereotyped: a GEF loads GTP, the GTP-bound protein presents a new binding surface, a GAP hydrolyses the γ-phosphate, and the protein returns to its GDP-bound, effector-incompetent state. What changes between families is the effector cohort — what the exposed surface recruits — and the compartment in which the switch is allowed to happen.

Fast, reversible, amplifiable

A single GTP-bound GTPase can recruit dozens of effector molecules before hydrolysis shuts it down, so one activated receptor can produce a large, graded output. Switching costs seconds, not hours — which is why GTPases carry the fast layer of signaling and kinases carry the slow one.

Spatial confinement is part of the signal

A lipid anchor (CAAX prenylation) and an extractant (GDI) tie "on/off" to "where". Rho-family GTPases spend most of their life GDP-bound and cytosolic, held by RhoGDI; only a local GEF at the right membrane loads them. A signal that is correct in bulk but wrong in place is a disease, not a signal.

The brake is as important as the accelerator

Cells express far more inactive than active GTPase at any moment. A GAP is a brake, not an off-switch on a running motor: losing one does not block signaling, it makes it persist. That is why the best-known tumour suppressors in this family — NF1 for Ras, TSC2 for Rheb — are GAPs.

What was missing was the connective tissue: which family does what, how the cycle generates the output, and where the switch ties into this wiki's three themes — autophagy (the [[Rab7]] fusion step), oxidative stress (the NOX complex), and nutrient sensing (the Rheb–mTORC1 brake).

The nucleotide cycle

A GTPase is a P-loop NTPase that reads the state of the guanine nucleotide it carries. Three classes of partner set the state, and one structural feature reads it.

How fast does the switch turn off?

Because the brake is a catalyst, the intrinsic hydrolysis rate of the GTPase sets the ceiling on how long a signal can persist without a GAP. Ras-family and Arf-family GTPases are intrinsically slow — a Ras-GTP molecule survives for minutes to hours — which is precisely why they need GAPs to be switch-like at all. Rab GTPases and dynamin are intrinsically far faster, so they can be regulated by localisation alone.

Reading the cycle as logic

GEF = accelerate (catalyse the slow step). GAP = brake (catalyse the reverse). GDI = localisation. A node that is only a GEF makes a signal that persists; a node that is only a GAP makes a signal that cannot be sustained; a node that is only a GDI makes a signal that cannot be localised. Disease follows when a brake is lost ([[NF1]] loss in neurofibromatosis, [[TSC2]] loss in tuberous sclerosis) or when a switch is pinned on (oncogenic [[KRAS]]).

What the switch is made of

Small GTPases are 20–25 kDa proteins built on the Ras fold. Two parts matter: a catalytic G domain that reads the nucleotide, and a C-terminal hypervariable region (HVR) that decides where the protein goes and which effectors it can engage.

Why switch I and switch II matter

When the γ-phosphate is present, the two switch regions rearrange relative to each other and create a high-affinity effector-binding surface. GAPs dock on switch II — which is why a GAP can catalyse hydrolysis and simultaneously occlude the effector site, making the switch turn off cleanly rather than merely slowly.

Bigger GTPases use the same chemistry

[[Dynamin]] (~100 kDa) and the trimeric G-protein α subunits keep the Ras-like catalytic fold and add effector-binding, oligomerisation and membrane-targeting modules. Dynamin turns the energy of hydrolysis into mechanical work — it is a GTPase that severs membranes rather than one that relays a signal.

Which family owns which process

A useful way to read the superfamily is as a partition of cellular labour. Each family has a domain it owns outright, a domain where it is a contributor, and a domain it does not touch.

The cross-theme reading

Three of this wiki's recurring themes land on this matrix. Autophagy needs Rab7 (fusion) and Rheb (the mTORC1 brake that gates initiation). Oxidative stress needs Rac1 and Cdc42 (NOX assembly). Cellular senescence needs all of them, because senescent cells have deranged actin, stalled lysosomes, and a chronically elevated ROS signal. The GTPase layer is where those three themes are wired together.

Case study — RhoA, Rac1, Cdc42: one cell, three jobs

The clearest demonstration that the GTPase family encodes compartment rather than pathway is migrating fibroblasts. Three Rho-family GTPases are active in the same cell at the same time, on the same actin, with opposing outputs. The balance between them — not the absolute level of any one — decides the cell's motility mode.

RhoA — the rear

RhoA–ROCK is the contractile arm: stress fibres, focal-adhesion maturation, the cytokinetic ring (delivered to the cleavage furrow by Ect2), and smooth-muscle contraction. Excessive RhoA/ROCK tone is a cardiovascular and fibrotic problem — which is why ROCK is one of the few druggable nodes in this family.

Rac1 — the front

Rac1 also sits upstream of the phagocyte respiratory burst (it is required to assemble NOX) and of NF-κB transcription, so it carries signals that have nothing to do with the cytoskeleton. Rac1 is one of the few mutated Rho GTPases: P29S in 4–9% of sun-exposed melanomas; the RAC1B splice variant is overexpressed in breast, colorectal and lung cancer.

Cdc42 — the compass

Cdc42 organises polarity, filopodia, the NADPH oxidase, and the actin cytoskeleton of haematopoietic cells via WASP — which is why dominant-negative Cdc42 alleles reproduce the actinopathy of Wiskott–Aldrich syndrome. Cdc42 is also the flagellar master regulator, setting cilium orientation and ciliary trafficking.

Why the antagonism is the point

Rock alone produces disordered random bundles; mDia is needed to correct them. Rac and Rho antagonise in migration but cooperate in tension sensing (adhesion through integrin and cadherin activates RhoA; force on those junctions activates AMPK and glucose uptake — a positive loop). The system is not a set of independent pathways but a ratio controller: the cell's shape and motility mode are set by the relative activity of two GTPases pulling in opposite directions.

Case study — the Rab5→Rab7 conversion gates autophagic flux

This is the case that ties the GTPase layer to this wiki's core theme. Autophagy is often described as a bulk pathway, but its terminal step — the fusion of an autophagosome with a lysosome — is executed by a Rab GTPase cascade. If the cascade stalls, autophagosomes still form, LC3-II still accumulates, and nothing is degraded: a high-flux-looking assay with no flux behind it.

The same cascade, three diseases

Rab7 mutations cause Charcot–Marie–Tooth type 2B neuropathy, reflecting the requirement for Rab7 in neuronal degradative traffic; defective Rab7 fusion contributes to aggregate accumulation in neurodegeneration and ageing. Rab27A defects cause Chediak–Higashi syndrome (lysosomal trafficking and degranulation failure). Rab8 interacts with the mitophagy receptor [[OPTN]] to move mitochondria-containing autophagosomes retrogradely to the cell body — and mutant huntingtin disrupts exactly that OPTN–Rab8 interaction in Huntington's disease, blocking autophagic progression.

Pathogens hijack the switch

The Legionella effector SetA glucosylates Rab5 (and Rab22), immobilising endosomes and blocking autophagosome–lysosome fusion to create a replication-permissive vacuole — xenophagy evasion by editing the address label. Overactive Rab5 signaling is also implicated in oncogenic growth and RTK-driven proliferation, which links endocytic trafficking to proliferative signaling.

Case study — the Rheb–TSC brake: a GAP as tumour suppressor

The cleanest example in the vault of GAP biology is the TSC1/TSC2 heterodimer acting as the GAP for [[Rheb]], the small Ras-related GTPase that directly activates [[mTORC1]]. The same node receives nutrient and energy signals, which makes it one of the few places where the cell's metabolic state and its growth machinery are wired through a single nucleotide switch.

The broader pattern

Both [[NF1]] (a Ras GAP) and [[TSC2]] (a Rheb GAP) are tumour suppressors, and both sit in the ageing-relevant part of this wiki. Losing a GAP does not switch signaling off — it makes the signal persist, which is how a cell acquires growth independence without a gain-of-function mutation in the switch itself. It is the same logic as the lipid-peroxidation page's dose argument: the difference between a signal and a lesion is concentration over time.

Case study — Rac1 and Cdc42 build the NADPH oxidase

The phagocyte respiratory burst is the clearest example of a GTPase acting as a licence rather than a relay: the enzyme is always in the membrane, but it cannot fire until the correct small GTPases hand it its cytosolic activators.

The ROS dial this sets

The same switch that licenses the oxidative burst also licenses the low-level ROS that acts as a signaling species — and that is the seam this wiki cares about. At low flux, ROS is a signal (NRF2/ARE induction, hormetic adaptation); at high flux, it is damage (lipid peroxidation, ferroptosis). GTPase signalling controls the position and duration of the burst, which is the upstream half of the redox decision.

When the switch misbehaves

GTPase disease comes in two shapes: a switch that cannot be turned off (oncogenic gain of function) and a switch that cannot be turned on, or that runs in the wrong place (loss of function, or mislocalisation).

Two recurring mechanisms

Locked on. Hot-spot mutations (Ras G12/G13/Q61, RhoA G17V, Rac1 P29S) do not change how much the protein can be activated — they change how fast it can be switched off. A fast-cycling allele and a GTP-locked allele are the same disease logic. Lost brake. Loss of a GAP (NF1, TSC2) or of a phosphatase-like restraint produces the same endpoint by a different route. The therapeutic targets differ accordingly: an activating mutation wants a ligand, a lost brake wants a downstream substitute — which is why TSC is treated with mTOR inhibitors rather than with a TSC activator.

What is druggable, and where

Direct ligands for small GTPases are rare. The tractable levels are (a) the switch itself, for the one family with approved direct inhibitors; (b) the prenylation step that makes the switch membrane-bound; and (c) the effector kinases downstream — which is where most of the clinical pharmacology actually happens.

The pattern

Where a direct switch ligand is not available, the drug hits one of three things: the lipid anchor (prenylation inhibition removes the switch from the membrane entirely), the GEF (blocking exchange keeps the switch in the off state), or the effector kinase (ROCK, PAK, MEK) — which is downstream of dozens of GTPases and therefore both broader and less specific.

Why the switch itself is a hard target

The GDP-binding pocket is one of the most conserved pockets in biology, and it has to bind GTP, GDP and Mg2+ with high fidelity for the cycle to work at all. Selectivity between family members therefore has to come from the shallow switch surfaces or the hypervariable region — and those are large, flat, and hard to drug. The two exceptions prove the rule: KRAS G12C is druggable because the mutation creates a novel pocket, and Ras prenylation is druggable because it is a covalent lipid chemistry problem rather than a binding problem.
