Five Bioactive Compounds, Four Hallmarks — Spermidine · Fisetin · Berberine · Urolithin A · PCC1

Summary of Rzeski & Rzeska, Nutrients 2026, 18, 2511 — a narrative review framing four mechanistically distinct, non-redundant compounds as complementary functional-food ingredients: spermidine (bulk autophagy via EP300), fisetin (senolysis via PI3K/Akt + Bcl-2/Bcl-xL), berberine (metabolic + microbiome via AMPK), and urolithin A (selective mitophagy via PINK1/Parkin) — plus a fifth from a separate source, procyanidin C1 (PCC1) (dual senomorphic/senolytic grape-seed flavonoid, Xu et al., Nat Metab 2021). Includes a weight-based dose-and-interval estimator below. Educational content; not medical advice.

The five compounds at a glance

Spermidine — the autophagy inducer

EP300 ↓ · AMPK ↑ · SIRT1 ↑ · mTOR-independent

Dietary polyamine (wheat germ ~337 mg/kg, soy, mushrooms, legumes, aged cheese, natto). Habitual intake ~8–12 mg/day. Inhibits acetyltransferase EP300, derepressing ATG5/ATG7/ATG12/LC3 autophagic flux; suppresses NLRP3/NF-κB inflammation.

  • Evidence: lifespan extension in yeast/worms/flies/mice (autophagy-dependent); Bruneck cohort (n=829, 20-yr) linked higher intake to ~10-point lower 20-yr mortality; SmartAge RCTs at 0.9–1.2 mg/day inconclusive.
  • Trial doses: 0.9–3.3 mg/day (wheat-germ extract), daily.
  • Food-first: works — enrichment/fortification is the strategy.
  • Preparation note: heat degrades spermidine during processing — favor minimal-heat sources (mature cheeses, natto, fermented foods, raw wheat germ) and gentle cooking over high-heat methods.

Fisetin — the senolytic flavonoid

PI3K/Akt ↓ · Bcl-2/Bcl-xL ↓ · SASP ↓ · NRF2 ↑

Flavonol (strawberries ~160 mg/kg, apples, persimmons, onions). Most potent senolytic of ten flavonoids screened (25–50% senescent-cell clearance at 1–10 µM); mouse lifespan/frailty benefit with intermittent dosing. Severely limited oral bioavailability (rapid glucuronidation).

  • Evidence: earliest-stage of the four — preclinical + early human safety; no completed phase III; STOP-Sepsis (NCT05758246) ongoing.
  • Trial doses: 100–1000 mg/day, intermittent (1-week on / 2-week off / 1-week on pulses, not chronic daily — Murray et al. 2025).
  • Formulation: galactomannan complexation raised bioavailability >25-fold.
  • Preparation note: fisetin concentrates in outer layers/skins — don't peel apples, cucumbers, or persimmons, eat strawberries whole, and minimize processing losses; eat the skin.

Berberine — the metabolic/microbiome modulator

AMPK ↑ · mTORC1 ↓ · NF-κB ↓ · Akkermansia/Bifidobacterium ↑

Isoquinoline alkaloid (Berberis, Coptis spp.; absent from common Western foods at relevant levels). Complex-I inhibition → AMPK activation; 1–5% oral bioavailability gives high intraluminal levels that reshape gut microbiota independent of systemic action.

  • Evidence: strongest of the four — 46-RCT meta-analysis: HbA1c −0.73%, fasting glucose −0.86 mmol/L, LDL-C −0.86 mmol/L; PREMOTE trial linked benefit to microbiome shifts.
  • Trial doses: 900–1500 mg/day, divided (TID with meals).
  • Caution: CYP3A4/CYP2D6/P-gp inhibitor; contraindicated in pregnancy; GI effects common.

Urolithin A — the mitophagy postbiotic

PINK1/Parkin ↑ · SIRT1/PGC-1α ↑ · NLRP3 ↓

Not in food — made by colonic microbiota (Gordonibacter, Ellagibacter) from pomegranate/berry/nut ellagitannins. Only metabotype A (~25–55% of people) produces it from diet; others need direct purified supplement (e.g. Mitopure) for consistent exposure.

  • Evidence: richest mitophagy clinical dataset — ATLAS RCT: ~12% hamstring-strength gain at 500 mg/day; endurance, VO₂, immune-remodeling signals; safe to 2000 mg single / 1000 mg/day.
  • Trial doses: 500–1000 mg/day, once daily (Tmax 4–6 h, half-life ~20 h).

PCC1 — the grape-seed senolytic

NOXA/PUMA ↑ · mtROS ↑ · SASP ↓ (low dose) · caspase-dependent

Epicatechin trimer procyanidin from grape seed extract (GSE). Dual action: senomorphic at low concentrations (NF-κB/SASP suppression) and senolytic at high concentrations (NOXA/PUMA upregulation → mitochondrial ROS → cytochrome c release → apoptosis). In vitro senolytic from 50 µM; normal cells spared until ≥600 µM.

  • Evidence: preclinical only — earliest-stage of the five; mouse lifespan/healthspan benefit (intermittent i.p. dosing); no human trials. Source: Xu et al. 2021, not the Nutrients review.
  • Dose (no trial range): HED ≈ 114 mg, biweekly — illustrative human-equivalent of the mouse 20 mg/kg i.p. dose (÷12.3), not a validated human regimen.
  • Caveats: administered i.p. in mice (not oral); oral bioavailability poor (procyanidin t½ <12 h); whole grape seeds cannot deliver therapeutic PCC1 — purified compound required.
  • Preparation note: grape-seed extract is a source but PCC1 is only a minor component — standardized/ purified forms only; food-first does not work here.

Combinations: what the review actually says

Paraphrase of the review's position (not verbatim): “No completed trial has tested all four together; the combination hypothesis is mechanistically grounded but prospectively unvalidated.” The review found no peer-reviewed direct evidence for all-four co-administration. The only registered trial combining any two is NCT06990256 (n=80, 45–70 y: urolithin A 500 mg and fisetin 500 mg single/combination arms incl. UA 300 mg + fisetin 200 mg; 12-week sleep, DNA-methylation age, inflammation, BMAL1, HOMA-IR endpoints).

Promising early signals cited: spermidine + urolithin A (broad autophagy + selective mitophagy) and spermidine + berberine (caloric-restriction mimetics via deacetylation/autophagy vs AMPK–microbiota axes) are argued to be additive rather than redundant; the autophagy–senescence threshold model rationalizes sequencing (spermidine prevents senescence initiation pre-threshold; fisetin clears established senescent cells post-threshold). Network-medicine logic supports multi-node modulation — but the review stresses pharmacokinetics, disease stage, and individual (incl. urolithin metabotype) variability may dominate outcomes.

PCC1 (the fifth compound) in combination: mechanistically complementary to spermidine (autophagy prevents senescence initiation pre-threshold; PCC1 clears established senescent cells post-threshold — the same slot fisetin occupies), additive/partly redundant with fisetin (both senolytics acting on the Bcl-2 family via different entry points — PCC1 through NOXA/PUMA + mitochondrial ROS, fisetin through PI3K/Akt + Bcl-2/Bcl-xL), and complementary to berberine (distinct axes; low-dose PCC1's SASP/NF-κB suppression echoes berberine's NF-κB effect) and urolithin A (PCC1 raises mtROS in senescent cells to kill them; UA enhances mitophagy in healthy cells — different cell populations, not a true conflict). No co-administration data exists for PCC1 with any of the four; its evidence base is mouse i.p. only, so oral PK interactions are entirely unstudied.

Interaction caution: berberine (CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitor) + fisetin (CYP3A4 substrate and partial inhibitor) may mutually elevate plasma levels. Interim mitigation proposed: temporal separation (berberine AM / fisetin PM); avoid co-administration with narrow-therapeutic-index CYP3A4 substrates. Berberine is contraindicated in pregnancy. Pharmacokinetic co-administration studies are an explicit unmet gap — do not combine without clinician supervision.

Fisetin is selective

Textbook senolytic diagrams draw a single arrow to apoptosis. In aged-mouse adipose/aorta (CyTOF + scRNA-seq, Yousefzadeh 2018 and follow-ups), intermittent fisetin clears 4 senescent sub-types and spares 2 — and effects differ by sex and disease stage (e.g. early-life dosing beneficial in males; no human sex-stratified data). Prodrug viability — fisetin 3,7-diisobutyrate

Fisetin's four phenolic OH groups (3, 7, 3′, 4′) make it a prime UGT/SULT conjugation substrate — the dominant reason oral exposure collapses. Masking 3-OH and 7-OH as isobutyryl esters (leaving the senolytic catechol intact) raises logP ~1.7 units, drops H-bond donors 4→2, and blocks the two major Phase II sites until tissue carboxylesterases (CES1/2) regenerate free fisetin.
