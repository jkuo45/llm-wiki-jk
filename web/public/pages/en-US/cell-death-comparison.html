Across ischemia–reperfusion models, female cells default to caspase-dependent apoptosis; male cells to PARP1–AIF parthanatos; the lytic and iron arms add context-specific exceptions. Scope: the cell-autonomous XX/XY split is established in murine ischemia–reperfusion models (MCAO stroke, neonatal HI, renal IRI) and XX/XY cultures; one human correlate — post-MI autopsy shows a ~10× higher apoptotic index in men (Biondi-Zoccai 2005). Direct human validation of the PARP1/AIF arm is still lacking. Eryptosis (RBC, anucleate) and NETosis (neutrophil) are blood-cell-specific and outside the XX/XY paradigm — no cell-autonomous sex split established.

12 s time-lapse loop · T+ clock = biological time · scroll for all 10

How to read them. Swelling + sudden burst with debris cloud = lytic (necrosis, necroptosis, pyroptosis, PANoptosis, late ferroptosis). Shrinkage + surface blebs pinching off into separate balls = apoptosis. PAR signal → AIF marching nucleus-ward with no swelling = parthanatos. Peroxidation patches + shrinking condensed mitochondria = ferroptosis. Crescent moons closing around cargo and fusing with a lysosome = autophagy. Each 12 s loop is a time-lapse — the T+ clock runs at biological speed, so identical loop lengths cover wildly different real durations: ≈20 min (necrosis) and ≈45 min (pyroptosis) at the fast end, ≈2 h starvation flux (autophagy) and ≈3–4 h (apoptosis, necroptosis) in the middle, ≈8 h (parthanatos, ferroptosis) and ≈12 h infection courses (PANoptosis) at the slow end, ≈2–4 h suicidal NETosis and hours-scale eryptosis alongside. Single-cell timing varies by cell type and stimulus — treat clocks as representative, not exact.
