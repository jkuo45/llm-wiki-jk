Cell death · classical dichotomy · Kerr–Wyllie–Currie model

Regulated deletion with silent clearance versus unregulated lysis with inflammatory spillage

The classical dichotomy of cell death, rendered as two three-dimensional models sharing an initial morphology. Apoptosis is ATP-dependent programmed death: death-receptor or p53/BH3-only signaling converges on MOMP, apoptosome assembly, and executioner caspases, producing shrinkage, chromatin pyknosis, phosphatidylserine externalization, and sealed apoptotic bodies cleared silently by efferocytosis. Accidental necrosis is unprogrammed death from irrecoverable bioenergetic failure — Na+/K+-ATPase arrest, oncosis, Ca2+ overload with mPTP opening, membrane rupture, and DAMP release driving sterile inflammation. Model design and scope

Both models initialize from identical geometry — icosahedral actin cortex, inner and outer phospholipid leaflets, nuclear envelope enclosing three chromatin domains, mitochondria with folded cristae — so morphology diverges only by bioenergetic state. The apoptosis arm preserves ATP for caspase activation, cortical contraction, and lipid remodeling; the necrosis arm depletes ATP from the trigger, so ion gradients, volume control, and membrane integrity fail progressively. Per NCCD criteria this page models accidental necrosis (ACD) only; regulated lytic programs (necroptosis, pyroptosis, ferroptosis) are tabulated in the ten-way comparison.

Secondary necrosis

Apoptotic bodies that escape engulfment — e.g., high cell density, in vitro culture, or impaired macrophage function — lose membrane integrity and release DAMPs, converging on a necrotic endpoint. The two trajectories are displayed on independent timescales to keep this distinction explicit.

Geometry is original to this page (geodesic lattice, Fibonacci bilayer, folded cristae, dual-model layout), distinct from the single-program models elsewhere on the site.
