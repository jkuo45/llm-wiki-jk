Necroptosis — Dhuriya & Sharma (2018)

The timeline review that systematized necroptosis — the RIPK3/MLKL alternative to apoptosis when caspase-8 is blocked — covering initiation, classical vs. non-classical necrosomes, phospho-site control, and coupling to inflammation, infection and neurodegeneration.

From: "Necroptosis: a regulated inflammatory mode of cell death" (J Neuroinflammation, 2018)

Figure 1 · Necroptosis 2018

Apoptosis vs. Necroptosis: The Caspase-8 Switch

Death receptors drive both extrinsic apoptosis and necroptosis. Active caspase-8 pushes the pathway toward apoptosis; its inhibition lets RIPK1 and RIPK3 interact through their RHIM domains to form the necrosome. RIPK3 phosphorylates MLKL, whose oligomers translocate to the plasma membrane — either recruiting Na+/Ca++ channels or binding phosphatidylinositol phosphates — to form pores and trigger an inflammatory, lytic death.
Two preconditions gate the switch: cells must express RIPK3/MLKL, and caspase-8 must be inhibited or lost. RIPK3 overexpression or forced dimerization alone suffices to drive MLKL-dependent death without RIPK1.
Source: PMC6035417 ↗
Figure 2 · Necroptosis 2018

RIPK-Dependent Inflammation

RIPK3 kinase activity — activated by RIPK1 as well as TLR3/TLR4-TRIF, ZBP1/DAI and interferon inputs — drives MLKL oligomerization that culminates in inflammation. Beyond DAMP release through MLKL pores, RIPK3 directly activates inflammasome formation (caspase-8-mediated and NLRP3-mediated arms → caspase-1/11 → mature IL-1β).
Whether MLKL is obligatory for NLRP3 activation is contested — some MLKL knockouts preserve IL-1β maturation. RIPK1 additionally drives cytokines as a scaffold independently of RIPK3 (TNF–NF-κB/JNK, TLR4–IL-1α, autocrine TNF).
Source: PMC6035417 ↗

Parthanatos Discovery — Andrabi et al. (2006)

The PNAS discovery paper proving PAR polymer itself — the product of PARP-1 hyperactivation, not NAD+ depletion — is a previously uncharacterized cell-death signal: purified PAR kills, degrading it with PARG or phosphodiesterase 1 blocks death, and PARG dosage sets stroke outcome in vivo.

From: "Poly(ADP-ribose) (PAR) polymer is a death signal" (Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A, 2006)

Figure 1 · Andrabi 2006

Purified PAR Polymer Is Directly Toxic

PAR polymer synthesized by in vitro PARP-1 automodification (mean ~40-mer, range 6–100-mer) delivered by BioPorter kills ~50% of cortical neurons at 80 nM; pretreatment with PARG or phosphodiesterase 1 abolishes toxicity while the charge control poly(A) is nontoxic. PAR also kills PARP-1-KO neurons — the polymer acts downstream of the enzyme.
The founding sufficiency test for parthanatos: in energy-replete cells, exogenous PAR is lethal and its enzymatic degradation is protective — NAD+ decrements alone are insufficient to explain PARP-1-dependent death.
Source: PMC1838747 ↗
Figure 2 · Andrabi 2006

Length, Dose, and Caspase Independence

Fractionated PAR: 16-mer weak, 30-mer modest, ~60-mer (40–100 range) causes ~80% neuronal death at 80 nM, with a dose response from 20 nM upward — sizes and concentrations matching intact cells during NMDA excitotoxicity (~80 nM at 60 min). The broad-spectrum caspase inhibitor z-VAD-fmk fails to protect neurons or HeLa cells, and PAR does not directly activate caspases.
Complexity encodes lethality: long branched polymers generated after PARP-1-dependent injury are the toxic species — the structural-toxicity rule reused across all later parthanatos work.
Source: PMC1838747 ↗
Figure 3 · Andrabi 2006

Blocking PAR Signaling Protects Neurons

Intracellular anti-PAR antiserum (immunodepletes purified PAR and nuclear-supernatant PAR) halves NMDA (500 μM, 5 min) neuronal death; preimmune/normal serum has no effect. Cytosolic exon-1-deleted wild-type PARG adenovirus lowers cytosolic PAR and halves NMDA death, while the catalytically dead mutant (E756A/E757A) and GFP controls do not. The same protection holds for MNNG killing of HeLa cells.
Necessity complements sufficiency: neutralizing or degrading endogenous PAR rescues two independent PARP-1-dependent models (excitotoxic neurons, genotoxic HeLa) — residual death reflects incomplete depletion (20 nM PAR is still toxic) or parallel pathways.
Source: PMC1838747 ↗
Figure 4 · Andrabi 2006

PARG Dosage Sets Vulnerability In Vivo

PARG+/− cortical neurons accumulate ~2× PAR after NMDA with higher basal levels and ~20% excess death — matching the purified-PAR dose response. PrP-promoter PARG transgenics show −62% hemisphere, −52% striatal, −59% cortical infarct after 120-min MCAO; PARG heterozygotes show +56%/+56%/+62% after 90-min MCAO, with perfusion, temperature and deficit scores matched.
In vivo validation of the PAR hypothesis: the PAR eraser bidirectionally sets stroke outcome, nominating PAR neutralization and PARG potentiation as therapeutic strategies for stroke, trauma and ischemia-reperfusion injury.
Source: PMC1838747 ↗

Nuclear–Mitochondrial Crosstalk — Andrabi / Dawson (2008)

The naming review that coined parthanatos (PAR + Thanatos): PARP-1 hyperactivation generates PAR polymer that translocates nucleus → cytosol, colocalizes with mitochondria, and triggers AIF release — a caspase-independent death distinct from apoptosis, necrosis and autophagy.

From: "Mitochondrial and Nuclear Cross Talk in Cell Death: Parthanatos" (Ann N Y Acad Sci, 2008)

Figure 1 · Andrabi 2008

PAR Metabolism: Repair vs. Death

PARP-1 uses NAD+ to build PAR on acceptor proteins (hetero-modification) or itself (auto-modification), releasing nicotinamide (a PARP feedback inhibitor feeding the NAD+ salvage cycle). PARG hydrolyzes PAR to free polymer or ADP-ribose. Mild DNA nicks activate repair via poly(ADP-ribosyl)ation; damage beyond the repair threshold generates excessive PAR that kills via parthanatos.
The dose-threshold model: the same enzyme is a genome guardian at low activity and a death initiator at overactivation — the life/death duality framing all parthanatos therapeutics.
Source: PMC4454457 ↗
Figure 2 · Andrabi 2008

PAR → Mitochondria → AIF Cascade

DNA damage activates nuclear PARP-1, which builds PAR on itself and acceptor proteins. PARG can trim nuclear PAR, but accumulated PAR translocates to the cytosol — free or protein-bound — where cytosolic PARG isoforms degrade it only when balanced against production. Cytosolic PAR engages mitochondria to release AIF, which translocates to the nucleus to drive large-scale DNA fragmentation and death.
Harlequin mice (~80% AIF reduction) resist NMDA, purified PAR, excitotoxic and stroke lesions, with re-expression restoring susceptibility — AIF is the executioner downstream of the PAR signal. Open candidates for the release step: mPTP (ANT/VDAC/cyclophilin D), BAX translocation, calpain truncation of AIF.
Source: PMC4454457 ↗

Messenger of Death — David et al. (2009)

The foundational synthesis arguing PAR polymer — not NAD+ consumption — is the cytotoxic effector downstream of PARP-1: PARP domain biology, PAR synthesis and degradation, the NAD+/AIF debates, PARG genetics, and the nuclear–mitochondrial wiring that remains to be mapped.

From: "Parthanatos, a messenger of death" (Front Biosci, 2009)

Figure 1 · David 2009

PARP-1 Domain Architecture

PARP-1 uses NAD+ to form ADP-ribose polymers on acceptors. Major domains: (1) two zinc fingers detecting DNA breaks, (2) bipartite nuclear localization signal with a caspase-cleavage site, (3) automodification domain with a BRCT protein-interaction motif, and (4) C-terminal catalytic site carrying the PARP-signature NAD+ fold shared with bacterial ADP-ribosylating toxins.
Structure explains regulation: DNA-break sensing (zinc fingers) is coupled to catalysis (NAD+ fold), with caspase cleavage and BRCT interactions gating the life (repair/transcription/mitosis) versus death outputs.
Source: PMC4450718 ↗
Figure 2 · David 2009

PAR Synthesis, Branching, and Breakdown

Initiation (1): PARP-1 converts NAD+ to ADP-ribose plus nicotinamide, attaching the first unit via an ester bond to Glu/Asp on the acceptor. Elongation (2) and branching (3) proceed through 2′-OH and 2″-OH ribose linkages (~1 branch per 20–50 units; up to 200–400 units long). Breakdown (4) is catalyzed by PARG endo-/exo-glycosidase activities, with mono-ADP-ribosyl-protein lyase removing the final unit.
Heterogeneity encodes function: free vs. bound, linear vs. branched, short vs. long polymers — varying by stimulus and PARP isoform — may distinguish life from death signaling across the 17-member PARP family.
Source: PMC4450718 ↗
Figure 3 · David 2009

Nuclear–Mitochondrial Crosstalk Model

Death stimuli causing excessive DNA damage (1) overactivate PARP-1 (2), releasing AIF from mitochondria (3). Excess free or protein-bound complex PAR moves nucleus → cytosol, where it disrupts protein interactions — targeting AIF-release, membrane-permeabilization or mitochondrial-function proteins against a backdrop of membrane-potential loss. Downstream events include calpain activation and BAX translocation, with calpain hypothesized to cleave AIF for release through BAX pores.
The audit concluding the review: NAD+/ATP falls are real but insufficient (mitochondrial NAD+ stays physiological; BAX/calpain KOs survive MNNG despite massive NAD+ drops; PARP-1-KO infarct protection occurs without energy-status difference) — PAR-induced AIF translocation defines parthanatos, with PAR→permeabilization/calpain/BAX wiring and druggable PAR-binders as open questions.
Source: PMC4450718 ↗

The cFLIP Switch — DISC, Ripoptosome & Necrosome (raw)

cFLIP isoforms as the fate switch between survival, apoptosis and necroptosis: cFLIPL activates procaspase-8 without full processing (survival), cFLIPS/R blocks it — and when RIPK1 cannot be cleaved, the ripoptosome hands off to the RIPK1–RIPK3–MLKL necrosome. Raw material pending ingestion.

From: "FLIP the Switch: Regulation of Apoptosis and Necroptosis by cFLIP" (Tsuchiya et al., Int J Mol Sci, 2015) · related raw: Feoktistova et al., Mol Cell 2011 (2 MDa ripoptosome; paywalled, no OA figures) · Micheau et al., J Biol Chem 2002 (FLIPL activates caspase-8 at the Fas DISC; no OA figures).

Figure 1 · Tsuchiya 2015 ◆

Gene & Protein Structures of Human cFLIP

The CFLAR gene on human chromosome 2q33-34 (adjacent to caspase-8/10, an ancient duplication) and its protein products: cFLIPL (55 kDa, two DEDs + catalytically dead caspase-like domain), cFLIPS (27 kDa, DEDs only) and cFLIPR (25 kDa) — plus the p43-FLIP/p22-FLIP fragments from caspase-8 cleavage at Asp376/Asp196.
All isoforms heterodimerize with caspase-8 via DED–DED interaction; the isoform, concentration and tissue context — policed by the ubiquitin-proteasome system — decide survival vs. death.
Source: PMC4691174 ↗
Figure 2 · Tsuchiya 2015 ◆

cFLIP in the DISC: Apoptosis or Survival

Death-ligand trimer → receptor → FADD → DED recruitment of procaspase-8, cFLIPL and cFLIPS into the DISC. Procaspase-8 homodimers yield fully processed caspase-8 and apoptosis; procaspase-8–cFLIPL heterodimers make p43/p22-FLIP, stay DISC-bound with restricted substrates (survival); procaspase-8–cFLIPS heterodimers are catalytically dead (apoptosis blocked).
Quantitative DISC stoichiometry (mass spec: procaspase-8 ≫ FADD, little cFLIP) plus DED-chain and two-step models explain how tiny cFLIP amounts set the death-ligand threshold — a bistable life/death switch.
Source: PMC4691174 ↗
Figure 3 · Tsuchiya 2015 ◆

cFLIP in the Ripoptosome → Necrosome Handoff

Genotoxic stress or IAP depletion exposes RIPK1's death domain → FADD → DED proteins assemble the 2 MDa ripoptosome (RIPK1/FADD/caspase-8/cFLIP) without death receptors. Homodimer caspase-8 cleaves RIPK1 and disassembles the complex (apoptosis); cFLIPL heterodimers also cleave RIPK1 but spare procaspase-8 (survival); cFLIPS heterodimers cannot cleave RIPK1 — so RIPK1–RIPK3–MLKL assemble the necrosome and execute necroptosis.
The bridge between the vault's necroptosis notes and raw: Cflip−/− embryonic lethality is rescued only by co-deleting Fadd + Ripk3 — cFLIPL restrains both apoptosis and necroptosis in vivo.
Source: PMC4691174 ↗

Necroptosis Network — Regulation & the MLKL/Drp1 Test (raw)

From the 2026 state-of-the-field synthesis of necroptosis regulation to the decisive genetic test that settled a controversy: RIPK3-driven necroptosis strictly requires MLKL — but not the mitochondrial fission GTPase Drp1. Raw material pending ingestion.

From: "Regulatory complexity and therapeutic targeting of the necroptosis network" (Niu et al., Front Immunol, 2026) & "Necroptosis induced by RIPK3 requires MLKL but not Drp1" (Moujalled et al., Cell Death Dis, 2014)

Figure 1 · Niu 2026 ◆

Necroptosis Signaling Pathways at a Glance

Necroptosis is executed via the RIPK3/MLKL axis fed by three major routes: (1) TNFR1–TNF engagement driving the RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL cascade, (2) ZBP1 sensing viral Z-RNA, and (3) TLR3/4–TRIF inputs — canonical and non-canonical induction converging on RIPK3 phosphorylation of MLKL and plasma-membrane pore formation.
The modern counterpart to the vault's 2018 timeline review: same core axis, expanded inducer inventory and explicit therapeutic-vulnerability framing (inflammatory, neurodegenerative, ischemic indications; oncology via apoptosis-resistant cells).
Source: PMC13171333 ↗
Figure 2 · Niu 2026 ◆

Multilayered Regulation of the Core Executors

Upstream signals converge on RIPK1, which integrates survival and death cues; RIPK3 amplifies and disseminates the death signal by phosphorylating MLKL. Each executor is tuned by post-translational modifications (phosphorylation, ubiquitylation) and epigenetic mechanisms that gate necrosome assembly and MLKL membrane targeting.
Directly extends the vault note's phospho-site catalogue (RIPK1 Ser89/161/166/321; RIPK3 Ser199/227/232) into a full regulatory architecture — the layer drug discovery must navigate.
Source: PMC13171333 ↗
Figure 3 · Niu 2026 ◆

Crosstalk with Apoptosis, Autophagy & Metabolism

Necroptosis interacts with apoptosis, autophagy, inflammatory (NF-κB) and metabolic (PDC-centered) signaling under different conditions — shared nodes (RIPK1, caspase-8, cFLIP) route cells between programs, which is why single-pathway inhibition can reroute rather than rescue death.
Frames the vault's cFLIP/ripoptosome and PANoptosis raw material as instances of one principle: cell-death modalities are a coupled network, not isolated pathways.
Source: PMC13171333 ↗
Figure 1 · Moujalled 2014 ◆

TNF + Smac-Mimetic: Apoptosis Without MLKL or Drp1

Genotype confirmation (MLKL/RIPK3/Drp1 blots — note Drp1−/− MEFs lack detectable RIPK3/MLKL) and TNF + smac-mimetic killing curves: WT and Mlkl−/− MEFs die rapidly by QVD-blockable, caspase-dependent apoptosis; Drp1−/− MEFs die more slowly (48–72 h) but still die — reconstituting Drp1 restores WT kinetics. Neither MLKL nor Drp1 is essential for this apoptotic arm.
The control experiment that isolates the question: TNF + smac-mimetic death here is apoptosis, so any MLKL/Drp1 requirement tested next is specific to RIPK3-driven necroptosis.
Source: PMC3944236 ↗
Figure 2 · Moujalled 2014 ◆

RIPK3 Dimerization Kills Without Drp1

Inducible RIPK3–gyrase dimerization (4HT + coumermycin, no TNF needed) kills ~70% of WT, Mlkl−/− and Drp1−/− MEFs. QVD halves WT death but fully protects Mlkl−/− (their death is purely apoptotic) while Drp1−/− cells still die under QVD. The Drp1 GTPase inhibitor mdivi-1 protects neither viability nor clonogenicity.
Refutes the PGAM5→Drp1 obligatory-step model (Wang et al. 2012): mitochondrial fragmentation via Drp1 is dispensable for necroptosis execution — MLKL is the obligatory effector.
Source: PMC3944236 ↗
Figure 3 · Moujalled 2014 ◆

Active MLKL Alone Suffices — No Drp1 Needed

Doxycycline-induced WT MLKL kills some WT MEFs; dominant-active mutants Q343A and phosphomimetic SSEE (S345E/S347E) kill potently — in both WT and Drp1−/− MEFs, unblockable by QVD. Co-inducing RIPK3–gyrase amplifies killing of Drp1−/− cells, still QVD-insensitive: necroptosis downstream of MLKL needs no Drp1.
Sufficiency to match the necessity result: MLKL activation is the terminal, Drp1-independent execution step — consistent with MLKL pore formation at the plasma membrane, not mitochondrial fission, as the lethal event.
Source: PMC3944236 ↗

Parthanatos Plus — Mechanism, Therapy & Open Questions (raw)

The vault's 2006–2009 parthanatos core, extended with raw material: the most-cited mechanistic review (Fatokun 2014), the PAR→AIF key-event synthesis (Wang 2009), the companion paper proving PAR releases AIF (Yu 2006), the 2024 Dawson-lab therapeutic update (Yang 2024), and the open-questions roadmap (Moura 2024). Raw material pending ingestion.

From: Fatokun et al., Br J Pharmacol 2014 · Wang et al., Exp Neurol 2009 · Yu et al., PNAS 2006 · Yang et al., Biochem Pharmacol 2024 · Moura et al., Genet Mol Biol 2024 · related raw: Yu et al., Science 2002 (PARP-1→AIF founding abstract; paywalled, no OA figures).

Fig 1 · Fatokun 2014 ◆

PARP-1 Primary Structure

The 116 kDa PARP-1 in three domains: N-terminal DNA-binding domain (two zinc fingers + NLS), central automodification domain (BRCT motif with regulatory phosphorylation sites), and C-terminal catalytic domain (NAD+-binding site + PAR-synthesizing domain) — ~92% human–mouse homology, highest in the catalytic PARP signature.
Structure explains the 500-fold activation on DNA damage: break sensing (zinc fingers) coupled to processive PAR synthesis — mild damage → repair, profound damage → long branched lethal polymers.
Source: PMC3976618 ↗
Fig 2 · Fatokun 2014 ◆

The Full Parthanatos Cascade

NMDA → Ca2+ → nNOS → NO + mitochondrial superoxide → peroxynitrite → DNA damage → PARP-1 overactivation → PAR accumulation (NAD+/ATP depletion marked as non-causal, ???) → PAR binds the PAR-binding site on AIF → mitochondrial release → nuclear translocation → ~50 kb DNA fragmentation and chromatin condensation via the still-unidentified PAAN (parthanatos AIF-associated nuclease).
The canonical diagram of the field (~600 citations): every arrow is a therapeutic hypothesis, and the two explicit unknowns — energy-depletion causality (rejected) and PAAN identity (open) — still organize the literature a decade later.
Source: PMC3976618 ↗
Fig 3 · Fatokun 2014 ◆

Therapeutic Opportunities in the Cascade

Intervention map from toxic stimulus to death: PARP blockers (most advanced, several in clinical trials), PAR neutralization / PARG potentiation, AIF-release blockade, and downstream nuclease inhibition — each boxed intervention linked to the cascade step it modulates, spanning stroke, trauma, Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, diabetes and ischemia-reperfusion indications.
Note the oncology inversion flagged in the review: in cancer the same PARP inhibitors are used to promote death (blocking repair in repair-deficient tumors) rather than prevent it.
Source: PMC3976618 ↗
Fig 1 · Wang 2009 ◆

AIF Isoforms & Processing

Five human AIF isoforms from the X-chromosome 16-exon gene: full-length AIF (613 aa) and AIF-exB with FAD-binding (D1), NADH-binding (D2) and C-terminal death (D3) domains plus MLS/NLS signals; short cytosolic AIFsh (exons 10–16, kills without the oxidoreductase part); mitochondrial AIFsh2 (no C-terminus, cannot kill). Precursor (67 kDa) → mature (62 kDa, Met53/Ala54 cleavage) → truncated (57 kDa) processing sets membrane anchoring vs. releasability.
Domain logic: oxidoreductase activity (life) is separable from the C-terminal death function — and from PAR binding, which uses a distinct site from DNA binding (Wang et al. 2011).
Source: PMC2752872 ↗
Fig 2 · Wang 2009 ◆

PAR Generation vs. Degradation

Severe DNA damage excessively activates PARPs (PARP-1 >90% of polymer): NAD+ → PAR + nicotinamide, conjugated onto PARP-1 itself and nuclear acceptors (10–500× polymer surge, hundreds of units long). PARG rapidly hydrolyzes ribose–ribose bonds to free ADP-ribose — the synthesis/degradation race whose outcome (PAR accumulation vs. clearance) decides parthanatos.
Pairs with the vault's PARG-dosage genetics (Andrabi 2006 Fig 4): PARG transgenics resist stroke, heterozygotes are sensitized, nulls die at E3.5 — regulation of PAR levels is survival-critical.
Source: PMC2752872 ↗
Fig 3 · Wang 2009 ◆

The Four-Step Parthanatos Model

Step 1: DNA damage (NMDA → Ca2+/nNOS/NO/superoxide → peroxynitrite; or MNNG, ROS, H2O2, radiation) activates PARP-1. Step 2: PAR polymer forms in the nucleus and translocates to mitochondria. Step 3: PAR mediates AIF release and nuclear translocation. Step 4: AIF drives chromatin condensation and large-scale (~50 kb) DNA fragmentation by an unknown mechanism.
The commitment-point framing: AIF nuclear translocation — not caspase activation, not energy collapse — is the point of no return, making PAR-mediated AIF release the prime therapeutic target for neurologic disorders.
Source: PMC2752872 ↗
Fig 1 · Yu 2006 ◆

The AIF-Releasing Factor Is Not Protein

PARP-1-activated HeLa/neuronal nuclear supernatant SN(PAR) releases AIF from isolated mouse-brain mitochondria (COX IV-controlled); control supernatant and NAD+ alone do not. Proteinase K degrades most supernatant protein yet AIF-releasing activity survives — the signal is non-proteinaceous.
The experiment that cornered PAR: whatever PARP-1 makes that releases AIF survives protease digestion — pointing at the nucleic-acid-like polymer, not a protein death factor.
Source: PMC1838748 ↗
Fig 2 · Yu 2006 ◆

Purified PAR Releases AIF — Length & Dose

Purified PAR induces AIF release like SN(PAR); proteinase K cannot stop it, but PARG/PD1 degradation of the polymer abolishes release. 16-mer releases little, 30-mer modest, >60-mer robust AIF release at 80 nM; 20 nM is the threshold with rising release to 160 nM — the same toxic species (Andrabi 2006) is the AIF-releasing species.
Companion-paper lockstep with Andrabi 2006: identical length/dose rules for killing (Fig 2 there) and for AIF release (here) — one polymer, one mechanism.
Source: PMC1838748 ↗
Fig 3 · Yu 2006 ◆

Cytosolic PAR After NMDA: 15 → 30 → 60 min

Confocal tracking after 5-min NMDA (500 μM): at 15 min PAR is nuclear; by 30–60 min it forms a cytoplasmic rim around the nucleus and reaches the mitochondrial fraction (colocalized with cytochrome oxidase) — the nucleus→cytosol→mitochondria journey visualized in intact neurons, confirmed by fractionation blots.
The spatiotemporal proof of the crosstalk model: PAR is made in the nucleus within minutes and arrives at mitochondria before AIF departs — signal precedes execution.
Source: PMC1838748 ↗
Fig 5 · Yu 2006 ◆

NMDA → PAR → AIF Translocation in Neurons

NMDA drives AIF mitochondrial→nuclear translocation in cortical neurons; degrading PAR (PARG overexpression) or neutralizing it prevents translocation and death, while Harlequin (AIF-low) neurons resist both NMDA and purified-PAR killing — with viral AIF re-expression restoring susceptibility.
Closing the in-vitro→in-vivo loop: endogenous PAR is necessary for NMDA-induced AIF translocation, and AIF is necessary for PAR-induced killing — the double-necessity proof of the PAR→AIF axis.
Source: PMC1838748 ↗
Fig 1 · Yang 2024 ◆

Parthanatos Mechanism — 2024 State

Up-to-date Dawson-lab mechanism diagram: PARP-1 hyperactivation → PAR accumulation → mitochondrial AIF release → AIF–MIF nuclease nuclear complex → large-scale DNA fragmentation — now including the H1.2 PAR carrier, Iduna (RNF146) PAR-dependent regulation, hexokinase-1 bioenergetics, HDAC6, and the neuroinflammatory arm driving Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, Huntington's, ALS and stroke pathology.
What's new since 2014: PAR is ferried (H1.2), AIF's nuclease partner is identified (MIF), and parthanatos is framed as a disease-modifying target — no FDA-approved anti-death drugs exist yet.
Source: PMC11410548 ↗
Fig 2 · Yang 2024 ◆

Intervention Points in Parthanatos

Druggable nodes mapped onto the pathway: PARP-1 inhibition, PAR accumulation blockade (PARG/Iduna axis), AIF-release and AIF–MIF interaction blockade, plus bioenergetic (HK-1) and neuroinflammation modulators — the medicinal-chemistry shortlist for disease-modifying parthanatos drugs.
The 2024 successor to Fatokun's 2014 Fig 3: same cascade logic, sharper targets now that the AIF–MIF nuclease and PAR-carrier steps are molecularly defined.
Source: PMC11410548 ↗
Fig 3 · Yang 2024 ◆

PARP-1 Structure & Inhibitors

Human PARP-1 domain architecture with inhibitor-binding pockets: the catalytic ART fold shared with bacterial ADP-ribosylating toxins, the helical autoinhibitory subdomain, and the DNA-binding zinc-finger / WGR activation apparatus — the structural basis for the PARP-inhibitor guide (clinical oncology drugs repurposed toward anti-parthanatos neuroprotection).
Structure-guided angle missing from earlier reviews: which inhibitor chemotypes trap vs. merely inhibit PARP-1 matters for whether blockade is cytoprotective or cytotoxic by context.
Source: PMC11410548 ↗
Fig 4 · Yang 2024 ◆

AIF & MIF: The Executioner Complex

Structures of AIF (FAD/NADH oxidoreductase folds + C-terminal death domain + distinct PAR-binding vs. DNA-binding surfaces) and macrophage migration inhibitory factor (MIF) — the long-sought PAAN: AIF recruits MIF's nuclease activity to chromatin to execute large-scale DNA fragmentation.
Closes the decade-old PAAN gap flagged in Fatokun 2014: the AIF-associated nuclease is MIF, and the AIF–MIF protein–protein / nuclease interfaces are now the most downstream druggable step in parthanatos.
Source: PMC11410548 ↗
Fig 1 · Moura 2024 ◆

Which Death Does PARP1 Hyperactivation Cause?

Branching diagram of candidate pathways downstream of PARP1 hyperactivation — parthanatos vs. NAD+-depletion / glycolysis-block vs. overlapping apoptosis/necrosis programs — with the discriminating tests (PARP1-dependence, PAR sufficiency, AIF necessity, caspase independence) that assign a given death to parthanatos rather than its mimics.
The skeptic's figure: strict PARP1-dependence defines parthanatos, but most downstream events remain mechanistically underdetermined — the review lists per-section open questions instead of answers.
Source: PMC11445734 ↗
Fig 2 · Moura 2024 ◆

The Energetic Cost of the Suicide Hypothesis

A full NAD+-salvage cycle costs four high-energy phosphate groups per NAD+ molecule consumed by PARP1 — the quantitative backbone of the energy-depletion ("suicide") hypothesis, presented here to weigh it fairly against PAR-signaling evidence (mitochondrial NAD+ preserved during genotoxic stress; PARP-1-KO infarct protection without energy-status difference).
Why the debate persists: the cost is real and large, yet compartment-specific measurements keep exonerating energy collapse as the primary cause — the field's longest-running unresolved quantity.

PANoptosis — The PANoptosome (raw)

Pyroptosis + apoptosis + necroptosis executed collectively through one cytoplasmic complex — the PANoptosome (NLRP3/ASC/RIPK1/RIPK3/caspase-8/FADD/ZBP1) — letting host defense circumvent pathogen blockade of any single pathway. Raw material pending ingestion.

From: Christgen et al., Front Cell Infect Microbiol 10:237 (2020) · Samir et al., Front Cell Infect Microbiol 10:238 (2020) · Malireddi et al., Front Cell Infect Microbiol 9:406 (2019)

Figure 1 · Christgen 2020 ◆

Pathogens That Trigger PANoptosis

Influenza A virus, vesicular stomatitis virus, Salmonella Typhimurium and Listeria monocytogenes each induce macrophage death with hallmarks of all three programs. Genetic blockade design: pyroptosis blocked by CASP1/11 or GSDMD deletion, necroptosis by RIPK3/MLKL deletion, extrinsic apoptosis by caspase-8 deletion — single deletions barely protect, combined deletion of caspase-1/11 + RIPK3 + caspase-8 largely does.
The functional proof of collective execution: no single-arm knockout rescues, the triple blockade does — death is the OR-gate output of three programs, not one.
Source: DOI 10.3389/fcimb.2020.00237 ↗
Figure 1 · Samir 2020 ◆

The PANoptosome: Components & Assembly

Review-scale model of the PANoptosome: putative components (ASC, caspase-1, RIPK1, RIPK3, ZBP1, caspase-8 and adaptors), their interaction domains/motifs (RHIM, DED, CARD, PYD) from phylogenetic analysis, genetic scaffolding vs. catalytic evidence, and alternative assembly models linking the complex to autoinflammatory, neurodegenerative, neoplastic, infectious and metabolic disease.
Domain grammar is the argument: shared RHIM/DED/CARD/PYD motifs make one multimeric complex physically plausible — the same motifs (RHIM, DED) that run the vault's necrosome/ripoptosome figures.

ZBP1 & TAK1: Master Regulators

The innate sensor ZBP1 and the survival kinase TAK1 orchestrate the RIPK1/RIPK3–FADD–caspase-8 complex and its versatility in executing pyroptosis, apoptosis and necroptosis (PANoptosis) — with therapeutic implications of targeting these regulators in infection, inflammation and cancer.
Connects to the vault's non-classical necrosome (ZBP1/DAI–RIPK3, RIPK1-independent): the same sensor that bypasses RIPK1 in necroptosis also commands the triple-program complex.
