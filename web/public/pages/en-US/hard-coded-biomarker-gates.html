Individual Biomarker Gates (hard-coded)

Mechanistic literature establishes what a compound does. It rarely establishes whether the effect occurs in a given individual. This page compiles the discrete, largely immutable individual values that determine treatment response, quantifies the proportion of exposed patients on the adverse side of each value, and provides a profile tool for recording measured values alongside current therapy.

Rationale

Response is a property of the compound and the host

The source corpus is organised around mechanism: mitohormesis, sirtuins, autophagy, senescence, oxidative stress, epigenetics, cell death, and catecholamine oxidation. The subject index contains 3,174 notes across 12 topic areas. Within that corpus, the to whom component of a treatment claim — the stratification variable that determines which patients respond — is addressed explicitly in three instances.

Three gates already established in the corpus

The profile tool below carries all seventeen, and the extended panel sets them out side by side. Three warrant naming here because they are the ones already worked through in this repository, and because each fails in a different mode: COMT reverses the direction of a benefit, the urolithin metabotype removes the mechanism's first step for most of the population, and karyotype changes which cell-death programme is available. Source documents, from the list above: task_output_comt_vitamin_e_genotype_gate_24_Sep_2026, task_output_gender_specific_attributes_vault_topics_02_SEP_2026, and task_output_hardcoded_individual_biomarker_gates_26_Sep_2026.

Interpretation. These findings do not indicate that α-tocopherol is unsuitable as a class. They indicate that in approximately one quarter of users the expected direction of effect is reversed, and that this is detectable before exposure. The correct response is measurement prior to treatment, not revision of the mechanistic account.

Definition

Criteria for a hard-coded gate

A hard-coded individual biomarker is a value assigned early in life and stable over decades: a germline genotype, a somatic loss-of-function allele, a microbial functional phenotype, or a karyotype. This is distinguished from a continuous laboratory value (ferritin, hs-CRP, fasting insulin, soluble Klotho), which is a modifiable readout of current physiological state.

The distinction is clinically relevant because hard-coded values are determinable before exposure. A continuous value describes the patient at the point of measurement and is subject to change; a hard-coded gate can be established before the first dose rather than inferred retrospectively from an unsuccessful trial. Because these values are not modifiable by behavioural intervention, an unrecognised gate remains unrecognised for the duration of treatment.

Evidence grading

GradeCriterionActionable implication
A Replicated in two or more independent human cohorts, or mechanistic data with concordant human pharmacogenetic findings Sufficient to inform treatment selection before exposure
B Single strong human cohort, or consistent human data with mechanistic support; not replicated Sufficient to justify measurement; not sufficient to alter treatment
C Association only, confounded by ancestry, or with documented genotype–phenotype discordance Assay activity directly; treat the genotype as a weak prior
D Mechanistically plausible without human outcome data Research priority only. The absence of routine measurement is itself the finding

Genotype–phenotype concordance determines the assay of choice. Where individuals within a genotype category differ by more than two-fold in measured enzyme activity, the genotype provides only a weak prior and the activity assay is the informative measurement. PON1 diazoxonase activity varies more within genotype categories than between them (Jarvik 2000), and the direction of the SOD2 rs4880 effect on MnSOD activity is inconsistent across primary studies. [[Val158Met]] is unusually reliable because COMT activity is closely determined by rs4680 (3–4-fold separation with limited within-category variance); this degree of concordance is uncommon. Where genotype predicts function, genotype is sufficient; where it does not, measure the enzyme.

Profile Tool

Recording measured values against current therapy

Enter the values that have been measured. A row left unassigned is recorded as not measured, which corresponds to the default condition in clinical practice. Select the therapies currently taken or under consideration; the output identifies those for which the stratifying value has not been established.

Each card carries three things in order: why the value is a gate, where it acts — the anatomical compartment in which it is expressed and in which its consequence is executed, which is often not the organ that produces it — and the pathway axis, assay and population shares. The extended panel sets the same gates side by side so the anatomical columns can be read across.

Population shares are approximations derived from the cited cohorts and vary substantially by ancestry. Several gates are uncommon in the populations in which they were first characterised and common elsewhere — for NQO1 T/T, 2–5% in European and Black populations against approximately 20% in Asian populations. Shares indicate the scale of exposure in a population and should not be applied to an individual.

One gate is exempt from all of the above. Karyotype is not sampled from a population — it is read directly off the individual, and reproductive stage is fixed by that individual's age. There is no distribution to draw a percentage from, so this gate carries no ancestry-varying shares, its two XX stages carry no percentage at all, and its row in Figure 3 and in the register is reported as n/a rather than as a small number. Karyotype composition is ancestry-invariant (46,XY ≈ 50%; 45,X pooled birth prevalence 31.5 per 100,000 female live births, 47,XXY 1 in 500–1000 male births). A short bar here would read as "a small fraction is at risk", which is the opposite of the truth: the gate applies in full, to every individual, once the value is known.

Unmeasured Gates

Treatment administered without measurement of the stratifying value

For each intervention in the corpus, the figure below reports the approximate proportion of exposed patients whose stratifying value places them in a consequential state, and the nature of that consequence. Consequential states are: reversal of the expected direction of effect, absence of the mechanism's first step, sub-therapeutic exposure recorded as non-response, and omission of an indicated intervention. A bar of 60% means that in roughly 60% of users the stratifying value has not been established and, on population frequencies, would be expected to alter the outcome.

Classification of consequences

ClassDefinitionClinical presentationExample
Increased risk The stratifying value reverses the direction of the treatment effect. No acute adverse event. Excess risk is detectable only at population scale, over years. α-tocopherol in Val/Val COMT carriers
Predicted no-op The mechanism's entry step is absent or substituted. Nothing downstream happens. Recorded as non-responder; excluded from the trial; the mechanism is attributed as ineffective. Dietary ellagitannins in metabotype-0 individuals
Under-exposed The agent is present at an inadequate concentration; apparent non-response is pharmacokinetic in origin. Non-responder classification applied to an agent that is effective at adequate exposure. Rapamycin in a CYP3A5 expressor maintained on a fixed low dose
Indicated, omitted An indicated intervention is withheld, or an unsuitable one is applied, because the value is unknown. Standard care delivered without reference to the subpopulation that requires modification. Iron management in a C282Y homozygote; hormone replacement in perimenopausal XX individuals
Not measured The value has not been measured in the patient. Not distinguishable from a favourable value without a biomarker, a trial, or an outcome event. The majority of patients for the majority of interventions

Five structural deficiencies

  1. 01 Documentation is concentrated on mechanism rather than on individual variation. The COMT directory contains 46 notes and includes the corpus's only dedicated genotype note, whereas the shared entity pool contains 1,812 notes. Mechanistic coverage is extensive; individual stratification is largely absent.
  2. 02 Microbial phenotypes are less developed than germline genotypes. Urolithin metabotype is characterised; TMAO producer status is not. This is relevant because spermidine, fisetin, berberine, quercetin, resveratrol, and urolithin all act partly through the microbiome, so an incorrect metabotype nullifies the intervention irrespective of dose.
  3. 03 Responder analyses are not reported by sex. Sex differences are documented at pathway level without being carried through to treatment response. SIRT3 rs11555236 was associated with longevity in males in one cohort and in females in another, which is only interpretable when both variables are considered jointly.
  4. 04 The term "metabotype" is applied to two distinct entities — germline genotype (molecular, immutable) and microbial conversion capacity (ecological, modifiable by prebiotics, next-generation probiotics, or antibiotics). These differ in stability, method of measurement, and potential for modification.
  5. 05 Clinical trials rarely stratify by these values. No senolytic trial in the corpus reports a responder analysis stratified by COMT genotype, and no senescence- or ferroptosis-induction trial reports a sex-stratified responder analysis. Fisetin is a validated COMT substrate inhibitor (IC50 2.6–5.8 µM); against a 3–4-fold activity span this implies approximately doubled senolytic exposure in slow metabolisers and halved exposure in fast metabolisers. This has not been measured.

Worked Examples

Four gates in detail

In each of the following, the stratifying value determines whether the mechanism can be engaged at all, rather than modulating its magnitude. These are the four relationships that alter whether an intervention is appropriate, as opposed to its dose.

Urolithin metabotype

Dietary ellagitannins from pomegranate, walnut, and strawberry are not [[Urolithin A]]. They are a substrate which a subset of the population converts to UA as a terminal metabolite, a conversion requiring carriage of Gordonibacter urolithinfaciens. Two further metabotypes are described: type B, in which the pathway proceeds through Ellagibacter isourolithinifaciens and Enterocloster to isourolithin A, and type 0, in which no urolithin is detectable.

COMT genotype and α-tocopherol

Two randomised trials (WGHS, ATBC) reported a gene-dose interaction between COMT genotype and α-tocopherol supplementation: per Val allele, HR 1.11 on supplement against 0.95 on placebo, Pinteraction < .001, with a concordant result at the sister SNP rs4818 (G/G HR 1.29). The association with increased incidence was present at both 50 IU/day and 600 IU alternate-day, indicating that the commonly applied upper limit of 400 IU/day does not mitigate the effect in this group. Val/Val occurs in approximately 29% of European-ancestry and 52% of Han Chinese individuals.

NQO1 C609T and the clearance of quinone redox signal

NQO1 determines the metabolic fate of a quinone. When the enzyme is present, the quinone undergoes two-electron reduction to a stable, detoxifiable hydroquinone. When the enzyme is functionally absent, the quinone is reduced by the CYP450 / b5-reductase pathway via a one-electron route, generating a semiquinone radical that undergoes redox cycling and produces superoxide. The direction of the effect is therefore determined by the genotype: the same exposure constitutes an adaptive redox signal in one group and an oxidative cycling load in another.

CYP3A5 and FKBP1A in rapamycin dosing

Between 20% and 40% of off-label rapamycin users report no benefit. Pharmacokinetic variation accounts for a substantial proportion of this. Sirolimus AUC has a inter-individual coefficient of variation exceeding 40%. CYP3A5 *1 expressors, comprising 15–25% of European-ancestry and 60–70% of African-ancestry individuals, clear sirolimus 40–60% faster. In the Mannick dose-escalation study, 22% of participants maintained on 2 mg weekly had trough concentrations below 1 ng/mL. Classifying such patients as non-responders without a whole-blood trough and a p70S6K1 Thr389 suppression measurement does not distinguish pharmacological non-response from inadequate exposure.

FKBP12 loss-of-function variants represent a separate and non-correctable category: the FKBP12–rapamycin complex does not form, so the mechanism is not initiated at any dose. Dose escalation cannot compensate for a defect in the first step of the pathway.

Recommended Testing

Minimum panel

Three assays account for the largest share of consequential exposure across the interventions in this analysis. Between them they address one intervention that increases risk in part of its user population, one that is ineffective in the majority of users, and one in which apparent non-response is attributable to sub-therapeutic exposure.

Minimal three-assay panel

COMT rs4680, urolithin metabotype, and CYP3A5 status, with whole-blood sirolimus trough and p70S6K1 Thr389 as the pharmacodynamic readouts. The first separates a harmful from an appropriate intervention, the second an ineffective from an effective route of administration, and the third pharmacological non-response from inadequate exposure.

Six patterns in the anatomical columns

These do not fit in a per-gate row, and they are the reason the column is worth reading across rather than down.

  1. 01 Produced in one organ, executed in another. APOE4 is made by hepatocytes and acts on brain microglia; PON1 is made in the liver and acts at the arterial intima; TMAO is produced in the colon, detoxified in the liver and acts on the vessel wall; urolithin A is produced in the colon and acts in muscle, adipose, liver and brain. For these the assay compartment and the consequence compartment are anatomically separated, which is why plasma surrogates misreport and why the organ that manufactures the effector is not the organ that suffers.
  2. 02 Gates that set a pathway's first step. MC1R sets which pigment is synthesised, NQO1 sets which reduction route a quinone takes, FKBP12 sets whether the drug–protein complex forms at all, TERT sets whether replicative immortality is available. No dose compensates for a missing first step, which is why these are determinants of admissibility rather than of magnitude.
  3. 03 Gates that set exposure, not response. CYP3A5, CYP1A2/ADORA2A and TMA producer status all change how much active compound reaches the tissue. All are correctable by dose, and all are currently misreported as pharmacological non-response.
  4. 04 Gates that set a window rather than a target. SOD2 rs4880 sets the width of the hormetic window through the SIRT3/SIRT4 ratio; reproductive stage sets a trajectory rather than a current value. A numeric threshold is the wrong output format for either.
  5. 05 Gates whose sign flips with the indication. NQO1 T/T is harmful in a quinone redox-donor/terminator protocol and disqualifying in NQO1-bioactivated chemotherapy. APOE4 is missed therapy for cGAS inhibition and predicted no-op for a ketogenic diet. Severity is therefore a property of the (genotype, intervention) pair, never of the genotype alone.
  6. 06 Gates that are modifiers of other gates. TERT germline rs2853669 expresses only against a somatic TERT promoter mutation; the SIRT3/SIRT6 longevity variants express only against karyotype; KL-VS expresses only against APOE ε4 dosage; menopausal status modifies the HFE iron threshold. This compositional structure is not visible in a flat list of gates and is the most common source of wrong conclusions drawn from single-assay panels.

Gates that already determine admissibility

A small number of values already determine whether an intervention is permissible rather than what its dose should be. [[G6PD deficiency]] is a documented contraindication for [[Methylene blue]]: an oxidant challenge in an enzyme-null individual is not adjustable by dose. Transferrin saturation above 45% with ferritin above 300 ng/mL in men and postmenopausal women, or above 200 ng/mL in premenopausal women, is the established threshold for phlebotomy. A C282Y homozygote above these thresholds has an indicated iron-directed intervention, and an antioxidant-only regimen addresses a consequence rather than the underlying iron accumulation. Prevalent iron overload is more common in men than in premenopausal women, since menstrual blood loss constitutes an iron sink; this connects the HFE gate to the karyotype gate.

Limitations

Scope and limitations

  • No entry constitutes a treatment recommendation. Several relationships — α-tocopherol in Val/Val COMT carriers, and quinone redox protocols in NQO1-null individuals — have sufficient mechanistic and observational support to warrant caution, but do not constitute validated clinical rules.
  • Findings do not generalise across ancestries. The COMT–α-tocopherol interaction was established in two European-ancestry cohorts, while Val/Val occurs in approximately 29% of European-ancestry and 52% of Han Chinese individuals. NQO1 T/T occurs in 2–5% of European and Black populations and approximately 20% of Asian populations. Application of any panel outside the population in which the relationship was established constitutes extrapolation.
  • The finding is unmeasured values, not genetic determinism. See Rationale.
  • Continuous biomarkers are excluded by design. Fasting insulin, hs-CRP, soluble Klotho, ferritin, and the epigenetic clocks are appropriate pharmacodynamic monitors and inappropriate stratification variables. They are therefore not included as inputs in the profile tool.
  • Grade D entries are research priorities. mtDNA haplogroup, KEAP1/NRF2 haplotypes as a dosing variable, and X-chromosome inactivation skewing are included because routine measurement is absent, not because a protocol can be specified.
  • Documented negative results are retained. GPX1 Pro200Leu does not substantially modify whole-blood GPx response to selenium supplementation (Miller 2012). A candidate gate that was tested and did not stratify response remains part of the evidence base.
