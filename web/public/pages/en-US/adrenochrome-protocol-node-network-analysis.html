Knowledge-Graph Trace Log · Adrenochrome

[Speculative] Network Analysis of the Adrenochrome Protocol: Agent Coupling to the Adrenochrome Node Relative to Mitohormesis, Autophagy, and Sirtuins

The proposed protocol combines methylene blue (electron shunt around Complex I/III) with aminoguanidine (dicarbonyl/AGE trapping) under a proposed redox-relay model. Whether this combination generates a controlled adrenochrome redox pulse in vivo is untested — the only direct chemical link in the literature is an in-vitro hemostatic manufacturing process (US4501923A). Engagement of mitohormesis, autophagy, and sirtuins is hypothesised, remains untested in humans, and is not quantified. This page reports stored-edge paths from each protocol agent to the adrenochrome node in the llm-wiki-jk knowledge graph. This page is not a clinical recommendation — see the safety note in §01.

01 · Objective and scope

Redox-Injury and Low-Dose Hormesis Interpretations of Adrenochrome

The vault contains two interpretations of adrenochrome. The redox-injury interpretation describes a redox-cycling catecholamine oxidation product that generates ROS and depletes glutathione; ferroptosis in cardiomyocytes and dopaminergic neurons is hypothesised and remains untested in humans. The protocol interpretation treats adrenochrome as a low-dose redox stressor whose ROS output is proposed to engage mitohormesis, autophagy, and sirtuins; this dose-dependent engagement is a hypothesis, not quantified and untested in humans. Both interpretations refer to the same adrenochrome node in the graph build.

A prior analysis traced stored paths from the adrenochrome node to the three adaptive programs. This analysis covers the proposed protocol agents: methylene blue (MB, electron shunt around Complex I/III), aminoguanidine (AG, dicarbonyl/AGE trapping), and the supporting agents (carbazochrome, vitamin C, urolithin A, spermidine, rapamycin, fisetin, creatine, resveratrol) proposed in the outline document under the mitohormetic-redox-relay (MRR), glyco-oxidative-proteostasis (GOPS) and SASP-remodeling-aminochrome (SRAC) mechanisms. These mechanisms are hypotheses, untested in humans. Each listed agent was traced against build 273eba0b1872c83844c9cf5dcabbde8c94c16060.

Primary objective

Classify each protocol agent's connection to the adrenochrome node as a direct chemical edge (reaction, derivation, reduction) or shared redox/injury bridge, and report its Fiedler coordinate and PPR coupling relative to the adaptive programs.

Sub-objectives: (a) compare PPR coupling of the core agents (MB, AG) with that of the adaptive programs; (b) identify coupling modes (direct edge vs shared bridges); (c) compare the protocol traces with the four-node baseline for reproduction, extension, or contradiction.

Dose dependence of the hormesis hypothesis

A knowledge graph stores edge relations, not dose-response data. The designation of adrenochrome as a low-dose hormetic agent is a low-dose hypothesis: its ROS/electrophile chemistry is proposed to engage adaptive signalling rather than saturate it. Dose is not quantified in this graph. The network analysis reported here does not depend on dose; the hormetic claim remains untested in humans and requires evaluation against primary literature (see §10).

Safety — why this page is not a protocol

  • Methylene blue: FDA boxed warning — potent reversible MAO-A inhibitor; serotonergic agents (SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs/meperidine) must be held (≥2 weeks, fluoxetine 5 weeks) and resumed ≥24 h after MB. Contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (hemolysis, Heinz bodies). Adverse effects above 7 mg/kg; the 0.5–4 mg "wellness" range is community convention, not an approved indication (label: methemoglobinemia, 1 mg/kg IV).
  • Aminoguanidine (pimagedine): Phase III ACTION I/II in diabetic nephropathy failed their primary endpoints and the programme was terminated for safety (glomerulonephritis, pyridoxine/B6 depletion, ANCA-associated vasculitis, diamine-oxidase inhibition/histamine intolerance). Never approved.
  • Adrenochrome / AMM / carbazochrome: no human safety, pharmacokinetic, or dosing data. Carbazochrome is a redox-silent hemostatic semicarbazone, not a hormetic trigger; AMM is a hydrazone-capped derivative — neither free-cycles like adrenochrome. Vitamin C + adrenochrome is pro-oxidant redox-cycling in vitro, not a clean antidote.
  • Evidence gaps flagged in this revision: adrenochrome → NF-κB inhibition / SASP suppression (SRAC) has no preclinical senescence data; GPX4 arylation by adrenochrome is an untested hypothesis (catecholamine ferroptosis per se is supported).

02 · Protocol agents

Protocol agents, proposed roles, and graph nodes

The outline document proposes three mechanisms: the Mitohormetic Redox-Relay (MRR) (a controlled oxidative pulse plus bioenergetic fuel), the Glyco-Oxidative Proteostasis Shield (GOPS) (aminoguanidine trapping dicarbonyls and AGEs), and the SASP-Remodeling Aminochrome Complex (SRAC) (adrenochrome/AMM as the senomorphic-redox component). These mechanisms are hypotheses; their biological effects are not quantified and remain untested in humans. Each agent was traced against graph build 273eba0b1872c83844c9cf5dcabbde8c94c16060.

03 · Coupling ladder

PPR ladder on the current build: one core agent at the top, the rest mid-pack

Personalized PageRank seeded at Adrenochrome ranks the traced nodes on one ladder. Only Aminoguanidine #4 (0.00686) sits in the top-15 on this build; the other direct-edge agents follow at Carbazochrome #22 (0.00409) and Vitamin C #29 (0.00341), Methylene blue #35 (0.00307), then the adaptive programs Mitohormesis #36 / Autophagy #38 / Sirtuins #232. With the seed reversed, Adrenochrome ranks #3 from Aminoguanidine and #16 from Methylene blue; from the adaptive programs it ranks #13 / #50 / #86. The previous build's "four core agents in the top-15" claim does not reproduce — AG's direct edge remains the strongest single coupling.

Rank separation between direct-reaction agents and the modulation agents

Direct-reaction agents (AG, Carbazochrome, Vitamin C) rank #4–#29 and the systemic modulation agents occupy #146–#272. Direct-reaction agents share walk mass with the adrenochrome node through stored edges; modulators reach it through intermediate programs and bridges. The PPR ladder therefore ranks agents by stored-edge connectivity rather than by the order proposed in the outline document.

MB ⇄ AG share two neighbours: Monoamine oxidase and Nitric Oxide Synthase (Jaccard 0.022)

MB and AG each carry an MAO-inhibition edge (0.92 / 0.9) and an NOS-family edge (MB inhibits sGC/NOS; AG blocks NO production and inhibits iNOS while not reducing eNOS). No stored chemical edge connects the two core agents; both connect to shared intermediaries. The graph structure is consistent with a two-agent protocol rather than a single reaction cascade; the biological interpretation is a hypothesis, untested in humans.

Chemistry pair: carbazochrome and vitamin C

Two further direct edges connect the chemistry group to adrenochrome: Carbazochrome --is_an_intermediate_for(0.9)--> Adrenochrome (plus is_derivative_of 0.95) and Vitamin C --reduces(0.75)--> Adrenochrome (vitamin C additionally prevents_oxidation_of(0.85)). Both sit on the same Fiedler side as every other traced node (Carbazochrome +0.0006, Vitamin C +0.0003, §07) — no chemistry/program sign split on this build. Their pairwise Jaccard(Carbazochrome, Vitamin C) = 0.045.

Their coupling depths differ: Vitamin C PPR #29, effective resistance 0.088 (z −1.27); Carbazochrome PPR #22, effective resistance 0.338 (z −0.77), the largest effective resistance among direct-edge nodes. Carbazochrome is in the adrenochrome community (k-core 3, degree 8). The direct stored edges are present; Carbazochrome is redox-silent by chemistry (semicarbazone-capped o-quinone), so its edge reflects derivation, not redox activity.

Three coupling modes in the trace set

  • Chemical reaction (AG reacts_with) — direct stored edge, no bridge, PPR #4.
  • Derivation / reduction (Carbazochrome is_an_intermediate_for, Vitamin C reduces) — direct stored edges on derivative / reducer nodes.
  • Functional modulation via shared bridges (MB and the modulation agents) — no direct edge; shares Complex I / oxidative-stress / ROS bridges with adrenochrome.

06 · Structural findings

Arm structure on the current build: multiplicity, weights, and the NRF2 sirtuin path

Three arms — updated multiplicities and weights

Arm A (mitohormetic signalling):  Mitohormesis --requires(0.91)--> ROS --generates(0.90)--> Adrenochrome   (1 of 6 shortest paths; others via Catalase, NRF2, Ferroptosis, Adrenochromatization, Reactive Oxygen Species)
Arm B (autophagic clearance):    Autophagy --associated_with(0.95)--> Neuromelanin --sequesters(0.80)--> Adrenochrome   (1 of 5 shortest paths)
Arm C (sirtuin/NRF2):            Sirtuins --is_co_activated_by(0.80)-- NRF2 --(Adrenochrome activates NRF2, 0.80)-- Adrenochrome   (sole shortest path, undirected)

The previous build's "exactly one rate-limiting bridge per arm" reading does not reproduce. Arms A and B each have multiple co-shortest paths on this build, so no single bridge is rate-limiting by hop count. Arm C no longer runs through SASP/NF-κB at all: the Adrenochrome —inhibits→ NF-κB 0.6 triple recorded in the prior build is absent from the current graph (no edge in either direction between adrenochrome and nf_kappab), so the senomorphic direction-audit story and the bridge-perturbation sweep computed against the old build are retired. Sirtuins now reach adrenochrome only via NRF2. The Sirtuins —suppress(0.85)→ SASP edge still exists but is not on a shortest path. The sweep is queued for re-computation (§09) and its old numbers are removed rather than carried forward.

What changed in the graph, and what it does to the old story

NF-κB entity fragmentation is largely resolved on this build (NF-κB is a single deg-140 hub; only NF-kB signaling deg 2 remains as a near-duplicate), so the former "≥7 variants distribute walk mass" caveat no longer applies to Arm C. The senomorphic hypothesis (adrenochrome inhibits NF-κB → suppresses SASP) has no preclinical senescence data and no stored triple on this build — it is retained only as a labelled hypothesis, not as a computed result.

07 · Community boundary

Fiedler coordinates on the current build: no chemistry/program split

λ₂ (algebraic connectivity) = 0.0994. On this build all 14 traced nodes cluster on the same side of the Fiedler axis at small positive values (Adrenochrome +0.0005; range +0.0003 Aminoguanidine/Vitamin C/Urolithin A to +0.0008 Autophagy). The prior build's negative/positive community boundary between the chemistry group and the program layer does not reproduce — and since Fiedler sign is eigenvector-arbitrary, only the magnitudes and λ₂ are meaningful. The reading adopted here is therefore negative: no sign-based layer separation is supported on this build. Fig.7 plots the coordinates at their true values on a compressed axis so the clustering is visible rather than implied by a split.

H2/H8 status after the re-run

The former H2/H8 ("adrenochrome and its chemical family share a negative Fiedler sign opposite the program layer") is retired as a result and kept only as a falsifiable hypothesis for future builds (§08): a re-run that produced a consistent sign split would revive it; the current build falsifies it. Peripheral low-degree nodes (Carbazochrome deg 8, Vitamin C deg 21) remain the least stable Fiedler members under rebuild regardless of sign.
