Glycation & Advanced Glycation End-Product Chemistry

The non-enzymatic chemistry that shares its electrophilic intermediates with catecholamine oxidation — Schiff base, Amadori product, dicarbonyl, and cross-link formation on lysine and arginine residues.

From: Luevano-Contreras & Chapman-Novakofski, Nutrients 2010 & Kim et al., J Exerc Nutrition Biochem 2017

Figure 1 · AGE Formation

Formation of Advanced Glycation End Products

Reducing sugars condense with the amino groups of proteins and other macromolecules to form a reversible Schiff base, which rearranges to a more stable Amadori product. Subsequent oxidation, dehydration, and fragmentation generate the heterogeneous AGE pool — N-(carboxymethyl)lysine (CML), N-(carboxyethyl)lysine (CEL), pentosidine, pyrraline, and hydroimidazolone — over days to weeks.
The electrophilic dicarbonyl intermediates on this route (methylglyoxal, glyoxal, 3-deoxyglucosone) are the same class of species the adrenochrome notes group under glyco-oxidative proteostasis; both pathways converge on lysine and arginine side chains.
Source: PMC3257625 ↗
Figure 2 · Receptor Signalling

Mechanism of AGE Action at the Cell Level

AGEs signal through RAGE and other receptors to activate NADPH oxidase, increase reactive oxygen species, and drive NF-κB-dependent transcription of inflammatory mediators. AGE–RAGE signalling is reported in endothelial cells, macrophages, and podocytes, and is linked to arterial stiffening through extracellular matrix cross-linking.
Receptor-level amplification, not the cross-link itself, is the step that converts accumulated glyco-oxidative damage into sustained inflammatory signalling — the mechanism linking dietary and endogenous AGE load to the chronic inflammation described in the adrenochrome notes.
Source: PMC3257625 ↗
Figure 1 · Reaction Sequence

Glycation Process Leading to AGE Formation

Sequence diagram of glycation: glucose or a reactive dicarbonyl attacks a protein amino group, the Schiff base rearranges into an Amadori product, and further oxidation and cross-linking produce fluorescent and non-fluorescent AGEs. Illustrations adapted from Bohlender et al. (2005).
The reaction is slow under normoglycaemia and accelerates with hyperglycaemia and oxidative tone, which is why the adrenochrome notes treat AGE accumulation as a time-integrated readout of combined glycaemic and redox load.
Source: PMC5643203 ↗
Figure 2 · AGE Structures

Biologically Relevant Advanced Glycation End-Products

Structures of the AGE species used as biomarkers and as cross-link measurements in tissue: CML, CEL, pentosidine, pyrraline, and the arginine-derived hydroimidazolones. Illustrations adapted from Sadowska-Bartosz & Bartosz (2016).
These are the same analytes the vault tracks as protein-damage readouts in its glyco-oxidative proteostasis notes, alongside the enzymatic removal routes (glyoxalase, PARK7/DJ-1, FN3K, cross-link breakers).
Source: PMC5643203 ↗

NAD+ Precursor Biology

De novo, Preiss–Handler, and salvage routes to NAD+, and the precursor-specific enzymes (NAMPT, NRK1/2, NMNAT) that set the cofactor supply used by sirtuins and PARPs.

From: Alegre & Pastore, Curr Nutr Rep 2023 & Mehmel et al., Nutrients 2020

Figure 1 · Precursor Structures

NMN and NR — Structures and Reported Effects

Chemical structures of nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) and nicotinamide riboside (NR) with the health effects attributed to each in the reviewed literature: NAD+ repletion, mitochondrial function, insulin sensitivity, and neuroprotection. Both are pyridine-nucleoside precursors entering the salvage pathway.
The adrenochrome protocol notes list NMN/NR as the NAD+-repletion arm, on the rationale that sirtuin activity and PARP-mediated repair are substrate-limited when NAD+ falls.
Source: PMC10240123 ↗
Figure 2 · Biosynthesis

NAD Biosynthesis Pathways in Humans and Plants

Human (A) and plant (B) NAD biosynthesis. The de novo route converts tryptophan through kynurenine and quinolinic acid; the Preiss–Handler route converts nicotinic acid via NAAD; the salvage route converts nicotinamide (NAMPT), NR (NRK1/2), and NMN (NMNAT) back to NAD+. Dotted arrows mark putative steps. NMN uptake is proposed via CD73-mediated dephosphorylation to NR or the SLC12A8 transporter.
Compartment matters: the adrenochrome notes distinguish cytosolic NRK1 from mitochondrial NRK2 when arguing that NR can raise mitochondrial NAD+ more directly than nicotinamide.
Source: PMC10240123 ↗
Figure 1 · Salvage Routes

NAD+ Synthesis Pathways

The de novo pathway from tryptophan through quinolinic acid (QA), the Preiss–Handler pathway from nicotinic acid (NA) through NAAD and NAD synthetase (NADS), and the salvage pathways from nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) via purine nucleoside phosphorylase, NAMPT, NRK, and NMNAT.
Salvage, not de novo synthesis, supplies most mammalian NAD+; this is the basis for the precursor-dosing component of the vault's NAD+-repletion notes.
Source: PMC7352172 ↗
Figure 2 · Cardiac NRK2

NMRK2 Activation in the Failing Heart

Activation of the NMRK2 pathway is reported as a common adaptive response in the failing heart, where NAD+ is low. Synthesis from NR via NRK enzymes costs one ATP per NMN, whereas synthesis from nicotinamide via NAMPT costs at least three ATP equivalents, so the NR route is favoured under energy stress.
A tissue-specific argument for precursor choice: cardiac NRK2 expression is the reported reason NR, rather than nicotinamide, is preferred in the cardiac NAD+ notes.
Source: PMC7352172 ↗

Mitophagy & Urolithin A

The gut-microbiota metabolite urolithin A as a mitophagy inducer — metabolism, bioavailability, and the AMPK / mTOR / PGC-1α / FOXO signalling attributed to it in muscle.

From: Zhao et al., Nutrients 2023

Figure 1 · Pharmacology

Pharmacological Effects of Urolithin A

Overview of the reported pharmacological actions of urolithin A — mitophagy induction, antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, and metabolic regulation — and their proposed contribution to muscle health and athletic performance.
The adrenochrome protocol notes include urolithin A as the mitophagy-inducing arm, used to clear damaged mitochondria after the hormetic redox exposure rather than during it.
Source: PMC10609777 ↗
Figure 2 · Metabolism

Metabolism, Bioavailability & Muscle Impact

Urolithin A is not a dietary compound as such: ellagitannins are converted by gut bacteria (Gordonibacter, Ellagibacter) into urolithins, which then undergo phase II conjugation. The figure maps that conversion, the resulting bioavailability, and the downstream effects on muscle.
Because production depends on the individual microbiota, urolithin A exposure is variable between subjects — a between-person variance the vault's ellagitannin/urolithin notes record as an unresolved dosing problem.
Source: PMC10609777 ↗
Figure 3 · Signalling

Urolithin A Signalling Pathways in Muscle

The signalling network attributed to urolithin A in muscle: PINK1/Parkin-dependent mitophagy, AMPK activation, mTOR modulation, PGC-1α-driven mitochondrial biogenesis, and FOXO transcription-factor activity.
PINK1/Parkin-dependent mitophagy is the same axis the sirtuin notes connect to SIRT1/SIRT3, which is where the adrenochrome, sirtuin, and mitophagy topics meet in the graph.
Source: PMC10609777 ↗

Aminochrome Neurotoxicity & Neuromelanin Synthesis

Adrenochrome is the epinephrine-derived member of the aminochrome class; this literature defines the class chemistry — o-quinone cyclization, redox cycling, DT-diaphorase and GSTM2 detoxification, and polymerization to neuromelanin. Selected 11 Nov 2026 to fill the neurotoxicity gap in the adrenochrome notes; none of these papers is ingested into the vault yet.

From: Segura-Aguilar et al., Antioxidants 2022 · Segura-Aguilar et al., Neural Regen Res 2022 · Silva & Segura-Aguilar, Neural Regen Res 2021

Figure 1 · GSTM2 Structure

Human Glutathione Transferase M2-2 — G-Site and H-Site

Human GST M2-2 rendered from PDB 2C4J, with the glutathione-binding G-site and the hydrophobic H-site marked in each subunit. The bound ligand shown is the glutathione conjugate of S-styrene 7,8-oxide, used as a positional proxy; no aminochrome or aminochrome–glutathione complex structure is available.
GSTM2 is the enzyme that conjugates aminochrome to 4-S-glutathionyl-5,6-dihydroxyindoline, and the structural mapping of the H-site is what makes that conjugation plausible for a bulky o-quinone substrate.
Source: PMC8868244 ↗
Figure 2 · Melanin Synthesis

Synthesis of Neuromelanin

Dopamine oxidation generates three sequential ortho-quinones: dopamine o-quinone, aminochrome, and 5,6-indolequinone. Dopamine o-quinone is stable only below pH 2.0 and cyclizes at physiological pH at 0.15 s−1; aminochrome is the most stable of the three and rearranges to 5,6-indolequinone at 0.06 min−1 (about 40 min), which then polymerizes to neuromelanin.
The rate constants matter for the adrenochrome hypothesis: the corresponding epinephrine-derived quinone cyclizes by the same intramolecular Michael addition, with adrenochrome as the stable branch point and melanin polymerization as the terminal sink.
Source: PMC8868244 ↗
Figure 3 · Neurotoxicity

Aminochrome-Induced Neurotoxicity

DT-diaphorase (NQO1) reduces aminochrome by two electrons to leukoaminochrome, preventing its neurotoxic effects. Leukoaminochrome autoxidizes slowly with dioxygen but substantially faster in the presence of superoxide, which is why cytosolic superoxide dismutase suppresses the reaction. When DT-diaphorase is inhibited, one-electron reduction yields the leukoaminochrome o-semiquinone, which reduces dioxygen and sustains redox cycling.
The NQO1-gated branch is the mechanistic basis for the NQO1 genotype-stratification recommendation in the adrenochrome gap analysis: the same quinone is either detoxified by two-electron reduction or driven into redox cycling.
Source: PMC8868244 ↗
Figure 4 · GSH Conjugation

Aminochrome Conjugation with Glutathione

GST M2-2 conjugates dopamine o-quinone with glutathione to 5-S-glutathionyldopamine, which degrades to 5-cysteinyldopamine — a product found in neuromelanin and in cerebrospinal fluid. GSTM2 also conjugates aminochrome to 4-S-glutathionyl-5,6-dihydroxyindoline, a conjugate resistant to dioxygen, superoxide, and hydrogen peroxide.
This is the second detoxification gate besides NQO1, and it is the reason glutathione status appears throughout the adrenochrome notes as a determinant of whether the quinone is conjugated or left to redox cycle.
Source: PMC8868244 ↗
Figure 5 · Astrocyte Protection

Astrocytes Protect Dopaminergic Neurons

Dopaminergic neurons express DT-diaphorase, but astrocytes provide a second layer: they take up released dopamine via DAT, OCT3, and PMAT, oxidize it to aminochrome, increase GSTM2 expression, and export GSTM2 in exosomes that neurons internalize.
Cell non-autonomous detoxification means neuronal vulnerability depends on glial capacity — a relevant caveat for any systemic adrenochrome exposure argument, where the glial compartment is not modelled.
Source: PMC8868244 ↗
Figure 1 · Metabolic Coupling

Astrocyte–Neuron Interactions

Astrocytes convert glucose to lactate for neuronal ATP production, recycle glutamate to glutamine, and export glutathione, which is degraded to Cys-Gly and then cysteine and glycine for neuronal glutathione synthesis. Astrocytes also secrete proteins including GSTM2, which neurons take up.
The figure frames antioxidant capacity as a shared, glia-dependent pool rather than a neuron-intrinsic property — the constraint on any glutathione-based mitigation proposed for adrenochrome exposure.
Source: PMC8848618 ↗
Figure 2 · Toxicity Cascade

Aminochrome Neurotoxicity

Neuromelanin synthesis requires dopamine o-quinone, which cyclizes to aminochrome; aminochrome rearranges to 5,6-indolequinone, the neuromelanin precursor. Once DT-diaphorase capacity is exceeded, aminochrome is reported to trigger α-synuclein (SNCA) oligomer formation, mitochondrial damage, oxidative stress, proteasomal and autophagic impairment, neuroinflammation, and ER stress, with progressive loss of dopamine release and increased GABA release.
The listed endpoints — proteostasis failure, mitochondrial damage, autophagic impairment — overlap directly with the senescence and mitohormesis arms of the adrenochrome protocol notes, so the toxicity and the intended hormetic mechanism share machinery and differ mainly in dose.
Source: PMC8848618 ↗
Figure 3 · Glial Transfer

Astrocyte Protection of Dopaminergic Neurons

Astrocytes take up dopamine through DAT, OCT3, and PMAT; cytosolic dopamine autoxidizes to dopamine o-quinone and cyclizes to aminochrome at 0.15 s−1. Aminochrome raises GSTM2 mRNA, and exosomes carry GSTM2 into dopaminergic neurons, where it conjugates dopamine o-quinone to 5-glutathionyldopamine (degraded to 5-cysteinyldopamine, deposited in neuromelanin or excreted to CSF) and aminochrome to the oxidation-resistant 4-S-glutathionyl-5,6-dihydroxyindoline.
GSTM2 transfer is the only described mechanism by which one cell type raises another cell's resistance to aminochrome; it is also the mechanism the vault's GSTM2 note is built on.
Source: PMC8848618 ↗
Figure 1 · Flavonoids

Flavonoid Neuroprotection Against Aminochrome

Aminochrome is reported to kill neurons through oxidative stress, α-synuclein aggregation, impaired protein degradation, mitochondrial dysfunction, ER stress, and neuroinflammation; the figure maps the reported inhibitory effect of flavonoids on each of these endpoints, and proposes aminochrome as a screening model for flavonoid neuroprotection.
Polyphenol co-administration is one of the mitigations proposed in the adrenochrome notes; this figure is the evidence map for that proposal, and it is a proposal rather than a demonstrated clinical effect.
Source: PMC8328783 ↗

Autophagosome–Lysosome Fusion & Mitochondrial Dysfunction

Downstream consequences of aminochrome exposure at the proteostasis level — autophagosome–lysosome fusion arrest, intermediate-filament aggregation, mitophagy failure, and ER stress. Selected 11 Nov 2026 to fill the proteostasis and mitophagy gaps in the adrenochrome notes; not yet ingested into the vault.

From: Briceño et al., Antioxidants 2026 · Segura-Aguilar & Huenchuguala, Front Neurosci 2018 · Segura-Aguilar, Front Neurosci 2019 · Segura-Aguilar, Oncotarget 2017

Figure 1A · Fusion

Autophagosome–Lysosome Fusion in SH-SY5Y Cells

Immunofluorescence double labelling of LC3β (autophagosome) and LAMP1 (lysosome) in SH-SY5Y cells treated for 16 h with 50 µM aminochrome, 10 µM vinblastine, 50 µM aminochrome plus vinblastine, 10 µM rapamycin, aminochrome plus rapamycin, vinblastine plus rapamycin, 100 mM trehalose, and the corresponding combinations.
The comparison set separates an inducer (rapamycin), a fusion inhibitor (vinblastine), and a mTOR-independent inducer (trehalose), which is how the autophagic-flux step is isolated from autophagosome formation alone.
Source: PMC13295424 ↗
Figure 2 · Vimentin

Vimentin Aggregation Induced by Aminochrome

Vimentin immunofluorescence in SH-SY5Y cells treated with 50 µM aminochrome for 16 h, with quantification of aggregate number (*** p < 0.001 versus control; n = 4 independent replicates). Aminochrome causes intermediate-filament aggregation, the morphological correlate of the cytoskeletal adduct formation reported for o-quinones.
Cytoskeletal aggregation is the mechanism proposed for the fusion block: if microtubule and intermediate-filament tracks are disrupted, autophagosomes cannot reach lysosomes.
Source: PMC13295424 ↗
Figure 3A · Aggregates

Vimentin and Nuclear Ubiquitin Aggregation

Double labelling of ubiquitin and vimentin after 50 µM aminochrome for 16 h, with Pearson coefficient quantification and Z-stack imaging (0.5–5.5 µm) showing ubiquitin aggregates in the nuclear region and vimentin aggregation at perinuclear and distal sites.
Colocalized vimentin–ubiquitin aggregates indicate that the ubiquitin–proteasome system is also engaged, so the lesion is a combined UPS and autophagy failure rather than an autophagy defect alone.
Source: PMC13295424 ↗
Figure 5 · Mechanism

Proposed Mechanism of Fusion Arrest

Proposed sequence: aminochrome forms tubulin adducts and induces microtubule depolymerization, altering autophagosome–lysosome fusion; reduced fusion plus disrupted UPS favours protein aggregate accumulation; vimentin and ubiquitin aggregates accumulate toward the perinuclear region and within cellular processes.
This is the most direct mechanistic account available for quinone-driven proteostasis failure, and it supplies the causal chain the adrenochrome notes previously stated only as an association with autophagic impairment.
Source: PMC13295424 ↗
Figure 1 · Mitophagy

Aminochrome Effects on Mitophagy

Panel A (protected): with DT-diaphorase two-electron reduction and GSTM2 conjugation intact, damaged mitochondria are recycled by mitophagy. Panel B (unprotected): when either enzyme is inhibited, aminochrome produces mitochondrial and lysosomal degradation dysfunction, and damaged mitochondria cannot be cleared.
Mitophagy failure is the point where the aminochrome literature meets the urolithin A / PINK1–Parkin arm of the protocol notes: the same clearance pathway is impaired by the quinone and induced by the intervention.
Source: PMC5859232 ↗
Figure 1 · ER Stress

DT-Diaphorase, GSTM2 and Endoplasmic Reticulum Stress

In dopaminergic neurons, DT-diaphorase reduces aminochrome to leukoaminochrome, preventing aminochrome-induced ER stress and mitochondrial dysfunction; leukoaminochrome rearranges to 5,6-dihydroxyindole, oxidizes to 5,6-indolequinone, and polymerizes to neuromelanin. Astrocytes conjugate dopamine o-quinone and aminochrome with GSH and secrete GSTM2 for neuronal uptake.
ER stress is a third proteostatic endpoint alongside the UPS and autophagy, and it is the branch most relevant to secretory-cell toxicity rather than to neurons alone.
Source: PMC6449441 ↗
Figure 1 · Model

Aminochrome as a Preclinical Parkinson's Disease Model

Summary figure for the use of aminochrome as a preclinical model of Parkinson's disease: the same aminochrome neurotoxicity endpoints (mitochondrial dysfunction, protein degradation impairment, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation) are used as readouts for candidate neuroprotective interventions.
Framing aminochrome as a model rather than only a toxin is the design logic behind the flavonoid screening proposal above; the model validation, not the clinical translation, is what the cited work supports.
Source: PMC5542161 ↗

Catecholamine Oxidation & Antioxidant Protection

Autoxidation and metal-promoted oxidation of dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine, and the protection reported for glutamate — the closest published experimental model for the epinephrine → adrenochrome step. Selected 11 Nov 2026; not yet ingested into the vault.

From: Wang et al., Antioxidants 2021

Figure 1 · Autoxidation

Dopamine Autoxidation and the Protective Role of Glutamate

Dopamine autoxidation followed at OD 410 nm (A), dopamine-derived ROS by DCFH-DA (B), quinoprotein formation on GAPDH (C), glutamate–copper complexes (D), and the effect of free versus preformed copper–glutamate complex on dopamine oxidation (E). Reactions in 0.15 M PBS pH 7.4 at 37 °C; mean ± range, n = 2.
OD 410 nm tracks the coloured o-quinone/aminochrome product, so this is effectively a direct absorbance assay for the same reaction class that converts epinephrine to adrenochrome.
Source: PMC8533062 ↗
Figure 4 · DNA Damage

Glutamate Protects Against Catecholamine-Induced DNA Damage

Plasmid DNA (10 µg/mL) incubated for 1 h at 37 °C with copper and dopamine (A), norepinephrine (B), or epinephrine (C). Band forms: I, supercoiled closed circular; II, relaxed open circle; III, linear. Glutamate reduced the conversion to the relaxed and linear forms for all three catecholamines.
This is one of the few experiments in the gallery that includes epinephrine itself, and it links the oxidation product to a genotoxic endpoint rather than only to redox-cycling ROS.
Source: PMC8533062 ↗
Figure 5 · Epinephrine Oxidation

Glutamate and Epinephrine/Norepinephrine Oxidation

Copper-promoted oxidation of norepinephrine (A) and epinephrine (B), and autoxidation of norepinephrine (C) and epinephrine (D), followed at OD 410 nm in 0.15 M PBS pH 7.4 at 37 °C. Glutamate slowed both the metal-promoted and the spontaneous oxidation of both catecholamines.
The rate data behind the claim in the adrenochrome notes that metal availability (free copper, ceruloplasmin) and amino-acid chelation set the adrenochrome formation rate independently of catecholamine concentration.
Source: PMC8533062 ↗

Methemoglobinemia — Safety & Monitoring

The safety cluster the adrenochrome gap analysis (09 Jul 2026) flags as the largest uncovered risk: superoxide from adrenochrome redox cycling can oxidize ferrous haemoglobin, and methylene blue is simultaneously the intended electron shuttle and the canonical antidote. Selected 11 Nov 2026; not yet ingested into the vault.

From: Cefalu et al., Adv Ther 2020

Figure 1 · Classification

Types of Methemoglobinemia

Classification of methemoglobinemia into congenital forms (cytochrome b5 reductase deficiency, Hb M variants) and acquired forms, with the oxidant exposures associated with the acquired category — nitrites, local anaesthetics (benzocaine, prilocaine), dapsone, and rasburicase.
Acquired methemoglobinemia is the relevant category for any adrenochrome exposure argument, and it is why the gap analysis recommends a drug-interaction list (benzocaine, prilocaine, dapsone, nitrites) before any protocol use.
Source: PMC7467467 ↗
Figure 2 · Diagnosis

Diagnosis of Methemoglobinemia

Diagnostic pathway for suspected methemoglobinemia: clinical context, the saturation gap, and confirmation by co-oximetry. Pulse oximetry does not distinguish methemoglobin from oxyhaemoglobin, so a normal SpO2 with a low calculated saturation is the pattern that triggers co-oximetry.
The gap analysis records co-oximetry, not pulse oximetry, as the required monitoring modality — this figure is the source of that recommendation.
Source: PMC7467467 ↗
Figure 3 · Time Course

Methemoglobin Levels Relative to Treatment

Chronological methemoglobin measurements in a treated patient, plotted against the interventions given. The curve shows the rise to peak level and the subsequent decline after treatment, and gives the timescale on which a decision on methylene blue would be made.
The kinetic window is the operational answer to the methylene-blue paradox: at the doses used as an electron shuttle the NADPH-dependent reduction route dominates, and at antidote doses the same compound reduces methaemoglobin directly — the dose boundary is not established.
Source: PMC7467467 ↗

Research Gaps — How These Papers Map

Coverage against the gap analysis

Gap status after the nine papers listed above.

  • Methemoglobinemia mechanism, risk, and diagnostics — partially filled: Cefalu 2020 (classification, co-oximetry pathway, treatment time course). G6PD screening criteria and the methylene-blue dose boundary remain protocol questions, not published answers.
  • Neuromelanin, substantia nigra, Parkinson's disease risk — partially filled: Segura-Aguilar 2022 (neuromelanin synthesis, DT-diaphorase/GSTM2 gates), 2019 (ER stress and mitochondrial dysfunction), 2018 (mitophagy failure), 2021/2022 reviews (flavonoid protection, astrocyte rescue). All aminochrome-derived; epinephrine-derived adrenochrome itself is not the experimental agent in any of them.
  • Proteostasis — autophagy vs autophagosome–lysosome fusion — filled: Briceño 2026 (tubulin adducts → microtubule depolymerization → fusion arrest, with LC3β/LAMP1, vimentin, and ubiquitin data at 50 µM aminochrome, 16 h in SH-SY5Y cells).
  • Catecholamine oxidation kinetics and antioxidant protection — filled: Wang 2021 (epinephrine and norepinephrine autoxidation and copper-promoted oxidation; glutamate protection; catecholamine-induced DNA damage).
  • AGE / glyco-oxidative arm — filled by the vault's own PMC documents: Luevano-Contreras 2010 and Kim 2017 (formation, receptor signalling, AGE structures).
  • NAD+ repletion rationale — filled by the vault's own PMC documents: Alegre & Pastore 2023 and Mehmel 2020 (pathways, NRK2 in the failing heart).
  • Mitophagy induction rationale — filled by the vault's own PMC document: Zhao 2023 (urolithin A, PINK1/Parkin, AMPK–mTOR–PGC-1α).

Still open: adrenochrome-specific in vivo exposure data (no paper here administers adrenochrome to an animal model); the methylene-blue dose boundary between electron shuttle and methaemoglobin reductase substrate; quantified hormetic window for any adrenochrome derivative; G6PD-stratified safety threshold.
