NAD+ Metabolism — substrate supply, enzymatic consumption, and the CD38–SARM1 competition

NAD+ is often presented as a concentration to be restored by supplementation. The underlying enzymology is more constrained: NAD+ is a shared substrate pool consumed by at least four enzymatic systems, each operating in a distinct cellular compartment and on a distinct timescale, and the resulting substrate deficit in every case propagates to the same downstream target — sirtuin-dependent NAD+-requiring reactions. This page covers the biosynthetic routes, the hierarchy of NAD+-consuming enzymes, the two NADases that dominate the flux (CD38, constitutively extracellular; SARM1, acutely intracellular in neurons), the NMN metabolite that links them, and the resulting pharmacological paradox together with the axotomy data that constrain it. It concludes with the current knowledge-graph readout for the NAD+ module, the therapeutic landscape organised by which consumption pathway is being targeted, and the sex divergence that determines whether NAD+ repletion is uniform or stratified.

Three functional categories of a single cofactor

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) performs three distinct functions: it is an obligate co-substrate for hundreds of enzymes, it sets the intracellular NADH:NAD+ ratio as the oxidant in NAD+-dependent dehydrogenases, and it is a signalling molecule in its own right. These functions are not independent — each draws on the same cellular substrate pool, so NAD+ consumed by one leaves less available to the others. The three are therefore necessarily considered together.

The thesis

Cellular NAD+ is not a static quantity that can simply be topped up — it is a shared substrate pool subject to consumption by at least four enzymatic systems on four different timescales, and the substrate deficit produced by each converges on the same downstream target: sirtuin-catalysed reactions. The sirtuins are themselves consumers: each deacetylation event hydrolyses one NAD+ into nicotinamide and OAADPr. NAD+ repletion is therefore never metabolically neutral.

Role Primary enzyme Consequence of reduced substrate availability Functional category
Catalytic SIRT1–SIRT7 (obligate co-substrate) Loss of deacetylation-dependent transcriptional and metabolic regulation Signal
Redox GAPDH, IDH, 3-KGDH, Complex I Glycolytic and respiratory collapse; NADH:NAD+ ratio tips reductive Energy
Signalling CD38, SARM1 (cADPR / NAADP synthesis) Dysregulated Ca²⁺ release; the messenger is itself a product of NAD+ catabolism Message

A fourth role is easy to overlook: NAD+ is also the substrate of the enzymes that mediate cell death. PARP1 consumes it during DNA repair, and the two NADases on this page consume it as their catalytic substrate. The same pool therefore serves as both the reaction substrate and the casualty.

Biosynthetic routes — three pathways, one rate-limiting enzyme

The salvage pathway is the dominant source of cellular NAD+ in most mammalian tissues: it recycles the nicotinamide generated by NAD+-consuming enzymes back into the pool. Two enzymes catalyse the sequence — NAMPT, which is rate-limiting, and NMNAT, which completes the conversion.

Route Key enzyme Precursor Rate-limiting step & aging effect
Salvage (dominant) NAMPT → NMNAT Nicotinamide (NAM) NAMPT activity falls with age in many tissues — synthetic capacity declines
Preiss–Handler Nicotinic acid pathway Nicotinic acid (NA) Bypasses NAMPT; relatively minor in most tissues
Riboside route NRK1/NRK2 → NMNAT Nicotinamide riboside (NR) Supplements enter here — and are themselves degraded by CD38

NAMPT is the same enzyme in two opposing strategies. It is the master regulator to preserve in longevity — circadian-controlled via CLOCK:BMAL1, activated by AMPK under energetic stress, and the route by which resveratrol's PDE4 inhibition eventually raises NAD+ and SIRT1 activity. It is also the target to inhibit in oncology: FK866 depletes NAD+ so that PARP-driven DNA repair fails in tumour cells. Same enzyme, opposite aim, and the same reason: NAD+ is the currency.

Precursor efficacy is gated by the drain

Orally administered precursors do not simply accumulate in the pool. CD38 degrades extracellular NMN and NR, so precursor bioavailability is determined by the same enzyme whose activity increases with age. Any regimen based on NR or NMN is therefore implicitly a regimen that must also account for CD38 activity.

NAD+ consumption — the hierarchy of NAD+-utilising enzymes

Four enzyme systems compete for the same substrate pool. They differ in cellular compartment, in kinetic timescale, and in the phenotype of their failure — which is why they constitute four distinct therapeutic targets rather than a single entity that might be addressed as "low NAD+".

Consumer Compartment Timescale Trigger Signature
CD38 Extracellular / cell surface Hours–years Inflammaging; CD38-high immune-cell infiltration NAD+ ↓, NMN ↓, cADPR ↑
SARM1 Intracellular (axon) Minutes Axonal injury → NMNAT2 loss NAD+ → ~0; Wallerian degeneration
PARP1 / PARP2 Nucleus Hours DNA damage NAD+ ↓, PAR accumulation
Sirtuins Nucleus / cytosol / mitochondria Continuous — 1 NAD+ consumed per deacetylation
CD73 Extracellular Continuous — NAD+ → adenosine (competitive substrate)

Net decline in aging is the sum of two terms moving the same way: reduced synthetic capacity (NAMPT activity falls with age) and increased consumption (CD38 expression rises with age).

CD38 — the chronic drain

CD38 is a bifunctional ectoenzyme and the largest age-related consumer of NAD+ in the vault's graph. It catalyses four reactions, and they differ in consequence: one constitutes the principal NAD+ sink, one controls precursor availability, and two generate calcium signals as products of that same catabolism.

Activity Reaction Function
NAD+ glycohydrolase NAD+ → ADP-ribose + nicotinamide Primary NAD+ consumption — the drain
ADP-ribosyl cyclase NAD+ → cADPR Ca²⁺ mobilisation from the ER via ryanodine receptors (~2–3% of total consumption)
NAADP synthase (base-exchange) NADP+ → NAADP Lysosomal Ca²⁺ release
Ecto-NMNase Extracellular NMN → nicotinamide + ribose Gatekeeper of the extracellular NMN pool — and thus of supplement pharmacokinetics

The kinetic difference from SARM1 is what makes CD38 the high-affinity consumer: it has the lower Km. SARM1 has the higher NAD-cyclising activity. Identical substrate and products, but different kinetics and different cellular compartments.

Why aging turns it on

CD38 expression rises with age, driven partly by inflammaging and the infiltration of CD38-high immune cells — macrophages and T cells — into white adipose tissue, liver and brain. The graph encodes this directly: CD38 is wired to increases_with → Aging, is_upregulated_on → Neutrophils and overexpress → CD4+ T Cells, and to Inflammaging, Inflammation, Tumor Microenvironment and Microglia. The ageing sequence the vault describes is: senescent cells → SASP → recruitment of CD38+ immune cells → extracellular NAD+ and NMN degradation → parenchymal cells depleted of precursors.

Finding Model Consequence

CD38 KO boosts SIRT3 activity Aged mice Improved mitochondrial function, exercise capacity and metabolic health

SARM1 — the acute drain

SARM1 (Sterile Alpha and TIR Motif Containing 1, ~724 aa / ~80 kDa) is the founding member of the TIR-domain family with intrinsic NADase activity, and the executor of Wallerian degeneration. Its architecture is a three-part autoinhibition mechanism with a catalysis switch.

Domain Function Role in the switch
ARM repeats (N-terminal) Helical armadillo repeats; contain the NMN-binding pocket Packs against the TIR domain in cis — this is the autoinhibition
SAM1 / SAM2 (central) Tandem sterile-alpha motifs driving multimerisation Octameric ring assembly is required for catalytic competence
TIR domain (C-terminal) Rossmann-fold NAD+ glycohydrolase core Blocked by ARM when autoinhibited; forms the composite active site on release

The trigger is the NMN/NAD+ ratio, not absolute NMN: NMN and NAD+ compete for the same ARM-domain pocket, and Figley et al. 2021 established the ratio as the primary metabolic sensor. This distinction is what makes the CD38 paradox resolvable rather than merely alarming — see the next section.

A fourth catalytic activity was identified in 2025: mono-ADP-ribosylation. SARM1 MARylates itself and other proteins with catalytic efficiency higher than its hydrolase activity, and auto-MARylation promotes a liquid-to-solid phase transition that spatially restricts activation to damaged axons. Recent work also implicates SARM1 in the mitochondrial unfolded protein response and mitophagy, in TIR-domain JNK/p38 signalling via TRAF6 (independent of NADase activity), in negative regulation of TLR3/TLR4, and in HSV-1 restriction.

The NMN linkage — and the pharmacological paradox it generates

The two NADases are connected by a single metabolite, and the connection runs in opposite directions on the two sides of the cell membrane.

The paradox

CD38 degrades extracellular NMN. SARM1 is activated by intracellular NMN accumulation. So high CD38 activity may protect against SARM1 activation by keeping NMN low — and therefore CD38 inhibition for longevity might sensitize neurons to SARM1-dependent Wallerian degeneration.

Step Mechanism
CD38 degrades extracellular NMN Ecto-NMNase activity limits the NMN available for cellular uptake — CD38 is one of the main enzymes degrading NMN in mouse tissues and controls NMN pharmacokinetics (Camacho-Pereira et al. 2016, Cell Metabolism, PMID 27133162; Covarrubias et al. 2020, Nature Metabolism, PMID 33028765)
Less NMN enters cells Lower intracellular NMN leaves the NMN/NAD+ ratio lower, keeping SARM1 further from its threshold
Injury reverses it NMNAT2 loss stops NMN consumption regardless of supply — the ratio rises from the injury side, not the supply side
Precursor supplementation is the sharp edge NR/NMN raises NMN without a guaranteed compensating NAD+ rise; Takaso et al. 2020 state explicitly that "accumulated NMN appeared to be an activator of the prodegenerative cADPR-forming enzyme SARM1" (PMID 33082370)

CZ-48 proves the enzymes are separable

CZ-48 (sulfo-ara-F-NMN) is the cleanest pharmacological demonstration that CD38 and SARM1 are two enzymes sharing a substrate and products, not one enzyme wearing two hats.

Compound Effect on CD38 Effect on SARM1 Reading
CZ-48 Inhibits Activates Pharmacologically separable

It is the only molecule in the vault with opposite effects on the two NADases. In the current graph it sits inside the wiki layer's NAD+ community with 4 distinct neighbours and clustering 1.000 — a divergence node in the strictest graph sense.

What the literature actually shows

The paradox is real as a mechanism, but the axotomy data do not support it as a risk of CD38 inhibition. CD38 knockout mice show delayed axon degeneration after facial nerve axotomy (Takaso et al. 2020) — the opposite of what SARM1 sensitization would predict.

The resolution: the trigger is the ratio, and CD38 deletion raises NAD+ as well as NMN, which lowers NMN/NAD+ even as absolute NMN rises. NMN accumulation alone is not sufficient to activate SARM1 without the concurrent injury-specific NMNAT2 loss context.

So the caution attaches to precursor supplementation, which raises NMN without the same compensatory NAD+ rise — not to CD38 inhibition itself. Stated precisely: NR/NMN supplementation during or after axonal injury carries an SARM1-activation risk that CD38 inhibition does not.

A third mechanism couples them anyway, so pharmacological separability does not mean mechanistic independence: 78c and the SARM1 inhibitors are both adduct-forming uncompetitive inhibitors of NAD-dependent enzymes — a conserved NAD-dependent mechanism (Bratkowski et al. 2022, Neuron).

The finding

The wiki layer has already unified the entire NAD+ module into a single NAD+ community — CD38, SARM1, NAMPT, NMNAT2, NR, CZ-48, Disulfiram, DSRM-3716, GSK-428, NB-4746, SLC25A51, Wallerian degeneration and CIPN all sit inside it. The combined dataset inherits the triples label for shared node ids, so the default UI shows the scattered labels even though the coherent wiki clustering is present in the data. Any analysis that reads community labels from the combined dataset will mis-scope this module — it should read the wiki layer's labels for these nodes.

The therapeutic landscape — organised by consumption pathway

The informative classification is not a list of compounds but the identity of the consumption pathway being targeted. An intervention directed at chronic, compartment-wide substrate catabolism and one directed at acute, axonal catabolism are not interchangeable.

Target Agents Mechanism Evidence & caveat
CD38 78c Adduct-forming uncompetitive inhibitor ~14% lifespan extension in male mice; severe adverse reactions in females requiring euthanasia — sex-specific
CD38 Quercetin, apigenin Natural flavonoids Weaker and pleiotropic — also act on sirtuins and PI3K
CD38 Daratumumab, isatuximab Anti-CD38 monoclonal antibodies (ADCC / direct apoptosis) Oncology — multiple myeloma; not a longevity tool
SARM1 Disulfiram Covalent TIR cysteine First-in-class repurposed; graph files it under Paracrine Senescence
SARM1 DSRM-3716 Non-covalent pocket binder Preclinical
SARM1 GSK-428 Quinazoline, nM IC₅₀ Preclinical
SARM1 NB-4746 Brain-penetrant Clinical-stage (Nura Bio)

Pool FK866 NAMPT inhibition — depletes NAD+ Oncology; the inverse strategy, same rate-limiting enzyme
PARP PARP inhibition Stops the third drain CD38 + PARP inhibition fully reversed LPS-induced NAD+ decline where single agents failed (Covarrubias 2020)

Two targeting strategies, and why combined coverage exceeds either alone

Strategy A — chronic: CD38 inhibition + NR repletion → restore substrate availability for sirtuin catalysis. Operates on the ageing and inflammaging timescale, hours to years.

Strategy B — acute: SARM1 inhibition → prevent axonal NAD+ collapse. Operates on the injury timescale, minutes.

The combination exceeds simple additivity because the two strategies address different compartments and different timescales of the same substrate pool, so combined coverage is broader than either alone.

Speculative — the synergy edge does not exist in the graph. No extracted edge connects a CD38 inhibitor to a SARM1 inhibitor; the proposition is AMBIGUOUS (0.45) by construction, inferred from convergent node logic. The only supporting precedent is CD38 + PARP, not CD38 + SARM1. Any claim of CD38+SARM1 synergy is untested and should be labelled as such. The NMN-sensitisation caveat still applies to the precursor component of Strategy A (NR/NMN) — but not to CD38 inhibition alone, for the reasons set out above.

Sex divergence — NAD+ is not the same problem in men and women

The vault's NAD+ note carries a substantial sex-difference section with no page home until now, and it changes the therapeutic logic rather than just adding a footnote.

Observation Direction Detail & source
Baseline whole-blood NAD+ Male higher 34.5 vs 31.3 µmol/L (Yang et al., Front Endocrinol 2022)
Age trajectory Male declines Males decline progressively from age 60; females fluctuate without monotonic decline
NAD+/NADH ratio Female higher 1.33 vs 1.09, P<0.001; the gap narrows with biological age (Schwarzmann et al., Biosci Rep 2021)
PARP-1 activity Male-specific rise PARP activity rises with age in male skin but not female skin; in males PARP inversely correlates with NAD+ (r=−0.639)
PARP-1 knockout Sex-reversed Protects males from ischemic brain injury but exacerbates damage in females (McCullough et al. 2005)
Ovarian NAD+ decline Female-specific CD38 rises with reproductive aging in the ovary; CD38-KO mice have larger primordial follicle pools, higher ovarian NAD+ and increased fecundity (Wang et al., Nature Aging 2023)
NMN lifespan effect Sex-split Females +8.5% median lifespan (no metabolic improvement); males improved metabolic health but no lifespan extension (Kane et al., bioRxiv 2024)
NMN metabolism route Sex-split Greater Preiss–Handler metabolism in females; greater liver salvage metabolism in males
Labelled hypothesis, not proof. The proposed mechanism — estrogen stabilising PARP-1 and thereby sparing NAD+ in females — is an inference, not a direct demonstration. The evidence it rests on (sex-reversed PARP-1 knockout phenotypes, ischemic injury data) is consistent with it but does not prove the mechanism. The pilot observation that women retain higher absolute SIRT1 and SIRT3 protein pools with age (Biology 2025) is preliminary and should not be presented as established.

The practical consequence: precursor dosing and gate choice may need sex stratification. Males may benefit more from PARP-1 inhibition to slow consumption; females more from CD38 inhibition to prevent ovarian and residual NAD+ decline. Post-menopausal women face a compounding "double decline" — hormonal shift layered on age-related NAD+ change — making them potentially the most responsive population for NAD+ restoration, and the 78c adverse-reaction signal in female mice argues against blanket CD38 inhibition in that group.

Open questions the vault itself flags

  1. Does CD38 inhibition alter SARM1-dependent axon degeneration? No study has tested this directly. The mechanism is sound; the evidence is absent.
  2. Does combined CD38 + SARM1 inhibition synergize? The only precedent is CD38 + PARP.
  3. CD38 / SARM1 co-expression in microglia — the graph nodes are stubs in the typed layer.
  4. NR + CD38-inhibitor dosing — does NMN preservation cross the SARM1 activation threshold?
  5. CD38 + PARP versus CD38 + SARM1 — which dual pairing gives the stronger NAD+ rescue?
  6. Which NMN ratio matters — plasma, tissue, or the axonal compartment?
  7. Is the CIPN convergence causal? Both NADases reaching one indication may be a shared causal axis or a co-mention artefact of the wiki layer.
Scope and status. This page is a graph-derived and preclinical synthesis. The 78c lifespan figure is mouse and male-specific. The CD38+SARM1 synergy is a hypothesis. NR/NMN human efficacy remains contested, and human NAD+ precursor trials to date have generally failed to produce the dramatic phenotypic effects seen in rodents. Nothing here is medical advice.
