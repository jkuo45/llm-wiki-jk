NAD+ Metabolism — substrate supply, enzymatic consumption, and the CD38–SARM1 competition

NAD+ is a shared substrate pool consumed by at least four enzymatic systems in distinct compartments and on distinct timescales; the resulting deficit reduces NAD+-dependent sirtuin activity. Biosynthetic routes, the hierarchy of NAD+-consuming enzymes, the two NADases that dominate the flux (CD38, constitutively extracellular; SARM1, acutely intracellular in neurons), the NMN metabolite that links them, competing hypotheses on CD38 inhibition and SARM1 activation, and the axotomy data that constrain them are described below. The recomputed knowledge-graph readout for the NAD+ module, interventions classified by the consumption pathway targeted, and sex-stratified differences in NAD+ metabolism are also reported.

Three functional categories of a single cofactor

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) performs three distinct functions: it is an obligate co-substrate for hundreds of enzymes, it sets the intracellular NADH:NAD+ ratio as the oxidant in NAD+-dependent dehydrogenases, and it is a signalling molecule in its own right. These functions are not independent — each draws on the same cellular substrate pool, so NAD+ consumed by one leaves less available to the others. The three are therefore necessarily considered together.

Shared substrate pool

Cellular NAD+ is not restored by supplementation alone without a change in consumption; it is a shared substrate pool subject to consumption by at least four enzymatic systems on four different timescales, and the substrate deficit produced by each converges on the same downstream target: sirtuin-catalysed reactions. The sirtuins are themselves consumers: each deacetylation event hydrolyses one NAD+ into nicotinamide and OAADPr. Repletion therefore alters flux through all NAD+-consuming enzymes, including the sirtuins.

Role Primary enzyme Consequence of reduced substrate availability Functional category
Catalytic SIRT1–SIRT7 (obligate co-substrate) Loss of deacetylation-dependent transcriptional and metabolic regulation Signal
Redox GAPDH, IDH, 3-KGDH, Complex I Reduced glycolytic and respiratory flux; NADH:NAD+ ratio shifts toward NADH Energy
Signalling CD38, SARM1 (cADPR / NAADP synthesis) Dysregulated Ca²⁺ release; the messenger is itself a product of NAD+ catabolism Message

A fourth role is documented: NAD+ is also the substrate of the enzymes that mediate cell death. PARP1 consumes it during DNA repair, and the two NADases on this page consume it as their catalytic substrate. NAD+ is therefore both the substrate for these enzymes and the pool depleted by their activity.

Biosynthetic routes — three pathways, one rate-limiting enzyme

The salvage pathway is the dominant source of cellular NAD+ in most mammalian tissues: it recycles the nicotinamide generated by NAD+-consuming enzymes back into the pool. Two enzymes catalyse the sequence — NAMPT, which is rate-limiting, and NMNAT, which completes the conversion.

Route Key enzyme Precursor Rate-limiting step & aging effect
Salvage (dominant) NAMPT → NMNAT Nicotinamide (NAM) NAMPT activity falls with age in many tissues — synthetic capacity declines
Preiss–Handler Nicotinic acid pathway Nicotinic acid (NA) Bypasses NAMPT; relatively minor in most tissues
Riboside route NRK1/NRK2 → NMNAT Nicotinamide riboside (NR) Supplements enter here — and are themselves degraded by CD38

NAMPT is the rate-limiting enzyme in two opposing intervention strategies. Longevity strategies preserve NAMPT activity — circadian-controlled via CLOCK:BMAL1, activated by AMPK under energetic stress, and the route by which resveratrol's PDE4 inhibition raises NAD+ and SIRT1 activity. Oncology strategies inhibit it: FK866 depletes NAD+ so that PARP-driven DNA repair fails in tumour cells. Both strategies act on the same rate-limiting step.

CD38 activity limits precursor bioavailability

Oral NR and NMN are substrates for extracellular CD38. CD38 degrades extracellular NMN and NR, so precursor bioavailability is determined by the same enzyme whose activity increases with age. NR- or NMN-based regimens are therefore affected by CD38 activity.

NAD+ consumption — the hierarchy of NAD+-utilising enzymes

Four enzyme systems compete for the same substrate pool. They differ in cellular compartment, in kinetic timescale, and in the phenotype of their failure, so they constitute four distinct therapeutic targets rather than a single entity addressed as "low NAD+".

Consumer Compartment Timescale Trigger Signature
CD38 Extracellular / cell surface Hours–years Inflammaging; CD38-high immune-cell infiltration NAD+ ↓, NMN ↓, cADPR ↑
SARM1 Intracellular (axon) Minutes Axonal injury → NMNAT2 loss NAD+ → ~0; Wallerian degeneration
PARP1 / PARP2 Nucleus Hours DNA damage NAD+ ↓, PAR accumulation
Sirtuins Nucleus / cytosol / mitochondria Continuous — 1 NAD+ consumed per deacetylation
CD73 Extracellular Continuous — NAD+ → adenosine (competitive substrate)

Net decline in aging reflects two changes: reduced synthetic capacity (NAMPT activity falls with age) and increased consumption (CD38 expression rises with age).

CD38 — chronic extracellular NAD+ consumption

CD38 is a bifunctional ectoenzyme and the largest age-related consumer of NAD+ in the vault's graph. It catalyses four reactions, and they differ in consequence: one constitutes the principal NAD+ sink, one controls precursor availability, and two generate calcium signals as products of that same catabolism.

Activity Reaction Function
NAD+ glycohydrolase NAD+ → ADP-ribose + nicotinamide Primary NAD+ consumption
ADP-ribosyl cyclase NAD+ → cADPR Ca²⁺ mobilisation from the ER via ryanodine receptors (~2–3% of total consumption)
NAADP synthase (base-exchange) NADP+ → NAADP Lysosomal Ca²⁺ release
Ecto-NMNase Extracellular NMN → nicotinamide + ribose Determines the extracellular NMN pool and supplement pharmacokinetics

The kinetic difference from SARM1 is what makes CD38 the high-affinity consumer: it has the lower Km. SARM1 has the higher NAD-cyclising activity. Identical substrate and products, but different kinetics and different cellular compartments.

Age-associated upregulation of CD38

CD38 expression rises with age, driven partly by inflammaging and the infiltration of CD38-high immune cells — macrophages and T cells — into white adipose tissue, liver and brain. The graph encodes this directly: CD38 is connected by stored edges to increases_with → Aging, is_upregulated_on → Neutrophils and overexpress → CD4+ T Cells, and to Inflammaging, Inflammation, Tumor Microenvironment and Microglia. The sequence reported in the vault is: senescent cells → SASP → recruitment of CD38+ immune cells → extracellular NAD+ and NMN degradation → parenchymal cells depleted of precursors.

SARM1 — acute axonal NAD+ consumption

SARM1 (Sterile Alpha and TIR Motif Containing 1, ~724 aa / ~80 kDa) is the founding member of the TIR-domain family with intrinsic NADase activity and mediates Wallerian degeneration. Three domains mediate autoinhibition and activation.

Domain Function Role in the switch
ARM repeats (N-terminal) Helical armadillo repeats; contain the NMN-binding pocket Packs against the TIR domain in cis — this interaction maintains autoinhibition
SAM1 / SAM2 (central) Tandem sterile-alpha motifs driving multimerisation Octameric ring assembly is required for catalytic competence
TIR domain (C-terminal) Rossmann-fold NAD+ glycohydrolase core Blocked by ARM when autoinhibited; forms the composite active site on release

The trigger is the NMN/NAD+ ratio, not absolute NMN: NMN and NAD+ compete for the same ARM-domain pocket, and Figley et al. 2021 established the ratio as the primary metabolic sensor. This ratio dependence constrains the competing hypotheses on CD38 inhibition and SARM1 activation.

A fourth catalytic activity was identified in 2025: mono-ADP-ribosylation. SARM1 MARylates itself and other proteins with catalytic efficiency higher than its hydrolase activity, and auto-MARylation promotes a liquid-to-solid phase transition that spatially restricts activation to damaged axons. Studies report SARM1 involvement in the mitochondrial unfolded protein response and mitophagy, in TIR-domain JNK/p38 signalling via TRAF6 (independent of NADase activity), in negative regulation of TLR3/TLR4, and in HSV-1 restriction.

NMN linkage between CD38 and SARM1

The two NADases are connected by a single metabolite, and the connection runs in opposite directions on the two sides of the cell membrane.

CD38 inhibition and SARM1 activation: competing hypotheses

CD38 degrades extracellular NMN. SARM1 is activated by intracellular NMN accumulation. One hypothesis is that high CD38 activity lowers intracellular NMN and reduces SARM1 activation, so that CD38 inhibition for longevity could increase susceptibility to SARM1-dependent Wallerian degeneration; this remains untested in humans.

Step Mechanism
CD38 degrades extracellular NMN Ecto-NMNase activity limits the NMN available for cellular uptake — CD38 is one of the main enzymes degrading NMN in mouse tissues and controls NMN pharmacokinetics (Camacho-Pereira et al. 2016, Cell Metabolism, PMID 27133162; Covarrubias et al. 2020, Nature Metabolism, PMID 33028765)
Less NMN enters cells Lower intracellular NMN leaves the NMN/NAD+ ratio lower, keeping SARM1 further from its threshold
Injury raises the NMN/NAD+ ratio NMNAT2 loss stops NMN consumption regardless of supply — the ratio rises from the injury side, not the supply side
Precursor supplementation without compensatory NAD+ rise NR/NMN raises NMN without a guaranteed compensating NAD+ rise; Takaso et al. 2020 report that "accumulated NMN appeared to be an activator of the prodegenerative cADPR-forming enzyme SARM1" (PMID 33082370)

Opposite effects of CZ-48 on CD38 and SARM1

CZ-48 (sulfo-ara-F-NMN) has opposite effects on the two NADases, which provides pharmacological evidence that they are separable.

Compound Effect on CD38 Effect on SARM1 Inference
CZ-48 Inhibits Activates Pharmacologically separable

It is the only molecule in the vault with opposite effects on the two NADases. In the current graph it sits inside the wiki layer's NAD+ community with 4 distinct neighbours and clustering 1.000 — a node with divergent edge signs in the wiki layer.

Reported axotomy findings

Competition for NMN between CD38 and SARM1 is mechanistically plausible, but the axotomy data do not support it as a risk of CD38 inhibition. CD38 knockout mice show delayed axon degeneration after facial nerve axotomy (Takaso et al. 2020) — the opposite of what SARM1 sensitization would predict.

The reported mechanism: the trigger is the ratio, and CD38 deletion raises NAD+ as well as NMN, which lowers NMN/NAD+ even as absolute NMN rises. NMN accumulation alone is not sufficient to activate SARM1 without the concurrent injury-specific NMNAT2 loss context.

The reported risk concerns precursor supplementation, which raises NMN without the same compensatory NAD+ rise, rather than CD38 inhibition itself. NR/NMN supplementation during or after axonal injury carries an SARM1-activation risk that CD38 inhibition does not.

A third mechanism couples the two NADases; pharmacological separability does not establish mechanistic independence: 78c and the SARM1 inhibitors are both adduct-forming uncompetitive inhibitors of NAD-dependent enzymes — a conserved NAD-dependent mechanism (Bratkowski et al. 2022, Neuron).

Wiki-layer community assignment of the NAD+ module

The wiki layer assigns the NAD+ module nodes to a single NAD+ community — CD38, SARM1, NAMPT, NMNAT2, NR, CZ-48, Disulfiram, DSRM-3716, GSK-428, NB-4746, SLC25A51, Wallerian degeneration and CIPN all sit inside it. The combined dataset inherits the triples label for shared node ids, so the default UI displays the triples community labels; the wiki-layer community assignment is present in the data. Analyses reading community labels from the combined dataset should use the wiki layer's labels for these nodes.

The therapeutic landscape — organised by consumption pathway

Interventions are classified by the consumption pathway targeted. An intervention directed at chronic, compartment-wide substrate catabolism and one directed at acute, axonal catabolism are not interchangeable.

Chronic and acute targeting strategies

Strategy A — chronic: CD38 inhibition + NR repletion → restore substrate availability for sirtuin catalysis. Operates on the ageing and inflammaging timescale, hours to years.

Strategy B — acute: SARM1 inhibition → prevent axonal NAD+ depletion. Operates on the injury timescale, minutes.

The two strategies address different compartments and timescales; combined coverage is broader than either alone.

Evidence status: no extracted edge connects a CD38 inhibitor to a SARM1 inhibitor. The CD38+SARM1 synergy proposition is classified AMBIGUOUS (0.45) in the graph, inferred from convergent node logic. The only supporting precedent is CD38 + PARP, not CD38 + SARM1. CD38+SARM1 synergy remains untested. The NMN-sensitisation caveat applies to the precursor component of Strategy A (NR/NMN), not to CD38 inhibition alone.

Sex-stratified differences in NAD+ metabolism

The vault NAD+ note reports sex-stratified differences in NAD+ metabolism; these affect intervention selection.

Observation Direction Detail & source
Baseline whole-blood NAD+ Male higher 34.5 vs 31.3 µmol/L (Yang et al., Front Endocrinol 2022)
Age trajectory Male declines Males decline progressively from age 60; females fluctuate without monotonic decline
NAD+/NADH ratio Female higher 1.33 vs 1.09, P<0.001; the gap narrows with biological age (Schwarzmann et al., Biosci Rep 2021)
PARP-1 activity Male-specific rise PARP activity rises with age in male skin but not female skin; in males PARP inversely correlates with NAD+ (r=−0.639)
PARP-1 knockout Sex-reversed Reduces ischemic brain injury in male mice but exacerbates damage in female mice (McCullough et al. 2005)
Ovarian NAD+ decline Female-specific CD38 rises with reproductive aging in the ovary; CD38-KO mice have larger primordial follicle pools, higher ovarian NAD+ and increased fecundity (Wang et al., Nature Aging 2023)
NMN lifespan effect Sex-split Female mice +8.5% median lifespan (no metabolic improvement); male mice improved metabolic health but no lifespan extension (Kane et al., bioRxiv 2024)
NMN metabolism route Sex-split Greater Preiss–Handler metabolism in females; greater liver salvage metabolism in males
Evidence status: hypothesis. The proposed mechanism — estrogen stabilising PARP-1 and thereby preserving NAD+ in females — is an inference, not a direct demonstration. The evidence it rests on (sex-reversed PARP-1 knockout phenotypes, ischemic injury data) is consistent with the hypothesis but does not establish it. The pilot observation that women retain higher absolute SIRT1 and SIRT3 protein pools with age (Biology 2025) remains preliminary.

Implications for intervention selection: precursor dosing and gate choice may require sex stratification. Males may benefit more from PARP-1 inhibition to slow consumption; females more from CD38 inhibition to reduce ovarian and residual NAD+ decline. Post-menopausal women show an age-related NAD+ decline together with a hormonal shift; a larger response to NAD+ restoration in this group is plausible but untested. The 78c adverse-reaction signal in female mice is reported as a constraint on unstratified CD38 inhibition in that group.

Unresolved questions in the vault

  1. The effect of CD38 inhibition on SARM1-dependent axon degeneration has not been tested directly. Mechanistic plausibility is reported; human or animal outcome data are absent.
  2. Whether combined CD38 and SARM1 inhibition is synergistic is unresolved. The only precedent is CD38 + PARP.
  3. CD38 / SARM1 co-expression in microglia is unresolved; the corresponding graph nodes are stubs in the typed layer.
  4. Whether NMN preservation under NR + CD38-inhibitor dosing crosses the SARM1 activation threshold is unresolved.
  5. The relative NAD+ rescue of CD38 + PARP versus CD38 + SARM1 dual inhibition has not been compared.
  6. The NMN/NAD+ ratio relevant to SARM1 activation (plasma, tissue, or axonal compartment) is unresolved.
  7. Whether the CIPN convergence is causal or a wiki co-mention artefact is unresolved. Both NADases reaching one indication may represent a shared causal axis or a co-mention artefact of the wiki layer.
Evidence status and scope. This page is a graph-derived and preclinical synthesis. The 78c lifespan figure is mouse and male-specific. The CD38+SARM1 synergy is a hypothesis. NR/NMN human efficacy remains contested, and human NAD+ precursor trials have not reproduced the phenotypic effects reported in rodents. Nothing here is medical advice.
