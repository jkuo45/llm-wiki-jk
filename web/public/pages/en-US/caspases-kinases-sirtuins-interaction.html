Caspases, kinases, and sirtuins

These enzyme superfamilies regulate cell death through different reaction chemistries, timescales, and subcellular locations. This page maps their direct interactions from protein modification to organelle function, cell-fate decisions, and tissue consequences.

Network overview

Layered control of cell death

interaction level · acute-to-slow regulation

Kinases provide rapid, reversible control; sirtuins provide NAD⁺-coupled control; caspases execute proteolytic changes. Their shared nodes integrate receptor signals, DNA damage, metabolism, mitochondrial integrity, and transcription.

Reaction chemistry

Different substrates, fuels, and reversibility

All three families alter existing proteins, but the chemical operation and the logic of activation differ. Phosphorylation and deacylation are paired with counter-enzymes; caspase proteolysis changes covalent structure and is not reversed by simply restoring a post-translational mark.

Kinases

ATP-dependent phosphotransfer · rapid · reversible

A kinase transfers the terminal γ-phosphate of ATP to an acceptor hydroxyl, most commonly on serine, threonine, or tyrosine. Phosphatases reverse the reaction.

  • Localization and scaffold binding determine which substrates are accessible.
  • Phosphorylation can change catalytic state, localization, binding, or degradation rate.
  • Kinase output is context-dependent; Akt can support survival in one setting and participate in stress signaling in another.

Sirtuins

NAD⁺-dependent deacylation · metabolic · reversible in principle

Sirtuins transfer an acyl group from a lysine-linked substrate to NAD⁺, generating nicotinamide and 2′-O-acyl-ADP-ribose. Acyltransferases can reinstall some marks.

  • NAD⁺ availability, nicotinamide feedback, localization, and post-translational modification regulate activity.
  • SIRT1/6/7 are predominantly nuclear; SIRT2 is broadly cytosolic; SIRT3/4/5 are principally mitochondrial.
  • Deacylation can change transcription-factor activity, protein interaction, localization, or metabolic enzyme function.

protein–NH₂+ NAM + OAADPR
Counter-reaction
Histone/protein acetyltransferases and other acyltransferases can restore selected marks.
Primary input
NAD⁺ links sirtuin reaction rate to cellular metabolic state.
Cell-death examples
SIRT1–p53/FOXO, SIRT5–cytochrome c, SIRT3–NLRP3/mitochondrial restraint.

Caspases

Cysteine proteolysis after Asp · amplification · difficult to reverse

Caspases hydrolyze peptide bonds C-terminal to selected aspartate residues. Initiators are activated on proximity platforms; executioners cleave structural, nuclear, and repair proteins.

  • DISC, apoptosome, inflammasome, and related assemblies control proximity and catalytic competence.
  • Limited cleavage can switch a regulator; broad cleavage can dismantle cellular substrates.
  • Caspase-1 additionally matures IL-1β and IL-18 and cleaves gasdermins; inflammatory caspases can support cytokine release before overt lysis.
protein–Asp↓X–peptide+ H₂O →
fragment A + fragment B+ 2 H⁺
Counter-reaction
Proteolytic products must be degraded or repolymerized; the original peptide bond is not restored by a phosphatase.
Activation control
Proximity, inhibitor binding, platform assembly, and initiator–executioner feedback.
Cell-death examples
Caspase-3/7 effectors; Caspase-1 inflammatory substrate processing; cleavage of SIRT1 or PKCδ can alter feedback.

Cell-fate scenarios

What changes when a control layer fails?

The same enzyme families support developmental cell removal, stress adaptation, immune regulation, and tumor persistence. The outcome is determined by which regulatory inputs dominate. Select a scenario to update the 3-D model and the mechanistic summary.

Kinase layer seconds → minutes

Akt, Src, and checkpoint kinases impose rapid inhibition or alter scaffold use. Phosphatases permit reset and repeated signaling.

Sirtuin layer minutes → hours

Adequate NAD⁺ supports SIRT1/SIRT3/SIRT5 restraint of transcription, mitochondria, and inflammasome assembly, subject to localization and substrate context.

Caspase layer minutes → irreversible

Initiator and executioner activity remain below the level required for broad substrate cleavage; inflammatory caspases are correspondingly constrained.

Research design

Measure state, reaction, location, and consequence separately

A convincing experiment distinguishes enzyme abundance from catalytic activity, modification state from total protein, and cellular outcome from pathway label. Perturb one layer, measure at least two spatial levels, and include an orthogonal viability or inflammatory endpoint.

Core measurement panel

  1. Define the initiating input: death receptor, DNA damage, ER stress, pathogen signal, metabolic stress, or treatment.
  2. Measure kinase state: site-specific phospho-antibodies where validated, plus total protein and phosphatase-sensitive readouts.
  3. Measure sirtuin function: NAD⁺/NADH, site-specific acetylation, subcellular localization, and a catalytic assay or target-specific rescue.
  4. Measure caspase processing: full-length and cleaved initiator/executioner caspases, activity reporter, direct substrate cleavage, and inhibitor rescue.
  5. Resolve location: imaging or fractionation for membrane DISC, apoptosome, mitochondria, nucleus, and inflammasome assembly.
  6. Measure outcome: clonogenic survival, membrane integrity, apoptosis markers, cytokine release, inflammatory pore formation, and clearance by phagocytes.

Perturbation logic

  1. Kinase inhibition or mutation: test whether rapid phosphorylation changes precede caspase activity.
  2. Phosphatase activation: test reversibility and whether a response requires the kinase rather than downstream protein abundance.
  3. SIRT1/3/5 genetic manipulation: separate catalytic function from scaffolding effects and control for localization.
  4. NAD+-directed perturbation: distinguish sirtuin-dependent effects from other NAD+-consuming processes such as PARP and CD38.
  5. Selective versus broad caspase blockade: distinguish initiator signaling, executioner apoptosis, and inflammatory cytokine processing.
  6. Combination conditions: test the shared nodes (p53, FOXO, XIAP, Bax, NLRP3) rather than only the three enzyme-family names.

Interpretive boundary. “Sirtuin inhibition is therapeutic in cancer” and “sirtuin activation is protective in aging” are not universal statements. Effects depend on paralog, localization, substrate, NAD+ availability, disease state, dose, duration, and cell type. The same reasoning applies to the frequently used phrase “caspase activation.” Confirm the specific caspase, active site, substrate, compartment, and phenotypic endpoint.
