Enzyme class

The caspase enzyme class, read through the graph

Caspases are cysteine-aspartic proteases — signalling enzymes that make one or two specific cuts after aspartate residues, converting target proteins into gain- or loss-of-function states rather than degrading them (McIlwain, Berger & Mak 2013, PMID 23545416). Humans encode 14: initiators (CASP-8/9/10/2) dock via DED/CARD prodomains onto activation platforms — the DISC at death receptors, the Apaf-1 apoptosome after mitochondrial permeabilization, or inflammasomes; executioners (CASP-3/6/7) dismantle the cell; inflammatory caspases (CASP-1/4/5/11) mature IL-1β/IL-18 and cleave Gasdermin D to trigger pyroptosis. CASP-2 (genome stability), CASP-12 (ER stress), and CASP-14 (skin differentiation) round out the family.

Activation is scaffolded — platform identity determines which caspase activates, in which cell, and with what outcome. Caspases also act outside cell death (non-apoptotic CASP-3 in senescence reinforcement, CASP-1 as a SASP amplifier).

This section walks the enzyme class from its taxonomy down to its pharmacology, and reads each layer against the combined graph: every Graph readout callout is a measurement on the 4,821-node / 47,925-edge union, reproducible from raw_caspase_class_aggregates_20_OCT_2026.json. The method sections that follow (communities, centrality, PPR) carry the full metric detail; here the class is the spine and the graph is the read-out.

Classification: where caspases sit in the enzyme hierarchy

Caspases are one branch of the peptidase (protease) superfamily — EC 3.4 hydrolases — and they are defined by how they cut. The catalytic type is set by the active-site nucleophile, which is the scheme used by both the MEROPS peptidase database and the IUBMB nomenclature: serine (Ser–His–Asp charge relay), cysteine (Cys–His dyad, thiolate nucleophile), aspartic (Asp–Asp dyad), metallo (Zn²⁺-activated water), and threonine (Thr nucleophile) types. Caspases belong to the cysteine (C) type, and within it to MEROPS clan CD (the papain superfamily), family C1, subfamily C1B — the caspases.

Graph readout

The vault models that hierarchy as a note, and the graph wires it thinly: the Protease node has union degree 38 and reaches exactly three caspase nodes — Caspase-1, Caspase-3, and the generic Caspases class node — while Cathepsins (degree 25) touches the family only through Caspases. The superfamily edge exists but carries almost no weight, so the graph will not surface cross-superfamily relations (caspase–calpain crosstalk, caspase–proteasome coupling) on its own: they have to be wired deliberately.

Catalytic mechanism and substrate specificity

All caspases share one catalytic construction: a Cys–His dyad (plus a third residue, Arg-179 in human caspase-3, that orients the substrate) generates a thiolate nucleophile that attacks the scissile peptide bond, with water completing the hydrolysis. The strict requirement is an aspartate at P1; preference at P2–P4 is what separates family members. These preferences are encoded in the S1–S4 substrate-recognition pocket, and the differences are small but decisive: executioner caspases carry a glycine at S2 and prefer acidic P2 residues, whereas initiator caspases carry a bulkier S2 side chain and accept hydrophobic P2 — caspase-8's IETD, caspase-9's FEAD, caspase-6's LETD, caspase-3/7's DEVD. Caspase-2 is the odd case: a small S2 pocket (Ala-338) plus a strong P5 requirement, which is why it recognises sites (e.g. IEKD) no other family member will cut.

The enzymological consequence is that a caspase is a signed, limited editor of its substrates, not a bulk degrader: one or two cuts at defined sites convert a target into a new active, inactive or fragmented state. The same cleavage can be activating in one substrate and inhibiting in another — which is why "cleaved" is not the same as "degraded" in every pathway below.

Graph readout

The most-connected node in the family is also the one carrying the most-used motif: Caspase-3 (DEVD) has union degree 76 with in-degree 59 — the family's most-cited member. Caspase-7 shares the DEVD specificity but sits at degree 26, and Caspase-6 (LETD) at 16 lands in its own community ("Apoptosis-Inducing Factor"), because its lamin edge dominates its neighbourhood. The reading: graph degree tracks how many notes a member participates in, which correlates with motif ubiquity but is not an enzymological ranking — Caspase-6 is narrowly specific, not weak.

Activation platforms: the class's control layer

Caspases are synthesized as inactive zymogens. Initiators are activated by induced proximity — dimerization on a scaffold, not proteolysis — which is why platform identity decides the outcome; executioners are activated by initiator-mediated cleavage of their interdomain linker. Graph readout

The class node Caspases carries the family's highest k-core (19) and a Master-regulator role with the largest out-degree (29) — and it is where the class meets its own enzyme class: its 43 neighbours include Protease, Cathepsins, Z-VAD-FMK, Livin, XIAP, Smac DIABLO, PARP1, ICAD, Gasdermin E and Unfolded Protein Response alongside nine other caspase nodes. No single member does that. The generic class node is doing the work that no individual enzyme can: it is the family's only representation of the protease superfamily and of its pharmacology.

Intra-family wiring: what the graph calls redundancy

Within-class edges were extracted from the union (19 intra-family edges over the 13 member nodes). Their pattern reproduces the activation hierarchy rather than the taxonomy:

  • Inflammatory group — complete 4-clique. All six edges among Caspase-1, -4, -5 and -11 are present. This is the mechanical reason their pairwise Jaccard is 0.818 (the highest redundancy in the source set): the members are wired to each other as well as to the shared platform.
  • Executioners — a hub and two spokes. Caspase-3 ↔ Caspase-6 and Caspase-3 ↔ Caspase-7 are present, but Caspase-6 ↔ Caspase-7 is absent: both executioners hang off the relay hub rather than each other, which is what a cascade topology predicts.
  • Initiators — a triad and one isolate. Caspase-8, -9 and -10 are mutually linked, but Caspase-2 has no intra-initiator edge at all; it reaches the other initiators only through the generic class node. The graph keeps the PIDDosome axis separate, mirroring its distinct platform (and the same fact that surfaces as the missing Caspase-2 → Apoptosis edge in the enrichment queue).

Graph readout — read the clique carefully

A 4-clique among Caspase-1/4/5/11 is not four independent biological statements. Two of these nodes (Caspase-4 and Caspase-5) are human genes whose ortholog pair is the single murine gene Caspase-11 (see Evolution). Part of the clique is therefore an artifact of how orthologous biology is written into notes — and part is real (same platform, same substrate). The honest reading of Jaccard 0.818 is "same functional neighbourhood", not "three redundant enzymes". The vault's per-member notes already flag 4/5 as human and 11 as murine.

Regulation at the class level

The family's activity is gated by three layers: decoys (IAPs — XIAP's BIR2 and BIR3 domains bind the executioner and initiator active sites; Livin and cIAP1/2 are further members), antagonists (Smac/DIABLO neutralizes XIAP; TBK1-driven XIAP degradation releases the brake), and pseudo-substrates (c-FLIP binds the DISC with higher affinity than procaspase-8, and in its long form converts Caspase-8 into a DISC-retained, necroptosis-blocking complex rather than an active enzyme). Viruses encode the same strategy — CrmA and SPI-2 are serpins that occlude the Caspase-1 and Caspase-8 active sites. On top of that sits a dense phosphorylation switch: Akt at Ser196 of Caspase-9, p38 MAPK at Ser150 of Caspase-3 and Ser364 of Caspase-8, Src/FYN/LYN at Tyr397 of Caspase-8 (reversed by SHP1), PAK2 at Ser30/Thr173/Ser239 of Caspase-7, CDK1 at Ser308 of Caspase-2 — and localization matters as much as cleavage (cytosolic Caspase-3 versus nuclear Caspase-6).

Graph readout

The class-level brake is well modelled on the executioners and invisible on the inflammatory arm. XIAP (degree 30), Smac DIABLO (degree 30) and TBK1 (degree 42) each wire to Caspase-3, Caspase-7 and Caspase-9; c-FLIP (degree 35) wires to Caspase-8 and Caspase-10 — and the mechanism itself lives in a dedicated note node, Caspase-8-c-FLIP Rheostat (degree 39, k-core 16), the highest-k-core node anywhere in the caspase neighbourhood. Note the asymmetry: no regulator reaches Caspase-1/-4/-5/-11. One normalization artifact shows up here too — phosphorylation sites are modelled as entities, so Caspase-3 (at Ser150) and Caspase-2 Ser135 are degree-1 stubs that should be attributes of their enzymes.

Assay toolbox

Caspase activity is measured either kinetically or morphologically, and the two answer different questions. Fluorogenic peptide substrates (DEVD-AMC, AC-DEVD-FMK) and chromogenic substrates give rate constants and inhibitor IC₅₀ values but read out only the dominant activity, and DEVD-based substrates cannot separate Caspase-3 from Caspase-7. Immunostaining for cleaved Caspase-3 (Asp175 neo-epitope) is the marker of record for apoptosis in tissue, and cleaved GSDMD N-term for pyroptosis — but cleavage is not activity: inhibitors, IAPs and reversible phosphorylation all decouple the two. Peptidomic substrate mapping (N- terminomics; CASBAH) is what turned "caspase" from a pathway label into an enzyme class, with more than a thousand mapped cleavage sites.

Graph readout

Pharmacology entered the graph as a class-level annotation: the pan-caspase inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK is a node (degree 10) whose only caspase neighbour is the generic Caspases class node, and the IAP Livin (degree 7) attaches the same way. The graph therefore asserts that the class has a pharmacology, but not which enzyme any given drug touches — the target-level edges are exactly the ones an enzyme-class account would want.

Pharmacology of the class

No caspase inhibitor has achieved regulatory approval. The reasons are structural: the catalytic Cys-His dyad is near-identical across the family, so selectivity must come from the S3/S4 pockets, and the executioners' active sites are also the ones most worth preserving in tumors. The clinically active arena has therefore shifted upstream — inflammasome and NLRP3 modulation, GSDMD pore blockade — or indirectly, through BCL-2 family inhibitors that restore executioner sensitivity in senescent cells.

Translational gap

No caspase inhibitor has achieved regulatory approval as of 2026. Pan-caspase inhibition risks dampening apoptotic clearance of damaged cells; the most active clinical arena is NLRP3/inflammasome modulation rather than direct caspase targeting. Pro-caspase "senolytic re-activation" remains an aspiration, not a realized pharmacology. The graph's prediction queue reaches the same conclusion from the other side: the strongest absent caspase pairs are the clinical ones (Caspase-3 → Cancer/Chemotherapy, Caspase-9 → Cancer — see Predictions), i.e. the class is best documented in its enzymology and worst documented in its therapeutic edges.

Evolution and orthology

Caspases are a metazoan invention: no caspase genes exist in yeast or plants, and the family traces to a bilaterian ancestor, with the epidermal Caspase-14 as the most divergent member. Two orthology facts shape the graph directly. First, human CASP-4 and CASP-5 are orthologs of the single murine gene Caspase-11 — so the vault's 13 caspase nodes cover 14 human genes, with one node carrying two of them. Second, human CASP-12 is a pseudogene in most populations (a functional ER caspase only in mouse and a few human alleles), which explains why its graph degree (7) is the family's lowest and why its community assignment ("Apoptosis") reflects the note's content rather than a distinct ER platform.

Graph readout

Orthology is visible in the graph as a clique and as a node count. The Caspase-1/-4/-5/-11 4-clique and the Jaccard 0.818 both trace to the murine-node convention (see Communities); the 13-node/14-gene mismatch is why "Caspase-14" — and, more consequentially, a distinct human Caspase-5 node — is on the enrichment queue rather than in the graph.

The class in one paragraph

Caspases are one enzyme class — cysteine peptidases of MEROPS clan CD — whose functional diversity is bought with one architecture (prodomain + p20 + p10, Cys-His dyad, (p20/p10)₂ tetramer) reused across three activation strategies (proximity on a scaffold, initiator relay, noncanonical lipid sensing). The combined graph reproduces that architecture rather than merely annotating it: platform edges are one-to-one with the class taxonomy, the degree ordering tracks motif ubiquity, the class node is where the superfamily and the pharmacology live, and the family's visible anomalies — Caspase-2's isolation, Caspase-6's lamin community, the Caspase-1/4/5/11 clique, Caspase-12's missing ER platform — are each explicable as enzymology rather than as noise.

Communities

Community structure: apoptotic vs inflammatory modules

Class taxonomy for reference: the caspase enzyme class — and the intra-family edges and platform adjacency quoted below are derived there.

Louvain community detection on the wiki layer (Figure 2) splits the caspases along their biological class boundary: c4 Apoptosis (110 nodes) holds Caspase-3/6/7/9/12, Caspases, and Executioner Caspase; c13 NF-κB (76) holds Caspase-4/5/11 together with Inflammasome; c11 Necroptosis (80) holds Caspase-1/8/10 with Pyroptosis, Gasdermin D, and the Caspase-8-c-FLIP Rheostat.

The initiator/executioner split is not resolved inside the Apoptosis community — intrinsic (CASP-9) and executioner (CASP-3/6/7) caspases share one community, reflecting that the wiki treats apoptosis as one tightly cross-linked module. Caspase-2 is an outlier in the wiki layer: it sits in the "Akt" community (c12, 78 nodes), not with the apoptosis module, while Caspase-8/10 group with Necroptosis (c11) — consistent with the death-receptor/RIPK axis. Caspase-1 now sits with Necroptosis in the wiki layer (c11) rather than in a dedicated IL-1α community: the September run's c41 IL-1alpha grouping dissolved as the wiki notes grew.

The combined dataset's own community labels (triples-layer metadata, which the merge prefers) draw the boundaries even finer: Caspase-1/4/5/11 cluster with Inflammasome and Gasdermin D under "Paracrine Senescence" (c16), Caspase-2 sits in "Akt" (c53), Caspase-6 in "Apoptosis-Inducing Factor" (c95), Executioner Caspase in "Senescence" (c51), and only Caspase-3/7/9/10/12 + Caspases sit in "Apoptosis" (c8), with Caspase-8 in its own "Caspase-8" community (c67). Either way, the two-module structure (apoptotic vs inflammasome–pyroptosis) is stable across all three views, and it is unchanged from the September run.

Takeaway

The triples layer contributes the caspases' regulatory/kinase and inflammasome-scaffold edges; the wiki layer contributes the death-program edges. Any caspase analysis that uses only one layer will systematically miss half the story — e.g. a wiki-only run falsely reports "no PIDDosome".

Literature

Literature deep dive: caspases

Classification

Full taxonomy — MEROPS clan CD / family C1 / subfamily C1B, per-member prodomain, activation platform, signature motif and graph metrics — is in the enzyme-class chapter, with the activation-platform and intra-family wiring read directly off the combined graph. In brief: 14 human caspases (cysteine-aspartic proteases, CASP1–CASP14) group by function and homology — initiators (CASP-8, -9, -10, -2; long prodomains, activation by induced-proximity dimerization), executioners (CASP-3, -6, -7; short prodomains, activated by initiator-mediated cleavage), inflammatory (CASP-1, -4, -5, -11), and the specialised CASP-12 (ER stress; truncated/pseudogene-derived in most humans) and CASP-14 (epidermal differentiation).

Domain structure & activation platforms

Conserved architecture — prodomain (CARD in CASP-1/2/4/5/9/12; DED in CASP-8/10; none in executioners) + large (p20) + small (p10) catalytic subunits, active enzyme a (p20/p10)₂ heterotetramer — and the platform table are covered in Activation platforms. The primary literature behind them:

  • Apoptosome (intrinsic): Cytochrome c + Apaf-1 + dATP → heptameric platform → CARD–CARD recruitment of pro-CASP-9 (Li et al. 2000, PMID 10529249; Riedl & Salvesen 2007, PMID 17344876).
  • DISC (extrinsic): death receptors (FasL/TRAIL/TNF-α) → FADD → pro-CASP-8/10 DED clustering; type-II cells amplify via CASP-8 cleavage of Bid (Li et al. 1998, PMID 9808642).
  • Inflammasomes: NLRP3/NLRP1/NLRC4/AIM2/pyrin + ASC → pro-CASP-1; non-canonical CASP-4/5/11 bind cytosolic LPS directly via CARD (Shi et al. 2014, PMID 25043016).

Caspases in aging biology

Apoptosis resistance in senescent cells — senescent cells upregulate BCL-2/BCL-xL/BCL-W, downregulate Bim/Puma/Noxa, and show blunted CASP-8/3 activation; senolytics (dasatinib+quercetin, navitoclax/ABT-263) partly act by re-engaging this machinery (de Keizer 2017, PMID 28266040). Graph correspondence: no edge path connects the senolytic nodes to caspases even in the combined graph (see Gaps below).

CASP-3 / senescence — CASP-3 is activated early in oncogene-induced senescence and exerts non-apoptotic, senescence-reinforcing functions (Tang et al. 2012, PMID 22863278); this role is disputed and under active investigation. Consistent with CASP-3 having the highest PPR mass among caspases for Senescence/SASP/Autophagy seeds.

CASP-1 / NLRP3 / inflammaging — NLRP3→CASP-1→IL-1β signaling increases with age and drives inflammaging, immunosenescence (Youm et al. 2013, PMID 23562091), atherosclerosis (PMID 31153200), metabolic decline (PMID 21802169), and Alzheimer's pathology (Heneka et al. 2013, PMID 23233273). CASP-1 is a core SASP amplifier via IL-1β→NF-κB→IL-6/IL-8. Graph correspondence: the c25 Inflammaging community contains this module.

CASP-2 — bona fide tumor suppressor (KO mice: centrosome excess, aneuploidy, carcinogen-hypersensitive tumorigenesis); cleaves Beclin-1, linking tumor suppression, autophagy suppression, and apoptosis; implicated in oocyte loss and DDR upstream of p53 (PMID 19339967, 31209125, 22940864). Graph correspondence: the combined graph restores its PIDDosome edges (§Layers).

CASP-14 — keratinocyte differentiation, filaggrin processing, UVB protection (PMID 17481861); reduced in aged/photodamaged skin (marker vs driver unresolved). Absent from the vault.

Pharmacology

The class-wide pharmacology — pan-caspase agents (emricasan, VX-765, pralnacasan), the NLRP3/GSDMD axis upstream and downstream of the family, and the senolytic route that restores executioner sensitivity indirectly — is tabulated in Pharmacology of the class, together with the graph's reading of it (the inhibitor nodes Z-VAD-FMK and Livin attach to the generic Caspases node rather than to their targets).

Key takeaways

  1. Enzymologically the family is one class (cysteine peptidases, MEROPS clan CD) deployed through three activation strategies — scaffold proximity, initiator relay, and direct lipid sensing — and the combined graph reproduces that architecture one-to-one: platform edges, class-degree ordering, and a class node that carries the family's pharmacology.
  2. Caspases are not solely death proteases — inflammation (1/4/5/11), differentiation (14), tumor suppression (2), and senescence regulation (3) are all represented.
  3. Activation is scaffolded (apoptosome/DISC/inflammasome) — platform identity determines outcome.
  4. Aging biology is caspase-entangled at three axes: CASP-1 inflammaging, CASP-2 genome stability, and senescent-cell apoptosis resistance.
  5. The combined graph reproduces the two-module structure and pyroptosis/necroptosis/apoptosis assignments, and no caspase pair now reaches the global AA top-150 — the family is fully embedded. Since August: Gasdermin E node added with Caspase-3→GSDME, Caspase-8→Necroptosis and Caspase-1→SASP / Caspase-3→Cellular Senescence directly wired, and Caspase-3↔Caspase-6 plus IAP↔XIAP resolved. Remaining gaps: CASP-14, cGAS-STING, Caspase-2→Apoptosis, Cancer/Chemotherapy clinical edges, BAX/Bim/Bad upstream wiring, therapeutic edges — see Gaps and enrichment queue.
