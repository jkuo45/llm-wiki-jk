SIRT3 in Aging and Aging-Related Disease

Sirtuin 3 (SIRT3) is an NAD+-dependent deacetylase localized mainly to the mitochondrial matrix. In the cited review, SIRT3 deacetylates substrates in the TCA cycle, fatty-acid oxidation, and the electron transport chain, and deacetylates SOD2 and IDH2; SIRT3 is also reported in the nucleus, where it is associated with heterochromatin maintenance and DNA repair. The common SIRT3 VNTR enhancer polymorphism was associated with survival at the oldest ages in the cited association studies; no human lifespan effect has been established in randomized data. Reduced SIRT3 expression or activity is reported in aging and disease models, accompanied by mitochondrial protein hyperacetylation, elevated mitochondrial ROS, NLRP3 inflammasome activation, fibrosis, and impaired mitophagy. Sections below state the mechanisms as reported in the review; preclinical findings are identified as rodent or in-vitro unless otherwise noted.

SIRT3 in the sirtuin family and aging

Mammalian sirtuins (SIRT1–7) differ in subcellular localization: SIRT1, SIRT6, and SIRT7 are primarily nuclear; SIRT2 is cytosolic; SIRT3, SIRT4, and SIRT5 are mitochondrial. SIRT3 is described in the review as the principal regulator of the mitochondrial lysine acetylome. The reported association between a SIRT3 VNTR enhancer polymorphism and survival at the oldest ages is an association finding; it does not establish a causal human lifespan effect.

Metabolic regulation

SIRT3 activity depends on NAD+ and is modulated by the matrix acetyl-CoA state. Reported substrates include TCA-cycle enzymes, fatty-acid oxidation enzymes, and ETC/OXPHOS complexes. In rodent studies, fasting and caloric restriction raised NAD+ and SIRT3 activity; caloric-restriction effects in several tissues were reduced when SIRT3 was absent.

Oxidative-stress regulation

SIRT3 deacetylates and activates SOD2/MnSOD, IDH2, catalase, and GPX in reported in-vitro and rodent systems, and maintains ETC efficiency, which is associated with lower electron leakage to oxygen. SIRT3 loss is reported with hyperacetylated antioxidant enzymes and increased mitochondrial ROS in those models.

Nuclear functions

Under oxidative or genotoxic stress, SIRT3 is reported to translocate to the nucleus, where it maintains heterochromatin in mesenchymal stem cells, decrotonylates histones, recruits 53BP1 for non-homologous end joining, and supports OGG1-mediated mtDNA repair. These nuclear functions remain unverified in human randomized studies.

Protein structure and subcellular localization

Human SIRT3 is a 399-residue polypeptide. The N-terminal mitochondrial targeting sequence is proteolytically removed on import, exposing the catalytic core: an N-terminal Rossmann fold that binds NAD+ and a C-terminal zinc-finger module that stabilizes the structure. Beyond deacetylation, SIRT3 also catalyzes ADP-ribosylation, demalonylation and desuccinylation.

Seven regulatory axes attributed to SIRT3

The review groups SIRT3-linked mechanisms into seven axes. The axes share substrates; reported outcomes include changes in mitochondrial metabolism and ROS, inflammatory signaling, and tissue-remodeling markers in rodent and in-vitro models.

Key excerpt · Oxidative stress (Sec 1.2)

Under caloric restriction, SIRT3 deacetylates and activates mitochondrial IDH2, driving NADPH biosynthesis that fuels antioxidant regeneration and prevents ETC overload — a central mechanism by which SIRT3 couples nutrient sensing to mitochondrial redox homeostasis.

Key excerpt · Epigenetics (Sec 1.3)

Beyond mitochondrial deacetylation, SIRT3 regulates genomic integrity through lysine decrotonylation of histones, heterochromatin maintenance in hMSCs, and 53BP1 recruitment that boosts NHEJ repair of double-strand breaks — establishing a nuclear role in epigenetic control of senescence.

SIRT3 and cellular senescence

Cellular senescence is defined by permanent cell-cycle arrest, senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) secretion, macromolecular damage, and metabolic dysregulation. The review attributes several SIRT3-linked effects on senescence markers, SASP components, and stem-cell maintenance:

Senescence markers

Deacetylation-driven antioxidant capacity minimizes DNA damage and delays onset of SA-β-gal positivity; SIRT3 inhibits the p53/p21 axis that executes arrest.

SASP suppression

SIRT3 loss increases SASP components — TNF-α, IL-1β, MMP9 — via mitochondrial dysfunction; activation ameliorates inflammatory tissue damage. A miR-494-3p ↓ SIRT3 feedback loop exacerbates senescence under oxidative stress.

Stem cell reserve

In hMSCs and hematopoietic stem cells, SIRT3 preserves heterochromatin and sustains the autophagy–SIRT3 positive feedback loop (FOXO3a → SIRT3 → PINK1/Parkin mitophagy → lower mtROS → stem-cell repopulation maintained).

Key excerpt · Autophagy (Sec 1.7)

Autophagy induces SIRT3 through FOXO3a, and SIRT3 in turn promotes PINK1/Parkin-mediated mitophagy to lower mitochondrial ROS — restoring hematopoietic stem cell repopulation and linking mitophagic quality control to stem cell maintenance during aging.

SIRT3 in heart failure and cardiovascular disease

Cardiac SIRT3 expression is reported decreased during heart failure, in parallel with mitochondrial proteome hyperacetylation, impaired oxidative metabolism, and elevated ROS. In rodent models, SIRT3 activation of FOXO3a is associated with reduced pathological remodeling; cardiac SIRT3 overexpression reduced hypertrophy and fibrosis in those models, whereas SIRT3-null mice developed hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and progressive ventricular dysfunction. No human heart-failure trial of SIRT3 activation is reported in the review.

Atherosclerosis

In rodent models, SIRT3 deficiency is reported with higher oxidative stress and plaque formation. SIRT3 suppresses ox-LDL-driven foam-cell formation via AMPK/UCP1 in vitro, is reported to preserve endothelial mitochondria and nitric oxide through the SIRT3/ATG5 axis in rodent models, restrains endothelial NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and deacetylates FOXO3a, with downstream catalase expression. Gut-microbiota metabolites modulate the SIRT3/SOD2/FOXO3a axis and the SREBP1c/FAS/DGAT2 lipogenic pathway in reported models. Reported inconsistency: one LDL-receptor-knockout study found no lesion change after SIRT3 deletion.

Key excerpt · HFpEF and the microbiota (Sec 2.2.2)

Gut-derived metabolites such as indole-3-propionic acid potentiate SIRT3 to improve myocardial bioenergetics and diastolic function in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction — a microbiota–mitochondria axis with translational potential.

Diabetic cardiomyopathy

In rodent models, SIRT3 activation improved mitochondrial respiratory capacity via the AGO2/cytochrome b axis. In the same models, melatonin protection is reported with Mst1 inhibition and SIRT3 upregulation; the FGF21–SIRT3 axis is reported to mediate exercise-associated changes in cardiac mitochondria. Human evidence is not reported in the review.

Hypertension and vascular wall

SIRT3 depletion in essential hypertension is linked to vascular inflammation and SOD2 hyperacetylation; deacetylation of CypD prevents endothelial mPTP-driven dysfunction; Ang-II-induced renal and cardiac fibrosis are both SIRT3-sensitive.

SIRT3 mechanisms by disease category

Summarized from the review, Sec. 2. Badges indicate the mechanism emphasized for each category in the review. Most findings are from rodent or in-vitro models; doses and sample sizes are not quantified in the review and are not restated here.

Alzheimer's disease mitophagy tau

In rodent and in-vitro models, brain SIRT3 levels are reported to be lower in Alzheimer disease tissue, with levels inversely associated with tau deposition. SIRT3 deacetylates mitochondrial p53 at Lys320 and is reported to maintain bioenergetics under Aβ exposure, support PINK1/Parkin mitophagy, activate MnSOD, and modulate PACAP signaling. Human efficacy is untested.

Parkinson's disease mitochondrial dynamics

In rodent models, SIRT3 deacetylates DRP1 (K711), reported to normalize mitochondrial dynamics and protect dopaminergic neurons; reports include PGC-1α-driven biogenesis, reduced α-synuclein aggregation, and lower NLRP3 inflammasome activity. Human efficacy is untested.

ALS OXPHOS NAD⁺ salvage

In rodent models, SIRT3 loss is associated with mitochondrial hyperacetylation and altered OXPHOS complex stoichiometry and ETC capacity. SIRT3 overexpression ameliorated SOD1(G93A) phenotypes in the reported model; NAD+-salvage potentiation with nicotinamide preserved neuronal ultrastructure in the same model. Muscle NAD+ salvage is also reported impaired in ALS models.

Huntington's disease bioenergetics AMPK

In rodent models, SIRT3 overexpression is reported to ameliorate symptoms and support striatal neuron survival against mutant huntingtin; SIRT3 deficiency increased vulnerability to glutamate excitotoxicity. Reported downstream effects include AMPK phosphorylation, PGC-1α programs, and reduced Drp1/Fis1 with preserved axonal transport. In Drosophila, the ortholog dSirt2 was reported to extend lifespan and limit neuropil degeneration. Human data are not reported.

Diabetes mellitus insulin sensitivity

SIRT3 expression is reported lower in diabetic tissues, in association with insulin resistance. In rodent models, skeletal-muscle SIRT3 loss impaired insulin-stimulated glucose uptake; β-cell-selective knockout disrupted glucose-stimulated insulin secretion (serotonin-dependent); hippocampal hyperglycemia suppressed SIRT3 with VDAC1/GRP75/IP3R-mediated neuronal apoptosis; and irisin bone protection in diabetic periodontitis required SIRT3.

Cancer context-dependent

In vitro and rodent studies report tumor-suppressive effects: destabilization of HIF-1α, genome-integrity support via OGG1 and H3K56 deacetylation, and IDH2 K143 deacetylation with reduced ROS-driven mutagenesis. In other cell systems, SIRT3 is reported as oncogenic — PYCR1 K228 (proline metabolism), SHMT2 K95 (serine metabolism), GLDC K514 (nucleotide synthesis), and ROS buffering associated with chemoresistance in chronic lymphocytic leukemia cells. Any SIRT3-targeting intervention would require cell-type-specific assessment.

Kidney — AKI → CKD fibrosis apoptosis

Renal SIRT3 expression is reported lower across nephropathies, in association with histological severity. In rodent septic and ischemia-reperfusion acute kidney injury (AKI) models, SIRT3 knockout worsened tubular apoptosis (BAX oligomerization, caspase-3) and fatty-acid oxidation failure; SIRT3 activation restored OXPHOS measures, Nrf2 signaling, and cristae integrity in those models. SIRT3 deficiency accelerated the AKI-to-CKD transition with early TGF-β/Smad3 activation; in CKD models, SIRT3 restrained epithelial-mesenchymal transition, β-catenin-driven fibroblast activation, and vascular calcification (PGC-1α/TFAM, sEH-dependent).

COPD senescence ferroptosis

In rodent and in-vitro COPD models, the PGC-1α/SIRT3 axis is reported to support mitochondrial biogenesis and redox balance in airway epithelium; SIRT3 deficiency worsened alveolar destruction and cigarette-smoke injury with SOD2 hyperacetylation, Nrf2/iNOS imbalance, and cigarette-smoke-induced ferroptosis. Oxidative stress upregulated miR-494-3p, which targets SIRT3, with increased p27 and airway-epithelial senescence. mtDNA leakage engaged cGAS-STING signaling in the reported models.

Intervertebral disc degeneration ferroptosis

SIRT3 expression is reported reduced in degenerated discs; SIRT3 knockdown worsened nucleus pulposus cell decline under oxidative stress in vitro. Lactate downregulated SIRT3 with ACSL4 lactylation and ferroptosis markers in the reported model. SIRT3 overexpression reduced disc-degeneration severity in vivo in the reported model; hydrogel microspheres co-delivering SIRT3/SOD2 supported nucleus pulposus cell viability in vitro; AMPK/PGC-1α signaling was reported to counter advanced glycation end-product (AGE)-associated mitochondrial dysfunction.

Osteoarthritis mitophagy

SIRT3 deacetylates COX4I2, reported to maintain respiratory-chain function; SIRT3 deletion exacerbated osteoarthritis in rodent models, with supporting human-sample association data. In chondrocytes, SIRT3 promoted PINK1/Parkin mitophagy and FOXO3a-regulated redox homeostasis and inhibited apoptosis via PI3K/Akt/mTOR in vitro; SUMOylation-mediated SIRT3 inhibition was associated with cartilage degeneration. Mitochondrial fusion linked to SIRT3 is reported to support mtDNA complementation.

Age-related hearing loss CR-dependent

In mice, caloric restriction reduced oxidative damage in cochlear cells and slowed age-related hearing loss; the effect was absent in SIRT3-deficient mice. SIRT3-driven IDH2 activation increased mitochondrial NADPH and ROS clearance, associated with preservation of cochlear hair cells in the same model.

Fibrosis (cross-cutting) GSK-3β Lys15

In peritoneal, cardiac, pulmonary, and hepatic rodent models, SIRT3 deacetylates GSK-3β at Lys15, associated with reduced Smad3 transcriptional complexes, and suppresses FOS/AP-1 and STAT3/NFATc2 in cardiomyocytes. Withaferin A hepatic anti-fibrotic effects were absent in SIRT3-knockout mice in the reported model.

SIRT3-dependence reported for

Honokiol, melatonin, and 2-APQC effects were reported absent or reduced in SIRT3-knockout or nicotinamide-blocked models. SIRT3-dependence in those models distinguishes the reported effects from association alone; it does not establish human efficacy.

Limitations and research priorities

Human evidence gap

The review reports no robust randomized human trials of SIRT3 activation and no aging-specific clinical endpoints. Proposed priorities include adaptive trial designs with composite geriatric outcomes, omics-based biomarkers to stratify and monitor SIRT3 activity, and longitudinal safety registries. All remain untested in humans.

Context dependence

SIRT3 is reported cytoprotective in most tissues but oncogenic in some cell systems (proline, serine, and nucleotide metabolism; ROS buffering). Single-cell multi-omics would be needed to resolve cell-type-specific SIRT3 activity; systemic SIRT3 agonism in cancer survivors remains untested.

Delivery

Proposed approaches include brain-permeable activators for Parkinson and Huntington disease models, cardiomyocyte- versus fibroblast-targeted delivery in heart-failure models, hydrogel microspheres for disc models, and microbiota-derived metabolites (indole-3-propionic acid) in HFpEF models. All remain preclinical.

Summary

SIRT3 is an NAD+-dependent deacetylase linked in the review to metabolism, redox, epigenetics, inflammation, autophagy, apoptosis, and fibrosis. Reduced SIRT3 is reported across several aging and disease models; restoration or activation effects are reported in rodent and in-vitro models. Human efficacy, optimal dose, and long-term safety remain untested.
