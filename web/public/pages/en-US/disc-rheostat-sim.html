Interactive

Set the DISC composition, read out the fate

Illustrative model, not a reproduction. The ODE system below is a deliberately small stand-in whose qualitative behaviour reproduces four behaviours reported in the literature: a ligand threshold set by c-FLIP abundance (Bentele et al. 2004), survival with p43-FLIP/NF-κB signalling below that threshold (Lavrik et al. 2007; Neumann et al. 2010), the blocking behaviour of stoichiometric c-FLIP and of c-FLIPS/R, and the c-FLIPL contribution to the first cleavage step (Fricker et al. 2010). Rate constants are not the published parameter sets; the model omits membrane trafficking, IAPs/XIAP, Bid-driven mitochondrial amplification and c-FLIP isoform synthesis/degradation balance, and its time axis is in arbitrary model units. Do not quote its numbers as measurements.

Isoforms

One CFLAR locus, three proteins — plus two that only exist after cleavage

The explorer above treats c-FLIP as two species, c-FLIPL and c-FLIPS/R. That collapse is convenient and defensible, but it hides three facts that matter for reading the model: the human gene CFLAR (2q33–q34, next to CASP8 and CASP10) encodes at least 13 splice transcripts of which three are translated — c-FLIPL (55 kDa), c-FLIPS (27 kDa) and c-FLIPR (25 kDa); all three share the same tandem death effector domains (DED1 + DED2) and differ only at the C-terminus; and two further proteins, p43-FLIP and p22-FLIP, are not splice products at all but caspase-8 cleavage fragments of c-FLIPL. The isoform composition of a cell, not its total c-FLIP, is what the DISC reads out.

c-FLIPL 55 kDa · human, mouse

DED1DED2p20p12
D196 → p22 D376 → p43

Two DEDs plus a caspase-like domain whose catalytic residues are substituted, so it cleaves nothing. It is nonetheless the only isoform that activates procaspase-8: the c-FLIPL/procaspase-8 heterodimer is a more efficient activator of the first cleavage step than procaspase-8 alone, and the pseudo-protease domain is what lets that heterodimer cleave RIPK1. This is the isoform that restrains necroptosis — and, at high local concentration, the one that blocks full caspase-8 maturation.

Explorer readout at these settings: apoptosis — a physiological c-FLIPL level does not block the platform, it accelerates the first cleavage step of procaspase-8.

c-FLIPS 27 kDa · human only

DED1DED219 aa
D196 → p22

The same DEDs, a 19-residue C-terminal tail, no caspase-like domain — a pure inhibitor. It occupies FADD DED sites and blocks procaspase-8 dimerisation without activating it, and it cannot cleave RIPK1. Apoptosis off with RIPK1 unopposed is exactly the necroptosis-permissive configuration, and c-FLIPS is the isoform that creates it.

Explorer readout at these settings: necroptosis-permissive — apoptosis off, RIPK1 unopposed. Note that this needs a dominant short isoform relative to the number of activated receptors: at high ligand the same amount of c-FLIPS is diluted across more DISC sites.

c-FLIPR 25 kDa · human; the murine short isoform

DED1DED2R tail

Not made by the exon-7 splice decision that produces c-FLIPS, but by retention of intron 6, so whether a human makes c-FLIPS or c-FLIPR is fixed by genotype: the rs10190751 A allele yields c-FLIPR. Its anti-apoptotic activity is indistinguishable from c-FLIPS, though its protein level is generally lower, and that genotype has been associated with increased follicular lymphoma risk.

Explorer readout: identical to c-FLIPS, because the model gives the two short isoforms the same anti-apoptotic behaviour.

p43-FLIP 43 kDa · fragment of c-FLIPL

DED1DED2p20

Produced by caspase-8 cleavage of c-FLIPL at Asp376 (Asp377 in the mouse) at the DISC. It recruits TRAF2, activates IKK/NF-κB, raises ERK and caspase-8 activity and supports T-cell survival. This is the species whose behaviour the explorer's p43-FLIP trace reports.

Explorer readout at these settings: survival via the p43-FLIP/NF-κB branch — abundant c-FLIPL at low ligand saturates the DISC, blocks full caspase-8 processing and lets the c-FLIPL/procaspase-8 heterodimer be cleaved to p43-FLIP.

p22-FLIP 22 kDa · human only

DED1DED2stub

Cleaved at Asp196, between the DEDs and the caspase-like domain, out of c-FLIPL or c-FLIPS. It acts in death-receptor-independent signalling, complexing with FADD and RIPK1 and promoting RIPK1 ubiquitylation, which feeds IKK activation. Asp196 exists only in human c-FLIPL, so mice make no p22-FLIP.

No explorer input for this one: the model has no p22-FLIP species, so this route is described but not simulated.

Both sets make the short forms several-fold faster-turning-over than c-FLIPL (3× in the model, 6× in the review), which is the ordering the model depends on; the absolute values differ, and neither is a measurement.

How the isoform ratio is set

Alternative splicing of exon 7 decides whether translation continues into the caspase-like domain (c-FLIPL) or stops early, leaving the 19-residue tail (c-FLIPS); retention of intron 6 generates c-FLIPR. Because both outcomes are drawn from the same transcript pool, the decisive quantity is a ratio, and the strongest in vivo evidence comes from a strain difference rather than from a cell line: the wild-derived MSM mouse carries a 21-bp insertion in the 3′ UTR of exon 5 of Cflar that biases splicing toward c-FLIPL in hepatocytes, and the mice are resistant to Fas-receptor-mediated liver failure, with caspase-8 held in its partially processed p43 form instead of being matured to p20/p10. Upstream, CFLAR transcription is driven by NF-κB, NFAT and Sp1 and repressed by c-Myc and FOXO3a, so inflammatory signalling raises total c-FLIP and, depending on the splicing machinery, changes its composition.

What each isoform does once it reaches the DISC

c-FLIP and procaspase-8 do not compete for the same surface on FADD: c-FLIP binds the α1/α4 face and procaspase-8 the α2/α5 face of the FADD DED. c-FLIP therefore occupies sites efficiently despite being far scarcer than procaspase-8 in the cell, which is why site ratios — not bulk concentrations — decide the outcome. Long and short isoforms also act as checkpoints on DED filament assembly: they interrupt the caspase-8 filaments that a DED scaffold would otherwise build.

Read isoform by isoform, the picture is coherent. c-FLIPL at physiological levels is pro-apoptotic, because the heterodimer it forms with procaspase-8 is a better activator of the first cleavage step than procaspase-8 alone; at high local concentration it becomes anti-apoptotic, because it saturates the platform and prevents full caspase-8 processing, and its cleavage products then carry the NF-κB/ERK survival signal. c-FLIPS and c-FLIPR are one-directional: they block procaspase-8 activation and cannot cleave RIPK1, so they leave the RIPK1–RIPK3–MLKL axis unopposed. p43-FLIP turns the blocked complex into a signalling platform, and p22-FLIP does the same for RIPK1 in death-receptor-independent signalling.

Turnover and post-translational control

All isoforms are short-lived and degraded through the ubiquitin–proteasome system, which is what makes the ratio adjustable on a minutes-to-hours timescale. Lys192 and Lys195 are the critical ubiquitin acceptors that set c-FLIPS turnover; phosphorylation at Ser193, a site present in every isoform, reduces ubiquitylation and stabilises c-FLIPS and c-FLIPR; reactive oxygen species drive Thr166 phosphorylation and Lys167 ubiquitylation of c-FLIPL; S-nitrosylation at Cys254/Cys259 blocks its ubiquitylation; and the E3 ligases are context-dependent — Itch downstream of JNK1 for c-FLIPL, Cbl for c-FLIPS, with MIB1, TRAF7 and CHIP reported in other settings. The same sites that set degradation also sit on the NF-κB-facing surface: DED1 is the binding site for NEMO/IKKγ in all FLIP proteins.

Species differences that block a direct mouse-to-human reading

Mice have no c-FLIPS: the exon that produces it does not exist in the mouse genome, and the only murine short isoform is a c-FLIPR homologue with an overlapping but distinct C-terminus. Mouse c-FLIPL carries Asp377 where human c-FLIPL carries Asp376, so p43-FLIP exists in both species, but Asp196 is human-specific and p22-FLIP does not exist in mice at all. A result obtained in murine cells is therefore a statement about c-FLIPL plus a murine c-FLIPR, not about the human c-FLIPL/c-FLIPS/c-FLIPR triad.

What the explorer does and does not model

  • c-FLIPS and c-FLIPR share one slider because their anti-apoptotic activity is indistinguishable in the published comparison; their different abundances, splicing control and turnover are not represented.
  • p43-FLIP appears as a readout of c-FLIPL–procaspase-8 adjacency, not as an independent species with its own recruitment; p22-FLIP and its RIPK1-ubiquitylation route are absent entirely.
  • Both isoforms compete for the same FADD sites with a single on-rate hierarchy, so the model cannot express the distinct FADD DED surfaces (α1/α4 versus α2/α5) or the filament-checkpoint behaviour of the two isoform classes.
  • Nothing in the model sets the isoform ratio: splicing regulation — including the MSM-type allelic shift — and transcription of CFLAR are upstream of the sliders and outside their scope.

Legacy names for the same proteins are still in circulation and appear in the older literature: c-FLIP-α (≈55 kDa) corresponds to c-FLIPL and c-FLIP-δ (25–28 kDa) to the short forms; the protein has also been called CASH, CASP8AP1, CLARP, Casper, FLAME, FLIP, I-FLICE, MRIT and usurpin. Viral FLIP proteins (v-FLIP) are the paralogues used as a benchmark: MC159 of molluscum contagiosum carries tandem DEDs and blocks caspase-8, whereas v-FLIP/K13 of KSHV constitutively activates NF-κB and does not block Fas-induced apoptosis.

Assumptions

What the model encodes

DISC binding sites = 3 × active CD95 (DED proteins exceed FADD several-fold; Schleich et al. 2012). Active receptors = 40 000 × (CD95L / 1000 ng/ml), capped at 40 000 (Bentele et al. 2004 measured ≈40 000 CD95 per cell and located the threshold near a ligand:receptor ratio of 1:10²). The effective on-rate of c-FLIP at FADD is set 1000× above procaspase-8's, reproducing the fitted ordering of Neumann et al. 2010 (procaspase-8 has the lower DISC affinity) at a magnitude the published papers do not specify. c-FLIP bound to the DISC is degraded with its isoform half-life (120 min for c-FLIPL, 40 min for c-FLIPS/R).

Two artefacts are known and worth stating: at saturating ligand the model becomes procaspase-8-limited, so removing c-FLIP does not accelerate death there; and the 1000-fold on-rate advantage, which is what makes the threshold visible, is not a measured value.
