Neurodegeneration Compared — AD, PD, HD, and the ALS–FTD Spectrum

The major neurodegenerative diseases share an aging-linked mechanistic matrix — proteostasis collapse, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative and nitrative stress, innate immune activation, and prion-like spread of misfolded proteins — yet they differ in the initial condition: which protein misfolds, in which neuron, under which local chemistry. Those differences determine how each disease begins, how it propagates, and why the therapeutic breakthroughs of 2023–2026 (anti-amyloid antibodies in Alzheimer's, gene silencing in Huntington's and SOD1-ALS) succeeded only where the modality matched the target. This page synthesizes the llm-wiki-jk knowledge base — including quantitative analysis of its own wiki graph (3,007 nodes / 35,150 edges) — with the 2024–2026 clinical literature.

Overview

Four diseases, one matrix, four different initial conditions

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is the most common cause of dementia: a progressive amnestic syndrome defined by extracellular amyloid-β plaques and intracellular tau neurofibrillary tangles. Parkinson's disease (PD) is the most common movement disorder: loss of neuromelanin-rich dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra pars compacta with α-synuclein Lewy pathology. Huntington's disease (HD) is the only one of the four with a fully deterministic genetic cause — a CAG repeat expansion in HTT — producing chorea, psychiatric disturbance, and cognitive decline. Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and frontotemporal dementia (FTD) are best understood as one spectrum: TDP-43 proteinopathy with different anatomical epicenters, motor or behavioral.

All four are age-related, all four involve misfolded proteins, and all four ultimately destroy neurons through overlapping stress biology. What distinguishes them clinically — and therapeutically — is summarized in the tempo chart below: a decades-long silent phase in AD and PD, a genotype known from birth in HD, and an abruptly fast course in ALS.

Shared mechanisms

The convergent matrix: five failure modes, weighted differently

Across the knowledge base's notes, four mechanistic themes recur in every disease, with a fifth — cellular senescence — acting as the aging bridge. Each disease carries its own weighting of these axes, which is why no single "anti-neurodegeneration" therapy exists.

Disease profiles

What drives each disease

The grid below compresses each disease's core mechanism into its strongest wiki-grounded causal chain; the profiles that follow add the clinical and therapeutic detail.

Alzheimer's disease — the amyloid/tau (± TDP-43) dementia

AD begins in the medial temporal lobe and spreads limbic-to-association-cortex across roughly two decades of preclinical biomarker positivity. The wiki's redox account is distinctive: Aβ binds redox-active copper and iron, catalyses Fenton chemistry, and the resulting H₂O₂ oxidatively inactivates PP2A — the principal tau phosphatase — locking tau into its hyperphosphorylated, seed-competent state. Epigenetic drift (global hypomethylation, histone deacetylation, miRNA dysregulation) and sirtuin biology (SIRT1/SIRT3 protective, SIRT2 harmful) modulate the trajectory. TDP-43 proteinopathy of aging (LATE) is now recognised as a common co-pathology that mimics or compounds AD and predicts poorer anti-amyloid response†.

Status 2025–2026†: lecanemab (traditional FDA approval 2023; maintenance and subcutaneous dosing 2025) and donanemab (2024; ≈35% slowing on iADRS at 76 weeks) are approved for early symptomatic AD with confirmed amyloid; both carry ARIA risk, highest in APOE ε4 homozygotes. Plasma p-tau217 assays received FDA clearance in 2025, shifting diagnosis toward blood-based staging; anti-tau immunotherapies and MAPT-ASOs (e.g. diranersen) are in mid-stage trials.

Parkinson's disease — the self-poisoning dopamine neuron

PD selectively kills the neuromelanin-laden dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra pars compacta. The wiki's synthesis identifies three intersecting mechanisms: dopamine's intrinsic autoxidation to quinones that covalently modify α-synuclein; a 30–40% Complex I deficit (pharmacologically reproduced by MPTP and rotenone); and microglial peroxynitrite that nitrates α-synuclein, accelerating Lewy body formation. The pathogenic threshold of neuromelanin model explains the selectivity: the pigment buffers catechols and metals beneficially until a late-life ceiling, beyond which lysosomal and proteostatic capacity fail. A circulating SASP signature distinguishes PD patients, placing PD within senescence-associated neurodegeneration.

Status 2025–2026†: no disease-modifying therapy is approved. The pipeline is the broadest in neurology: prasinezumab (anti-α-synuclein) entered Phase 3 (PARAISO, ≈900 participants, late 2025) after Phase 2 slowing in biomarker-defined rapid progressors; ambroxol (GCase chaperone) is in Phase 3 (ASPro-PD, GBA1-enriched); brain-penetrant LRRK2 inhibitors advance with GPNMB as a lysosomal-stress pharmacodynamic marker; the α-synuclein seed amplification assay is qualified for trial enrichment. Dopaminergic replacement (levodopa, COMT and MAO-B inhibitors, deep-brain stimulation) remains symptomatic.

Huntington's disease — the deterministic repeat expansion

A CAG expansion beyond 36 repeats in exon 1 of HTT produces mutant huntingtin with a toxic gain of function; the genotype fully predicts disease, with onset inversely tracking repeat length. Striatal medium-spiny neurons of the indirect pathway degenerate first, driven by proteostatic overload (autophagy is the principal clearance route; TFEB/PGC-1α activation is protective) and by disruption of the CRTC1–CREB axis that supplies trophic BDNF from cortex to striatum. In the wiki's sirtuin layer, SIRT1 is strongly protective — brain-specific knockout worsens the phenotype — while SIRT2 inhibition is neuroprotective.

Status 2025–2026†: September 2025 brought the first positive disease-modifying pivotal result in HD: AMT-130, a one-time AAV5-microRNA therapy infused into the striatum, slowed decline on the composite cUHDRS by 75% at 36 months against a propensity-matched external control, with CSF neurofilament light below baseline. A biologics licence application was targeted for Q1 2026. Caveats: a small treated sample and an external-control comparison. Tominersen (HTT ASO) continues in a restricted trial after its Phase 3 failure; VMAT2 inhibitors remain the symptomatic standard for chorea.

ALS and the ALS–FTD spectrum — RNA-binding proteostasis failure

TDP-43, a nuclear RNA-binding protein, mislocalises to the cytoplasm in ≈97% of ALS and ≈45% of FTD, releasing cryptic exons in transcripts such as STMN2 and UNC13A that destabilise axons and synaptic transmission. C9orf72 repeat expansions — the most common genetic cause — add toxic RNA foci and dipeptide-repeat proteins on top of haploinsufficiency of a protein that itself regulates autophagy. Motor-neuron selectivity reflects local chemistry: weak astrocytic EAAT2 glutamate clearance permits excitotoxic calcium overload (NOX2, calpain), and SARM1-mediated NAD⁺ depletion executes axon destruction. Mitochondrial rupture engages cGAS–STING, making innate immunity a therapeutic axis.

Status 2025–2026†: four FDA-approved drugs exist — riluzole (1995), edaravone (2017), sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol (withdrawn April 2024 after Phase 3 failure), and tofersen (2023), the first precision neurodegenerative therapy: an intrathecal SOD1-ASO approved on biomarker grounds (plasma NfL −55%) and now tested presymptomatically in the ATLAS trial. The ASO wave extends to C9orf72 (allele-selective WVE-004), FUS (ION363), and — applicable in principle to ≈97% of ALS — ATXN2 and UNC13A targeting; masitinib entered a confirmatory Phase 3 in 2025. Plasma NfL is the field's validated pharmacodynamic surrogate.

Propagation

Routes of spread: templated misfolding along anatomical circuits

Each disease propagates its misfolded protein in a prion-like manner, but along different anatomical rails — a property first systematised by Braak staging and now mechanistically supported by seeding assays. The route determines the symptom sequence; the tempo determines how quickly it unfolds.

Therapeutic logic

Selective vulnerability and the rule that therapy follows target topology

The sharpest question in comparative neurodegeneration is why one neuron population dies while its neighbours survive, given that the responsible protein is usually widely expressed. The knowledge base's answer is local chemistry: the protein is the tinder, and the cell's local biochemistry is the spark. Dopamine neurons are poisoned by the neurotransmitter they carry; motor neurons are killed by their own excitatory drive once EAAT2-dependent glutamate clearance fails; striatal spiny neurons die of a transcriptional and trophic shortfall; hippocampal neurons succumb to the slow metal–redox/tau cascade seeded by Aβ.

Therapy succeeds only when the modality matches where the toxic species resides. Extracellular aggregates are antibody-accessible; intracellular, genetically defined toxins require nucleic-acid therapeutics; and where no dominant species exists, only shared-axis interventions remain. The 2023–2026 record follows this rule precisely†.

Why co-pathology dilutes anti-amyloid benefit

AD brains frequently carry TDP-43 (LATE), Lewy and vascular co-pathologies that progress independently of amyloid. Clearing Aβ therefore removes only one of several clocks. This is the mechanistic rationale behind amyloid-positive-but-decline-heterogeneous trial outcomes, and behind the emerging practice of staging patients with plasma p-tau217 and tau PET before treatment†.

Takeaways

Convergence and divergence

The one-sentence synthesis

Neurodegenerative diseases share a matrix — aging, proteostasis failure, mitochondrial and oxidative stress, innate immune activation — but differ in the initial condition, and therapy succeeds only when matched to that initial condition: extracellular aggregates receive antibodies; genetically defined intracellular toxins receive gene silencing; everything else receives shared-axis support.
