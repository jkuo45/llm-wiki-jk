Pleiotropic Roles of SIRTs in Tumor Cell Metabolism & Therapeutic Targeting

The seven mammalian sirtuins (SIRT1–SIRT7) are NAD+-dependent acyltransferases that act as a compartmentalized, context-dependent metabolic rheostat. The same enzyme can be tumor-suppressive or oncogenic depending on subcellular location, tumor type, stage, and NAD+ availability. This page turns the task-output synthesis into interactive charts of the opposing sirtuin behaviors.

Why the same enzyme can fight cancer and feed it

Pleiotropy stems from three interacting sources.

1 · Compartmentalization

Nuclear [[SIRT1]]/6/7 control transcription of metabolic programs.
Cytosolic [[SIRT2]] tunes cytoskeleton & cell cycle.
Mitochondrial [[SIRT3]]/4/5 set the metabolic setpoint (TCA, glutamine, redox). "SIRT output" is the integration of a mitochondrial setpoint and a nuclear transcriptional gate.

2 · Catalytic promiscuity

Beyond deacetylation: demalonylation, desuccinylation, deglutarylation, and ADP-ribosylation. [[SIRT5]] has almost no classical deacetylase activity (a desuccinylase/demalonylase/deglutarylase), and resists nicotinamide inhibition — staying active where SIRT1–3 are suppressed.

3 · Context-dependence

Same substrate, opposite sign: SIRT1 and SIRT3 both deacetylate [[p53]] yet with pro- vs anti-tumor consequences; SIRT7's H3K18 deacetylation both lifts p21 (pro-proliferative) and silences EMT genes (anti-metastatic). No sirtuin is purely "pro-" or "anti-" cancer.

7
NAD+-coupled isoforms — tumor metabolic state (Complex I, glutaminolysis, NAMPT/CD38) directly gates their output
3
Nuclear SIRT1/6/7 · cytosolic SIRT2 · mitochondrial SIRT3/4/5
0
Sirtuins that are purely anti-cancer — every isoform is context-dependent

SIRT by SIRT — dual faces

Every isoform carries a suppressor face and an oncogenic face. The balance depends on dose, stage, and genetic background.

[[SIRT1]]

the archetypal "double-edged sword" Nucleus
+ cytosol

Tumor-suppressive

  • Deacetylates [[p53]], [[Ku70]], p73, [[FOXO]] → favors repair over apoptosis under acute stress
  • hnRNP A1 deacetylation → suppresses glycolysis/growth in [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma|HCC]]
  • AR (Lys630) deacetylation → represses prostate growth
  • [[NF-κB]] p65 & HMGB1 suppression → anti-angiogenic in [[Ovarian Cancer|ovarian cancer]]
  • [[PAK1]] K420 deacetylation → hinders [[Glioblastoma]]

Oncogenic / pro-tumor

  • p53 deacetylation in chronic setting blunts the tumor-suppressor brake
  • Partners with [[c-Myc]] & [[HIF-1α]]
  • EMT/metadherin → chemoresistance in TNBC
  • cisplatin resistance (VEGF-A) in NSCLC; tamoxifen resistance (MRP2/FoxO1)
  • T-ALL via CDK2 deacetylation → p27 degradation
Context variables: dose ([[Colorectal Cancer|CRC]]: heterozygous KO → c-Myc/glutamine/proliferation; homozygous KO → apoptosis), tumor type (suppressor in glioma/HCC; promoter in gastric/endometrial/leukemia), severity (cardiac: 7.5× protective, 12.5× pathogenic).

[[SIRT2]]

epithelial suppressor, basal-like/leukemia promoter Cytosol

Tumor-suppressive

  • [[BRCA1]]–BARD1 heterodimerization → homologous recombination (breast)
  • IDH1 deacetylation → inhibits CRC liver metastasis
  • AKR1C1 + [[STAT3]] inhibition → suppresses NSCLC migration
  • Sensitizes ovarian cancer to cisplatin

Oncogenic / pro-tumor

  • Slug deacetylation → stabilization → basal-like breast invasion
  • Phosphoglycerate mutase K100 → NADPH flux → lung tumor growth
  • Endometrial cancer stemness / MEK-ERK signaling
  • LMO2 deacetylation → T-ALL growth/engraftment
Context: cytosolic sirtuin that often opposes the protective SIRT1/3/6 axis. Harmful in neurodegeneration ([[Alzheimer's Disease|AD]], [[Parkinson's Disease|PD]], [[Huntington's Disease|HD]]), protective in metabolic/renal/joint disease. In leukemia it splits: NAMPT/SIRT2 activation reduces AML proliferation, but SIRT2 inhibition suppresses T-ALL.

[[SIRT3]]

mitochondrial "guardian", double-edged oxidative phenotype Mitochondria

Tumor-suppressive (metabolic)

  • [[MnSOD]]/SOD2 + IDH2 activation → lowers ROS → destabilizes HIF-1α (anti-Warburg)
  • Stimulates pyruvate dehydrogenase → oxidative TCA flux
  • Twist downregulation → suppresses ovarian metastasis
  • GST-π1 downregulation → sensitizes HCC to sorafenib/chemo
  • Disrupts ERα–p53 interaction in breast

Oncogenic (same biology, flipped)

  • Suppresses ROS so well → radiore/chemoresistance (ATM–Chk2 in NSCLC)
  • Improves drug resistance in CRC (SOD2/PGC-1α)
  • p53 deacetylation (onco-route)
  • ACC1 upregulation → lipogenesis → cervical cancer invasion
  • Ku70–Bax stabilization → glioma viability
Core tension: low-ROS/intact-OXPHOS "guardian" phenotype prevents cancer initiation but keeps established tumors alive by preventing stress-induced death. Genuinely dual in NAFLD and [[Osteoarthritis]]. Elevated in node-positive [[Breast Cancer]] and OSCC.

[[SIRT4]]

most consistently tumor-suppressive — with a metabolic cost Mitochondria

Tumor-suppressive (dominant)

  • [[GDH]] mono-ADP-ribosylation → limits glutamine→α-KG flux → directly attacks glutamine addiction
  • Represses [[mTOR]] via amino-acid-stimulated insulin secretion
  • p16/p21 upregulation + CyclinB1/Cdc2/Cdc25c suppression → HCC apoptosis
  • Restores E-cadherin in CRC
  • Negatively regulates SIRT1 via glutamine-metabolism repression

Why context still matters

  • GDH inhibition → reduced amino-acid-stimulated insulin secretion
  • Suppresses hepatic fatty-acid oxidation (MTPα) → lipid accumulation
  • Only sirtuin with adverse cardiac effects (aggravates Ang II hypertrophy/fibrosis)
  • Metabolically "thrifty" — antitumor but costly
Loss is uniformly prognostic for poor survival in HCC, [[Gastric Cancer|gastric]], CRC, bladder, breast cancer. Therapeutic angle: preserve/induce as a glutamine-addiction brake.

[[SIRT5]]

deacylase with pro-tumor metabolic bias Mitochondria

Tumor-suppressive / contextual

  • Pancreatic cancer: high expression → favorable prognosis (loss → GOT1 acetylation → proliferation)
  • HCC: suppresses immune evasion / bile-acid-metabolism-driven HCC
  • SOD1/IDH2 desuccinylation → antioxidant defense

Pro-tumor metabolic reprogramming

  • Overexpressed in HCC, CRC, NSCLC → pushes glutaminolysis + glycolysis
  • LDHB desuccinylation → hyperactive autophagy → nutrient recycling
  • Deglutarylation-dependent glutaminolysis in CRC
  • cisplatin resistance in ovarian cancer via [[Nrf2]]/HO-1
  • HCC: promotes growth/metastasis via OXPHOS→glycolysis switch
Note: almost no classical deacetylase activity (desuccinylase/demalonylase/deglutarylase) and resists nicotinamide inhibition — stays active in the tumor microenvironment where SIRT1–3 are inhibited.

[[SIRT6]]

most consistently protective — dual only in lung/HCC Nucleus (heterochromatin)

Anti-cancer (dominant)

  • Corepresses HIF-1α → suppresses glycolytic enzymes → anti-Warburg
  • Represses [[Survivin|survivin]], c-MYC, SREBP1/2, [[ChREBP]] (anti-lipogenesis)
  • Coordinates CHD4 → DNA repair (DDR)
  • [[JAK2]]/STAT3 inhibition → HCC growth suppression
  • hnRNP A1 deacetylation → anti-glycolysis in HCC

Dual exceptions + chemoresistance

  • Lung: snail-dependent [[KLF4]] transrepression → [[EMT]]/metastasis
  • HCC: high SIRT6 correlates with chemoresistance
  • Suppresses apoptosis (survivin/HIF-1α) so effectively → chemoresistance in breast/prostate
  • The DDR/austerity program that prevents initiation later keeps established tumors alive in therapy
Bottom line: the most unambiguously anti-cancer sirtuin (anti-Warburg, pro-DDR). Its protective genome-stability program is a double-edged sword during therapy.

[[SIRT7]]

proliferative biosynthetic driver Nucleus → nucleolus

Anti-metastatic (contextual)

  • H3K18 deacetylation at EMT-related promoters → suppresses metastasis
  • Same H3K18 mark that lifts p21 (pro-proliferative) also silences EMT genes — sign flips by promoter context

Pro-tumor (most cancers)

  • RNA Pol I activation → ribosome biogenesis → growth capacity
  • p21/CDKN1A promoter H3K18 deacetylation → cell-cycle progression
  • Overexpressed in breast, thyroid, HCC
  • E-cadherin inhibition (EMT) → CRC invasion
  • Suppresses p53 in HCC
Biomarker note: expression rises with positive [[Leukemia]] treatment response and falls on relapse — monitoring utility rather than a fixed tumorigenic role. Undrugged target.

Dual role in different cancers

Sirtuins act as both tumor suppressors and oncogenes depending on tumor type, stage, and context. The same isoform can be up-regulated (often pro-tumor) in one cancer and down-regulated (loss-of-suppressor) in another.

Stage legend

Preclinical = cell/animal or research tool · Phase I/II = human trials (largely completed, no pivotal advancement) · Clinical trials (ongoing) = NAD+-precursor strategies · Approved = marketed for non-sirtuin indication.

Selectivity caveat

Early inhibitors (sirtinol, cambinol, tenovins, suramin) are dual/multi-isoform. Truly isoform-selective allosterics (EX-527→SIRT1, AGK2/SirReal2→SIRT2, MDL-800/801 & UBCS039→SIRT6) are recent and preclinical.

Context governs use

Activators help when the isoform is tumor-suppressive (SIRT6 in HCC, SIRT3 guardian) but harm when oncogenic (SIRT1 in gastric/endometrial, SIRT3 radioresistance, SIRT6 chemoresistance); inhibitors face the mirror risk.

Cross-cutting patterns & the bottom line

ROS and DNA repair are the fault line. SIRT3/SIRT6 protect against oxidative damage and fix DNA — this prevents tumor initiation but mediates therapy resistance in established disease (SIRT3 radioresistance, SIRT6 chemoresistance).
Substrate sharing with opposite sign. SIRT1/SIRT3 both deacetylate [[p53]] — SIRT1's repression is protumorigenic, SIRT3's in some models an "oncogene" route; SIRT2 deacetylates Slug (onco) but BRCA1 (suppressive) via entirely different complexes.
Compartment matters. The mitochondrial trio (SIRT3/4/5) governs metabolic state, nuclear SIRT1/6/7 govern the transcription of the same programs — cellular "SIRT output" is the integration of a mitochondrial setpoint and a nuclear transcriptional gate.
Dose and stage dominate. CRC SIRT1, cardiac SIRT1, and SIRT7's H3K18ac show the same principle: low/moderate activity is protective, high/chronically activated flips to pathology; early-stage loss favors initiation, late-stage loss sensitizes to therapy.
Caveat. Every sirtuin shows dual/context-dependent behavior — metabolic phenotype cannot be read from a single sirtuin in isolation. Pleiotropy is not random: it's the product of NAD+ availability, subcellular compartment, target-site selectivity, and tumor evolutionary stage. That is why NAD+ depletion is emerging as a synthetic-lethal lever in MYC-high / glutamine-dependent cancers, and why sirtuin modulation must be context-aware.
