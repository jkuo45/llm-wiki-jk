Hallmark Modules in the Human Interactome

A network-medicine graph analysis of aging: 2,358 longevity-associated genes (OpenGenes) mapped onto the human interactome (524,156 interactions · 18,223 proteins), 1,250 of them assigned to the 11 hallmarks of aging. The central graph result is that each hallmark's genes coalesce into a statistically significant connected subgraph — a hallmark module — and the 11 modules sit in one shared neighborhood, the longevity module. Drug repurposing (proximity + pAGE, the SHARP pipeline) is a downstream application of this topology, covered after the graph results.

Source Gross, Ehlert, Gladyshev, Loscalzo & Barabási, Nature Aging 2026 · Code/data: Longevity-module · sharp-aging

The graph and the gene sets

The substrate is a human protein–protein interactome assembled from 26 databases: binary Y2H (HI-Union, HINT, HIPPIE), kinase–substrate maps, AP–MS, structure-derived (3D/co-fractionation), signalling/regulatory (SignaLink, ENCODE, RAIN, RISE) and complex-derived (BioPlex, QUBIC, lncRNome) interactions — 524,156 edges among 18,223 proteins. Robustness was checked against the STRING network (18,506 proteins, 728,844 interactions) and an edge-permutation null.

Three points from Figure 1 carry through the whole analysis. First, the confidence skew: only 26 genes reach level 1 (evidence that modulating them extends mammalian lifespan), so any result that ignored confidence would rest mostly on weak associations — this is why proximity and pAGE are computed at five separate strata rather than once. Second, module sizes are unequal by an order of magnitude (89 down to 7 genes), which matters when comparing z-scores: a 7-gene module can be significant yet fragile to a single annotation change, whereas the 89-gene epigenetic module is marginal despite its size. Third, the 390 shared genes already show, before any network is drawn, that hallmarks are overlapping categories — TP53 sitting in 7 hallmarks is the extreme case of a hub that any single-hallmark drug will inevitably touch.

The hallmarks are not independent gene sets: 390 of 1,250 genes span multiple hallmarks, and 47 of the 55 hallmark pairs share genes significantly (Jaccard index). Highest overlap: genomic instability × cell senescence × telomere attrition. Lowest: extracellular-matrix changes (near-zero overlap — a topologically distinct module).

Modules

Hallmark modules: LCC significance

Figure 2 is the methods figure for everything that follows, and its most important detail is the dashed null. Without a degree-matched null, any large gene set would look clustered simply because hubs connect to everything; the paper's claim is precisely that the observed LCC exceeds what hub-biased randomness predicts. Note also what the figure does not show: it is a statement about induced subgraphs (do these genes connect to each other?), not about function — a module can be significant in Fig. 2 yet mechanistically diffuse in Fig. 3, as epigenetic alterations is. Separation and proximity (panel a) are then reused twice: between hallmark modules below, and later between drug targets and modules.

Overlap

Overlap and the longevity module

Two complementary graph measures place the modules relative to each other:

  • Separation S(A,B) = ⟨dA,B⟩ − (⟨dA,A⟩+⟨dB,B⟩)/2 — S < 0 means modules overlap (share neighborhood); S > 0 means topologically distinct. Most hallmark pairs overlap; ECM is positive against all others.
  • Proximity between module pairs — significant (z < −1.96) for most pairs except ECM.

Figure 3 turns the numbers above into geography. Read the per-module panels (a–k) for what each hallmark's core genes look like as a drawn subgraph — dense and hub-centric for genomic instability, sparse for ECM — and read panel l for the argument: when the 11 subgraphs are overlaid, high-membership black nodes bridge them, forming one connected region rather than 11 islands. That single region is the result the drug analysis later exploits: a drug proximal to one module is rarely far from the others, which is why multi-hallmark candidates such as bms-754807 and aspirin (six hallmarks) appear downstream. The flip side, visible in panel k, is that ECM only ever connects through its own 7 genes — it is the one module a longevity drug would have to target deliberately.

Consequence stated in the paper: from a network perspective, targeting "theories" (causal drivers) vs "hallmarks" (downstream processes) lands in the same neighborhood — the traditional why/how distinction collapses topologically.

Signed metric

pAGE as a signed perturbation metric

Undirected topology cannot say whether a perturbation is beneficial or harmful. pAGE adds sign over the same genes Λ (2,025 with known age direction):

  • Longevity vector Γ: σg = +1 if gene g goes up with age, −1 if down.
  • Perturbation Θ: σ′g ∈ {−1, 0, +1} from CMap expression after drug treatment.

pAGE = −(1/|Λ∅|) · Σ σi·σ′i, in [−1, +1]. The minus sign: matching the aging signature scores negative (age-accelerating); opposing it scores positive (pro-longevity).

Worked example: gene up with age (σ = +1), drug pushes it down (σ′ = −1) → σ·σ′ = −1 → contributes +1/|Λ∅| to pAGE (reversal). Gene up with age, drug pushes it up further → +1 → contributes −1/|Λ∅| (mimics aging). Unchanged (σ′ = 0) → excluded from numerator and denominator.

Figure 4 exists because topology alone is unsigned: a drug sitting next to a module in Fig. 3 could push it either way. The figure's color bookkeeping makes the Eq. (3) intuition concrete — each gene contributes a vote (σ·σ′), positive when the drug's change matches the aging change and negative when it opposes it, and the minus sign in the formula converts "matches aging" into a negative score. Two subtleties visible in the panels: unchanged genes (white) drop out of the denominator entirely, so pAGE measures direction among affected genes rather than overall transcriptional displacement; and the same gene can contribute to different hallmarks' pAGE with opposite module-level results, which is why lifespan drugs in the validation set still show some negative hallmark-level pAGE values.

Caveat: not every age-associated shift is harmful — stress-response activation can be adaptive (hormesis). Validation suggests this confounds pAGE only modestly.

Downstream application

Proximity and SHARP

Proximity scores every DrugBank compound's targets against each module:

P(S,T) = (1/|T|) · Σt∈T mins∈S d(s,t) — z < −1.96 vs 1,000 degree-matched random sets, at five OpenGenes confidence strata. Proximity is direction-blind: pimasertib and selisistat are proximal to the senescence module yet promote senescence. SHARP intersects proximity (can perturb?) with pAGE (which direction?).

Figure 6 is where the two scores meet, and it explains both the power and the error rate of the approach. The horizontal axis (proximity) alone would nominate many drugs: the grey band in panels a–b shows that most of the 1,346 DrugBank∩CMap compounds are not significantly close to a given module, so the upper-left quadrant is a small set — but it still contains age-accelerating compounds (negative pAGE), which is exactly the false-positive class measured at 42.8% in the ITP validation. Panels c–d decompose a single dot into its per-gene votes, showing that a near-zero pAGE usually means conflicting votes rather than no effect. The practical reading: proximity filters 6,442 → 370, pAGE then splits those 370 into the 21 / 23 / 16 groups in the text — and the 310 drugs without CMap data cannot be placed on this plot at all until they are profiled.

Output: 370 proximity-significant drugs; of 60 with CMap profiles, 21 positive pAGE (pro-longevity candidates), 23 negative (age-accelerating), 16 unclear. The other 310 lack CMap data; ~35% (~108) are extrapolated to be pro-longevity. 83 of 370 are network drugs — they perturb a module without directly targeting a hallmark gene, and would be missed by target-overlap methods (cf. 76/77 effective COVID-19 drugs in Gysi et al.).

Worked example

Oxymetazoline: perturbation traveling the graph

Predicted for altered intercellular communication. Targets ADRA1A/B/D and HTR1A/B/D; ADRA1A is itself a hallmark gene (level 1). The direct module neighbours (ALB, NR3C1, TGFB1) show weak perturbation, so the signal detours through ACKR3 — a non-hallmark neighbour — into NFKB1, TP53 and AKT1, counteracting age shifts in sterile-inflammation genes (CCL5, NFE2L2, AGER, RELA, CEBPB, C3, MIF, NFKBIA, HDAC4, PTGS2, HLA-DRB1). Result: pAGE = 0.46, z = 2.35 — a falsifiable gene-level prediction akin to a shortest-path claim that can be assayed.

Figure 8 is the paper's argument that the pipeline is interpretable rather than a black-box score: it decomposes one prediction into its four falsifiable parts. Panel a is a topology claim (targets ADRA/HTR, entry points ALB/NR3C1/TGFB1, and ADRA1A being both target and hallmark gene). Panel b is a propagation claim — the strongest perturbation neighbour is ACKR3, not a shortest-path neighbour, so any assay that only checked direct neighbours would miss the mechanism. Panel c is the reference aging signature, and panel d is the vote count (pAGE = 0.46, z = 2.35) with the specific genes listed in the text. Each panel can be tested independently in cells, which is the sense in which the authors call these predictions experimentally falsifiable.

Limits

Graph caveats

  • 11 vs 12 hallmarks: this ontology (OpenGenes, via Pun et al. 2022) has ECM changes but no standalone chronic inflammation or dysbiosis (folded into intercellular communication / not gene-curated). The canonical López-Otín 2023 framework has 12. Results are ontology-dependent.
  • Tissue blindness: pAGE uses mainly MCF7 (cancer) CMap profiles; aging differs by tissue. WI38/IMR90 checks show minimal variation, but tissue-specific interactomes (GTEx-filtered, single-cell) are the stated next step.
  • No dose / non-linearity / stage: pAGE treats ∂expression as ±1; benefit windows by age are unmodelled.
  • Coverage: 1,347 of 6,442 compounds have CMap profiles, so 310 of 370 candidates currently lack a pAGE value.
  • PPI bias: poorly studied proteins are under-connected; modules are partially shaped by research attention (STRING/permutation checks mitigate, not eliminate).
  • All results are computational predictions validated against published mouse/clinical lists; no new human efficacy data.
