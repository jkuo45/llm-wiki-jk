# Mitochondria-Lysosome Contact Site

## Overview

Mitochondria–lysosome (M-L) contact sites are close membrane appositions that permit exchange between the two organelles without fusion, autophagosome markers, or bulk cargo transfer. They are therefore distinct from [[Mitophagy]] and [[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles]]. The contacts function as hubs in which ion exchange, lipid transfer, redox homeostasis, and signaling converge to coordinate organelle behavior. They are enriched at mitochondrial fission sites and are remodeled in mitochondrial disease, lysosomal storage disease, cardiac injury, seizures, and aging.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> M-L contacts are RAB7-GTP-dependent, lack autophagosome markers, and show no bulk transfer of mitochondrial or lysosomal content; they are present at a substantial fraction of [[DRP1]]-positive mitochondrial fission sites, linking contact position to mitochondrial dynamics.

## Tethering and Untethering

Lysosomal GTP-bound [[Rab7]] binds an as-yet unidentified mitochondrial partner protein to tether the membranes. On the mitochondrial outer membrane, [[Fis1]] recruits [[TBC1D15]], a RAB7-specific GAP, which converts RAB7-GTP to RAB7-GDP and triggers untethering. The tether is therefore regulated by GTP hydrolysis rather than by vesicle formation. RAB7 mutations that impair GTP hydrolysis lengthen mitochondrial contacts in Charcot–Marie–Tooth type 2, whereas [[Parkin]] stabilizes active, GTP-bound RAB7 at contacts and thereby promotes amino-acid transfer.

A second tether system uses [[GDAP1]] on the mitochondrial side and [[LAMP1]] on the lysosomal membrane. GDAP1-dependent contacts behave as specialized redox-sensitive microdomains, raising the possibility that altered contact-site chemistry contributes to GDAP1-associated axonopathy independently of contact abundance. Whether the GDAP1–LAMP1 and RAB7-dependent interfaces are distinct contact populations, partially overlapping machineries, or different regulatory states of one interface remains unknown.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> [[Fis1]] recruits [[TBC1D15]] to M-L contacts to promote RAB7-GTP hydrolysis and untethering. [[GDAP1]] interacts with [[LAMP1]] to promote a distinct class of contacts, and GDAP1-dependent membranes constitute redox-sensitive microdomains; the relation between the two tether systems is unresolved.

## Ion and Metal Transfer

Ca²⁺ released from lysosomes through [[TRPML1]] is taken up by mitochondria at contacts: it crosses the outer membrane through VDAC1 and the inner membrane through [[Mitochondrial calcium uniporter|MCU]]. Disrupting this route alters mitochondrial Ca²⁺ homeostasis and quality control across disease models. Iron transfer in melanoma cells depends on [[BDH2]], which localizes to M-L contacts and synthesizes 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid, a siderophore that carries lysosomal Fe²⁺ into mitochondria. A separate route transmits lysosomal iron to mitochondria through TRPML1 and VDAC2/VDAC3; reduced efficiency of this route, or of BDH2-dependent transfer, increases ferroptosis vulnerability under stress.

## Amino Acids, Cholesterol, and Proton Transfer

M-L contacts have been implicated in amino-acid movement from lysosomes to mitochondria, a process promoted by [[Parkin]]-mediated stabilization of RAB7-GTP and disrupted in [[Parkinson's Disease]] models. Cholesterol export from lysosomes to mitochondria depends on [[STARD3]]: in NPC1-deficient cells, STARD3 is required both for contact formation and for mitochondrial cholesterol accumulation. TM4SF5 and the hexokinase-domain protein HKDC1, a [[TFEB]] target, have also been linked to sterol transfer at the interface, suggesting a role in sugar and cholesterol homeostasis that remains incompletely defined. Mitochondria additionally transfer protons to lysosomes at contacts, contributing to lysosomal acidification; this mechanism declines during senescence as contact abundance falls.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> In melanoma cells, [[BDH2]] at M-L contacts produces the siderophore 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid, which carries lysosomal Fe²⁺ into mitochondria and shapes [[Ferroptosis]] vulnerability across melanoma cell states. In NPC1-deficient cells, [[STARD3]] is required for M-L contacts and for mitochondrial cholesterol accumulation; lysosomal Fe²⁺ can also reach mitochondria via [[TRPML1]]–VDAC2/3.

## Coupling to Fission and Disease

Contact untethering is coupled to division: [[TBC1D15]] recruits [[DRP1]] to M-L sites to drive asymmetric mitochondrial fission and maintain organelle quality control. In acute myocardial infarction, contact distance decreases and duration increases; [[TBC1D15]] overexpression reverses these structural changes and improves cardiac systolic function. During seizures, contact duration increases, and TBC1D15 overexpression reduces mitochondrial Ca²⁺ overload and restores mitochondrial function, indicating a neuroprotective effect. In GBA1-mutant dopaminergic neurons, defective contact resolution produces excessive tethering that is rescued by TBC1D15 overexpression. Extreme contacts have also been described: NPC1-deficient cells show lysosomes sandwiched between enlarged mitochondria, and hypoxic cells show megamitochondria surrounding lysosomes; whether these represent numerous contacts over an extended area or a different contact type is unresolved.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> In acute myocardial infarction models, [[TBC1D15]] recruits [[DRP1]] to M-L contacts to drive asymmetrical mitochondrial fission; TBC1D15 overexpression normalizes contact distance and duration and improves cardiac function. In seizure models, increased contact duration is alleviated by TBC1D15 overexpression, limiting mitochondrial Ca²⁺ overload.

## Unresolved Questions

The mitochondrial partner of RAB7-GTP has not been identified. It is undetermined how acute and chronic mitochondrial, lysosomal, and metabolic stresses affect contact formation, stability, and dissociation. The fusion machinery that would explain how [[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles|MDV]] membranes enter the lysosomal membrane remains poorly defined, and whether MDV delivery routes are MIRO/DRP1-independent biogenesis pathways or downstream trafficking steps is unresolved.
