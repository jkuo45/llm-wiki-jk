# Mitochondria-Lysosome Crosstalk

## Overview

Mitochondria and lysosomes are coordinated through a network of communication pathways rather than functioning as autonomous organelles. Four modalities connect the compartments: selective vesicular trafficking, diffusible metabolic signals, intracellular signaling cascades, and membrane contact sites. Degradative communication carries mitochondrial cargo to lysosomes for hydrolysis; non-degradative communication is bidirectional and permits each organelle to modify the function of the other. Primary defects in one organelle produce secondary abnormalities in the other: lysosomal lipid storage diseases repress mitochondrial biogenesis, while mitochondrial myopathies such as progressive external ophthalmoplegia show reduced lysosomal autophagic capacity.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> The review defines a unified framework in which [[Mitophagy]], [[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles]], [[Membrane Contact Site|membrane contact sites]], reactive oxygen species, amino-acid and lysosomal Ca²⁺ signals, and the [[AMPK]]–[[mTORC1]]–[[TFEB]] network form one communication system; disruption of one or more pathways recurs in mitochondrial disease, lysosomal storage disease, neurodegeneration, cardiovascular disease, and aging.

## Degradative Communication

[[Mitophagy]] delivers whole mitochondria to lysosomes. In the PINK1–Parkin route, [[PINK1]] accumulates on the outer membrane of depolarized mitochondria and recruits [[Parkin]], which polyubiquitinates outer-membrane proteins; the cargo is enclosed in autophagosomes, fused with lysosomes, and hydrolyzed. Mitochondrial nucleic acids are not merely substrates: poorly methylated [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]] is sensed by lysosomal [[TLR9]], and mitochondrial RNA by TLR7/TLR8, with adapter recruitment through MYD88 and downstream [[NF-κB]]-dependent cytokine production. Mitophagy therefore transfers information as well as cargo.

[[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles|Mitochondria-derived vesicles]] (MDVs) provide a parallel, cargo-selective route. These ~70–100 nm carriers bud independently of the core fission and autophagy machinery and can deliver oxidized proteins to lysosomes without mitochondrial depolarization. Lysosomal delivery can compensate for loss of mitophagy, and MDVs carrying the fission factor [[MFF]] remodel damaged lysosomes rather than only supplying degradation substrate.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Mitophagic cargo is processed as information: mtDNA is poorly methylated and sensed by lysosomal [[TLR9]], mitochondrial RNA is sensed by TLR7/TLR8 (species-dependent for TLR8), and receptor dimerization recruits MYD88 to activate [[NF-κB]] and pro-inflammatory cytokine production. MDVs (approximately 70–100 nm) bud independently of the core fission and autophagy machinery, and lysosomal MDV delivery does not require depolarization.

## Non-Degradative Communication

Bidirectional signaling and membrane contacts operate independently of bulk degradation. Acute respiratory-chain inhibition triggers lysosomal biogenesis and engages [[AMPK]] and [[TFEB]], whereas chronic mitochondrial perturbations—mtDNA depletion, respiratory-chain malfunction, or defective dynamics—suppress both pathways, lower lysosomal PtdIns(3,5)P₂, reduce hydrolytic capacity, and compromise autophagic flux. Lysosomal nutrient sensing likewise signals to mitochondria: amino-acid activation of lysosomal [[mTORC1]] stimulates mitochondrial activity, and lysosomes support the mitochondrial unfolded protein response through mTORC1-dependent ATF4 phosphorylation.

At [[Mitochondria-Lysosome Contact Site|mitochondria–lysosome contact sites]], the lysosomal GTPase [[Rab7]] tethers the two membranes to an incompletely identified mitochondrial partner; [[Fis1]] on the outer membrane recruits [[TBC1D15]], a RAB7 GAP, to hydrolyze RAB7-GTP and dissolve the contact. These interfaces exchange Ca²⁺ released through [[TRPML1]], iron delivered by [[BDH2]]-generated 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid, amino acids, and cholesterol transported by [[STARD3]]. [[GDAP1]]–[[LAMP1]] contacts form a second, redox-sensitive tether system. Proton transfer from mitochondria contributes to lysosomal acidification.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Acute mitochondrial respiratory-chain inhibition triggers lysosomal biogenesis and activates [[AMPK]] and [[TFEB]], whereas chronic respiratory-chain deficiency, mtDNA loss, or impaired dynamics downregulate both pathways and reduce lysosomal hydrolytic activity. In yeast and mammalian cells, mitochondrial proton transfer at membrane contacts contributes to lysosomal acidification, a mechanism that declines in cellular senescence as contact number falls.

## Physiological Coordination

The outcome of signaling depends on stimulus duration. Acute mitochondrial stress activates adaptive lysosomal programs that restore homeostasis; persistent dysfunction progressively dismantles the same pathways and establishes self-reinforcing organelle damage. Communication with canonical lysosomes maintains metabolic homeostasis, stress adaptation, and quality control. The same molecular principles extend to [[Lysosome-Related Organelle|lysosome-related organelles]], where the outcome shifts toward biogenesis and maturation of cell-type-specific compartments, as in the [[Melanosome|melanosome]].

## Disease Associations

Disruption of the network is documented across disease categories. In Parkinson's disease, GBA1 deficiency prolongs mitochondria–lysosome tethering in dopaminergic models, while α-synuclein overexpression reduces contact formation and mitochondrial Ca²⁺ buffering. In Niemann–Pick type C, cholesterol accumulates in mitochondria through STARD3-dependent contacts. Cardiomyopathy in Danon disease follows LAMP2 loss and blocked autophagosome–lysosome fusion, whereas acute myocardial injury involves TBC1D15-dependent contact remodeling. Aging combines declining mitophagy, dysregulated AMPK–mTORC1–TFEB signaling, and loss of contact-mediated lysosomal acidification.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> In dopaminergic neuronal models of [[Parkinson's Disease]], GBA1 deficiency produces prolonged mitochondria–lysosome tethering and defective contact resolution, with mitochondrial dysfunction rescued by restoring dynamics through [[TBC1D15]] overexpression. Suppression of basal mitophagy is sufficient to drive mitochondrial dysfunction and senescence in cellular models; enhancing neuronal mitophagy delays systemic aging in Drosophila.

## Therapeutic Framing

The review argues that restoring communication between the organelles, rather than targeting either organelle in isolation, is the relevant therapeutic objective. Stimulating [[TRPML1]]-dependent Ca²⁺ release rescues lysosomal acidification and mitochondrial physiology in cellular and animal models of mitochondrial disease. Partial [[mTORC1]] inhibition benefits mouse models of Leigh syndrome and frataxin deficiency, but is not universal across mitochondrial diseases; AMPK activation appears beneficial in skeletal muscle and potentially adverse in neuronal tissue. An optimal AMPK–mTORC1 balance, rather than maximal inhibition or activation of either pathway, is the proposed target.

## Unresolved Questions

The molecular identity of the mitochondrial RAB7 partner is undetermined. Whether GDAP1–LAMP1 and RAB7-dependent contacts are distinct populations, partially overlapping machineries, or regulatory states of one interface is unknown. The machinery that fuses MDV membranes with lysosomes is poorly defined, and the effect of acute versus chronic stress on contact formation, stability, and dissociation remains to be established. The review also identifies tissue-specific in vivo communication programs and the transition from adaptive to maladaptive signaling as open problems.

## Connections

- [[Mitochondria]] — source of degradative cargo and of the proton transfer that supports lysosomal acidification; respiration status determines adaptive versus maladaptive signaling
- [[Lysosome]] — degradation endpoint and signaling hub that registers mitochondrial stress through Ca²⁺, nutrient sensing, and membrane integrity
- [[Mitochondria-Lysosome Contact Site]] — the physical platform for bidirectional ion, lipid, and metabolite exchange; tethered by RAB7-GTP and resolved by Fis1–TBC1D15
- [[Membrane Contact Site]] — the general inter-organelle apposition mechanism of which M-L contacts are one specialization
- [[Mitophagy]] — unidirectional delivery of whole mitochondria; also transmits mtDNA and RNA to lysosomal nucleic-acid sensors
- [[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles]] — selective cargo carriers that complement mitophagy and can remodel the recipient lysosome
- [[Lysosome-Related Organelle]] — extends the communication principles to melanosomes, lytic granules, and platelet dense granules
- [[TRPML1]] — lysosomal Ca²⁺ channel whose activation links oxidative stress to mitochondrial Ca²⁺ uptake and lysosomal recovery
- [[AMPK]] — activated by acute mitochondrial stress, suppressed in chronic disease, and coupled to TFEB and organelle biogenesis
- [[mTORC1]] — lysosomal nutrient sensor that stimulates mitochondrial activity and supports the mitochondrial unfolded protein response
- [[TFEB]] — transcriptional effector of the acute adaptive response that is downregulated in chronic mitochondrial dysfunction
- [[GDAP1]] — provides a redox-sensitive LAMP1-dependent contact population distinct from the RAB7 machinery
- [[STARD3]] — mediates cholesterol transfer into mitochondria at contacts, with pathological accumulation in NPC1 deficiency
- [[BDH2]] — generates the siderophore that carries lysosomal Fe²⁺ into mitochondria in melanoma cells
- [[Ferroptosis]] — iron transfer and mitochondrial oxidative stress determine vulnerability to lipid peroxidation
- [[Parkinson's Disease]] — exemplar of contact dynamics failure caused by GBA1 deficiency and α-synuclein overexpression
