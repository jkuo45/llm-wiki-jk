# FADD

**FADD** (Fas-associated via death domain) is a critical adaptor protein that bridges death receptors to initiator caspases in the [[Extrinsic Pathway]] of [[Apoptosis]].

## Function
FADD contains both a death domain (DD) and a death effector domain (DED).
- **DD binding**: The DD allows FADD to bind to activated death receptors like [[Fas]], [[TNFR1]] (via [[TRADD]]), [[DR4]], and [[DR5]].
- **DED binding**: The DED allows FADD to recruit pro-[[Caspase-8]], leading to the formation of the **Death-Inducing Signaling Complex** (DISC) and the subsequent activation of caspase-8.

## Role in Cancer
Downregulation of FADD is observed in several cancers, such as thyroid and lung carcinomas, contributing to resistance against death receptor-mediated apoptosis.

## Role in Erythrocyte Death
FADD is retained in mature erythrocytes, which keep the Fas/FasL → FADD → [[Caspase-8]] arm of the extrinsic pathway even though they have lost the mitochondrial machinery. In aged red cells [[Fas]] colocalizes with lipid-raft markers and oxidative stress drives Fas translocation into rafts, DISC assembly and caspase-8/-3 activation. Cigarette smoke extract triggers this cascade: [[p38 MAPK]] phosphorylation initiates Fas oligomerization and FADD recruitment, and erythrocytes from smokers show elevated p-p38 and assembled Fas-FADD complexes. In erythrocyte necroptosis, FADD instead assembles a RIPK1/FADD/caspase-8 necrosome in which caspase-8 activity *prevents* death — the mirror image of nucleated cells, and the reason caspase-8 acts as the fate switch between [[Eryptosis]] and [[Erythronecroptosis]].
