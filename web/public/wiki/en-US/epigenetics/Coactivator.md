# Coactivator

## Overview

A **coactivator** is a protein that binds DNA-bound transcription factors and amplifies their output without binding DNA itself. Coactivators are the mechanistic bridge between a signal — hormone, stress, nutrient state, or growth factor — and the chromatin that executes the response. Unlike coregulators that simply repress, coactivators typically carry enzymatic or scaffolding activity: histone acetyltransferase domains, methyltransferase domains, or the ability to recruit Mediator and basal transcription machinery. Because most coactivators are large multi-domain scaffolds with hundreds of interacting partners, they are a principal site where signalling pathways converge on [[Transcription]].

## Structure & Domains

The canonical coactivator families illustrate the modular logic. **p300/CBP** are ~240 kDa acetyltransferases with a catalytic SET domain, a bromodomain (see [[Bromodomain]]), and a C-terminal zinc-finger that binds phosphorylated CREB; they acetylate histones and non-histone proteins including p53 and NF-κB. **PCAF** is a paralogue with related bromodomain and acetyltransferase modules. The **SRC/p160 family** (SRC-1, SRC-2/GRIP1, SRC-3) are scaffolds with multiple LxxLL motifs that dock nuclear receptors and recruit p300/CBP, Mediator, and histone methyltransferases. **PGC-1α** is an atypical coactivator: it is not intrinsically enzymatic but recruits HATs, Mediator, and demethylases to promoters of mitochondrial and metabolic genes.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Ligand-dependent recruitment**: Nuclear receptors such as [[Estrogen Receptor]] and other members of the [[Nuclear Receptor]] superfamily switch on their cognate steroid or retinoid and recruit coactivators via LxxLL motifs, which in turn recruit p300/CBP and Mediator, acetylating histones and allowing enhancer–promoter looping and productive elongation.
- **Metabolic coactivation**: [[PGC-1α]] is induced by [[AMPK]] and [[NRF2]] in response to exercise, fasting, and cold, and it drives the transcriptional programme of [[Thermogenesis]] and [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] — including [[UCP1]] — by co-activating nuclear receptors and by recruiting chromatin remodellers to thermogenic gene promoters.
- **Signal-responsive acetylation**: Coactivator activity is gated by signalling-dependent phosphorylation and acetylation, and by the metabolic state of the cell via [[Acetyl-CoA]] availability and [[NAD+]] levels.
- **Negative control by sirtuins and HDACs**: The NAD+-dependent [[SIRT1]] deacetylates p300/CBP, suppressing their acetyltransferase activity and shutting off coactivator programmes; [[Sirtuins]] similarly deacetylate other coactivators such as PGC-1α and SRC-3. [[HDAC]] enzymes provide a second brake by removing the acetyl marks that coactivators install. Loss of the sirtuin brake is a well-described feature of many cancers and of ageing, and it converts coactivator hyperactivity into persistent transcriptional output.

## Physiological and Clinical Relevance

Coactivator dysregulation underlies multiple disease classes. In [[Cancer]], recurrent amplification and rearrangement of p300/CBP and of the SRC family, together with loss of sirtuin and HDAC brakes, sustains oncogenic transcription programmes; p300/CBP inhibition is an active therapeutic strategy. In metabolic disease and ageing, blunted PGC-1α coactivation contributes to mitochondrial decline and loss of [[Oxidative Phosphorylation]] capacity. In the immune system, coactivators such as SRC-3 amplify inflammatory cytokine transcription, linking them to [[Inflammation]] and to the output of nuclear factor κB.
