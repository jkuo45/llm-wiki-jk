# TNFR2

**TNFR2** (TNFRSF1B, CD120b) is the second member of the [[TNFR]] family, the counterpart of [[TNFR1]] (CD120a). Unlike TNFR1 it carries **no intracellular death domain**; its cytoplasmic tail recruits [[TRAF2]] and [[PI3K]] to activate NF-κB and Akt survival programmes, and it can amplify TNFR1 signalling by releasing surface TNFR1 into soluble form.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- TNF binds TNFR2 with higher affinity than TNFR1; the complex recruits TRAF2/TRAF3 and [[cIAPs]]/[[LUBAC]], driving canonical NF-κB (RelA/p52) signalling.
- Shedding of the TNFR2 ectodomain yields **soluble TNFR2**, which scavenges TNF-α and thereby modulates the ligand pool available to TNFR1 — the basis of etanercept pharmacology.
- TNFR2 can modulate the necrosome indirectly: because it controls TNF availability and NF-κB-dependent survival gene expression, its presence raises the apoptotic threshold in neighbouring cells. Direct necroptosis signalling through TNFR2 is weak and remains largely undefined compared with TNFR1.
- Its absence from mature [[Erythrocytes]] is one reason erythrocyte necroptosis is triggered by Fas/FasL, CD59 ligation, [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]], [[Ceramide]] and AGEs rather than by TNF-family death receptors.

## Clinical Relevance

TNFR2 signalling is a target in autoimmune and inflammatory disease (etanercept, anti-TNF biologics) and in cancer immunotherapy, where agonism can support CD8+ T-cell survival. It is also implicated in regulatory T-cell function and in adipose-tissue inflammation.
