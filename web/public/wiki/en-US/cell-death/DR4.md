# DR4

**DR4** (TRAIL-R1, TRAILRS1, TNFRSF10A, CD261) is one of the two functional death receptors for [[TRAIL]] (the other being [[DR5]]). It is a type I transmembrane protein of the TNF-receptor superfamily whose cytoplasmic **death domain** makes it a canonical death receptor.

## Structure & Activation

Extracellular cysteine-rich domains bind the trimeric TRAIL ligand; binding clusters the receptor and recruits [[FADD]] and procaspase-8 to form the death-inducing signalling complex (DISC), also known as Complex II in [[TNFR1 complex II|death-complex]] nomenclature.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Extrinsic apoptosis:** DISC assembly activates [[Caspase-8]], which directly executes via [[Caspase-3]]/[[Caspase-7]] or amplifies through [[Bid]] and the mitochondrial loop.
- **Necroptosis:** when caspase-8 is inhibited or deleted, the same receptor complex recruits [[RIPK1]] and [[RIPK3]] to form the [[necrosome]], and RIPK3 phosphorylates [[MLKL]] — the switch that converts apoptosis into necroptosis.
- **Decoy competition:** DR4 also binds soluble TRAIL bound to the decoy receptors DcR1 and DcR2, which lack functional death domains and blunt signalling on normal cells.
- **Resistance:** decoy-receptor overexpression, FLIP, XIAP, and SHP1-mediated dephosphorylation of caspase-8 all reduce DR4-driven apoptosis; agonistic antibodies against DR4/DR5 have been developed as cancer therapeutics.

## Erythrocytes

Mature [[Erythrocytes]] lack DR4 (and DR5, [[TNFR1]], [[TNFR2]]), so TRAIL cannot initiate their death; erythrocyte necroptosis ([[Erythronecroptosis]]) is instead triggered through Fas/FasL, CD59 ligation, ROS and ceramide.
