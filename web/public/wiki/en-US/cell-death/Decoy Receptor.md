# Decoy Receptor

A **decoy receptor** is a receptor that binds its ligand with high affinity but lacks the intracellular signalling machinery — typically a functional death domain — required to launch a death programme. Ligand capture by the decoy is therefore "wasted": it down-tunes the effective agonist concentration available to signalling receptors on the same cell or on neighbouring cells.

## Mechanism

- **TRAIL system:** [[DcR1]] (TRAIL-R3) and [[DcR2]] (TRAIL-R4) bind [[TRAIL]] but lack a death domain; they compete with [[DR4]] and [[DR5]]. Normal cells express appreciable decoy-receptor levels, which is a major reason TRAIL spares healthy tissue.
- **RANKL system:** [[Osteoprotegerin]] acts as a soluble decoy for RANKL, restricting osteoclastogenesis and bone resorption; the RANK/RANKL/OPG axis is the same logic in soluble form.
- **Tumour resistance:** decoy-receptor overexpression (and soluble decoy shedding) is one of the classic mechanisms of TRAIL resistance in tumours, alongside [[c-FLIP]], [[XIAP]] and caspase-8 silencing.
- **Therapeutic implication:** agonistic death-receptor antibodies (mapatumumab, conatumumab) are combined with decoy blockade or with IAP antagonists ([[Smac mimetics]]) to restore killing.

## Clinical Relevance

Decoy-receptor biology explains much of the therapeutic window of death-receptor agonism, and it is a target itself: neutralising decoys (or restoring the signalling/balance ratio) sensitises resistant tumours while risking on-target inflammation in normal tissue.
