# DcR2

## Overview

**DcR2** (decoy receptor 2), also known as TRAIL-R4, DCR2, or TNFRSF10D, is a TNF-superfamily receptor that binds [[TRAIL]] but is structurally incapable of assembling a death-inducing signalling complex. Like [[DcR1]], it is a membrane-anchored receptor whose cytoplasmic tail is short and lacks a functional death domain; it therefore acts as a molecular decoy that lowers the effective concentration of free TRAIL available to the signalling receptors [[DR4]] and [[DR5]]. DcR2 is functionally distinct from DcR1 in that it can also be shed from the membrane as a soluble form and, unlike DcR1, it can form mixed complexes with DR4/DR5 on the cell surface.

## Structure & Domains

The *TNFRSF10D* gene encodes a type I membrane glycoprotein of about 210 amino acids. Its extracellular region contains two cysteine-rich domains that bind TRAIL with an affinity broadly comparable to that of DR4/DR5. The cytoplasmic portion is truncated and contains only a single cysteine and no death domain, which is the structural basis of the decoy function. Metalloproteinase-mediated shedding releases a soluble DcR2 fragment that circulates and buffers TRAIL systemically.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Ligand sequestration and heteromerisation**: DcR2 binds TRAIL trimers, and can recruit them to DR4/DR5-containing complexes that fail to productively recruit [[FADD]] and pro-[[Caspase-8]], blunting DISC formation.
- **Local modulation of apoptotic threshold**: By lowering the effective ligand, DcR2 shifts the response from apoptosis toward survival, particularly in cells with marginal receptor numbers.
- **Soluble buffering**: Shed DcR2 in the circulation limits the bioavailability of therapeutic TRAIL and can blunt responses systemically.

> [!info] DcR1 versus DcR2
> Both are TRAIL decoys, but they differ in expression and in trafficking: DcR1 is prominent in liver, lung and haematopoietic tissue and is mostly GPI-anchored or soluble; DcR2 is often co-expressed with DcR1, is readily shed, and in some settings forms signalling-incompetent heterotrimers with DR4/DR5 on the same cell.

## Physiological Function

DcR2 helps protect normal tissues from excessive TRAIL signalling and shapes the threshold for receptor-mediated killing in immune and parenchymal cells. Its expression pattern is dynamic and is modulated by cytokines and by cellular stress, and circulating soluble DcR2 has been proposed as a biomarker of TRAIL-pathway activity.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Tumour resistance**: DcR2 over-expression is documented in a range of solid tumours and haematological malignancies and correlates with poor response to TRAIL agonists.
- **Therapeutic strategy**: Combining a TRAIL agonist — recombinant ligand or a DR4/DR5-targeting antibody such as mapatumumab or conatumumab — with an antibody that neutralises DcR1/DcR2, or with an agent that removes decoy receptors from the surface, is a rational way to widen the therapeutic window.
- **Inflammatory context**: Because TRAIL/TDcR2 signalling is also implicated in cytokine release and endothelial activation, decoy-receptor status influences the toxicity profile of systemic TRAIL administration.
