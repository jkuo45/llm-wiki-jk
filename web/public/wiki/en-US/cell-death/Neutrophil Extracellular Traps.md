# Neutrophil Extracellular Traps

**Neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs)** are webs of neutrophil-derived DNA coated with [[Histone|histones]] and antimicrobial proteins that entrap microbes. They are the physical output of [[NETosis]], a program distinct from [[Apoptosis]] and [[Necroptosis]]. [[Neutrophils]] cast NETs when they encounter microbes too large for [[Phagocytosis]] or microbes that have escaped the phagosome into the cytosol, so NETs function as a barrier to pathogen dissemination when phagocytosis and degranulation are insufficient (Burgener & Schroder 2020).

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> NETs are extruded by three pathways — [[Suicidal NETosis]], [[Noncanonical NETosis]], and [[Vital NETosis]] — that differ in trigger, machinery, and whether the neutrophil survives. All three converge on chromatin decondensation and extrusion of a DNA scaffold decorated with a conserved set of nuclear, granular, and cytoplasmic proteins.

## Composition and structure

The NET scaffold is decondensed [[Chromatin]] — extracellular DNA plus core histones H2A, H2B, H3, and H4 — released after [[Nuclear Envelope]] disassembly. The DNA is decorated with a conserved, not exhaustive, set of proteins:

- **Granule effectors**: [[Neutrophil Elastase]] (NE), [[Myeloperoxidase]] (MPO), cathepsin G (CatG), proteinase-3 (PR-3), lactotransferrin, azurocidin, lysozyme C, and α-defensins. The granule proteases reportedly remain enzymatically active on NETs even in the presence of endogenous protease inhibitors.
- **Cytoplasmic and cytoskeletal proteins**: S100 calcium-binding proteins, [[Actin]], [[Myosin]], and cytokeratin.
- **Stimulus-dependent composition**: proteomics identified 24 proteins in [[PMA]]-induced NETs and 80 proteins in NETs induced by nonmucoid and mucoid *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* strains, so the eliciting stimulus influences NET cargo (Urban et al. 2009; O'Donoghue et al. 2013; Dwyer et al. 2014).

## Morphological sequence of NETosis

NETosis proceeds through distinct, sequential changes in the neutrophil (Fuchs et al. 2007; Yipp and Kubes 2013):

1. Loss of the lobulated nuclear architecture.
2. Permeabilization of nuclear and granular membranes.
3. Histone inactivation by clipping and/or citrullination, driving chromatin expansion into the cytosol.
4. Mixing of chromatin with granule content.
5. Disappearance of cytosolic organelles through loss of internal membranes.
6. Plasma-membrane permeabilization and NET release into the extracellular space.

## Antimicrobial functions

NETs contribute to host defense by three mechanisms:

- **Physical entrapment** that prevents microbial dissemination to secondary infection sites. Intravital microscopy captured circulating *E. coli* in NETs within hepatic sinusoids (McDonald et al. 2012).
- **Direct antimicrobial cargo**: histones, NE (which cleaves bacterial outer-membrane proteins and virulence factors of enterobacteria), MPO (which generates [[Hypochlorous Acid]] from [[Hydrogen Peroxide]]), and GSDMD-p30 pores that target bacterial membranes.
- **Immune recruitment**: GSDMD-driven [[NLRP3]] activation and [[IL-1β]] secretion recruit and activate newly arriving neutrophils that kill NET-entrapped bacteria (Chen et al. 2018; Monteleone et al. 2018).

> [!warning]
> Source: [[_document_ - Neutrophil Extracellular Traps in Host Defense]]
> Whether NETs directly kill microbes remains debated; reported efficiency varies by microbial species. DNase I dismantling of NETs at a primary infection site promoted bacterial dissemination in wild-type but not *Casp11*- or *Gsdmd*-deficient mice, supporting a host-defense role for the NET structure itself (Chen et al. 2018).

## Microbial evasion and pathological roles

Microbes counter NETs by blocking upstream signaling, secreting DNases that degrade the DNA web, or resisting NET-bound antimicrobial proteins. Examples include *Bordetella pertussis* adenylate cyclase toxin (suppresses the [[ERK2|ERK]]-dependent oxidative burst), group A *Streptococcus* nucleases and Scl-1 (suppresses MPO release), and *Staphylococcus aureus* protein A and Eap. Beyond infection, NETs are implicated in thrombosis, [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], [[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]], and autoimmune vasculitis; whether distinct NETosis pathways produce NETs with different immunomodulatory properties remains untested (Burgener & Schroder 2020).

## Connections

- [[NETosis]] — ordered morphological program whose output is the NET web.
- [[Neutrophils]] — sole source of NET DNA and granule cargo; size-sensing determines whether NETs are deployed.
- [[Suicidal NETosis]] — ROS/MPO/NE/GSDMD-driven lytic route; NETs released on plasma-membrane rupture.
- [[Noncanonical NETosis]] — caspase-4/11/GSDMD-driven lytic route triggered by cytosolic [[Gram-Negative Bacteria]].
- [[Vital NETosis]] — PAD4-dependent, vesicular route that externalizes chromatin without killing the neutrophil.
- [[Neutrophil Elastase]] — dominant non-histone NET protease; clips histones, degrades F-actin, and cleaves [[Gasdermin D]].
- [[Myeloperoxidase]] — NET effector that generates hypochlorous acid and, with NE, forms the azurosome complex.
- [[Gasdermin D]] — GSDMD-p30 pores permeabilize granule, nuclear, and plasma membranes and directly target bacterial membranes.
- [[Histone]] — principal antimicrobial effector of the DNA web; inactivated by citrullination or NE clipping.
- [[NLRP3]] — GSDMD-driven inflammasome activation on NETs licenses IL-1β release and neutrophil recruitment.
- [[Phagocytosis]] — phagosome formation is the checkpoint that suppresses NETosis for small microbes.
