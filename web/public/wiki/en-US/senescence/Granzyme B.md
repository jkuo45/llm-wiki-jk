# Granzyme B

**Granzyme B** (encoded by *GZMB*) is a serine protease stored in the cytotoxic granules of [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]] and [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]]. It enters target cells through [[Perforin]] pores and cleaves aspartate-containing substrates, activating caspase-dependent and caspase-independent apoptosis (including Bid-mediated mitochondrial amplification). Along with perforin, it is the canonical effector of cytotoxic lymphocyte killing.

## Loss in senescent CD8+ T cells

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> Transcriptomic profiling of high-senescence-score CD8+ T cells from UC patients shows lower expression of the canonical cytotoxic molecules GZMB and PRF1, together with upregulated pro-inflammatory mediators (IFNG, TNF, CCL3, CCL4) and senescence genes. Bulk RNA-seq of FK866-treated CD8+ T cells likewise shows downregulated Gzmb and Prf1 alongside upregulated Cdkn2a, Cdkn1a and Klrg1.

GZMB downregulation is part of the cytotoxic-to-secretory switch in [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|senescent CD8+ T cells]]: reduced killing capacity coexists with an antigen-independent [[SASP]]. This distinguishes the senescent state from [[T Cell Exhaustion|exhaustion]] primarily by checkpoint-marker profiling and by the retained, heightened pro-inflammatory output in [[Ulcerative Colitis]].
