# FK866

**FK866** (also APO866 / daporinad) is a small-molecule inhibitor of [[NAMPT]] (nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase), the rate-limiting enzyme of the [[NAD+ Salvage Pathway]]. By blocking NAD+ biosynthesis it lowers intracellular and mitochondrial [[NAD+]] pools (Ye et al., 2026). It is used experimentally to model the [[NAD+ Metabolic Reprogramming|NAD+ metabolic dysregulation]] observed in [[Ulcerative Colitis]].

## Use in senescence induction

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> Primary murine [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]] treated with FK866 (100 nM, 72 h) show downregulated intracellular NAD+, an expanded SA-β-gal+ population, upregulated [[p16]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21]], severely impaired proliferative capacity (CFSE/EdU), accelerated [[Telomere|telomere]] attrition, and a transcriptomic shift toward terminal differentiation (KLRG1 up; CD27/CD28, GZMB, PRF1 down) with high TNF/IFNG/CCL3/CCL4.

[[NMN]] supplementation prior to FK866 exposure largely abrogates the induced senescence, and siRNA-mediated NAMPT knockdown reproduces the phenotype, confirming that NAD+ depletion — rather than an off-target effect — drives the senescent program. The authors selected FK866 over [[SRT1720]] (a SIRT1 agonist with pleiotropic effects) to restrict NAD+ availability specifically.

FK866-treated (senescent) CD8+ T cells adoptively transferred into DSS-colitic mice accelerate disease, establishing a direct pathogenic role for NAD+-depleted senescent T cells. Mechanistically, FK866 activates [[cGAS-STING Pathway]] signaling (upregulated cGAS/[[IRF3]], increased p-[[STING]]/p-IRF3) downstream of [[mtDNA]] leakage; the STING inhibitor [[C178]] rescues and [[STING]]-knockout abolishes FK866-induced senescence.

## Parallel NAD+-depleting intervention: SRT1720

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> To test whether intracellular NAD+ depletion promotes CD8+ T-cell senescence in vitro, primary murine [[CD8 T cells|CD8+ T cells]] were treated with [[FK866]] (an inhibitor of the NAD+ salvage enzyme [[NAMPT]]) or [[SRT1720]] (an agonist of the NAD+-consuming deacetylase [[SIRT1]]). Both pharmacological interventions effectively downregulated intracellular [[NAD+]] levels and significantly expanded the SA-β-gal+ senescent population, indicating that lowering intracellular NAD+ is sufficient to induce CD8+ T-cell senescence (Figure 4f,g). Given the pleiotropic cellular effects of SIRT1 (Shen et al., 2024), the authors focused on NAMPT inhibition via FK866 for subsequent experiments to specifically restrict NAD+ availability.

The paired design establishes that the senescent phenotype tracks NAD+ availability rather than one enzyme's off-target action: FK866 blocks NAD+ *production* at NAMPT, whereas SRT1720 increases NAD+ *consumption* by activating the NAD+-dependent deacetylase [[SIRT1]]. Convergent depletion from opposite directions, together with [[NMN]] rescue and NAMPT siRNA knockdown, supports a causal metabolic etiology for [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|CD8+ T-cell senescence]] in [[Ulcerative Colitis]]. See [[SRT1720]] for the compound's broader sirtuin-activator pharmacology.

## Pharmacological context

FK866 belongs to the same NAD+-lowering strategy as NAMPT knockdown and SIRT1 activation; its effect is reversible by NAD+ precursors (NMN), which distinguishes a metabolic, NAD+-dependent senescence pathway from genotoxic or replicative senescence inducers.
