# KLRG1

**KLRG1** (killer cell lectin-like receptor subfamily G member 1) is a type-II transmembrane C-type lectin-like receptor expressed on differentiated [[T Cell|T cells]] and [[Natural Killer Cells|NK cells]]. It binds [[E-cadherin]] and transduces an inhibitory signal; surface expression rises with cumulative antigen-driven differentiation, so KLRG1 is widely used as a marker of terminally differentiated, senescent or end-stage T cells.

## Role in CD8+ T-cell senescence

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> Colitic mice show increased proportions of CD27-KLRG1+ CD4+ and CD8+ T cells in the intestine. High-senescence-score (HSS) CD8+ T cells from UC patients upregulate KLRG1 alongside [[p16]] and [[p21 CIP1|p21]], while downregulating cytotoxic molecules. FK866-induced senescent CD8+ T cells upregulate KLRG1 and the terminal-differentiation program while losing the co-stimulatory molecules [[CD27]] and CD28.

The CD27-KLRG1+ phenotype is used operationally to identify [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|senescent CD8+ T cells]] in [[Ulcerative Colitis]]: the frequency of KLRG1+CD27- CD8+ T cells increases after [[Adoptive Transfer|adoptive transfer]] of NAD+-depleted senescent cells and decreases after [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic therapy]]. Because KLRG1 also marks terminal differentiation adjacent to [[T Cell Exhaustion|exhaustion]], it is interpreted together with checkpoint markers rather than alone.
