# T Cell Exhaustion

**T cell exhaustion** is a state of T-cell dysfunction arising from chronic antigenic stimulation and inflammation, characterized by upregulation of inhibitory checkpoints ([[PD-1]], [[CTLA-4]], [[LAG-3]], HAVCR2/TIM-3), progressive loss of effector function, and a distinct transcriptional program (Zhao et al. 2020; Slaets et al. 2024). It is an adaptive, hypofunctional state and, unlike cellular senescence, is in principle reversible by checkpoint blockade.

## Distinction from senescence in ulcerative colitis

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Aging Cell - 2026 - Ye - NAD  Metabolic Reprogramming Drives CD8  T Cells Senescence and Exacerbates Ulcerative Colitis|Ye et al., 2026, Aging Cell]]
> Ye et al. explicitly distinguish the senescent CD8+ T cells driving UC from canonical exhaustion: classical exhaustion checkpoint markers (PDCD1, [[CTLA-4]], LAG3, HAVCR2) show no differential expression between high- and low-senescence-score CD8+ T cells, and FK866-induced senescent cells do not upregulate [[PD-1]]. Instead these cells exhibit irreversible arrest with [[p16]], [[p21 CIP1|p21]], [[KLRG1]] and a robust [[SASP]].

Where exhaustion is a hypofunctional, checkpoint-driven state, [[Senescent CD8+ T Cells|senescent CD8+ T cells]] are highly bioactive and tissue-destructive: they combine irreversible cell-cycle arrest with an antigen-independent pro-inflammatory [[SASP]] (IFNG, TNF, CCL3, CCL4) while losing cytotoxic molecules (GZMB, PRF1). The distinction has therapeutic implications: while checkpoint blockade targets exhaustion in cancer, the authors argue that [[Senolytic Therapy|senolytic clearance]] of senescent cells is the more logical approach for chronic inflammatory and autoimmune disease such as [[Ulcerative Colitis]].

## Relevance to the senescence knowledge base

The exhaustion/senescence boundary is used in the vault to keep the [[cGAS-STING Pathway]]– and [[NAD+ Metabolic Reprogramming|NAD+]]-driven senescent T-cell program separate from checkpoint-biology nodes such as [[PD-1]]/[[PD-L1]], [[CTLA-4]], and [[HLA-E]]–[[NKG2A]] immune-evasion signaling.
