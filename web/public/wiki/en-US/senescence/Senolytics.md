# Senolytics

**Senolytics** are a class of small molecules and peptides that selectively induce death in [[Senescent Cells]] by targeting [[Senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways|SCAPs]]. The conceptual breakthrough (Kirkland, Tchkonia, Zhu and colleagues) was that senescent cells are not merely arrested but are actively kept alive by upregulated pro-survival pathways; transiently disabling those pathways tips them into [[Apoptosis]] while sparing healthy cells (Zhu et al., Nat Med 2015; PMID 25594185).

## Mechanism of Action & Signaling

- **SCAP disruption**: Senolytics neutralize anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family members and survival kinases, permitting [[BAX]]/[[BAK]]-driven [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]] and [[Caspase-3]] execution.
- **Heterogeneity of targets**: Different senolytic classes exploit different dominant SCAPs, underlying cell-type selectivity.

## Key Senolytics

- **[[Dasatinib]] + [[Quercetin]] (D+Q)**: First-in-class combination; dasatinib inhibits tyrosine kinases, quercetin inhibits BCL-2 family proteins and PI3K. Shown to clear senescent cells in vitro, in aged mice, and in human trials.
- **[[Navitoclax]] (ABT-263)**: Potent BCL-2/BCL-XL inhibitor; highly effective but limited by thrombocytopenia from platelet BCL-XL dependence.
- **ABT-737**: Precursor BH3-mimetic that inhibits BCL-2/BCL-XL/BCL-W.
- **[[Fisetin]]**: Naturally occurring flavonoid with broad senolytic activity across tissues.
- **Piperlongumine**: Alkylating-agent-derived senolytic engaging oxidative-stress–related SCAPs.
- **FOXO4-DRI**: Cell-penetrating peptide that disrupts the FOXO4–p53 interaction, selectively inducing apoptosis in senescent cells.

## Role in Senescence

- Senolytics convert a chronic, SASP-producing cell into an eliminated one, reducing the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] burden and [[Inflammaging]].
- They address the driver (the cell) rather than only the effector (the secretome), complementing [[Senomorphic Therapy]].
- Selectivity depends on the SCAP composition of the target cell, motivating biomarker-guided, tissue-specific regimens.

## Sex Differences in Efficacy

Senolytic outcomes are **strikingly sex- and age-dependent**, and no single agent is uniformly beneficial across both sexes:

| Model | Finding | Citation |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **C57BL/6, early-life dosing (4–13 mo)** | Fisetin beneficial in males (↓SASP, ↑glucose/energy metabolism, ↑cognition); D+Q **detrimental in females** (↑WAT, ↑SASP, ↓cognition); D+Q negligible in males | Guo et al., *GeroScience* 2023 |
| **C57BL/6, HCC model** | D+Q at a dose beneficial in females **worsened HCC in males** | Cited in Guo 2023 / *GeroScience* 2024 |
| **Tauopathy (rTg4510) head-to-head** | Both D+Q and fisetin enhanced female cognitive performance (y-maze); fisetin affected running speed in males only; D+Q uniquely increased body weight/length in females | Garbarino et al., *Alzheimers Dement* 2020 |
| **Aged F344 rats** | Senolytics preserved cognition in males but failed to rescue memory in older females (estradiol depletion largely independent of senescence) | *Int J Mol Sci* 2025 review |
| **[[Navitoclax]] (ABT-263)** | **No sex-stratified data exist** — an open translational gap | — |

### Mechanistic Interpretation

- Early-life continuous clearance can be **counterproductive in females** because their sex-hormonal anti-inflammatory/anti-aging systems already suppress SASP burden; removing senescent cells prematurely alters tissue-replacement/regenerative dynamics (Guo et al., 2023).
- **Hormonal interplay:** quercetin in the D+Q cocktail may lower estrogen (via thyroid-peroxidase inhibition), predisposing estrogen-dependent females to metabolic dysfunction — a proposed mechanism for the female-detrimental D+Q phenotype.
- Sex-specific glucocorticoid sensitivity also contributes: the anti-inflammatory actions of glucocorticoids are more effective in males, partly explaining fisetin's male-selective benefit.

> [!caution]
> Because senolytic efficacy, toxicity, and even benefit-vs-harm can reverse between the sexes, **sex-stratified dosing and endpoint analysis are required** in senolytic trials. This aligns with the vault-wide sex-difference finding (see [[Apoptosis]], [[SASP]]) that estrogen is a master determinant of senescent-cell metabolism and clearance.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

- D+Q has entered pilot/phase trials in diabetic kidney disease (NCT02848131), idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (NCT02874989), and osteoarthritis (NCT04210986).
- Safety considerations (e.g., Navitoclax thrombocytopenia) drive intermittent-dosing and next-generation, more selective agents.

## Documents

- [[_document_ - The-senescence-associated-secretory-phenotype-and-its-physiological-and-pathological-implications|SASP Review 2024]]: The review frames senolytics as agents that target SCAPs to selectively push senescent cells into apoptosis while sparing healthy cells, thereby reducing the SASP burden and inflammaging. By eliminating the secreting cell rather than only the secretome, they complement senomorphics and address senescence-driven pathology across aging and disease models.

- [[_document_ - Cellular senescence and SASP in tumor progression and therapeutic opportunities|Dong et al. 2024 Senescence and SASP in Tumors]]: Details newer senolytic strategies in cancer: BET family protein degraders (ARV825) targeting BRD4/NHEJ repair; CAR-T cells targeting uPAR for liver cancer and fibrosis; antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) using beta-2 microglobulin (B2M) and apolipoprotein D (ApoD) as markers; galacto-oligosaccharide nanoparticle delivery systems activated by SA-β-gal; and galactose-modified doxorubicin prodrugs. Emphasizes intermittent dosing as optimal for senolytic administration and highlights the "one-two punch" combination strategy of inducing senescence followed by senolytic clearance.
