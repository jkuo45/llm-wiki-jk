# 4-Hydroxynonenal

**4-Hydroxynonenal (4-HNE, 4-hydroxy-2-nonenal)** is a highly reactive α,β-unsaturated aldehyde generated as a major end-product of n-6 polyunsaturated fatty acid ([[Lipid Peroxidation|peroxidation]]), particularly during the oxidative degradation of arachidonic acid and linoleic acid.

## Formation & Chemistry

4-HNE is produced through the peroxidation of n-6 PUFAs via a cascade of free radical-mediated reactions. Following hydrogen abstraction from bis-allylic methylene groups and subsequent peroxidation, β-scission of lipid hydroperoxides yields the 4-hydroxyalkenal structure. At physiological pH, 4-HNE exists in equilibrium between its aldehyde form and a cyclic hemiacetal. It is a bifunctional electrophile, possessing both an aldehyde group and a C=C double bond conjugated to the carbonyl, making it highly reactive toward nucleophilic residues in proteins, DNA, and phospholipids.

## Biological Targets

- **Proteins**: 4-HNE forms covalent Michael adducts with cysteine (Cys), histidine (His), and lysine (Lys) residues via the C=C bond, and Schiff bases via its aldehyde group. These modifications can inactivate enzymes (e.g., [[GLUT4]], [[GAPDH]], [[Sodium Potassium ATPase]]), alter receptor function, and trigger protein aggregation. 4-HNE modification of [[Tau Protein]] promotes Tau oligomerization and is implicated in [[Alzheimer's Disease]].
- **DNA**: 4-HNE forms etheno-DNA adducts (e.g., 1,N⁶-etheno-dA, 3,N⁴-etheno-dC), which are mutagenic and linked to genomic instability and [[Cancer]].
- **Signaling**: At low concentrations, 4-HNE acts as a signaling molecule, activating stress-responsive transcription factors including [[NRF2]] (via KEAP1 modification), [[NF-κB]], and [[AP-1]], and inducing phase II detoxification enzymes as part of a hormetic adaptive response.

## Detoxification and Clearance

Because 4-HNE is a bifunctional electrophile, cells clear it by thiol-dependent routes rather than by excretion:

- **Glutathione conjugation**: [[Glutathione S-Transferase]]s (GSTA4, GSTP1) form GSH–HNE conjugates exported via MRP transporters. This is quantitatively the major route, and it couples 4-HNE clearance directly to [[Glutathione]] status.
- **Aldo-keto reductases (AKRs)**: reduce 4-HNE to 1,4-dihydroxynonene; AKR1B1 and AKR1C1 contribute.
- **Aldehyde dehydrogenases (ALDHs)**: oxidize 4-HNE to 4-hydroxynonenoic acid; mitochondrial ALDH2 is important in heart.
- **Protein carbonylation and export**: a fraction is sequestered as protein adducts and cleared by the proteasome and autophagy.

When antioxidant capacity is saturated, 4-HNE escapes clearance and adducts proteins — including the astrocytic glutamate transporter GLT-1 (EAAT2), impairing glutamate reuptake and contributing to excitotoxicity.

## Measurement

4-HNE is quantified as free aldehyde (HPLC with DNPH derivatization, LC-MS/MS) or, more stably, as protein adducts (ELISA, mass-spectrometric adductomics, or the Michael adduct with cysteine/His/Lys measured as 4-HNE-mercapturic acid in urine). Urinary 4-HNE-MA and 4-HNE–protein adducts are the least artifact-prone indices.

## Clinical Relevance

Elevated 4-HNE levels are detected in [[Oxidative Stress|oxidative stress]]-associated diseases: [[Cardiovascular Disease|cardiovascular disease]] (atherosclerotic plaques), [[Neurodegeneration|neurodegenerative diseases]] ([[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]], [[Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis]]), [[Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease|COPD]], [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], and [[Multiple Sclerosis]]. 4-HNE is measured as a biomarker of lipid peroxidation in plasma, CSF, and tissue samples using HPLC, LC-MS/MS, or ELISA against protein-conjugated 4-HNE.
