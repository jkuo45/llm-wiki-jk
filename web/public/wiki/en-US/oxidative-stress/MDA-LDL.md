# MDA-LDL

**MDA-LDL** is [[Malondialdehyde]]-modified [[LDL]] in which MDA, the dialdehyde end-product of [[Lipid Peroxidation]], reacts with lysine ε-amino groups on apolipoprotein B-100 to form Schiff-base (imine) and more stable enamine adducts. It is one of the two major immunologically defined forms of oxidatively modified lipoprotein — the other being [[Oxidized LDL]] — and circulates as an autoantigen recognized by anti-MDA-LDL antibodies.

## Formation

MDA is generated during the propagation phase of [[Lipid Peroxidation]], when hydroperoxides of [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|PUFAs]] with three or more double bonds (arachidonic, adrenic, docosahexaenoic acid) undergo β-scission. Because MDA is a bifunctional dialdehyde, it can cross-link two lysine residues (intra- or intermolecularly), producing the fluorescent Schiff-base adducts classically measured as TBARS.

In LDL, the apoB-100 particle presents ~200 lysine residues whose positive charge normally mediates electrostatic binding to the [[LDL]] receptor. MDA modification neutralizes these charges and:

- blocks receptor-mediated uptake, prolonging particle residence time in the intima
- creates neo-epitopes recognized by scavenger receptors ([[CD36 Receptor]], SR-A) on [[Macrophage]]s
- promotes unregulated uptake and [[Foam Cells|foam cell]] formation

## Biological Activity

MDA-LDL is not inert debris. It is immunogenic and pro-inflammatory:

- **Autoantibodies**: IgG anti-MDA-LDL titers correlate with carotid intima-media thickness and predict cardiovascular events in several cohorts, though the association is weaker and less consistent than for oxidized phospholipid markers.
- **Endothelial activation**: MDA-LDL upregulates [[VCAM-1]] and MCP-1 in endothelial cells, and activates [[NF-κB]] signaling.
- **Macrophage priming**: uptake via scavenger receptors delivers a lipid load that inflames the macrophage and accelerates atherosclerotic plaque growth.
- **Platelet and coagulation effects**: MDA-modified lipoproteins promote platelet aggregation and tissue factor expression.

## Measurement

MDA-LDL is quantified by sandwich ELISA using anti-MDA-LDL capture antibodies and peroxidase-labeled anti-apoB detection, or by LC-MS/MS of MDA-lysine adducts. It is distinct from the [[TBARS]] assay, which measures free MDA plus any MDA-reactive substances in a sample rather than the LDL-specific adduct.

> [!info] MDA-LDL vs. Oxidized LDL
> [[Oxidized LDL]] denotes the full spectrum of oxidatively modified particles (lipid peroxides, oxidized phospholipids, fragmented apoB) and is typically detected by antibodies against oxidized phospholipid epitopes (e.g. E06). MDA-LDL is narrower: it reports specifically on the aldehyde-adducted fraction. The two correlate but are not interchangeable, and their relative abundance shifts with the type of oxidative insult.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Atherosclerosis**: MDA-LDL accumulates in human atherosclerotic lesions and is a mechanistic bridge between systemic [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and plaque formation.
- **Diabetes and metabolic syndrome**: elevated MDA-LDL tracks with glycemic burden and insulin resistance, reflecting increased lipid peroxidation in a dyslipidemic milieu.
- **Smoking and chronic inflammation**: smoking and chronic inflammatory disease raise MDA-LDL, consistent with the oxidant burden of these exposures.
- **Antioxidant interventions**: [[Vitamin E]] and [[Resveratrol]] supplementation reduce MDA-LDL in some trials, though clinical benefit has not consistently followed the biomarker change — a recurring theme in the [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox]].
