# PRDX1

## Overview

**PRDX1** (peroxiredoxin-1, JC-16, MER5) is the founding member of the 2-Cys peroxiredoxin family and one of the most abundant cellular proteins. It is a thioredoxin-dependent peroxidase that reduces hydrogen peroxide and lipid hydroperoxides to water/alcohol using peroxidatic and resolving cysteines, and it is also an inducible chaperone that stabilises unfolded proteins. PRDX1 is among the earliest and most sensitive intracellular sensors of [[Hydrogen Peroxide]] because its catalytic cysteine is unusually reactive, and it therefore links local peroxide flux to both antioxidant capacity and redox signalling.

## Structure & Domains

PRDX1 is a ~24 kDa homodimer. Each monomer carries two conserved cysteines: the N-terminal peroxidatic Cys52 (oxidation-prone) and the C-terminal resolving Cys173, separated by ~20 residues and buried within a thioredoxin-fold core. A long flexible C-terminal tail wraps around the partner subunit and is required for efficient thioredoxin-mediated recycling. The dimer is further wrapped into higher-order oligomers (hexamers and higher), which are catalytically competent and act as a molecular chaperone.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Catalytic cycle**: Peroxide oxidises Cys52 to a sulfenic acid; this reacts with resolving Cys173 to form a disulfide; thioredoxin (via thioredoxin reductase and [[NAD+]]) then reduces the disulfide. Substrate oxidation outpaces the resolving step at high peroxide, causing reversible hyperoxidation of Cys52 to a sulfinic acid that turns the enzyme into a chaperone rather than a peroxidase — the classic switch between antioxidant and folding function.
- **Redox signalling**: Because PRDX1 reacts with peroxide at rates approaching diffusion limits, it sets the local peroxide tone that governs the activity of NF-κB, HIF-1α, and other redox-sensitive transcription factors. It thus acts as a throttle on [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] signalling rather than a simple sink.
- **Ferroptosis restraint**: PRDX1 and PRDX2 neutralise lipid hydroperoxides before they can propagate; loss of PRDX1 or of thioredoxin recycling increases lipid hydroperoxide burden and promotes [[Ferroptosis]] alongside [[GPX4]] loss, while [[Lipid Peroxidation]] is the substrate that PRDX1 competes with GPX4 for.
- **Chaperone and stress function**: Hyperoxidised PRDX1 binds unfolded proteins and prevents their aggregation, contributing to proteostasis and to survival under heat, oxidative and ER stress.

## Physiological Function and Clinical Relevance

PRDX1 is broadly expressed and is essential for red-cell antioxidant defence, which depends almost entirely on the thioredoxin/peroxiredoxin system. It supports cardiac and neuronal survival under ischaemia-reperfusion injury and contributes to the maintenance of [[Mitochondria]] integrity. PRDX1 levels are elevated in many [[Cancer]] types and in [[Oxidative Stress]] states, and the PRDX1–thioredoxin axis intersects with [[Sirtuins]] and [[NAD+]] depletion, since thioredoxin recycling and sirtuin activity draw on the same reduced pyridine nucleotide pool. PRDX1 is also a target of the paralogue [[PRDX6]], which has distinct substrate preference and signalling roles.
