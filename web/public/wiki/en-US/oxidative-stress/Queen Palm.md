# Queen Palm

**Queen Palm** (*Syagrus romanzoffiana*), native to South America and widely planted as an ornamental in USDA zones 9–11, produces dense hanging infructescences of hundreds of orange drupes (locally **jerivá** or **pino**). The fruit pulp (mesocarp) and seed kernel (endosperm) carry a distinct, tissue-partitioned profile of lipophilic antioxidants and lipids that food-chemistry literature (mainly Brazilian and Argentine) links to defense against [[Lipid Peroxidation]].

> [!tip] Why it matters here
> Queen Palm is a rare fruit where the antioxidant suite (β-carotene, lutein, zeaxanthin, α-tocopherol) and its lipid vehicle (oleic/linoleic pulp oil, lauric-rich kernel oil) occur together — the exact combination required for compounds to partition into [[Cell Membranes]] and intercept LOO• chains.

## Biochemical Nutrient Profile

- **Carotenoids** — β-carotene and α-carotene dominate the orange pericarp (provitamin A; quench [[Singlet Oxygen]]), with lutein and zeaxanthin as membrane-integrated photoprotectants. Pulp oil extracts reach ~1,200 μg/g total carotenoids.
- **Phenolics & flavonoids** — hydroxycinnamic acids (ferulic, caffeic conjugates) and flavonols ([[Quercetin]] glucosides, [[Kaempferol]] derivatives); mature fruit ~590–1,100 mg GAE/100 g extractable polyphenols with high DPPH/ABTS/ORAC radical-scavenging activity. Vitamin C is low — antioxidant capacity runs through [[Polyphenols]] and carotenoids rather than ascorbate.
- **Lipids, partitioned by tissue** — kernel oil is medium-chain saturated (lauric C12:0 at 30–40%, myristic C14:0, palmitic); pulp oil is unsaturated (oleic C18:1, linoleic C18:2) and carries α-tocopherol (>320 mg/kg) — i.e. the [[Vitamin E]] pool rides on the same triglycerides that feed membrane pools.
- **Fiber & sugars** — pectin, cellulose and lignin networks (13–36% dry matter depending on fraction) plus fructose, glucose, sucrose; minerals rich in potassium and magnesium.

## Rank Among Lipid-Peroxidation Defenders

Structural protection against [[Lipid Peroxidation]] requires lipophilic antioxidants that embed in bilayers ([[Vitamin E]] chain-breaking LOO•, carotenoids quenching ¹O₂, [[Coenzyme Q10]] regenerating tocopherol, [[GPX4]] reducing LOOH). Against that metric the Queen Palm pulp sits in a **high-potency peer tier**, below record-holders and above standard crops:

- **Tier 1 (outranks it):** Buriti (*Mauritia flexuosa*, 5–10× the carotenoid load), sea buckthorn, and red palm oil (tocotrienols).
- **Tier 2 (its tier):** comparable to or above jelly palm (*Butia capitata*) and açaí in provitamin-A carotenoid protection — açaí leads on hydrophilic anthocyanins, jerivá on lipophilic carotenoids.
- **Tier 3 (it outranks):** carrots and sweet potatoes have more β-carotene by weight but lack the intrinsic lipid matrix for direct membrane delivery; common fruits are hydrophilic-polyphenol dominated.

> [!info] Evidence status
> Rankings and compositional numbers are in vitro / food-chemistry measurements. Human clinical trials are essentially absent; applications studied are functional-food fortification, animal feed, and kernel-oil biotechnology.

## Metabolic & Enzymatic Activities (in vitro)

- **α-Glucosidase inhibition** — slows intestinal carbohydrate hydrolysis; interest for postprandial glucose control in [[Diabetes]] and [[Insulin Resistance]] research.
- **Acetylcholinesterase inhibition** — extracts slow acetylcholine breakdown in vitro; speculative neuroprotective angle near [[Neurodegenerative Diseases]].

## Edibility, Toxicity & Availability

Pulp is non-toxic and sweet (plum/banana-like) but intensely fibrous — chewed for juice, fiber spat out; kernel nutmeat is coconut-like behind a rock-hard shell. Not toxic to dogs, cats or horses (unlike sago palm, *Cycas revoluta*), but the indigestible seeds cause foreign-body intestinal obstruction, choking and dental fractures in dogs; fermented fallen pulp can cause ethanol toxicity. Not sold commercially — low pulp yield, rapid fermentation, high processing cost — sourced by urban foraging, nurseries, and artisanal use in southern Brazil/Argentina.

## Biochemical Twins

Closest compositional analogues: jelly palm (same Arecaceae tribe, near-identical pulp/kernel split), buriti (carotenoid record-holder), African oil palm (red palm oil + lauric kernel oil mirrors the dual framework), coconut (kernel lauric/myristic profile), and sea buckthorn (non-palm orange pulp with rare palmitoleic-rich pulp oil).
