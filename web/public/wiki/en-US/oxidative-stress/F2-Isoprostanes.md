# F2-Isoprostanes

**F2-Isoprostanes (F2-IsoPs)** are prostaglandin-like compounds formed non-enzymatically by free radical-mediated [[Lipid Peroxidation]] of [[Arachidonic acid]], and are widely considered the gold-standard biomarker for endogenous [[Oxidative Stress]].

## Formation Chemistry

Unlike cyclooxygenase-derived prostaglandins, F2-IsoPs are generated independently of COX enzymes, in situ on esterified phospholipids within cell membranes. The mechanism begins with free-radical hydrogen abstraction from the bis-allylic C7, C10, or C13 positions of arachidonic acid (20:4, n-6). The resulting carbon-centered radical undergoes endocyclic addition of O₂ and rearrangement to a bicyclic endoperoxide (PGG₂-like), which is reduced to the F-ring prostane. After phospholipase A₂ (PLA₂) release, free F2-IsoPs enter plasma and urine. Because formation requires radical attack on intact membrane lipids, F2-IsoPs report cumulative, non-enzymatic oxidative modification rather than enzymatic eicosanoid signaling.

## Isoform Diversity

Over 60 F2-IsoP isomers exist. The most frequently measured are 8-iso-PGF₂α (iPF₂α-III, also called 15-F₂t-IsoP), which is used as the reference biomarker in both plasma and urine. Other regioisomers (e.g., ent-7-epi-iso-PGF₂α) are also quantified in research settings.

## Analytical Detection

F2-IsoPs are measured by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC-MS) or liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), with the latter offering superior specificity and throughput. Rigorous sample handling (antioxidant addition, cold processing, avoidance of air exposure) is mandatory to prevent ex vivo autoxidation, a major source of artefactual elevation in older studies.

Two sampling conventions matter for interpretation:

- **Total (esterified + free) F2-IsoPs** — released by alkaline hydrolysis of plasma or tissue phospholipids; reflects the *cumulative* oxidative modification of membrane lipids, which is the more informative measure of tissue oxidative burden.
- **Free F2-IsoPs** in urine (often normalized to creatinine) — reflects recent release and excretion; more practical for large cohorts and repeated sampling.

Immunoassays exist but cross-react with prostaglandin metabolites and are not recommended for quantitative work.

> [!info] F2-IsoPs vs. isofurans vs. MDA
> [[F2-Isoprostanes]] are the reference marker for free-radical lipid peroxidation because they form non-enzymatically, are chemically stable, are present in detectable amounts in healthy subjects, and increase proportionally with oxidant stress. Companion markers are worth pairing: **isofurans** (furan-ring isomers) dominate when oxygen tension is high, [[Malondialdehyde]]/[[TBARS]] is cheaper but nonspecific, and oxidized phospholipid epitopes report on the LDL-specific fraction. A rise in F2-IsoPs with stable enzymatic prostanoids is strong evidence for radical rather than COX-driven oxidation.

## Clinical Interpretation

Elevated F2-IsoPs are found in virtually every disease involving oxidative stress: [[Cardiovascular Disease]] (atherothrombosis, reperfusion injury), [[Neurodegeneration]] ([[Alzheimer's Disease]], [[Parkinson's Disease]]), [[Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease]], [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]], and [[Aging]]. They correlate with disease severity and decline with effective antioxidant or lifestyle intervention, supporting their use as pharmacodynamic endpoints in redox trials.

Practical caveats:

- **Non-invasive**: urinary F2-IsoPs are among the few validated whole-body oxidative-stress readouts obtainable without tissue biopsy.
- **Intervention responsiveness**: smoking cessation, weight loss, exercise training, and Mediterranean-style diets lower F2-IsoPs within weeks to months, which makes the marker useful for tracking exposure change rather than only disease state.
- **Antioxidant trials**: F2-IsoPs generally fall with high-dose antioxidant supplementation even when clinical endpoints do not improve — a reminder that biomarker movement is not equivalent to benefit, consistent with the [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox]].
- **Pre-analytical discipline**: haemolysis, prolonged storage, and freeze–thaw cycles generate artifactual F2-IsoPs; consistent handling is essential for cross-study comparison.

## Biological Activity

Beyond being biomarkers, F2-IsoPs possess biological activity. They act as weak vasoconstrictors of pulmonary and systemic vessels and can potentiate platelet activation and smooth-muscle proliferation through thromboxane receptor (TP) binding, linking oxidative lipid damage to vasomotor and thrombotic pathology.
