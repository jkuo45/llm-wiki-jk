# M1dG

**M1dG** (pyrimido[1,2-a]purin-10(3H)-one deoxyguanosine) is the major exocyclic DNA adduct formed when [[Malondialdehyde]] (MDA) reacts with the exocyclic amino group of deoxyguanosine. It is the best-characterized DNA lesion of [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and serves as a biomarker linking oxidative lipid damage to mutagenesis and carcinogenesis.

## Formation

MDA is a bifunctional electrophilic dialdehyde generated during the β-scission of [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|PUFA]] hydroperoxides. When it encounters deoxyguanosine in DNA, the two aldehyde groups condense with the N¹ and N² positions of guanine to close a pyrimido ring, releasing water. The resulting tricyclic adduct is stable enough to persist through DNA isolation and quantification, but its cyclic structure distorts the double helix.

Alternative exocyclic adducts (M1dA from deoxyadenosine, M1dC from deoxycytidine) form in smaller amounts. M1dG is the dominant species because guanine is the most electron-rich base and the most oxidation-prone.

## Mutagenicity and Repair

M1dG is pro-mutagenic. In bacterial and mammalian polymerase assays it causes G→T and G→A transversions and frameshift mutations, consistent with mispairing during replication. Its bulkiness and loss of Watson–Crick hydrogen-bonding capacity explain the misincorporation pattern.

Repair of M1dG is **incompletely settled**, with both major pathways implicated:

- **Base excision repair (BER)**: alkyladenine glycosylase (AAG/MPG) and related glycosylases (Fpg/NEIL family in bacteria) excise the damaged base, followed by AP endonuclease, polymerase, and ligase.
- **Nucleotide excision repair (NER)**: mammalian cell studies indicate a substantial NER contribution, consistent with M1dG being a bulky helix-distorting lesion.

Relative contribution varies by species, chromatin context, and lesion occupancy. What is clear is that repair capacity is limiting: combined MDA load with impaired repair elevates steady-state M1dG and is a plausible driver of mutation accumulation in aging tissue.

## Measurement

M1dG is quantified by:

- **³²P-postlabeling / HPLC** — sensitive but labor-intensive
- **immunoaffinity–HPLC–fluorescence** — the historical workhorse for tissue and leukocyte DNA
- **LC-MS/MS with isotope-dilution** — current gold standard, giving absolute adducts per 10⁸ nucleotides

Steady-state levels in human leukocyte DNA are typically 1–50 adducts per 10⁸ nucleotides and rise with smoking, inflammation, and diets rich in oxidized lipid.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Carcinogenesis**: elevated M1dG is detected in colon, breast, and liver tumors and in surrounding tissue, supporting the lipid-peroxidation route to cancer. MDA itself is classified as reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen.
- **Atherosclerosis**: M1dG in vascular smooth muscle and endothelial DNA correlates with lesion burden, and MDA also modifies LDL (see [[MDA-LDL]]) and proteins, so the same aldehyde pool damages lipid, protein, and DNA in the plaque.
- **Aging**: accumulation of exocyclic adducts with age parallels the rise in [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and the decline in BER capacity; the lesion is a molecular record of cumulative oxidative lipid damage.
- **Diet and exposure**: fried and processed foods contribute exogenous MDA; smoking and chronic inflammatory disease raise endogenous production.
