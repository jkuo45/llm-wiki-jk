# NAFLD

**NAFLD** (non-alcoholic fatty liver disease) is hepatic steatosis in the absence of significant alcohol consumption, and is the most common liver disease worldwide, with a global prevalence of roughly 25% in adults. It spans a continuum from simple steatosis, through [[NASH|non-alcoholic steatohepatitis]] (NASH/MASH), to fibrosis and [[Cirrhosis|cirrhosis]], and it is the hepatic expression of [[Metabolic Syndrome]] — the reason it is often labelled a manifestation rather than a liver-specific disease.

> [!info] Nomenclature
> The 2023 multisociety Delphi consensus replaced NAFLD with **MASLD** (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) and NASH with **MASH**. The important change is not cosmetic: MASLD is *defined* by the presence of steatosis plus **at least one of five cardiometabolic risk criteria** (BMI, waist circumference, fasting glucose or HbA1c, triglycerides, hypertension), whereas NAFLD was defined by exclusion of alcohol. The change makes the diagnosis positive rather than residual, and it was intended to improve recognition at the primary-care level. Mnemonic aside: "NASH" was the mnemonic for the second, and the field declined to invent a new one.

## Pathophysiology

NAFLD is best understood as a **multi-hit** disease in which systemic insulin resistance is upstream of everything else.

> [!info] Mechanism
> Insulin resistance means adipose tissue fails to suppress [[Free Fatty Acid|free fatty acid]] flux to the liver, and the liver becomes both a passive recipient and an active contributor. Delivered FFA plus hepatic [[De Novo Lipogenesis|de novo lipogenesis]] under SREBP-1c and ChREBP drive triglyceride accumulation in [[Lipid Droplet|lipid droplets]]. Lipotoxic species — saturated FFA, free cholesterol, ceramides — then accumulate and stress the organelle, producing mitochondrial dysfunction and [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] generation. Dying hepatocytes release damage-associated patterns that are sensed by Kupffer cells and recruited [[Monocyte|monocytes]], which release [[TNFα]] and [[IL-6]]. Sustained injury activates hepatic stellate cells, which deposit collagen: fibrosis. Continued injury exhausts regenerative capacity, giving cirrhosis, and the cirrhotic liver is a recognised risk setting for hepatocellular carcinoma even without an intervening fibrosis stage.
>
> Two newer layers complicate the picture. **ER stress** engages the [[Unfolded Protein Response]] and, via [[PERK]]–ATF4–CHOP, feeds both apoptosis and [[Inflammasome|inflammasome]] priming — the branch the UPR contributes to NASH. And **Gut-derived factors** including bacterial translocation, endotoxin, and the [[Intestinal Barrier|intestinal barrier]] leak contribute a constant low-grade inflammatory drive.

> [!warning] NASH is not simply "fat with inflammation"
> This is a clinically important and frequently over-stated point. Around 90% of NAFLD patients have plain steatosis with no necroinflammation, and steatosis alone is not reliably associated with progression to fibrosis. The prognostic variable that matters is **fibrosis stage** — stage 3 fibrosis carries substantially increased cardiovascular, hepatic, and cancer mortality — and many patients with advanced fibrosis have never had an NASH diagnosis at all. Diagnosis and management are consequently moving away from liver-inflammation-based endpoints toward fibrosis staging, which is why non-invasive fibrosis assessment (elastography, or FIB-4 as a rule-out test) has become the practical first step in clinical pathways.

## Genetics and the metabolic context

Genetic modifiers make the disease neither uniform nor inevitable. The strongest common variant is *PNPLA3* p.I148M, which shifts triglyceride partitioning from the retinyl ester pool to free fatty acids and independently raises hepatic fat, fibrosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma risk — a gene–environment interaction with an unusually clean mechanistic story. The *TM6SF2* p.E432K variant impairs VLDL export and drives steatosis at the cost of a protective lipid profile, a classic trade-off. Most cases nonetheless remain polygenic with strong environmental load.

Risk tracks almost every component of metabolic syndrome: [[Obesity]] (particularly visceral adiposity), [[Insulin Resistance]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], [[Hyperlipidemia]] with high triglycerides and low HDL, and hypertension. The parallel vocabulary of metabolic dysfunction — ectopic lipid, lipotoxicity, [[Inflammaging|low-grade inflammation]] — is the same vocabulary used in the insulin-resistance and [[Aging]] literature.

## Systemic consequences

NAFLD is not confined to the liver. It is independently associated with [[Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus]], cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, [[Obstructive Sleep Apnoea|obstructive sleep apnea]], colorectal cancer, and postmenopausal breast cancer, which is why the mortality of NAFLD is dominated by cardiovascular rather than hepatic causes at the steatosis stage.

## Management

> [!warning] What actually works
> Lifestyle intervention remains the only clearly effective first-line therapy: **weight loss is the intervention**, and 7–10% body-weight reduction is the threshold most consistently associated with resolution of steatosis, resolution of MASH, and regression of fibrosis. This is achievable with diet, exercise, and — where indicated and tolerated — [[GLP-1 receptor agonist|GLP-1 receptor agonists]] and [[Resmetirom]], the first drug approved for MASH with compensated fibrosis. Vitamin E improves steatosis and inflammation in selected non-diabetic patients but has no effect on fibrosis and carries prostate-cancer risk signal in older men. [[Statins]] are safe in NAFLD — the historical concern about hepatic toxicity was not borne out — and are indicated for the accompanying dyslipidaemia, making them the single highest-yield prescription in a NAFLD population.

The path forward in the literature is metabolic rather than hepatobiliary: the [[FGF21]] analog class (efruxifermin, pegbelfermin) targets the underlying driver; [[Ezetimibe]] and other cholesterol-flux interventions are being repurposed; and [[Autophagy]] induction is a live mechanistic interest, on the theory that restoring hepatic autophagic flux relieves the ER-stress and lipotoxic load that sustains injury.
