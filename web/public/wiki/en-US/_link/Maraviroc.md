# Maraviroc

Maraviroc is a Food and Drug Administration-approved small-molecule antagonist of [[CCR5]], used clinically as an HIV-1 entry inhibitor (blocking gp120–[[CD4]]/CCR5-mediated fusion) (Lieberman-Blum et al., 2008).

## Pharmacodynamics

- Reversible, allosteric CCR5 antagonist; blocks chemokine ([[CCL5]], CCL3, CCL4) signaling through CCR5 without affecting the receptor's other functions.
- Repurposing horizon: CCL5–CCR5 signaling drives pro-tumorigenic myelopoiesis, senescence-associated reproductive decline, and stroke/neuroinflammation outcomes.

## Geroscience Application (preclinical)

- In 23-month-old mice, 35 mg/kg/day i.p. for one month normalized age-associated myeloid skewing: reduced GMP and LS−K progenitor fractions (HSCs unaffected), reduced circulating and tissue-infiltrating ([[Myeloperoxidase|Myeloperoxidase]]+) [[Neutrophils]] in lung and liver, and normalized the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio.
- Lowered serum inflammaging cytokines (IL-6, TNF, IL-12p70, IL-18, IL-22, CXCL1), improved nonfasting glucose, albumin, ALT/AST, respiratory exchange ratio, forelimb grip strength, and rotarod performance. No effect in young mice (Gabandé-Rodríguez et al., 2026, *Nat Aging*, doi:10.1038/s43587-026-01209-9).
- Human plausibility: CCR5-Delta32 loss-of-function carriers show better outcomes after stroke and in multiple sclerosis (Joy et al., 2019).
