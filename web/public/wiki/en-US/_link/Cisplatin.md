# Cisplatin
Cisplatin is a platinum-based chemotherapy drug widely used to treat various types of [[Cancer]], whose therapeutic efficacy can be enhanced by [[Intermittent Fasting]].
## Pharmacology
Cisplatin is a platinum-based DNA crosslinking agent that causes intra- and interstrand DNA adducts, triggering apoptosis. It is used for testicular, ovarian, and lung cancers.
> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - FLIP the Switch Regulation of Apoptosis and Necroptosis by cFLIP|Tsuchiya et al. 2015]]
> Cisplatin sensitizes cells partly by lowering [[c-FLIP]]: treatment induces an Itch–p53–cFLIP complex and cFLIP polyubiquitylation, and [[ATM]] phosphorylates Itch at Ser161 to promote cFLIP_L degradation. Gelsolin is a further component of the Itch–p53–cFLIP complex, and its expression confers cisplatin chemoresistance in gynecologic cancers — connecting DNA-damage signaling to death-receptor sensitization.

## Autophagy Connection
Cisplatin induces [[Autophagy]] as a resistance mechanism in cancer cells. Autophagy inhibition enhances cisplatin cytotoxicity. Cisplatin also damages [[Mitochondria]] and triggers [[Mitophagy]].
**Cisplatin** is a chemotherapy medication used to treat a number of cancers, including testicular, ovarian, cervical, head and neck, bladder, and lung cancers. It belongs to the class of platinum-based antineoplastic drugs.
## Use with Melittin
- **Synergy**: Synergistic anticancer effects have been reported between [[melittin]] and cisplatin in cervical and laryngeal malignancies.
