# handgrip strength

**Overview:** **Handgrip strength** (HGS) is the maximal force produced by squeezing a hand dynamometer, normalised to body size or reported in kilograms. It is one of the most reproducible single-site measures of whole-body [[Muscle]] function and is used in sarcopenia and frailty screening.

## Clinical significance

- HGS is a component of the [[Frailty Index]] and a key criterion in the [[Sarcopenia]] definition of age-related weakness (see [[Muscle Atrophy]]).
- Low HGS independently predicts falls, disability, longer hospital stays and mortality in older adults, and is used as a functional outcome in [[Skeletal Muscle]] interventions.
- Inflammatory and oxidative ageing depress grip force: the adrenochrome/advanced-glycation literature reports an inverse correlation between grip strength and circulating carboxymethyllysine, linking [[Aging]] and [[Inflammation]] to loss of muscle function.
- Because the measurement is cheap and non-invasive, HGS is widely used as a geroscience endpoint and as a proxy for whole-body strength in trials of anti-ageing interventions.
