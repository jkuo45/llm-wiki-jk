# PECAM1

## Overview

PECAM1 (Platelet Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule 1, CD31) is a 130-kDa transmembrane glycoprotein belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily. It is constitutively expressed on endothelial cells, platelets, and subsets of leukocytes. PECAM1 mediates cell-cell adhesion and regulates vascular integrity, leukocyte transmigration, and signaling pathways involved in angiogenesis and inflammation.

## Structure & Domains

- Six extracellular immunoglobulin-like domains (Ig-like domains 1–6)
- Single transmembrane domain
- Short cytoplasmic tail with immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motifs (ITIMs)
- Phosphorylation of ITIM motifs recruits SHP-1/SHP-2 phosphatases

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- Homophilic binding mediates endothelial cell-cell adhesion at junctions
- Heterophilic binding to integrins (αvβ3, αIIbβ3) and CD38 on leukocytes
- ITIM-mediated signaling inhibits integrin activation and modulates immune cell activation
- Contributes to endothelial barrier function via junctional organization
- Loss of PECAM1 expression is a hallmark of [[Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition]] (EndoMT)

> [!info] Source: CXCL12 → Endothelial Dysfunction Mechanistic Trace
> PECAM1 is downregulated 55% during CXCL12-driven EndoMT, representing the most pronounced loss of endothelial identity markers alongside CDH5 downregulation.

## Physiological Function

- Maintains endothelial cell-cell adhesion and vascular integrity
- Facilitates leukocyte transmigration during inflammation (diapedesis)
- Regulates platelet activation and thrombosis
- Modulates angiogenic signaling through VEGF receptor interactions
- Acts as a mechanosensor in endothelial cells

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- PECAM1 polymorphisms are associated with cardiovascular disease risk
- Loss of PECAM1 expression during EndoMT contributes to age-related vascular dysfunction
- PECAM1 is used as an endothelial marker in immunohistochemistry and flow cytometry
- Soluble PECAM1 (sPECAM1) levels are elevated in inflammatory and cardiovascular conditions

## Live-cell "Don't-Eat-Me" Signal

Homophilic CD31–CD31 engagement between a live leukocyte (or endothelial/platelet cell) and a phagocyte delivers an inhibitory signal — via the cytoplasmic ITIMs and SHP-1/2 recruitment noted above — that blocks phagocytosis of the CD31-serving target cell. Apoptotic cells lose the signaling competence of their CD31 pair, so the "don't-eat-me" badge switches off on the same surface that then exposes eat-me signals ([[Phosphatidylserine]]): CD31 contributes to the convergence of inhibited/active display that phagocytes read (reported in vitro; Tier 4). See [[Efferocytosis]].
