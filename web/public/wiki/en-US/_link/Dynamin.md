# Dynamin

**Dynamin** is a large (≈100 kDa) GTPase that severs [[Cell Membranes]]. It is unusual among signalling GTPases because its activity is not primarily about relaying a signal: dynamin is a *mechanical enzyme* that constricts a necked membrane and pulls it apart, generating the free energy for scission from GTP hydrolysis. Its best-known roles are in [[Clathrin]]-mediated [[Endocytosis]] and in the scission of [[Synaptic Vesicles]] at nerve terminals.

> [!info] Three dynamins, three compartments
> **Dynamin-1** (*DNM1*) is almost exclusively neuronal and dominates synaptic vesicle recycling. **Dynamin-2** (*DNM2*) is ubiquitously expressed and dominates constitutive clathrin-mediated endocytosis and general membrane remodelling. **Dynamin-1-like** (*DNM1L*), also called mitofusin-like, is a dynamin-family GTPase dedicated to [[Mitochondrial Fission]].

## Structure & Domains

Dynamin has three functional modules arranged along a flexible neck:

- **GTPase (G) domain** — a Ras-like P-loop NTPase fold. Mutation of the catalytic lysine (K44 in dynamin-1) abolishes hydrolysis and converts the protein into a dominant-negative "GTP-locked" inhibitor, which is the basis of most dynamin loss-of-function experiments.
- **Pleckstrin homology (PH) domain** — binds [[PtdIns(4,5)P2]], the phosphoinositide enriched in clathrin-coated pits and synaptic vesicle membranes. This recruitment step is what localises dynamin to the membrane it will sever; PH-domain mutations abolishing PIP2 binding also block scission.
- **Proline-rich domain (PRD)** — binds SH3-domain proteins including amphiphysin, endophilin and SNX9/15, which connect dynamin to the coat machinery, to actin and to bulk membrane.

Dynamin also self-assembles: the G domains dimerise in trans (or in a "crossed" dimer in the absence of nucleotide), and multiple dimers polymerise into helical collars or rings around the neck of a budding vesicle.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

1. **Recruitment.** PIP2 generated at the coated pit recruits dynamin via its PH domain; SH3 partners dock the PRD.
2. **Oligomerisation.** Dimers polymerise into a collar encircling the membrane neck.
3. **Constriction.** Helical polymerisation narrows the neck; the collar is thought to slide or spiral toward the membrane, converting the collar's stored polymerisation energy into mechanical work.
4. **Scission.** GTP hydrolysis — plus the membrane itself, which resists constriction below a critical radius — drives the final severing event. Evidence for an "intermediate" filament or for pure osmotic lysis is still debated.

Downstream, dynamin is reused: it is required for the fission of [[Endocytic Lysosome Reformation|endocytic]] and autophagic vesicles, for Golgi and [[Membrane Trafficking]] scission events, and for the budding of enveloped viruses (including [[HIV-1]]), which has made dynamin an antiviral target.

## Physiological Function

- **Synaptic vesicle recycling.** In nerve terminals, dynamin-1 recruits to vesicle buds and severs them; mice lacking dynamin-1 arrest vesicle recycling and develop progressive neurodegeneration.
- **Receptor internalisation.** Clathrin-mediated endocytosis of transferrin and LDL receptors requires dynamin; its inhibition blocks receptor down-regulation and nutrient uptake.
- **Mitochondrial fission.** The dynamin-relative [[MFN2]]/[[MFN1]] mitofusins perform the analogous scission in mitochondria, and dynamin-like proteins cooperate with [[DRP1]] in the fission machinery.
- **Actin and microtubule coupling.** Through its PRD, dynamin links scission to the [[Actin]] and [[Microtubule]] cytoskeleton, aligning fission with the contractile and trafficking machinery of the cell.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Centronuclear myopathy.** Dominant mutations in *DNM2* cause a congenital myopathy with centrally placed nuclei, weakness and abnormal myofibrillar organisation — one of the "centronuclear" or myotubular myopathies.
- **Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2M.** *DNM2* mutations also cause this autosomal dominant axonal neuropathy, linking dynamin-2 to [[Neurodegeneration]] and to defects in axonal trafficking.
- **Cleft lip and palate.** A rare *DNM2* variant segregates with orofacial clefting, consistent with dynamin-2 roles in craniofacial development and in [[Dystrophin|dystrophin]]-associated membrane homeostasis in muscle.
- **Neurodegeneration.** Failure of dynamin-dependent vesicle scission impairs synaptic vesicle recycling and autophagic flux, contributing to proteinopathy; dynamin-2 also participates in [[Muscular Dystrophies (MD)]]-related membrane repair.
- **Pharmacology.** Small-molecule dynamin inhibitors (Dynasore, Ryngo 1-23, and bis-T-22b) are used as research tools and explored as antiviral and anti-invasive agents.
