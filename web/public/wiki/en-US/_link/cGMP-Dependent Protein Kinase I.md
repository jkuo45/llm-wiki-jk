# cGMP-Dependent Protein Kinase I

**cGMP-dependent protein kinase type I** (cGKI; gene *PRKG1*) is the principal effector of [[Cyclic Guanosine Monophosphate|cGMP]] and the most abundant cGMP target in [[Erythrocytes|erythrocytes]]. It sits downstream of the [[Nitric Oxide|NO]] → [[Soluble Guanylyl Cyclase|soluble guanylyl cyclase]] → cGMP axis and acts as a **survival kinase**: cGKI suppresses cytosolic Ca2+ rise, which in turn prevents membrane scrambling and [[Phosphatidylserine|PS]] externalization.

## Structure & Domains

cGKI is a cGMP-dependent holoenzyme of two functional units: a **regulatory (R) subunit** with two cGMP-binding domains (one regulatory, one stimulatory) and a **catalytic (C) subunit** carrying the ATP-binding site, the serine/threonine kinase domain, and an autoinhibitory pseudosubstrate segment that blocks substrate access until cGMP binding releases it. cGKI is expressed as two splice isoforms, PKG1α (myocyte/smooth muscle) and PKG1β (platelets and erythrocytes), differing in the N-terminal regulatory region; PKG1β dominates in RBCs. A second PKG gene, *PRKG2* (PKG II, cGKII), encodes a related but distinct kinase expressed in smooth muscle, intestine and spleen.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Anti-eryptotic arm.** NO donors and membrane-permeable cGMP analogues suppress stress-induced eryptosis; the effect is abolished in cGKI-deficient cells. Mechanistically, cGKI lowers cytosolic Ca2+ — by inhibiting Ca2+-permeable cation channels and stimulating Ca2+-ATPase — which prevents Ca2+-activated [[Scramblase]] activity, PS exposure and cell shrinkage. cGKI therefore acts upstream of the Ca2+ threshold that commits the cell to death.
- **Vascular and platelet roles.** In smooth muscle, NO–sGC–cGMP–cGKI drives relaxation and lowers intracellular Ca2+; in platelets cGKI limits activation and shape change.
- **Caveat.** cGKI can also be activated independently of cGMP by oxidative stress, so the pathway is not purely cGMP-gated.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **cGKI knockout mice** (Föller et al., PNAS 2008) develop pronounced anemia and splenomegaly by 10 weeks: lower RBC count, hematocrit and hemoglobin, compensatory reticulocytosis and raised erythropoietin, and a spleen packed with PS-exposing Ter119+ cells. Knockout erythrocytes show raised cytosolic Ca2+, increased PS exposure and faster clearance in vivo — i.e. enhanced [[Eryptosis]]. Anemia precedes splenomegaly, so shortened erythrocyte survival is the primary lesion and splenic retention amplifies it.
- **Readout used in the field:** 8-Br-cGMP or dibutyryl-cGMP rescues eryptosis in wild-type but not cGKI-null erythrocytes, giving a clean pharmacological test of the NO–cGMP–cGKI survival axis.
