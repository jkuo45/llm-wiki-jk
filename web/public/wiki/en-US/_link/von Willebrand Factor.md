# von Willebrand Factor

**von Willebrand factor (vWF)** is a large, multimeric plasma glycoprotein produced by endothelial cells and megakaryocytes (stored in platelet α-granules). It performs two indispensable functions in primary haemostasis: it mediates platelet adhesion to exposed subendothelium at sites of high shear stress, and it binds and protects coagulation factor VIII from clearance.

## Overview

von Willebrand factor is the multimeric adhesion protein of primary haemostasis. It performs two independent but complementary jobs: bridging platelets to exposed subendothelium under flow via the GPIb-IX-V complex, and binding and stabilizing coagulation factor VIII. It is produced by endothelial cells and stored in platelet alpha-granules, and its quantity and multimer size are the molecular basis of von Willebrand disease.

## Structure & Domains

- **Gene:** *VWF* on chromosome 12p13; a very large gene with more than 40 exons, reflecting the complexity of processing.
- **Multimer architecture:** the mature protein is a ~2,250 kDa disulfide-linked homodimer of pro-VWF subunits. The A1 domain of the N-terminal region carries the platelet GPIb binding site (and the botrocetin site); the A3 domain binds subfibrillar [[Collagen]]; and the C-terminal D′D3 region carries the factor VIII binding site.
- **Propeptide (D1D2)** is required for multimerization and for von Willebrand factor type 1 antigen binding; its loss (as in type 2M/3 variants) produces defects in multimer size.
- **Cleavage** by ADAMTS13 at Tyr1605–Met1606 generates the circulating multimer; the ultra-large forms released from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies are the preferred substrate of ADAMTS13.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

1. Endothelial vWF is stored in Weibel-Palade bodies; shear stress or thrombin (via PAR1) triggers release, and platelet α-granules release vWF on platelet activation.
2. **Platelet adhesion:** vWF binds exposed subendothelial collagen and platelet GPIb (the GPIb-IX-V complex), forming a bridge that arrests the platelet in flowing blood. This is a shear-dependent interaction: unlike integrin-mediated adhesion, vWF–GPIb binding requires the multimer to be long enough and the flow to be fast enough to unroll the A1 domain and expose the binding site.
3. **Factor VIII carriage:** vWF stabilizes factor VIII (binding to the D′D3/A1 region), protects it from proteolysis and clearance, and localizes it to the platelet surface, where it supports tenase activity and thrombin generation.
4. **Signalling:** vWF binding to GPIb triggers platelet activation via the Gαi–PLCβ–calcium pathway, raising cytosolic [[Calcium]] and inducing integrin αIIbβ3 activation and fibrinogen binding for aggregation.

## Physiological Function

vWF is the primary molecule of platelet adhesion and the anchor of the primary haemostatic plug. It also functions as a carrier and chaperone for factor VIII, so that vWF deficiency reduces both platelet adhesion and the intrinsic coagulation cofactor pool. Its release is a canonical marker of endothelial activation: circulating vWF and the vWF/[[Platelets]] ratio are used to assess endothelial injury. Endothelial vWF also contributes to inflammation and leukocyte recruitment, and its interaction with the integrin αvβ3 on smooth muscle cells supports vascular smooth muscle migration and neointimal growth.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **von Willebrand disease (vWD):** the most common inherited bleeding disorder (autosomal dominant in most types), characterized by mucocutaneous bleeding. **Type 1** (quantitative deficiency), **type 2** (qualitative variants: 2A/2M/2B defects of multimer size or GPIb binding, 2N defective factor VIII binding), and **type 3** (severe, homozygous deficiency with undetectable vWF and very low factor VIII). Diagnosis uses vWF antigen, vWF activity (ristocetin cofactor or GPIb-based assays), factor VIII level, and multimer analysis; bleeding is typically treated with desmopressin (type 1 and selected type 2), vWF-containing concentrates (type 3, type 2A/2N), and antifibrinolytics; desmopressin is contraindicated in type 2B.
- **Aortic stenosis and Heyde syndrome:** high shear in aortic stenosis cleaves high-molecular-weight vWF multimers, producing acquired type 2A vWD with GI bleeding from angiodysplasia (Heyde syndrome).
- **Cardiovascular disease:** elevated vWF is a biomarker of endothelial dysfunction and predicts thrombotic events in [[Atherosclerosis]], stroke and myocardial infarction; low vWF relates to bleeding risk in thrombocytopenia.
- **Diabetes and chronic inflammation:** endothelial vWF is raised in [[Diabetes]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes]], contributing to microvascular thrombosis; [[Albuminuria]] and nephropathy accompany this endothelial activation.
- **Acquired vWD** also occurs in aortic stenosis, myeloproliferative neoplasms (acquired vWD type 1 with loss of high-molecular-weight multimers) and autoantibody-mediated forms.
- **Therapeutic frontiers:** recombinant vWF (vonvogens) and gene therapy approaches are in development; vWF-platelet GPIb interaction antagonists are explored for thrombotic indications.
