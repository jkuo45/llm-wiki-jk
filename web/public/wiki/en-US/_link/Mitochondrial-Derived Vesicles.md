# Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles

**Mitochondrial-derived vesicles (MDVs)** are small vesicles that bud from mitochondrial membranes and carry selected mitochondrial cargo — proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids — to lysosomes for degradation or to [[Extracellular Vesicles|extracellular vesicles]] for export. They provide a selective disposal route that operates alongside [[Mitophagy]] and can influence the release of [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|mitochondrial DAMPs]].

## Mechanism and Cargo Selection

MDV formation is a selective, cargo-specific process rather than a bulk fragmentation of the organelle. MDVs can deliver oxidized or damaged mitochondrial components to lysosomes, complementing [[Mitophagy]] when the mitophagic load exceeds capacity. Their formation and cargo selection involve mitochondrial quality-control machinery, including components of the [[PINK1]]–[[Parkin]] pathway and ESCRT-dependent membrane remodelling.

## Role in Inflammation and Ageing

By exporting selected mitochondrial cargo, MDVs can either limit DAMP release (when cargo is degraded lysosomally) or propagate inflammatory signalling (when cargo reaches the extracellular space). In ageing, declining [[Mitochondrial Quality Control]] and [[Lysosome|lysosomal]] function may shift the balance toward DAMP export, favouring persistent activation of [[TLR9]], the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING pathway]], and [[NLRP3]] and contributing to [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]].
