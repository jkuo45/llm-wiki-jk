# HO-1
**Heme oxygenase 1 (HO-1)** is a stress-inducible enzyme that catalyzes the rate-limiting step in heme degradation, producing [[biliverdin]], [[carbon monoxide]] (CO), and [[ferrous iron]] (Fe²⁺). It is a key cytoprotective enzyme induced by [[NRF2]].
## Protective Functions
- **Antioxidant**: Biliverdin is converted to [[Bilirubin]], a potent antioxidant
- **Anti-inflammatory**: CO has anti-inflammatory and vasodilatory effects
- **Cytoprotective**: Induced in response to [[Oxidative Stress]], [[Hypoxia]], and inflammation
## Regulation
HO-1 expression is primarily regulated by [[NRF2]] via the [[ARE|Antioxidant Response Element]]. [[Sulforaphane]] and other NRF2 activators upregulate HO-1.
## Catalytic Mechanism
HO-1 catalyzes the rate-limiting step in heme degradation, cleaving the porphyrin ring at the α-meso carbon bridge. The reaction requires molecular oxygen and [[NADPH]]-dependent [[Cytochrome P450 reductase]] as an electron donor. Three successive monooxygenation reactions convert heme (Fe³⁺-protoporphyrin IX) to [[Biliverdin IXα]], with concurrent release of [[Carbon monoxide]] (CO) and ferrous iron (Fe²⁺). Biliverdin is subsequently reduced to [[Bilirubin]] by [[Biliverdin reductase]].
## Cytoprotective Functions
Each product of HO-1 activity exerts distinct cytoprotective effects: (i) biliverdin and bilirubin are potent [[Antioxidant]] scavengers of [[Reactive Oxygen Species|peroxyl radicals]] at micromolar concentrations; (ii) CO functions as a [[Gasotransmitter]] with anti-inflammatory, anti-apoptotic, and vasodilatory properties, acting through soluble guanylyl cyclase and [[MAPK]] signaling; and (iii) Fe²⁺ induces [[Ferritin]] expression, which sequesters free iron and prevents [[Fenton chemistry]]-mediated [[Hydroxyl radical]] generation. Together, these activities confer robust protection against [[Oxidative Stress]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[Inflammation]].
## Regulation by Nrf2 and Therapeutic Induction
HO-1 is among the most highly inducible antioxidant enzymes, driven primarily by [[NRF2]] binding to the [[Antioxidant Response Element]] in its promoter. Induction occurs in response to diverse stimuli including [[Heme]], heavy metals, [[Nitric Oxide]], electrophiles, ultraviolet radiation, and [[Inflammatory cytokines]]. Pharmacological inducers of HO-1, such as hemin, cobalt protoporphyrin, [[Curcumin]], and [[Sulforaphane]], are investigated for therapeutic benefit in [[Diabetes]], [[Atherosclerosis]], ischemia-reperfusion injury, and [[Chronic Kidney Disease]].
## Connection to Adrenochrome and Oxidative Stress
In the context of the [[Adrenochrome Pathway]], HO-1 induction represents an adaptive antioxidant response to catecholamine-derived [[Redox Cycling]] and heme dysregulation. HO-1 activity reduces the availability of free heme that could otherwise catalyze adrenochrome formation via peroxidase-like reactions. Additionally, the CO produced by HO-1 may counteract [[Vasoconstriction]] induced by adrenochrome. The Nrf2-HO-1 axis constitutes a critical node at the intersection of heme metabolism, [[Aging]], and aminochrome toxicity.


## Role in erythrocyte biology and efferocytosis

HO-1 also marks the antiinflammatory, antioxidant macrophage phenotype reached after efferocytosis — including after uptake of PS-exposing [[Erythrocytes|Erythrocytes]] undergoing [[Eryptosis]].

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Efferocytosis of eryptotic cells downregulates proinflammatory cytokines and upregulates antiinflammatory and antioxidant factors, prominently HO-1; the same PS-mediated uptake of erythrocytes promotes recovery from experimental intracerebral hemorrhage, which is the basis for proposing accelerated eryptosis as protective in hemorrhagic stroke. Conversely, heme that escapes [[Hemopexin]]/[[Haptoglobin]] scavenging is itself a DAMP driving [[Necroptosis]], [[Ferroptosis]], [[NLRP3]] and [[Pyroptosis]] — so HO-1 induction is the buffer against that flood.
