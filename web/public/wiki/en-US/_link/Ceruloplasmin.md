# Ceruloplasmin

**Overview:** Ceruloplasmin is a **blue, copper-containing glycoprotein** of ~1065 amino acids (~151 kDa) that circulates in plasma at 20–60 mg/dL and is the most abundant copper protein in the body. It performs two distinct jobs: it is the principal **copper transport protein** of plasma, and it is the only known **iron**-oxidising enzyme (**ferroxidase**) in the body, converting Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺ so that iron can bind apotransferrin.

## Structure and domains

Ceruloplasmin belongs to the **multicopper oxidase (MCO)** family, which in vertebrates includes hephaestin and mossy-heart tyrosinase, and in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* includes FET3. The human protein is a **six-domain** (Cp1–Cp6) construct with the ancestral trimeric MCO core duplicated and fused:

| Domain | Region | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cp1 | 1–255 | Partial trimerisation domain; binds neither copper nor substrate |
| Cp2 | 256–507 | Binuclear **Cu(II)/Cu(III)** centre ("blue" site, His369, His375, His419, His423, Met440) plus a **type 2 "white" Cu(II)** — ferroxidase site with the Fe-binding ligands **Glu272, Glu935, His940, Asp1025** |
| Cp3 | 508–782 | Second trimerisation domain; structural linker |
| Cp4 | 783–1026 | Trinuclear cluster with **three type 1 "integral" copper atoms** (His-4 ligands) — the site that gives the protein its blue colour |
| Cp5 | 1027–1065 | Fragment of a further duplicated domain |
| Cp6 | part of Cp2/Cp4 junction | Provides the disulfide linking the two halves |

Ceruloplasmin is unusual among MCOs in containing **three type 1 "integral" copper ions** in the even-numbered domains, whereas other MCOs have one. It binds **6–7 copper atoms per molecule**, giving an absorbance maximum near 610 nm in the oxidised state.

> [!info] Names
> The name means "blue copper" (*cerulum*, sky-blue). "Serum copper" in clinical chemistry is measured indirectly as ceruloplasmin-bound copper; only a small fraction of total body copper is in plasma at any time, so low ceruloplasmin is a marker of copper deficiency rather than of copper overload.

## Mechanism of function

**Ferroxidase activity.** Fe²⁺ is loaded into Cp2's type 2 ("white") site, oxidised by O₂ to Fe³⁺, and released to apotransferrin. This is essential: transferrin binds **only Fe³⁺**, so ceruloplasmin sets the rate at which iron becomes available for transport. Cp2 and Cp4 copper sites are redox-cycled through the type 2 and type 1 centres during the two-electron transfer to oxygen.

**Copper handling.** Ceruloplasmin carries most plasma copper, delivers it to [[Ceruloplasmin|hephaestin]]-dependent export at the basolateral enterocyte membrane for transcellular copper absorption, and is itself internalised by hepatocytes via the asialoglycoprotein receptor (which recognises its desialylated N-glycans) — the main route of copper clearance from plasma.

**Acute-phase behaviour.** Ceruloplasmin is an **acute-phase reactant** driven by interleukin-6, rising 2–4-fold in inflammation, infection, pregnancy, and estrogen therapy. Plasma ceruloplasmin is therefore an unreliable single marker; it must be interpreted alongside CRP.

## Clinical relevance

| Disorder | Mechanism | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wilson disease** (ATP7B mutations) | Defective biliary copper excretion and impaired incorporation of copper into ceruloplasmin | Low ceruloplasmin, low serum copper, high urine copper, hepatic copper accumulation |
| **Aceruloplasminemia** (*CP* loss-of-function) | Absence of ferroxidase | Diabetes mellitus, retinal degeneration, neurological disease (dystonia, ataxia, tremor), basal ganglia and retinal iron overload, hepatic iron accumulation, hypogonadism; serum ceruloplasmin undetectable, low serum iron, very low transferrin saturation, high ferritin |
| **Iron deficiency** | Ferroxidase substrate (plasma Fe²⁺) depleted | Ferroxidase activity falls, so transferrin saturation falls further than expected — a direct functional test of copper status |
| [[Anemia]] of chronic disease | Inflammation-driven hepcidin traps iron in the macrophage reticuloendothelial system, starving the ferroxidase | Low transferrin saturation with normal-high ferritin |
| [[Neurodegeneration]] generally | Ferroxidase activity falls with age and in neurodegeneration | Reduced Cu activity has been reported in [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]], [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer's disease]], and ALS |
| [[Atherosclerosis]] | Ceruloplasmin is an antioxidant but also pro-oxidant | Low ceruloplasmin has been associated with increased cardiovascular risk; ceruloplasmin-derived free copper can oxidise LDL |
| [[Diabetes]] | Type 2 diabetes itself lowers ceruloplasmin independent of iron status | Contributes to a pro-oxidant plasma milieu |
| Iron overload syndromes | Secondary overload still requires ferroxidase | Ceruloplasmin falls in advanced hereditary hemochromatosis |

> [!warning] Low ceruloplasmin is not diagnostic of Wilson disease
> Ceruloplasmin is also low in [[Proteinuria|nephrotic syndrome]] (urinary loss), malnutrition, severe liver disease, [[Anemia|anemia]], and in heterozygotes for *ATP7B*. Conversely, ceruloplasmin is *normal or high* in most Wilson patients with intact liver synthetic function and acute inflammation, so a normal value does not exclude the diagnosis.

**Emerging neurobiology.** Brain ceruloplasmin is expressed predominantly by **astrocytes**, and astrocyte-specific ceruloplasmin expression is reported to be critical for iron handling and neuronal survival in retina and basal ganglia. This is the mechanistic bridge between systemic aceruloplasminemia and its neurological phenotype, and it is being pursued as a target in [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] and related disorders.

## Connections
- [[Iron]] — ceruloplasmin's ferroxidase activity is what makes Fe²⁺ loadable onto transferrin; without it, iron delivery to erythroid precursors and export fail.
- [[Transferrin]] — the sole acceptor of ceruloplasmin-oxidised Fe³⁺; the two proteins are functionally inseparable for systemic iron transport.
- [[Ferritin]] — ceruloplasmin activity determines how much iron is available for ferritin loading and storage.
- [[Copper]] — ceruloplasmin is the major copper-carrying protein of plasma and delivers copper for hephaestin-dependent export.
- [[Oxidative Stress]] — ceruloplasmin is an antioxidant copper oxidase, but its labile copper pool can become pro-oxidant and oxidise LDL.
- [[ApoE]] — ceruloplasmin-derived free copper oxidises LDL, and the two act on the same atherosclerosis substrate.
- [[Astrocytes]] — the main CNS source of brain ceruloplasmin; astrocyte ceruloplasmin loss causes retinal and basal ganglia neurodegeneration.
- [[Hepatocyte]] — hepatocytes clear plasma ceruloplasmin via the asialoglycoprotein receptor, the main route of copper elimination.
- [[Parkinson's Disease]] — brain ceruloplasmin activity falls in Parkinson's disease and in aging brains, contributing to nigral iron accumulation.
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]] — reduced ceruloplasmin ferroxidase activity has been reported in AD brain, linking it to the iron dysregulation literature.
- [[Diabetes]] — type 2 diabetes lowers ceruloplasmin independently of iron status, adding to oxidative stress.
- [[Oxidized LDL]] — ceruloplasmin's released copper is one route to LDL oxidation and atherogenesis.
- Iron metabolism — ceruloplasmin sits at the export step of the iron cycle, alongside hephaestin and hepcidin.
