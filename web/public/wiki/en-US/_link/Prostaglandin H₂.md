# Prostaglandin H2

**Prostaglandin H₂ (PGH₂)** is the prostaglandin endoperoxide produced when the peroxidase site of [[Cyclooxygenases|cyclooxygenase]] reduces the C15 hydroperoxide of [[Prostaglandin G₂]] to a hydroxyl group. It is the common precursor from which all terminal prostanoids are made.

## Formation and Chemistry

PGH₂ retains the bicyclic endoperoxide bridge (C9–C11) formed during the bis-dioxygenation of [[Arachidonic acid]], but has a C15 alcohol rather than a hydroperoxide. The reduction of PGG₂ to PGH₂ is the second catalytic step of COX-1/COX-2 and requires a reducing co-substrate — phenol or other hydroperoxide activators serve this role in vivo.

PGH₂ is chemically unstable and short-lived (half-life minutes at physiological pH), which is why it is never measured directly in clinical samples. It is either isomerized or reduced by terminal synthases within the same cell that produced it.

## Terminal Products

| Product | Enzyme | Principal actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[Prostaglandins\|PGE₂]] | PGE synthase | pain, fever, vasodilation, mucosal protection |
| PGD₂ | PGD synthase | allergy, sleep, bronchoconstriction |
| PGF₂α | PGF synthase | uterine contraction, luteolysis |
| [[Prostacyclin]] (PGI₂) | prostacyclin synthase | vasolilation, inhibition of platelet aggregation |
| [[Thromboxane A₂]] | thromboxane synthase | vasoconstriction, platelet aggregation |

The balance between [[Prostacyclin]] and [[Thromboxane A₂]] derived from the same PGH₂ pool is the central determinant of vascular thrombotic tendency. Endothelial cells preferentially make PGI₂; platelets preferentially make TXA₂.

## Relationship to Lipid Peroxidation

PGH₂ fragmentation is a recognized source of [[Malondialdehyde]], so prostanoid turnover contributes to measured MDA independent of free-radical [[Lipid Peroxidation]]. This is the reason basal MDA cannot be read as pure oxidative stress — pairing it with [[F2-Isoprostanes]] separates the two sources.

> [!info] Aspirin's selectivity
> Aspirin acetylates Ser530 of COX-1 irreversibly, blocking PGH₂ formation in platelets. Because platelets are enucleate and cannot resynthesize COX, low-dose aspirin suppresses platelet [[Thromboxane A₂]] for the platelet's ~10-day lifespan while endothelial [[Prostacyclin]] (COX-2-dependent and recoverable) is largely preserved. This is the mechanistic basis of low-dose aspirin prophylaxis.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Haemostasis**: platelet TXA₂ drives aggregation and vasoconstriction at injury sites.
- **Vascular homeostasis**: endothelial PGI₂ opposes aggregation and maintains blood flow.
- **Gastric mucosa**: PGE₂ and PGI₂ from PGH₂ stimulate mucus and bicarbonate secretion; NSAID gastropathy follows suppression of this PGH₂ flux.
- **Renal**: PGH₂-derived prostaglandins maintain glomerular filtration, especially under volume depletion.
- **Thrombosis**: COX-2 inhibitors raise cardiovascular risk partly by shifting the PGI₂/TXA₂ balance toward thrombosis.
