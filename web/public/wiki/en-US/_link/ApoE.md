# ApoE

**Overview:** Apolipoprotein E (ApoE) is a 34 kDa, 299-amino-acid lipoprotein of the exchangeable (precursor) apolipoprotein family. It is a key regulator of lipid transport in the plasma and the central nervous system, and it is the major genetic risk factor for late-onset [[Alzheimer's Disease|Alzheimer disease]].

## Structure and domains

ApoE has a two-domain architecture separated by a flexible, protease-sensitive hinge at ~residue 191–210:

- **N-terminal domain (residues 1–191)** — a four-helix bundle carrying the **receptor-binding region** (residues ~130–150) responsible for high-affinity binding to members of the [[Low-Density Lipoprotein|LDL receptor]] family.
- **C-terminal domain (residues 210–299)** — the major lipid-binding region, featuring a hydrophobic pocket lined by residues Leu264, Phe265, Ala267, Leu279, Leu284, Val286, and Val287. This domain is also prone to self-association, which drives the oligomeric, poorly-lipidated state seen in [[Astrocytes|astrocytes]].

> [!info] The isoform distinction is two residues
> All three common human isoforms differ only at positions **112 and 158**: apoE2 (Cys112, Cys158), apoE3 (Cys112, Arg158), apoE4 (Arg112, Arg158). Because Arg158 destabilizes salt bridges present in E2 and E3, apoE4 opens its C-domain, which improves lipid binding but reduces domain interaction — explaining its "poorly self-associating, better lipidated" phenotype.

## Mechanism and physiological role

1. **Lipoprotein clearance in the periphery.** ApoE is synthesized chiefly by hepatocytes and, in small amounts, by [[Macrophages|macrophages]] and [[Astrocytes|astrocytes]]. It loads onto nascent triglyceride-rich particles (VLDL, chylomicrons) and returned lipid is delivered to hepatocytes via the LDL receptor, LRP1, and VLDL receptor. ApoE2 is defective in LDL receptor binding (~50–100-fold weaker than E3/E4), causing type III hyperlipoproteinemia and dysbetalipoproteinemia.
2. **Reverse cholesterol transport in brain.** CNS ApoE is produced almost entirely by astrocytes and microglia. It solubilizes lipids and cholesterol released by dying neurons and myelin and shuttles them to neurons for reuse, mediated by LRP1 on the neuron side and the LDL receptor family on glia.
3. **Amyloid handling.** ApoE binds soluble Aβ and modulates its aggregation. Well-lipidated ApoE (E2 > E3 > E4) solubilizes Aβ and promotes clearance via LRP1-mediated uptake; poorly lipidated ApoE4 instead favours oligomerization and plaque formation.
4. **Antioxidant and immune functions.** ApoE is lipidated by ABCA1/ABCG1-dependent lipid efflux and carries antioxidant capacity; it also modulates [[Inflammation|inflammatory]] and [[TREM2]]-linked microglial responses.

## Isoform-specific pathology

**Cardiovascular disease.** ApoE4 homozygosity raises plasma LDL cholesterol and coronary artery disease risk; apoE-knockout mice are the standard atherosclerosis model. The isoform acts both through plasma lipoprotein levels and through plaque biology.

**Alzheimer disease.** APOE ε4 is the largest genetic risk factor for late-onset AD — roughly a 3-fold increase in odds per allele. Proposed mechanisms, none of which is established as primary:

- **Impaired Aβ clearance** via reduced astrocyte/microglia efflux and ABCA1 trafficking defects (partly ARF6-dependent).
- **Aβ seeding and oligomer stabilization** rather than plaque deposition.
- **Microglial dysfunction** — reduced P2RY12-guided motility toward plaques, pro-inflammatory shift, lipid droplet accumulation, dampened disease-associated microglia response.
- **Impaired [[Tau|tau]] clearance** and increased tau seeding/nephropathy.
- Blood-brain barrier and neurovascular effects.

**Other associations.** ApoE4 has been linked to [[Parkinson's Disease|Parkinson's disease]] risk, [[Multiple Sclerosis|multiple sclerosis]] severity, and ischemic stroke outcomes — these associations are weaker and more context-dependent.

> [!warning] Therapeutic translation has been disappointing
> ApoE has obvious appeal as a drug target, yet direct manipulation (including an antibody originally developed to clear apoE from blood) has produced lipid metabolic disturbances and hepatic toxicity without clinical benefit. Most current effort goes into **lipidation** — raising the lipidated fraction of astrocyte-derived ApoE via [[LXRα]]-mediated ABCA1 induction — rather than changing the isoform itself.

## Connections
- [[LDL]] — apoE delivers cholesterol-rich lipoproteins to the LDL receptor; the E4 isoform raises plasma LDL and cardiovascular risk.
- [[Astrocytes]] — the main source of CNS ApoE, and where ABCA1-dependent lipid efflux determines the lipidation state that governs amyloid clearance.
- [[Amyloid Beta]] — ApoE binds and modulates Aβ aggregation; poorly lipidated ApoE4 favours oligomers and plaques while well-lipidated ApoE promotes clearance.
- [[Alzheimer's Disease]] — APOE ε4 is the strongest common genetic risk factor and, as a downstream consequence, modifies anti-amyloid antibody response and ARIA risk.
- [[Microglia]] — CNS ApoE controls microglial lipid handling, plaque guidance, and the disease-associated microglia state.
- [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]] — ApoE is the central carrier of cholesterol efflux from glia back to neurons.
- [[LXRα]] — LXR agonism induces ABCA1 and increases apoE lipidation, the leading non-genetic strategy for apoE4.
- [[Iron]] and [[Copper]] — apoE deficiency alters copper and iron handling, and the resulting oxidative phenotype contributes to its endothelial effects.
- [[SIRT1]] — sirtuin activity is atheroprotective on an ApoE-deficient background, positioning SIRT1 and ApoE on the same cardiovascular axis.
