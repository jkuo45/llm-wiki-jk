# Vitamin C

**[[Vitamin C]]** (ascorbic acid, L-ascorbic acid) is a water-soluble vitamin and essential nutrient with potent antioxidant properties. It is the terminal water-phase reductant of the antioxidant network — the molecule that regenerates [[Vitamin E]] from its radical form at membrane interfaces and keeps the [[Glutathione]] pool reduced — and an obligate cofactor for [[Collagen]] synthesis.

## Structure and Redox Chemistry

Ascorbic acid is a six-carbon lactone bearing an enediol group (C2–C3 hydroxyls). That enediol donates one or two electrons at a low reduction potential (+0.06 V at pH 7 for the ascorbyl radical/ascorbate couple), making it a strong yet kinetically controlled reductant. Its one-electron oxidation product, the **ascorbate semiquinone radical**, is resonance-stabilized and unreactive enough to dismutate rather than propagate damage — the property that makes ascorbate an antioxidant rather than a pro-oxidant in most biological settings.

Because it is fully water-soluble and anionic at physiological pH, vitamin C partitions to the aqueous phase and the membrane–water interface. It cannot substitute for the lipid-phase chain-breaking role of [[Vitamin E]]; instead it works in series with it.

## Absorption, Transport, and Metabolism

Intestinal uptake is sodium-dependent and saturable, mediated by **SVCT1** (SLC23A1). Tissue uptake uses **SVCT2** (SLC23A2), which is highly expressed in the adrenal medulla, pituitary, and neurons — tissues that also concentrate catecholamines. Oxidized vitamin C (**[[Dehydroascorbate]]**) is taken up by GLUT transporters and reduced intracellularly, an important salvage route.

Humans lack L-gulonolactone oxidase and cannot synthesize ascorbate from glucose, so the vitamin is an essential dietary requirement. The corresponding gene is intact in most mammals and birds.

Plasma is tightly controlled near the renal threshold; tissue pools (adrenal, pituitary, brain, lens) are 10–100× plasma concentrations. Smokers, diabetics, and critically ill patients show markedly lower plasma ascorbate.

## Antioxidant Network

- **Recycles [[Vitamin E|α-tocopherol]]** from the α-tocopheroxyl radical at the membrane interface — the key link between the aqueous and lipid antioxidant pools, and the reason the two vitamins are synergistic rather than interchangeable.
- **Scavenges [[Superoxide Radicals|superoxide]], [[Hydroxyl Radicals|hydroxyl radicals]], [[Singlet Oxygen]], and hypochlorous acid** directly, at diffusion-limited rates for •OH.
- **Regenerates [[Glutathione]]** from GSSG via dehydroascorbate reductase and the glutaredoxin system, so ascorbate status and GSH status are coupled.
- **Spares other one-electron reductants**, including tetrahydrobiopterin, keeping [[Nitric Oxide Synthase]] uncoupling in check and preserving NO bioavailability.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - berrougui2009_resveratrol_as_atheroprotective_compound|Berrougui et al., Atherosclerosis 2009]]
> Water-soluble antioxidants such as vitamin C cannot enter the lipid phase to trap peroxyl radicals directly; they are instead spent recycling the α-tocopheroxyl radical. This phase complementarity is why edible oils carry [[Vitamin E]] while the surrounding aqueous compartments carry ascorbate.

## Cofactor Functions

Ascorbate maintains iron and copper in the reduced state required by several enzymes:

- **Prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases** in [[Collagen]] synthesis — the basis of scurvy, where underhydroxylated collagen fails to cross-link and connective tissue fragments.
- **Dopamine β-hydroxylase** — converts dopamine to [[Norepinephrine]] in catecholamine vesicles.
- **[[Carnitine]] biosynthesis** (trimethyllysine and γ-butyrobetaine hydroxylases) — links vitamin C status to fatty-acid transport into mitochondria.
- **Peptide amidation and steroidogenesis** in the adrenal cortex, which holds the body's highest ascorbate concentration.

## Catecholamine Protection

Ascorbate is co-localized with catecholamines in secretory vesicles, where it prevents auto-oxidation. It can "reverse" the oxidation of [[Epinephrine]] by donating electrons back to the reactive [[o-quinone]] intermediate, converting it back to stable epinephrine and blocking formation of [[Adrenochrome]] and its aminochrome downstream products. Vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2) loads both ascorbate and catecholamine into the same granule, so depletion of ascorbate shifts the balance toward quinone formation and [[Adrenochrome Hypothesis|adrenochrome-mediated damage]].

## Dual Pro-Oxidant Role

At pharmacological concentrations and in the presence of free transition metals, vitamin C can act as a pro-oxidant via the [[Fenton Reaction]] (Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ cycling with H₂O₂ → •OH). This underlies the rationale for high-dose intravenous ascorbate in [[Cancer]] therapy: millimolar plasma ascorbate generates [[Hydroxyl Radicals]] preferentially in tumor tissue, which has high labile iron and low catalase. Physiological dietary intake does not produce this effect — a pharmacology that is route- and dose-dependent.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Scurvy**: the classic deficiency — fatigue, petechiae, gingival bleeding, poor wound healing, perifollicular hemorrhage; reversed within days of repletion.
- **Immune support**: ascorbate concentrates in neutrophils and enhances chemotaxis, phagocytosis, and ROS generation during the respiratory burst; supplementation reduces cold duration modestly in the general population and more in those under physical stress.
- **Cardiovascular**: preserves NO bioavailability, reduces [[Oxidized LDL]] formation, and improves endothelium-dependent vasodilation; epidemiological intake associates inversely with cardiovascular risk, though supplementation trials are mixed.
- **Neurodegeneration**: brain ascorbate declines with age and is lower in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] and [[Parkinson's Disease]]; it protects against catecholamine quinone and lipid-peroxidation damage in the substantia nigra.
- **Iron absorption**: reduces dietary Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ at the enterocyte, substantially increasing non-heme iron uptake.

## Relationship to the Lipid-Peroxidation Axis

Vitamin C does not stop [[Lipid Peroxidation]] on its own — it is out of phase. Its contribution is regenerating the lipid-phase trap ([[Vitamin E]]) and keeping the [[Glutathione]]/[[GPX4]] system running. Ascorbate deficiency therefore amplifies lipid peroxidation indirectly: the tocopheroxyl radical is not recycled, membrane PUFA chains run to completion, and [[Malondialdehyde]], [[4-Hydroxynonenal]], and [[Acrolein]] accumulate.
