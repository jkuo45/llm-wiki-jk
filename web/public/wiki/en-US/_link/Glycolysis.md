# Glycolysis
Glycolysis is the metabolic pathway that converts glucose into pyruvate. A byproduct of this pathway is the formation of [[Methylglyoxal]], a potent precursor for [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]].

> [!warning] Parthanatos input
> Free [[PAR]] chains bind/inhibit [[Hexokinase-1]] via its PAR-binding motif (HK activity falls ~15 min post-MNNG, before [[NAD+]] loss; PARG-predigested PAR inactive; PBM-mutant HK1 resists) and drive HK1–[[VDAC]] dissociation, blocking glycolysis and PPP (NADPH/GSH fall). Pyruvate/α-ketoglutarate bypass rescues downstream mitochondrial failure. Full NAD+ salvage costs ~4 ATP per ADP-ribose; [[Nudix Hydrolases]]-derived AMP feeds AMPK–mTORC1 overlay. ## Glycolysis and Cancer
- **Metabolic Reprogramming:** Cancer cells rely on aerobic glycolysis to support rapid proliferation and biomass production.
- **Key Enzymes:** Enzymes such as [[Hexokinase 2]] (HK2), [[Phosphofructokinase]], and [[Pyruvate Kinase M2]] (PKM2) are frequently overexpressed.
- **Glucose Transport:** Up-regulation of glucose transporters like [[GLUT1]] increases [[Glucose uptake]].
- **Targeting Glycolysis:** Many anticancer strategies, including the use of [[Benzimidazole]] compounds like [[Fenbendazole]], aim to inhibit glycolysis to starve tumor cells.

## Glycolysis in erythrocytes

Mature [[Erythrocytes]] have no mitochondria, so glycolysis is their **only** source of ATP — a dependency created by the mitophagy that strips mitochondria during [[Erythropoiesis]]. Glucose enters through [[GLUT1]] and [[Hexokinase-1]] commits it; the pentose phosphate pathway hangs off the same hexose-6-phosphate pool to supply NADPH for the GSH/glutathione-reductase defence.

Because glycolytic ATP is the sole energy budget, the erythrocyte's death threshold is set by glycolytic flux:

- Glucose removal or [[2-deoxy-D-glucose]] → fast ATP loss → [[PKC|PKCα]] membrane translocation → serine phosphorylation of the Ca2+-permeable cation channel → cytosolic Ca2+ rise → [[Phosphatidylserine]] exposure and shrinkage, i.e. cation channel-driven [[Eryptosis]].
- [[AMPK]] senses the ATP/AMP deficit and restrains death (AMPKα-null mice: splenomegaly, severe anemia), so the ATP-depletion arm is opposed by an energy sensor rather than being a one-way trigger.
- Energy depletion also impairs Ca2+ extrusion itself (plasma-membrane Ca2+-ATPase and Na+/Ca2+ exchanger), adding to the cytosolic Ca2+ rise.

This makes glycolysis the hinge between energy metabolism and cation channel-driven eryptosis: no glycolysis, no ATP, no PKCα translocation, no Ca2+ influx, no PS exposure.
