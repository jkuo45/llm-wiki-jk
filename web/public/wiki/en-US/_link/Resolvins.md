# Resolvins

**Resolvins** (resolution-phase interaction products, Rv) are specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators (SPMs) derived from omega-3 [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids]] — eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA, 20:5) and [[Docosahexaenoic acid]] (DHA, 22:6). They actively terminate inflammation and promote tissue repair, rather than simply blocking the inflammatory cascade.

## Biosynthesis

Resolvins are produced by the sequential action of [[Lipoxygenase]] enzymes (5-LOX, 12-LOX, 15-LOX) and sometimes cyclooxygenase-2 acting on EPA or DHA, generating distinct series:

- **E-series resolvins (RvE1, RvE2, RvE3)** from EPA, via 18*S*-HEPE and 5-LOX
- **D-series resolvins (RvD1–RvD6)** from DHA, via 17*S*-HDHA and 5/12-LOX
- **Aspirin-triggered resolvins** — aspirin acetylated COX-2 generates 17*R*-HDHA precursors yielding AT-RvD1 and epimers with prolonged half-life

Structurally related DHA-derived mediators include **protectins/neuroprotectins** (PD/NPD1) and **maresins** (MaR1, from macrophage 12-LOX); these are often grouped with resolvins under the SPM umbrella.

## Mechanism of Action

Resolvins act through G-protein-coupled receptors at nanomolar concentrations:

- **RvE1** binds ChemR23 (CMKLR1) and BLT1 (antagonizing leukotriene B₄ signaling)
- **RvD1** binds ALX/FPR2 and GPR32
- **RvD2** signals via GPR18

Downstream effects include stopping neutrophil infiltration, promoting non-phlogistic monocyte recruitment, enhancing macrophage phagocytosis of apoptotic cells (efferocytosis), and stimulating tissue repair. This is the active "resolution" program — inflammation does not merely fade, it is deliberately switched off.

## Relationship to Lipid Peroxidation

Resolvins occupy the same chemical real estate as the products of [[Lipid Peroxidation]], but their formation is enzymatic and stereospecific. The same substrate — DHA and EPA — yields either cytotoxic aldehydes ([[4-Hydroxynonenal]], [[Acrolein]]) under unregulated radical attack, or pro-resolving mediators under tight enzymatic control.

> [!info] Two fates of the same PUFA
> [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids]] are the shared substrate. When [[Vitamin E]], [[GPX4]], and [[Glutathione]] are adequate, oxygenation is channeled through [[Lipoxygenase]] into SPMs. When defenses fail, [[Lipid Peroxidation]] proceeds non-enzymatically and generates aldehydes and [[F2-Isoprostanes]] instead. High n-3 intake raises the potential for *both* — the outcome depends on oxidative context.

Dietary EPA/DHA thus has a dual effect: it increases the substrate pool for peroxidation (raising [[Lipid hydroperoxide|LOOH]] potential) while simultaneously supplying the parent lipids for resolution signaling.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Inflammation**: resolvins are the endogenous "off switch"; their deficiency prolongs inflammation and contributes to chronic inflammatory disease.
- **Cardiovascular**: RvE1 and RvD1 limit atherosclerotic plaque inflammation and improve plaque stability.
- **Neurodegeneration**: neuroprotectin D1 (NPD1) is reduced in [[Alzheimer's Disease]] brain and protects retinal and neural tissue against oxidative injury.
- **Metabolic**: resolvins improve insulin sensitivity and resolve adipose-tissue inflammation in obesity models.
- **Infection**: resolvins enhance bacterial clearance while dampening collateral tissue damage.
- **Biosynthesis capacity**: omega-3 status, [[Lipoxygenase]] activity, and [[Aspirin]] use all determine SPM output; aspirin-triggered epimers are one reason low-dose aspirin has anti-inflammatory effects beyond platelet inhibition.
