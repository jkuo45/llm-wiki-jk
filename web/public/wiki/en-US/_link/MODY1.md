# MODY1

**MODY1** is **maturity-onset diabetes of the young type 1** — the diabetes
subtype caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in ***HNF4A***, the gene
encoding [[HNF4α]] (hepatocyte nuclear factor 4 alpha). It is a form of
**monogenic diabetes mellitus** (MODY), the autosomal dominant, non-autoimmune,
insulin-sensitive form of early-onset type 2-like diabetes.

## Nomenclature and classification

> [!warning] The MODY numbering is historical and inconsistent
> MODY subtypes are numbered by the order in which their genes were
> identified, so the numbers do not correspond to frequency. Also note
> that the modern genetic literature generally refers to the subtypes **by
> gene** (HNF4A-MODY, HNF1A-MODY, GCK-MODY) rather than by number, precisely
> because the numbering is confusing. Some sources use "MODY1" for HNF1A
> disease, following the older convention in which the first identified
> locus was *MODY1*; the modern convention — and the one used here — is
> **MODY1 = HNF4A**, and **MODY3 = HNF1A**.

Of the ~14 genetic subtypes described, three genes account for the large
majority of cases (roughly 95%): **HNF1A** (~50–60%, MODY3),
**GCK** (~30–40%, MODY2), and **HNF4A** (~5–10%, MODY1). **HNF1B**
(MODY5, associated with renal cysts and genital tract anomalies, sometimes
called MODY with renal agenesis) accounts for a further few percent. HNF4A
is the rarest of the three common subtypes, which matters for diagnostic
panels — a gene panel that omits HNF4A will miss MODY1.

## Pathophysiology

HNF4α is a **nuclear receptor** (NR2A1) that binds [[DNA]] as a
homodimer and acts as a pioneer factor, opening chromatin at its target
enhancers. It is a master regulator of **hepatocyte differentiation** and
of hepatic glucose and lipid metabolism, and — critically — it acts
upstream of, and transactivates, **[[HNF1A]]**.

> [!info] The upstream mechanism of MODY1 hyperglycaemia
> The dominant explanation is a **gene-regulatory cascade failure**, not a
> direct loss of glucose handling:
>
> 1. Loss of one HNF4A allele reduces HNF4α transcriptional output
>    in hepatocytes and in pancreatic β-cells.
> 2. HNF4α is a required activator of the *HNF1A* promoter. Reduced
>    HNF4α → reduced [[HNF1A]] expression.
> 3. HNF1A is itself a key transcriptional activator of a large
>    liver-restricted gene programme — the hepatic enhancer
>    landscape co-bound by HNF1A, HNF4A, [[FOXA2]] and [[CEBPB]],
>    which controls the enzymes of hepatic glucose handling:
>    [[Glucokinase]] (the β-cell and hepatocyte glucose sensor),
>    [[Glucose-6-Phosphatase]] (the final step of
>    gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis, and the site of the
>    fasting defect), [[GLUT2]]/[[SLC2A2]], [[PCK1]] (PEPCK),
>    [[SLC13A2]], and [[ABCC8]]/[[KCNJ11]] at the β-cell
>    (affecting the ATP-sensitive potassium channel and insulin
>    secretion threshold).
> 4. Net result: **reduced first-phase insulin secretion**, an
>    **impaired hepatic response to glucagon** with inappropriate
>    hepatic glucose production during fasting, and reduced
>    glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. Together these produce
>    **progressive, non-ketotic, non-autoimmune hyperglycaemia
>    that worsens over time** — insulin secretion progressively
>    fails and insulin therapy becomes necessary.

> [!info] The MODY1 macroglossia/neonatal hypoglycaemia phenotype
> A striking and mechanistically distinctive feature: HNF4A variants in the
> **P1/P2 promoter region** (the "birth-weight" locus) are associated with
> **low birth weight**, reduced neonatal insulin secretion and
> **transient neonatal hypoglycaemia**, and with **macroglossia** in some
> individuals. This links MODY1 to the **permanent neonatal diabetes**
> and **hyperinsulinism** literatures and suggests a broader
> HNF4A-related spectrum than simple adolescent-onset diabetes. The
> macroglossia association is not fully explained and should be
> treated as an observed correlation rather than a settled mechanism.

## Clinical presentation

- **Onset** typically in adolescence or early adulthood, though
  HNF4A-MODY has a *later* median age of diagnosis than HNF1A-MODY —
  often in the third or fourth decade — which means it is easily
  mistaken for type 2 diabetes. Mean age of onset in series ranges
  roughly from the late teens to about 30.
- **Non-ketotic, non-autoimmune** diabetes; islet autoantibodies are
  absent, distinguishing it from [[Type 1 Diabetes]].
- **Progressive** — unlike GCK-MODY (MODY2), which is stable and mild
  throughout life and typically requires no treatment, MODY1 worsens
  and insulin therapy is often eventually needed.
- **Mild fasting and postprandial hyperglycaemia**; ketosis is rare
  but possible with intercurrent illness.
- **Complications do occur.** In contrast to GCK-MODY, HNF4A-MODY
  carries a real risk of microvascular complications —
  [[Retinopathy]], [[Nephropathy]], and
  [[Neuropathy]] — so the "MODY is benign" framing is wrong for
  this subtype. Retinopathy rates are modest but non-trivial.
- **Extra-pancreatic features are uncommon** in HNF4A-MODY, unlike
  HNF1B-MODY (renal cysts, genital anomalies, Müllerian agenesis) and
  HNF1A-MODY (renal glycosuria, genital anomalies, sensitivity to
  sulfonylureas).
- **Extrahepatic insulin resistance** and, in some descriptions,
  dyslipidaemia and [[Nonalcoholic Steatohepatitis]]-like liver
  phenotypes.

## Management

> [!warning] The therapeutic and diagnostic point
> **MODY1 and HNF1A-MODY are highly sensitive to low-dose
> sulfonylureas** (glibenclamide, gliclazide, glipizide), because the
> defect is reduced insulin secretion from otherwise functional
> β-cells rather than absolute β-cell failure. This is the single
> most useful clinical consequence of a genetic diagnosis, and it
> avoids unnecessary insulin therapy in many patients. [[Metformin]]
> is generally less effective in MODY than in type 2 diabetes;
> [[GLP-1]] receptor agonists and [[SGLT2]] inhibitors are
> increasingly used but the evidence base is much smaller.
>
> **GCK-MODY (MODY2) is the opposite**: the glucose set-point is
> slightly raised but stable, complications do not develop, and
> pharmacological treatment is *not* indicated. Treating MODY2 as
> type 2 diabetes is a real and common harm.
>
> **Diagnosis matters commercially and reproductively**: MODY is
> autosomal dominant with 50% transmission, so genetic diagnosis
> enables genetic counselling, predictive testing of asymptomatic
> relatives, and — where parents are affected and carrier status is
> known — preimplantation genetic testing.
>
> A practical diagnostic clue: **HNF1A/HNF4A-MODY patients are
> unusually sensitive to sulfonylureas**, and low doses that
> would be inadequate in type 2 diabetes normalise their glucose.

## Molecular genetics

- **Gene**: *HNF4A*, chromosome **20q13.12**, encodes [[HNF4α]].
- **Inheritance**: autosomal dominant; penetrance is high (90–95%)
  for hyperglycaemia but the biochemical and clinical severity varies
  widely within families, with the common **p.Arg177Ter** variant
  showing variable expressivity and being associated with lower
  BMI at diagnosis.
- **Variants**: over 500 reported; most are in the ligand-binding
  and DNA-binding domains, but **P1/P2 promoter variants** form a
  functionally distinct group with the neonatal phenotype described
  above. **De novo** variants are more frequent in HNF4A than in
  HNF1A, which matters because a de novo variant excludes a
  family-history-based diagnosis.
- **Somatic mosaicism** has been reported, causing unusually
  severe disease.
- Note that *HNF4A* is itself a **[[Sirtuins|sirtuin]]-regulated
  gene**: HNF4α activity is modulated by [[SIRT1]]
  deacetylation and by [[AMPK]]-driven [[NAD+]] metabolism,
  which is a mechanistic reason this note sits in a vault concerned
  with NAD+ biology.

## Connections

- [[HNF4α]] — The gene product mutated in MODY1. HNF4α is a pioneer
  nuclear receptor that opens chromatin at hepatic enhancers and
  directly transactivates [[HNF1A]], so the HNF4A → HNF1A →
  hepatic enzyme cascade is the whole mechanism of the disease.
- [[Hepatocyte]] — The cell type in which the HNF4A defect has its
  largest transcriptional effect, and where the fasting
  hyperglycaemia is generated.
- [[Diabetes Mellitus]] — MODY is a monogenic subtype of diabetes;
  distinguishing it from type 1 and type 2 changes treatment
  entirely.
- [[Glucagon]] — The paradoxical aspect of MODY1 is that the
  fasting hyperglycaemia is driven by **inadequate suppression of
  hepatic glucose production by glucagon**, so the HNF4A–HNF1A axis
  also governs glucagon-responsive transcription in liver.
- [[Hepatocellular Carcinoma]] — Reduced HNF4A transcriptional
  output is one of the mechanisms proposed for loss of hepatic
  differentiation and liver cancer risk, and the hepatic enhancer
  programme HNF4α controls is a genuine tumour-suppressor axis.
- [[Nonalcoholic Steatohepatitis]] — HNF4A variants and hepatic
  steatosis are associated, and HNF4α is a direct regulator of
  hepatic lipid metabolism, so the HNF4A–MASLD relation is
  mechanistically coherent.
- [[Insulin]] — The disease is an insulin-secretory defect first
  and foremost; MODY1 progresses to insulin deficiency.
- [[Epigenetics]] — HNF4α is a pioneer factor whose function is
  fundamentally about chromatin accessibility, which links this
  monogenic disease to the vault's epigenetics material.
- [[Sirtuins]] and [[SIRT1]] and [[NAD+]] — HNF4α activity is
  regulated by SIRT1 deacetylation in a NAD+-dependent manner, and
  by AMPK; this is the mechanistic reason NAD+-boosting interventions
  could in principle influence HNF4A output.
- [[NAD+]] — upstream of the SIRT1/AMPK regulation of HNF4α
  described above; the mechanistic bridge to the vault's NAD+
  biology.
- [[Lipid Metabolism]] — HNF4α is a central regulator of hepatic
  lipid metabolism, and its dysregulation connects MODY1 to the
  dyslipidaemia and steatosis reported in HNF4A-MODY.
