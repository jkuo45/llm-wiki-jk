# ITM2C

## Overview

ITM2C (integral membrane protein 2C) is an endosomal/lysosomal membrane protein reported as the docking receptor for a subpopulation of [[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles|mitochondrial-derived vesicles]] carrying [[MFF]]. On damaged lysosomes, vesicle-associated [[Miro|MIRO2]] engages ITM2C; the delivered MFF then recruits [[DRP1]] to the lysosomal surface for budding-type fission. The evidence is cell-based (Tier 3) and derives from a 2026 *Nature Cell Biology* study.

> [!info]
> The MIRO2–ITM2C–MFF–DRP1 axis couples mitochondrial cargo selection to remodelling of the recipient lysosome. Neither the vesicle donor nor the lysosome is destroyed in the exchange.

## Mechanism

MFF-bearing vesicles bud from mitochondria without loss of organelle identity, travel on microtubules, and dock through MIRO2–ITM2C. MFF is a ~38 kDa outer-membrane adaptor that recruits the ~80 kDa mechanochemical GTPase DRP1. Budding-type fission releases daughter lysosomes estimated at 100–300 nm. This route is non-degradative: it changes the lysosomal network rather than delivering cargo for hydrolysis.

## Unresolved Questions

Whether ITM2C is the sole receptor for MFF-bearing vesicles, and whether the same docking mechanism operates in chronic disease states, remains untested. The fusion machinery for STX17-negative vesicle populations is not identified.
