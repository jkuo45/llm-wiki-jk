# Withania somnifera

**Withania somnifera** (ashwagandha) is a plant of the Solanaceae family whose roots and leaves produce withanolide steroidal lactones. Its primary bioactive is [[Withaferin A]]; other minor withanolides are also present, with preclinical activity only. Withaferin A mediates most of the plant's reported activities: anti-inflammatory via covalent modification of [[IKKbeta]] and [[Vimentin]]; anti-fibrotic via [[SIRT3]]; and pro-apoptotic in cancer models.

## Key bioactives

- [[Withaferin A]]: The principal withanolide; see its note for the mechanisms of its SIRT3-dependent anti-fibrotic activity and other effects.
- Minor withanolides: Minor composites from root and leaf, with limited preclinical data.

## Anticancer activity (preclinical, Tier 4)

- [[TRAIL]] sensitisation: Withaferin A increases cFLIP degradation, lowering the anti-apoptotic barrier to TRAIL-mediated apoptosis in renal carcinoma cells (Henrich et al., Cell Death Dis 2015, PMID 25719250; 17β-hydroxywithanolide E-based TRAIL sensitiser structure-activity study, J Med Chem 2017).
- FOXO3a/BIM-dependent apoptosis: Withaferin A induces intrinsic mitochondrial apoptosis through FOXO3a-mediated BIM expression in cancer models; it also inhibits Hsp90.
- Viwithan standardized root extract: B16F1 IC50 220 µg/mL, more active than other cell lines tested (Sudeep 2017, Phc Mag).
- Fractional cytotoxicity on Ca9-22 oral cancer cells: root fraction CC50 values 51.8/40.1 µg/mL (ethanol/EA/butanol series) (Hosami 2024, Front Pharmacol).
- Aqueous root extract raised caspase-8/9/3 and lowered glutathione in THP-1 cells without toxicity to PBMC (Mandal 2018, PMC5891897).

## Clinical data

- Phase I in advanced-stage osteosarcoma: withaferin A doses 72–216 mg/day, tolerated, no dose-limiting toxicity; no detectable plasma levels (Pires et al., 2021, PMID 30904387).
- Phase 1/2 of withaferin A + liposomal doxorubicin in recurrent ovarian cancer (NCT05610735).
- One human breast cancer trial (non-randomised): 2 g ashwagandha twice daily during chemotherapy; quality-of-life gain only (Biswal et al., Int J Cancer, PMID 23142798).
