# uPA/uPAR

**The uPA/uPAR system** comprises the serine protease [[uPA]] (urokinase plasminogen activator) and its GPI-anchored receptor [[uPAR]] (CD87, PLAUR). Together they convert [[Plasminogen]] to [[Plasmin]] at a cell-surface "cockpit", coupling fibrinolysis to cell migration, extracellular matrix remodelling and, through receptor trafficking, to gene expression.

> [!info] Note on naming
> This note covers the uPA/uPAR axis as a unit. The component entities are documented separately as [[uPA]] and [[uPAR]].

## Overview

The uPA/uPAR system couples proteolysis to signalling. The serine protease uPA, tethered to the GPI-anchored receptor uPAR, generates plasmin at the cell surface; uPAR occupancy simultaneously recruits integrins and formyl peptide receptors to drive migration and matrix remodelling, and internalized receptor is routed to the lysosome to engage mTORC1 and reshape transcription. Because both components are already documented individually in this vault, this note treats them as one functional axis.

## Structure & Domains

- **uPA:** a 54 kDa single-chain pro-serine protease (pro-uPA) with an N-terminal growth factor domain (binding [[uPAR]]), a kringle domain and a C-terminal catalytic domain; limited proteolysis converts it to the active two-chain form. [[Plasminogen]] also carries a C-terminal lysine-binding site (kringle) and binds to uPAR via its kringle, so plasminogen is recruited alongside uPA.
- **uPAR:** a GPI-anchored protein with three domains — D1 binds uPA, D2 binds vitronectin (linking the receptor to [[Extracellular Matrix]] adhesion), D3 carries the GPI anchor. It has no intrinsic enzymatic activity; signalling is transduced through co-receptors (integrins αvβ3, α5β1; formyl peptide receptors FPRL1/2).
- **Inhibitors:** plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 ([[PAI-1]]) blocks both uPA and tPA; α2-antiplasmin inhibits plasmin.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

1. uPA binds uPAR D1; pro-uPA and plasminogen co-receptor assemble on the cell surface, so plasminogen activation is concentrated where it is needed.
2. Generated [[Plasmin]] cleaves fibrin (fibrinolysis) and activates pro-matrix metalloproteinases ([[MMP-3]], [[MMP-9]]), degrading [[Extracellular Matrix]] and releasing matrix-bound growth factors.
3. **uPAR signalling:** occupancy of uPAR with uPA triggers integrin co-signalling, FAK activation, Rac1/RhoA remodelling, and MAPK and PI3K–AKT survival signals; the uPAR D2–D3 cleaved fragment (uPAR[84-95]) is a chemotactic signal through FPRL1.
4. **Lysosomal-transcription axis:** internalized uPA–uPAR is routed to the lysosome, where the uPA–uPAR complex recruits and activates [[mTORC1-Lysosome Compartment|mTORC1 on the lysosomal surface]] via PI3K-dependent re-synthesis of PI(4)P. Transcriptional output includes [[NF-κB]] target genes and, in senescent cells, the proteolytic arm of the SASP.

## Physiological Function

- **Fibrinolysis:** uPA is the dominant plasminogen activator in most tissues, in contrast to [[Tissue Plasminogen Activator|tPA]] which dominates in the circulation; together they clear fibrin from vessels and healing wounds.
- **Cell migration and tissue repair:** uPAR is a classical marker of migratory cells; the vitronectin–uPAR–integrin interaction anchors cells on matrix during wound healing.
- **Immune cell behaviour:** monocytes, neutrophils and macrophages express uPAR, which supports extravasation and tissue infiltration during [[Inflammation]].
- **Homeostasis of proteolysis:** spatial confinement of plasmin generation protects bystander tissue from indiscriminate proteolysis.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Cancer:** high uPA and low PAI-1 (the "uPA/PAI-1 ratio") is one of the most validated prognostic markers in breast cancer, and uPA/uPA-receptor levels guide adjuvant therapy decisions. The system drives invasion, [[Metastasis]] and, in [[Tumor Microenvironment|tumour microenvironments]], the pro-migratory, matrix-degrading arm of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype; soluble uPAR (suPAR) is a biomarker of prognosis and of immune activation.
- **Fibrinolysis and thrombosis:** suppressed uPA activity (high PAI-1) contributes to hypofibrinolytic states and thrombotic risk, including in [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Metastasis]].
- **Inflammatory disease:** uPAR shedding from the neutrophil surface promotes fibrinolysis and drives tissue damage in [[Inflammation]] and [[Sepsis]].
- **Therapeutics:** uPA inhibitors (upamostat), PAI-1 inhibitors, and uPAR-directed antibodies/antibody–drug conjugates (e.g. B7-H3/uPAR ADC) are in clinical development.
