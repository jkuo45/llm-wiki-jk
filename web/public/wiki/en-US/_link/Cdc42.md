# Cdc42

**Cdc42** (cell division cycle 42) is a small GTPase of the Rho family, sibling to [[Rac1]] and [[RhoA]], that acts as a master regulator of cell polarity. In its GTP-bound form it switches on downstream effectors that rebuild the [[Actin]] cytoskeleton, and it is a required component of [[NADPH Oxidase]] assembly in phagocytes. Its dysregulation is seen in immunodeficiency, in cancer invasion, and in developmental disorders.

## Structure & Domains

Cdc42 is a 191-residue small GTPase with the canonical Ras-like fold plus:

- **C-terminal CAAX motif** (Cys-Val-X) that is geranylgeranylated and anchors the protein to the inner face of the plasma membrane.
- **Switch I and switch II regions** that change conformation between the GDP- and GTP-bound states and create the binding surface for effectors.
- **Insertion loop**, a short non-conserved segment that distinguishes Cdc42 from other Rho GTPases and confers its distinctive exchange-factor specificity.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Nucleotide cycle.** Cdc42 is switched on by GDP/GTP exchange factors (Dbl-family GEFs such as WASP-interacting protein and p115-RhoGEF) and switched off by RhoGAPs, which accelerate hydrolysis. Active, GTP-bound Cdc42 exposes the switch regions.
- **WAVE and WASP effectors.** The WAVE complex (WAVE1–3) is activated by Cdc42 (and Rac1) and promotes [[Actin]] nucleation through the Arp2-3 complex; filopodia and lamellipodia depend on this axis. Conversely, Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome protein (WASP) links Cdc42 to actin in haematopoietic cells, explaining the actinopathy of Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome.
- **PAK effectors.** Cdc42 and Rac1 activate PAK family kinases — [[PAK1]], [[PAK4]] — which phosphorylate targets including [[ROCK]] and the myosin light-chain kinase pathway, coordinating protrusion, contractility and [[Cell Migration]].
- **NADPH oxidase assembly.** In neutrophils and macrophages, Cdc42 (with Rac1) binds the cytosolic activator [[p67phox]], which together with [[p47phox]] and [[p22phox]] translocates to the membrane to complete the catalytic NOX2 complex and trigger the [[Respiratory Burst]]. Cdc42 is also required for the localised ROS needed for killing engulfed microbes.
- **Cytoskeletal polarity.** Cdc42 establishes the front–rear polarity of migrating cells and the orientation of the primary cilium, working with [[Rac1]], [[RhoA]] and the [[Cytoskeleton]] as a whole.

## Physiological Function

- Establishment of cell polarity in migrating cells, epithelial morphogenesis and neurite outgrowth.
- Filopodia and membrane ruffles, the substrate for macropinocytosis, phagocytosis and invadopodia.
- Immune-cell function: neutrophil chemotaxis, degranulation, and the oxidative burst; dendritic-cell and lymphocyte synapse formation.
- Endocytosis and trafficking, partly via effects on the actin-driven steps of [[Endocytosis]].

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Wiskott-Aldrich syndrome** and related actinopathies: dominant-negative Cdc42 mutants cause thrombocytopenia, immunodeficiency and impaired cytoskeletal organisation, mirroring WASP deficiency.
- **Cancer.** Overexpressed or hyperactive Cdc42 promotes proliferation, migration and invasion; elevated nuclear Cdc42 and CDC42 mutations are reported in melanoma and other tumours, and Cdc42-driven [[Metastasis]] depends on WAVE/PAK signalling.
- **Neurodegeneration.** Altered Cdc42 signalling is implicated in tauopathy and in cytoskeletal failure, connecting to [[Neurodegeneration]].
- **Therapeutic targets.** Small-molecule Cdc42 inhibitors (Rhosin, ML141) are used as research tools; PAK and WAVE inhibition are explored in fibrotic and malignant disease.
