# Tocotrienols

**Tocotrienols** are the four naturally occurring forms of [[Vitamin E]] carrying an unsaturated 16-carbon isoprenoid (farnesyl) tail with three *trans* double bonds: **α-, β-, γ-, and δ-tocotrienol**. They share the chromanol ring with [[Tocopherols]] and trap lipid peroxyl radicals by the same phenolic hydrogen donation, but the unsaturated tail changes their membrane behavior and gives them biological activities that α-tocopherol lacks.

## Structure and Distribution

The chromanol head is identical to the corresponding tocopherol — the ring methylation pattern (tri-, di-, or mono-methyl) defines the α/β/γ/δ designation exactly as in [[Tocopherols]]. The distinguishing feature is the tail: an unsaturated farnesyl chain rather than a saturated phytyl chain. This makes tocotrienols more planar and more mobile within the lipid bilayer, allowing faster lateral diffusion and more efficient quenching of lipid peroxyl radicals in some membrane systems.

Natural distribution is narrow and oil-specific:

| Source | Tocotrienol content | Predominant form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annatto seed | ~90% of tocochromanols | δ, γ |
| Rice bran oil | 25–40% of tocochromanols | γ, α |
| Palm oil (crude) | 25–35% | α, γ |
| Barley | 10–25% | α |
| Wheat germ, sunflower, olive | trace | — |

Because [[Alpha-tocopherol]] is preferentially retained by α-tocopherol transfer protein (α-TTP), tocotrienols are poorly maintained in plasma even when dietary intake is high — a kinetic discrimination rather than evidence of lesser importance.

## Mechanism of Action

**Radical-trapping antioxidant activity.** Tocotrienols donate the chromanol phenolic hydrogen to [[Lipid Peroxyl Radical|lipid peroxyl radicals]] with rate constants comparable to or exceeding those of the corresponding tocopherols in phospholipid bilayers. The unsaturated tail keeps the molecule mobile in the membrane, so it can intercept radicals over a wider lateral radius.

**HMG-CoA reductase downregulation.** Independently of their antioxidant function, tocotrienols suppress cholesterol biosynthesis by accelerating degradation of HMG-CoA reductase — the same rate-limiting enzyme targeted by [[Statins]]. This post-transcriptional mechanism operates at physiological concentrations and is more pronounced for γ- and δ-tocotrienol than for α-tocopherol.

**NF-κB and STAT3 suppression.** Tocotrienols inhibit IκB kinase activation and STAT3 phosphorylation, reducing transcription of [[TNF-alpha]], IL-6, [[VCAM-1]], and COX-2. δ-Tocotrienol is the most potent isoform in most of these assays.

**Anticancer selectivity.** Tocotrienols suppress proliferation and induce apoptosis in malignant cells at concentrations that spare normal cells, attributed to preferential uptake via lipoprotein-independent pathways (transferrin receptor-mediated endocytosis) and to higher prenylated Ras/Raf signaling dependence in transformed cells. This is distinct from the [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox|mixed results]] of high-dose α-tocopherol trials.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Cardiovascular**: tocotrienols lower LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, improve arterial compliance, and reduce [[Oxidized LDL]] formation; randomized trials show consistent lipid-lowering with 150–300 mg/day.
- **Neuroprotection**: δ-tocotrienol protects against glutamate-induced excitotoxicity and stroke injury in preclinical models, partly by suppressing 12/15-[[Lipoxygenase]]-mediated [[Lipid Peroxidation]].
- **Metabolic**: improved insulin sensitivity and reduced hepatic steatosis in rodent models, linked to AMPK activation and reduced [[SREBP-1c]]-driven lipogenesis.
- **Senescence**: tocotrienols reduce markers of [[Cellular Senescence]] and the [[SASP]] in endothelial and fibroblast models.
- **Bioavailability**: absorption requires dietary fat and is improved by self-emulsifying formulations; plasma levels remain low relative to α-tocopherol due to α-TTP selectivity and CYP4F2 ω-hydroxylation.

> [!info] Vitamin E activity units undervalue tocotrienols
> Historical IU values assign tocotrienols only 30% of α-tocopherol activity, which reflects reproductive-endpoint assays in rats, not antioxidant capacity or the non-antioxidant effects above. Functional assays routinely place γ- and δ-tocotrienol above α-tocopherol for lipid-lowering and antiproliferative endpoints.
