# GDAP1

## Overview

GDAP1 (ganglioside-induced differentiation-associated protein 1) is a mitochondrial protein that promotes a distinct class of [[Mitochondria-Lysosome Contact Site|mitochondria–lysosome contacts]] through interaction with the lysosomal membrane protein [[LAMP1]]. Its discovery as a contact component established that mitochondrial–lysosome tethering is not mediated solely by the RAB7 system. GDAP1-related contacts are defective in Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease, linking contact-site composition to peripheral axon integrity.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> GDAP1 promotes mitochondria–lysosome tethering through interaction with the lysosomal membrane protein [[LAMP1]]. GDAP1-dependent contacts constitute specialized redox-sensitive microdomains, raising the possibility that altered contact-site chemistry, in addition to altered contact abundance, contributes to GDAP1-associated axonopathy.

## Mechanism at Contacts

The GDAP1–LAMP1 interaction provides a physical bridge between the mitochondrial and lysosomal membranes. Evidence from Charcot–Marie–Tooth models indicates that distinct GDAP1 clinical variants affect contact sites differentially. The finding that GDAP1 contacts possess redox-sensitive microdomains implies local control of redox state at the interface rather than a purely structural tethering role. Whether GDAP1–LAMP1 and RAB7-dependent contacts are molecularly distinct populations, partially overlapping machineries, or alternative regulatory states of the same membrane interface is unknown.

## Clinical Relevance

Mutations in GDAP1 cause the axonal form Charcot–Marie–Tooth type 2K (CMT2K), the most common GDAP1-related presentation, and the demyelinating form CMT4A. Mitochondria–lysosome membrane contacts are defective in GDAP1-related CMT. In the review's framework, these observations support a mechanism in which loss of contact-mediated redox organization, rather than mitochondrial bioenergetic failure alone, contributes to axonal degeneration.

## Unresolved Questions

The mitochondrial binding partner on the lysosomal side beyond LAMP1, and the precise redox reactions organized at GDAP1 contacts, are not specified in the review. Whether contact abundance or contact chemistry is the primary determinant of axonopathy remains unresolved.
