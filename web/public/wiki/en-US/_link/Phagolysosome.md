# Phagolysosome

**Phagolysosome** is the terminal compartment of the phagocytic pathway: the fusion product of a [[Phagosome]] with one or more [[Lysosome|Lysosomes]]. It is a single acidic, degradative, ROS-rich space in which engulfed microbes are killed and broken down and in which the resulting peptides are loaded onto [[MHC type II|MHC class II]] for [[Antigen Presentation]]. It is functionally distinct from the [[Autolysosome]], even though both are "phagosome/vesicle + lysosome" hybrids.

## Structure & Domains

A phagolysosome is defined by its membrane composition and lumenal chemistry rather than by a unique protein:

- **Membrane.** A single membrane enriched in the lysosomal proteins [[LAMP1]]/[[LAMP2]] and the V-ATPase; lipid composition shifts towards cholesterol and sphingolipid as the compartment matures.
- **Lumen.** pH ≈ 4.5–5, maintained by [[V-ATPase]]; high concentrations of lysosomal hydrolases — [[Cathepsins]] (cysteine cathepsins B, L, K; aspartyl cathepsin D), acid phosphatase, glycosidases, and RNases.
- **Reactive species.** Superoxide and hydrogen peroxide generated by the assembled [[NADPH Oxidase]] on the phagosomal membrane, plus iron-dependent Fenton chemistry and lysosomal ROS.
- **Membrane repair machinery.** The phagosome is a target for damage and is repaired by lysosomal exocytosis and by membrane-sealing pathways; the [[Phagocytic Lysosome Reformation]] pathway restores the phagolysosome after damage.

## Mechanism of Action

1. **Phagosome maturation.** After engulfment, the phagosome fuses with early endosomes (via [[Rab5]]) and then with lysosomes (via [[Rab7]]), acidifies and acquires lysosomal membrane proteins — this is the "phagolysosome".
2. **Oxidative kill.** In neutrophils and macrophages, the NADPH oxidase complex assembles on the phagosomal membrane and generates superoxide, which is converted to hydrogen peroxide; combined with lysosomal hydrolases and defensins this kills the pathogen. Phagosome maturation and ROS are interdependent.
3. **Proteolysis and antigen processing.** Cathepsins degrade the pathogen and its proteins into peptides; MHC class II molecules traffic through the compartment, being loaded with peptides and then exported to the cell surface for CD4⁺ T-cell activation.
4. **Lysis and recycling.** After digestion, residual material is expelled by exocytosis or degraded further; the damaged membrane is repaired and the organelle is rebuilt by lysosome reformation, which recycles lysosomal components rather than requiring new synthesis.

## Physiological Role

The phagolysosome is the antimicrobial effector compartment of [[Neutrophils]] and [[Macrophage|Macrophages]] and of tissue-resident phagocytes such as the [[Kupffer Cell]]. Beyond killing microbes, it is a major site of protein and lipid turnover, of antigen presentation to the adaptive immune system, and of clearance of apoptotic cells via [[Efferocytosis]]. It also participates in autophagy-related turnover: the [[Autophagosome-lysosome fusion|autophagosome–lysosome]] fusion product (the [[Autolysosome]]) is the analogous degradative compartment for intracellular cargo.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Infection.** *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* blocks phagosome maturation and acidifies the compartment only partially, surviving in the phagolysosome; [[Legionella]] pneumophila hijacks the phagosome and forms its own replication niche, escaping into the cytosol. Both illustrate how pathogens neutralise the phagolysosome.
- **Chronic granulomatous disease.** Defective NADPH oxidase (e.g. NOX2 or p47phox mutations) abolishes the oxidative burst, so phagolysosomes fail to kill catalase-positive organisms — a direct link from [[NADPH Oxidase]] to recurrent infection and granuloma formation.
- **Lysosomal storage diseases.** Failure of lysosomal degradation in phagolysosomes causes substrate accumulation, as in Gaucher disease and in the lysosomal storage diseases generally.
- **Inflammation and cell death.** Lysosomal membrane permeabilisation releases cathepsins into the cytosol, which can trigger [[Autophagic Cell Death]] or apoptosis; persistent phagolysosomal activation drives the chronic inflammation of arthritis, atherosclerosis and [[Inflammaging]].
- **Antigen presentation failure.** Defects in phagolysosomal maturation (as in Hermansky–Pudlak or Chediak–Higashi syndrome) impair MHC class II loading and produce combined immunodeficiency.
