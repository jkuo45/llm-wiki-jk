# Mitochondrial RNA

**Mitochondrial RNA (mtRNA)** comprises the transcripts of the [[Mitochondrial DNA|mitochondrial genome]] — mRNAs, rRNAs, and tRNAs — produced by [[Mitochondrial Translation|mitochondrial transcription and translation]]. Under homeostatic conditions mtRNA is compartmentalized within the [[Mitochondrial Matrix|mitochondrial matrix]] and efficiently processed. Mitochondrial stress can expose mtRNA species, including double-stranded RNA, to innate immune sensors, where they act as [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)]].

## Immunogenicity

Because mitochondria retain bacterial ancestry, their nucleic acids carry microbe-like features. Exposed mtRNA can engage antiviral-like pathways, reinforcing the concept that damaged mitochondria mimic microbial signatures and activate [[Innate Immune System|innate immunity]] even in the absence of infection. mtRNA therefore contributes an additional layer of immunogenicity alongside [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]].

## Release Mechanisms

mtRNA escapes when mitochondrial membranes become permeable during stress, apoptosis-associated [[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]], [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition|mitochondrial permeability transition (MPT)]], or defective [[Mitochondrial Quality Control|quality control]]. Release is favoured when [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] fails and damaged mitochondria persist, or when cargo is actively packaged into [[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles|mitochondrial-derived vesicles]] and [[Extracellular Vesicles|extracellular vesicles]]. In ageing, declining quality control and accumulation of damaged organelles facilitate persistent nucleic-acid leakage and [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]].
