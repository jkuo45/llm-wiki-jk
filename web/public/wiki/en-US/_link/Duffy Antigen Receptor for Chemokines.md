# Duffy Antigen Receptor for Chemokines

**Duffy antigen receptor for chemokines** (DARC, ACKR1/ACKM1) is a seven-transmembrane chemokine receptor on the erythrocyte surface. It binds a broad set of inflammatory chemokines (notably CXCL1/IL-8, CXCL2, CCL2/MCP-1) and ferries them into the red cell, effectively acting as a chemokine sink.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> By scavenging chemokines, erythrocytes change their availability in plasma and thereby regulate immune cell migration — one of the non-canonical immune functions that make red cells immunologically active. DARC is simultaneously the classic *Plasmodium vivax* / *P. knowlesi* invasion receptor, which explains the Fy-negative (Duffy-null) enrichment of these species in African populations.

## Mechanism

- Chemokine binding to the erythrocyte surface sequesters CXC and CC chemokines away from leukocyte receptors, blunting local leukocyte recruitment and systemic inflammatory tone.
- Duffy antigen also determines Fy(a/b) blood group serology; absence (Fy*⁰) abolishes the receptor entirely.
- Evolutionarily this is dual-use by the parasite: DARC is the invasion ligand for *P. vivax*/*P. knowlesi*, whereas *P. falciparum* uses other receptors.
