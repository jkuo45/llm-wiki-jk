# HDL3

**HDL3** is the smaller, denser subfraction of high-density lipoprotein, defined classically by hydrated density (1.063–1.21 g/mL) and by apolipoprotein composition. It comprises nascent discoidal particles formed from lipid-free [[Apolipoprotein A1|apolipoprotein A-I]] and phospholipid, plus cholesteryl ester-poor spherical intermediates. As these particles mature and accumulate cholesteryl ester they become larger and less dense, transitioning into the HDL2 subfraction.

## Structure & Subfractions

HDL is operationally separated into two major subfractions by ultracentrifugation or by agarose gel electrophoresis:

- **HDL3** — small, dense, protein- and phospholipid-rich; the dominant *nascent* particle, and the main acceptor of cholesterol and phospholipid effluxed from peripheral cells.
- **HDL2** — larger, lighter, cholesteryl ester- and triglyceride-rich; the end-product of HDL3 maturation.

HDL3 can be further resolved into HDL3C (cholesteryl ester-poor, the discoidal nascent form), HDL3B, and HDL3A (the more mature, lipid-rich species). The discoidal HDL3 particle is a flattened bilayer of phospholipid with a horseshoe arrangement of apoA-I molecules; spherical HDL3 contains a cholesteryl ester core.

## Biosynthesis & Metabolism

Nascent HDL3 originates in liver, intestine, and peripheral tissues through spontaneous efflux of phospholipid and cholesterol from cells, with apoA-I acting as the acceptor. Two ATP-binding cassette transporters perform the loading:

- [[ABCA1]] — exports cholesterol and phospholipid to lipid-poor apoA-I, generating discoidal pre-beta HDL3. Loss of ABCA1 (Tangier disease) abolishes HDL3 formation and causes cholesterol accumulation in macrophages.
- [[ABCG1]] — exports cholesterol to larger, more mature HDL particles.

Maturation of discoidal HDL3 into spherical HDL2 requires two activities. Lecithin-cholesterol acyltransferase (LCAT), activated by apoA-I, esterifies free cholesterol on the disc surface; the now hydrophobic cholesteryl ester migrates into the particle's core, converting the disc into a sphere. Meanwhile, hepatic triglyceride lipase and endothelial lipase remodel the phospholipid layer and ultimately degrade HDL into surface remnants. HDL3 also participates in [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]] as the proximal acceptor before handing cholesterol to larger HDL and ultimately to [[Liver]] via [[SR-BI]].

## Physiological Function

- **Cholesterol efflux**: HDL3 is the principal acceptor of cholesterol effluxed from macrophages and other peripheral cells, the first and rate-limiting step of [[Reverse Cholesterol Transport]] and [[Cholesterol Efflux]].
- **Antioxidant protection**: HDL-associated [[PON1]] hydrolyses lipid peroxides in HDL phospholipids; HDL also limits oxidative modification of [[LDL]]. Oxidation of HDL3 (by copper, or by iron/ascorbate-driven [[Oxidative Stress]]) alters phospholipid fluidity, modifies apoprotein structure, and reduces PON1 activity, thereby impairing efflux capacity.
- **Anti-inflammatory signalling**: HDL-bound apoA-I and associated enzymes dampen [[NF-κB]]-dependent activation in endothelium and macrophages, and HDL3 attenuates neutrophil and monocyte activation.
- **Acute-phase behaviour**: HDL3 is preferentially lost in the acute-phase response, which helps explain the low HDL seen in chronic inflammatory states.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Atherosclerosis**: HDL3 is the fraction most closely tracking cholesterol efflux capacity; low HDL3 and impaired maturation predict higher lesion burden. High HDL-C alone is not uniformly protective, since the composition of the HDL pool matters more than its concentration.
- **Oxidative stress and ageing**: with age, HDL becomes more oxidised and glycated, HDL3 cholesteryl ester transfer protein activity alters, and efflux capacity declines even at normal HDL-C, contributing to increased cardiovascular risk.
- **Tangier disease**: biallelic ABCA1 loss abolishes HDL3, causes corneal opacity, neuropathy, and accelerated atherosclerosis — direct evidence that HDL3 formation is causally anti-atherogenic.
- **Lipid peroxidation of HDL** reduces PON1 activity and reverses the protective effect, linking HDL3 to the oxidative arm of the atherosclerosis literature.
