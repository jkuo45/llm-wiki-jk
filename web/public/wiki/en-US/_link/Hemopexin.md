# Hemopexin

**Hemopexin** is a plasma glycoprotein with a dedicated heme-binding pocket. It captures free heme (the iron-containing fragment of hemoglobin) and delivers it to CD91 (LRP1) receptors, chiefly on hepatocytes and macrophages, where [[HO-1|heme oxygenase-1]] converts it to biliverdin/bilirubin, CO and iron.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Hemolysis liberates hemoglobin and its derivatives (methemoglobin, ferrylhemoglobin, heme) along with ATP, HSP70 and IL-33. [[Haptoglobin]] scavenges the hemoglobin while hemopexin scavenges the heme; once that adaptive capacity is exceeded the products act as damage-associated molecular patterns — driving TLR4/NF-κB endothelial and macrophage activation, NADPH oxidase/heme-iron-driven NET formation, [[NLRP3]] and [[Pyroptosis]], heme-driven [[Necroptosis]]/[[Ferroptosis]], complement recruitment, and renal hemoprotein filtering injury. Low hemopexin is therefore a clinical marker of intravascular hemolysis.

## Mechanism and disease relevance

- Hemopexin binds heme with extremely high affinity; the hemopexin–heme complex is endocytosed via CD91 and cannot release its cargo back to plasma, keeping heme iron out of redox cycling.
- Hemopexin deficiency in mice or loss in patients (e.g. dapsone- or ceftriaxone-related hemolysis) amplifies vascular injury because heme-driven oxidative stress goes unbuffered.
- Heme/Hp balance also matters in [[Eryptosis]]: heme is pro-eryptotic and heme oxygenase-1 activity is part of the anti-inflammatory reprogramming macrophages undergo after efferocytosis of PS-positive cells.
