# Reticulocyte

**Reticulocytes** are the immature erythrocytes released from bone marrow. They have already expelled their nucleus but still carry residual ribosomal RNA (hence the reticular network seen on supravital stain), and they are the stage at which mitochondria and other organelles are being cleared.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko et al. 2025 eryptosis consensus review]]
> Reticulocytes are the penultimate step of [[Erythropoiesis]] and the stage where mitophagy removes mitochondria — precisely the step whose loss of mitochondria leaves mature erythrocytes dependent on Ca2+ signaling rather than the mitochondrial apoptotic machinery. Clinically, the reticulocyte percentage tracks erythropoietic response: it is high in hemolytic states (compensatory output) and low in hypoproliferative anemia. In dialysis patients the percentage of PS-exposing erythrocytes correlates with both erythropoietin dosage and the reticulocyte fraction, and in hepatic disease hyperbilirubinemia raises reticulocyte counts alongside falling erythrocyte counts.

## Key points

- Residual ribosomes → reticulocyte RNA; polychromasia on Wright stain; supravital new-methylene-blue staining for the reticular network.
- Final maturation (cAMP-driven cAMP-PKA-CREB → AMPK) completes organelle clearance; premature reticulocyte release signals erythropoietin-driven marrow stress.
- Reticulocyte-rich RBC populations are more deformable and less prone to eryptosis than aged cells; the reticulocyte fraction therefore confounds absolute eryptosis measurements in cohorts.
- Used clinically as the RPI (reticulocyte production index) after transfusion or in hemolysis to separate hypoproliferative from regenerative anemia.
