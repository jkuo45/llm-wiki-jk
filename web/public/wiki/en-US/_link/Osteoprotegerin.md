# Osteoprotegerin

**Osteoprotegerin** (OPG, gene *TNFRSF11B*) is a secreted member of the TNF-receptor superfamily that acts as a **decoy receptor** for RANKL (and for TRAIL). Because it binds RANKL without transducing a signal, it functions as a brake on osteoclast formation and bone resorption.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- RANKL, expressed by osteoblasts and osteocytes, engages RANK on osteoclast precursors and drives their differentiation, survival and activation — this is the target osteoprotegerin sequesters.
- The **RANKL/RANK/OPG axis** therefore sets the balance between bone formation and resorption; the OPG/RANKL ratio is the clinically used measure of bone turnover.
- OPG is also a decoy for [[TRAIL]] and interacts with [[DcR1]]/[[DcR2]]-type logic, linking it to death-receptor biology.
- OPG expression is stimulated by oestrogen and by mechanical loading, and is suppressed by glucocorticoids, PTH excess and some cytokines (IL-1, TNF-α).

## Clinical Relevance

- **Osteoporosis:** increased RANKL/OPG ratio underlies postmenopausal bone loss; denosumab (a RANKL antibody) works by the same logic, and OPG itself is an investigational therapeutic (gene therapy, Fc-OPG constructs).
- **Renal osteodystrophy and CKD-MBD:** OPG is cleared renally, so chronic kidney disease combines high RANKL with low OPG, driving secondary hyperparathyroidism and bone loss.
- **Vascular calcification:** OPG is expressed by vascular smooth muscle; its role in calcification is context-dependent (both pro- and anti-calcific effects have been reported).
- **Cancer:** osteoblasts and tumour cells manipulate the RANKL/OPG axis to create osteolytic or osteoblastic metastases; denosumab is used in bone metastases and giant-cell tumour of bone.
