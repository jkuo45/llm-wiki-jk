# Hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acids

**Hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acids (HETEs)** are monohydroxylated metabolites of [[Arachidonic acid]] (20:4 n-6) formed by the addition of a single hydroxyl group at defined positions along the carbon chain. They are the hydroxy counterparts of the hydroperoxy intermediates (HPETEs) generated during [[Lipid Peroxidation]] and by [[Lipoxygenase]] catalysis, and they act both as signaling lipids and as indices of oxidative burden.

## Formation

HETEs arise by three mechanistically distinct routes:

- **Lipoxygenase (LOX) pathway.** 5-, 12-, and 15-[[Lipoxygenase]] insert molecular oxygen at C5, C12, or C15 of arachidonic acid to yield 5-, 12-, or 15-HPETE, which is reduced to the corresponding HETE by peroxidases. 5-HETE is the precursor of the [[Leukotrienes]] via LTA4 synthase.
- **Cytochrome P450 (CYP) pathway.** CYP epoxygenases produce EETs, while CYP ω-hydroxylases (notably CYP4A and CYP4F) generate 20-HETE, a potent vasoconstrictor and regulator of renal tubular function.
- **Non-enzymatic peroxidation.** Radical-mediated [[Lipid Peroxidation]] of arachidonic acid produces a mixture of regioisomeric HETEs (5-, 8-, 9-, 11-, 12-, 15-HETE) in non-stereoselective ratios. The loss of stereospecificity is the diagnostic feature distinguishing free-radical peroxidation from enzymatic synthesis.

> [!info] Stereochemistry as an enzymatic fingerprint
> LOX-derived HETEs are predominantly the *S* enantiomer (e.g. 15*S*-HETE from 15-LOX), whereas non-enzymatic peroxidation yields racemic mixtures. Chiral lipidomics therefore separates 15*S*-HETE (enzyme) from 15*R*-HETE plus racemate (oxidative stress), allowing quantification of the radical component of tissue lipid oxidation.

## Major Species and Their Roles

- **5-HETE**: Chemoattractant for neutrophils; precursor to leukotriene B4 and the cysteinyl leukotrienes. Elevated in asthma, atherosclerosis, and psoriasis.
- **12-HETE**: Produced by platelet-type 12-LOX and 12/15-LOX; promotes angiogenesis, tumor cell adhesion, and [[VCAM-1]] expression in endothelium.
- **15-HETE**: Product of 15-LOX; precursor to lipoxin A4 (pro-resolving) and a substrate for further oxidation to 15-oxo-ETE. 15-LOX-2 is downregulated in several cancers.
- **20-HETE**: CYP4A/4F product; constricts renal and cerebral arterioles, stimulates smooth-muscle proliferation, and contributes to hypertension and ischemia–reperfusion injury.
- **11-HETE and 8-HETE**: Predominantly non-enzymatic markers; 8-HETE is often quantified alongside [[F2-Isoprostanes]] as an in vivo index of oxidative stress.

## Biological Activity

Beyond their roles as pathway intermediates, HETEs activate PPARγ (15-HETE), modulate ion channels, and influence leukocyte trafficking. Because they are oxygenated fatty acids, they are also substrates for further peroxidation, feeding back into the [[Lipid Peroxidation]] chain when antioxidant defenses ([[Vitamin E]], [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione]]) are saturated.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Inflammation and asthma**: 5-HETE and cysteinyl leukotrienes are central to bronchoconstriction and eosinophil recruitment; 5-LOX inhibitors (zileuton) and leukotriene receptor antagonists target this axis.
- **Cardiovascular disease**: 12- and 15-HETE correlate with atherosclerotic burden; 20-HETE drives hypertension and renal vasoconstriction.
- **Cancer**: 12-HETE promotes metastatic seeding and tumor angiogenesis; 15-LOX-1/2 are tumor-suppressive in colorectal and prostate tissue.
- **Oxidative stress biomarkers**: total HETE profiles in plasma, urine, and tissue are measured by LC-MS/MS to gauge enzymatic versus radical lipid oxidation.
- **Neurobiology**: 12/15-LOX-derived HETEs contribute to excitotoxic and ferroptotic injury in neural tissue, linking this class to [[Ferroptosis]].
