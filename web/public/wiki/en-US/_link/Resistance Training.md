# Resistance Training

**Resistance training** (strength or weight training) is exercise in which muscles contract against an external load — free weights, machines, bands or body weight — to increase maximal strength, muscle mass and metabolic health. It is the training mode with the strongest evidence for preserving lean mass and physical function in older adults.

## Mechanism & Adaptations

- **Mechanical signalling:** load-dependent tension activates the mechanosensor [[Piezo1]] and focal-adhesion signalling, and raises intracellular calcium via [[Ryanodine Receptor|RyR]]/[[mTOR]]-related pathways, driving myofibrillar protein synthesis and hypertrophy.
- **Energy sensing:** work increases the [[AMP]]/ATP ratio, activating [[AMPK]] (energy sensing, mitochondrial biogenesis) while chronic mechanical load activates [[mTORC1]] (anabolism). The two pathways must be balanced: AMPK suppresses mTORC1 when energy demand is unmet.
- **Mitochondrial remodelling:** resistance training raises mitochondrial density and oxidative capacity in muscle, with [[PGC-1α]] as the transcriptional master switch; this is the opposite of the mitochondrial loss that accompanies ageing and sarcopenia.
- **Myokines and inter-organ signalling:** contracting muscle releases [[Irisin]], [[Myostatin]]-related signals, [[IL-6]] and other myokines that act on adipose tissue (browning), bone and brain; [[Irisin]] links muscle to [[Mitochondria]] in other tissues.
- **Systemic benefits:** improved insulin sensitivity and [[Glucose]] disposal, lower blood pressure, better bone loading (relevant to [[Osteoblast]] activity and [[Osteoporosis]]), improved endothelium-dependent dilation ([[Endothelium-Dependent Dilation]]) through shear-stress-driven NO release, and better sleep and mood.

## Clinical Relevance

Resistance training is a core component of exercise prescriptions for sarcopenia, type 2 diabetes, hypertension and post-cancer therapy, and it is one of the few interventions with signal for preserving independence in late life. Protein intake, creatine supplementation and adequate recovery determine the hypertrophic response; overreaching and injury are the main risks, and progressive load adjustment rather than fixed load is key in older adults.
