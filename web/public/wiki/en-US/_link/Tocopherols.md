# Tocopherols

**Tocopherols** are the four naturally occurring forms of [[Vitamin E]] carrying a saturated 16-carbon phytyl tail: **α-, β-, γ-, and δ-tocopherol**, differing only in the number and position of methyl groups on the chromanol ring. Along with the unsaturated [[Tocotrienols]], they constitute the eight vitamers of vitamin E. α-[[Alpha-tocopherol]] is the form preferentially retained in human plasma and tissues.

## Structure

All tocopherols share the chroman-6-ol core — a phenolic ring whose hydroxyl group is the hydrogen atom donor that terminates [[Lipid Peroxidation]] — plus a lipophilic tail that anchors the molecule in the lipid bilayer or lipoprotein core. The distinction is the ring methylation pattern:

| Vitamer | Ring methyls | Relative vitamin E activity | Plasma abundance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| α-Tocopherol | 5,7,8-trimethyl | 100 (reference) | ~90% of vitamin E |
| β-Tocopherol | 5,8-dimethyl | 25–50 | minor |
| γ-Tocopherol | 7,8-dimethyl | 10–30 | ~5–10% |
| δ-Tocopherol | 8-methyl | ~1 | minor |

The more highly methylated the ring, the higher the electron density on the phenolic oxygen and the faster the H-atom transfer to a lipid peroxyl radical — which is why α-tocopherol is both the most potent radical trap and the best ligand for the α-tocopherol transfer protein ([[α-TTP]]).

## Mechanism of Action

Tocopherols are **chain-breaking antioxidants**. In the propagation phase of [[Lipid Peroxidation]], a lipid peroxyl radical (LOO•) abstracts the phenolic hydrogen from tocopherol with a rate constant near 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹, forming a lipid hydroperoxide ([[Lipid hydroperoxide|LOOH]]) and a tocopheroxyl radical. The tocopheroxyl radical is resonance-stabilized and too unreactive to abstract H from a neighboring PUFA, so the chain terminates.

> [!info] Tocopherol is not consumed stoichiometrically
> Because [[Vitamin C]] (ascorbate) regenerates α-tocopherol from its tocopheroxyl radical at the membrane–water interface, one molecule of vitamin E can trap many peroxyl radicals. This is the basis of the tocopherol–ascorbate–glutathione antioxidant network: [[Vitamin C]] is regenerated by [[Glutathione]] and NADPH-dependent [[Glutathione Reductase]], and [[Glutathione]] is regenerated by the pentose phosphate pathway.

Tocopherols also inhibit non-radical oxidants such as [[Singlet Oxygen]], quench it physically, and at high local concentration can act as pro-oxidants — reducing transition metals and generating tocopherol-mediated peroxidation (TMP). This dual behavior is one explanation for the mixed results of high-dose vitamin E supplementation trials.

## Absorption, Transport, and Metabolism

Tocopherols are absorbed in the intestine with dietary fat via mixed micelles, packaged into chylomicrons, and delivered to the liver. There, **α-tocopherol transfer protein (α-TTP)** selectively binds the α form and resecretes it in VLDL, while other vitamers are preferentially ω-hydroxylated by CYP4F2 and excreted in bile. This selective retention is why plasma is dominated by α-tocopherol despite γ-tocopherol being the major form in most Western diets.

Metabolism proceeds by ω-oxidation to 13'-OH and 13'-COOH metabolites (CEHCs), which are water-soluble and renally excreted. Urinary α- and γ-CEHC are used as functional biomarkers of vitamin E status and turnover.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Membrane and LDL protection**: tocopherols are the dominant radical trap in LDL; LDL oxidizability is inversely related to its α-tocopherol content, and the peroxidation of LDL is a key step in [[Atherosclerosis]].
- **Ferroptosis suppression**: by scavenging lipid peroxyl radicals in the membrane, tocopherols (and [[Coenzyme Q10]]) act as radical-trapping antioxidants parallel to the [[GPX4]]/[[Glutathione]] axis. [[Ferrostatin-1]] and [[Liproxstatin-1]] are synthetic analogs of this mechanism.
- **Neuroprotection**: the brain's high [[Docosahexaenoic acid|DHA]] content makes tocopherol defense critical; familial vitamin E deficiency (AVED, mutations in α-TTP) causes ataxia and neurodegeneration.
- **Supplementation outcomes**: the ATBC and CARET trials reported no benefit or harm (increased cancer risk) at high dose in smokers, while the SELECT trial found no prostate benefit and a possible hemorrhagic stroke signal. Genetic context matters — see the [[COMT]] Val158Met interaction under [[Vitamin E]].
- **Pro-oxidant caveat**: high-dose α-tocopherol can displace other antioxidants and regenerate oxidized LDL under iron/copper load, consistent with the [[Antioxidant Supplementation Paradox]].

## Dietary Sources

α-Tocopherol dominates wheat germ and sunflower oils, olive oil, and almonds; γ-tocopherol dominates soybean, corn, and canola oils. See [[Vitamin E]] for a comparative oil table and the effects of refining on tocochromanol retention.
