# Myofibroblast

Myofibroblasts are activated fibroblast derivatives that combine the matrix-synthesising output of fibroblasts with the contractile apparatus of smooth muscle. They are the cell type that closes a wound and, when they fail to resolve, the cell type that produces organ fibrosis.

> [!info] The defining marker is a contractile filament
> The diagnostic feature is expression of [[ACTA2|alpha-smooth muscle actin]] (α-SMA) in stress fibres, together with the calcium-independent contractility it confers and a matching increase in the calcium-binding protein calponin. α-SMA is the standard marker but is imperfect — it is also expressed in activated pericytes and vascular smooth muscle, and some myofibroblast-like cells are α-SMA-negative.

## Differentiation Triggers

Fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation is driven by mechanical and biochemical signals acting together:

- **[[TGF-beta|TGF-β1]]** is the dominant and best-characterised inducer, acting through SMAD2/3/4 signalling to upregulate α-SMA and promote both contractility and collagen synthesis. Its autocrine amplification loop is what makes fibrosis self-propagating.
- **Mechanical tension.** Stiff matrix itself drives differentiation, through integrin-based mechanotransduction; the process is a stiffening feedback loop rather than a one-way response to a single signal.
- **[[Angiotensin II]]**, endothelin and serotonin, which signal contractility through G-protein-coupled receptors.
- **Reactive oxygen species** acting directly on fibroblasts, an effect that is attenuated by the redox-scavenging capacity associated with [[NAD+]] availability.
- **Hypoxia**, which acts through [[HIF-1α]] to select a myofibroblast phenotype in the wound core.

Differentiation is a spectrum: single cells expressing α-SMA transiently during healing, and highly proliferative, apoptosis-resistant, matrix-secreting myofibroblasts persisting in established fibrosis. Senescent myofibroblasts are notable for releasing [[SASP]] components, which sustains inflammation in a self-reinforcing way rather than allowing resolution.

## Physiological Function and the Resolution Requirement

After injury, myofibroblasts do three things: they contract the wound by force-generating actomyosin sliding inside stress fibres; they produce [[Collagen]] and other [[Extracellular Matrix|extracellular matrix]] components to close the defect; and they secrete the growth factors that drive the repair response. The physiological endpoint of healing is that myofibroblasts undergo apoptosis, so that the matrix is laid down without the contractile force that would deform the tissue.

> [!warning] Persistence is the pathology
> Fibrosis is not the excess of a normal process but the failure of its termination. Almost all antifibrotic strategies being developed — TGF-β1 neutralisation and receptor inhibition, PDGF and CTGF blockade, fibroblast apoptosis induction, and senolytic removal — attack the same idea: make the myofibroblast go away.

## Disease Relevance

- [[Fibrosis]] in lung ([[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]]), liver (cirrhosis, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis), kidney, and heart ([[Cardiac Fibrosis]]), including the fibrotic response to chronic pressure overload.
- [[TGF-beta Signaling Pathway|TGF-β]] signalling blockade: the active trial programme is built on it.
- [[Cardiac Fibrosis]] and [[Vascular Remodeling]] — intimal and adventitial [[Fibroblast]] responses to injury in the vessel wall, where myofibroblast persistence drives restenosis.
- [[SASP]] and [[Senescence]] — myofibroblast senescence is a distinct phenotype: matrix production continues while proliferative capacity is lost, so senolytics can reduce fibrotic matrix output without killing the cell outright.
- [[CCN1]] — a matricellular stress protein whose expression in myofibroblasts scales with fibrotic severity and which amplifies the TGF-β and [[Hippo Pathway|YAP]] circuits.
- Cancer — myofibroblasts in the tumour stroma are often termed cancer-associated fibroblasts; myofibroblast differentiation promotes both ECM deposition and, through the YAP pathway, a mechanosensitive proliferative program.
- Peripheral nerve repair, where Schwann-cell-like myofibroblast differentiation is a proposed (though contested) mechanism of neuroma formation after nerve injury — noted here as uncertain.

> [!info] A crosstalk target for this vault
> Myofibroblast behaviour is downstream of [[SIRT1]] and [[NAD+]] status, upstream of [[Collagen]] output, tightly coupled to [[Autophagy]] because matrix turnover requires it, and modulated by [[Oxidative Stress]]. It is the mechanistic hub where the ageing and fibrosis arms of this vault meet.

## Connections

- [[Fibroblast]] — the parent cell; myofibroblasts are the activated, contractile state of this mesenchymal lineage.
- [[ACTA2]] — the α-SMA gene whose product is the standard myofibroblast marker and the source of contractile force.
- [[TGF-beta]] — the dominant differentiation signal, acting through SMAD2/3/4 to establish the myofibroblast phenotype.
- [[Fibrosis]] — the disease state produced by persistent myofibroblasts, in every organ.
- [[Extracellular Matrix]] — the substrate they deposit and the source of the mechanical feedback that drives differentiation.
- [[Collagen]] — the principal product of myofibroblast matrix synthesis and the direct cause of tissue stiffness.
- [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]] — the disease in which myofibroblast persistence is most directly implicated, and the origin of the two antifibrotic drugs that target this axis.
- [[Cardiac Fibrosis]] — myocardial fibrosis after injury and pressure overload, driven by activated fibroblasts and myofibroblasts.
- [[Vascular Remodeling]] — intimal and adventitial myofibroblast accumulation underlies restenosis and vessel-wall thickening.
- [[CCN1]] — a stress-inducible matricellular protein that scales with myofibroblast activation and amplifies TGF-β signalling.
- [[Hippo Pathway]] — the mechanosensitive pathway through which matrix stiffness converts into transcriptional myofibroblast identity.
- [[SASP]] — the senescent myofibroblast's inflammatory output, which sustains the fibrotic microenvironment.
- [[Senescence]] — a myofibroblast phenotype in which matrix production persists alongside proliferative arrest.
- [[Angiotensin II]] — a direct inducer of myofibroblast differentiation and contractility.
- [[NAD+]] — its availability constrains the redox tone of the fibroblast and thereby the fibrotic response.
- [[SIRT1]] — sirtuin activity restrains the fibrotic programme; loss of NAD+–dependent sirtuin function with age is one proposed reason fibrosis prevalence rises.
- [[Oxidative Stress]] — reactive oxygen species act directly on fibroblasts as profibrotic signals.
- [[HIF-1α]] — hypoxia-driven transcription selecting a myofibroblast phenotype in the wound or fibrotic core.
- [[Matrix Metalloproteinase]] — required for the matrix turnover that resolution of fibrosis demands.
- [[Wound Healing]] — the physiological process whose myofibroblast phase must terminate for the outcome to be repair rather than scar.
- [[Actin Cytoskeleton]] — the force-generating apparatus that makes the myofibroblast phenotype distinct from that of the fibroblast.
- [[Fibrosis|Anti-fibrotic therapy]] — the pharmacological strategy of inducing myofibroblast apoptosis, which is where [[Senolytics]] enter.
