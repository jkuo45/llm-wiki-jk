# Semicarbazide-Sensitive Amine Oxidase

**Semicarbazide-sensitive amine oxidase (SSAO)**, also called vascular adhesion protein-1 (VAP-1) or AOC3, is a copper-containing amine oxidase of the vascular surface that converts primary amines into aldehydes, hydrogen peroxide and ammonia. It sits on the outer membrane of endothelial cells, adipocytes, vascular smooth muscle, fibroblasts and activated inflammatory cells, and functions as a local generator of oxidative stress and reactive aldehydes.

## Overview

Semicarbazide-sensitive amine oxidase (SSAO), also known as VAP-1/AOC3, is a copper-dependent amine oxidase displayed on the outer membrane of endothelial cells, adipocytes and activated inflammatory cells. It oxidizes primary amines to aldehydes, generating hydrogen peroxide and reactive aldehydes that cross-link extracellular matrix, drive endothelial NF-kappaB signalling and promote monocyte recruitment. Its activity rises with hyperglycemia, glycation and age, making it a convergence point for vascular ageing and inflammation.

## Structure & Domains

- **Gene:** *AOC3* on chromosome 4q31; the protein is a homotetramer (tetrameric A2B2 assembly) of ~85 kDa subunits, each with an N-terminal transmembrane or GPI anchor, five seed region domains (S1–S5), and a C-terminal catalytic domain.
- **Catalytic centre:** a **type-3 copper (CuB) active site** together with a topologically bound pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) cofactor; the active site lies at the interface between subunits, so activity requires proper quaternary assembly.
- **Active-site loop:** the "flap" (residues ~158–176) gates substrate access and determines substrate and inhibitor specificity. The loop "open" conformation is catalytically permissive; Tyr393 blocks the active site and is displaced upon substrate binding.
- **Semicarbazide sensitivity:** hydrazine derivatives such as semicarbazide and aminoguanidine coordinate the copper and irreversibly inhibit the enzyme — the basis of its historical name and of most experimental pharmacology.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

1. **Substrate oxidation:** primary amines (e.g. benzylamine, butylamine) bind the Cu/PQQ site; ammonia is released and the amine is converted to an aldehyde, generating **hydrogen peroxide** as a by-product. Amine oxidase activity is thus a local ROS source.
2. **Lysyl oxidase cross-linking pathway:** lysine residues of [[Collagen]] and elastin are oxidatively deaminated by lysyl oxidase enzymes to reactive aldehydes, initiating covalent cross-linking of the matrix. SSAO amplifies this chemistry: it oxidizes the resulting hydroxylysine/glycine products and semialdehydes to final cross-link precursors, and its own substrate amino groups on the collagen chain are converted to aldehydes that form the mature cross-links with lysine residues.
3. **Signalling:** SSAO-generated H2O2 and aldehydes activate endothelial NF-κB and MAP kinase signalling, upregulating VCAM-1, ICAM-1 and monocyte recruitment, and inducing a pro-inflammatory, senescence-like phenotype in endothelial cells and in [[Cellular Senescence|senescent cells]].
4. **Adhesion:** VAP-1 was first described as an endothelial adhesion molecule binding lymphocytes; its role is now understood largely as a consequence of its enzymatic activity.

## Physiological Function

SSAO is normally induced by inflammatory cues and by advanced glycation, its expression rising with age. Physiological roles include clearance of circulating primary amines, control of vascular tone (via H2O2 as a vasoconstrictor/relaxant signal depending on concentration), regulation of extracellular matrix turnover, and modulation of adipocyte function and insulin sensitivity. Its activity is markedly increased at sites of vascular injury and inflammation.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Diabetes and diabetic complications:** SSAO expression and activity rise in hyperglycaemia and with [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]] and [[Methylglyoxal]], contributing to [[Type 2 Diabetes]]-associated endothelial dysfunction, retinopathy, nephropathy ([[Albuminuria]]), and accelerated [[Atherosclerosis]]. Its product H2O2 contributes to the oxidative stress of [[Oxidative Stress]].
- **Inflammatory bowel disease and other inflammatory conditions:** elevated SSAO in inflammatory bowel disease, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis and chronic inflammatory states correlates with disease activity and with monocyte recruitment.
- **Aging and inflammaging:** SSAO is a senescence-associated and age-associated inflammatory enzyme; its activity rises in aged tissue and in [[Inflammaging]].
- **Cancer:** SSAO is overexpressed in many tumours and in tumour endothelium, contributing to angiogenesis, invasion and metastasis, and it is a marker of aggressive disease in breast and colon cancer.
- **Pharmacology:** semicarbazide and aminoguanidine inhibit SSAO, as do newer specific inhibitors (e.g. PXS-4728A/BI 1467335, ASP8232, and the antibody-based approaches); the aminoguanidine family has been explored in diabetic nephropathy and retinopathy but has poor selectivity, limiting clinical use. [[Aminoguanidine]] is the prototype semicarbazide-sensitivity reagent.
