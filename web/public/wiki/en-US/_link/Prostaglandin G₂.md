# Prostaglandin G2

**Prostaglandin G₂ (PGG₂)** is the 15-hydroperoxy prostaglandin endoperoxide produced by the bis-dioxygenation of [[Arachidonic acid]] by cyclooxygenase (COX-1 and COX-2). It is the first committed intermediate of the prostanoid pathway and the substrate for the peroxidase activity that reduces it to [[Prostaglandin H₂]].

## Formation and Chemistry

[[Cyclooxygenases]] catalyze two sequential reactions at a single heme-containing active site:

1. **Bis-dioxygenation**: two molecules of O₂ are added to arachidonic acid to form the bicyclic endoperoxide PGG₂, retaining a C15 hydroperoxide group. This is the "cyclooxygenase" activity and is the site of aspirin's irreversible acetylation of Ser530.
2. **Reduction**: the same enzyme's peroxidase site reduces the C15-OOH to C15-OH, yielding [[Prostaglandin H₂]] (PGH₂), the common precursor of prostaglandins, [[Thromboxane A₂]], and prostacyclin.

PGG₂ is therefore a genuine **lipid hydroperoxide** — the only one produced enzymatically in bulk — and its peroxide group is a potential source of radicals during the peroxidase reaction. The ferryl-oxo protoporphyrin radical cation generated at the peroxidase site can leak and oxidize nearby lipids and proteins, a mechanism implicated in COX-dependent peroxidation of membrane [[Polyunsaturated fatty acids|PUFAs]].

> [!warning] PGG₂ is both a signal intermediate and a radical source
> The peroxidase cycle of COX requires a hydroperoxide activator (which is why the enzyme has a lag phase), and the reaction produces high-valent iron-oxo intermediates. Under conditions of glutathione depletion, this couples prostanoid synthesis to local [[Lipid Peroxidation]] — a link between inflammation and oxidative membrane damage.

## Downstream Products

PGH₂ (from PGG₂) is isomerized or reduced by terminal synthases:

- **Prostaglandin E₂** (via PGE synthase) — inflammation, pain, fever
- **Prostaglandin D₂** (via PGD synthase) — mast cells, sleep, allergy
- **Prostaglandin F₂α** (via PGF synthase) — uterine contraction, luteolysis
- **Prostacyclin / PGI₂** (via prostacyclin synthase) — vasodilation, anti-aggregatory
- **Thromboxane A₂** (via thromboxane synthase) — vasoconstriction, platelet aggregation

The balance between [[Prostacyclin]] and [[Thromboxane A₂]] at the vascular wall is a key determinant of thrombotic tendency, and low-dose aspirin shifts it by acetylating platelet COX-1.

## Physiological and Clinical Significance

- **Inflammation**: PGG₂/PGH₂ flux is the rate-limiting step in prostaglandin generation; COX-2 induction in inflamed tissue raises PGG₂ production orders of magnitude above baseline.
- **Atherosclerosis**: COX-2 in macrophage foam cells and endothelium drives prostanoid-mediated inflammation within plaques; the peroxidase radical leak contributes to [[Oxidized LDL]] formation.
- **Pharmacology**: NSAIDs inhibit the cyclooxygenase oxygenase site, lowering PGG₂; aspirin additionally blocks thromboxane synthesis irreversibly in platelets.
- **Cancer**: COX-2 overexpression and elevated PGE₂ mark colorectal, breast, and lung tumors; PGG₂-derived prostanoids promote angiogenesis and immune evasion.
- **Oxidative stress measurement**: because PGG₂ is a hydroperoxide, prostanoid flux is often measured alongside [[F2-Isoprostanes]] to distinguish enzymatic from radical-derived eicosanoids.
