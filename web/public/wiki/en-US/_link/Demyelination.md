# Demyelination

**Demyelination** is the loss of the insulating myelin sheath surrounding CNS and PNS axons, either because oligodendrocytes/Schwann cells fail to maintain myelin (primary demyelination) or because myelin is destroyed by inflammation, infection, toxins or ischemia (secondary demyelination). It is the defining lesion of multiple sclerosis and its relatives, and the core mechanism of a family of inherited leukodystrophies.

## Overview

Myelin is produced by [[Oligodendrocytes]] in the CNS and by Schwann cells in the periphery. It is a lipid-rich, multilamellar extension of the glial plasma membrane, built around [[Myelin Basic Protein]] and other myelin proteins. Its function is to enable saltatory conduction: current jumps between nodes of Ranvier, raising conduction velocity from ~1 m/s (unmyelinated) to up to ~100 m/s, while reducing the energetic cost of the action potential.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Inflammatory demyelination:** autoreactive CD4+ T cells (Th1/Th17) and CD8+ cytotoxic T cells cross the blood–brain barrier, recognize myelin antigens presented by [[Antigen Presentation|MHC class II]] on microglia and astrocytes, and release [[TNF-alpha|TNF]], IFN-γ ([[Interferon-gamma]]), IL-17 and reactive oxygen species. This triggers [[Complement System]] activation, oligodendrocyte apoptosis and blood–brain barrier leakage.
- **Microglial and astrocytic contribution:** [[Microglia]] and [[Astrocytes]] are activated and contribute both to myelin damage and to repair; failure of remyelination is now considered as decisive as initial damage. Key repair regulators include [[TGF-beta|TGF-β]], IGF-1 and retinoids, and oligodendrocyte precursor differentiation is required for restoration.
- **Viral/post-infectious demyelination:** e.g. measles-associated SSPE, HTLV-1 tropical spastic paraparesis, and post-infectious encephalomyelitis.
- **Toxic/metabolic:** copper deficiency (myeloneuropathy), vitamin B12 or folate deficiency (subacute combined degeneration), methanol, and carbon monoxide.
- **Vascular/infarction:** ischemic white matter injury demyelinating axons secondarily (bilateral posterior leukoencephalopathy, CADASIL).
- **Axonal degeneration coupling:** repeated demyelination and remyelination shortens internodes, exposes axons to inflammatory mediators and oxidative stress, and eventually triggers [[Axonal Degeneration]]. Neurofilament light in CSF and blood is the marker of this destructive component, and axonal loss is the main correlate of permanent disability.

## Clinical Presentation & Biomarkers

- **Multiple sclerosis:** relapsing-remitting or progressive course with optic neuritis, sensory symptoms, ataxia, internuclear ophthalmoplegia, and lesions disseminated in space and time on MRI (Dawson fingers).
- **Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (Guillain–Barré syndrome):** rapidly progressive ascending weakness with areflexia, albuminocytologic dissociation in CSF, and risk of respiratory failure — an emergency requiring plasma exchange or IVIG.
- **Leukodystrophies:** childhood-onset white matter diseases, including [[Metachromatic Leukodystrophy]] (arylsulfatase A deficiency) and [[Adrenoleukodystrophy]] (ABCD1 defect in peroxisomal VLCFA transport).
- **Biomarkers:** MRI T2/FLAIR hyperintense and T1 hypointense white matter lesions; CSF oligoclonal IgG bands; CSF and serum neurofilament light for axonal injury; evoked potentials for conduction.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Neuroinflammation:** demyelinating lesions show perivascular inflammation, microglial activation and reactive astrocytosis; chronic lesions develop a "shadow" of axonal loss, a leading cause of irreversible disability.
- **Mitochondrial vulnerability:** oligodendrocytes are among the most metabolically demanding cells in the brain, and their high myelin lipid load makes them exquisitely sensitive to mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress — linking demyelination to mitochondrial disease and to ageing biology.
- **Therapeutic logic:** disease-modifying therapy in MS acts on the immune axis (interferon-β, monoclonal antibodies against [[TNF-alpha]]-independent targets, natalizumab, anti-CD20 antibodies, S1P modulators) rather than on myelin itself; remyelination-promoting approaches (clemastine, liothyronine) remain investigational. Plasma exchange is the proven rescue therapy in antibody-mediated and severe acute demyelinating disease.
