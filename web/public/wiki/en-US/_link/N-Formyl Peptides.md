# N-Formyl Peptides

**N-formyl peptides** are short peptides derived from [[Mitochondrial Translation|mitochondrial translation]] products. Because mitochondrial protein synthesis initiates with N-formylmethionine — as in bacteria — these peptides retain bacterial-like immunogenic features and function as [[Damage-Associated Molecular Patterns|damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)]] when released from damaged mitochondria.

## Signalling

N-formyl peptides signal through [[Formyl Peptide Receptor|formyl peptide receptors]], recruiting and activating innate immune cells in a manner analogous to bacterial peptides. They are not always classical receptor ligands, but they strongly amplify inflammatory signalling, facilitating [[NLRP3]] activation, redox-sensitive transcriptional responses, and further mitochondrial damage.

## Role in Sterile Inflammation

Together with [[Mitochondrial DNA|mtDNA]], [[Mitochondrial RNA|mtRNA]], [[Cardiolipin|cardiolipin]], and [[Mitochondrial ROS|mtROS]], N-formyl peptides link mitochondrial injury to innate immune activation and sterile inflammation. Their release during [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] and defective [[Mitophagy|mitophagy]] contributes to the persistent inflammatory tone of [[Inflammaging|inflammaging]].
