# Ki67

**Ki67** (MKI67) is a nuclear protein expressed in proliferating cells during G1, S, G2, and M phases, and absent in quiescent (G0) cells. Its detection by immunostaining is a standard method for quantifying the proliferative fraction of a tissue. Because it marks cycling rather than resting cells, Ki67 is used alongside differentiation markers to assess tissue regeneration.

## Role in Skin Assessment

In epidermis, Ki67-positive cells are concentrated in the basal layer. Aging reduces the proliferative compartment; interventions that restore epidermal proliferation increase Ki67 counts normalized to the dermal–epidermal junction length.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Topical application of urolithin A activates mitophagy and enhances mitochondrial function to promote skin longevity|Topical urolithin A skin study]]
> In a full-thickness skin model reconstructed from an aged donor, 1 μM [[Urolithin A]] for 25 days significantly increased Ki67-positive cells per μm of dermal–epidermal junction (p < 0.0001), together with [[Filaggrin]] and keratin 10. The result was interpreted as restored epidermal proliferation and differentiation.
