# Skp2

**Skp2** (S-phase kinase-associated protein 2) is the substrate-recognition subunit of the [[SCF Complex]] (Skp1–Cullin1–F-box). By recognising phosphorylated substrates — chiefly the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors [[p27]] and [[p21 CIP1]] — Skp2 links the [[Cell Cycle]] to the [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]]. It is a bona fide oncogene in many tumours and one of the mechanisms by which cancer cells escape [[Cellular Senescence]].

## Structure & Domains

- **F-box domain** (N-terminal) — mediates interaction with Skp1, the adaptor of the SCF complex, and determines substrate specificity.
- **Leucine-rich repeat (LRR) domain** — the substrate-binding surface; a phosphodegron-binding pocket recognises the DSGXXS motif of phosphorylated cyclin-D/CDK and CDK-inhibitor substrates.
- **N-terminal localisation motif** and a putative SUMO-interacting domain, both implicated in Skp2 nuclear localisation and stability.
- Skp2 is present in the SCF^Skp2 complex alongside [[FBW7]] (SCF^β-TrCP-like) and other F-box proteins, but it has its own, non-redundant substrate set.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Cyclin-CDK inhibition.** In early G1, [[p27]] and [[p21 CIP1]] restrain CDK2/4/6 and block S-phase entry. When CDK activity rises, Skp2 binds the phosphorylated forms of these inhibitors and ubiquitinates them, marking them for degradation through the [[Ubiquitin-Proteasome System]] and releasing the brake on proliferation. This is the classic "RhoA–Skp2–p27" axis of the G1/S transition.
- **FoxO repression.** Skp2 ubiquitinates the FoxO transcription factors [[FOXO3]] and [[FOXO3a]], sending them out of the nucleus and suppressing FoxO-dependent genes (including p27, p21, Bim and stress-response genes). Because FoxO activity promotes arrest and stress tolerance, its destruction is pro-proliferative and anti-senescence. Skp2 also binds and sequesters FOXO1 in the cytoplasm in a ubiquitin-independent fashion.
- **p53 and the p53–p63 axis.** Skp2 interacts with the Mdm2–p53 complex and promotes p53 ubiquitination and degradation, while suppressing p63; this weakens the p53-dependent senescence and apoptosis programme. Skp2 also destabilises the p53 family member p73.
- **Oncogene stability.** Skp2 binds and stabilises oncogenic Myc, and cooperates with [[c-Myc]] and with [[SKN-1]]/Daxx pathways that control the [[Ubiquitin Ligase]] network.
- **Other targets.** Skp2 ubiquitinates the E3 ligase [[FOXO]] partners, the actin regulator [[RhoA]], and proteins involved in receptor trafficking and DNA repair; its output is broad, which is why it is a drug target rather than a pure cell-cycle regulator.

## Physiological Function

Skp2 is essential for the G1/S transition, for thymocyte development and for full T-cell activation, and it is required for normal somatic hypermutation and germinal-centre maintenance. It also participates in DNA-damage tolerance, and in the control of [[Cell Proliferation]] during tissue regeneration and stem-cell turnover. Skp2-null mice are viable but small, with reduced cell numbers and impaired proliferative responses.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Overexpression in cancer.** Skp2 levels are elevated in prostate, breast, lung, colorectal, ovarian and haematological cancers, and high Skp2 is an adverse prognostic marker. Mechanistically, Skp2-driven p27 degradation is a recurring route to bypassing the [[p16INK4A]]/[[CDKN1A]]-Rb checkpoint.
- **Senescence bypass.** By degrading p21 and repressing [[FOXO Transcription Factors|FoxO]] and p53, Skp2 allows cells carrying [[DNA Damage]] to keep proliferating, which is central to the escape from [[Oncogene-Induced Senescence]] and therapy-induced senescence.
- **Stemness and drug resistance.** Skp2 supports cancer stem-cell phenotypes and confers resistance to [[Chemotherapy]] and targeted agents.
- **Prognostic and therapeutic use.** Skp2 is used as an immunohistochemical prognostic marker, and inhibitors (natural compounds such as SC79-pathway modulators, and small molecules targeting the Skp1–Skp2 interface) remain preclinical.
