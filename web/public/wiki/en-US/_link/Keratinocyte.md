# Keratinocyte

**Keratinocytes** are the predominant cell type of the epidermis. They proliferate in the basal layer and undergo terminal differentiation while migrating outward, producing keratins, [[Filaggrin]], and lipid structures that form the permeability barrier. They are the first skin cell population encountered by topically applied compounds.

## Function in Skin Homeostasis

- **Barrier formation.** Terminal differentiation yields corneocytes embedded in a lipid matrix; filaggrin aggregates keratin filaments and is required for a functional barrier.
- **[[Mitochondrial Respiration]].** Most ATP supporting keratinocyte health is mitochondrial. Low-dose [[Urolithin A]] (0.5–2.5 μM; most robust at 1 μM) increased basal and maximal respiration in normal human epidermal keratinocytes.
- **Immune signaling.** Keratinocytes respond to stress by secreting [[IL-6]] and other cytokines; topical urolithin A reduced [[IL-6]] in cytokine-stimulated keratinocytes.

## Response to Topical Urolithin A

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Topical application of urolithin A activates mitophagy and enhances mitochondrial function to promote skin longevity|Topical urolithin A skin study]]
> In reconstructed human epidermis, 1% urolithin A increased PINK1 and PARK2 expression. In aged-donor full-thickness skin it increased [[Ki67]], keratin 10 and [[Filaggrin]], improved epidermal thickness and differentiation, and reduced [[p16]] abundance — consistent with enhanced proliferation/differentiation and reduced senescence. In UVB-irradiated reconstructed epidermis, 1% urolithin A reduced PGE2 release (p = 0.0257).
