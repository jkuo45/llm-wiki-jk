# p50

> [!warning]
> **Naming ambiguity — read first.** The term "p50" is used for at least two unrelated entities in the literature. In the [[NF-κB]] field, p50 is the mature NF-κB1 subunit (this note). Separately, "p50" was the original name of a cell-surface protein on rat microglia/macrophages, now known as **TMEM119**, widely used as a microglia-specific marker. Nothing in the biology connects them. This note is about NF-κB p50.

**p50** is the C-terminal half of the **NFKB1** gene product (UniProt P19838). The primary translation product is **p105**, a 968-residue protein; constitutive, ubiquitin–proteasome-dependent processing of the C-terminal half generates the mature **p50** subunit (UniProt isoform, ~361 residues). A distinct isoform, **p52**, arises the same way from the *NFKB2* gene product p100.

> [!info]
> **The one thing to understand about p50.** p50 is the *structural* half of canonical NF-κB: it supplies the Rel homology domain that binds κB sites in DNA and that dimerises with Rel-family proteins. But it has **no transactivation domain**. Its own homodimer is therefore generally a *repressor* of κB-site transcription. p50 is a permissive partner, not an effector.

## Structure and domains

| Feature | Residues (approx., p105 numbering) | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rel homology domain (RHD) | 42–367 | DNA binding to κB sites; dimerisation; binding of [[IκBα]] |
| Glycine-rich region | 372–394 | transactivation; a processing boundary — 15-deoxy-Δ12,14-PGJ2 modifies a cysteine here and blocks p105 cleavage |
| N-terminal tail | 1–42 | nuclear localisation signal region |
| Six ankyrin repeats | 542–747 | **IκB function** — p105's C-terminus is itself an IκB protein and retains NF-κB in the cytoplasm |
| Death domain | 805–892 | mediates p105-induced apoptosis; removed during processing, so mature p50 cannot kill |

> [!info]
> **p105 is an IκB protein.** This is underappreciated. Because its C-terminal half consists of ankyrin repeats, p105 binds and sequesters the NF-κB dimers in the cytoplasm just as [[IκBα]] does. The same gene therefore supplies both the inhibitor and the active subunit. Genetic *NFKB1* knockout is consequently hard to interpret: it removes an IκB, a repressor (p50 homodimers) **and** an activator (RelA–p50) simultaneously.

## Mechanism

**Assembly.** p50 homodimerises or heterodimerises with RelA ([[p65]]), [[c-Rel]], or RelB. The **RelA–p50 heterodimer is the most abundant NF-κB species** in most cell types.

**Nuclear entry.** Dimer bound to IκBα is cytoplasmic and inactive. In the canonical pathway, [[IKK complex|IKK]] phosphorylates IκBα (Ser32/36 in human IκBα), triggering ubiquitination and [[Proteasome|proteasomal]] degradation; the freed dimer translocates to the nucleus.

**Activity depends entirely on the partner.**

- RelA–p50 → transcriptional activator.
- p50–p50 → generally repressive; recruits [[Histone Deacetylase|HDAC]]-containing complexes to κB sites.
- p50–p50 bound to **Bcl-3** → converts into an activating complex. Bcl-3, an IκB-family protein, is thus not purely inhibitory.

**Transactivation domains.** Different Rel proteins use different TADs. RelA uses TA1 and TA2; its TA3 does not contribute. p50 uses its glycine-rich region for transactivation, which is why p50 cannot substitute for RelA in full activation.

**Non-canonical connection.** The *p105* C-terminus (residues 650–684) mediates NIK-dependent processing that contributes to non-canonical NF-κB activation; in the p100 case this half is called IκBδ.

## Regulation

- **Constitutive vs signal-induced processing.** p50 generation from p105 is largely constitutive, which is why p50 protein abundance is relatively stable and why NF-κB activation is fast — no new transcription is needed.
- **Expression induced** by phorbol esters and [[TNFα]].
- **S-nitrosylation at Cys61** affects DNA binding; 15-deoxy-Δ12,14-prostaglandin J2 covalently modifies a cysteine in the glycine-rich region and blocks processing.
- **Phosphorylation** at Ser337 in the RHD modulates activity.

## Clinical relevance

- **Cancer.** Constitutive NF-κB activity is one of the most common transcription-factor abnormalities in human tumours, driving proliferation, anti-apoptotic gene expression (TRAF1, TRAF2) and resistance to immune clearance. RelA–p50 is the dominant complex in most of these settings.
- **NFKB1 deficiency.** Biallelic loss-of-function in *NFKB1* causes common variable immunodeficiency (CVID) type 12 with autoimmunity — consistent with p50's role in both lymphocyte activation and tolerance.
- **Inflammation.** p50–RelA drives the pro-inflammatory cytokine programme (IL-6, TNF-α, chemokines) that underlies chronic inflammatory disease and [[SASP|SASP]] induction.
- **Inhibitors.** IκB kinase inhibitors such as MLN120B and proteasome-blocking agents act on the pathway upstream of p50 release; no clinically approved agent targets p50 directly.
- **Haploinsufficiency caution.** In the CNS, careful work in purified neuronal cultures finds little or no NF-κB activity; many reported neuronal NF-κB signals appear to originate in glia. Treat neuron-intrinsic p50 claims with caution.

## Connections

- [[p65]] — the obligate activating partner. p50 without RelA is a repressor; RelA without p50 can still activate but the RelA–p50 heterodimer is the dominant canonical species.
- [[NF-κB]] — p50 is one of the five mammalian subunits; this note is the NFKB1/p50 half of that family.
- [[IκBα]] — binds the p50 Rel homology domain and masks its nuclear localisation signal; p105's ankyrin repeats do the same job, making p105 a second, built-in inhibitor.
- [[IKK complex]] — phosphorylating IκBα is the step that liberates p50 dimers; [[IKKbeta]] is the principal catalytic subunit in the canonical pathway.
- [[Proteasome]] — required twice over: for degrading IκBα to release NF-κB, and for processing p105 into p50.
- [[TNFα]] — the archetypal stimulus that triggers IκBα phosphorylation and the nuclear accumulation of p50 dimers.
- [[SIRT1]] — deacetylates the p65 partner of the p65/p50 complex at Lys310, shutting down the heterodimer without needing to remove p50 itself.
- [[c-Rel]] — a second Rel-family partner for p50; the RelA–p50 and RelA–Rel complexes are the canonical transcriptional activators.
