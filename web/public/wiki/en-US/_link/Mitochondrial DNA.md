### Overview
Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) is a 16.5 kb double-stranded circular genome located in the [[Mitochondrial Matrix]]. It encodes 13 proteins of the oxidative phosphorylation system, 2 ribosomal RNAs, and 22 transfer RNAs essential for mitochondrial translation.

### Origin and Inheritance
mtDNA is a vestige of the bacterial ancestor described by [[Endosymbiosis]], supporting the theory that mitochondria arose from an engulfed prokaryote. It is maternally inherited and present in many copies per cell.

### Mutation and Dysfunction
Mutations in mtDNA are frequently induced by [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] generated as by-products of respiration. Such damage produces mitochondrial heterogeneity and bioenergetic dysfunction across a cell population. mtDNA defects are strongly linked to [[Parkinson's Disease]] and a broad spectrum of mitochondrial disorders.

### Relevance to Dynamics
Because mtDNA integrity depends on balanced [[Mitochondrial Fusion|fusion]] and [[Mitochondrial Fission|fission]] for complementation and quality control, impaired dynamics can exacerbate mtDNA mutations and decline.

> [!info] Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondrial dysfunction and inflammaging a mechanistic bridge between bioenergetic failure and senescence|Nesci & Algieri 2026]]
> Age-related clonal expansion of somatic mtDNA mutations increases the number of cells in which mutant load crosses the threshold for OXPHOS failure, linking stochastic mutagenesis to focal respiratory-chain deficiency in human tissues (colorectal crypt study, 207 individuals aged 17–78). Cytosolic mtDNA also acts as a DAMP sensed by [[TLR9]] and the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS–STING pathway]].

## Release Mechanisms

- **[[Mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization|MOMP]]**: Typically associated with [[Apoptosis]]; massive [[BAX]]/[[BAK]] pore formation releases mtDNA and apoptogenic factors.
- **[[Minority MOMP]] (miMOMP)**: Sublethal permeabilization by BAX/BAK that releases mtDNA without triggering cell death — the principal route in senescent cells (McArthur et al., 2018; PMID 30257233).
- **Vacuole-like structures / mitophagy defects**: Impaired [[Mitophagy]] allows damaged mitochondria to leak mtDNA.
- **Extracellular export**: mtDNA is found in extracellular vesicles and cell-free in plasma as an ageing biomarker.

## Role in Senescence

In [[Senescent Cells]], [[Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] and miMOMP cause cytosolic mtDNA accumulation. This activates the [[cGAS-STING Pathway|cGAS-STING]] innate-immune sensor (cGAS binds mtDNA, generating cGAMP that activates STING → [[TBK1]]/[[IRF3]] and [[NF-κB]]), driving type I interferons and pro-inflammatory cytokines that are core components of the [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] (Dougherty et al., 2019/2020; PMID 31624094; Glück et al., 2020).

## Retrograde Signaling

Beyond innate immunity, mtDNA stress engages mitochondrial retrograde signaling (e.g., via [[ATF4]]/CHOP and [[AMPK]]) that remodels nuclear gene expression, contributing to the senescent metabolic state and [[Senescence-associated mitochondrial dysfunction|SAMD]].

## Damage and Depletion in Senescence

Senescent cells accumulate mtDNA mutations, strand breaks, and copy-number alterations. mtDNA depletion or mutation can itself induce a senescence-like arrest, linking mitochondrial genome integrity to the senescence program.

## Clinical / Pathological Relevance

Cytosolic and circulating mtDNA are major drivers of sterile [[Inflammaging]] and age-related diseases (atherosclerosis, [[Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis]], neurodegeneration). Elevated cell-free mtDNA in plasma is a candidate biomarker of senescent burden. Therapeutically, blocking mtDNA release (miMOMP inhibitors) or its sensing (NRTIs such as lamivudine; STING inhibitors) is an active [[Senomorphic Therapy|senomorphic]] strategy.
