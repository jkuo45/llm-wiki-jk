# p65

**p65** (UniProt Q04206; **RELA**, NFKB3; 551 residues; human locus 11q13.2) is the Rel-family subunit of [[NF-κB]] and the one that does the transcriptional work. It belongs to a five-protein family — p65/RelA, RelB, c-Rel, and the p50/p52 products of *NFKB1*/*NFKB2* — and it is the most abundant single subunit in most cell types.

> [!info]
> **p65 is a rapid-acting primary transcription factor.** Like STATs and nuclear hormone receptors, NF-κB dimers are pre-formed and sequestered in the cytoplasm, so activation requires no new protein synthesis. That is what allows [[NF-κB]] to be a first responder to [[TNFα]], [[IL-1β]], LPS, [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] and UV — the signals a cell must react to within minutes. The same property makes p65 fast to switch *off* only through degradation, which is why so much therapeutic work targets the p65 half-life.

## Structure and domains

| Feature | Residues | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rel homology domain (RHD) | 19–306 | DNA binding at κB sites, dimerisation with p50/c-Rel/RelB, binding of [[IκBα]] |
| NLS | within RHD | masked by IκBα; unmasked on IκBα destruction |
| Transactivation domain TA1 | ~309–390 | recruits [[P300|CBP/P300]] and basal transcription machinery |
| TA2 / TA3 | ~415–476, ~506–551 | additional recruitment surfaces; TA3 does not contribute to activation |
| Gly-rich linker | ~372–394 | regulatory, cleavage-sensitive |

RelA can homodimerise, but in vivo the dominant species is the **RelA–p50 heterodimer**. Different dimers have distinct κB-site preferences, which is why NF-κB "activity" is not a single number.

> [!warning]
> **IκBα binds RelA primarily.** UniProt is explicit that the cytoplasmic retention of NF-κB is exerted "primarily through the interaction with RELA." This has a practical corollary for interpreting knockouts: losing p50 leaves RelA dimerisable with c-Rel and RelB, but losing RelA removes the anchoring point for the whole canonical complex.

## Mechanism

**Sealing and release.** IκBα masks the nuclear localisation signal of the Rel homology domain. [[IKK complex|IKK]] — principally [[IKKbeta]] — phosphorylates IκBα (Ser32/36 in human IκBα), which triggers [[Ubiquitination]] and [[Proteasome|proteasomal]] degradation, freeing the dimer to translocate to the nucleus.

**Negative feedback.** NF-κB drives its own repressor: newly transcribed IκBα accumulates, re-imports the complex, and terminates the response. This produces the characteristic **oscillating** NF-κB activity rather than a square-wave pulse, and the oscillation frequency and amplitude carry information about signal identity that simple endpoint assays discard.

**Transcriptional output.** RelA recruits CBP/P300 — the interaction *requires* phosphorylated RelA — to acetylate histones and open chromatin. RelA can also modulate promoter accessibility for other factors without itself recruiting a polymerase, which is a second, indirect route to gene control.

**Anti-apoptotic arm.** RelA induces TRAF1 and TRAF2 and thereby inhibits the caspases, which is why constitutive NF-κB makes tumour cells resistant to chemotherapy and immune clearance.

## Post-translational control of p65

p65 is arguably the best-characterised acetylation switch in biology. Acetylation occurs at Lys221, Lys310, Lys435 and multiple other sites and **reduces** transcriptional activity by promoting deacetylation-dependent turnover.

| Modifier | Effect on p65 |
| --- | --- |
| [[SIRT1]] | Deacetylates **Lys310** → reduces DNA affinity and transcriptional activity, and supports proteasomal degradation |
| [[SIRT2]] | Deacetylates p65 → suppresses microglial activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine release |
| [[SIRT6]] | Deacetylates histone H3K9 at NF-κB target promoters; also lowers p65 protein and activity |
| [[HDAC3]] | Recruited by IFRD1 to deacetylate p65 at Lys310; forms trimolecular complexes with p65 |
| BRD4 | Binds **acetylated** p65 at Lys310 and sustains NF-κB activation |
| CBP/P300 | Acetylates p65; the interaction requires phosphorylation |
| IKKB | Phosphorylates Ser468 and Ser536 |

Other modifications: S-nitrosylation at **Cys38** inactivates p65; **sulfhydration of Cys38** mediates its anti-apoptotic activity; SUMOylation by PIAS3 negatively regulates DNA-bound NF-κB; monomethylation at Lys310 by SETD6; phosphorylation at Ser276, Ser281 and Ser311; ubiquitination by RNF182 and by SCF-FBXO16 leading to proteasomal degradation; caspase cleavage within the N-terminus.

> [!info]
> **Cys38 is a redox switch worth noting.** S-nitrosylation inactivates p65 while S-sulfhydration at the same residue mediates anti-apoptotic activity — the same cysteine carrying opposite signals depending on the modification. Any [[Oxidative Stress]] or nitrosative-stress intervention that touches p65 lands here.

## Clinical relevance

- **Inflammation.** Constitutive or inappropriately sustained RelA activity underlies chronic inflammatory disease, and the RelA half-life is the single most tractable node: IKK inhibitors, proteasome inhibitors and deacetylase inhibitors all converge on it.
- **Cancer.** Aberrant RelA signalling is near-universal in solid tumours — proliferation, anti-apoptosis, angiogenesis, and resistance to immune checkpoint blockade.
- **Infectious disease.** RelA is a key transcription factor regulating the interferon response during SARS-CoV-2 infection. Viruses co-opt it directly: HIV-1 carries κB sites in its long terminal repeat, and multiple herpesviruses encode proteins that bind p65.
- **Familial Behçet-like autoinflammatory disease 3 (AIFBL3).** A heterozygous RELA loss-of-function variant causes an autosomal dominant autoinflammatory syndrome — direct evidence that RelA tone is set point for immune tolerance.
- **Neuronal caveat.** As with the rest of the family, the evidence for neuron-intrinsic NF-κB transcriptional activity in highly purified neuronal cultures is weak; many reported signals are glial, and some appear to be antibody artefacts.

## Connections

- [[p50]] — the dominant partner; p50 supplies DNA binding and dimerisation, p65 supplies transactivation, and the RelA–p50 heterodimer is the most abundant NF-κB species.
- [[IκBα]] — masks p65's nuclear localisation signal and is the principal anchor of the cytoplasmic NF-κB pool; its degradation is the rate-limiting step of canonical activation.
- [[IKK complex]] — chiefly [[IKKbeta]], which phosphorylates Ser468/Ser536 on p65 and phosphorylates IκBα to trigger its destruction; p65 phosphorylation is also what permits CBP/P300 recruitment.
- [[SIRT1]] — deacetylates p65 at Lys310, the single most cited anti-inflammatory action of any sirtuin, and also supports p65 degradation.
- [[SIRT2]] — deacetylates p65 in microglia and in arthritis models; the SIRT2 inhibitor AK-7 worsens traumatic brain injury through increased p65 acetylation and nuclear translocation.
- [[SIRT6]] — reduces p65 protein and activity, including by lowering P300 in cardiomyocytes and by deacetylating H3K9 at NF-κB promoters.
- [[TNFα]] — the archetypal stimulus; TNF-α/TNFR signalling drives IKK activation, p65 release, and the inflammatory gene programme that feeds back to produce more TNF-α.
- [[P300]] — CBP/P300 acetylates p65 and is required for its transcriptional activity; acetyl-p65 at Lys310 then recruits BRD4 to sustain the response.
- [[Apoptosis]] — RelA induces TRAF1/TRAF2 and thereby blocks caspase activation, which is the mechanistic basis of both tumour chemotherapy resistance and the anti-apoptotic arm of NF-κB.
- [[SARS-CoV-2]] — RelA is a key regulator of the interferon response during SARS-CoV-2 infection, connecting NF-κB control to the acute and long-term inflammatory sequelae of COVID.
- [[SASP]] — sustained RelA activity is one of the transcriptionally dominant inputs sustaining the senescence-associated secretory phenotype.
