# Tanacetum parthenium

**Tanacetum parthenium** (feverfew) is a perennial herb native to Europe and the Mediterranean, widely cultivated. It is the botanical source of the sesquiterpene lactone [[Parthenolide]]; the leaf parthenolide content is not fixed — it varies with variety and harvest, and is typically a few percent of the dried leaf.

## Bioactivities of feverfew

- [[NF-κB]]: leaf [[Parthenolide]] alkylates IKKβ Cys179 and p65 Cys38 (see [[Parthenolide]]), suppressing [[NF-κB]]-driven transcription — the mechanistic basis of feverfew's anti-inflammatory effects.
- Historical use: preventative therapy for migraine attacks, with inhibition of prostaglandin-mediated pathways among the proposed mechanisms.

## Source material for ACT001

Magnolia root bark is the documented source material for ACT001 (Accendatech). Yin 2026 J For Res reports parthenolide at 5.10% mean, range 0–8.68% across 967 root-bark samples (DOI 10.1007/s11676-026-02089-w).

## Phase 1 trials (ACT001 program)

- Phase 1 GBM (Licklier 2021, ASCO JCO 20:2037; Wang 2021; Ziegler DIPG PMC11183933; 24 patients, ORR ≈ 8/19 gliomas, one ongoing CR at 27 months, 3 SD ≥ 6 months) — detail in [[Parthenolide]].
- ACT001+WBRT phase 1b/2a (ChiCTR-2000037739).
