# Curcuma longa

**Curcuma longa** is a perennial rhizome plant (turmeric); the **rhizome** is the drug of interest. Its dominant phenolic compound is **Curcunin** — a polyphenolic diferuloylmethane derived from the rhizome (per Curcunin).

## Mechanisms of Interest

- **P300/CBP histone acetyltransferase inhibition** — [[Curcumin]] is a selective [[P300]]/[[CBP]] HAT inhibitor (IC50 ~25 µM; per Curcunin), blocking histone acetylation at p300/CBP target genes.
- **H3K27ac at super-enhancers, NF-κB targets down** — repression of [[NF-κB]] targets ([[IL6]], [[IL8]], [[TNFα]]) and reduced [[H3K27ac]] at super-enhancers of pro-proliferative genes (per Curcunin).
- **Senescence via p16INK4A, p38 MAPK, SASP** — At 10–40 µM in vitro, curcumin induces [[p16INK4A|p16^INK4a^]]-dependent senescence via oxidative stress and [[p38 MAPK]] activation, producing [[SASP|Senescence-Associated Secretory Phenotype]] (per Curcunin; Tossetta et al., Mol Biol Rep 2025 — low doses induce senescence-like effects while higher concentrations are cytotoxic).
