# Membrane Contact Site

## Definition

A membrane contact site (MCS) is a region where two organelles approach within a few nanometers without membrane fusion. The apposition is maintained by protein tethers and supports local exchange of ions, lipids, and metabolites as well as signaling that is spatially restricted to the interface. Contact sites are distinct from vesicular trafficking: no cargo-filled vesicle buds at the interface and the two membrane bilayers remain separate. Contacts are dynamic; tethering and untethering are regulated by small GTPases and their regulatory proteins.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Mitochondria and lysosomes communicate by unidirectional vesicular pathways and by bidirectional pathways including signaling and membrane contact sites. M-L contacts contain no autophagosome markers and show no bulk transfer of mitochondrial or lysosomal content, distinguishing them from [[Mitophagy]] and [[Mitochondrial-Derived Vesicles]].

## Molecular Tethers

Tethers are typically protein complexes that bridge the two cytosolic membrane faces. Well-characterized examples include the [[MFN2]]-dependent linkage between mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum, the ORP1L-mediated PI(4)P signaling platform at a three-way ER–lysosome–mitochondrion contact, and the RAB7-GTP-dependent tether between lysosomes and mitochondria. Contact identity can depend on the lipid composition of both membranes: PtdIns(4)P and PtdIns(3,5)P₂ recruit distinct tethering and effector proteins. Tethering is reversible; hydrolysis of the GTPase that holds the interface releases the two membranes.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> Lysosomal GTP-bound [[Rab7]] binds an undetermined mitochondrial partner to promote M-L tethering, while [[Fis1]] on the outer mitochondrial membrane recruits [[TBC1D15]], a RAB7 GAP, converting RAB7-GTP to RAB7-GDP and causing untethering. ORP1L-mediated PI(4)P signaling at an ER–lysosome–mitochondrion three-way contact contributes to mitochondrial division.

## Functions

MCSs perform four classes of function. Ion transfer is exemplified by Ca²⁺ flux from lysosomes into mitochondria through [[TRPML1]], VDAC1, and [[Mitochondrial calcium uniporter]]. Lipid transfer is exemplified by cholesterol movement from endolysosomes to mitochondria through [[STARD3]] and by sterol exchange between the ER and endocytic organelles. Metabolite and metal transfer includes iron delivery and amino-acid movement. Signaling functions include ROS-dependent regulation of lysosomal channels and proton transfer that contributes to lysosomal acidification. The same interface can also coordinate organelle dynamics: M-L contacts are enriched at [[DRP1]]-positive mitochondrial fission sites.

## Organelle Examples

ER–mitochondria contacts, historically termed mitochondria-associated membranes, are platforms for Ca²⁺ transfer and lipid synthesis. Peroxisome–mitochondria contacts couple the redox metabolism of the two organelles, with ROS transfer at the interface regulating mitochondrial redox state. ER–lysosome contacts mediate cholesterol egress and participate in three-way contacts with mitochondria. In lipid storage disease, enlarged or donut-shaped mitochondria have been proposed to increase surface area available for contacts with other organelles, although this interpretation remains a suggestion rather than an established mechanism.

## Mitochondria-Lysosome Contacts

[[Mitochondria-Lysosome Contact Site|M-L contacts]] are RAB7-GTP-dependent and exchange Ca²⁺, iron, amino acids, and cholesterol. They are not a single molecular species: [[GDAP1]]–[[LAMP1]] contacts form a second population that behaves as a redox-sensitive microdomain. Whether these populations are molecularly distinct, partially overlapping, or alternative regulatory states of one membrane interface remains unknown. Contact number declines during cellular senescence, with consequent loss of mitochondria-to-lysosome proton transfer and impaired lysosomal acidification.

> [!info]
> Source: [[_document_ - Mitochondria-lysosome crosstalk in health and disease]]
> M-L contact sites function as hubs where ion exchange, lipid transfer, redox homeostasis, and signaling converge to coordinate organelle behavior, rather than as points of simple membrane apposition. Whether GDAP1–LAMP1 and RAB7-dependent contacts are distinct populations, partially overlapping machineries, or different regulatory states of the same interface remains unknown.

## Unresolved Questions

The review identifies the molecular language exchanged at contacts, the spatial and temporal reorganization of contact networks across seconds to years, and the integration of pairwise contacts into a multi-organelle interactome as open problems. Most mechanistic data derive from cultured cells; tissue-specific contact programs in vivo remain to be defined.
