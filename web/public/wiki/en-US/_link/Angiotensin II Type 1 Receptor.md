# Angiotensin II Type 1 Receptor

The **angiotensin II type 1 receptor (AT1R)** is the dominant cell-surface receptor for [[Angiotensin II]] and the molecular anchor of the classic pressor arm of the renin-angiotensin system. It is encoded by *AGTR1* and couples preferentially to Gαq/11, transducing angiotensin II binding into phospholipase C activation, intracellular calcium rise, and a broad transcriptional program. Because it sits upstream of vasoconstriction, inflammation, oxidative stress, aldosterone release, and TGF-β-driven matrix deposition, AT1R is one of the most heavily drug-targeted receptors in cardiovascular medicine.

> [!info] Why it matters for aging research
> Sustained AT1R signaling is a driver of vascular stiffness, cardiac and renal fibrosis, and chronic low-grade inflammation — the same substrate that underlies arterial hypertension and cardiac remodeling in aged organisms. This makes the AT1R axis a recurring node between cardiovascular aging, [[Inflammaging]], and redox biology.

## Structure & Organization

- AT1R is a 359-residue class A rhodopsin-like GPCR, with seven transmembrane helices connected by three extracellular loops and three intracellular loops.
- A disulfide bond between Cys101 and Cys260 in the extracellular loops forms the ligand-binding pocket. Angiotensin II engages the receptor mainly through its C-terminal carboxylate interacting with a positively charged pocket that includes Arg169, His272, and Asp74 — an extended binding mode not seen in most peptide GPCRs.
- The intracellular C-terminus contains a canonical PDZ-binding motif that binds MAGI-2–type scaffolding adaptors (MAGI-2 has no vault note; see Linking Summary), anchoring AT1R into basolateral membrane microdomains in epithelial cells.

> [!warning] Receptor subtype nomenclature
> Rodents carry two paralogs, *Agtr1a* and *Agtr1b*, whereas humans have a single *AGTR1*. Mouse *Agtr1a* knockout mice — the strain used for essentially all cardiovascular AT1R work — recapitulate the broad human phenotype, so cross-species inference about cardiovascular AT1R is reasonable. Pharmacology differs modestly: suramin and losartan have different affinity profiles at murine versus human AT1R, a common source of failed cross-species translation.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

Angiotensin II binding to AT1R drives three interlocked arms:

- **Gαq/11 arm.** Phospholipase Cβ hydrolyzes PIP2 into IP₃ and DAG. IP₃ gates calcium release from the endoplasmic reticulum; DAG together with calcium activates PKC. The resulting calcium transients are what make AT1R a vasoconstrictor and a trigger of cardiomyocyte hypertrophy.
- **RhoGEF arm.** AT1R couples to p115-RhoGEF, activating [[RhoA]] and hence [[Cytoskeleton]] remodeling. This underlies smooth-muscle contraction, endothelial junction destabilization, and much of the fibrotic and migratory behavior of cells exposed to angiotensin II.
- **Transcription.** Activated PKC and calcium-dependent calmodulin kinases converge on [[ERK]], [[JNK]], and p38 [[MAPK]], and on [[NF-κB]]. In vascular endothelium this upregulates [[VCAM-1]], [[ICAM-1]], and selectin-family adhesion molecules, driving monocyte recruitment into the subendothelial space — a mechanistically central step in [[Atherosclerosis]].

> [!important] Biased agonism is a real and contested concept
> AT1R signaling is the textbook case for "functional selectivity": ligands such as TRV027 ([[Valsartan]]-adjacent academic work) and TRV055 have been reported to favor β-arrestin recruitment over G-protein activation, and TRV027 was developed specifically to retain blood-pressure-lowering benefit while minimizing pro-fibrotic Gq signaling in heart failure. Structural work confirms that G-protein-biased and arrestin-biased ligands stabilize different conformations of the receptor–arrestin complex (PMID: 40974303). The magnitude and in vivo relevance of this bias remain disputed, since many of the original results relied on reconstituted systems and pharmacokinetic confounding.

## Physiological Function

AT1R is expressed on vascular smooth muscle, cardiac myocytes, adrenal zona glomerulosa cells, renal proximal tubule and glomerulus, hepatocytes, adipocytes, fibroblasts, monocytes/macrophages, and in circumventricular brain regions where it mediates the central pressor response and thirst drive.

- **Vascular tone** — Gq-dependent calcium and RhoA-driven contraction; opposes [[Nitric Oxide|NO]]-mediated relaxation.
- **Aldosterone and sodium retention** — adrenal stimulation promotes sodium reabsorption at the distal nephron.
- **Proximal tubular sodium handling** — AT1R drives Na⁺/H⁺ exchange and increases luminal sodium delivery, suppressing the tubuloglomerular feedback that would otherwise limit filtration.
- **Ventral respiratory and fluid-balance circuits** — hypothalamic AT1R mediates Ang II–evoked thirst and the angiotensin-driven component of ventilatory drive.
- **Long-term structural effects** — chronic stimulation raises TGF-β signaling, matrix metalloproteinase expression, and fibroblast-to-myofibroblast conversion.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **Essential hypertension.** Excess AT1R signaling is the proximate effector of angiotensin II-driven blood pressure elevation; [[Hypertension]] and its complications, including [[Stroke]] and left ventricular hypertrophy, remain the core clinical rationale for AT1R blockade.
- **Heart failure and cardiac remodeling.** Angiotensin II acting on cardiomyocyte AT1R drives pathologic [[Cardiac Hypertrophy]], interstitial [[Cardiac Fibrosis]], and maladaptive remodeling. AT1R blockade reduces adverse remodeling and is one of the four foundational neurohormonal drug classes in chronic systolic heart failure.
- **Kidney disease.** AT1R-mediated intraglomerular efferent arteriolar constriction maintains filtration pressure at the cost of chronic hyperfiltration, contributing to albuminuria and glomerulosclerosis; AT1R blockade is renoprotective in diabetic kidney disease and slows progression of [[Chronic Kidney Disease]] with [[Proteinuria]].
- **Atherosclerosis and vascular aging.** Beyond blood pressure, AT1R-dependent endothelial activation, monocyte recruitment, and pro-inflammatory transcription accelerate plaque initiation and vascular stiffening with age.
- **Metabolic crosstalk.** AT1R signaling is antagonistic to [[Insulin]] action in vascular and skeletal muscle tissue and is linked to [[Insulin Resistance]], adipocyte dysfunction, and hepatic steatosis.
- **Mitochondrial quality.** Angiotensin II promotes [[Mitochondrial ROS|mtROS]] generation, in part by activating [[NADPH Oxidase]] at the mitochondrial membrane and through Ca²⁺ overload. In cardiomyocytes this contributes to impaired [[Autophagy]], defective mitophagy, and opening of the [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore|mPTP]] — linking AT1R to the cardiomyocyte cell-death machinery.

> [!warning] An unresolved mechanistic question
> Whether AT1R activation generates ROS mainly via NADPH oxidase, via mitochondrial complex I reverse electron flow downstream of Ca²⁺ overload, or via a vicious cycle between the two remains incompletely settled. Sources differ, and the relative contribution is tissue- and dose-dependent; mechanistic claims should be scoped accordingly.

## Pharmacology

- **Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs)** — losartan, valsartan, telmisartan, candesartan, irbesartan, and eprosartan competitively occupy the Ang II-binding pocket. The collective vault note is [[Valsartan]]. Candesartan and telmisartan have documented partial agonist activity at AT1R in some systems, which is a debated source of their pleiotropic off-target profiles.
- **Bias toward arrestin.** TRV027 (a peptide ligand) and related ligands are the principal clinical-development effort in the arrestin-biased direction.
- **Degradation and signaling termination.** GRK2/3-mediated phosphorylation recruits β-arrestins, which uncouple G-protein activation and route AT1R to clathrin-dependent [[Endocytosis]]. Internalized AT1R continues to signal from endosomes, and recycling versus lysosomal degradation is regulated in part by [[Doxorubicin]]- and oxidative-stress-sensitive mechanisms — relevant because cardiotoxic anthracyclines and chronic oxidative stress both impair receptor trafficking.

> [!info] Contrast with AT2R
> The type 2 receptor mediates vasodilation, anti-proliferation, and anti-inflammatory effects, largely via Mas/Src signaling and nitric oxide release. AT1R/AT2R balance — rather than AT1R absolute activity — is increasingly treated as the functionally relevant quantity, which complicates the interpretation of single-receptor blockade experiments.

## Connections

- [[Angiotensin II]] — AT1R is the receptor through which this peptide executes nearly all of its pressor and pro-inflammatory actions; the two notes are the ligand–receptor half of the renin-angiotensin axis and should be read together.
- [[NADPH Oxidase]] — AT1R signaling is a major activator of NADPH oxidase in vascular smooth muscle and endothelium, making it a dominant source of Ang II-induced [[Oxidative Stress]] and a major contributor to vascular ROS in [[Atherosclerosis]].
- [[eNOS]] — AT1R opposes nitric oxide bioavailability by suppressing endothelial NOS activity and by scavenging NO through superoxide, so the AT1R/eNOS ratio is a functional readout of endothelial health.
- [[SIRT1]] — SIRT1 activation has been reported to downregulate AT1R expression and to attenuate Ang II–induced hypertension in vascular smooth muscle, establishing a direct sirtuin–receptor crosstalk edge that motivates the [[Resveratrol]] literature.
- [[Cardiomyopathy]] — Ang II acting on cardiomyocyte AT1R drives pathological hypertrophy and interstitial fibrosis; the resulting structural remodeling is a core mechanism in hypertensive and diabetic cardiomyopathy.
- [[Autophagy]] — AT1R activity suppresses autophagic flux in cardiomyocytes and renal cells, and SIRT6-dependent restoration of autophagy mitigates Ang II–induced damage, tying the receptor to proteostasis.
- [[Mitochondrial Permeability Transition Pore]] — sustained AT1R signaling raises mitochondrial calcium and ROS, promoting mPTP opening, [[Ferroptosis]]-adjacent oxidative injury, and cardiomyocyte death.
- [[Chronic Kidney Disease]] — AT1R-mediated efferent arteriolar constriction and mesangial expansion underlie hyperfiltration, albuminuria, and progressive renal functional loss, which is why ARBs are first-line in proteinuric kidney disease.
