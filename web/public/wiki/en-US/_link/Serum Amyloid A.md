# Serum Amyloid A

**Serum amyloid A (SAA)** is a family of plasma apolipoproteins (SAA1 and SAA2 in humans) synthesized mainly by hepatocytes as acute-phase reactants. SAA rises up to 1000-fold within 24–48 hours of an acute inflammatory stimulus, driven by IL-6 and IL-1 acting on the hepatic acute-phase programme, and is among the most sensitive serum markers of inflammation.

## Overview

Serum amyloid A is a family of plasma apolipoproteins, SAA1 and SAA2, that constitute the dominant acute-phase response of the human liver. Synthesized within 24-48 hours of an inflammatory stimulus under the control of IL-6 and IL-1, SAA rises up to three orders of magnitude, transports cholesterol and other hydrophobic cargo, chemoattracts monocytes and neutrophils, and feeds back to amplify cytokine production. Sustained elevation is the precursor to AA amyloidosis.

## Structure & Domains

- **Genes:** *SAA1* and *SAA2* (and *SAA3*/*SAA4* pseudogenes or minor variants in some species), clustered on chromosome 11p15.5. SAA1 and SAA2 are acute-phase genes; *SAA4* is constitutively expressed.
- **Sequence:** ~127 amino acids after signal-peptide removal; the N-terminal region contains the amyloidogenic motif and the highly hydrophobic "HE1–HE5" heptapeptide repeats that form the fibril core; the C-terminal region binds HDL and the CD36 receptor.
- **Acute-phase regulation:** hepatic transcription depends on IL-6/STAT3 and IL-1/NF-κB acting through the proximal and distal IL-6 response elements of the SAA promoter; glucocorticoids and NF-κB2/p52 further modulate expression. Local SAA is also produced by macrophages, hepatocytes, adipocytes and cancer cells at sites of inflammation.

## Mechanism of Action & Pathways

- **Acute-phase transport:** SAA is secreted with HDL and acts as a carrier for cholesterol and other hydrophobic cargo back to the liver (a reverse cholesterol-transport-like role), and it modulates HDL's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory functions.
- **Signalling:** SAA binds CD36, TLR2/TLR4, RAGE and the formyl peptide receptor FPR2, activating NF-κB and MAPK pathways and amplifying cytokine production (TNF, IL-1, IL-6) — a feedback loop that sustains inflammation.
- **Chemoattraction:** SAA is a potent chemoattractant for monocytes and neutrophils, recruiting them to inflamed tissue and promoting [[Inflammation]].
- **Matrix effects:** SAA induces metalloproteinases and promotes local matrix degradation; in the joint it drives protease and cytokine release in rheumatoid arthritis.
- **Amyloidogenesis:** with sustained high serum SAA (chronically >10–50 mg/L or more), the N-terminal fragments misfold into beta-sheet-rich amyloid fibrors that deposit in kidney, spleen, liver, adrenals, bone marrow and gut, producing systemic AA amyloidosis. Deposited AA fibrils are cytotoxic and provoke [[Autophagy]] failure and [[Apoptosis]] in surrounding cells.

## Physiological Function

SAA is a rapid, sensitive and inexpensive biomarker of acute inflammation, used clinically to monitor disease activity in infections, autoimmune disease and cancer. Its baseline concentration is near zero in healthy adults and rises steeply with any stimulus that raises IL-6. It contributes to host defence by opsonizing bacteria and by modulating neutrophil and monocyte behaviour.

## Pathology & Clinical Relevance

- **AA amyloidosis:** the classic complication of chronic uncontrolled inflammation — rheumatoid arthritis (historical, now rare with effective therapy), chronic infections (bronchiectasis, osteomyelitis, tuberculosis, chronic malaria), autoinflammatory diseases (Familial Mediterranean fever, cryopyrin-associated syndromes), and chronic kidney disease with nephrotic proteinuria. Secondary (AA) amyloidosis predominantly involves kidney, causing proteinuria and renal failure; diagnosis rests on histology with Congo red staining and typing by immunohistochemistry or mass spectrometry.
- **Cardiovascular risk:** SAA is an independent predictor of cardiovascular events; it is also a component of the "amyloidogenic" acute-phase response in chronic kidney disease and is linked to [[Atherosclerosis]] and [[Inflammaging]].
- **Cancer:** tumour-derived SAA supports tumour growth, angiogenesis and [[Metastasis]], and serum SAA is used prognostically in several cancers including [[Melanoma]] and colorectal cancer.
- **Metabolic disease:** SAA is an adipokine elevated in [[Type 2 Diabetes]] and [[Insulin Resistance]], linking adipose inflammation to atherogenesis.
- **Therapeutics:** control of the underlying inflammation is the definitive treatment of AA amyloidosis (biologics in rheumatoid arthritis and autoinflammatory disease, colchicine in familial Mediterranean fever, anti-IL-1/IL-6 blockade). SAA itself is not currently a direct drug target, though SAA-derived peptides and CD36 antagonists are explored.
