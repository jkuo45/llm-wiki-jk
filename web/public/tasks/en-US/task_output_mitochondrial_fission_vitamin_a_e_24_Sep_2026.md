# Mitochondrial Fission and the Fat-Soluble Vitamins: How Vitamins A and E Set the Fission–Fusion Balance

**Date:** 24_Sep_2026 10:51 PM PDT

---

## Summary

Mitochondrial fission is usually taught as a protein-proproblem: recruit [[DRP1]], constrict, cut. The web research for this output shows the cut is gated by the membrane it happens in. Three control layers sit on top of the GTPase — post-translational modification of DRP1 (phosphorylation, SUMOylation, ISGylation, and cysteine oxidation), receptor availability (MFF, MID49/51, FIS1), and the lipid substrate itself (cardiolipin externalized to the outer membrane under stress). The two fat-soluble vitamins plug into different layers:

- **[[Vitamin E]]** is physically *there*: ~90% of mitochondrial α-tocopherol resides in the outer mitochondrial membrane — the exact leaflet where DRP1 assembles — and is recycled by ubiquinol from the electron transport chain. It sets the redox tone that determines whether DRP1's redox-sensitive Cys644 stays reduced or gets sulfenylated into a hyperactive state.
- **Vitamin A** acts twice through two different metabolites: retinol is a non-genomic cofactor for mitochondrial PKCδ that drives pyruvate dehydrogenase and oxidative phosphorylation, while all-trans retinoic acid raises DRP1 protein levels and shifts the network toward fission. Excess retinoid is independently pro-oxidant — mitochondrial swelling, lipid peroxidation, permeability transition — and that damage is blunted by vitamin E analogs.

> [!NOTE]
> **Core finding**
> Fission is a redox sensor with a lipid cofactor. Oxidative conditions (sulfenylated DRP1, cardiolipin externalization) accelerate it; the membrane-phase antioxidant tier (vitamin E + ubiquinol) brakes it. Vitamin A is the rheostat on the other side — physiological retinol supports respiration, retinoic acid raises DRP1, and supraphysiological retinoid burns the membrane the machinery runs on. The A/E interaction is not hypothetical: Trolox (a vitamin E derivative) rescues retinol-induced mitochondrial damage in isolated mitochondria and retinol-deprivation cell death in lymphocytes.

## The Fission Machinery

### Assembly sequence

[[Mitochondrial Fission]] runs in defined steps:

1. **Pre-constriction at ER contact sites** — ER tubules wrap the mitochondrion; INF2/Spire1C nucleate actin, myosin II pinches the tube to ~70 nm before any DRP1 arrives.
2. **Recruitment** — cytosolic DRP1 is captured by outer-membrane receptors. MFF is the dominant receptor and also stimulates DRP1's GTPase activity (further potentiated by cardiolipin); MID49/MID51 bind inactive dimeric DRP1 and, per the 2026 *Nature Cell Biology* mito-scanner paper, co-diffuse with DRP1 oligomers as they browse the surface for preconstricted sites. Simultaneous loss of MFF + MID49 + MID51 phenocopies DRP1 knockout — the three receptors are non-redundant. FIS1's role is debated; genetic knockout has little effect on housekeeping fission but FIS1 coordinates peripheral, mitophagy-linked division.
3. **Helical assembly and scission** — GTP-driven polymerization around the tube; a cryo-EM structure of full-length DRP1 (2024) shows an auto-inhibited dimer whose BSE lock and variable domain (VD) must unlock before assembly-competent conformers form. The 136-residue intrinsically disordered VD is the lipid-sensing element.

### Post-translational control of DRP1

| Modification | Site / enzyme | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phosphorylation | Ser616 (CDK1/cyclin B, PKCδ) | Promotes fission |
| Phosphorylation | Ser637 (PKA) | Inhibits translocation/GTPase; calcineurin dephosphorylation releases the brake |
| SUMOylation | MAPL, multiple Lys in VD | Stabilizes DRP1 on mitochondria; SENP5 de-SUMOylates and inhibits |
| ISGylation | HERC5 | Fission-competent; deISGylation → hyperfusion and TRIM25-mediated degradation |
| **Sulfenylation** | **Cys644** (oxidation) | **Increases GTPase activity → fragmentation** |
| S-nitrosylation | Cys (NO) | Reported activation, contested |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The redox switch**
> PDIA1 is a thiol reductase that keeps DRP1-Cys644 reduced. Loss of PDIA1 (as in diabetic endothelium) sulfenylates Cys644, raises DRP1 GTPase activity, and fragments mitochondria — and the resulting mtROS feeds back to oxidize more DRP1, a self-reinforcing loop. MitoTEMPO and mito-catalase break the loop; the C644A mutant is protected. This is the molecular junction where [[Mitochondrial ROS]] becomes morphology.

### Cardiolipin: the lipid cofactor

An NMR/structural study (Mahajan et al., *PNAS* 2021) identified a conserved **WRG motif** in the DRP1 variable domain that intercalates into the bilayer and binds cardiolipin specifically. Cardiolipin normally lives in the inner membrane but is externalized to the outer membrane under stress (apoptosis, mitophagy), where it:

- stabilizes assembled DRP1 and promotes stress-induced **hyperfragmentation** (the punctiform morphology needed for autophagosome engulfment),
- drives a disorder-to-order transition of the VD that relieves autoinhibition,
- is required — WRG mutants fail specifically under CCCP stress while housekeeping fission is only partly impaired.

Unbound outer-membrane cardiolipin is converted to phosphatidic acid by mitochondrial phospholipase D; excess PA hyperactivates Mfn1/2 and can produce hyperfusion or mitophagy-resistant "donut" mitochondria. So the same lipid, in the right place, cuts; in the wrong place, fuses.

## Fission as Quality-Control Triage

Fission is not destruction — it is **segregation**. A single asymmetric division produces one high-ΔΨm daughter (fusion-competent) and one low-ΔΨm daughter (mitophagy-competent). Drp1-mediated severing isolates damaged segments so [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] mitophagy runs only on the condemned half; the healthy network is spared. This is why both excessive fission (fragmentation, bioenergetic failure) and fission *blockade* (damaged mitochondria accumulate) are pathological — the balance in [[Mitochondrial Dynamics]] is the phenotype, not the absolute rate.

Aging shifts it: DRP1 activity falls in old mice (neurons, skeletal muscle, oocytes), MFN2/DRP1 ratio rises, networks elongate — an aged cell loses the ability to prune, not the ability to fuse.

## Vitamin E at the Fission Site

### Location and recycling

The 2003 MITOCHONDRIAL α-tocopherol fractionation study (rat muscle mitochondria) found **~90% of mitochondrial α-tocopherol in the outer membrane** — precisely the leaflet where DRP1 assembles and where cardiolipin externalization is read. Despite the outer-membrane location, succinate and other respiratory substrates spare that tocopherol from oxidative loss, and the sparing is blocked by TTFA but not myxothiazol: **ubiquinol is the electron donor that recycles α-tocopherol in situ**. Ferricytochrome c oxidation of the vitamin raises total F2-isoprostanes; succinate prevents both.

This gives a three-member lipid-phase relay inside the organelle itself:

> ETC → ubiquinol → α-tocopheroxyl radical → α-tocopherol (plus the ascorbate arm at the interface; see [[Lipid Peroxidation]] task output)

Note the structural echo with the FSP1–CoQ10 axis: the same reduced-quinone pool that backs up GPX4 in ferroptosis is the local reductant for mitochondrial vitamin E.

### What vitamin E deficiency does to mitochondria

- Rats vitamin-E-depleted 48 weeks: undetectable tissue vitamin E, MDA +82% in gastrocnemius vs +41% in liver; **complex I and complex IV activities fall in muscle** while liver MRC activities rise or are unchanged. Skeletal muscle and neuron are selectively vulnerable — the same tissue hierarchy seen clinically in AVED.
- In vitro NADPH-driven lipid peroxidation of submitochondrial particles inhibits **complex I first**, complex IV less — complex I is the peroxidation-sensitive node.
- α-Tocopherol deficiency in rats produces the classic electron-microscopy triad: swelling, cristae fragmentation, dense matrix granules — i.e., a *morphological* phenotype, not only a biochemical one.
- α/γ-tocopherol in APP-overexpressing SH-SY5Y cells raise respiratory capacity, membrane potential, and complex IV activity in a dose-dependent manner (5 µM inactive; 80–100 µM active).

### The mechanism linking vitamin E to fission rate

No paper yet shows "α-tocopherol binds DRP1." The supported chain is indirect but tight:

1. mtROS from the ETC oxidize DRP1 Cys644 (and cardiolipin).
2. Sulfenylated DRP1 is hyperactive; oxidized cardiolipin externalizes and licenses hyperfragmentation.
3. Outer-membrane α-tocopherol, recycled by ubiquinol, intercepts the lipid-peroxidation chain before it reaches cardiolipin and keeps local ROS below the sulfenylation threshold.
4. Depleting the vitamin removes the brake → feedforward fragmentation (exactly the loop that MitoTEMPO interrupts experimentally).

So vitamin E is an **anti-hyperfragmentation** nutrient: it does not stop housekeeping fission, it stops stress-driven fission by removing its trigger.

## Vitamin A: Two Metabolites, Two Directions

Vitamin A is not one signal. Retinol, retinal, and [[Retinoic Acid]] diverge sharply in their mitochondrial effects.

### Retinol — the non-genomic energy cofactor

Acin-Perez et al. (*PNAS* 2010) showed retinol is required for mitochondrial metabolic fitness **without conversion to other retinoids**:

- Retinol deprivation drops respiration and ATP synthesis to basal; physiological retinol restores higher output within minutes, and disulfiram (blocking retinal dehydrogenase) does not prevent it — retinoic acid is excluded as the mediator.
- Retinol is a **required cofactor for mitochondrial PKCδ**; the PKCδ/retinol complex signals the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, increasing acetyl-CoA flux into the TCA cycle. Only pyruvate (not glutamate/malate or succinate) supports the effect — the control point is PDH, not the ETC directly.
- In vitamin A-deficient LRAT-knockout mice, the baseline response is blunted and corrected within 3 h by intraperitoneal vitamin A.

This positions retinol as a **nutritional sensor** for oxidative phosphorylation — the mechanistic answer to why vitamin A deficiency causes pathologies that retinoic acid cannot reverse (immune deficiency, male sterility).

The deprivation side: retinol withdrawal or anhydroretinol competition in Jurkat T cells causes mitochondrial-derived ROS within 4 h, ΔΨm collapse, ATP/NAD⁺ crash, PARP-1 hyperactivation, and caspase-independent necrotic programmed cell death. Myxothiazol and CCCP prevent the ROS; **the vitamin E derivative Trolox rescues ROS, ATP/NAD⁺, and largely the cell death** — the cleanest experimental A/E interaction in this literature.

### Retinoic acid — the fission inducer

Chidipi et al. (*Cells* 2021):

- ATRA (0.1–10 µM, 24 h) dose-dependently raises **DRP1 protein** in HEK293 and HL-1 cardiomyocytes, and in mouse heart in vivo (150 mg/kg i.p.).
- DRP1 redistributes to mitochondria; OPA1, MFN1/2, and FIS1 are unchanged — the effect is specific to the fission arm.
- Network quantification (stochastic modeling): increased tip-to-tip fission rate; smaller area/perimeter, higher circularity — a fragmented network.
- Earlier work (Vantaggiato et al.) found the same DRP1 upregulation during ATRA-driven neuronal differentiation of P19 cells; RIP140/PGC-1α axis carries the accompanying mitochondrial maturation in SH-SY5Y neural differentiation.

Retinoic acid also drives **biogenesis** through a separate route: p38 MAPK → PGC-1α → NRF1/TFAM (demonstrated in sheep muscle and primary myoblasts; si-PGC-1α abolishes the effect), and in human hepatocytes through **PPARδ** (RAR knockdown does not block it; PPARδ knockdown does) — raising PGC-1α/β, NRF1, mtDNA, β-oxidation, and ATP. So RA increases *both* mitochondrial number and the fission rate per mitochondrion — consistent with its role in differentiation, where new biomass must be pruned and distributed.

In muscle stem cells, vitamin A signaling maintains quiescence: dietary vitamin A restriction in young animals mimics aging (premature MuSC activation, oxidative DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction); RAR/RXR agonists reduce oxidative damage and boost OXPHOS; **STRA6 knockdown raises mtROS, fragments mitochondria, depolarizes them, and increases lipid peroxidation (BODIPY C11)** — again linking retinoid status to the lipid-peroxidation/fission axis.

### Excess retinoid — the pro-oxidant pole

| Observation | Direction | Reference frame |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retinol 3–40 µM → swelling, ΔΨm loss, cyt c release, superoxide, lipoperoxidation | damage | Isolated rat liver mitochondria; **blocked by cyclosporin A *and* Trolox** |
| All-trans/9-cis/13-cis RA → MPT, swelling, cyt c release | damage | Rigobello et al. |
| Vitamin A supplementation → mitochondrial superoxide, lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation, complex IV decline in brain regions | damage | Rat cerebral cortex, cerebellum, striatum, substantia nigra |
| Retinol acetate 3000 IU/kg → ↑LPO, swelling, cyt c release in tumor-bearing rats; 30 IU/kg neutral | dose-dependent damage | Physiological dose harmless, high dose pro-oxidant |
| Vitamin A deficiency → ↓cardiolipin, ↓PUFA, ↑MDA rate, ↓SDH protection | damage on the low end | Rat liver mitochondria |

Both deficiency and excess damage the organelle — a U-shaped dose response with a wide, physiological plateau. Note also the older finding that vitamin E co-treatment alleviated retinol-induced hemolysis, whether by antioxidant action or by membrane fluidity/permeability effects.

## Where A and E Converge

> [!TIP]
> **The shared substrate is the membrane**
> Vitamin A excess generates lipid peroxidation in mitochondrial membranes; vitamin E is the chain-breaker that stops it. Trolox blocks retinol-induced MPT and swelling in isolated mitochondria and retinol-deprivation ROS in lymphocytes. Conversely, vitamin A deficiency alters membrane lipid composition (↓cardiolipin, ↑lysolecithin, ↓PUFA unsaturation) so that mitochondria peroxidize faster in vitro — vitamin A status sets the *substrate* vitamin E has to defend.

Three mechanistic meeting points:

1. **Cardiolipin.** Its peroxidation and externalization license DRP1 hyperfragmentation and mitophagy; vitamin A deficiency lowers cardiolipin content, vitamin E defends cardiolipin from oxidation. Both vitamins therefore set the input to the WRG-motif checkpoint.
2. **DRP1 Cys644 sulfenylation.** The ROS feedforward loop that drives pathological fragmentation is quenched by the membrane antioxidant tier (vitamin E/ubiquinol) — and the retinol-deprivation ROS burst runs through the same mitochondrial source, rescued by Trolox.
3. **PGC-1α vs DRP1.** Retinoic acid raises both (biogenesis and fission) — new mitochondria plus the pruning machinery. Vitamin E acts on neither transcriptionally; it protects the product. Supraphysiological retinoid tips the balance: DRP1 up + membrane oxidation up = fragmentation without adequate replacement.

### Practical asymmetry

- **Vitamin E**: dietary-level tocopherols are meal-coupled and transient; deficiency is the damaging state (myopathy, ataxia, MRC failure), not ordinary intake. Get it from oils/nuts; the outer-membrane pool is maintained by ubiquinol recycling, not by megadosing.
- **Vitamin A**: the therapeutic index is real — retinoic acid at pharmacologic doses (APL therapy) is both useful and mitotoxic; chronic high-dose retinol supplementation shows oxidative markers in rat brain with **no antioxidant benefit observed**. Physiological doses were neutral in every pro-oxidant assay cited above.

## Open Questions

1. **Direct vitamin E–DRP1 contact?** No study localizes α-tocopherol to DRP1 assembly sites or measures DRP1 GTPase activity as a function of tocopherol status. The sulfenylation chain is inferred from separate literatures (MitoTEMPO experiments; tocopherol fractionation). The experiment — DRP1 oxidation state ± vitamin E depletion ± ubiquinol repletion — is straightforward and apparently unperformed.
2. **Cardiolipin peroxidation species.** Which oxidized cardiolipin species (vs. total externalized cardiolipin) engage the WRG motif, and are they substrates for GPX4 or for the vitamin E/ubiquinol pool?
3. **Retinol–PKCδ scope.** Does the retinol/PKCδ cofactor mechanism operate in post-mitotic tissues at large (it would explain non-RA-responsive deficiency pathologies), and is its output sensitive to vitamin E status?
4. **ATRA's DRP1 transcription.** The promoter mechanism for RA-induced DRP1 upregulation is unreported; RAR occupancy at the *DNM1L* promoter has not been demonstrated.
5. **Tissue hierarchy.** Why muscle and neuron show vitamin-E-deficiency MRC failure while liver does not — outer-membrane tocopherol content, ubiquinol turnover, or PUFA composition?
6. **STRA6 and Ca²⁺-mediated dynamics.** STRA6 is Ca²⁺-sensitive; whether retinol flux through STRA6 tunes fission/fusion via mitochondrial calcium is explicitly proposed and untested.
