# Topical Creatine and Topical Urolithin A in Skin Longevity — Comparative Effects and Combined Use

Date: 17_Nov_2026 09:30 AM PST

## Evidence basis and tiers

Vault notes are used as ground truth for mechanism. [[Creatine]] is the phosphocreatine/[[Creatine Kinase]] [[ATP]]-buffering system. [[Urolithin A]] is the [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] [[Mitophagy]] inducer. Web sources supplement topical effect sizes, since the vault contains one paragraph on topical creatine (`src/notes/_link/_document_ - Creatine in Health and Disease.md` §6.11) and no entity note for topical [[Urolithin A]] beyond the `raw/` iScience paper (D'Amico et al., 2026-09-30, `PMC13657230`). Vault graphify shows no direct [[Creatine]]–[[Mitophagy]] edge; the shortest path is [[Creatine]]→[[Exercise]]→[[Mitophagy]].

Evidence tiers apply as follows: Tier 1 = human randomized trial; Tier 4 = in vitro or ex vivo human cells and tissue models; Tier 5 = hypothesis. Human efficacy claims are stated only for Tier 1 data. Animal and in vitro findings are reported as "in vitro" or "in rodents". Industry sponsorship is noted where it constrains interpretation: topical-UA trials are Amazentis/Timeline and L'Oréal sponsored, with authors as employees and patent holders; topical-creatine studies are Beiersdorf folic-acid/creatine programs; the creatine monograph review is AlzChem-funded.

## Topical creatine

### Mechanism in skin cells

In keratinocytes and fibroblasts, exogenous [[Creatine]] is phosphorylated by [[Creatine Kinase]] to [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)]], regenerating [[ATP]] from [[ADP]] — the creatine phosphate shuttle between mitochondrial production and cytosolic consumption. [[Creatine]] synthesis consumes approximately 40% of whole-body methyl flux; that methyl cost applies to endogenous synthesis via [[Guanidinoacetate (GAA)]] and GAMT, not to topical delivery. Vault graphify links [[Creatine]]→[[ATP]] (regenerates), [[Creatine]]→[[Reactive Oxygen Species]] (reduces), and [[Creatine]]→[[AMPK]] (may modulate).

### Effects in vitro and ex vivo

Stress-induced [[Oxidative Stress]] reduces [[Creatine Kinase]] activity in cutaneous cells. In aged-donor keratinocytes, 24-h pretreatment with 150 μM creatine increased CK activity by 23% and partially restored H₂O₂-suppressed activity by 10-19%, depending on oxidant concentration (Lenz et al. 2005; Tier 4). In dermal fibroblasts, creatine pretreatment reduced oxidant-induced ROS, preserved IGF-1 expression, and reduced IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α (Mahajan et al. 2021, `PMC8622652`; Tier 4). These data are consistent with an antioxidant and bioenergetic mechanism; mitophagy flux was not measured.

### Effects in vivo and formulation confounding

Web review (Werpachowski et al. 2026, `PMC13668104`) identified 4 publications with 8 substudies, cumulative n=344, duration 2-8 weeks. All substudies except Lenz (n=8) used a combination active ingredient: folic acid + creatine (Knott/Fischer) or creatine + guarana + glycerol (Peirano). Reported outcomes include Cutometer firmness increases to +14.6% at 4 weeks, elasticity (Ur/Ue) increases of +7% at 2-5 weeks, crow's-feet wrinkle volume to 89.6% of baseline at 4 weeks, collagen-1A1 mRNA RQ 1.24-1.28, procollagen secretion +14.7%, and jowl volume −0.27 mL at 6 weeks. Because folic acid co-modulates collagen metabolism and DNA synthesis, the creatine-only contribution is not quantified. No biopsy RNA-seq, histology, or [[Mitophagy]]-flux markers were reported in these trials.

### Penetration and tolerability

Dermal penetration of creatine is reported as rapid in pig-skin models, via passive diffusion and the creatine transporter, frequently formulated with liposomes or penetration enhancers (Peirano et al. 2011). No treatment-related irritation, erythema, or hypersensitivity was reported in the 344-participant set at standard concentrations; creatine was non-cytotoxic and non-mutagenic in vitro and did not increase UV sensitivity. Long-term tolerability remains untested.

## Topical urolithin A

### Mechanism

[[Urolithin A]] is a gut-microbial ellagitannin metabolite. In vault terms it is the selective-[[Mitophagy]] arm: depolarization stabilizes [[PINK1]] on the outer mitochondrial membrane, recruits [[Parkin]], ubiquitinates MFN1/MFN2/VDAC1, and engages LC3/p62 clearance, with concurrent mTORC1 inhibition and [[AMPK]]→ULK1 activation. The 50% effective concentration for mitophagy in primary myotubes is 2-5 μM. Oral human data show reduced acylcarnitines and increased mitochondrial gene expression (phase I), and improved muscle endurance with null primary 6-minute walk and maximal ATP outcomes in one 66-person randomized trial (vault note on Nesci & Algieri 2026). Mitophagy mediation in humans remains untested.

### Effects in 2D, 3D, and ex vivo models

In normal human epidermal keratinocytes, 0.5-2.5 μM UA increased basal and maximal respiration, most robust at 1 μM. In reconstructed human epidermis, 1% UA cream increased PINK1/PARK2 expression. In aged-donor full-thickness models (1 μM, 25 d), TOMM20, Ki67, keratin-10, and filaggrin were increased, p16 was reduced, and IL-6 was reduced in cytokine-stimulated keratinocytes. In dermal fibroblasts, old-donor (69-year) respiration was rescued; phospho-ubiquitin (PINK1–Parkin activation marker) was increased to a level similar to the FCCP positive control; CXCL1 (senescence-associated secretory phenotype chemokine) was downregulated while p21 was unchanged, a senomorphic profile without senolysis; vinculin focal-adhesion and actin disassembly defects were rescued; MMP1 was reduced; dermal collagen III, hyaluronic acid, elastin, and collagen VII were increased. In ex vivo skin, PM10+UV-A–induced PINK1 loss and histone carbonylation were attenuated; UVB-induced prostaglandin E2 in reconstructed epidermis was reduced (p=0.0257).

### Effects in human randomized trials

Human repeat-insult patch testing showed no irritation or sensitization. In the aging efficacy trial (NCT05079607, split-face/arm, n=48 women, mean 58.6 years, 8 weeks), 1% UA reduced wrinkle depth −3.4% versus placebo +4.0% at 8 weeks (p=0.0437); 0.5% showed no significant effect. Forearm biopsy RNA-seq (GEO GSE264314) showed the collagen-fibril-organization pathway as the most upregulated gene set, with inflammation and SenMayo senescence gene sets downregulated. In the photodamage trial (n=21 completers, mean 44 years), 1% UA reduced UVB 1.6-minimal-erythema-dose erythema (a*) by −13% versus untreated at 24 h (p=0.02); 0.5% showed no significant benefit. Skin-penetration quantification by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry is absent; durability after discontinuation is not quantified. Authors state that mechanistic effects are penetration-dependent but that clinical improvement suggests biologically active tissue concentrations.

## Comparative effects on skin hallmarks

| Domain | Topical creatine | Topical UA |
|---|---|---|
| Primary mechanism (vault) | [[Creatine]]→[[Phosphocreatine (PCr)]]→[[ATP]] via [[Creatine Kinase]]; antioxidant, MMP-1 counteraction | [[PINK1]]/[[Parkin]] [[Mitophagy]]; [[AMPK]]↑, mTORC1↓ |
| Collagen / extracellular matrix | In vitro collagen-1A1 RQ 1.24-1.28, procollagen +14.7%; in vivo firmness +7% to +14.6%, wrinkle volume −10% (confounded by folic acid) | In vitro/3D collagen III, hyaluronic acid, elastin, collagen VII↑; RNA-seq collagen-fibril-organization↑; wrinkle depth −3.4% (p=0.0437) |
| Inflammation / senescence | IL-6/IL-8/TNF-α↓ in oxidant-stressed fibroblasts (in vitro) | IL-6↓ in keratinocytes/fibroblasts; CXCL1↓ with p21 unchanged (senomorphic); biopsy inflammation/senescence gene sets↓ |
| Photoaging | Reduced UV DNA lesions (olive-tail moment −17% at 5 h); maintained mitochondrial membrane potential after UV (in vitro, n=8) | Reduced prostaglandin E2, histone carbonylation, erythema −13% (p=0.02) |
| Dose threshold | Not isolated (0.2% with guarana/glycerol; folic-acid combinations vary) | 1% active, 0.5% null in both randomized trials |
| Penetration quantified | Yes in pig skin; human dermal data reported as rapid | No; authors state LC-MS/MS data are absent |
| Tolerability | No irritation reported in 344 participants | No irritation/sensitization in patch test and randomized trials |

Both agents converge on mitochondrial fitness and extracellular-matrix preservation, but by opposite mitochondrial actions — ATP buffering in the case of [[Creatine]] versus removal of depolarized mitochondria in the case of [[Urolithin A]]. Oral [[Urolithin A]] is FDA GRAS (2018) with EU novel-food pending; that status does not apply to topical use. Topical creatine has no equivalent regulatory clearing for skin use.

## Combined topical creatine and topical urolithin A

### Status of combination data

The vault has no topical creatine + [[Urolithin A]] study. The only vault pairing is muscle-health context: [[Muscle Hypertrophy]] notes [[Urolithin A]] may augment hypertrophy by enhancing mitochondrial function, ATP supply, and recovery; [[Leucine]] notes creatine or [[Urolithin A]] is often paired in muscle-health strategies. Web search finds no topical or oral creatine + UA randomized trial. The registered UA combination trial indexed in the vault is NCT06990256 (UA 300 mg + [[Fisetin]] 200 mg, oral), not creatine. A creatine + UA skin outcome is therefore untested at Tier 5.

### Mechanistic complementarity

The vault positions the two agents as non-redundant mitochondrial arms, analogous to the Rzeski framework in which [[Urolithin A]] is the selective-[[Mitophagy]] partner to bulk-autophagy agents. A combined exposure would place ATP refueling of the surviving mitochondrial network alongside clearance of depolarized mitochondria in the same cell. This arrangement is consistent with additivity on shared downstream readouts (collagen preservation, SASP suppression, photoaging defense) rather than a shared binding target. Additive effect size is not quantified; no interaction term has been estimated.

### Possible antagonism

Formation of [[Phosphocreatine (PCr)]] requires [[ATP]]. In the skin models reported, UA at 1 μM increased basal and maximal respiration and TOMM20 content, so a net [[ATP]] deficit is not reported in vitro. Whether 1% topical UA plus topical creatine alters respiration differently remains untested. UA inhibits mTORC1 in muscle models; collagen translation requires mTORC1-dependent protein synthesis. Topical UA nevertheless increased dermal collagens in 3D models, so net collagen synthesis was not suppressed in that context; the combined effect on phospho-S6 and procollagen secretion is untested. Both agents reduce reactive-oxygen-species markers; vault hormesis literature notes that moderate ROS pulses are required for Nrf2 and [[AMPK]] signaling. Dual antioxidant exposure could blunt hormetic signaling in principle; neither single-agent skin trial measured Nrf2 nuclear translocation with and without the second agent.

### Formulation and safety of a combined product

[[Creatine]] is hydrophilic and zwitterionic; UA is a lipophilic polyphenol. Single-cream co-formulation has no published stability or penetration data. Creatine degrades to creatinine under acidic pH and heat; UA is phenolic and oxidation-sensitive. Separate-layer or sequential application is also untested. Each agent at tested concentrations showed no irritation individually. Irritation, sensitization, and phototoxicity of a combined formulation have not been reported. No combined patch-test or photodamage study exists.

## Unresolved items

- No 2×2 factorial human trial (vehicle, creatine, UA, combined) with split-face design at 8-12 weeks has been reported. Without such data, additive, subadditive, and antagonistic interaction cannot be distinguished.
- Skin LC-MS/MS tissue concentrations are absent for UA and reported only in pig skin for creatine. Dose–tissue exposure relationships are therefore not quantified.
- Topical creatine trials do not isolate creatine from folic acid or guarana/glycerol; topical UA trials lack long-term follow-up and durability assessment after discontinuation.
- Transcriptional sampling (RNA-seq, SenMayo) exists for topical UA but not for topical creatine; creatine's effect on collagen-fibril-organization and senescence gene sets is untested.
- Combined-product stability, penetration, and combined HRIPT tolerability are not quantified.
