# Summary

The central thesis of [[Hormesis]] — that subtoxic stress triggers adaptive resilience — is now well-established in exercise physiology, [[Caloric Restriction]], and [[Mitohormesis]]. Yet **at least ten other biological domains** exhibit the same biphasic pattern, where the beneficial low-dose response is systematically overlooked or underexplored because the stressor itself carries overwhelmingly negative connotations (radiation, toxins, pain, inflammation, infection, cold, heat, adversity).

The 2024 *Nature* review by Li et al. (101 citations, 36k accesses) and the 2026 Springer "Crucible of Resilience" paper both argue that **80–90% of hormesis research is invisible under standard search terms** because different fields use different language — "adaptive response," "preconditioning," "repeated bout effect," "eustress" — without recognizing they share a common biological substrate.

This report identifies **twelve overlooked or underexplored domains** where negative-connotation stressors produce measurable, clinically relevant benefits through mechanisms convergent with mitohormesis.

## Low-Dose Radiation Hormesis — The Most Controversial Blind Spot

### The Problem
Radiation is so universally framed as harmful that the concept of beneficial low-dose radiation (LDR) faces enormous cultural and regulatory resistance. The Linear No-Threshold (LNT) model — which assumes zero safe dose — dominates regulatory policy despite a growing body of contradictory evidence.

### What the Data Shows
- **Ramsar, Iran**: Inhabitants receive up to **260 mSv/year** (100× global average). Cytogenetic studies show **no significant increase in DNA damage** compared to normal-background populations. Lymphocytes from Ramsar residents show enhanced DNA repair capacity, increased CD69 expression on CD4+ T cells, and elevated serum IgE — consistent with immune priming (Ghiassi-Nejad et al., 2004; Kanani, 2025 scoping review).
- **Atomic bomb survivors near the blast periphery**: Paradoxically lower cancer mortality and longer average lifespans than non-exposed cohorts, possibly via hormetic immune/repair activation.
- **NASA's interest**: Adaptive responses prompted by low-dose radiation may enhance astronaut resilience against infections during long-term spaceflight (Mortazavi studies).
- **Mechanistic evidence**: LDR activates ATM signaling, MAPK/ERK/JNK pathways, p53-mediated DNA repair, and NF-κB — all overlapping with [[NRF2]]/ARE antioxidant defense pathways. The response is biphasic: beneficial at <10 cGy, harmful above.

### Why It's Overlooked
The LNT model is deeply embedded in radiation protection regulation. Acknowledging hormesis would require restructuring safety standards and public communication about nuclear energy, medical imaging, and background radiation.

> [!important]
> **Key insight**: LDR hormesis operates through the same ATM → DNA damage response → FOXO → antioxidant defense cascade seen in [[Mitohormesis]]. The "stress signal" is ionizing radiation rather than mitochondrial ROS, but the downstream adaptive architecture is conserved.

## ROS Signaling — The Fall of the Free Radical Theory of Aging

### The Problem
For decades, [[Reactive Oxygen Species|ROS]] were cast as unambiguous cellular villains. The "Free Radical Theory of Aging" (FRTA) spawned a massive antioxidant supplement industry built on the premise that quenching ROS = health. This framing has been one of the greatest obstacles to understanding hormetic redox signaling.

### What the Data Shows
- **ROS as essential signaling molecules**: At physiological levels, ROS regulate cell proliferation, differentiation, migration, autophagy, and immune defense (Ansari et al., 2025, 19k accesses). Superoxide (O₂⁻) and hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) are second messengers for [[NRF2]], AMPK, HIF-1α, and NF-κB.
- **Exercise-induced ROS**: The 2026 *Free Radical Biology and Medicine* review (Foro Italico group) documents how exercise-generated ROS at moderate levels orchestrate **mitochondrial biogenesis, antioxidant defense, neuroplasticity, metabolic regulation, and vascular homeostasis**. Only when excessive do they drive pathology.
- **The antioxidant supplement paradox**: High-dose [[Antioxidant]] supplementation (β-carotene, vitamin E) has **increased** mortality in multiple large RCTs (ATBC, CARET, SELECT trials) and disrupted cellular homeostasis. The 2026 MDPI review on oxidative stress explicitly notes "high-dose antioxidant supplementation has been associated with paradoxical pro-oxidant effects."
- **Exercise with antioxidant co-supplementation** blunts the adaptive benefits — identical to the [[Mitohormesis]] finding that antioxidants administered alongside [[Metformin]] attenuate its longevity benefits.

### The Overlooked Paradigm
The shift from "ROS = damage" to "ROS = signal" has been slow because the oxidative stress → disease narrative is so deeply embedded in medical education, marketing, and patient communication. The 2025 *Signal Transduction and Targeted Therapy* review calls for "precision redox medicine" — modulating rather than eliminating ROS.

> [!tip]
> **Analogy to mitohormesis**: Exercise-induced mitochondrial ROS → AMPK → PGC-1α → [[Mitochondrial Biogenesis]] is the canonical mitohormetic cascade. The insight that *supplementing with antioxidants can block exercise benefits* is precisely the kind of paradigm-shifting finding that the "ROS = bad" framing obscures.

## Inflammation — The Enemy That Heals

### The Problem
"Inflammation" has become nearly synonymous with disease in public health messaging. Chronic inflammation is real and harmful, but the acute inflammatory response is **the fundamental mechanism of tissue repair and immune defense**. Anti-inflammatory overuse (NSAIDs, steroids) can impair healing.

### What the Data Shows
- **Exercise-induced muscle damage (EIMD)**: Eccentric exercise triggers transient inflammation (TNF-α, IL-6, IP-10, MCP-1) that activates [[Satellite Cells]], drives [[Satellite Cells|satellite cell]] proliferation, and enables muscle regeneration. NSAIDs taken post-exercise **inhibit satellite cell proliferation** (Mikkelsen et al., 2009) and impair long-term adaptation.
- **The repeated bout effect**: After an initial damaging bout, subsequent bouts cause progressively less damage — a form of tissue-level hormetic memory involving neural, architectural, and molecular adaptations.
- **Fever as immune amplifier**: Mild fevers enhance immune cell activity, increase [[T cells|T cell]] proliferation, and improve pathogen clearance. Routine antipyretic use during infection may prolong illness (though severe fever remains dangerous).
- **Acute inflammation vs. chronic inflammation**: The 2026 *IJMS* systems biology framework for EIMD shows inflammation as a "stage-dependent system state transition" — adaptive in early phases, maladaptive when persistent. The same molecular players serve opposite functions depending on timing and magnitude.

### The Overlooked Paradigm
Clinical anti-inflammatory intervention is often applied without distinguishing acute adaptive inflammation from chronic pathological inflammation. The repeated bout effect and preconditioning exercise (low-intensity eccentric contractions before high-intensity work) represent clinically applicable hormetic strategies that are underused in sports medicine and rehabilitation.

## Intermittent Hypoxia — Low Oxygen as a Brain and Heart Stimulant

### The Problem
Hypoxia is a feared clinical event — stroke, altitude sickness, sleep apnea. But *intermittent, controlled* mild hypoxia activates profoundly beneficial adaptive pathways. The negative framing of "low oxygen" obscures a therapeutic window that parallels [[Mitohormesis]] almost exactly.

### What the Data Shows
- **Cognitive benefits**: A 2026 *AIMS Neuroscience* review finds that intermittent hypoxia conditioning (IHC) improves cognition and mental health in multiple pilot studies. A 2024 *J Alzheimer's Disease* systematic review of 7 studies and 5 registered trials confirms that IHT/IHHT improved cognitive function in older adults, including those with mild cognitive impairment.
- **Cardiovascular preconditioning**: Intermittent hypobaric hypoxic preconditioning provides neuroprotection by increasing antioxidant activity, erythropoietin expression, and preventing apoptosis (Coimbra-Costa et al., 2021). Remote ischemic preconditioning improves spatial memory and sleep at high altitude (Wu et al., 2023).
- **Brain health overlap with exercise**: The 2026 review notes that exercise in hypoxic conditions may combine the hormetic benefits of both stressors — mitochondrial adaptation + HIF-1α stabilization + BDNF upregulation.
- **HIF-1α as a "hormetic amplifier"**: In *C. elegans*, mild hypoxia → transient mtROS → HIF-1 → positive ROS feedback → AMPK negative feedback → net ROS homeostasis with lifespan extension and enhanced immunity (Li et al., 2024).

### Why It's Overlooked
Hypoxia in clinical contexts (sleep apnea, stroke, perioperative events) is catastrophic. The idea that *controlled* low oxygen could be therapeutic seems counterintuitive to clinicians trained to prevent and treat oxygen deprivation. The field also suffers from confusing terminology — "therapeutic hypoxia" vs. "intermittent hypoxia training" vs. "altitude training" — which fragments the literature.

> [!important]
> **Mechanistic convergence**: Intermittent hypoxia → HIF-1α → mitochondrial ROS → AMPK → PGC-1α is nearly identical to the mitohormetic cascade. The "signal" is oxygen deprivation rather than electron transport chain inhibition, but the adaptive circuitry overlaps heavily.

## Caloric Stress and Fasting — Hunger as Cellular Cleanup

### The Problem
Fasting is framed clinically as a deprivation state — a risk factor for hypoglycemia, muscle wasting, and metabolic stress. Yet intermittent fasting and caloric restriction are among the most robust longevity interventions across taxa. The 2026 *Frontiers in Nutrition* paper explicitly frames intermittent fasting as a **hormetic signal for adaptation**.

### What the Data Shows
- **Metabolic switching**: After 12–36 hours without food, the brain switches from glucose to β-hydroxybutyrate (BHB) as fuel. BHB induces [[BDNF]] expression via histone deacetylase inhibition, enhancing hippocampal neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity (Seidler & Barrow, 2022).
- **Autophagy activation**: Fasting activates [[AMPK]] → [[TFEB]] → lysosomal biogenesis → [[Autophagy]], clearing damaged mitochondria and protein aggregates. This overlaps directly with the [[UPRmt]] and [[Mitophagy]] arms of mitohormesis.
- **The energy deficit IS the signal**: A 2025 *Nature Communications* study in 3xTg Alzheimer's mice found that energy restriction with prolonged fasting — not just reduced calories — was required for cognitive improvement, Aβ plaque reduction, tau pathology reduction, and mTORC1 suppression. The *fasting duration* was the hormetic trigger.
- **A 2025 DZD study**: Intermittent fasting without caloric reduction (isocaloric time-restricted eating) shifted circadian clocks but did **not** improve cardiometabolic health — suggesting the metabolic stress component, not just meal timing, drives the hormetic response.

### The Overlooked Paradigm
Clinical nutrition guidelines focus on *what* to eat, not *when* or *how little* to eat intermittently. The finding that the fasting state itself is a hormetic stressor — engaging autophagy, mitochondrial quality control, and neurotrophic pathways — suggests that periodic caloric deficit could be prescribed as deliberately as exercise, but the clinical framing of fasting as "malnutrition risk" blocks this.

## The Hygiene Hypothesis / Old Friends — Infection as Immune Education

### The Problem
Modern hygiene, while essential for infectious disease prevention, has inadvertently stripped the immune system of the microbial training signals it co-evolved with over millions of years. The resulting epidemic of autoimmune and allergic diseases represents a massive, population-scale experiment in what happens when a hormetic input (microbial exposure) is removed.

### What the Data Shows
- **The "Old Friends" hypothesis** (Graham Rook): Not about infections per se, but about commensal bacteria, saprophytic mycobacteria, and helminths — organisms with which humans co-evolved — that train regulatory T cells (Tregs) and calibrate immune tolerance (PMC 10524266, 2023).
- **Farm protective effect**: Children raised on farms with diverse microbial exposure have dramatically lower rates of asthma and allergies. Exposure to barn dust, animal microbiota, and environmental microbes creates a "tolerogenic mucosal symbiosis" with local T-cell balanced anti-inflammatory milieu.
- **Targeted hygiene**: The modern recommendation is not to abandon hygiene but to practice "targeted hygiene" — maintaining handwashing after handling raw chicken while encouraging outdoor play, pet ownership, and garden exposure.
- **Regulatory T cell education**: Bacteroides sp. and Clostridium clusters IV and XIVa colonizing the gut stimulate epithelial cells to release TSLP, TGF-β, and IL-25, inducing tolerogenic dendritic cells that promote Treg differentiation. Without these signals, the immune system becomes "trigger-happy."

### Why It's Overlooked
The pharmaceutical and hygiene product industries are built on pathogen elimination. Admitting that microbial exposure is beneficial challenges deeply held beliefs about sterility. The clinical framing of all microbes as threats — without distinguishing training signals from pathogens — misses the hormetic logic entirely.

> [!tip]
> **The helminth therapy connection**: Clinical trials using *Trichuris suis* (pig whipworm) showed 79% remission/response in Crohn's disease patients vs. 17% with placebo. Helminths secrete immunomodulatory products that skew Th1/Th17 → Th2/Treg, essentially providing the missing hormonal training signal. This is deliberate infection-as-medicine — the most extreme example of "a little bad is good."

## Heat and Cold Exposure — Thermal Stress as Cellular Maintenance

### The Problem
Both extreme heat and cold are lethal. This is undisputed. But the clinical and public health response has been to minimize *all* thermal stress rather than recognize that controlled, intermittent exposure activates deeply conserved protective cascades. The 2026 *Frontiers in Rehabilitation Science* paper calls this "untapped potential in rehabilitation."

### What the Data Shows

**Heat (Sauna/Hyperthermia):**
- Finnish sauna studies (Laukkanen, 2,000+ men, >20 years): 4–7 sessions/week → **50% reduction in fatal CVD**, 40% all-cause mortality reduction, 65% reduction in Alzheimer's/dementia risk.
- Mechanism: Heat stress → [[Heat Shock Proteins|HSP70/HSP90]] → molecular chaperones repair misfolded proteins (the same proteostasis network central to longevity science). Norepinephrine release comparable to maximal exercise.
- HSPs are the same proteins activated by [[Caloric Restriction]], exercise, and [[Mitohormesis|mitohormetic]] signaling. They are central to [[Proteostasis]] — one of the hallmarks of aging.

**Cold (Cryotherapy/Cold Water Immersion):**
- Brown adipose tissue (BAT) activation → mitochondrial uncoupling (UCP1) → increased energy expenditure and insulin sensitivity. A 2024 meta-analysis confirmed repeated cold exposure increases BAT volume and metabolic rate by 10–15%.
- Cold Shock Protein RBM3: shown to regenerate lost synapses in the brain, offering neuroprotection against neurodegeneration.
- Sustained 250% dopamine increase post-cold exposure — comparable to pharmacological interventions but without addiction potential.
- The Søberg Protocol: 11 minutes/week cold + 57 minutes/week heat, ending on cold for maximum metabolic benefit.

**Contrast Therapy:**
- Alternating heat (vasodilation) and cold (vasoconstriction) trains endothelial function and autonomic nervous system flexibility — essentially a "vascular workout" leveraging the hormetic principle.

### Why It's Overlooked
- Heat exposure is framed as a heat stroke / dehydration risk.
- Cold exposure is framed as a hypothermia / cardiac event risk.
- Neither has a pharmaceutical equivalent, so there is limited industry incentive to research or promote them.
- The Finnish sauna data is largely confined to epidemiological cohorts and has not been translated into clinical guidelines.

## Psychological Stress Inoculation — Adversity as Cognitive Training

### The Problem
Psychological stress is overwhelmingly framed as pathological. The concept of "eustress" (positive stress) exists in psychology but is rarely applied clinically. The Yerkes-Dodson inverted-U (1908) predicted this a century ago, yet most mental health interventions focus on stress *reduction* rather than stress *titration*.

### What the Data Shows
- **Yerkes-Dodson Law**: Performance improves with arousal up to an optimal point, then declines. The "inverted U" is the psychological equivalent of the hormetic dose-response curve.
- **Stress inoculation training (SIT)**: Evidence-based intervention where controlled, graduated exposure to manageable stressors builds resilience. Monkey studies show that inoculated animals develop **higher cognitive control, greater curiosity, and larger prefrontal cortex volume** than controls — a literal, measurable brain structure change from moderate stress exposure (Lyons & Parker, 2007).
- **Post-traumatic growth (PTG)**: ~50% of trauma survivors report meaningful positive psychological change (Tedeschi & Calhoun, 1996). A 2026 "Breakpoint Neuroplasticity" hypothesis proposes that extreme trauma creates a hyper-plastic state comparable to post-stroke neural reorganization — destabilization of old patterns forcing adaptive rewiring.
- **Cognitive reserve**: Moderate life adversity, well-managed, builds greater cognitive resilience than a difficulty-free life. The brain physically adapts to challenge through dendritic branching, synaptogenesis, and increased hippocampal volume.

### Why It's Overlooked
The mental health field is oriented toward symptom reduction and distress avoidance. Suggesting that *some* stress is beneficial risks being misinterpreted as endorsing trauma or dismissing suffering. The distinction between acute managed stress (hormetic) and chronic unmanaged stress (pathological) is critical but rarely communicated.

> [!warning]
> **Dose matters**: Chronic, overwhelming stress physically shrinks the hippocampus, dysregulates the HPA axis, and causes allostatic overload. The hormetic benefit applies only within the manageable, recoverable range. This is identical to the mitohormetic principle — sublethal mitochondrial stress extends lifespan; lethal mitochondrial stress kills cells.

## Low-Dose Toxin Exposure — Xenohormesis and Phytochemicals

### The Problem
The word "toxin" triggers immediate avoidance. But plants produce thousands of mild toxic compounds — polyphenols, alkaloids, isothiocyanates — that animals have evolved to sense as environmental challenge signals. Our [[Xenohormesis]] note covers this in the wiki, but the clinical implications are underexplored.

### What the Data Shows
- **Sulforaphane** (broccoli sprouts): Activates [[NRF2]] at subtoxic doses, upregulating endogenous antioxidant enzymes 10-100× more effectively than dietary antioxidants.
- **Resveratrol** (red wine): At physiologically achievable concentrations, activates [[SIRT1]] and AMPK. Though the high-dose supplement narrative has been largely debunked, the hormetic micro-dose concept remains valid.
- **Curcumin**: Activates [[HSF1]] and NRF2 at low doses. The poor bioavailability may actually be advantageous — ensuring tissue-level concentrations stay in the hormetic window.
- **4-Hydroxynonenal (4-HNE)**: A lipid peroxidation product conventionally framed as a damage marker. At low concentrations, it activates [[NRF2]] as part of a hormetic adaptive response (existing wiki note in oxidative_stress/).
- **Metformin**: The canonical pharmaceutical example — Complex I inhibition triggers adaptive cytoprotective programs via [[SIRT1]]/PGC-1α/[[FOXO]]. Antioxidant co-administration attenuates benefits.

### The Overlooked Paradigm
The phytochemical-to-health pipeline is dominated by "antioxidant" framing — as if these compounds work by scavenging free radicals. In reality, most phytochemicals work by *being mildly toxic*, triggering the cell's own far more powerful endogenous defense systems. This is the exact [[Xenohormesis]] insight, but it is rarely communicated to patients or the public.

## Low-Dose Alcohol — The Contested J-Curve

### The Problem
The "J-shaped curve" — where low-to-moderate alcohol consumption correlates with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality than abstinence — has been one of the most debated phenomena in epidemiology. Recent Mendelian randomization studies have challenged the causal interpretation, but the mechanistic evidence for biphasic effects remains compelling.

### What the Data Shows
- **Epidemiological evidence**: The Copenhagen City Heart Study (13,285 people, 12 years): red wine consumption was associated with a steady decrease in cardiovascular mortality, reaching RR 0.51 at 3–5 glasses/day. The 2026 *American Journal of Cardiology* review confirms the "biphasic" nature of alcohol's cardiovascular effects.
- **Proposed mechanisms**: Low-dose alcohol increases HDL-C, reduces fibrinogen and platelet aggregation, improves insulin sensitivity, and activates eNOS — all potentially hormetic.
- **The confounding problem**: A 2025 commentary (Miller, *Alcohol: Clinical and Experimental Research*) and Mendelian randomization studies (Kember et al., 2024, *MVP*, >250,000 individuals) suggest the J-curve may partly reflect systematic biases — "sick quitter" abstainer groups, socioeconomic confounders, and lifestyle correlates.
- **The resolution may be hormetic**: If alcohol *does* activate low-dose adaptive pathways (e.g., mild ER stress → UPR, or mild ROS → NRF2), the effect would be small and easily confounded by lifestyle factors. The hormetic zone for any single agent is typically only 30–60% above baseline — easily lost in noisy epidemiological data.

### Why It's Overlooked
Public health messaging cannot safely recommend alcohol ("no safe level" per WHO 2023), so the J-curve is systematically minimized. The hormetic framing — that *a* substance can be harmful at high doses and mildly beneficial at low doses through adaptive stress response activation — is theoretically consistent but practically impossible to translate into clinical guidelines.

## Microbial Colonization and the Gut — Dysbiosis as Signal

### The Problem
Modern medicine frames all bacterial overgrowth as infection requiring elimination. But the gut microbiome exists on a continuum where moderate microbial challenge drives immune maturation, metabolic regulation, and even neurotransmitter production. Antibiotic overuse has created a secondary extinction event for commensal species.

### What the Data Shows
- **Germ-free animal studies**: Animals raised without microbes have stunted immune systems, impaired gut barrier function, reduced [[BDNF]], and abnormal brain development. Adding back commensals rescues these deficits.
- **Fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT)**: Donor microbiome transfer achieves >80% cure rates for recurrent *C. difficile* — essentially "re-seeding" the ecosystem. The therapeutic agent is *living bacteria* — infection as cure.
- **Microbial metabolites as hormetic signals**: Short-chain fatty acids (butyrate, propionate) from gut fermentation activate GPR41/43 receptors, regulate [[Autophagy]], and influence systemic inflammation. These are mild metabolic stress products with adaptive benefits.
- **Post-antibiotic recovery**: After antibiotic-mediated microbial depletion, the reconstitution period represents a window of vulnerability but also of adaptation — the gut microbiome can reorganize into new stable states, sometimes more resilient than the original.

### The Overlooked Paradigm
The entire antibiotic prescribing paradigm could benefit from hormetic thinking. Rather than targeting complete bacterial eradication, a "minimum effective dose" approach that preserves commensal populations while controlling pathogens would maintain the training signals the immune system requires.

## Mechanical Stress on Bone and Connective Tissue — Wolff's Law as Hormesis

### The Problem
Bone loss in space, disuse osteoporosis, and fragility fractures are well-known. But the positive flipside — that mechanical loading within the hormetic window is the *primary signal* for bone remodeling and tendon strengthening — receives far less attention than it deserves.

### What the Data Shows
- **Wolff's Law**: Bone adapts its architecture to the loads placed on it. Bone mineral density increases in response to weight-bearing exercise and decreases in microgravity — a textbook hormetic response.
- **Tendon adaptation**: Tendons strengthen with progressive loading but degenerate with sudden overload or complete rest. The "sweet spot" for tendon health involves moderate mechanical challenge with adequate recovery — the inverted-U.
- **Muscle-bone crosstalk**: Mechanical loading of muscle releases myokines (IL-6, IGF-1, irisin) that directly stimulate osteoblast activity — another example of a mild stressor producing systemic adaptive benefit.

### Why It's Overlooked
Fracture prevention focuses on calcium/vitamin D supplementation and fall avoidance rather than *progressive mechanical loading* — the actual stimulus for bone strength. The framing of mechanical stress as "wear and tear" (rather than "adaptive signal") discourages the loading-based interventions that would most effectively prevent osteoporosis.

## Synthesis: The Common Architecture

Despite the diversity of these twelve domains, they all share a convergent biological logic:

| Feature | Mitohormesis | All Other Domains |
|---|---|---|
| **Stress signal** | Mitochondrial ROS / ETC disruption | Ionizing radiation, hypoxia, toxins, thermal stress, infection, mechanical load, psychological challenge |
| **Sensor** | AMPK, HIF-1α, NRF2, SIRT1 | Same conserved nutrient/stress sensors |
| **Adaptive effectors** | PGC-1α → mitochondrial biogenesis, UPRmt, antioxidant enzymes | HSP70/90, endogenous antioxidants, Treg expansion, BDNF, BAT activation, bone remodeling |
| **Memory** | Epigenetic reprogramming of stress defense genes | Immunological memory, repeated bout effect, cognitive reserve, BAT recruitment |
| **Dose window** | ~30–60% improvement over baseline | Consistently 30–60% — the quantitative signature of hormesis across taxa (Calabrese) |
| **Consequence of excess** | Cell death, SAMD, inflammaging | Disease, tissue damage, cognitive decline, immune dysregulation |

> [!important]
> **The unifying principle**: Life evolved in an environment of constant, variable stress. Removing all stressors — whether through antioxidant supplements, anti-inflammatory drugs, antibiotic overuse, thermal comfort, physical inactivity, or stress avoidance — deprives cells of the training signals they require for homeostatic competence. The resulting pathology is not the absence of health but the *absence of challenge*.
