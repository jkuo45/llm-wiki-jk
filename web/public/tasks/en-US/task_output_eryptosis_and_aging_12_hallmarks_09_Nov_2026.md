# Eryptosis and aging — a 12-hallmark mapping

Compiled 09_Nov_2026 11:18 AM PST. Vault basis: [[Eryptosis]], [[Erythrocytes]], [[Hallmarks of Aging]], the twelve hallmark notes, and [[_document_ - Current understanding of eryptosis mechanisms, physiological functions, role in disease, pharmacological applications, and nomenclature recommendations|Tkachenko 2025 source document]]. Web basis: Europe PMC and Crossref searches run 09 Nov 2026; every cited PMID and DOI was verified against its Europe PMC record. Where vault and published record differ, the published record is used and the vault reading is noted.

Evidence tiers: Tier 1 = human RCT / approved intervention; Tier 2 = human cohort / association; Tier 3 = rodent or other in-vivo animal; Tier 4 = in-vitro or ex-vivo human blood/cells; Tier 5 = hypothesis. No Tier 1 evidence exists for eryptosis modulation as an aging intervention.

## Origin of the framing and source audit

The seed passage is the opening of the "Eryptosis and aging" section of Tkachenko et al. 2025 (*Cell Death Dis* 16:467; PMID 40592821). That section cites López-Otín et al. 2023 for the twelve hallmarks but does not work through them. It makes three points: [[Cellular Senescence]] is classified as an antagonistic aging hallmark, whereas "resisting cell death" is a cancer hallmark (Hanahan & Weinberg 2011), so [[Eryptosis]] and [[Senescence|erythrocyte senescence]] may be distinct mechanisms; the discriminator is the clearance time constant, with ionomycin-treated eryptotic mouse cells removed within minutes and normal healthy mouse erythrocytes over several days; and old erythrocytes are more susceptible to oxidation-induced eryptosis (Ghashghaeinia 2012). The section identifies the link between donor age and erythrocyte blood parameters as unresolved, citing a systematic review in which hemoglobin concentration, mean corpuscular volume (MCV) and erythrocyte metabolism change with human age (Yadav 2024), and states that the underlying mechanisms are unelucidated.

A Europe PMC audit found no published hallmark-by-hallmark treatment of eryptosis: `"eryptosis" AND "hallmarks of aging"` returns 3 records (Tkachenko 2025 plus two conference abstract books), and `"12 different hallmarks of aging"` returns 1 record (Tkachenko 2025). The mapping below is a synthesis, not a summary of an existing review. Two terminological variants are retained: the framework writes "deregulated nutrient-sensing" and "disabled macroautophagy", while the seed text writes "dysregulated nutrient-sensing".

> [!important]
> The hallmarks were defined for nucleated, dividing, organelle-bearing cells. Mature human erythrocytes are anucleate, lack mitochondria, ribosomes, endoplasmic reticulum and lysosomes, and cannot synthesize protein, divide, or run transcription. Six hallmarks therefore have no cell-autonomous substrate in the circulating red cell and act through [[Hematopoietic Stem Cell|hematopoietic stem cells]], erythroid progenitors or the systemic milieu; two have a mature-erythrocyte analogue with different machinery; four act on the cell from outside. The "locus" column names that level.

## Hallmarks without a cell-autonomous substrate in mature erythrocytes

[[Genomic Instability]], [[Telomere Attrition]], [[Epigenetic Alterations]], disabled macroautophagy, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]] and [[Stem Cell Exhaustion]] share one constraint: the mature circulating red cell has no genome, no telomeres, no chromatin, no autophagosomes, no mitochondria and no clonogenic capacity. Their only routes to eryptosis are indirect. In the marrow they set the number and quality of cells entering circulation — age-related [[Clonal Hematopoiesis]] (Jaiswal 2014, n = 17,182; Tier 2), myeloid-biased aged HSC (Pang 2011; Tier 2), telomere-biology marrow failure (Calado & Young 2009; review), and NIX/BNIP3L-dependent mitochondrial clearance during reticulocyte maturation (Schweers 2007; Sandoval 2008; Tier 3 in mice). In the plasma they set the oxidative and inflammatory load that the circulating cell experiences. No published study closes either route by measuring donor-age HSC state and the eryptotic fraction of the same donor's RBCs. Blood-based [[Epigenetic Clock|epigenetic age]] and leukocyte [[Telomere]] length are properties of nucleated cells; they are not measurements of the erythrocyte.

## Hallmarks with a mature-erythrocyte analogue

[[Proteostasis]] loss and [[Cellular Senescence]] have erythrocyte counterparts with different machinery. The anucleate cell cannot replace damaged protein, so oxidative and glycative lesions accumulate over its ≈120 d lifespan: Hb oxidation, [[Protein Carbonylation]], [[Advanced Glycation End Products|AGEs]], [[Anion Exchanger 1]] oxidation into SESA, hemichromes and loss of deformability (Ghashghaeinia 2012; Zhang 2026, n = 81, ages 20–90 y). Ghashghaeinia 2012 separated erythrocytes into five density/volume fractions and found annexin V binding after 48 h rising with cell age, not accompanied by altered GSH and reversed in vitro by NAC; NAC also prolonged mouse RBC half-life in vivo (Tier 4/3). The 2025 GeroScience review frames the result as a threshold: suberyptotic stimuli gradually build senescence markers (SESA, desialylated glycoproteins, [[CD47]] loss, glycophorin depletion) with slow clearance over days, whereas severe damage produces intense Ca²⁺ influx, oxidative/nitrosative stress, caspase-3 activation and [[Phosphatidylserine]] exposure with swift erythrophagocytosis over minutes. Vesicle shedding is proposed as the anucleate substitute for intracellular degradation, removing tagged membrane patches to extend lifespan. [[CD47]] downregulation on erythrocyte ghosts via ubiquitin–proteasome degradation and the E3 ligase MARCH1 was reported in 2026 (Wang 2026; Tier 4).

> [!note]
> The minutes-versus-days clearance distinction is reported in Tkachenko 2025 and in the 2025 GeroScience review, and is carried into the vault document note. The consensus review cites Akel et al. 2007 (*Am J Physiol Cell Physiol* 292:C1759–67; PMID 17251326) for the mouse clearance kinetics, but that paper's abstract describes enhanced suicidal death in AE1-deficient mice rather than a clearance-time measurement. The primary attribution is not independently re-derived here.

Two consequences follow for assays. Annexin V binding alone cannot separate a senescent RBC with low-level PS exposure from an eryptotic RBC with high-level exposure, and no consensus PS threshold is defined. The 2025 GeroScience review issues practical guidelines for distinguishing the two states; the vault [[Eryptosis]] note scores senescent PS-positive cells as eryptotic.

## Systemic hallmarks acting on circulating erythrocytes

Deregulated nutrient-sensing, altered intercellular communication, [[Chronic Inflammation]] and [[Dysbiosis]] converge on the same erythrocyte execution module: cation-channel Ca²⁺ entry → [[Gardos Channel]] K⁺ efflux and shrinkage, [[Scramblase]] activation with [[Flippase]] inhibition → PS externalization, calpain-mediated cytoskeletal proteolysis, then [[Efferocytosis]] or, if clearance is saturated, [[Hemolysis]]. Glycolytic ATP depletion, [[Methylglyoxal]] and hyperglycemia, uremic toxins, [[IL-6]]-linked oxidative load and gut-derived indoxyl sulfate all act upstream of that module (Tkachenko 2025; Deng 2025; Virzì 2025; Coll 2024; Kim 2026). The downstream outputs are anemia from accelerated clearance, PS-dependent thrombin generation, CXCL16/SR-PSOX-mediated endothelial adhesion and microvascular impairment (Borst 2012; van der Meijden 2012; Tkachenko 2026). Deng 2025 reports RBC lifespan shortened from 120 d to 60–90 d in [[Chronic Kidney Disease]], with >90% of dialysis patients affected and roughly half of ESA-treated patients showing suboptimal correction (Tier 2, associational). The vault task output of 04 Nov 2026 describes a feed-forward loop — oxidative/uremic/inflammatory load → eryptosis → anemia → hypoxia and ESA escalation → more susceptible circulating cells — but each link is reported individually and the loop is not experimentally closed in humans.

Eryptosis is therefore a convergent effector and readout rather than an independent hallmark: it integrates several hallmarks and feeds [[Anemia]] and vascular decline back into them. Under the 2023 "hallmarks of health" framing (spatial compartmentalization, maintenance of homeostasis, adequate responses to stress), it belongs to homeostatic quality control — the erythrocyte tier of the damaged-cell-removal principle that apoptosis serves in nucleated tissue.

## Position of eryptosis within the hallmarks framework

López-Otín et al. 2023 require three premises of a hallmark: age-associated manifestation, acceleration of aging when experimentally accentuated, and deceleration, arrest or reversal of aging by therapeutic intervention. Applied to eryptosis:

- **Premise 1, partially supported.** RBC indices (hemoglobin concentration, MCV, RDW, membrane composition, oxidative status, metabolism) change with human age (Yadav 2024, systematic review; Kyriacou & Shibeeb 2025, review; Tier 2), and aged erythrocytes are more susceptible to oxidation-induced eryptosis (Ghashghaeinia 2012; Tier 4/3). A normal-population, age-stratified eryptotic fraction is **not quantified**; annexin V also binds non-eryptotic states.
- **Premise 2, not met.** No experiment reports that experimentally accentuating eryptosis accelerates organismal aging. Disease cohorts show accelerated eryptosis with anemia and vascular dysfunction, not with an aging-rate endpoint.
- **Premise 3, not met.** No human RCT of anti-eryptotic therapy with aging, healthspan or lifespan endpoints exists. NAC in mice (Ghashghaeinia 2012), ion-channel modulators, NO/cGMP-axis agents and [[Erythropoietin]] are Tier 3–4 only. Suppression is further bounded by loss of *Plasmodium*-infected-cell clearance ([[Malaria]]; Tier 2/3).

Eryptosis is not a candidate thirteenth hallmark under the framework's own criteria. "Resisting cell death", invoked in the seed section, is a cancer hallmark (Hanahan & Weinberg 2011), not an aging hallmark; it is a contrast, not a classification.

## Evidence limits

- No published work maps eryptosis onto the twelve hallmarks; the seed section uses the list only to contrast cellular senescence with erythrocyte senescence.
- No human RCT of eryptosis modulation for aging or age-related disease; all intervention data are animal or ex vivo.
- Normal-population and age-stratified eryptotic fractions are not quantified. Annexin V is not eryptosis-specific, and assay standardization is an active recommendation of Tkachenko 2025.
- Most human data are cohort or ex-vivo associations in [[Chronic Kidney Disease]], [[Diabetes]], [[Sepsis]] and [[Anemia]], confounded by uremia, inflammation and ESA dose.
- Donor-age RBC biomarker drift is reported without an elucidated mechanism; no longitudinal study links donor age → eryptotic susceptibility → clinical outcome.
- The senescent-versus-eryptotic RBC distinction rests partly on mouse clearance kinetics whose primary attribution is unresolved (note above).
- The dysbiosis → metabolite → eryptosis → anemia chain is incomplete in humans; the metabolite step is largely Tier 4.

## Bottom line

The twelve hallmarks of aging do not map uniformly onto [[Eryptosis]]. Six ([[Genomic Instability]], [[Telomere Attrition]], [[Epigenetic Alterations]], disabled macroautophagy, [[Mitochondrial Dysfunction]], [[Stem Cell Exhaustion]]) have no cell-autonomous substrate in the anucleate, organelle-free mature erythrocyte and act through erythroid progenitors, marrow output or the systemic milieu. Two (loss of [[Proteostasis]], [[Cellular Senescence]]) have mature-erythrocyte analogues with different machinery — irreversible macromolecular damage over the ≈120 d lifespan, and a surface-marker clearance program separated from eryptosis by a minutes-versus-days time constant. Four (deregulated nutrient-sensing, altered intercellular communication, [[Chronic Inflammation]], [[Dysbiosis]]) act on the circulating cell and converge on the Ca²⁺/scramblase/PS execution module. Human evidence supports an age-associated drift in RBC indices and greater oxidation-induced eryptosis susceptibility in old cells (Tier 2–4), but not the experimental-accentuation or therapeutic-reversal premises required of a hallmark. Eryptosis is best positioned as a downstream effector and biomarker of systemic oxidative, inflammatory, metabolic and microbial load, and as an upstream contributor to [[Anemia]] and vascular decline — not as an established anti-aging target or a thirteenth hallmark. All intervention data are preclinical.
