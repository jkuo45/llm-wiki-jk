> [!NOTE]
> The molecular controls that govern the differentiation of embryonic stem (ES) cells remain poorly understood. [[DGCR8]] is an [[RNA-Binding Proteins|RNA-binding protein]] that assists the [[Ribonuclease III|RNase III]] enzyme [[Drosha]] in the processing of [[MicroRNA|microRNAs]] (miRNAs). Here we study the role of miRNAs in ES cell differentiation by generating a *Dgcr8* knockout model. Analysis of mouse knockout ES cells shows that [[DGCR8]] is essential for biogenesis of miRNAs. On the induction of differentiation, DGCR8-deficient ES cells do not fully downregulate pluripotency markers and retain the ability to produce ES cell colonies; however, they do express some markers of differentiation. This phenotype differs from that reported for *Dicer1* knockout cells, suggesting that [[Dicer]] has miRNA-independent roles in ES cell function. Our findings indicate that miRNAs function in the silencing of ES cell self-renewal that normally occurs with the induction of differentiation.

*Published in final edited form as:* Nat Genet. 2007 Jan 28;39(3):380–385. doi: [10.1038/ng1969](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng1969) (Author manuscript; available in PMC 2010 Dec 22). PMID: [17259983](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17259983/)

## Background

[[Embryonic Stem Cells|Embryonic stem (ES) cells]] provide a tool for the study of the molecular mechanisms of early mammalian development. Differentiation of ES cells requires large and rapid shifts in their mRNA and protein constitution. [[MicroRNA|miRNAs]] are well suited to perform this task by inhibiting the translation of several targets [^1]. miRNAs are predicted to regulate hundreds of genes individually and simultaneously [^2]<sup>,</sup>[^3]. An essential role for small RNAs in ES cell differentiation has been inferred from the phenotype of mouse *Dicer1* knockout ES cells [^4]. [[Dicer]] is required for the maturation of at least two classes of small RNAs: miRNAs and short interfering RNAs (siRNAs) [^5]<sup>-</sup>[^7]. Thus, it remains unknown whether the lack of miRNAs alone is the cause of the *Dicer1* knockout phenotype. The double-stranded [[RNA-Binding Proteins|RNA-binding protein]] [[DGCR8]] and the [[Ribonuclease III|RNase III]] enzyme [[Drosha]] form the [[Microprocessor complex|microprocessor complex]], which processes long primary miRNAs (pri-miRNAs) into short hairpins called precursor miRNAs (pre-miRNAs) [^8]<sup>-</sup>[^9]. The resulting hairpins are exported into the cytoplasm [^10]<sup>,</sup>[^11] and are processed by [[Dicer]] into mature miRNAs [^1]<sup>,</sup>[^6]. Unlike cytoplasmic processing by [[Dicer]], nuclear processing by the microprocessor complex seems to be specific to miRNAs. By contrast, [[Drosha]] has been reported to have a role in ribosomal RNA processing, possibly in a distinct protein complex [^12]. Therefore, [[DGCR8]] may be the only member of the processing pathway that is specific to miRNAs.

## DGCR8 is essential for miRNA biogenesis

To confirm that [[DGCR8]] is essential for miRNA biogenesis, the authors performed RNA blot analysis of ES cells with probes specific to miR-293, miR-294 and miR-130. In *Dgcr8* knockout cells, neither fully mature nor intermediate pre-miRNA products were present for any of these miRNAs. The miR-293 probe identified two large RNA transcripts (~3 kb) in the *Dgcr8* knockout samples, consistent with the size expected for pri-miR-293 transcripts [^14]. By contrast, mature and pre-miRNA bands, but not pri-miRNA bands, were present in wild-type and *Dgcr8* heterozygous ES cells. These results show that [[DGCR8]] is absolutely required for the processing of some, if not all, pri-miRNAs to pre-miRNAs.

To determine whether [[DGCR8]] is required for the maturation of all miRNAs, the authors performed [[MicroRNA|miRNA]] microarray analysis. Using RNA from wild-type ES cells as the reference sample, they observed a **global loss of miRNAs** in *Dgcr8* knockout cells, but normal expression in *Dgcr8* heterozygous cells. Of the 89 miRNA array probes that showed significant signals with wild-type RNA, 82 were greatly reduced in the *Dgcr8* knockout cells. The remaining seven were not significantly altered, but signals for at least four seemed due to unavoidable contamination from [[Murine embryonic fibroblast|mouse embryonic fibroblast (MEF)]] feeder cells.

> [!NOTE]
> **Specificity of DGCR8 for miRNA processing**
> The [[Ribonuclease III|RNase III]] enzyme [[Drosha]] is required for processing 12S pre-rRNA to 5.8S rRNA and may be involved in cleavage of 32S pre-rRNA [^12]. Because [[DGCR8]] associates with [[Drosha]] to form the microprocessor complex, the authors tested whether [[DGCR8]] also assists in rRNA processing. RNA blot analyses of 45S primary, 30S, 32S, 12S intermediate, and 5.8S terminal rRNA transcripts showed no qualitative or quantitative differences between wild-type and knockout ES cells. Together with findings showing precise binding of [[DGCR8]] to pri-miRNA stem loops [^9], this suggests [[DGCR8]] has a specific role in [[MicroRNA]] processing.

*Dgcr8* knockout embryos arrested early in development. The authors therefore focused on *Dgcr8* knockout ES cells, which had an extended population doubling time relative to wild-type and heterozygous counterparts but were morphologically normal and continued to express ES cell–specific markers. The knockout ES cells accumulated in the [[G1 Phase|G1 phase]] of the cell cycle, increasing from ~14% of wild-type/heterozygous cells in G1 to 22% of homozygous *Dgcr8* knockout cells. Re-introduction of [[DGCR8]] by homologous recombination rescued the population doubling time and G1 phenotypes. The accumulation of DGCR8-deficient ES cells in G1 is reminiscent of loss of the miRNA-specific Dicer gene, *Dcr-1*, in the *Drosophila melanogaster* germ line [^15].

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Proliferation defect**
> [[DGCR8]]-deficient ES cells show a stable and subtle proliferation defect with [[G1 Phase|G1]] accumulation and prolonged population doubling time — rescued by re-expression of [[DGCR8]]. This establishes [[DGCR8]] as required for normal ES cell proliferation and [[Cell Cycle|cell-cycle progression]].

## Failure to silence ES cell self-renewal upon differentiation

To assess differentiation capacity, knockout ES cells were cultured as [[Embryoid Bodies|embryoid bodies]] (EBs) without [[Leukemia inhibitory factor|leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF)]]. Unlike wild-type EBs, *Dgcr8* knockout EBs did not form a cyst. RT-PCR showed abnormal expression of differentiation markers. *Fgf5* (marker of primitive ectoderm) was delayed: it peaked at day 8 of differentiation in knockouts and did not reach wild-type levels, versus high expression by day 2 in wild-type. With the exception of *Krt18*, *Eomes* and *Ddx4*, expression of endoderm (*Hnf4a*, *Afp*), mesoderm (*T/brachyury*), ectoderm (*Sox1*), epithelium (*Krt18*), trophectoderm (*Eomes*) and germ line (*Ddx4/Vasa*) markers was absent or severely reduced in *Dgcr8* knockout EBs.

Teratomas generated by injecting knockout ES cells into immunodeficient mice appeared largely undifferentiated apart from some glandular-like structures — consistent with the EB differentiation defects.

When treated with [[Retinoic Acid|retinoic acid]] (a strong inducer of differentiation), both wild-type and *Dgcr8* knockout ES cells took on mesenchymal morphology. Even so, the pluripotency markers *Oct4*, *Rex1*, *Sox2* and *Nanog* were not completely silenced in *Dgcr8* knockout cells. Returning retinoic acid–treated cells to ES cell culture conditions showed colony reformation frequency ~100-fold greater for knockouts than wild-type/heterozygous cells. In a clonal differentiation assay, wild-type colonies flattened and lost alkaline phosphatase staining with or without LIF; *Dgcr8* knockout colonies did not flatten and continued to express alkaline phosphatase even without LIF.

> [!TIP]
> **Key conclusion**
> [[DGCR8]]-deficient ES cells cannot efficiently silence the ES cell program even under stringent differentiation conditions. The failure to silence ES self-renewal upon differentiation is secondary to a loss of [[MicroRNA|miRNAs]] that normally inhibit translation of proteins that directly or indirectly maintain ES cell pluripotency.

The *Dgcr8* knockout ES cell phenotype differs from *Dicer1* knockout [^4]. *Dicer1* knockout EBs stop growing after ~8 d and do not express differentiation markers during EB culture, whereas *Dgcr8* knockout cells continue to grow and differentiate even after 16 d. *Dicer1* knockout cells show a more profound initial proliferation defect overcome over time, whereas *Dgcr8* knockout cells show a stable and more subtle proliferation defect. These differences suggest [[Dicer]] has miRNA-independent roles in ES cell function, and that *Dgcr8* knockout ES cells are a cleaner tool to dissect miRNA function in self-renewal and lineage commitment.

> [!NOTE]
> **Placement among global differentiation regulators**
> The failure to silence the stem cell program in a *Dgcr8* mutant parallels phenotypes of the [[NuRD complex]] (nucleosome remodeling and histone deacetylation), the G9a [[Histone Methyltransferase]], and the [[Dnmt3a]]/[[Dnmt3b]] DNA methyltransferases — placing [[DGCR8]] among a unique group of global regulators of cellular differentiation.

## Methods (summary)

- **Targeting strategy:** *Dgcr8* knockout via exon 3 excision → premature stop codons; Cre-mediated generation of Δ/Δ and Δ/flox ES cells; rescue by retargeting.
- **Differentiation assays:** embryoid bodies (suspension, no LIF); retinoic acid monolayer; teratoma formation in immunodeficient mice.
- **Readouts:** RNA blot (pri-/pre-/mature miRNA, pre-rRNA), miRNA microarray, RT-PCR/qPCR of pluripotency/differentiation markers, MTT proliferation, flow-cytometry cell-cycle/apoptosis.
- **Accession:** Gene Expression Omnibus [GSE6586](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE6586).
