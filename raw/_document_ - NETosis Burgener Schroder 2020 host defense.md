## Abstract

Neutrophils bind, engulf, and kill microbes via phagocytosis, degranulation, and
neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) extruded through NETosis. This review covers
signaling and cell biology of key NETosis pathways during infection and antimicrobial
functions of NETs. (Cold Spring Harb Perspect Biol. 2020 Jul;12(7):a037028. doi:
10.1101/cshperspect.a037028. PMCID: PMC7328462, PMID: 31767647.)

## Key points for wiki

- Three pathways: (1) suicidal (PMA/microbe -> ERK/NADPH oxidase -> ROS -> MPO/NE
  release -> histone cleavage -> GSDMD-p30 pores -> rupture, slow); (2) noncanonical
  (cytosolic Gram-negative LPS -> caspase-4/5/11 -> GSDMD -> nuclear pores ->
  caspase-11 histone cleavage -> rupture); (3) vital (TLR2/4, platelet P-selectin,
  complement -> Ca2+ -> PAD4 -> citrullination -> nuclear blebs/vesicular release,
  membrane kept, minutes, migration retained).
- PAD4 nuance: often accompanies suicidal NETosis but frequently dispensable there
  (NE cleavage redundant); crucial for vital NETosis. Ionophore NETosis can be
  NE/MPO/ROS-independent.
- Executioner: NE cleaves GSDMD to p30 in suicidal NETosis (feedforward granule
  rupture); GSDMD-p30 also permeabilizes nuclear membrane.
- Detection: extracellular DNA (Sytox), cit-H3, MPO-DNA complexes, NE; DNase dismantles
  NETs; PAD4/NE/MPO inhibition blocks respective arms.
- Relevance to page: validates suicidal (~hours, ruptures) vs vital (minutes, membrane
  kept) split, PAD4/cit-H3/MPO/elastase machinery, NLRP3/IL-1beta handoff, and the
  NOX-independent-routes caveat added to the table.
