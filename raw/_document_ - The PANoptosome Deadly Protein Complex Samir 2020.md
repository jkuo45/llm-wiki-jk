# The PANoptosome: A Deadly Protein Complex Driving Pyroptosis, Apoptosis, and Necroptosis (PANoptosis)

- Authors: Parimal Samir, R. K. Subbarao Malireddi, Thirumala-Devi Kanneganti (Department of Immunology, St. Jude Children's Research Hospital)
- Journal: Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology, 10:238 (received 02 Apr 2020, accepted 24 Apr 2020, published 03 Jun 2020 per the PDF; frontmatter uses 2020-05-06 as specified)
- Full citation: Samir P, Malireddi RKS and Kanneganti T-D (2020) The PANoptosome: A Deadly Protein Complex Driving Pyroptosis, Apoptosis, and Necroptosis (PANoptosis). Front. Cell. Infect. Microbiol. 10:238. doi: 10.3389/fcimb.2020.00238

> [!note]
> Abstract — [[Programmed Cell Death]] is regulated by evolutionarily conserved pathways critical for development and immunity. A newly recognized proinflammatory programmed cell death pathway called [[PANoptosis]] is controlled by a recently identified cytoplasmic multimeric protein complex named the [[PANoptosome]]. The [[PANoptosome]] engages, in parallel, three key modes of programmed cell death — [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]]. Its components are implicated in autoinflammatory diseases, neurodegenerative diseases, cancer, microbial infections, and metabolic diseases. This review covers putative components, a phylogenetic analysis of molecular domains and interaction motifs supporting assembly, genetic data on scaffolding and catalytic coordination, and mechanistic assembly models.

Keywords: PANoptosome, [[PANoptosis]], [[ASC]], [[Caspase-1]], [[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]], [[ZBP1]], [[Caspase-8]]

## Introduction

[[Programmed Cell Death]] is an evolutionarily conserved process central to organismal homeostasis. Three key pathways have been studied in detail:

- [[Pyroptosis]] — [[Inflammasome]]-dependent death executed by [[Gasdermin D]] family members.
- [[Apoptosis]] — death mediated by the [[Apoptosome]] and executioner caspases.
- [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] — death mediated by [[RIPK3]] and the downstream effector [[MLKL]].

> [!info]
> Extensive crosstalk exists between [[Inflammasome]]/[[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]].
> Certain challenges induce all three together: [[Influenza A Virus]] infection, inhibition of the homeostasis regulator [[TAK1]] (TGF-beta-activated kinase 1), and functional alterations in [[RIPK1]].

This raised the question: are the three pathways activated independently, or does a single death-inducing complex control all three? Studies in myeloid Tak1-deficient mice and with small-molecule [[TAK1]] kinase inhibitors suggested the latter, leading to identification of a single cell-death-inducing complex driving a phenomenon called [[PANoptosis]]. The complex was subsequently named the [[PANoptosome]].

The [[PANoptosome]] was initially shown to contain [[RIPK1]], [[ASC]] (apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase recruitment domain), [[NLRP3]], and [[Caspase-8]]. A subsequent study added [[RIPK3]], [[Caspase-6]], [[ZBP1]], and [[Caspase-1]] as components of the [[PANoptosome]] assembled in response to [[Influenza A Virus]] infection. Collectively, the [[PANoptosome]] contains molecules critical for [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] and activates all three to execute proinflammatory cell death.

> [!tip]
> [[PANoptosis]] gives the host a fail-safe: if a pathogen compromises one or more death pathways, alternative cell-death defenses still activate.

## Established Mechanisms of PANoptosis

### Viral infection: the ZBP1 PANoptosome

Pathogen sensing by the innate immune system activates intracellular cascades producing proinflammatory responses; many innate sensors also trigger death directly or via secreted [[Cytokines]]. [[Influenza A Virus]] causes yearly seasonal flu, with hypervirulent strains (e.g. 1918 Spanish flu, ~50 million dead) triggering dramatic host inflammation culminating in rapid [[Cytokine Storm]], severe lung tissue damage, and death.

- The [[NLRP3 Inflammasome]] is a major antiviral defense controlling lung inflammation during [[Influenza A Virus]] infection.
- But IAV-induced death is not entirely [[NLRP3]]-dependent: loss of a single-pathway component does not protect cells.
- Cells collectively lacking [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] (genetic loss of Ripk3 plus Casp8 or [[FADD]]) are protected; combinatorial pharmacological inhibition of all three also protects.
- IAV-induced death depends on [[Type I Interferon]] signaling — cells lacking [[IFNAR1]] resist death — implying upstream regulator(s) under interferon control.
- The upstream molecule is [[ZBP1]] (Z-DNA-binding protein 1): Zbp1-deficient cells are fully protected, and [[ZBP1]] drives [[RIPK3]]-dependent death during IAV infection.

> [!important]
> Mechanism — [[ZBP1]] drives [[PANoptosis]] by recruiting [[RIPK3]] and [[Caspase-8]] to the [[PANoptosome]] upon sensing [[Influenza A Virus]] infection.
> This was the first description of [[PANoptosis]].

### Organismal homeostasis: RIPK1 restrains ZBP1

> [!note]
> Lethality from the Ripk1 RHIM mutation is rescued by deleting [[ZBP1]] or mutating its RHIM domain, suggesting [[RIPK1]] inhibits aberrant [[ZBP1]]-driven [[PANoptosis]].
> [[PANoptosis]] thus regulates development and homeostasis beyond infection.

[[RIPK1]] regulation of [[PANoptosis]] is essential for homeostasis, death, and inflammation:

- Scaffolding function: [[RIPK1]] promotes survival via complex-I assembly, blocking early and late death checkpoints downstream of [[TNFR1]] signaling.
- Kinase/RHIM function: [[RIPK1]] kinase activity or homotypic interaction motif enables death.
- Ripk1 deletion is embryonically lethal in mice, possibly via [[RIPK3]]-regulated PANoptosis-like death through [[Caspase-8]] and [[FADD]] with systemic inflammation.
- Inactivating [[Caspase-8]] catalytic activity causes embryonic lethality mediated by [[PANoptosis]] through [[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]]-[[MLKL]], and [[Caspase-1]].
- Mutating the [[Caspase-8]] cleavage site in [[RIPK1]] (essential for regulating [[RIPK1]] activity) also causes embryonic lethality and inflammation.

### TAK1: a key regulator

> [!info]
> [[TAK1]], a complex-I component, is important for [[NLRP3]] [[Inflammasome]] activation and modulates [[RIPK1]] activity to regulate [[PANoptosis]].
> Without [[TAK1]] activity (pharmacological inhibition or genetic loss), [[RIPK1]] activates [[PANoptosis]] and inflammation via its kinase activity.
> A follow-up study found [[RIPK1]] kinase-independent [[PANoptosis]], challenging the dogma that [[RIPK1]] kinase function is required for death.

Loss or deregulation of [[RIPK1]] homeostasis can thus engage [[PANoptosis]] and affect development and immunity.

### Autoinflammation

> [!warning]
> Pstpip2 mutant (Pstpip2cmo) mice develop autoinflammation via [[Caspase-8]]- and [[NLRP3]]/[[Caspase-1]]-regulated death.
> Combined deletion of Ripk3, Casp1, and Casp8 — blocking all three [[PANoptosis]] arms — rescues the mice, while blocking a single arm does not. This implicates [[PANoptosis]] in the disease.

> [!warning]
> Sharpin mutant (Sharpincpdm) mice: inflammation is rescued by [[TNF-alpha]] deletion or kinase-dead [[RIPK1]], by hemizygous [[Caspase-8]] deletion plus [[RIPK3]] loss, and skin inflammation is delayed by Nlrp3 loss or loss of [[IL-1β]].
> All three [[PANoptosis]] arms are implicated, suggesting death plus [[IL-1β]]/[[IL-18]] and [[DAMP]] release from PANoptotic cells drive inflammation.

Other complex-I molecules — [[HOIP]], [[HOIL-1]], and [[A20]] — when deleted also cause embryonic lethality and autoinflammatory disease, suggesting roles in restraining [[PANoptosis]]. Genetic evidence indicates [[PANoptosome]] activation is inhibited by [[TAK1]], [[PSTPIP2]], [[SHARPIN]], [[HOIP]], [[HOIL-1]], and [[A20]].

### Execution and disease significance

Whether induced by infection, homeostasis perturbation, or autoinflammation, [[PANoptosis]] is executed by the [[PANoptosome]], which integrates apoptotic, necroptotic, and [[Inflammasome]] components. Downstream [[IL-1β]], [[IL-18]], and [[DAMP]]s released by PANoptotic cells act as alarmins initiating and amplifying inflammation. Defects in [[PANoptosome]] components are implicated in autoinflammatory and neurodegenerative diseases, cancer, and infection susceptibility.

## Components of the PANoptosome

Genetic and biochemical data group [[PANoptosome]] proteins into three classes:

1. Sensors — [[ZBP1]] and [[NLRP3]] as putative PAMP/[[DAMP]] sensors.
2. Adaptors — [[ASC]] and [[FADD]].
3. Catalytic effectors — [[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]], [[Caspase-1]], and [[Caspase-8]].

> [!info]
> [[Caspase-6]] is a critical [[PANoptosome]] component promoting assembly by reinforcing the [[RIPK3]]–[[ZBP1]] interaction — a novel scaffold function for this presumed apoptotic executioner [[Caspase]]. Whether its catalytic activity is required remains unclear.

> [!note]
> [[RIPK1]] classification is challenging: although its kinase activity was thought required for [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]], kinase-dead [[RIPK1]] is recruited to the [[PANoptosome]] and still induces [[PANoptosis]] — so [[RIPK1]] acts as adaptor in some contexts and catalytic effector in others.
> [[PANoptosome]] components likely have multiple biochemical functions in executing [[PANoptosis]].

## The Inflammasome, Apoptosome, and Necroptosome as Assembly Templates

[[PANoptosome]] assembly likely requires domain-specific homotypic and heterotypic interactions. Two functional aspects dominate death complexes: multimeric protein interactions and catalytic activities.

- [[Pyroptosis]]: executed by the hetero-multimeric [[Inflammasome]]. Assembly uses homotypic CARD and PYD interactions among sensors, adaptor [[ASC]], and catalytic actuator [[Caspase-1]].
- Intrinsic [[Apoptosis]]: driven by the hetero-multimeric [[Apoptosome]], exploiting homotypic CARD interactions to assemble and activate catalytic actuator [[Caspase-9]].
- Extrinsic [[Apoptosis]]: death receptors use DED and death-domain homotypic interactions to assemble the [[DISC]] (death-inducing signaling complex), activating [[Caspase-8]].
- [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]]: the [[Necroptosome]] composition and assembly mechanism are not fully clear, but data suggest RHIM homotypic interactions plus [[RIPK3]] kinase activity execute death.

> [!tip]
> The [[PANoptosome]] likely combines [[Inflammasome]], [[DISC]], and [[Necroptosome]] interactions into one versatile complex using necroptosome-associated kinase plus [[Apoptosis]]- and [[Pyroptosis]]-associated protease activities.

## Evolution of PANoptosome Components

> [!note]
> Some conserved motifs in death-executing complexes exist across all branches of life; others occur only in higher eukaryotes and viruses.
> [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] molecules share domains, indicating similar assembly mechanisms for large hetero-multimeric death complexes.

To identify assembly-critical (coevolving) domains, the authors classified them:

- Assembly domains: CARD, DEATH, DED, PYD, RHIM.
- Catalytic domains: Peptidase_C14 ([[Caspase]] domain), Pkinase (kinase domain in [[RIPK3]]), Pkinase_Tyr (kinase domain in [[RIPK1]]), NACHT (ATPase domain in [[NLRP3]]).
- Sensing domains: z-alpha, LRR_6.

Using the Pfam database: the four death-fold domains (CARD, DEATH, DED, PYD) occur essentially only in multicellular organisms and a few of their viruses. Pkinase_Tyr, RHIM, z-alpha, and LRR_6 exist in prokaryotes but mostly in eukaryotes (few viral exceptions). Pkinase, Peptidase_C14, and NACHT are widely distributed across kingdoms — catalytic motifs may predate the eukaryote–prokaryote split.

> [!important]
> A functional [[PANoptosome]] may have coevolved with multicellularity, since the death-fold domains essential for assembly occur mainly in metazoans — consistent with [[PANoptosis]] modulating organismal homeostasis.

## Phylogenetic Assembly Model

Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic analysis of assembly domains yields a testable assembly model: homotypic interactions track sequence similarity, so domains capable of heterotypic interaction should cluster together.

- One [[Caspase-8]] DED motif (DED2) clusters with the PYDs of [[NLRP3]] and [[ASC]] — consistent with reports that ASC PYD is required for [[Caspase-8]] polymerization via DED2–PYD heterotypic interaction.
- [[Caspase-8]] DED1 clusters with the [[FADD]] DED — the known homotypic interaction driving [[DISC]] assembly in extrinsic [[Apoptosis]].
- ASC CARD clusters closer to [[ZBP1]] RHIM2 than to [[Caspase-1]] CARD or [[ZBP1]] RHIM1 — suggesting ligand-sensing [[ZBP1]] recruits [[ASC]] via a RHIM2–CARD heterotypic interaction.

> [!info]
> Proposed [[ZBP1]] [[PANoptosome]] assembly upon sensing IAV vRNP: activated [[ZBP1]] binds [[ASC]] (RHIM2–CARD heterotypic interaction) and induces [[ASC]] polymerization; ASC PYD recruits [[NLRP3]] via PYD–PYD interaction; [[NLRP3]] recruitment must precede [[Caspase-1]] recruitment (IAV [[Caspase-1]] activation is strictly [[NLRP3]]-dependent).
> A conformational change in ASC CARD after [[NLRP3]] PYD binding — or increased valency from the [[NLRP3]] NACHT domain boosting ASC polymerization — may then permit [[Caspase-1]] binding.
> Meanwhile free [[ZBP1]] RHIM1 recruits [[RIPK1]] and/or [[RIPK3]]; [[Caspase-8]] joins via [[RIPK1]] DED–[[Caspase-8]] DED interaction and/or ASC PYD–[[Caspase-8]] DED2 interaction.
> In [[TAK1]]-inhibited cells, [[Caspase-1]] activation is likewise [[NLRP3]]-dependent but the upstream sensor is unknown.

## Alternative Assembly Strategies

> [!note]
> Intrinsically disordered region (IDR)-mediated interactions also induce death: the [[NLRP3]]–[[DDX3X]] IDR interaction is critical for [[Pyroptosis]] downstream of [[NLRP3]] [[Inflammasome]] activation, and an IDR-mediated [[Caspase-6]]–[[RIPK3]] interaction boosts [[RIPK3]] recruitment to the [[ZBP1]] [[PANoptosome]].
> All known [[PANoptosome]] components have predicted IDRs, and some assembly domains undergo prionoid phase transitions — so assembly may amplify signals via polymerization into large cytoplasmic supramolecular structures visible by immunofluorescence.

The [[PANoptosome]] induces death by multiple mechanisms, so a full complex need not assemble before death occurs. Blocking one [[PANoptosis]] arm is insufficient to prevent death — which both complicates molecular dissection and enables study of pathway crosstalk. Identifying apical sensors controlling [[PANoptosis]] as a whole is critical: [[ZBP1]] is the only one identified (for IAV), while [[TAK1]]-inhibition-induced [[PANoptosis]] proves alternative, [[ZBP1]]-independent activation exists — pathogen-induced perturbations detected as danger signals triggering inflammatory death.

> [!tip]
> [[PANoptosis]] evolved as a robust fail-safe driving inflammatory death and antimicrobial immunity, especially against pathogens targeting inflammatory signaling or death pathways.
> Additional sensors likely exist, possibly forming [[PANoptosome]] variants with differing compositions — analogous to [[Inflammasome]] sensors (NLRP1b, [[NLRP3]], NLRC4) with differential [[ASC]] requirements. The [[ZBP1]] [[PANoptosome]] is the founding member of this complex class.

## Summary

Crosstalk between death and signaling cascades establishes multifaceted signaling platforms. The [[PANoptosome]] engages [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] as the collective inflammatory death pathway of [[PANoptosis]], using domain-based scaffolds plus enzyme-based catalytic modifications. This lets it orchestrate homeostasis during development while driving robust inflammatory death against infections. Because many components are disease-associated (autoinflammatory, infectious, neurodegenerative diseases, cancer), understanding the [[PANoptosome]] should inform targeted inhibitors and activators of inflammatory death for therapeutic immunomodulation.
