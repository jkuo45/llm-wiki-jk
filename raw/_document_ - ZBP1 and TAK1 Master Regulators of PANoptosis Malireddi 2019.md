# ZBP1 and TAK1: Master Regulators of PAN-optosis

Malireddi RKS, Kesavardhana S and Kanneganti T-D (2019). Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology 9:406. Department of Immunology, St. Jude Children's Research Hospital, Memphis, TN, USA.

## Abstract

[[Cell Death]] is central to development, organismal homeostasis, and immune responses. The cell death field has delineated the molecular programs specific to each of the apoptotic and inflammatory cell death pathways. Moreover, the discovery of the [[Inflammasome]]s and [[Pyroptosis]] and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] pathway regulators have provided the genetic basis for the programmed inflammatory cell death pathways. Earlier research highlighted the unique regulation of each of these pathways, but emerging studies discovered co-regulation and crosstalk between these seemingly different cell death complexes. The progress in this area has led to an idea that master regulators play central roles in orchestrating multiple cell death pathways. Here, we provide a brief review of the master regulators, the [[Innate Immune System|innate immune]] sensor [[ZBP1]] and the essential cell survival kinase [[TAK1]], that play vital roles in the regulation of [[RIPK1]]/[[RIPK3]]–[[FADD]]–[[Caspase-8]] cell death complex assembly and its versatility in executing pyroptosis, [[Apoptosis]], and necroptosis, which we dubbed here as [[PANoptosis]]. Furthermore, we discuss the implications and therapeutic potential of targeting these master regulators in health and disease.

> [!info]
> One-sentence summary
> [[ZBP1]] and [[TAK1]] regulate PAN-optosis.

Keywords: [[Caspase-1]], [[Gasdermin D]], [[MLKL]], inflammasome, infection, innate immunity, [[Inflammation]], [[TLR]] priming

## Introduction

Cell death is fundamentally important for organismal development, homeostasis, and host response to infection. Several cell death pathways have been identified with specific genetically encoded requirements; these have collectively been named programmed cell death pathways. Apoptosis is the most well-studied form of cell death and was originally considered immunologically silent with major roles in the homeostatic development of multicellular organisms. Unlike apoptosis, pyroptosis and necroptosis are considered immunologically active due to their association with membrane lysis and extracellular release of damage-associated molecular patterns ([[DAMP]]s), resulting in amplification of inflammation.

[[RIPK1]] and RIPK3 play key roles in phosphorylation and activation of the protein mixed lineage kinase domain-like pseudokinase ([[MLKL]]) that forms pores in the membrane to execute necroptosis. It is now established that pyroptosis is executed by [[Gasdermin D]] (GSDMD)-mediated membrane lysis as a result of cleavage by inflammatory caspases to release the functionally active N-terminal fragment.

> [!info]
> Pyroptosis execution
> Inflammatory caspases cleave [[Gasdermin D]], releasing the N-terminal fragment that forms membrane pores, driving lytic cell death and release of [[IL-1β]] and [[IL-18]].

The last decade has provided a wealth of information about the molecular details of the inflammasome and inflammatory caspase activation processes that promote pyroptosis and the release of the inflammatory cytokines interleukin (IL)-1 and IL-18. As understanding of these cell death pathways increases, it is becoming clear that in addition to the unique regulation of each of these pathways, there is also significant co-regulation and crosstalk.

## The Manifestation of PAN-optosis (Pyroptosis, Apoptosis, and Necroptosis)

Cell death plays key roles in infection and immunity, as programmed cell death is often part of the host anti-microbial strategy. The innate immune system recognizes microbial infections through its pattern recognition receptors ([[PAMP|PRRs]]), which can induce a robust pro-inflammatory immune response and cell death. However, membrane-bound PRRs, such as [[Toll-like Receptor]]s (TLRs) and cytokine receptors such as tumor necrosis factor ([[TNF-alpha|TNF]]) receptor 1 ([[TNFR1]]), often show a bias toward induction of the inflammatory immune response through the [[NF-kB]] and [[MAPK]] pathways, despite having all the principal components required for the induction of cell death.

> [!tip]
> Danger-signal logic
> Pathogen- or pharmacologically mediated obstruction of survival signaling acts as a key danger signal to trigger the assembly of PAN-optotic cell death complexes. Hosts sense perturbations in key inflammatory signaling nodes (NF-kB, MAPK) and initiate multifaceted cell death complexes to drive PAN-optosis, promoting inflammation, immune responses, and protection against infection.

Master sensors and regulators have evolved to integrate the complex signals coming from multiple ligands and stimuli associated with live microbial infections. These molecules act as critical sensors of specific microbial infections and as central hubs to trigger both multifaceted cell death in the form of PAN-optosis and inflammatory immune responses. This review focuses on two such key master regulators, Z-DNA binding protein 1 ([[ZBP1]], also called DAI/DLM1) and transforming growth factor beta-activated kinase 1 ([[TAK1]], also called [[MAP3K7]]).

## ZBP1 Acts as a Master Switch for PAN-optosis

[[Influenza A Virus]] (IAV) is a single-stranded RNA virus which infects millions worldwide and poses a major threat to public health. Early reports provided the first evidence of a crucial role for nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain (NOD)-like receptor (NLR) family pyrin domain-containing 3 ([[NLRP3]]) in sensing IAV infection and dsRNAs to assemble the inflammasome complex via the adapter protein apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a caspase recruitment domain ([[ASC]]) and drive caspase-1–mediated inflammation. Furthermore, IAV-induced activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome controls viral spread and protects against lung damage, promoting host-protective immune responses. While IAV RNA recognition is known to induce inflammation and cell death, the identity of innate sensors that mediate programmed inflammatory cell death and inflammation were not known for many years.

[[ZBP1]] was identified as an innate sensor of IAV infection. Upon sensing IAV infection, ZBP1 activation leads to NLRP3 inflammasome activation and PAN-optosis. ZBP1 also critically regulates the IAV-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine response.

> [!important]
> ZBP1 domain logic
> ZBP1 is unique among cell death proteins: it contains a [[RHIM]] domain (RIP homotypic interaction motif) to mediate cell death via [[RIPK3]], and a Z-alpha domain which binds [[Z-DNA|Z-nucleic acids]]. It both senses aberrant nucleic acids and instructs the assembly of cell death signaling scaffolds.

ZBP1 is one of the RHIM-containing proteins and activates necroptosis via RIPK3, another RHIM-containing protein. ZBP1 directly senses IAV infection and facilitates RIPK3 and caspase-8 activation. ZBP1, in association with RIPK3 and caspase-8, promotes assembly of the signaling complexes that mediate PAN-optosis (Figure 1 of the paper). Deletion of ZBP1 completely abolishes IAV-induced NLRP3 inflammasome activation and the release of leaderless cytokines.

Key mechanistic details:

- ZBP1-mediated PAN-optosis after IAV infection is dependent on the RIPK3–caspase-8 complex but independent of RIPK1.
- ZBP1 is essential for the RIPK1-dependent pro-inflammatory cytokine response during IAV infection, although ZBP1 and RIPK1 do not directly interact under endogenous conditions.
- ZBP1 mediates necroptosis and inflammation when the RIPK1 RHIM domain is mutated, causing perinatal lethality in Ripk1^RHIM/RHIM mice. ZBP1 promotes activation of RIPK3 in the absence of RIPK1-RHIM function.
- In physiological settings, RIPK1-RHIM function counteracts ZBP1-mediated cell death and inflammation for organismal homeostasis. A putative signaling complex may control RIPK1-mediated inhibition of ZBP1 function, independent of the canonical [[Ripoptosome]] complex which contains RIPK1 as a core scaffolding protein.
- ZBP1 activation requires upstream activation of other nucleic acid sensors: recognition of IAV RNA by the [[RIG-I]] receptor initiates [[Type I Interferon]]/[[IFNAR]] signaling to license ZBP1 upregulation and activation. ZBP1 senses the viral ribonucleoprotein complexes (vRNPs) of IAV generated during replication to activate cell death pathways.
- Open questions: how ZBP1 is activated in the absence of RIPK1-RHIM function, and what the endogenous nucleic acid ligands that activate ZBP1 are, remain unexplored.

> [!info]
> Figure 1 (paper): ZBP1 triggers PAN-optosis
> ZBP1 senses nucleic acids, interacts with RIPK3, and recruits caspase-8 to form cell death scaffolds engaging NLRP3 inflammasome-dependent pyroptosis, CASP8-mediated apoptosis, and RIPK3–MLKL-driven necroptosis. ZBP1 also induces RIPK1-driven NF-kB activation and inflammation in response to influenza infection.

## TAK1 Acts as a Master Regulator for PAN-optosis Quiescence

The [[TNF-alpha|TNF]] family of cytokine receptors plays important roles in inflammation and cell death and shapes the nature of host innate and adaptive immune responses. Often, the default outcome of triggering these cell surface receptors is cell survival and cytokine production. In immune cells, the TNF–TNFR1 complex proximal to the membrane enables the formation of the RIPK1-containing complex-I which promotes prosurvival and inflammatory responses. The inactivation of essential complex-I components such as [[TAK1]] results in the assembly of a cytosolic ripoptosome-like cell death complex with the potential to drive multifaceted cell death, PAN-optosis.

> [!important]
> TAK1 keeps RIPK1 in check
> Inhibitory phosphorylation of [[RIPK1]] by [[TAK1]] is critical to restrict its activation and block spontaneous PAN-optosis. Loss of TAK1 function unleashes a RIPK1-dependent PAN-optotic complex.

The PAN-optotic death complex containing RIPK1, [[Fas]]-associated death domain (FADD), and caspase-8 acts as the core and is assembled through their death domain (DD) interactions. This complex promotes FADD–caspase-8–dependent apoptosis through the activation of effector [[Caspase-3]] and [[Caspase-7]], and promotes necroptosis by RIPK3-mediated phosphorylation of MLKL (Figure 2 of the paper). This ripoptosome-like complex also plays a key role in activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and pyroptosis when TAK1 is inhibited.

The essential role of TAK1 in innate immunity against microbes resulted in the evolution of microbial inhibitors that block its function to promote immune evasion. For example, the intracellular bacteria [[Yersinia]] produces the toxin [[YopJ]] that inactivates TAK1. However, feedback mechanisms in the host sense the perturbation of TAK1 signaling as an intracellular pathogenic insult and trigger RIPK1 kinase activity-dependent PAN-optosis.

> [!tip]
> Caspase-8 cleaves gasdermin D
> Pharmacological- or pathogen-based inhibition of [[TAK1]] revealed a direct role for [[Caspase-8]], activated in the RIPK1–FADD–caspase-8 complex, in driving [[Gasdermin D]] cleavage and pyroptosis independent of [[Caspase-1]]. Microbial priming bypasses the requirement for RIPK1 kinase activity to drive PAN-optosis when TAK1 is inactivated. Mice deficient in components of the PAN-optotic complex are susceptible to Yersinia infection, demonstrating functional importance.

> [!info]
> Figure 2 (paper): TAK1 as master switch for PAN-optosis quiescence
> Genetic deletion or microbial/pharmacological inactivation of TAK1 triggers RIPK1-dependent assembly of PAN-optotic complexes. RIPK1 with FADD and CASP8 triggers NLRP3 inflammasome-dependent and CASP8-mediated cleavage of GSDMD (pyroptosis), CASP8-mediated apoptosis, and — when CASP8 is inhibited — RIPK3–MLKL-dependent necroptosis. Regulatory inputs include [[cIAP1|cIAPs]], [[LUBAC]], [[TAB]], [[NEMO]], [[IKKalpha]]/[[IKKbeta]].

## Converging Mechanisms of Programmed PAN-optosis

Research on inflammasomes and inflammatory caspases led to the discovery of extensive crosstalk between the apoptotic and inflammatory cell death pathways. Studies on the RHIM domain-containing proteins (RIPK1 and RIPK3) and their crosstalk with FADD–caspase-8–mediated pathways improved understanding of the apoptotic and necroptotic pathways. In parallel, studies of innate immune receptors showed unique and overlapping roles in assembling caspase-activating inflammasomes and activating pyroptotic cell death. Of these, the NLRP3 inflammasome has emerged as an extremely versatile sensor of stress responding to microbial and damage-promoting cytotoxic insults. Generation and characterization of NLRP3-deficient mice provided the first genetic evidence for its importance in sensing bacterial and viral components and in induction of inflammatory [[Caspase-1]] activation and maturation of pro–IL-1beta and pro–IL-18.

More recently, studies revealed crosstalk between pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis:

- Early studies: caspase-8 contributes to inflammatory functions by driving maturation of inflammatory IL-1 cytokines.
- Gurung et al.: first definitive evidence of direct crosstalk — FADD and caspase-8 are crucial for canonical and non-canonical inflammasome activation and inflammatory cell death.
- [[ASC]], an important adapter for inflammasome assembly, is recruited to the caspase-8–containing cell death complex.
- Studies on ZBP1 and TAK1 strengthened understanding of the versatility of the RIPK1/RIPK3–FADD–caspase-8 cell death complex and provided the foundation for PAN-optosis.
- Defects in TAK1-associated cell survival signaling molecules (cIAPs, [[SHARPIN]], IKKs, NEMO, LUBAC components) also trigger multifaceted cell death and inflammatory immune responses.

> [!warning]
> PAN-optosis framing
> The PAN-optotic death complexes activated by the master regulators are multifaceted, with the potential to execute diverse forms of caspase-mediated cell death and promote necroptosis when caspases are inhibited — e.g. via the [[necrosome]] (RIPK1/RIPK3–MLKL axis) and [[Caspase-11]]-linked non-canonical inflammasome routes.

## PAN-optosis in Inflammatory Diseases

There is constant competition between host and microbes to exploit cell death and inflammation, and the outcome is not always beneficial to the host. Loss- or gain-of-function mutations in key components of cell death pathways result in dysregulated immune responses to infection or debilitating inflammatory diseases.

- Liberation of constraints on ZBP1 function leads to autoinflammation and perinatal lethality in mice (Ripk1 RHIM mutants), indicating manifestations of PAN-optosis in pathophysiology and organismal development.
- Inhibition of TAK1 can lead to unwanted activation of PAN-optosis, predisposing to inflammatory diseases. Genetic deletion of TAK1 in mice results in spontaneous cell death and embryonic lethality. Mutations reducing TAK1 function in humans and mice lead to loss of immune homeostasis and myeloid-proliferation syndromes.
- Aging-associated reduction of TAK1 expression cooperates with other genetic risk factors to promote RIPK1-dependent neuroinflammation and [[ALS|amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)]] in humans.
- Loss of TAK1 in myeloid cells in mice results in hypersusceptibility to [[LPS]] shock and enhanced neutrophilic proliferation ([[Neutrophil|neutrophils]]), supporting a crucial role for TAK1-mediated inhibition of PAN-optosis in preventing inflammation and maintaining immune homeostasis.
- Genetic deficiency of NF-kB and MAPK components implicates [[necrosome]] components RIPK1 and RIPK3 in promoting NLRP3 inflammasome activation, multiple forms of cell death, and inflammatory disease.
- Disease in Pstpip2^cmo mice (missense mutation in [[Pstpip2]] causing debilitating inflammatory arthritis similar to chronic multifocal [[Osteomyelitis|osteomyelitis]] (cmo)) is rescued when caspase-8 is inactivated in combination with either caspase-1 or the NLRP3 inflammasome — indicating redundant roles of inflammasome, caspase-1, and [[Caspase-8]] in a multifaceted PAN-optotic complex driving inflammatory disease.

## Summary and Future Perspectives

Recent work has resulted in tremendous progress in understanding cell death pathways with the discovery of novel regulators and the crosstalk between pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis. These studies revealed master regulators such as ZBP1 and TAK1 that act as key signaling nodes for cell death, and an intimate connection between the RIPK1/RIPK3–FADD–caspase-8 signaling complex and the execution of PAN-optosis. The PAN-optotic death complexes activated by the master regulators are multifaceted in nature with the potential to execute diverse forms of caspase-mediated cell death and promote necroptosis when caspases are inhibited.

> [!tip]
> Therapeutic outlook
> Further understanding of the master regulators of PAN-optosis may allow superior therapeutic approaches to target [[Cancer]], infection, and inflammatory diseases. Future studies should reveal additional mechanisms of PAN-optosis, its evolutionary relevance, and significance in health and disease.
