Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1) is a heme-containing enzyme that catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step in the kynurenine pathway (KP) of tryptophan (Trp) catabolism. It oxidizes L-tryptophan (and some other indoleamine substrates) to N-formylkynurenine in an O₂-dependent reaction. N-formylkynurenine is then rapidly converted to kynurenine (Kyn).

Enzymatic Function and Structure

IDO1 is a monomeric cytosolic protein (~45 kDa) encoded by the IDO1 gene on human chromosome 8p11.21. It belongs to the family of heme-dependent dioxygenases (EC 1.13.11.52), distinct from but functionally related to tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase (TDO, mainly hepatic) and IDO2 (a less active paralog).

[ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/3620)

The catalytic mechanism involves:
- A heme prosthetic group (iron center) that binds O₂.
- Reduction of inactive ferric (Fe³⁺) heme to active ferrous (Fe²⁺) form.
- Oxidative cleavage of the indole ring of Trp at the 2,3-position, producing N-formylkynurenine.
	[frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2021.800630/full)
Crystal structures (e.g., PDB entries like 2D0T, 5EK2) reveal a large domain with a deep heme-binding pocket and a smaller domain; the active site shows plasticity that allows inhibitor and substrate binding. IDO1 can act on multiple substrates, including D-Trp, 5-hydroxy-Trp, tryptamine, and serotonin, though L-Trp is primary.

[nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-01725-8)

Downstream, the KP leads to metabolites like kynurenic acid, 3-hydroxykynurenine, quinolinic acid (a neurotoxin and NAD⁺ precursor), and ultimately NAD⁺. This pathway accounts for ~95% of Trp degradation outside protein synthesis and serotonin production.

[sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1568163722000150)

Regulation

Induction: IDO1 is strongly induced by inflammatory cytokines, especially interferon-γ (IFNγ) via JAK/STAT signaling, but also by IFNα/β, TNF-α, IL-1, LPS, and other signals in immune and other cells. Glucocorticoids can synergize in some contexts.

[link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12967-026-07758-2)

Expression: High in placenta, lymph nodes, lungs, small intestine, and certain immune cells (dendritic cells/DCs, macrophages, monocytes). It is inducible in many tissues during inflammation.

[ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/3620)

Post-translational control: Phosphorylation of ITIM motifs recruits SOCS3, leading to ubiquitination and degradation (negative feedback). It can also mediate non-enzymatic signaling (e.g., PI3K activation).

[link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12967-026-07758-2)

Other modulators: IDO2 may compete for heme and act as a negative regulator in some settings.

Immunological Roles

IDO1 exerts immunosuppression through two main mechanisms:
1. Local Trp depletion: Tryptophan is essential for T-cell proliferation; depletion activates the GCN2 stress kinase, leading to cell cycle arrest, anergy, or apoptosis in effector T cells.
2. Kynurenine and metabolite accumulation: Kyn and derivatives activate the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR), promoting regulatory T cell (Treg) differentiation and suppressing effector T/NK cells. This creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment.
	frontiersin.org +1
Physiological roles include:
- Maternal-fetal tolerance (high placental expression).
- Antimicrobial defense (starving Trp-dependent pathogens like certain bacteria or viruses).
- Regulation of inflammation and autoimmunity.
- Tumor immune escape (often upregulated in cancers and tumor microenvironment).
	[en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoleamine_2,3-dioxygenase)
IDO1 is expressed in antigen-presenting cells (e.g., DCs) and modulates T-cell responses peripherally.

IDO1 in Diabetes (Context from Your Query)

Both T1D and T2D involve disrupted NAD⁺ homeostasis, with the KP (via IDO1/TDO) being a major Trp-to-NAD⁺ route.

[frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/oncology/articles/10.3389/fonc.2023.1142838/full)

Type 1 Diabetes (T1D):
- Pediatric studies link altered Trp–Kyn profiles and increased urinary kynurenine to specific IDO1 genotypes.
- Reduced IDO1 activity/response to IFNγ in some patients, potentially impairing immune regulation and contributing to autoimmunity against β-cells.
- Defective IDO1 in DCs (e.g., in NOD mouse models) and loss of IDO1 expression in human pancreatic β-cells observed in T1D. This may fail to maintain tolerance, exacerbating autoimmune destruction.
	pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov +2
Type 2 Diabetes (T2D):
- Often shows elevated KP activity/IDO1-related changes, linked to chronic low-grade inflammation, insulin resistance, and complications (e.g., nephropathy, retinopathy).
- Higher Kyn/Trp ratios (proxy for IDO activity) sometimes correlate with poor glycemic control or oxidative stress.
- KP metabolites may influence NAD⁺ levels, inflammation, and vascular complications. Metformin and other interventions can modulate KP in T2D contexts.
	pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov +1
Overall, the pathway's role is context-dependent: protective via tolerance in autoimmunity but potentially detrimental when overactive in metabolic inflammation.

Therapeutic Targeting

IDO1 is a major target in immuno-oncology due to its role in tumor evasion. Inhibitors (e.g., epacadostat, navoximod, linrodostat/BMS-986205) have been tested in trials, often in combination with checkpoint inhibitors (PD-1/PD-L1), though results have been mixed.Potential in autoimmunity, transplantation, and neurological conditions (due to neuroactive KP metabolites). Challenges include isoform selectivity, compensatory pathways (TDO, IDO2), and context-specific effects.

[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9786828/)

Additional Notes

- Polymorphisms: IDO1 SNPs associate with altered activity, infection risk (e.g., aspergillosis), autoimmunity predisposition, and possibly diabetes-related traits.
	[pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6514051/)
- Broader links: KP dysregulation implicated in neurodegeneration, depression, chronic inflammation, and infections (e.g., HIV modulates IDO1).
IDO1 sits at the intersection of metabolism, immunity, and inflammation. Its activity integrates nutrient sensing (Trp availability) with immune modulation and NAD⁺ biosynthesis, making it relevant to diabetes, cancer, and beyond. Research continues on precise roles in disease subtypes and optimal therapeutic windows.
