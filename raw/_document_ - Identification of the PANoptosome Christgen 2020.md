# Identification of the PANoptosome: A Molecular Platform Triggering Pyroptosis, Apoptosis, and Necroptosis (PANoptosis)

- Authors: Shelbi Christgen, Min Zheng, Sannula Kesavardhana, Rajendra Karki, R. K. Subbarao Malireddi, Balaji Banoth, David E. Place, Benoit Briard, Bhesh Raj Sharma, Shraddha Tuladhar, Parimal Samir, Amanda Burton and Thirumala-Devi Kanneganti (Department of Immunology, St. Jude Children's Research Hospital, Memphis, TN, USA)
- Journal: Frontiers in Cellular and Infection Microbiology, Volume 10, Article 237 (received 02 April 2020, accepted 24 April 2020, published 29 May 2020; doi: 10.3389/fcimb.2020.00237)
- Keywords: [[PANoptosis]], [[PANoptosome]], [[NLRP3]], [[ASC]], [[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]], [[Caspase-1]], [[Caspase-8]]

## Abstract

Programmed cell death plays crucial roles in organismal development and host defense. Recent studies have highlighted mechanistic overlaps and extensive, multifaceted crosstalk between [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]], three programmed cell death pathways traditionally considered autonomous. The growing body of evidence, in conjunction with the identification of molecules controlling the concomitant activation of all three pathways by pathological triggers, has led to the development of the concept of [[PANoptosis]]. During [[PANoptosis]], inflammatory cell death occurs through the collective activation of pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis, which can circumvent pathogen-mediated inhibition of individual death pathways. Many of the molecular details of this emerging pathway are unclear. Here, the authors describe the activation of [[PANoptosis]] by bacterial and viral triggers and report protein interactions that reveal the formation of a [[PANoptosome]] complex. Infection of macrophages with [[Influenza A Virus]], [[Vesicular Stomatitis Virus]], [[Salmonella Typhimurium]], or [[Listeria monocytogenes]] resulted in robust cell death and the hallmarks of [[PANoptosis]] activation. Combined deletion of the PANoptotic components caspase-1, caspase-11 ([[Caspase-11]]), [[RIPK3]], and [[Caspase-8]] largely protected macrophages from cell death induced by these pathogens, while deletion of individual components provided reduced or no protection. Further, molecules from the pyroptotic, apoptotic, and necroptotic cell death pathways interacted to form a single molecular complex termed the [[PANoptosome]].

> [!important]
> Core claim — infection of wild-type [[Bone marrow-derived macrophages|BMDMs]] with [[Influenza A Virus]] (IAV), [[Vesicular Stomatitis Virus]] (VSV), [[Salmonella Typhimurium]] (S. Typhimurium strain SL1344) or [[Listeria monocytogenes]] (strain 10403S) triggers concomitant pyroptosis, extrinsic apoptosis and necroptosis; combined deletion of caspase-1/11 + [[RIPK3]] + [[Caspase-8]] largely protects against S. Typhimurium- and IAV-induced death and partially protects against L. monocytogenes- and VSV-induced death.

## Introduction

The execution of programmed cell death is a complex process required for proper organismal development and homeostasis. Activation of multiple programmed cell death pathways, including [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]], is vital for host defense against pathogenic invaders. [[Pyroptosis]] and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] are characterized as lytic, immunologically active forms of cell death, while [[Apoptosis]] has historically been considered immunologically silent.

### Pyroptosis machinery

[[Innate Immune System|Innate immune sensors]] respond to pathogen-associated and damage-associated molecular patterns ([[PAMPs]] and [[DAMPs]]) to mediate formation of multimeric signaling complexes known as the [[Inflammasome]].

> [!info]
> Canonical pyroptosis sequence — sensor activation leads to recruitment of [[ASC]] (apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a CARD), then recruitment and self-activation of the inflammatory cysteine protease [[Caspase-1]], which processes the pyroptosis executioner [[Gasdermin D]] (GSDMD); the GSDMD N-terminus oligomerizes to form membrane pores, allowing release of [[Pro-inflammatory Cytokines|proinflammatory cytokines]].

### Necroptosis machinery

Necroptosis is primarily regulated by proteins containing a RIP homotypic interaction motif ([[RHIM]]), including [[RIPK1]] and [[RIPK3]]. Activation of [[RIPK3]] leads to phosphorylation of [[MLKL]] (mixed lineage kinase domain-like), the executioner of necroptosis.

> [!info]
> MLKL mechanism — phosphorylated [[MLKL]] self-oligomerizes, translocates to the [[Plasma Membrane|plasma membrane]], and forms pores, releasing [[Cytokines|cytokines]] and [[DAMPs]] that propagate inflammatory signals, analogous to [[Gasdermin D]] pore function in [[Pyroptosis]].

### Apoptosis machinery

Similar to pyroptosis regulation by the [[Inflammasome]], activation of [[Apoptosis]] is regulated through large signaling platforms. The intrinsic pathway is mediated by oligomerization of [[APAF]] (apoptotic protease-activating factor) to form a [[Caspase-9]]-activation platform known as the [[Apoptosome]], while signaling through death receptors and formation of the [[Death-Inducing Signaling Complex|death-inducing signaling complex]] (DISC) mediates activation of [[Caspase-8]] and the extrinsic pathway. Activation of [[Caspase-8]] or [[Caspase-9]] ultimately activates executioner caspases including [[Caspase-3]] and [[Caspase-7]].

### PANoptosis concept and master regulators

> [!tip]
> Master regulators — [[ZBP1]] (Z-DNA-binding protein 1) was found crucial for activation of all three pathways after [[Influenza A Virus]] infection; loss of [[TAK1]] (TGF-beta-activated kinase 1) activity through genetic deletion or pathogen-mediated inhibition leads to activation of [[Pyroptosis]], [[Apoptosis]], and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]].

These findings led to conceptualization of [[PANoptosis]], a form of inflammatory cell death whereby pyroptosis, apoptosis and necroptosis become activated. The coordinated activation provides an effective backup strategy to circumvent pathogenic evasion strategies — consistent with the plant "guard hypothesis," whereby blockade of an innate immune signaling pathway by a pathogen results in activation of another pathway. When pyroptosis is blocked, [[Caspase-8]] can utilize assembled [[Inflammasome]] machinery to induce a [[Caspase-8]]-dependent death referred to as both apoptosis and secondary pyroptosis. The authors therefore hypothesize a common master death complex — the [[PANoptosome]] — forming a flexible skeleton to which core components of different cell death pathways are recruited. Supporting this, loss-of-[[TAK1]] studies revealed physical interactions between pyroptotic, apoptotic and necroptotic molecules.

## Materials and Methods (condensed)

- Mice: Casp1/11-/-, Gsdmd-/-, Ripk3-/-, Ripk3-/-Casp8-/-, Casp1/11-/-Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- and Gsdmd-/-Mlkl-/- (generated by crossing Gsdmd-/- with Mlkl-/-), all backcrossed to C57BL/6 for at least 8 generations; SNPs sequenced to confirm background; St. Jude IACUC-approved protocols.
- [[Bone marrow-derived macrophages|BMDMs]]: primary BMDMs cultivated 6 days at 37 C in IMDM + 10% FBS + 30% L929-conditioned medium + 1% non-essential amino acids + 1% penicillin/streptomycin; seeded antibiotic-free at 1 x 10^6 cells/well overnight before stimulation/infection.
- Viral culture: IAV A/Puerto Rico/8/34 H1N1 (PR8) by reverse genetics, propagated in embryonated chicken eggs, titered by plaque assay in MDCK cells; VSV propagated in Vero cells at MOI 0.01, titered in Vero cells.
- Bacterial culture: S. Typhimurium SL1344 in LB broth aerobically at 37 C overnight then 1:10 subculture 4 h to log phase; L. monocytogenes 10403S in Brain-Heart Infusion broth aerobically at 37 C.
- Stimulation/infection: IAV MOI 20 and VSV MOI 1 in high-glucose DMEM, 2 h adsorption then 10% FBS supplementation; S. Typhimurium MOI 2 for 6 h and L. monocytogenes MOI 5 for 8 h; [[TAK1]] inhibition with 0.1 uM [[5Z-7-oxozeaenol]] (TAK1i) for 1 h followed by [[LPS]] (100 ng/mL) post-treatment.
- Immunoblot: caspase-1 analysis from combined lysate + supernatant (caspase lysis buffer + 4x SDS loading buffer); signaling analysis from RIPA lysates after supernatant removal and PBS wash; 8-15% polyacrylamide gels, PVDF transfer, 5% skim milk block; antibodies against caspase-1, caspase-3/cleaved caspase-3, caspase-7/cleaved caspase-7, caspase-8/cleaved caspase-8, [[ZBP1]], [[NLRP3]], [[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]], pMLKL, GFP, Flag, [[Gasdermin D]], GAPDH, mCherry, [[FADD]], [[ASC]].
- Real-time cell death: BMDMs seeded 0.5 x 10^6/well in 24-well plates, 20 nM SYTOX Green; IncuCyte S3 imaging with red masks marking dead cells.
- Immunoprecipitation (overexpression): HEK293T cells transfected with FLAG-[[NLRP3]], [[RIPK3]]-GFP, mRIPK1, mCherry-[[ZBP1]], mASC-turboGFP, CMV-[[Caspase-8]] constructs (600 ng each, 30 h); pan-caspase inhibitor zVAD-fmk to prevent overexpression-induced death; NP-40 lysis, 13,000 x g clearing, overnight primary antibody + Protein A/G-plus agarose, lysis-buffer washes, boiling in 2x SDS buffer.
- Immunoprecipitation (endogenous): wild-type C57BL/6 BMDMs stimulated with [[LPS]] alone, [[LPS]] + TAK1i, or [[LPS]] + TAK1i + zVAD (30 mM per text); NP-40 lysis, 3 mg indicated primary antibody overnight at 4 C + Protein A/G agarose 2 h, washes, elution in sample buffer.
- Confocal microscopy: wild-type BMDMs infected with S. Typhimurium MOI 1 for 1.5 h, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized in 0.5% Triton X-100, blocked in 5% BSA/PBS; primaries anti-[[ASC]], anti-cleaved [[Caspase-3]], anti-phospho-[[MLKL]]; Alexa Fluor 488/568/647 secondaries; DAPI counterstain; Leica SP-8, FIJI/ImageJ analysis.

## Results

### Loss of PANoptotic molecules protects against infection-induced cell death

Necroptosis contributes to viral infections, and necroptotic pathways are activated after S. Typhimurium or L. monocytogenes infection, so the authors tested [[PANoptosis]] activation after S. Typhimurium, L. monocytogenes, IAV and VSV infections. Infection of wild-type BMDMs with each pathogen produced robust death (Figure 1A-B). Genetic blockade design: [[Pyroptosis]] blocked by CASP1/11 or GSDMD deletion; [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] blocked by [[RIPK3]] or [[MLKL]] deletion; [[Apoptosis]] (extrinsic) blocked by [[Caspase-8]] deletion.

> [!info]
> Single-pathway knockout outcomes — loss of pyroptotic molecules ([[Caspase-1]]/[[Caspase-11]] or [[Gasdermin D]]) partially protected against S. Typhimurium-induced death, while loss of necroptotic ([[MLKL]], [[RIPK3]]) or extrinsic apoptotic ([[Caspase-8]]) molecules had little impact; L. monocytogenes death was not robustly inhibited by loss of any single pathway nor by combined pyroptosis + necroptosis or necroptosis + apoptosis loss; for viral infections, loss of necroptotic and apoptotic molecules partially protected; [[RIPK3]] + [[Caspase-8]] loss appeared to fully protect against IAV death.

Because IAV activates [[Pyroptosis]] through the [[NLRP3]] [[Inflammasome]] and [[Caspase-8]] regulates [[NLRP3]] [[Inflammasome]] activation, pyroptosis is also blocked in Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- BMDMs after IAV infection.

> [!important]
> Triple-pathway blockade — Casp1/11-/-Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- BMDMs were protected from S. Typhimurium- or IAV-induced death (6 h and 16 h respectively); L. monocytogenes- and VSV-induced death was reduced but mild death persisted, implying additional pathways beyond [[PANoptosis]] contribute in those infections.

### Bacterial and viral infections activate PANoptosis

Readouts by western blot: [[Pyroptosis]] via [[Caspase-1]] and [[Gasdermin D]] cleavage; [[Apoptosis]] via [[Caspase-8]], [[Caspase-3]] and [[Caspase-7]] cleavage; [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] via [[MLKL]] phosphorylation. Infection of wild-type BMDMs with S. Typhimurium, L. monocytogenes, IAV or VSV activated all three arms (Figures 2A-D). Confocal imaging after S. Typhimurium infection confirmed [[ASC]] speck formation (pyroptosis initiation, consistent with [[Caspase-1]] activation), cleaved [[Caspase-3]] (apoptosis effector), and phosphorylated [[MLKL]] localized to the cell membrane (necroptosis) in wild-type BMDMs (Figure 2E, scale bar 10 um). These activations were absent in Casp1/11-/-Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- BMDMs after S. Typhimurium or IAV infection.

> [!warning]
> VSV exception — VSV infection of Casp1/11-/-Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- BMDMs still produced [[Caspase-7]] and [[Caspase-3]] cleavage, consistent with prior reports of VSV-driven intrinsic ([[Caspase-9]]-mediated) [[Apoptosis]]; intrinsic apoptosis therefore contributes alongside [[Pyroptosis]], extrinsic apoptosis and [[_document_ - Necroptosis (2014), Linkermann, Green]] during VSV infection. No [[Caspase-3]]/[[Caspase-7]] cleavage was seen in triple-knockout BMDMs infected with L. monocytogenes, so residual L. monocytogenes death is not explained by intrinsic apoptosis.

### Formation of the PANoptosome

The involvement of large signaling complexes in programmed cell death, plus prior colocalization of PANoptotic molecules, motivated testing whether pyroptotic, apoptotic and necroptotic molecules form one [[PANoptosome]] complex. Transient overexpression in HEK293 cells: pulldown of FLAG-tagged [[NLRP3]] co-immunoprecipitated [[ZBP1]], [[RIPK3]] and [[RIPK1]] individually (Figure 3A); immunoprecipitation of [[RIPK3]] from cells expressing all three pathways' components co-precipitated [[Caspase-8]], [[ASC]], [[RIPK1]], [[NLRP3]] and [[ZBP1]] (Figure 3B).

> [!important]
> Interaction finding — pyroptotic ([[NLRP3]], [[ASC]]), apoptotic ([[Caspase-8]]), necroptotic ([[RIPK1]], [[RIPK3]]) and sensor ([[ZBP1]]) molecules co-immunoprecipitate as a single complex, supporting direct interactions and assembly of a [[PANoptosome]].

### TAK1 inhibition drives PANoptosis and endogenous PANoptosome formation

[[TAK1]] regulates cellular homeostasis; its loss spontaneously activates [[PANoptosis]]. Pathogenic [[Yersinia]] species inhibit [[TAK1]], and [[TAK1]] inhibition during [[Yersinia]] infection activates [[PANoptosis]] — so TAK1i + [[LPS]] priming models [[PANoptosis]] in sterile and infectious settings. TAK1i (5Z-7-Oxozeaenol) + [[LPS]] priming induced death in wild-type BMDMs (Figure 4A); Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- and Casp1/11-/-Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- BMDMs were completely protected, attributed to multifaceted [[Caspase-8]] roles in apoptosis and pyroptosis. Immunoblots of TAK1i + [[LPS]] wild-type BMDMs showed cleavage of [[Caspase-1]], [[Gasdermin D]], [[Caspase-8]], [[Caspase-3]] and [[Caspase-7]] plus [[MLKL]] phosphorylation, while [[LPS]] alone did not activate these pathways (Figure 4B).

> [!tip]
> Capturing the endogenous complex — with TAK1i + [[LPS]] alone, minimal interaction of [[RIPK1]] or [[ASC]] with other PANoptotic molecules was recovered; because rapid death/proteolysis was hypothesized to destroy the complex, adding pan-caspase inhibitor zVAD-fmk allowed co-immunoprecipitation of [[RIPK1]], [[NLRP3]], [[ASC]], [[Caspase-8]] and [[FADD]] upon [[RIPK1]] or [[ASC]] pulldown (Figure 4C). Caspase inhibition therefore enables efficient recovery of the TAK1-inhibition-induced [[PANoptosome]].

Figure 5 schematizes a representative (IAV-induced) [[PANoptosome]], highlighting domains hypothesized to mediate assembly: [[Death Domain|DEATH domains]], [[DED|DEDs]], [[CARD|CARDs]], [[PYRIN|PYRIN domains]] and [[RHIM|RHIMs]].

## Discussion

Recent work established extensive crosstalk between inflammasome/pyroptosis, apoptosis and necroptosis, including dual roles of [[FADD]] and [[Caspase-8]] in [[NLRP3]] activation, [[TAK1]]-[[RIPK1]] control of homeostasis and [[Yersinia]]-mediated pyroptosis, and [[RIPK3]] regulation of pyroptosis, necroptosis and apoptosis. [[PANoptosis]] is defined as pyroptosis, apoptosis and necroptosis becoming activated in the same cell population. This study demonstrates IAV, VSV, S. Typhimurium and L. monocytogenes each activate [[PANoptosis]], and that deleting [[Caspase-1]], [[Caspase-11]], [[RIPK3]] and [[Caspase-8]] protects against S. Typhimurium and IAV death and partially against L. monocytogenes and VSV death.

> [!info]
> Open regulator question — [[TAK1]] and [[ZBP1]] are demonstrated master regulators of [[PANoptosis]], but additional undiscovered regulators likely exist; S. Typhimurium activates pyroptosis independently of [[ZBP1]] or [[TAK1]], implying at least one other unidentified regulator.

The authors propose that, analogous to inflammasomes, [[PANoptosome]] composition differs by stimulus and sensing receptor, but each [[PANoptosome]] uses homotypic-interaction domains ([[Death Domain|DEATH]], [[DED]], [[CARD]], [[PYRIN]], [[RHIM]]) to assemble. A defined set of assembly-promoting proteins may exist across stimuli — e.g. [[Caspase-6]] promotes IAV-induced [[PANoptosis]] by facilitating [[ZBP1]]-[[RIPK3]] interaction as a component of the [[ZBP1]] [[PANoptosome]], though its role in other stimuli is unclear.

> [!warning]
> Residual-death question — VSV and L. monocytogenes still kill Casp1/11-/-Ripk3-/-Casp8-/- BMDMs; VSV residual death maps to intrinsic ([[Caspase-9]]-mediated) [[Apoptosis]], while L. monocytogenes residual death involves an unidentified mechanism. This raises whether [[PANoptosis]]/[[PANoptosome]]s also regulate other programmed death pathways (e.g. intrinsic apoptosis) or undescribed death pathways — requiring in-depth dissection, structural interaction mapping, and evaluation of the [[PANoptosome]] as a druggable target for inflammatory death and aberrant immunity.
