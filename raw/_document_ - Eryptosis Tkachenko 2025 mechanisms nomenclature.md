## Abstract

Early studies have shown that erythrocytes have caspase-3 and caspase-8 and are capable
of dying through an apoptotic-like cell death triggered by Ca2+ ionophores, with cell
shrinkage, membrane blebbing, and phosphatidylserine externalization. As mature
erythrocytes lack apoptotic mitochondrial machinery, this modality was named
"eryptosis". This review summarizes current understanding, formulates nomenclature
and interpretation guidelines, and covers morphology, biochemistry, health/disease
roles, and druggability. (Cell Death Dis. 2025 Jul 1;16(1):467. doi:
10.1038/s41419-025-07784-w. PMCID: PMC12216432, PMID: 40592821. CC-BY 4.0.)

## Key points for wiki

- Core triggers: Ca2+ influx via cation channels (TRPC6 human / TRPC4-5 mouse, NMDA/AMPA,
  PIEZO1, Cav2.1) -> Gardos channel (KCa3.1/KCNN4) K+ efflux -> shrinkage; flippase
  inhibition + scramblase activation -> [[Phosphatidylserine]] exposure; calpain-1
  cytoskeleton breakdown -> blebbing.
- Parallel arms: ROS-mediated, ceramide/sphingomyelinase-driven (PAF regulated),
  Fas/FasL extrinsic, caspase-dependent (caspase-3/8 present but dispensable for
  Ca2+-driven death), JAK3/CK1alpha/p38/PKC/AMPK kinase regulation, NO/cGKI inhibition,
  energy (glucose/G6PD/GSH) sensing.
- Nomenclature: distinguish eryptosis (regulated, cleared in minutes) from senescence
  (cleared in days) and hemolysis (accidental). NCCD avoids the term; authors defend it.
- Crosstalk: mature RBCs also express RIPK1/RIPK3/MLKL -> "erythronecroptosis";
  ferroptosis in mature RBCs unconfirmed -> "iron overload-driven cell death" preferred.
- Relevance to page: validates Ca2+/ceramide/energy-depletion triggers, Annexin V /
  Fluo-3 / forward-scatter / ceramide detection, silent macrophage clearance, and the
  EPO-inhibits-eryptosis note (erythropoietin blunts cation-channel Ca2+ entry).
