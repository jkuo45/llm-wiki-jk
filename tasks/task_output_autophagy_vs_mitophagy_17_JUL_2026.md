## Mechanistic Differences (Autophagy vs. Mitophagy)

**Relationship:** Mitophagy is a *subtype* of selective autophagy targeting only mitochondria. Both converge on the same terminal step (autophagosome → lysosome → autolysosome) but differ in selectivity, initiation, and cargo machinery.

| Axis               | Autophagy (bulk/selective)                                   | Mitophagy                                                                         |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cargo              | Cytosol, protein aggregates, whole organelles                | Damaged/dysfunctional mitochondria only                                           |
| Initiation trigger | Global nutrient/energy stress — AMPK↑, mTORC1↓               | *Local* mitochondrial damage — loss of ΔΨm (depolarization)                       |
| Key pathway        | ULK1 complex → Beclin1/Vps34 → Atg12-5-16L1 → LC3 lipidation | PINK1/Parkin ubiquitin-dependent OR receptor-mediated (BNIP3, NIX, FUNDC1)        |
| Prerequisite step  | None (phagophore engulfs cargo)                              | **Mitochondrial fission** (DRP1) required to sever damaged segment                |
| Threshold          | Gradual with AMPK/mTOR shift                                 | **PINK1 input threshold** (positive feedback) — sub-threshold stress does nothing |
| Receptors          | p62/SQSTM1 (general)                                         | PINK1→Parkin→ubiquitin recognized by p62, OPTN, NDP52, NBR1                       |

**Shared:** Atg12-Atg5-Atg16L1 & LC3 lipidation; TFEB/TFE3 transcriptional coordination; lysosomal degradation. Impairment of either → Aging, Senescence, Neurodegeneration (PD, AD).

## Fasting Dose & Cadence — Autophagy vs. Mitophagy

**Principle:** Mitophagy engages *downstream of and more slowly than* general autophagy. It requires sustained depolarization signaling that short fasts may not reach.

| Protocol | Autophagy | Mitophagy | Primary effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily IF (16:8, ~14–16 h) | Basal recurrent pulses | Light, tonic | Maintenance; prevents accumulation |
| Quarterly 72 h fast | Strong systemic surge | Robust selective clearance | "Reset" of mitochondrial pool |
| Annual 72 h fast | Same magnitude | Same magnitude, longer gap | Deep clean; weaker cumulative benefit |

Notes: TRF = "simplest way to activate AMPK and mitophagy." 72 h fast crosses into deep ketogenesis + maximal mTORC1 suppression, engaging mitophagy at scale.

## Impact by Life Stage

- **Youth:** Autophagic/mitophagic flux naturally high. Fasting = training/hormetic stimulus (Mitohormesis, Hormetic Window). Risk: prolonged fasting impairs growth (IGF-1 suppression undesirable).
- **Mid-age:** Flux begins declining — SWEET SPOT for intervention. Quarterly 72 h fasts counteract early mitophagy drop, limit ROS→Inflammaging. Maximal benefit on Metabolic Health, Neuroprotection, Cancer Prevention.
- **Old age:** Basal mitophagy markedly impaired (PD/AD hallmark). Daily IF becomes MORE valuable (recurrent low-dose signal elderly cells can't generate). BUT prolonged 72 h fasts carry higher risk (sarcopenia, frailty, bone density). Prefer periodic IF over aggressive fasting.

## Lifestyle Phenotype Comparison (Gym Trainer vs. Desk 9–5)

| Axis | Gym Trainer (high activity) | Desk 9–5 (sedentary) |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline AMPK/mitophagy | High (exercise-driven) | Low (sedentary) |
| Daily IF (16:8) | Maintenance, easy | **Essential floor** |
| 72 h fast frequency | Annual / post-deload | **Quarterly** |
| mTORC1 concern | Avoid chronic suppression (recovery) | Beneficial (breaks anabolic stagnation) |
| Risk of over-fasting | Sarcopenia / poor recovery | Low; mainly adherence |

**Summary:** Gym trainer is already "exercising" his autophagy system → needs less fasting volume, must protect mTORC1 for repair. Desk worker is metabolically detrained → needs daily IF floor + periodic 72 h shocks to hit mitophagy thresholds lifestyle never reaches.

## Lifestyle Adjustments for Mitophagy (NOT required for Autophagy)

Research confirms fasting triggers autophagic machinery but often stops short of the PINK1/Parkin depolarization threshold that only **mitochondrial work** crosses.

### Generate mitochondrial STRESS, not just energy deficit
- General autophagy: AMPK↑/mTORC1↓ via any fast.
- Mitophagy: needs ΔΨm depolarization → PINK1 stabilization → Parkin recruitment (positive-feedback threshold; Cell Reports 2023).
- **Adjustments:**
  - **Acute HIIT / exhaustive exercise** — PINK1-Parkin mitophagy "greatly increases under exhaustive exercise" (ScienceDirect 2025). Moderate aerobic → autophagy + biogenesis; HIIT = specific mitophagy trigger.
  - **Cold exposure** — drives mito-respiration (UCP/PGC-1α thermogenesis); mitophagy-relevant stress distinct from fasting.

### Drive mitochondrial FISSION first
- Mitophagy needs DRP1-mediated fission to sever damaged segment (prerequisite; notes). Bulk autophagy has no topological requirement.
- **Adjustment:** Exercise (FNDC5/irisin signaling, Frontiers 2024) promotes fission→mitophagy. Fasting alone may leave elongated, network mitochondria that RESIST mitophagy (senescent phenotype). Movement = fission stimulus fasting can't provide.

### Mitophagy-specific nutraceuticals
- Autophagy inducers (rapamycin, spermidine, Metformin) = mTOR/AMPK broad; not mito-selective.
- **Adjustment:** **[[Urolithin A]]** — only compound with human RCT evidence (Singh et al. 2022 Cell Reports Medicine; Nature Aging 2026) for directly inducing mitophagy; improves muscle strength/endurance + T-cell fitness in middle-aged adults. Pomegranate/ellagitannin sources support endogenous production.

### Protect/feed the BIOGENESIS counterpart
- Mitophagy is half the loop; operates with Mitochondrial Biogenesis (PGC-1α). Removing without replacing → energy deficit. Autophagy lacks this strict rebuild coupling.
- **Adjustment:** Ensure protein/amino-acid sufficiency + recovery windows for PGC-1α biogenesis. Over-fasting (chronic mTORC1 suppression) blunts biogenesis — risk unique to aggressive mitophagy pursuit.

### Avoid chronic CALCIUM overload & oxidative suppression
- Mitophagy uniquely sensitive to Parkin S-nitrosylation and calcium stress (notes: MERCS/ER contact sites). General autophagy far less calcium-gated.
- **Adjustment:** Manage chronic psychological stress, ER-calcium dysregulation (sleep, vagal tone) — specifically impairs PINK1/Parkin limb.

### Summary Table — Lever Specificity
| Lever | Autophagy only | Mitophagy-specific |
|---|---|---|
| Fasting / IF | ✅ sufficient | ⚠️ sub-threshold alone |
| HIIT / exhaustive exercise | helps | ✅ **required** for fission + depolarization |
| Cold exposure | minimal | ✅ mito-respiratory stress |
| Urolithin A | — | ✅ selective human evidence |
| Post-fast protein/biogenesis support | optional | ✅ necessary |

**Bottom line:** To get mitophagy beyond autophagy, you must *stress and fragment mitochondria* (hard exercise, cold) and can selectively amplify with Urolithin A. Fasting triggers autophagic machinery but often stops short of the PINK1/Parkin depolarization threshold that only mitochondrial work crosses.
