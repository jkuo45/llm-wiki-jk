To optimize for mitohormesis and autophagy using the compounds listed in your protocol, the core principle is to **separate the stressors (which trigger the hormetic response) from the antioxidants and recovery agents (which would blunt the stress signal).** 

Mitohormesis relies on a transient burst of Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) to signal mitochondrial biogenesis and cellular repair. If antioxidants are present concurrently, they neutralize the ROS before the adaptive signal can be transmitted. Autophagy is best triggered in a fasted, low-energy state (inhibiting mTOR, activating AMPK).

Here is a pharmacological and physiological analysis of how these compounds interact on a daily/hourly timescale, and a proposed scheduling strategy to maximize their distinct mechanisms.

### Pharmacological Analysis & Interactions

#### 1. The Oxidative Pulse & Mitohormesis (Morning/Fasted)
*   **Target Compounds:** Adrenochrome / Carbazochrome / Aminoguanidine, Methylene Blue.
*   **Mechanism:** These compounds act as mild pro-oxidants or electron cyclers in the mitochondria. Methylene Blue directly cycles electrons in the electron transport chain (ETC), generating a mild ROS signal. Adrenochrome products can also induce oxidative stress.
*   **Interaction Warning:** **Do not combine with direct antioxidants** (like GlyNAC or high-dose Carnosine) concurrently. Antioxidants will quench the ROS pulse, completely nullifying the mitohormetic benefit.
*   **Synergy:** **NAD+ Precursors (NMN/NR)**. Activating mitohormesis requires NAD+ as a cofactor for SIRT proteins (sirtuins), which drive mitochondrial biogenesis (PGC-1α). Taking NMN/NR with or slightly before the oxidative pulse provides the "fuel" for the adaptive response.

#### 2. Autophagy & Senolytics (Morning/Fasted)
*   **Target Compounds:** Rapamycin, Spermidine, Fisetin, Urolithin A.
*   **Mechanism:** Rapamycin inhibits mTOR; Spermidine induces autophagy via EP300 inhibition; Fisetin is a senolytic; Urolithin A induces mitophagy (clearing dysfunctional mitochondria).
*   **Interactions:** These work best in a low-nutrient state. Combining them with the morning oxidative pulse is highly synergistic, as both pathways (AMPK activation/mTOR inhibition and mitohormesis) represent a cellular "stress and clean-up" mode.

#### 3. Recovery, Antioxidant & Structural Support (Afternoon/Evening)
*   **Target Compounds:** GlyNAC, Carnosine, Creatine, EDTA.
*   **Mechanism:** GlyNAC replenishes glutathione (the master intracellular antioxidant); Carnosine prevents advanced glycation end-products (AGEs); Creatine buffers mitochondrial ATP.
*   **Interactions:** These should be introduced *hours after* the oxidative pulse. Once the stress signal has successfully triggered nuclear transcription (e.g., Nrf2 pathway), introducing GlyNAC and Carnosine helps the cell manage the aftermath, reduce collateral damage, and rebuild.

### Proposed Daily / Hourly Optimization Protocol

To optimize the pharmacokinetics and avoid signal cancellation, the day is split into a **Stress/Cleanup Phase** and a **Recovery/Rebuild Phase**.

#### Phase 1: The Fasted "Stress & Clean" Window (06:00 - 10:00)
*Goal: Induce autophagy, trigger mitochondrial ROS signaling, inhibit mTOR.*

*   **06:00 (Upon Waking, Fasted):**
    *   **Autophagy Inducers:** Spermidine & Urolithin A (initiates mitophagy and cellular cleanup while glucose/insulin are low).
    *   **Senolytic/mTOR Inhibition:** Rapamycin (if dosing daily/weekly) and Fisetin.
*   **07:00 (Pre-Activity/Exercise):**
    *   **NAD+ Support:** NMN, NR (primes the SIRT1 pathways).
*   **08:00 (The Mitohormetic Pulse):**
    *   **Oxidative Stressors:** Methylene Blue & Adrenochrome/Aminoguanidine complex. 
    *   *Note:* Taking these before a period of metabolic demand (like light exercise or deep cognitive work) amplifies the mitohormetic signal.

#### Phase 2: The Refeed & Transition Window (12:00 - 14:00)
*Goal: Provide building blocks, transition out of catabolism.*

*   **12:00 (First Meal):**
    *   Break the fast. The spike in amino acids and insulin will naturally transition the body out of autophagy (mTOR activation begins).

#### Phase 3: The Recovery & Antioxidant Window (16:00 - 20:00)
*Goal: Quench residual ROS, prevent glycation, replenish energy buffers.*

*   **16:00 (Afternoon):**
    *   **Energy Buffer:** Creatine. (Supports mitochondrial ATP production during the recovery phase).
*   **18:00 - 20:00 (Evening/With Dinner):**
    *   **Antioxidant & Glycation Defense:** GlyNAC, Carnosine, EDTA.
    *   *Why here?* Administering glutathione precursors (GlyNAC) 10-12 hours after the oxidative pulse ensures you do not blunt the morning's hormetic signal, but you provide maximum antioxidant protection while the body repairs during sleep.

### Key Considerations for the Adrenochrome/Aminoguanidine Protocol
You asked if all three (Adrenochrome, Carbazochrome, Aminoguanidine) are required. 
*   **Aminoguanidine** is primarily an inhibitor of advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) and diamine oxidase (DAO), and also an inhibitor of inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS).
*   **Carbazochrome** is an oxidized derivative of adrenaline used as a hemostatic agent.
*   Using these together creates a highly specific, potent oxidative/glycation-modulating environment. If optimizing for *pure* mitohormesis, a simpler electron cycler like Methylene Blue is often sufficient. If you are specifically targeting the intersection of catecholamine oxidation (adrenochrome pathways) and glycation, then the Aminoguanidine pairing is necessary to prevent the reactive quinones from causing uncontrolled damage.
